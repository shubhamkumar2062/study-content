# How to use this guide

Adult Health Nursing II (N-AHN(II)225) is the continuation of Adult Health Nursing I, moving from the foundational medical-surgical nursing you studied in Semester 3 into specialist systems — ENT, eyes, kidneys and urinary tract, burns and reconstructive surgery, neurology, HIV/AIDS and oncology, emergency and critical care, and geriatric and occupational health nursing. It is a large and demanding subject and accounts for a significant portion of the Semester 4 theory examination marks. Study it in parallel with Pathology II and Pharmacology II, because those subjects explain the diseases and drugs you will use in your AHN-II patient care.

## Exam pattern

N-AHN(II)225 is examined at the end of Semester 4 as a **standalone theory paper** following the standard INC 2021 B.Sc Nursing exam format. Confirm the exact paper pattern and marking scheme with your college, as universities may print the paper in slightly different formats.

**Typical INC B.Sc Nursing end-semester theory paper (75 marks, 3 hours)**

| Section | Type | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 20 objective questions (MCQ or fill-in-the-blank) — attempt all | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 questions | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 questions | 7 × 5 = 35 |
| **Total** | | **75** |

There is no internal choice in Section A. Section B and C give you a choice of which questions to attempt. Always read all Section C questions before choosing — pick the ones you can answer most fully.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. **Start every answer with the nursing assessment.** For any disorder, the examiner expects you to describe how you would recognise and assess the patient before discussing management. Use the nursing process frame: Assessment → Nursing Diagnosis → Planning → Intervention → Evaluation.

2. **Learn the BCLS sequence precisely.** The sequence (C–A–B, 30:2, rate 100–120, depth 5–6 cm, AED steps) is asked every year — in Section A as single MCQs and in Section C as a short answer.

3. **Distinguish airway management priorities.** In emergencies — anaphylaxis, status epilepticus, unconscious patient — airway comes first. Name the specific airway technique used (recovery position, oropharyngeal airway, endotracheal intubation, tracheostomy).

4. **Give pathophysiology before management.** Even in a 5-mark short answer, one sentence of pathophysiology (why the problem occurs) lifts the answer above a nursing-care list.

5. **Use comparison tables.** The examiner values a well-drawn table for: burn degrees (look vs sensation vs healing), nephritic vs nephrotic syndrome, AKI vs CKD, delirium vs dementia, ischaemic vs haemorrhagic stroke. Practise drawing each from memory.

6. **Name drugs with their class.** For ART, name at least one drug per class (NRTI, NNRTI, PI, INSTI). For cancer pain, state the WHO ladder step and an example drug at each step. For epilepsy, name the AED and its main side effect.

7. **For long essays (10 marks):** use a structured heading for each of the five standard areas (definition/classification, causes/risk factors, clinical features, investigations, management and nursing care) and write 2–3 lines per heading. This reliably scores 7–8 marks even if your knowledge of one area is limited.

8. **End every nursing-care answer with patient education.** At least one or two discharge teaching points show the examiner that you think of the whole patient, not just the hospital stay.

## Probability ratings

- **High** — asked in most years; commit the full answer to memory.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a broader question.
- **Low** — appears rarely; useful for MCQs and to round off a long essay.

These ratings are estimates based on the weight of each topic in the INC syllabus and common patterns in B.Sc Nursing university papers. They are a guide, not a guarantee.

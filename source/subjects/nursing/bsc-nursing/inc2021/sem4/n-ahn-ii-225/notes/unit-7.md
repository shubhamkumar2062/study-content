# Unit 7: Emergency, disaster and critical care nursing

This unit covers INC Unit VII: emergency nursing principles, basic and advanced life support, management of major medical and traumatic emergencies, critical care nursing, and disaster nursing. Speed and correct priorities save lives in emergencies.

---

## 7.1 Emergency nursing principles and triage

**Triage** is the sorting of patients to prioritise treatment when resources are limited.

### Emergency Severity Index (ESI) — in-hospital

| Level | Criteria | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (Resuscitation) | Life-threatening; immediate | Cardiac arrest, major trauma, respiratory failure |
| 2 (Emergent) | High risk, in distress; minutes | Chest pain, stroke, severe asthma |
| 3 (Urgent) | Stable but needs resources; 30–60 min | Fracture, moderate asthma |
| 4 (Less urgent) | Stable, needs one resource | UTI, minor laceration |
| 5 (Non-urgent) | Minor; no resources needed | Minor rash, cold |

### START triage (Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment) — mass casualty / field

Sort in 30 seconds per patient using:
1. Can the patient walk? YES → Green (minor), move aside.
2. If not walking: check respirations — absent after repositioning → Black (deceased).
3. Respirations > 30/min → Red (immediate).
4. Radial pulse absent or capillary refill > 2 s → Red.
5. Cannot follow commands → Red.
6. Otherwise → Yellow (delayed).

```text
   Walk? → Green
   No walk → Breathing?
              No (after airway) → Black (deceased)
              Breathing:
                 Rate > 30 → Red (immediate)
                 Pulse absent/CRT > 2s → Red
                 Cannot obey → Red
                 Otherwise → Yellow (delayed)
```

## 7.2 Basic Cardiac Life Support (BCLS)

BCLS can be performed by any trained person. The earlier CPR is started, the better the outcome (outcome halves for every minute without CPR).

### Adult BCLS sequence

1. **Safety**: ensure the scene is safe.
2. **Check responsiveness**: tap shoulders firmly and call loudly.
3. **Call for help**: shout for help; ask someone to call emergency services and get an AED.
4. **Check breathing**: look for chest rise for no more than 10 seconds. Occasional gasps (agonal breathing) = patient is not breathing normally.
5. **Start chest compressions** (C first — Circulation, then Airway, Breathing):
   - Heel of hand on **lower half of sternum** (not over xiphoid).
   - Arms straight, body weight over hands.
   - **Rate**: 100–120 compressions/minute.
   - **Depth**: 5–6 cm (at least 5 cm, not more than 6 cm).
   - Allow **full chest recoil** between compressions (do not lean).
   - Minimise interruptions (< 10 seconds).
6. **Open airway**: head-tilt chin-lift (if no cervical spine injury); jaw thrust if suspected spine injury.
7. **Rescue breaths**: 30 compressions : 2 breaths. Each breath over 1 second; watch for chest rise. If breaths do not go in, try repositioning before next attempt.
8. **Continue 30:2** until: AED arrives, patient shows obvious signs of life, or you are too exhausted to continue.

### AED (Automated External Defibrillator)

- Switch on; apply pads (right shoulder/left axilla, or one anterior one posterior).
- Follow voice prompts; ensure no one is touching the patient when the AED analyses or shocks.
- Shock → immediately resume CPR for 2 minutes → AED re-analyses.

### Paediatric differences

- Infants (< 1 year): 2-finger chest compressions, depth 4 cm; infant mask; head slightly extended (not hyperextended).
- Children (1–8 years): 1–2 hands depending on size; depth one-third AP diameter; paediatric AED pads if available.
- Ratio: 30:2 for single rescuer; 15:2 for two healthcare providers with a child.

## 7.3 Major medical emergencies

### Anaphylaxis

**Anaphylaxis** is a severe, life-threatening allergic reaction. Mast cells release histamine and other mediators causing vasodilation, bronchospasm and angioedema.

**Features**: urticaria, flushing, angioedema (swelling of face/tongue/throat), stridor, wheeze, dyspnoea, hypotension, tachycardia, nausea, collapse.

**Management** (know the sequence):
1. **Adrenaline (epinephrine) 0.5 mg (0.5 mL of 1:1000) IM into the lateral thigh — FIRST AND MOST IMPORTANT STEP.**
2. Call for emergency help.
3. Position: supine with legs elevated (unless respiratory distress → sitting); if unconscious → recovery position.
4. 100% oxygen via non-rebreather mask.
5. IV access: crystalloid (0.9% NaCl) 500–1000 mL bolus for hypotension.
6. Antihistamine (chlorpheniramine 10 mg slow IV) — for skin symptoms; not lifesaving.
7. Hydrocortisone 200 mg IV — reduces late phase and biphasic reactions; not for acute stabilisation.
8. Nebulised salbutamol for bronchospasm.
9. Repeat adrenaline every 5–15 minutes if no improvement.
10. Admit and observe for at least 6–12 hours (risk of biphasic reaction).

**Nurse's immediate action mnemonic: A–B–C–D (Adrenaline, Breathing/Airway, Circulation/fluids, Drugs secondary)**

### Acute myocardial infarction (MI)

**Features**: central crushing/pressure chest pain radiating to left arm/jaw, diaphoresis, nausea, dyspnoea, pale and clammy.

**Initial management (MONA)**:
- **M** — Morphine 2–5 mg IV for pain (or GTN sublingual if SBP > 90)
- **O** — Oxygen (only if SpO₂ < 94%)
- **N** — Nitrates (GTN sublingual; IV if persistent pain)
- **A** — Aspirin 300 mg chewed (if not already on aspirin and not allergic)

**ECG**: ST elevation in affected leads (STEMI) or ST depression/T-wave changes (NSTEMI).

**Reperfusion**: STEMI → primary PCI (percutaneous coronary intervention, "stenting") within 90 minutes of arrival, or thrombolysis (streptokinase, tenecteplase) within 12 hours if PCI not available. NSTEMI → anticoagulation, antiplatelet therapy, PCI within 72 hours.

**Nursing priorities**: 12-lead ECG within 10 minutes; continuous cardiac monitoring; IV access; blood (troponin, FBC, BMP); pain management; bed rest; NPO (may need PCI); thrombolysis contraindication check (recent surgery/stroke, active bleeding, BP > 180/110); observe for complications (arrhythmias, heart failure, cardiogenic shock).

### Acute stroke — time is brain

Management is covered in Unit 5. Key nursing actions:
- Activate stroke protocol immediately on suspected stroke.
- CT head within 25 minutes of arrival.
- Thrombolysis decision within 60 minutes of arrival if eligible (ischaemic, within 4.5 hours, no contraindications).
- Neurological monitoring every 15 minutes; blood glucose (hypoglycaemia mimics stroke; hyperglycaemia worsens outcome).
- Dysphagia screen before oral intake.

### Status epilepticus

Management sequence is covered in Unit 5. Nursing priorities: protect airway, do not restrain, IV access, glucose check (hypoglycaemia can cause seizures), time the seizure, give benzodiazepine as prescribed, call physician.

### Diabetic emergencies

**Hypoglycaemia** (blood glucose < 3.9 mmol/L or < 70 mg/dL):
- Features: sweating, pallor, tremor, palpitations, anxiety (sympathetic); headache, confusion, agitation, seizure, coma (neuroglycopenic).
- **If conscious**: 15–20 g fast-acting carbohydrate (glucose gel, 150 mL fruit juice, 3–4 glucose tablets); recheck glucose in 15 minutes; repeat if still low.
- **If unconscious/unable to swallow**: IV dextrose 50% (25–50 mL) or IM/SC glucagon 1 mg; recheck; give carbohydrate meal once conscious.
- Find and treat the cause; adjust insulin/drug regimen.

**Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)** — Type 1 diabetes primarily:
- Features: polyuria, polydipsia, vomiting, abdominal pain, Kussmaul breathing (deep rapid), acetone (fruity) breath, dehydration, confusion, coma.
- Diagnosis: blood glucose > 11 mmol/L, pH < 7.3, bicarbonate < 15 mmol/L, ketones in blood and urine.

**Management (DKA)**:

```text
   1. FLUIDS: 0.9% NaCl 1 L in first hour → 1 L in next 2 h → 1 L in next 4 h (adjust to output)
   2. INSULIN: IV insulin infusion (0.1 unit/kg/h) — after first litre of fluid
   3. POTASSIUM: add to fluids once K⁺ < 5.5 mmol/L and urine output established (DKA depletes K⁺)
   4. MONITORING: hourly glucose, 2-hourly electrolytes, urine output, pH
   5. SWITCH: from IV to SC insulin once eating and drinking; glucose < 14 mmol/L → add 10% dextrose (keep glucose > 6 and < 14)
```

**Hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state (HHS)** — Type 2 diabetes; very high glucose (> 30 mmol/L), no significant ketosis, severe dehydration, slow onset; rehydrate slowly (risk of cerebral oedema if too fast), low-dose insulin.

### Poisoning management

**General principles**:
- ABC first: airway (vomiting is common — recovery position), breathing, circulation.
- Call Poisons Information Centre.
- Do not induce vomiting (risk of aspiration, oesophageal injury with corrosives).
- Activated charcoal (1 g/kg) within 1–2 hours if the patient is alert and alert toxin is adsorbed by charcoal (not for corrosives, alcohols, metals, hydrocarbons).
- Specific antidotes:

| Poison | Antidote |
| --- | --- |
| Paracetamol (overdose > 150 mg/kg or staggered high dose) | N-acetylcysteine (Parvolex) IV — most effective within 8 hours |
| Opioids | Naloxone 0.4–2 mg IV (or IM/SC); short duration — may need infusion or repeat |
| Benzodiazepines | Flumazenil 0.2 mg IV (use with caution — can precipitate seizures in dependent patients) |
| Organophosphate | Atropine IV (large doses) + pralidoxime |
| Iron | Desferrioxamine IV |
| Cyanide | Hydroxocobalamin IV |
| Carbon monoxide | 100% oxygen; hyperbaric oxygen if severe |

## 7.4 Critical care nursing

### ICU equipment and environment

| Equipment | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Cardiac monitor | Continuous ECG, heart rate, arrhythmia detection |
| Pulse oximetry (SpO₂) | Continuous oxygen saturation |
| Invasive arterial line (A-line) | Continuous blood pressure, frequent blood gas sampling |
| Central venous catheter (CVC) | CVP monitoring, vasoactive drugs, TPN |
| Pulmonary artery catheter (Swan-Ganz) | Cardiac output, pulmonary artery pressures (selected patients) |
| Mechanical ventilator | Breathing support |
| Urinary catheter | Hourly urine output |
| NG/NJ tube | Enteral nutrition, drug administration |

### Ventilator care bundle (VCB) — prevents VAP (Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia)

The VCB is a set of evidence-based practices consistently applied together:

| Element | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| Head-of-bed elevation | 30–45° (semi-recumbent) unless contraindicated |
| Oral hygiene | Chlorhexidine 0.12% mouth rinse every 6–12 hours; suction secretions from mouth and oropharynx |
| Sedation hold (daily awakening trial) | Assess whether the patient can be lightened or weaned from sedation |
| Spontaneous breathing trial (SBT) | Assess readiness for extubation daily |
| DVT prophylaxis | Compression stockings, low-molecular-weight heparin |
| Stress ulcer prophylaxis | Proton pump inhibitor (unless enterally feeding) |
| Subglottic secretion drainage | Use cuffed ETT with subglottic suction port where available |

### Pain and sedation assessment

- Pain: Behavioural Pain Scale (BPS) or Critical-Care Pain Observation Tool (CPOT) for non-verbal/intubated patients.
- Sedation: Richmond Agitation-Sedation Scale (RASS; target usually −1 to 0 — alert/lightly sedated, not deeply sedated).
- Delirium: Confusion Assessment Method for ICU (CAM-ICU) — screen twice daily.

### Communication with intubated patients

An intubated patient often hears and understands but cannot speak.
- Introduce yourself; explain every procedure before doing it.
- Use yes/no questions; head nods.
- Communication boards (letters, symbols, pictures).
- Writing board or letter card.
- Lip reading — look at the patient when they try to mouth words.
- Never assume the patient cannot hear — speak kindly and professionally always.

## 7.5 Disaster nursing

**Disaster** = an event (natural or man-made) that overwhelms local capacity to respond.

### Hospital incident command system (HICS)

Hospitals activate HICS for mass casualty incidents: a clear chain of command with an Incident Commander, Operations Section (patient care), Logistics (supplies), Finance, and Public Information Officer.

**Nurse's role**:
- Report to designated assembly area; await assignment.
- May be assigned to triage, treatment areas, or family reception.
- Document all interventions with time (legal record, compensation claims).
- Do not leave post without relief — patient safety depends on staffing levels.

### Mass casualty triage in the field

Apply START or SALT triage; tag patients (Red, Yellow, Green, Black).

### Psychological first aid (PFA)

PFA is the initial psychosocial support for disaster survivors:
- Ensure basic safety and meet immediate needs (water, shelter, medical care).
- **Look, listen, link** — approach calmly, listen without forcing the person to talk, link with practical help and social support.
- Do not force people to discuss their experience.
- Provide information about available services and what to expect.
- Respect cultural practices.
- Self-care for responders (signs of secondary trauma, stress debriefing).

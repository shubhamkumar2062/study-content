# Unit 8: Geriatric nursing and occupational health disorders

This unit covers INC Unit VIII: ageing, geriatric assessment, common geriatric conditions and their nursing management, and an introduction to occupational health nursing. India's population is ageing rapidly — knowledge of geriatric care is essential for all nurses.

---

## 8.1 The ageing process

**Ageing** is a universal, progressive, irreversible biological process. The number of Indians aged over 60 is expected to reach 340 million by 2050.

### Physiological changes of normal ageing

| System | Age-related changes | Clinical implications |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cardiovascular | Reduced cardiac output, arterial stiffening (systolic BP rises), decreased heart rate response | Lower exercise tolerance; orthostatic hypotension; atypical MI presentation |
| Respiratory | Reduced vital capacity, weaker cough, decreased mucociliary clearance | Increased risk of chest infections; reduced response to hypoxia |
| Renal | GFR falls ~1 mL/min/year after 40; tubular function declines | Drug accumulation; dehydration risk; reduced ability to conserve sodium |
| Musculoskeletal | Sarcopenia (muscle loss), decreased bone density, joint cartilage loss | Falls, fractures, weakness, reduced mobility |
| Nervous | Slower conduction velocity, reduced neuroplasticity, mild cognitive slowing | Slower reactions; risk of delirium with illness; may need more time for tasks |
| Sensory | Presbyopia, presbycusis (hearing loss), reduced proprioception | Communication difficulties; falls; isolation |
| Skin | Thinner epidermis, reduced subcutaneous fat, less collagen | Pressure sores, thermoregulation problems, delayed healing |
| Immune | Immunosenescence (weakened B and T cell responses) | Atypical infection presentations (no fever); reduced vaccine responses |
| GI | Slower GI motility, reduced saliva and gastric acid | Constipation; nutritional deficiencies (B12, iron); drug interactions |
| Endocrine | Reduced insulin sensitivity, lower T3/T4 | Type 2 diabetes risk; hypothyroidism more common |

**Atypical presentations in older patients**: infections may present as falls, confusion or reduced appetite rather than fever; MI may present as fatigue or dyspnoea without chest pain; hypothyroidism may mimic dementia. The nurse must have a high index of suspicion.

## 8.2 Geriatric assessment

**Comprehensive Geriatric Assessment (CGA)** is a multidimensional assessment by a multidisciplinary team, addressing medical, functional, cognitive, social and psychological domains.

### Functional assessment

**Katz Activities of Daily Living (ADL) Scale** — basic self-care:
Bathing, dressing, toileting, transferring (getting in/out of bed/chair), continence, feeding. Score 1 for independent, 0 for dependent. Total 6 = fully independent, 0 = fully dependent.

**Lawton Instrumental ADLs (IADL)** — higher-level function:
Telephone use, shopping, cooking, housekeeping, laundry, transport, medication management, finances. More sensitive to early functional decline.

### Cognitive assessment

**Mini Mental State Examination (MMSE)**: 30 questions testing orientation, registration, recall, attention, language and copying. Score 24–30 = normal; 18–23 = mild impairment; 10–17 = moderate; < 10 = severe.

**Mini-Cog** (bedside screening): 3-word recall + clock drawing; quick and reliable for dementia screening.

### Depression screening

**Geriatric Depression Scale (GDS)**: 15-item (short form) or 30-item questionnaire. Score ≥ 5 on the 15-item version = depression likely.

### Nutritional assessment

**Mini Nutritional Assessment (MNA)**: screens for malnutrition; BMI, weight loss, mobility, psychological stress, neuropsychological problems, dietary intake.

### Fall risk

**Morse Fall Scale**: six items — history of falls, secondary diagnosis, ambulatory aid, IV/heparin lock, gait, mental status. Score ≥ 45 = high risk.

**Timed Up and Go (TUG) test**: patient rises from a chair, walks 3 metres, turns, walks back and sits; > 12 seconds = significant fall risk.

### Pressure sore risk

**Braden Scale**: six subscales — sensory perception, moisture, activity, mobility, nutrition, friction/shear. Total ≤ 18 = risk.

## 8.3 Dementia

**Dementia** is a syndrome of progressive cognitive decline severe enough to impair social and occupational functioning. It is not a normal part of ageing.

### Alzheimer's disease (AD)

The most common dementia (~60–70%); amyloid plaques and neurofibrillary tau tangles in the brain.

**Stages**:
- **Early**: forgetfulness (especially recent events), word-finding difficulty, mild personality change; ADLs mostly preserved.
- **Moderate**: significant memory loss, disorientation (time, place), need help with ADLs, may become lost, behavioural symptoms (agitation, wandering, sleep disturbance, depression).
- **Late**: loss of speech, incontinence, bed-bound, cannot recognise family, total dependence, risk of aspiration pneumonia, pressure sores.

**Drugs** (modest symptomatic benefit, do not alter disease course):
- Cholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine) — mild to moderate AD.
- Memantine (NMDA receptor antagonist) — moderate to severe AD.

**Non-pharmacological care** (more important):
- Reality orientation: use patient's name, say date/time/place regularly; large-print calendars, clocks.
- Validation therapy: acknowledge the patient's feelings rather than correcting their confusion.
- Routine and consistency: familiar environment, same care-givers, predictable daily schedule.
- Safe environment: remove hazards, door alarms (wandering), stair gates, supervised outdoor activity.
- Activity: music (preserved even in late dementia — music of the patient's era), gentle exercise, familiar tasks.
- Communication: short simple sentences, one question at a time, non-verbal cues, eye contact.

**Wandering**: do not lock the person in; use tracking devices; ensure the person wears an ID bracelet; modify the environment (disguise exits with curtains); address the unmet need (pain, boredom, full bladder).

**Family support**: the burden on caregivers is enormous. Provide education about the disease course, respite services, caregiver support groups, advice on legal/financial planning (power of attorney while patient still has capacity).

### Vascular dementia

Second most common. Caused by cerebrovascular disease (multiple strokes or small vessel disease). Course is stepwise (sudden worsening after each stroke event). Control risk factors (blood pressure, diabetes, atrial fibrillation, smoking, statins).

### Delirium vs Dementia

| Feature | Delirium | Dementia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Onset | Acute (hours to days) | Gradual (months to years) |
| Course | Fluctuating (better and worse during the day) | Progressive, stable day to day |
| Consciousness | Impaired (clouded, confused) | Clear (until late stages) |
| Attention | Severely impaired | Less affected early |
| Reversibility | Often reversible if cause treated | Irreversible (most types) |
| Main causes | Infection, drugs, constipation, urinary retention, pain, metabolic | Alzheimer's, vascular, Lewy body, frontotemporal |
| Hallucinations | Common (especially visual) | Less common (except Lewy body dementia) |

**Delirium management**: find and treat the cause (treat infection, stop culprit drugs, relieve constipation/urinary retention, correct metabolic disturbance); re-orient; ensure hearing aids and glasses are worn; familiar face/voice; mobilise early; natural light during day; sleep hygiene at night; haloperidol (low dose) for severe distressing symptoms only.

## 8.4 Falls

**Falls** are the leading cause of injury in older adults; hip fracture after a fall carries 20–30% one-year mortality.

**Multifactorial causes**:

| Intrinsic | Extrinsic |
| --- | --- |
| Muscle weakness, reduced balance and proprioception | Wet floors, loose rugs, poor lighting, low furniture |
| Visual impairment, vestibular problems | Incorrect walking aids; inappropriate footwear |
| Cognitive impairment, delirium | Unfamiliar environment (hospital) |
| Polypharmacy (sedatives, antihypertensives, diuretics, antidepressants) | Lack of grab rails in toilet/bathroom |
| Postural hypotension | High bed height |
| Previous falls | Inadequate call system |

**Prevention**:
- Morse Fall Scale on admission and when condition changes.
- Fall prevention care plan: bed low with brakes locked; call bell within reach; non-slip footwear; walking aid within reach; assistance with mobility as needed; bed/chair alarm for confused patients; hourly rounding.
- Review medications: reduce or stop sedatives, antihypertensives (especially in those with orthostatic hypotension).
- **Post-fall assessment**: examine for injury (hip fracture — shortened, externally rotated leg), neurological check, blood pressure, glucose.

**Hip fracture**: urgent surgery (within 36 hours for most patients if medically stable) — hemiarthroplasty or dynamic hip screw; early mobilisation (day 1 post-op with physiotherapy); venous thromboembolism prophylaxis; pain management; cognitive status monitoring (delirium risk post-op).

## 8.5 Polypharmacy and medication safety in older adults

**Polypharmacy** (commonly defined as taking ≥5 medications) is common in older adults with multiple comorbidities. It increases the risk of adverse drug reactions, drug interactions, falls, and poor adherence.

**Beers Criteria**: a list of potentially inappropriate medications for older adults (e.g. benzodiazepines → falls/cognitive impairment; tricyclic antidepressants → anticholinergic effects; first-generation antihistamines → sedation/confusion; NSAIDs → GI bleeding, renal failure, fluid retention; dipyridamole → orthostatic hypotension).

**Medication reconciliation**: at admission and discharge, compare all medications being taken at home with the prescribed list; resolve discrepancies; simplify regimens where possible; consider deprescribing (safely stopping medications that are no longer beneficial or are causing harm).

## 8.6 Elder abuse

**Elder abuse** is any act or failure to act that causes harm or distress to an older person. Types:
- Physical abuse: hitting, restraining, rough handling.
- Psychological/emotional abuse: threats, humiliation, isolation.
- Financial abuse: misuse of money or property.
- Sexual abuse.
- Neglect: failure to provide food, medication, hygiene, medical care.

**Signs**: unexplained injuries, fear or anxiety in the presence of the carer, poor hygiene and nutrition, unexplained financial transactions, the older person not being allowed to speak alone.

**Nurse's role**: maintain a high index of suspicion; create a safe private opportunity for the older person to speak; document all findings carefully; report to the hospital social worker or safeguarding team per institutional policy; in India, report to the police if criminal abuse is suspected.

## 8.7 End-of-life care in older patients

Older patients with advanced dementia, cancer, or multi-organ failure need compassionate end-of-life care:
- Review goals of care (is active treatment still appropriate or is comfort the priority?).
- Communicate sensitively with family (and with the patient if they retain capacity).
- Manage symptoms: pain, dyspnoea, mouth care, skin care.
- Anticipatory prescribing: prescribe PRN analgesia and anxiolytics in advance so there is no delay if the patient deteriorates.
- Support the family before and after death; acknowledge their grief.

## 8.8 Occupational health nursing

**Occupational health nursing** focuses on the health, safety and wellbeing of workers.

### Scope

- Pre-employment health screening (fitness for work, baseline investigations).
- Routine health surveillance (workers exposed to known hazards).
- Health promotion and education at the workplace.
- Management and referral of work-related illness and injury.
- Return-to-work assessment after illness or injury.
- Maintenance of occupational health records.
- Collaboration with occupational physicians, safety officers, and management.
- Advice on workplace ergonomics, hazard reduction, and safe systems of work.

### Occupational diseases

| Disease | Causative agent / occupation | Pathology | Key nursing points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Silicosis | Crystalline silica (mining, quarrying, sandblasting, construction) | Silicotic nodules in upper lung lobes; fibrosis; increased TB risk | Dusty job surveillance, respiratory symptoms; check for TB; no cure; prevent with engineering controls and masks |
| Coal workers' pneumoconiosis (CWP) | Coal dust | Simple (coal macules) → progressive massive fibrosis | Same; spirometry surveillance; smoking worsens |
| Asbestosis | Asbestos fibres (shipbuilding, insulation, demolition) | Diffuse interstitial fibrosis; pleural plaques; risk of mesothelioma and lung cancer | Long latency (20–40 years); X-ray and CT; legal compensation; smoking cessation reduces lung cancer risk |
| Occupational asthma | Isocyanates, latex, flour, animal allergens, chemicals | Reversible airflow obstruction triggered at work | Peak flow diary (at work vs away); remove from exposure; spirometry |
| Noise-induced hearing loss (NIHL) | Prolonged exposure to > 85 dB (industry, construction) | Permanent sensorineural hearing loss, starting at 4 kHz | Audiometry surveillance; hearing protection (ear muffs/plugs); cannot recover |
| Vibration white finger (hand-arm vibration syndrome) | Vibrating tools (drills, grinders) | Vasospasm and nerve damage in fingers; Raynaud-like blanching | Use anti-vibration tools; limit exposure time; warm gloves |
| Occupational skin disease | Chemicals, rubber, wet work | Contact dermatitis | Barrier creams, gloves, patch testing for allergen identification |
| Occupational cancer | Benzene (leukaemia), asbestos (mesothelioma), vinyl chloride (angiosarcoma of liver), radiation (many types), UV (melanoma) | Varies | Exposure records; surveillance; early detection |

### Ergonomics and injury prevention

- **Manual handling**: train all staff in safe lifting (bend knees, straight back, hold load close, turn feet not waist); use hoists and slide sheets for patients; musculoskeletal injury is the commonest occupational injury in nurses.
- **Display screen equipment (DSE) / computer work**: ergonomic chair and desk setup, frequent breaks, adjustable monitor position to prevent repetitive strain injury (RSI) and neck/back pain.
- **Needle stick and sharps safety**: covered in HIV section; safety-engineered devices reduce risk.
- **Workplace violence**: de-escalation training; panic alarms; zero-tolerance policy; post-incident support.

### Industrial accidents

Responsibilities after a workplace accident:
1. First aid and secure the scene.
2. Report to the occupational health department and supervisor.
3. Record in the accident book.
4. Investigate root cause (not to blame, but to prevent recurrence).
5. Notify the labour inspectorate if serious (as required by the Factories Act 1948 in India).
6. Compensation claim processing under Employee's State Insurance (ESI) or Workmen's Compensation Act.

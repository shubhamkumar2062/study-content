# Unit 6: Immunological (HIV/AIDS) and oncological conditions

This unit covers INC Unit VI: the immune system, HIV/AIDS nursing care, and principles of cancer nursing including chemotherapy, radiotherapy and palliative care. HIV/AIDS and cancer are priority health problems in India and globally.

---

## 6.1 Brief review of the immune system

The immune system defends the body against pathogens, cancer cells and foreign substances.

| Component | Type | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Skin, mucosa, stomach acid | Innate (physical/chemical barriers) | First line; non-specific |
| Neutrophils, macrophages, NK cells | Innate (cellular) | Phagocytosis, killing; non-specific, rapid |
| Complement system | Innate (humoral) | Opsonisation, lysis |
| B lymphocytes → plasma cells | Adaptive (humoral) | Antibody (immunoglobulin) production; antigen-specific |
| T helper (CD4+) cells | Adaptive (cellular) | Coordinate immune response; activate B cells and cytotoxic T cells |
| T cytotoxic (CD8+) cells | Adaptive (cellular) | Kill virus-infected and cancer cells |
| Memory cells (B and T) | Adaptive | Long-term immunity after infection or vaccination |

**CD4+ T-lymphocytes** are the central coordinators of adaptive immunity — the specific cells destroyed by HIV.

## 6.2 HIV/AIDS

### Pathophysiology

**Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV)** is a retrovirus that infects and destroys **CD4+ T-lymphocytes** (T-helper cells). As CD4 count falls, the ability to fight infections and cancers fails. Without treatment, HIV infection progresses to **Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome (AIDS)** over 8–10 years on average.

**HIV life cycle**: HIV binds to CD4 receptor and CCR5/CXCR4 co-receptor → fuses with cell membrane → reverse transcriptase converts viral RNA to DNA → integrase inserts viral DNA into host chromosome → host cell makes new virus → protease cleaves viral proteins → new virus particles bud off → cell is destroyed.

**Transmission**: only via specific body fluids:
- Unprotected sexual intercourse (commonest worldwide).
- Contaminated blood (transfusion, sharing needles/syringes).
- Mother to child (during pregnancy, delivery, breastfeeding) — called PMTCT or MTCT route.
- Needlestick or sharps injury (healthcare workers — risk ~0.3%).

**Not transmitted** by casual contact, shaking hands, sharing food/utensils, coughing, sneezing, mosquitoes.

### WHO clinical staging (based on CD4 count and clinical features)

| Stage | CD4 count (cells/µL) | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (Asymptomatic) | Usually ≥ 500 | Asymptomatic or persistent generalised lymphadenopathy |
| 2 (Mild) | 350–499 | Unexplained weight loss < 10%, minor mucocutaneous infections (recurrent oral ulcers, seborrhoeic dermatitis, herpes zoster one episode, recurrent RTI) |
| 3 (Advanced) | 200–349 | Unexplained weight loss > 10%, chronic diarrhoea, oral candidiasis, pulmonary TB, unexplained anaemia, severe bacterial infections |
| 4 (Severe/AIDS) | < 200 | AIDS-defining illnesses: PCP, CMV retinitis, cryptococcal meningitis, toxoplasma encephalitis, Kaposi's sarcoma, disseminated MAC, oesophageal candidiasis, HIV wasting syndrome |

### Opportunistic infections (OIs)

| OI | Organism | Features | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pneumocystis pneumonia (PCP) | *Pneumocystis jirovecii* | Dyspnoea, dry cough, fever, low SpO₂; CD4 < 200 | Cotrimoxazole; prophylaxis when CD4 < 200 |
| Cryptococcal meningitis | *Cryptococcus neoformans* | Headache, meningism, raised ICP; CSF India ink: encapsulated yeast | Amphotericin B + flucytosine → fluconazole maintenance |
| CMV retinitis | Cytomegalovirus | Vision loss, floaters; CD4 < 50 | IV ganciclovir or valganciclovir |
| Toxoplasma encephalitis | *Toxoplasma gondii* | Focal neurological signs, ring-enhancing lesions on CT/MRI | Pyrimethamine + sulfadiazine + folinic acid |
| Oesophageal candidiasis | *Candida* | Dysphagia, odynophagia (painful swallowing) | Fluconazole |
| Kaposi's sarcoma | HHV-8 (human herpesvirus 8) | Violaceous skin/mucosal lesions | ART; chemotherapy if extensive |
| Disseminated MAC | *Mycobacterium avium complex* | Fever, night sweats, weight loss, anaemia; CD4 < 50 | Azithromycin + ethambutol; prophylaxis with azithromycin |

### Antiretroviral therapy (ART)

ART suppresses viral replication, allows CD4 count recovery, prevents OIs and prevents onward transmission (Undetectable = Untransmittable, U=U).

**ART classes and mechanism**:

| Class | Examples | Mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NRTIs (Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors) | Tenofovir (TDF), Lamivudine (3TC), Zidovudine (AZT), Emtricitabine (FTC), Abacavir (ABC) | Terminate the growing DNA chain (chain terminators); inhibit reverse transcriptase |
| NNRTIs (Non-Nucleoside RTIs) | Efavirenz, Nevirapine, Rilpivirine | Bind and inactivate reverse transcriptase at a different site |
| PIs (Protease Inhibitors) | Lopinavir/ritonavir, Atazanavir, Darunavir | Block protease enzyme → immature, non-infectious viral particles |
| INSTIs (Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitors) | Dolutegravir, Raltegravir, Bictegravir | Block integrase → viral DNA cannot insert into host chromosome |
| Entry/Fusion inhibitors | Enfuvirtide (fusion), Maraviroc (CCR5 antagonist) | Prevent HIV from entering or fusing with the CD4 cell |

**Standard first-line regimen in India (National AIDS Control Organisation)**: TDF + 3TC + DTG (tenofovir + lamivudine + dolutegravir) — a fixed-dose combination once daily.

**Monitoring**: viral load (goal: undetectable, < 50 copies/mL at 6 months) and CD4 count (expect rise of ~100 cells/µL per year on effective ART).

**Adherence**: ART must be taken every day at the same time — even a few missed doses → viral rebound → resistance → treatment failure. Counselling and support are as important as the medicine.

**Drug interactions**: Efavirenz is an inducer of CYP450 enzymes — interacts with rifampicin (TB treatment), oral contraceptives, warfarin. Dolutegravir is preferred and has fewer interactions.

### PMTCT (Prevention of Mother-to-Child Transmission)

- All pregnant women should be tested for HIV at first antenatal visit.
- HIV-positive pregnant women should start ART immediately (and continue lifelong).
- Baby gets single-dose nevirapine at birth plus a 6-week course (or longer if breastfeeding).
- Breastfeeding is recommended if the mother is on ART and virally suppressed (formula is not safe if clean water access is poor); use exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months.

### Nursing care for HIV/AIDS patients

**Infection control**: standard precautions for all patients; HIV is not transmitted by casual contact; gloves for blood/body fluids; safe disposal of sharps; PEP (post-exposure prophylaxis) with ART within 72 hours for needlestick.

**Psychosocial care**: address stigma and discrimination (by staff and others); maintain confidentiality; support disclosure to partners; psychological support for depression and anxiety; connect with peer support groups.

**Nutrition**: maintain adequate intake; manage OIs (treat thrush, diarrhoea); refer to dietitian if wasting.

**Teaching**: ART adherence (never miss a dose), recognise OI symptoms (fever, cough, vision changes, headache → seek care), safer sex (condoms), not sharing needles, PMTCT if pregnant, HIV status disclosure to sexual partners.

**Occupational exposure (needlestick)**:
1. Squeeze wound to encourage bleeding; wash with soap and water (do not suck).
2. Report to occupational health or emergency department immediately.
3. PEP (TDF + 3TC + DTG) must be started within 72 hours (ideally within 1 hour); course is 28 days.
4. Baseline HIV, hepatitis B and C test; follow-up at 6 weeks, 3 months.

## 6.3 Cancer nursing

### Cancer epidemiology in India

The leading cancers in India by incidence are: breast, cervical, oral/lip, lung, colorectal, stomach, leukaemia, oesophageal, ovarian and thyroid cancers. Tobacco use (smoking, smokeless tobacco) is the leading preventable cause.

### Nursing assessment of the cancer patient

A comprehensive assessment includes:
- **Symptoms**: pain (site, character, severity — 0–10 NRS), fatigue (impact on ADLs), dyspnoea, nausea, weight loss, appetite.
- **Psychological state**: anxiety, depression, fear of death, hope, coping strategies.
- **Functional status**: ECOG/WHO performance status (0 = fully active; 4 = completely disabled).
- **Nutritional status**: weight, BMI, prealbumin, intake history.
- **Social support**: family, finances, transport to hospital, employment impact.
- **Existing disease and medications** (many cancer drugs interact with common drugs).

### Chemotherapy administration

**Safety before every administration (5 rights for cytotoxics)**:
1. Right drug (check against prescription protocol).
2. Right dose (check calculation, body surface area).
3. Right route (most are IV; some are oral, intrathecal — route errors can be fatal).
4. Right patient (two identifiers — name + DOB; check consent).
5. Right time (some drugs must be given in sequence; pre-hydration may be required — e.g. with cisplatin).

**Vesicant vs non-vesicant drugs**:
- **Vesicants** (e.g. doxorubicin, vincristine) cause severe tissue necrosis if they extravasate outside the vein.
- **Non-vesicants** (e.g. cyclophosphamide) are irritants but cause less severe local injury.

**Extravasation protocol** (if suspected — pain, swelling, no blood return, change in flow rate):
1. Stop infusion immediately; do not remove the cannula.
2. Aspirate any residual drug through the cannula.
3. Notify the doctor; document site, time, estimated volume.
4. Apply cold pack for most vesicants (heat for vincristine/vinca alkaloids).
5. Dexrazoxane antidote (for anthracyclines — doxorubicin); hyaluronidase (for vinca alkaloids).
6. Photograph and monitor for tissue breakdown.

**Safe handling of cytotoxic drugs** (nurses preparing or administering):
- Prepare in a **biological safety cabinet (class II)**.
- Wear double gloves, chemotherapy gown, goggles, and mask.
- Use Luer-lock connections and closed-system drug transfer devices.
- Do not eat, drink or apply cosmetics in the preparation area.
- Dispose of all waste in clearly labelled cytotoxic waste bags/sharps containers.
- **If skin exposure**: wash immediately with soap and water for 15 minutes.
- **If eye exposure**: flush with water for 15 minutes; seek medical review.

**Common side effects and nursing management**:

| Side effect | Cause | Nursing management |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nausea and vomiting | Direct effect on CTZ; gastric irritation | Pre-medicate with 5-HT3 antagonists (ondansetron) ± dexamethasone; antiemetics after; small frequent meals; avoid strong odours |
| Alopecia | Hair follicle cells rapidly dividing | Counsel before treatment; cold cap (scalp cooling) may reduce hair loss; hair grows back 3–6 months after treatment ends |
| Mucositis | Damage to GI mucosa | Oral hygiene 4-hourly (soft brush, saline/sodium bicarbonate rinses); cold food soothes; topical lignocaine for pain; ensure nutrition |
| Neutropenia (low WBC) | Bone marrow suppression | Full blood count before each cycle; neutrophil count < 1.0 × 10⁹/L → neutropenic precautions; count < 0.5 → G-CSF (granulocyte colony-stimulating factor); fever in neutropenia is an emergency (neutropenic sepsis) → IV antibiotics within 1 hour |
| Anaemia | Bone marrow suppression | Monitor Hb; transfuse if Hb < 8 g/dL and symptomatic; erythropoietin-stimulating agents |
| Thrombocytopenia (low platelets) | Bone marrow suppression | Platelet count < 50 → bleeding risk; avoid IM injections, invasive procedures; soft toothbrush; platelet transfusion if < 10 × 10⁹/L or bleeding |

### Radiotherapy nursing

Radiotherapy uses ionising radiation to destroy cancer cells by damaging DNA. It may be curative (early-stage head/neck, cervix, prostate), adjuvant (after surgery for breast), or palliative (for pain from bone metastases, brain metastases).

**General nursing care for a patient receiving radiotherapy**:
- **Skin care**: use mild, non-perfumed soap; avoid shaving the treatment field; apply prescribed moisturiser (aqueous cream); protect from sun; do not use metal-based products (zinc oxide, talc) in the field (reflects radiation).
- **Fatigue**: the most universal side effect; plan rest periods; moderate activity as tolerated.
- **Specific field effects**: head/neck → mucositis, xerostomia (dry mouth), dental care before RT; pelvic → diarrhoea (low-residue diet), urinary frequency; lung → oesophagitis, pneumonitis.
- **Safety during treatment**: radiotherapy staff and equipment are designed so the patient is not radioactive after treatment; brachytherapy (radioactive implants/seeds) is different — isolation precautions and limited visitor time are required while the source is in place.

### Cancer pain management

The **WHO analgesic ladder** guides cancer pain management:

```text
   Step 1 (mild pain):    Non-opioid — paracetamol ± NSAID ± adjuvant
   Step 2 (moderate):     Weak opioid (codeine, tramadol) ± non-opioid ± adjuvant
   Step 3 (severe):       Strong opioid (morphine, oxycodone, fentanyl) ± non-opioid ± adjuvant
```

**Principles**: by mouth (oral preferred), by the clock (regular dosing, not PRN only), by the ladder, for the individual (titrate to the patient's pain — there is no maximum dose of morphine if tolerated), with attention to detail (anticipate side effects).

**Adjuvant analgesics**: steroids (for nerve compression, raised ICP), gabapentin/pregabalin (neuropathic pain), amitriptyline (neuropathic), bisphosphonates (bone pain from metastases), ketamine (refractory neuropathic pain).

**Common opioid side effects and management**: nausea (usually self-limiting; anti-emetics for first 1–2 weeks), constipation (laxatives must always be co-prescribed with opioids — do not wait for the patient to become constipated), sedation (usually resolves), respiratory depression (rare at correct doses; treat with naloxone if breathing rate < 8/min and patient difficult to rouse).

### Palliative care

**Palliative care** is specialised care for patients with life-threatening illness. Its aim is to **prevent and relieve suffering** through early identification, assessment and treatment of pain and other problems — physical, psychological, social and spiritual.

**Key principles**:
- **Affirms life** and regards dying as a normal process — neither hastens nor postpones death.
- **Integrates the psychological and spiritual** aspects of patient care.
- Offers a **support system** for family during illness and in bereavement.
- Uses a **team approach**: doctor, nurse, social worker, chaplain, physiotherapist, pharmacist.

**Common symptoms in palliative care and nursing management**:

| Symptom | Nursing management |
| --- | --- |
| Pain | WHO ladder; regular review of pain control; non-pharmacological (position, heat, massage, distraction) |
| Dyspnoea | Fan to face, sitting upright, low-dose opioids reduce breathlessness sensation, anxiolytics (if anxiety-driven), oxygen only if hypoxic |
| Nausea | Metoclopramide, haloperidol, cyclizine; small meals; treat constipation and hypercalcaemia |
| Constipation | Laxatives (lactulose + senna); manual evacuation if impacted; adequate fluids |
| Delirium | Identify and treat reversible causes (infection, drugs, constipation, urinary retention); haloperidol for distressing symptoms; calm environment; family presence |
| Anorexia | Encourage small amounts of preferred foods; acknowledge that poor appetite is part of the illness — do not force |
| Communication | Honest, compassionate communication; listen actively; use preferred name; allow silence; involve family with patient consent |
| Spiritual needs | Respect beliefs; facilitate religious practices; chaplaincy referral; life review and legacy work |

**End-of-life care** (last days): discontinue non-essential medications and monitoring; focus on comfort; anticipatory prescribing of subcutaneous morphine, midazolam and hyoscine; involve family; allow time with the patient; support after death (breaking bad news, bereavement resources, body care with dignity and according to cultural/religious practices).

# Unit 1 — Nursing management of disorders of the ear, nose and throat

This unit covers INC Unit I. It deals with the common medical and surgical problems of the ear, nose and throat (ENT), their causes, how they show, how they are diagnosed, and how the nurse cares for the patient. For each disorder, learn the pattern: cause (etiology) → what goes wrong (pathophysiology) → signs and symptoms → tests → medical/surgical treatment → nursing care and health education.

## 1.1 Review of anatomy and physiology

**Ear.** Three parts. The **external ear** (pinna and the external auditory canal up to the eardrum) collects and guides sound; the canal makes protective wax (cerumen). The **middle ear** is an air-filled cavity holding three tiny bones (malleus, incus, stapes) that carry vibration from the eardrum to the inner ear; the **Eustachian (auditory) tube** links it to the throat and equalises pressure. The **inner ear** has the **cochlea** (hearing) and the **vestibular system** (semicircular canals and vestibule, for balance). The eighth cranial nerve carries hearing and balance signals to the brain.

**Nose.** Warms, moistens and filters air, and holds the sense of smell. It has a rich blood supply; the front part of the septum (Little's area / Kiesselbach's plexus) bleeds easily. The paranasal sinuses (maxillary, frontal, ethmoid, sphenoid) are air spaces that drain into the nose.

**Throat.** The pharynx is the shared passage for air and food; the larynx (voice box) holds the vocal cords and protects the airway during swallowing; the tonsils are lymphoid tissue guarding the throat.

## 1.2 Health history, physical assessment and diagnostic tests

Ask about pain (otalgia), discharge, hearing loss, ringing (tinnitus), giddiness (vertigo), nasal block, discharge, bleeding, loss of smell, sore throat, hoarseness and difficulty swallowing. Note ear or chemical exposure, loud-noise work, and medicines that can harm the ear.

Physical examination uses **otoscopy** (looking at the canal and eardrum), the **tuning-fork tests** (Rinne and Weber, to tell conductive from sensorineural loss), **pure-tone audiometry** (a graph of the softest sounds heard at each pitch), **tympanometry** (middle-ear pressure and eardrum movement), **anterior and posterior rhinoscopy**, **nasal endoscopy**, X-ray/CT of the sinuses, indirect/direct **laryngoscopy**, and throat swab for culture.

## 1.3 Disorders of the ear

**External ear.** *Deformities* may be present from birth (microtia). *Otalgia* (earache) may come from the ear itself or be referred from teeth, throat or jaw joint. *Foreign bodies* (insects, beads) are removed by syringing or forceps by trained staff; never push an object deeper. *Impacted wax* blocks the canal and dulls hearing; it is softened with drops and then syringed out with warm water. *Otitis externa* is infection of the canal (swimmer's ear) causing pain, itching and discharge, treated with cleaning and medicated drops.

**Middle ear.** *Tympanic membrane perforation* follows infection or injury and causes hearing loss and discharge; small holes often heal on their own, large ones may need surgical repair (myringoplasty/tympanoplasty). *Otitis media* is middle-ear infection:
- *Acute otitis media* — sudden pain, fever, reduced hearing, a bulging red eardrum; common in children because their Eustachian tube is short and flat. If pus builds up the drum may rupture and discharge.
- *Serous (secretous) otitis media* — fluid without acute infection, giving a blocked, muffled feeling.
- *Chronic suppurative otitis media* — long-standing discharge and perforation; may damage the ossicles and needs ear care, drops and sometimes surgery.
A **myringotomy** (a small cut in the eardrum, sometimes with a grommet tube) drains fluid.

**Inner ear.** *Ménière's disease* is excess fluid (endolymph) in the inner ear causing attacks of spinning vertigo, fluctuating hearing loss, tinnitus and a feeling of fullness; a low-salt diet and medicines reduce attacks. *Labyrinthitis* is inflammation of the balance organ causing severe vertigo, nausea and unsteadiness. *Ototoxicity* is damage to the inner ear or its nerve by certain drugs (some antibiotics, diuretics, anti-cancer and anti-malarial drugs), shown by new hearing loss, tinnitus or imbalance; the nurse reports these early so the drug can be reviewed.

## 1.4 Disorders of the nose and throat

**Upper respiratory airway infections.** *Rhinitis* (runny, blocked, sneezing nose) may be infective or allergic. *Sinusitis* is infection of the sinuses with facial pain/pressure, blocked nose, thick discharge and fever; steam inhalation, decongestants and antibiotics help, and chronic cases may need endoscopic sinus surgery. *Tonsillitis* is sore red swollen tonsils with pain on swallowing and fever; repeated attacks may lead to **tonsillectomy**. *Laryngitis* is inflammation of the larynx causing hoarse voice and cough, helped by voice rest, fluids and steam.

**Epistaxis (nosebleed).** Caused by nose-picking, dry air, trauma, high blood pressure, infection or bleeding disorders. First aid: sit the patient up and lean forward (so blood does not run back and is not swallowed), pinch the soft part of the nose firmly for about 10 minutes, breathe through the mouth, and apply a cold pack to the bridge. If bleeding continues, the doctor may cauterise the vessel or insert an **anterior or posterior nasal pack**.

**Nasal obstruction** may come from a deviated septum, polyps, enlarged turbinates or growths; it is treated according to the cause, sometimes surgically.

**Laryngeal obstruction** is an emergency: swelling, a foreign body or a growth blocks the airway, giving noisy breathing (stridor), struggling to breathe and bluish colour (cyanosis). Clear the airway, give oxygen, and prepare for intubation or an emergency **tracheostomy**.

## 1.5 Deafness and its management

**Conductive deafness** is a block in the outer or middle ear (wax, fluid, perforation, ossicle problem); much of it can be corrected. **Sensorineural deafness** is damage to the cochlea or nerve (ageing, noise, drugs, infection); it is usually permanent and managed with hearing aids. **Mixed deafness** has both. Management includes removing the cause, hearing aids (devices that amplify sound), cochlear implants for severe nerve deafness, lip-reading and sign language, and prevention — ear protection against loud noise, careful use of ototoxic drugs, and early treatment of ear infections.

## 1.6 Nursing procedures and care (with rationale)

**Instilling ear drops.**
1. Check the order, warm the drops to body temperature in the hand — *cold drops cause giddiness.*
2. Position the patient lying with the affected ear up.
3. In an adult, pull the pinna up and back to straighten the canal; in a small child pull it down and back.
4. Drop the medicine along the side of the canal, not straight onto the drum.
5. Keep the position for a few minutes and gently press the tragus so the drops spread.

**Instilling nasal drops.** Position with the head tilted back (or lying with the head over the edge of the bed) so the medicine reaches the right area; ask the patient to breathe through the mouth and stay still for a few minutes.

**Nasal packing care.** Keep the patient sitting up, watch for bleeding at the back of the throat (swallowed blood), provide mouth care because the patient breathes through the mouth, and report headache or fresh bleeding.

**Tracheostomy care.** A tracheostomy is an opening in the windpipe with a tube.
1. Explain the procedure and reassure the patient, who cannot speak with the tube.
2. Keep a spare tube, obturator and dilator at the bedside — *so the airway can be re-opened if the tube comes out.*
3. Suction secretions using sterile technique, for no more than about ten seconds at a time, with oxygen before and after — *suction removes air as well as secretions.*
4. Clean the inner tube and the skin (stoma) around it, and change the dressing and tapes to prevent infection and skin damage.
5. Humidify the inspired air — *the nose that normally warms and moistens air is bypassed.*
6. Keep the patient in a semi-upright position, provide a call bell and a writing pad for communication.

**Hearing aid care.** Teach the patient to switch off and remove the aid at night, keep it dry, change or charge the battery, and clean the ear mould gently.

## Remember

- Ear = external (collects sound), middle (conducts sound, holds the three ossicles) and inner (cochlea for hearing, vestibular system for balance).
- Rinne and Weber tuning-fork tests separate conductive from sensorineural deafness; audiometry measures the hearing level.
- Acute otitis media is common in children because their Eustachian tube is short and flat; myringotomy drains the fluid.
- Ménière's disease = vertigo + fluctuating hearing loss + tinnitus + ear fullness; manage with a low-salt diet and medicines.
- Ototoxic drugs can cause new hearing loss, tinnitus or imbalance — report early.
- Epistaxis first aid: sit up, lean forward, pinch the soft nose for 10 minutes, breathe through the mouth, cold pack.
- Laryngeal obstruction and stridor are airway emergencies — oxygen and prepare for intubation or tracheostomy.
- For ear drops: adult pinna up and back, child down and back; warm the drops first.
- At every tracheostomy bedside keep a spare tube, obturator and dilator, suction and humidification.

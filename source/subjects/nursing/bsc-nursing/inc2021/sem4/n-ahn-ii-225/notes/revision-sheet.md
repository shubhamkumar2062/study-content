## Must-know comparisons

### AKI vs CKD

| Feature | AKI | CKD |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Onset | Sudden (hours to days) | Gradual (months to years) |
| Kidney size on USS | Normal or enlarged | Small, shrunken |
| Creatinine | Rises rapidly | Gradually elevated |
| Anaemia | Less common early | Yes (erythropoietin deficiency) |
| Reversibility | Often reversible | Irreversible; progressive |
| Bone disease | Not initially | Renal osteodystrophy (↑PTH, ↓Ca, ↑PO₄) |

### Nephritic vs Nephrotic syndrome

| Feature | Nephritic | Nephrotic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proteinuria | Mild | Massive (> 3.5 g/day) |
| Haematuria | Yes (RBC casts in urine) | Minimal |
| Oedema | Mild (periorbital) | Generalised (anasarca) |
| Hypertension | Yes | Variable |
| Serum albumin | Normal/mildly low | Very low (< 25 g/L) |
| Example cause | Post-strep GN, IgA nephropathy | Minimal change disease (children), membranous GN |

### Ischaemic vs Haemorrhagic stroke

| Feature | Ischaemic | Haemorrhagic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanism | Clot blocks artery | Blood vessel ruptures |
| Onset | May be gradual / TIA precursor | Sudden, often with headache |
| CT head | Hypodense (dark) area | Hyperdense (white) blood |
| Thrombolysis (tPA) | Yes — if within 4.5 h, no contraindication | CONTRAINDICATED |
| Preceding headache | Unusual | Common (worst headache of life — SAH) |

### Delirium vs Dementia

| Feature | Delirium | Dementia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Onset | Acute (hours to days) | Gradual (months to years) |
| Course | Fluctuating during the day | Progressive, stable day to day |
| Consciousness | Impaired (clouded) | Clear until late stages |
| Attention | Severely impaired | Less affected early |
| Reversibility | Often reversible | Irreversible (most types) |
| Main causes | Infection, drugs, constipation, metabolic | Alzheimer's, vascular, Lewy body |

### Burns — depth classification

| Degree | Old name | Appearance | Sensation | Healing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Superficial | First degree | Red, dry, no blisters | Painful | 3–5 days; no scar |
| Superficial partial thickness | Second degree | Red, blistered, moist | Very painful | 7–21 days; minimal scar |
| Deep partial thickness | Deep second degree | Pale/mottled, moist or leathery | Reduced pain | > 21 days; often needs grafting |
| Full thickness | Third degree | White/grey/black, dry, leathery | No pain (nerve destroyed) | Cannot heal; requires grafting |

## Sequences to draw from memory

### BCLS sequence (adult)

```text
   Safe? → Check responsiveness → Call for help + get AED
   → Check breathing (≤ 10 s, no normal breathing = arrest)
   → Compressions: heel of hand, lower sternum
     Rate: 100–120/min | Depth: 5–6 cm | Full recoil | 30:2
   → Open airway (head-tilt chin-lift)
   → 2 rescue breaths (each 1 s, watch chest rise)
   → Repeat 30:2 until AED arrives or signs of life
   → AED: on → pads → analyse → no contact → shock → CPR 2 min → re-analyse
```

### Anaphylaxis management

```text
   1. Adrenaline 0.5 mg IM lateral thigh — FIRST
   2. Call for emergency help
   3. Position: supine + legs raised (if no respiratory distress)
   4. 100% O₂ via non-rebreather mask
   5. IV access + 0.9% NaCl 500–1000 mL bolus
   6. Chlorpheniramine 10 mg IV (secondary — for skin)
   7. Hydrocortisone 200 mg IV (secondary — late phase)
   8. Salbutamol nebulised (for bronchospasm)
   9. Repeat adrenaline every 5–15 min if no improvement
   10. Admit; observe 6–12 hours (biphasic reaction risk)
```

### DKA management

```text
   Fluids: 0.9% NaCl 1 L/h → 1 L/2h → 1 L/4h
   Insulin: 0.1 unit/kg/h IV (start after first litre of fluid)
   K⁺: add to fluids once K⁺ < 5.5 mmol/L + urine output
   Monitor: glucose hourly, electrolytes 2-hourly, urine output
   Switch: IV→SC insulin when eating + glucose < 14 mmol/L
   Add 10% dextrose when glucose < 14 to protect brain
```

### Status epilepticus sequence

```text
   0–5 min:  Protect airway, recovery position, O₂, IV access, glucose
   5–10 min: Lorazepam 4 mg IV (or diazepam 10 mg IV/PR)
   10–20 min: Repeat benzodiazepine if still seizing
   20–30 min: Phenytoin 20 mg/kg IV infusion (or levetiracetam)
   > 30 min:  Anaesthetic agent (propofol/thiopentone) + ICU intubation
```

### Glasgow Coma Scale — quick recall

```text
   Eye opening:   4 Spontaneous | 3 To voice | 2 To pain | 1 None
   Verbal:        5 Oriented | 4 Confused | 3 Words | 2 Sounds | 1 None
   Motor:         6 Obeys | 5 Localises | 4 Withdraws | 3 Flexion | 2 Extension | 1 None
   Maximum = 15 | Intubate if ≤ 8 | Minimum = 3
```

## WHO analgesic ladder (3 steps)

```text
   Step 1 (mild pain):    Paracetamol ± NSAID ± adjuvant
   Step 2 (moderate):     Weak opioid (codeine / tramadol) ± Step 1
   Step 3 (severe):       Strong opioid (morphine / fentanyl) ± Step 1
   Always: by mouth | by the clock | by the ladder | laxative with every opioid
```

## Exam reminder

- Section A (20 marks): read carefully; eliminate wrong options first; never leave blank.
- Section B (20 marks): write 2 of 3 essays; use the 5-part structure (definition/classification → causes → features → investigations → management + nursing care); 10 marks = 5 × 2-mark points well written.
- Section C (35 marks): 7 of 9 short answers; choose the ones you know best; 2 marks go to any answer that shows you understand the question — always write something.
- For any emergency/procedure answer: state the nursing priorities in order (what comes first, second, third) and name specific drugs with dose and route.
- For any comparison question: draw a two-column table — it shows the examiner you can synthesise, and it is faster to write than prose.
- If you run out of time: outline-and-leave — a heading with 3–4 bullet points scores more than a blank page.

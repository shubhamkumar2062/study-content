# Unit 4: Clinical pathology — body cavity fluids, semen, urine and faeces

This unit covers laboratory examination of body fluids and excretions. For each specimen, learn: what is normal, how to collect it correctly, and how to interpret common abnormalities. Nurses collect most of these specimens, so technique matters as much as theory.

---

## 4.1 Body cavity fluid examination

### Principles: transudate vs exudate

Whenever fluid accumulates in a cavity (pleural, pericardial, peritoneal), the first question is whether it is a transudate (leaking from intact vessels due to pressure or protein imbalance) or an exudate (leaking from damaged or inflamed vessels).

**Light's criteria** (pleural fluid — any two of three features present = exudate):

| Criterion | Transudate | Exudate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pleural fluid protein / serum protein | < 0.5 | > 0.5 |
| Pleural fluid LDH / serum LDH | < 0.6 | > 0.6 |
| Pleural fluid LDH | < ⅔ upper limit of normal serum LDH | > ⅔ upper limit |

| Feature | Transudate | Exudate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specific gravity | < 1.012 | > 1.020 |
| Protein | < 3 g/dL | > 3 g/dL |
| Appearance | Clear, pale yellow | Cloudy, may be bloody or purulent |
| Cells | Few (<1000/µL) | Many (leucocytes, RBCs depending on cause) |
| Glucose | Normal | Low (infection, malignancy) |

### Pleural fluid

- Collected by thoracocentesis (pleural tap).
- **Transudate causes:** Heart failure, nephrotic syndrome, cirrhosis, hypoalbuminaemia.
- **Exudate causes:** Pneumonia (parapneumonic effusion), TB (lymphocytes, very low glucose, high protein), malignancy (cytology for cancer cells), pulmonary embolism.
- **Additional tests:** Glucose (low in empyema and TB), pH (low in empyema), adenosine deaminase (ADA — raised in TB), Gram stain and culture, cytology for malignant cells.
- **Nursing (thoracocentesis):** Position the patient sitting upright leaning forward on a table (or lateral decubitus); help maintain sterile field; monitor for pneumothorax (sudden dyspnoea, reduced breath sounds) and hypotension; label specimen correctly with time and date.

### Pericardial fluid

- Collected by pericardiocentesis (a cardiologist procedure).
- Causes of pericardial effusion: viral pericarditis (most common), TB, uraemia, malignancy, post-MI.
- Large effusion → cardiac tamponade (Beck's triad: raised JVP, low BP, muffled heart sounds).

### Ascites (peritoneal fluid)

- Collected by paracentesis (abdominal tap).
- **Transudate:** Cirrhosis, heart failure, nephrotic syndrome, Budd-Chiari syndrome.
- **Exudate:** TB peritonitis, malignant ascites (ovarian, bowel, liver), spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP).
- **SAAG (Serum-Ascites Albumin Gradient):** Serum albumin − ascites albumin.
  - SAAG ≥ 1.1 g/dL → portal hypertension (cirrhosis, heart failure) — transudate mechanism.
  - SAAG < 1.1 g/dL → non-portal hypertension cause (TB, malignancy) — exudate mechanism.
- **Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP):** Complication of cirrhosis; polymorphonuclear cell (PMN) count >250/µL in ascites; treat with cefotaxime IV.
- **Nursing (paracentesis):** Empty bladder first; assist patient in supine or semi-recumbent position; sterile technique; label samples; watch for fluid leak from site, hypotension, electrolyte disturbance after large-volume paracentesis (give albumin infusion as ordered).

## 4.2 Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) examination

CSF is obtained by **lumbar puncture (LP)** between L3-L4 or L4-L5 vertebrae (below the conus medullaris).

**Contraindications to LP:** Raised ICP with space-occupying lesion (risk of brainstem herniation — always do CT head before LP if focal deficit or papilloedema).

### Normal CSF values

| Parameter | Normal value |
| --- | --- |
| Pressure | 70–180 mmH₂O (measured with patient lying flat) |
| Appearance | Crystal clear, like water |
| Protein | 15–45 mg/dL |
| Glucose | 50–80 mg/dL (or ≥60% of blood glucose) |
| Cells | <5/µL (all mononuclear; no RBCs) |
| Colour | Colourless |

### CSF findings in common meningeal diseases

| Disease | Appearance | Pressure | Cells | Protein | Glucose | Other |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | Clear | 70–180 | <5 lymphocytes | 15–45 | 50–80 (≥60% blood) | — |
| Bacterial meningitis | Turbid/purulent | Raised | Hundreds–thousands; neutrophils | High (>100 mg/dL) | Very low (<40) | Gram stain/culture positive |
| Viral meningitis | Clear | Normal or mildly raised | Tens–hundreds; lymphocytes | Mildly raised | Normal or mildly low | PCR for virus |
| TB meningitis | Clear or slightly yellow | Raised | Tens–hundreds; lymphocytes | Very high (>200) | Very low | AFB smear/culture; fibrin web; ADA raised |
| Subarachnoid haemorrhage | Uniformly blood-stained → xanthochromic (yellow) after centrifugation | Raised | RBCs | High | Normal | Xanthochromia confirms SAH (not traumatic tap) |

**Nursing care post-LP:**
- Lie flat for 2–4 hours to prevent post-LP headache (from low CSF pressure).
- Encourage fluids.
- Monitor for headache (treat with simple analgesia, caffeine; blood patch for severe headache).
- Watch for signs of meningism, neurological deterioration, or infection at puncture site.
- Label all three numbered tubes correctly (cell count is usually from tube 3 to avoid false positive from blood in tube 1).

## 4.3 Semen analysis

Semen analysis is the first-line investigation for male infertility. The nurse's role is in correct collection instruction and labelling.

### Collection technique (nurse to teach)

- Abstain from ejaculation for 2–5 days before collection (not less, not more).
- Collect entire sample by masturbation directly into the sterile container provided.
- Collect at home or in a private room at the lab; deliver within 30–60 minutes; keep the container at body temperature (inside clothing) during transport.
- Label with name, date, time of collection, days of abstinence.

### WHO 2021 reference values (lower reference limits)

| Parameter | Lower reference limit |
| --- | --- |
| Volume | ≥ 1.4 mL |
| Total sperm count | ≥ 39 million |
| Sperm concentration | ≥ 16 million/mL |
| Total motility (progressive + non-progressive) | ≥ 42% |
| Progressive motility | ≥ 30% |
| Morphology (strict Kruger criteria) | ≥ 4% normal forms |

### Terminology

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Azoospermia | No sperm (obstructive or non-obstructive) |
| Oligospermia | Low sperm count |
| Asthenospermia | Poor motility |
| Teratospermia | Abnormal morphology |
| Oligoasthenoteratospermia (OAT) | All three abnormal |

**Nursing:** Use non-judgmental communication; explain the procedure privately; ensure the patient understands the significance of proper collection (incomplete samples or time delay give false results).

## 4.4 Urinalysis

A complete urinalysis has three parts: macroscopic (visual), dipstick (chemical), and microscopic.

### Macroscopic

| Colour/appearance | Significance |
| --- | --- |
| Pale yellow, clear | Normal |
| Dark yellow/amber | Concentrated urine; dehydration |
| Red/brown | Haematuria (RBCs), haemoglobinuria (haemolysis), myoglobinuria (rhabdomyolysis) |
| Cloudy | Pyuria (pus), phosphaturia, chyluria |
| Frothy | Proteinuria |
| Foamy orange-yellow | Bilirubinuria (obstructive jaundice) |

### Dipstick (chemical)

| Parameter | Normal | Abnormal and significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pH | 4.5–8.0 | Acid in ketoacidosis; alkaline in UTI (urea-splitting bacteria) |
| Specific gravity (SG) | 1.001–1.035 | Fixed 1.010 = poor renal concentrating ability (CKD) |
| Protein | Negative (trace allowed) | 1+ or more = proteinuria; always measure 24-h urine protein to quantify |
| Glucose | Negative | Glycosuria: diabetes mellitus, Fanconi syndrome |
| Ketones | Negative | Diabetic ketoacidosis, starvation |
| Bilirubin | Negative | Conjugated (direct) hyperbilirubinaemia — obstructive jaundice |
| Urobilinogen | Small amount | Raised in hepatic or haemolytic jaundice; absent in complete biliary obstruction |
| Blood | Negative | 1+ or more = haematuria or haemoglobinuria |
| Nitrites | Negative | Positive = nitrate-reducing bacteria (Gram-negative UTI — E. coli) |
| Leucocyte esterase | Negative | Positive = pyuria (UTI) |

### Microscopic (urine sediment)

| Finding | Significance |
| --- | --- |
| RBCs | Haematuria: GN (dysmorphic RBCs), cystitis, stone, tumour |
| WBCs / pus cells | UTI, interstitial nephritis; >5 WBCs/HPF is abnormal |
| Bacteria | UTI (send for culture/sensitivity) |
| Hyaline casts | Normal in small numbers; dehydration, strenuous exercise |
| Granular casts | ATN, GN |
| RBC casts | Glomerulonephritis (pathognomonic) |
| WBC casts | Pyelonephritis, interstitial nephritis |
| Waxy/broad casts | Advanced CKD (dilated tubules) |
| Fatty casts / oval fat bodies | Nephrotic syndrome |
| Crystals | Uric acid (gout, uric acid stones), calcium oxalate (hyperoxaluria, some stones) |

### 24-hour urine protein and creatinine clearance

- **24-h urine protein:** Quantifies proteinuria; >3.5 g/day = nephrotic range.
- **Creatinine clearance (mL/min) = (Urinary creatinine × Urine volume) ÷ Serum creatinine** — estimates GFR.
- **Spot urine protein : creatinine ratio** can be used instead of 24-h collection (ratio >3.5 ≈ nephrotic range proteinuria).

**Nursing — urine collection:**
- Mid-stream urine (MSU) for culture: clean perineum/glans, start to void, then catch mid-stream into sterile container; deliver to lab within 2 hours or refrigerate.
- 24-h urine: discard first morning void, collect all subsequent urine including the next morning void into the large container (keep refrigerated); record total volume and send 20 mL aliquot.
- Catheter specimen of urine (CSU): aspirate from the port on the catheter with a sterile syringe (never from the bag).

## 4.5 Faeces examination

### Macroscopic

| Appearance | Significance |
| --- | --- |
| Pale, greasy, bulky | Steatorrhoea — fat malabsorption (chronic pancreatitis, coeliac disease) |
| Tarry black (melaena) | Upper GI bleeding (blood digested during transit) |
| Bright red blood | Lower GI bleeding (rectum, sigmoid, haemorrhoids) |
| Mucus | Dysentery (Amoeba, Shigella), inflammatory bowel disease |
| Rice-water stool | Cholera |

### Microscopy (ova, cysts and trophozoites)

| Organism | Form found | Disease |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entamoeba histolytica | Trophozoites (in acute dysentery, with RBCs), cysts (in chronic carrier) | Amoebic dysentery, liver abscess |
| Giardia lamblia | Cysts (most common), trophozoites | Giardiasis — greasy diarrhoea, bloating |
| Ascaris lumbricoides | Ova | Ascariasis — most common helminth infection worldwide |
| Hookworm (Ancylostoma, Necator) | Ova | Iron-deficiency anaemia from blood loss |
| Trichuris trichiura | Ova | Rectal prolapse in heavy infection |

### Occult blood (FOBT — faecal occult blood test)

- Detects blood in faeces that is not visible to the naked eye.
- **Purpose:** Screening for colorectal cancer (annual FOBT in adults ≥50 years) and upper GI bleeding.
- **Guaiac-based test (gFOBT):** Detects haem; patient must avoid red meat, NSAIDs, vitamin C for 3 days before the test.
- **Immunochemical test (FIT):** Detects human haemoglobin specifically; no dietary restriction; more specific for lower GI bleeding.

### Stool culture

- Indicated for acute diarrhoea lasting >48 hours, bloody diarrhoea, immunocompromised patient, food-handler.
- Common pathogens: Salmonella, Shigella (dysentery), Campylobacter jejuni (most common bacterial cause of diarrhoea in developed countries), Vibrio cholerae (rice-water stool), pathogenic E. coli (ETEC — traveller's diarrhoea; EHEC/O157:H7 — haemolytic uraemic syndrome).
- **Clostridium difficile (C. diff):** Send stool for C. diff toxin (ELISA) in patients who develop diarrhoea after antibiotics; treat with oral metronidazole or vancomycin; contact precautions (alcohol hand rub does NOT kill spores — use soap and water).
- **Rotavirus antigen:** Rapid test for Rotavirus antigen in stool (most common cause of severe gastroenteritis in children under 5); prevented by Rotavirus vaccine.

**Nursing — stool collection:**
- Fresh stool into a clean, dry container; aim for thumb-sized amount (5–10 g) or sufficient quantity as instructed.
- For microscopy and culture: deliver within 2 hours; if delay, use transport medium (Cary-Blair for bacterial culture, PVA fixative for parasites).
- For C. diff: liquid or unformed stool only (formed stool in an asymptomatic patient is not tested).
- Universal precautions; strict hand hygiene after handling.

## Remember

- Transudate: low protein, low SG, clear; causes = heart failure, nephrotic, cirrhosis.
- Exudate: high protein, high SG, cloudy; causes = infection, TB, malignancy.
- Light's criteria for pleural fluid: any one of three ratios = exudate.
- SAAG ≥1.1 → portal hypertension (cirrhosis); SAAG <1.1 → TB or malignancy.
- CSF bacterial meningitis: turbid, thousands of neutrophils, very high protein, very low glucose; Gram stain positive.
- CSF viral meningitis: clear, lymphocytes, mild protein rise, normal glucose.
- CSF TB meningitis: clear, lymphocytes, very high protein, very low glucose, AFB.
- Semen: 2–5 days abstinence, deliver within 60 min; normal concentration ≥16 million/mL, progressive motility ≥30%.
- RBC casts = glomerulonephritis (pathognomonic); granular casts = ATN; waxy/broad casts = CKD.
- FOBT screening for colorectal cancer; FIT is more specific, needs no dietary restriction.
- C. diff: after antibiotics → test toxin; soap and water (not alcohol gel) for hand hygiene with spores.

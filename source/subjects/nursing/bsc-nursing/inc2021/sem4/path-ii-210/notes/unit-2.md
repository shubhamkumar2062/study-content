# Unit 2: Special pathology — male and female genital systems

This unit covers diseases of the male and female reproductive tracts — tumours, inflammatory conditions, and premalignant lesions. Learn each as cause → morphology → features → treatment → nurse's role.

---

## PART A — MALE GENITAL SYSTEM

## 2.1 Testicular tumours

The testis produces two main tumour categories: **germ cell tumours** (95% — derived from spermatogenic cells) and sex-cord stromal tumours.

### Seminoma

- Most common testicular germ cell tumour; peak age 25–45 years.
- **Morphology:** Homogeneous, pale grey-white cut surface; large uniform cells with clear cytoplasm and central nuclei; lymphocytic infiltrate.
- **Tumour markers:** β-HCG mildly raised in a minority; AFP is **not** raised (a seminoma that raises AFP is actually a non-seminomatous component).
- **Features:** Painless, smooth enlargement of one testis; retroperitoneal lymph node metastasis; lung metastasis in advanced disease.
- **Treatment:** Orchidectomy via inguinal approach (not scrotal — avoids scrotal lymphatic seeding) + radiotherapy ± chemotherapy; excellent cure rate.

### Non-seminomatous germ cell tumours (NSGCT)

Includes embryonal carcinoma, yolk sac tumour (most common in infants — AFP raised), choriocarcinoma (β-HCG raised, most aggressive, haematogenous spread early), and teratoma (contains mature tissue from all three germ layers — may have teeth, hair, cartilage; mature teratoma is benign in children, malignant in adults).

| Tumour marker | Useful in |
| --- | --- |
| AFP (alpha-fetoprotein) | Yolk sac tumour, mixed NSGCT — not raised in pure seminoma |
| β-HCG (beta-human chorionic gonadotrophin) | Choriocarcinoma, some seminomas |
| LDH (lactate dehydrogenase) | Bulk marker of disease |

Markers guide treatment response and detect recurrence.

## 2.2 Cryptorchidism

- Undescended testis (retained in the abdomen or inguinal canal).
- Risk of **malignancy is 4–10× higher** than in a normally descended testis; contralateral normally descended testis risk is also slightly elevated, suggesting a systemic predisposition.
- Also causes infertility if bilateral (high temperature impairs spermatogenesis).
- **Management:** Orchidopexy (surgical fixation in the scrotum) ideally before age 2 years to reduce malignancy risk and preserve fertility.

## 2.3 Epididymo-orchitis

- Acute infection of the epididymis and testis.
- In young sexually active men: **Chlamydia trachomatis** and **Neisseria gonorrhoeae**.
- In older men and children: **E. coli** and other Gram-negative bacilli (associated with urinary tract infection or structural abnormality).
- Features: Painful, tender, hot, swollen scrotum; fever; dysuria.
- Distinguish from torsion of testis (torsion is an emergency with sudden onset, no fever, negative Prehn's sign — lifting does not relieve pain — requires immediate surgery).
- **Treatment:** Antibiotics based on cause; scrotal support; analgesia.

## 2.4 Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH)

- Very common in men over 50; nearly universal by 80 years.
- Affects the **periurethral (transition zone)** of the prostate — compresses the urethra.
- **Morphology:** Nodular hyperplasia of glands and stroma; not a cancer, does not become cancer.
- **Features:**
  - Lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS): hesitancy, poor stream, dribbling, sensation of incomplete emptying (obstructive); frequency, urgency, nocturia (irritative).
  - Acute urinary retention is an emergency needing immediate catheterisation.
  - Chronic retention → back pressure on kidneys → hydronephrosis → renal failure.
- **Diagnosis:** PSA (mildly elevated in BPH; much higher in cancer), uroflowmetry, ultrasound residual urine.
- **Treatment:** Alpha-1 blockers (tamsulosin — relax smooth muscle), 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors (finasteride — shrink gland), transurethral resection of the prostate (TURP) for severe cases.
- **Nursing:** Pre-TURP: explain procedure, bowel preparation; Post-TURP: continuous bladder irrigation (watch for clots blocking the catheter, TUR syndrome — hyponatraemia from fluid absorption), measure urine colour and output; catheter care; teach that some haematuria is expected initially; explain that TURP can cause retrograde ejaculation.

## 2.5 Carcinoma of the prostate

- Most common cancer in men in the West; increasing in India.
- Arises from the **peripheral zone** of the prostate — does not cause urinary obstruction early (unlike BPH which is in the periurethral zone).
- **Risk factors:** Age (most common after 65), family history, Black African descent.
- **Morphology:** Adenocarcinoma; Gleason grading system (5 patterns of gland differentiation, two most prominent areas scored 1–5 each; Gleason score 2–10; score ≥7 = high grade, aggressive).
- **Features:** Often asymptomatic; found by raised PSA on screening. When locally advanced: LUTS, haematuria, haemospermia. When metastatic: bone pain from **osteoblastic** (bone-forming) metastases — in lumbar spine, pelvis, femur (high alkaline phosphatase and PSA).
- **Diagnosis:** PSA + digital rectal examination (DRE) → transrectal ultrasound biopsy.
- **Treatment:**
  - Localised: active surveillance, radical prostatectomy, or radiotherapy.
  - Advanced: androgen deprivation therapy (castration — surgical or chemical with LHRH agonists), abiraterone, chemotherapy.
- **Nursing:** PSA counselling; pre-operative bowel preparation; post-prostatectomy: catheter care, pelvic floor exercises for urinary incontinence; emotional support (erectile dysfunction and incontinence affect quality of life); teach bone metastasis warning signs (new back pain).

## PART B — FEMALE GENITAL SYSTEM

## 2.6 Cervical pathology

### Cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN)

- CIN is **premalignant** dysplasia of the squamocolumnar junction (transformation zone) of the cervix.
- **Cause:** Human papillomavirus (HPV) infection — types **16 and 18** are responsible for about 70% of cervical cancers.

| Grade | Dysplasia extent | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CIN 1 | Lower ⅓ of epithelium | Most regress spontaneously |
| CIN 2 | Lower ⅔ | Intermediate risk |
| CIN 3 | Full-thickness (carcinoma in situ) | High risk of progression if untreated |

- **Diagnosis:** Pap (Papanicolaou) smear → colposcopy and biopsy.
- **Treatment:** CIN 1: observe; CIN 2–3: ablation (cryotherapy, laser) or excision (LLETZ/cone biopsy).
- **Prevention:** HPV vaccination (girls 9–14 years — most effective before first exposure); regular Pap smear screening.

### Cervical carcinoma

- Most common gynaecological cancer in India and many low-income countries.
- **Squamous cell carcinoma** (80%); adenocarcinoma (20% — from endocervical glands).
- **Risk factors:** HPV 16/18, multiple sexual partners, early first sexual intercourse, immunosuppression, smoking, lack of screening.
- **Features:** Irregular vaginal bleeding (especially post-coital), offensive vaginal discharge, pelvic pain in advanced disease.
- **Staging (FIGO):**
  - Stage I: confined to cervix.
  - Stage II: extends beyond uterus but not to pelvic wall or lower vagina.
  - Stage III: reaches pelvic wall or lower vagina or causes hydronephrosis.
  - Stage IV: beyond true pelvis or involves bladder/rectum.
- **Treatment:** Early (I–IIA): surgery (Wertheim's hysterectomy) or RT; advanced: concurrent chemoradiation (cisplatin + RT).
- **Nurse's role in prevention:** Educate women on HPV vaccination; promote Pap smear uptake (especially for women 21–65 years); reduce stigma; explain that early-stage disease is curable; support women during RT (perineal skin care, diarrhoea management, fatigue, emotional support).

## 2.7 Endometrial pathology

### Endometrial hyperplasia

- Excess oestrogen stimulation without progesterone opposition → glandular overgrowth.
- Risk factors: obesity (peripheral oestrogen from fat), polycystic ovary syndrome, unopposed oestrogen therapy, oestrogen-secreting ovarian tumour.
- **Simple hyperplasia without atypia:** low cancer risk; treat with progestogens.
- **Atypical hyperplasia:** significant cancer risk; hysterectomy recommended.

### Endometrial carcinoma

- Most common gynaecological cancer in Western countries; second in India.
- **Type I (most common):** Endometrioid adenocarcinoma; linked to excess oestrogen; presents early (stage I); good prognosis.
- **Type II:** Serous or clear cell; not oestrogen-driven; aggressive; worse prognosis.
- **Features:** Post-menopausal bleeding (PMB) — any PMB must be investigated until cancer is excluded. In pre-menopausal women: irregular heavy bleeding.
- **Diagnosis:** Transvaginal ultrasound (endometrial thickness) → endometrial biopsy.
- **Treatment:** Total abdominal hysterectomy + bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy ± radiotherapy; progestogen therapy for early-stage in women who want fertility.

## 2.8 Ovarian tumours

Ovarian tumours are the most common cause of death from gynaecological cancer in Western countries because they are often silent until late.

**Broad classification:**

| Group | Main types | Tumour markers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Surface epithelial (most common — 65%) | Serous cystadenoma/cystadenocarcinoma, mucinous cystadenoma/cystadenocarcinoma, endometrioid | CA-125 (serous), CEA (mucinous) |
| Germ cell (more common in young women) | Dermoid cyst (mature cystic teratoma — benign, most common in young women), dysgerminoma (malignant), yolk sac tumour | AFP (yolk sac), β-HCG (choriocarcinoma) |
| Sex cord-stromal | Granulosa cell tumour (oestrogen-secreting → precocious puberty in girls, PMB in women), thecoma, fibroma, Sertoli-Leydig | Inhibin (granulosa cell) |
| Metastatic (Krukenberg tumour) | Bilateral metastasis from stomach, colon or breast | Varies with primary |

- **Dermoid cyst (mature teratoma):** Most common benign ovarian cyst in reproductive age; contains teeth, hair, sebaceous material; benign but can tort (twist), causing sudden severe pelvic pain.
- **Serous cystadenocarcinoma:** Most common malignant ovarian tumour; bilateral in 60%; spreads by peritoneal seeding; psammoma bodies (calcification) on microscopy; presents with abdominal distension (ascites).
- **Krukenberg tumour:** Bilateral ovarian metastases from gastric carcinoma (signet-ring cells in a fibroblastic stroma); malignant.

## 2.9 Vulval conditions

- **Lichen sclerosus:** Thin white plaques; itching and soreness; benign but slight increased risk of squamous carcinoma; treated with potent topical steroids.
- **Vulval intraepithelial neoplasia (VIN):** Premalignant dysplasia; associated with HPV 16/18; treated by excision or laser.
- **Squamous cell carcinoma of the vulva:** Older women; presents as an ulcerative or exophytic lesion; treated by wide local excision ± inguinal lymph node dissection.

## 2.10 Common inflammatory conditions

| Condition | Cause | Features | Nursing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) | Chlamydia, gonorrhoea, mixed anaerobes ascending from cervix | Bilateral lower abdominal pain, cervical motion tenderness, purulent discharge, fever | IV antibiotics (broad spectrum), IV fluids, pain management, contact tracing, education on STI prevention |
| Cervicitis | Chlamydia, gonorrhoea | Mucopurulent discharge; often asymptomatic | Screen and treat sexual partner; use condoms |
| Trichomonas vaginitis | Trichomonas vaginalis (STI) | Frothy greenish offensive discharge, pruritus, "strawberry cervix" | Metronidazole for patient AND partner |
| Candida vaginitis | Candida albicans | Thick white "cottage-cheese" discharge, intense pruritus; recurs with antibiotics, diabetes, immunosuppression | Topical or oral azole antifungals |
| Bacterial vaginosis (BV) | Overgrowth of anaerobes (Gardnerella vaginalis) | Thin grey discharge with fishy smell; not an STI | Metronidazole; can recur |

## Remember

- Testicular germ cell tumours: seminoma = radiosensitive; NSGCT = AFP/β-HCG raised depending on type. Orchidectomy via inguinal route.
- Cryptorchidism: 4–10× cancer risk; orchidopexy before age 2.
- BPH: periurethral zone, obstructive symptoms; TURP — post-op continuous irrigation, watch for TUR syndrome.
- Prostate carcinoma: peripheral zone, osteoblastic bone metastases, PSA raised; androgen deprivation therapy for metastatic.
- HPV 16/18 → CIN → cervical carcinoma; Pap smear screening + HPV vaccine prevent it.
- PMB (post-menopausal bleeding) must be investigated — endometrial carcinoma until proved otherwise.
- Ovarian cancers are silent until late; most common cause of gynaecological cancer death.
- Krukenberg tumour = bilateral ovarian metastasis from gastric carcinoma.
- PID: bilateral pelvic pain + cervical motion tenderness; treat with broad-spectrum antibiotics including anaerobic cover and trace sexual contacts.

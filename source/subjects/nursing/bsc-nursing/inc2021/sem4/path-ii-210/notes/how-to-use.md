# How to use this guide

Pathology II and Genetics (PATH(II)210) is a fourth-semester subject of the INC 2021 B.Sc Nursing curriculum. It has two parts:

1. **Pathology II** — special pathology of the kidneys, genital systems (male and female), breast, and central nervous system; and clinical pathology (examination of body fluids, semen, urine, and faeces). This completes the pathology started in Pathology I (Semester 3).
2. **Genetics** — a full module covering chromosomes and inheritance, teratogenesis, cancer genetics, prenatal diagnosis, genetic testing, and genetic counselling.

Together with the Pharmacology II (PHAR(II)205) paper, this subject is examined in one combined 75-mark paper at the end of Semester 4.

---

## The combined Semester 4 examination

PATH(II)210 does **not** have a separate university paper. It is examined as two sections of a **combined 3-hour, 75-mark** paper:

| Section | Subject | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Applied Pharmacology II | 38 |
| B | Pathology (Pathology I + Pathology II combined) | 25 |
| C | Genetics | 12 |
| **Total** | | **75** |

The pathology section (Section B) is **25 marks** and covers both Pathology I (Semester 3) and Pathology II (this subject). There is typically no internal choice in Section B.

The genetics section (Section C) is **12 marks** — a focused module that is worth learning thoroughly because the questions are specific and factual. There is typically no internal choice in Section C.

Confirm the exact paper pattern with your university — different universities implement the INC Regulations 2020 (Appendix 3) slightly differently.

## Probability ratings

- **High** — comes up in most papers in some form; learn the full structured answer.
- **Medium** — appears every few papers, or as part of a larger question.
- **Low** — worth knowing for MCQs and very short answers.

These are estimates based on the weight of each topic in the INC syllabus and the typical style of nursing pathology and genetics papers.

## Scoring tips

1. **Pathology answers:** Use cause → mechanism → morphology → features → nursing. This frame works for every disease question.
2. **Comparisons are easy marks:** Nephritic vs nephrotic, AKI vs CKD, transudate vs exudate, CSF in different types of meningitis, benign vs malignant tumour — answer these as tables.
3. **Know normal values:** Normal CSF (protein 15–45, glucose 50–80, <5 cells), normal urine (SG 1.001–1.035, protein negative, glucose negative), semen (count ≥16 million/mL, progressive motility ≥30%).
4. **Genetics questions reward exact terminology:** Autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive, X-linked recessive — state the pattern name and give one clinical example.
5. **Draw the pedigree correctly when asked:** Use standard symbols; mark the proband with an arrow; a 3-generation pedigree earns marks.
6. **For very short answers (2 marks):** One definition + one example. Do not write a paragraph.
7. **Add nursing points:** Even in a pathology question, one or two nursing relevance lines lift the answer.
8. **Genetics counselling:** Always emphasise **non-directive** counselling and the patient's right to decide.
9. **Night before:** Read the "Remember" boxes at the end of each unit; run the Quick MCQ test; re-draw the CSF comparison table, nephritic vs nephrotic table, and pedigree symbols from memory.

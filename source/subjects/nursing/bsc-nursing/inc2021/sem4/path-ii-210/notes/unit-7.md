# Unit 7: Genetics — testing in neonates, children, adolescents and adults

This unit covers the clinical application of genetics: newborn screening, prenatal diagnosis, and the management and nursing care of common genetic conditions across the life span.

---

## 7.1 Newborn (neonatal) screening

The goal of newborn screening is to detect treatable conditions **before symptoms appear**, so early treatment can prevent permanent disability.

### Methods

- **Guthrie test (bacterial inhibition assay):** Original method for PKU using heel-prick blood on filter paper; now largely replaced by tandem mass spectrometry.
- **Tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS):** One blood spot can screen for >30 conditions simultaneously (amino acid disorders, organic acid disorders, fatty acid oxidation defects).
- **Immunoassays:** For congenital hypothyroidism (TSH) and congenital adrenal hyperplasia (17-OHP).
- **Molecular (DNA-based):** For cystic fibrosis, haemoglobin disorders (sickle cell, thalassaemia).

### Conditions screened in India (National Programme)

India's National Neonatal Screening Programme focuses on a core set:

| Condition | Test | Treatment if detected early |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Congenital hypothyroidism | TSH on heel-prick | Levothyroxine → prevents cretinism (intellectual disability, growth failure) |
| Phenylketonuria (PKU) | Phenylalanine level (MS/MS) | Phenylalanine-free formula + low-Phe diet → prevents intellectual disability |
| Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH) | 17-hydroxyprogesterone | Steroids → prevents adrenal crisis and virilisation |
| G6PD deficiency | Enzyme assay | Avoidance of oxidant triggers → prevents haemolytic crises |
| Sickle cell disease / thalassaemia | Haemoglobin electrophoresis or DNA | Early treatment, parental counselling, penicillin prophylaxis |
| Galactosaemia | Galactose enzyme (Galactose-1-phosphate uridylyltransferase) | Lactose-free diet → prevents liver failure, intellectual disability |

**Timing:** Blood spot collected at 48–72 hours of life (after feeding to ensure metabolites have risen). A second sample may be taken at 2 weeks.

**What happens if screening is missed:** These conditions cause irreversible damage in the first weeks and months of life — intellectual disability, seizures, organ failure, or death. The investment in screening is justified by the small cost of a heel-prick versus lifelong care for a disabled child.

**Nursing role in newborn screening:**
- Collect the heel-prick sample correctly (warm the heel, use the lateral aspect — never the central heel pad, which risks bone injury).
- Complete the form accurately (name, date, time, gestational age, feeding status).
- Explain to parents why the test is done and when results will come.
- Ensure follow-up is arranged for screen-positive results (screen-positive ≠ diagnosis; confirmatory testing is needed).
- Do not give false reassurance ("it's just a blood test, nothing to worry about") — parents whose child later has the condition need to trust that they were given adequate information.

## 7.2 Prenatal diagnosis

Prenatal diagnosis allows detection of chromosomal abnormalities and some single-gene disorders before birth, enabling informed decision-making.

### Combined first trimester screening (CFTS)

- Ultrasound at 11–14 weeks: **nuchal translucency** (NT) measurement (increased NT → risk of Down syndrome, cardiac defects, other chromosomal abnormalities, Turner syndrome, hydrops).
- Maternal serum: free β-hCG and PAPP-A (pregnancy-associated plasma protein A).
- Combined with maternal age → risk estimate (not diagnosis).

### Chorionic villus sampling (CVS)

- **Timing:** 10–13 weeks.
- **Method:** Sample of placental trophoblast via transabdominal or transcervical route.
- **Advantages:** Early diagnosis; result available in 1–2 weeks (karyotype) or 48 h for FISH/QF-PCR.
- **Risk:** 0.5–1% miscarriage (slightly higher than amniocentesis).

### Amniocentesis

- **Timing:** 15–18 weeks (occasionally earlier).
- **Method:** 15–20 mL amniotic fluid aspirated; foetal cells cultured for karyotype.
- **Risk:** Approximately 0.5% (1 in 200) risk of miscarriage.
- **Additional tests from amniotic fluid:** Biochemistry (AFP — raised in NTD), enzyme assays for metabolic disorders, DNA analysis.

### Cell-free foetal DNA (cffDNA / NIPT)

- Foetal DNA fragments circulate in maternal blood from ~10 weeks.
- **Non-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT/NIPS):** Analyses cffDNA from a maternal blood sample.
- **Detects:** Trisomy 21, 18, 13 with >99% sensitivity; sex chromosome aneuploidies; foetal sex (for X-linked conditions).
- **Limitations:** A screening test (not diagnostic); can be falsely positive if placental mosaicism; cannot detect all chromosomal abnormalities or most single-gene disorders (expanding to some genes, but not yet mainstream).
- Positive NIPT must be confirmed by CVS or amniocentesis before any clinical decision.
- **Foetal sex determination ban:** In India, the **Pre-Conception and Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques (PCPNDT) Act** prohibits sex determination (except for sex-linked disease) and sex-selective abortion. Any clinician found in violation faces imprisonment and loss of registration.

### Nursing role in prenatal testing

- Provide balanced, non-directive information: explain what the test does and does not detect, the risk of the procedure, what a positive result would mean.
- Ensure informed consent is documented.
- Support the woman emotionally before, during and after the procedure.
- Explain that all invasive procedures carry a small risk of pregnancy loss; allow time for questions.
- After amniocentesis: advise rest for 24 hours, avoid heavy lifting, attend if cramping or fluid loss occurs.

## 7.3 Genetic conditions presenting in childhood

### Down syndrome (trisomy 21)

- **Features:** Characteristic facial appearance (flat face, upward-slanting eyes, epicanthal folds, Brushfield spots on the iris), small ears, protruding tongue, single palmar crease (Simian line), hypotonia at birth, broad hands with short 5th finger (clinodactyly), 2.5-line gap between first and second toes.
- **Associated problems:** Intellectual disability (mild to moderate), congenital heart disease (40–50% — most commonly AV septal defect, VSD, ASD), duodenal atresia, hypothyroidism, leukaemia (acute megakaryoblastic), Alzheimer's disease (typically 40s–50s), recurrent respiratory infections, hearing and vision problems.
- **Lifespan:** Median >60 years with modern care.
- **Nursing care:**
  - Feed the infant with patience (hypotonia affects suck); positioning, special teats.
  - Cardiac monitoring; arrange echocardiogram.
  - Early intervention (physiotherapy, speech therapy, occupational therapy).
  - Education in mainstream or special schools — promote inclusion.
  - Counsel parents without pity; support self-advocacy as the child grows.
  - Annual thyroid function test; hearing and vision screening.
  - Explain Alzheimer's risk to families as the person ages.

### Cystic fibrosis (CF)

- Most common serious autosomal recessive disease in Europeans; also increasingly recognised in India.
- **Gene:** CFTR (Cystic Fibrosis Transmembrane conductance Regulator) on chromosome 7; most common mutation is ΔF508 (deletion of phenylalanine at position 508).
- **Mechanism:** Defective chloride channel → thick viscid mucus in lungs, pancreas, intestine, reproductive tract.
- **Features:**
  - Neonatal: meconium ileus (10%), prolonged jaundice.
  - Recurrent chest infections (Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Staphylococcus aureus) → bronchiectasis.
  - Pancreatic insufficiency → malabsorption, steatorrhoea, failure to thrive.
  - Infertility (most males — absent vas deferens; some females).
- **Diagnosis:** Sweat test (sweat chloride >60 mmol/L) + CFTR mutation analysis.
- **Treatment:**
  - Chest physiotherapy (postural drainage) 2× daily; Flutter device; Active Cycle of Breathing.
  - Inhaled DNase (dornase alfa) — thins mucus.
  - Inhaled antibiotics (tobramycin); oral azithromycin (anti-inflammatory).
  - Pancreatic enzyme replacement (Creon) with every meal.
  - High-energy, high-fat diet plus fat-soluble vitamins.
  - **CFTR modulators:** Ivacaftor (a potentiator, for specific mutations like G551D) and elexacaftor-tezacaftor-ivacaftor (Trikafta/Kaftrio) for ΔF508 — greatly improve lung function and quality of life; now first-line in eligible patients.
  - Lung transplantation for end-stage disease.
- **Nursing:** Teach physiotherapy technique to parents and patient; explain enzyme dose before meals; infection control (CF patients should not mix — cross-infection risk); emotional support for a chronic progressive disease; genetic counselling for family planning.

### Phenylketonuria (PKU)

- Autosomal recessive; PAH gene mutation → phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency → phenylalanine (Phe) accumulates.
- Phe is neurotoxic → intellectual disability, seizures, eczema, musty urine odour (from phenylacetic acid).
- **Detected by newborn screening** — before symptoms appear.
- **Treatment:** Lifelong low-phenylalanine diet (avoid high-protein foods — meat, fish, eggs, dairy, nuts, regular bread); supplement with phenylalanine-free amino acid formula for protein needs.
- **Maternal PKU:** Women with PKU must maintain strict dietary control before and during pregnancy — high maternal Phe crosses placenta → teratogenic (microcephaly, cardiac defects, intellectual disability in a non-PKU foetus).
- **Nursing:** Explain the diet and its importance lifelong; support adherence in adolescence (teenagers often rebel); connect with dietitian; newborn testing teaches parents the importance of the heel prick.

## 7.4 Genetic conditions presenting in adolescence and adulthood

### Familial hypercholesterolaemia (FH)

- Autosomal dominant; LDL receptor mutation → LDL not cleared → markedly elevated LDL-C.
- **Features:** Tendon xanthomas (especially Achilles, extensor tendons of hands), xanthelasma (around eyes), corneal arcus before age 45; premature coronary artery disease (MI before age 50 in men, 60 in women).
- **Diagnosis:** Fasting lipid profile (LDL-C typically >6 mmol/L in heterozygous); clinical scoring (Simon Broome, Dutch Lipid criteria); genetic testing confirms LDLR/APOB/PCSK9 mutation.
- **Treatment:** High-intensity statins (atorvastatin, rosuvastatin); if target not met add ezetimibe or PCSK9 inhibitor (evolocumab); LDL apheresis for homozygous FH.
- **Cascade testing:** When FH is diagnosed, first-degree relatives should be tested (50% chance of inheriting). Genetic testing from one index case can identify multiple at-risk family members — cost-effective population health strategy.

### Haemochromatosis

- Autosomal recessive; HFE gene mutation (C282Y homozygous is most common); excessive iron absorption → iron deposits in liver, heart, pancreas, joints, skin, pituitary.
- **Features (usually presents in middle age, men earlier than women):** Liver disease → cirrhosis → HCC risk; diabetes mellitus ("bronze diabetes"); cardiomyopathy; arthropathy (metacarpophalangeal joints); skin bronzing; hypogonadism (pituitary iron deposition).
- **Diagnosis:** Serum ferritin raised + transferrin saturation >45%; HFE gene testing; liver biopsy or MRI for iron quantification.
- **Treatment:** Regular **venesection** (phlebotomy) — remove 500 mL blood weekly or fortnightly until ferritin normal; then maintenance venesection; dietary modification (avoid iron supplements and vitamin C with meals); screen first-degree relatives.
- **Nursing:** Explain that venesection is safe and effective; monitor Hb before each session (do not venesect if Hb <11 g/dL); observe for vasovagal reactions; encourage fluid before the procedure.

### Wilson's disease

- Autosomal recessive; ATP7B gene mutation → copper cannot be excreted into bile → accumulates in liver, brain, cornea, kidneys.
- **Features:** Liver disease (hepatitis, cirrhosis, fulminant liver failure); neurological (tremor, dysarthria, dysphagia, personality change, psychiatric symptoms); **Kayser-Fleischer (KF) rings** — golden-brown copper deposits at the periphery of the cornea (pathognomonic; seen by slit-lamp).
- **Diagnosis:** Serum caeruloplasmin (low); 24-h urinary copper (raised); liver biopsy (copper quantification); slit-lamp for KF rings.
- **Treatment:** D-penicillamine (chelates copper; side effects — renal, skin, blood); trientine (better tolerated); zinc (blocks intestinal copper absorption — maintenance); liver transplantation for fulminant or end-stage disease.
- **Age of onset:** 5–35 years.

### Huntington's disease (HD)

- Autosomal dominant; HTT gene (chromosome 4); CAG repeat expansion (>36 → disease; >60 → juvenile onset).
- **Features:** Usually onset 40s–50s; chorea (involuntary jerky movements), psychiatric symptoms (depression, irritability, psychosis appear before motor symptoms), dementia; relentlessly progressive; mean survival 15–20 years from onset.
- **Anticipation:** With paternal transmission, the CAG repeat tends to expand → earlier onset in the next generation.
- **Predictive testing:** Available for at-risk individuals before symptoms. This is ethically complex (see Unit 8); the protocol includes pre-test counselling sessions and post-result support; a negative result does not reduce lifetime population risk perception; a positive result means certain future disease.
- **Treatment:** No disease-modifying treatment; tetrabenazine (reduces chorea); antidepressants; antipsychotics; physiotherapy, speech therapy; multidisciplinary palliative approach.
- **Nursing:** Provide safe environment (fall prevention); adapt communication to stage of disease; support family caregivers; explain anticipation; connect to Huntington's Disease Association; discuss advance care planning.

### BRCA1 and BRCA2

- Autosomal dominant tumour suppressor genes; heterozygous mutation → hereditary breast and ovarian cancer (HBOC) syndrome.
- **Lifetime risk:**
  - BRCA1: breast cancer ~72%; ovarian cancer ~44%.
  - BRCA2: breast cancer ~69%; ovarian cancer ~17%; also male breast cancer, prostate, and pancreatic cancer.
- **Who to test:** Personal/family history of early-onset breast cancer (<50), bilateral breast cancer, male breast cancer, ovarian cancer, two or more close relatives with breast/ovarian cancer, Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry (higher carrier frequency).
- **Management options for BRCA carriers:** Enhanced surveillance (annual MRI + mammography from age 25–30); risk-reducing mastectomy (reduces breast cancer risk by >90%); risk-reducing salpingo-oophorectomy (after completing family — reduces ovarian risk and also breast cancer risk post-menopausally); chemoprevention (tamoxifen, raloxifene).
- **Cascade testing:** Identify other at-risk family members.
- **Nursing:** Pre-test genetic counselling; support emotionally through the decision to test and after results; explain that a positive test does not mean cancer is present; explain surveillance and management options neutrally; respect the right to know and the right not to know.

## 7.5 Comparison: common genetic conditions by age of presentation

| Age group | Condition | Presentation | Key action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Neonatal | Congenital hypothyroidism, PKU, CAH, CF | Screening — often asymptomatic at first | Heel-prick screening; early treatment |
| Infancy | Down syndrome, Pompe disease, SMA | Hypotonia, heart defect, feeding difficulty | Cardiac echo; early intervention programme |
| Childhood | Duchenne MD, cystic fibrosis, haemophilia | Gait abnormality, recurrent chest infections, prolonged bleeding | Physiotherapy; enzyme replacement; factor infusions |
| Adolescence | Marfan syndrome, FH, Huntington (family) | Tall stature + cardiac risk; tendon xanthomas; predictive testing decision | Echocardiogram; statins; non-directive counselling |
| Adults | BRCA1/2, haemochromatosis, Wilson's disease, HD | Cancer risk assessment, organ failure | Surveillance; venesection; chelation; advance planning |

## Remember

- Newborn screening: 48–72 h heel prick; treats congenital hypothyroidism, PKU, CAH, G6PD, CF, galactosaemia before symptoms.
- Collect the heel prick from the lateral heel (not the central pad — risks bone injury).
- Amniocentesis: 15–18 weeks, 0.5% miscarriage risk; CVS: 10–13 weeks, slightly higher risk.
- NIPT (cell-free DNA): non-invasive, >99% detection of trisomies 21/18/13 — a screening test, needs confirmation.
- PCPNDT Act (India): foetal sex determination for sex selection is illegal.
- Down syndrome: flat face, hypotonia, single palmar crease, congenital heart disease (AV septal defect most common); screen thyroid annually.
- CF: sweat test (Cl >60); physiotherapy + enzymes + CFTR modulators (ivacaftor) for eligible patients.
- Haemochromatosis: HFE C282Y; bronze diabetes + cirrhosis + cardiomyopathy; treat with venesection.
- Wilson's disease: Kayser-Fleischer rings (pathognomonic); low caeruloplasmin; treat with D-penicillamine or zinc.
- Huntington disease: CAG repeats; chorea + dementia + psychiatric symptoms; predictive testing requires careful pre- and post-test counselling.
- BRCA1/2: high breast and ovarian cancer risk; test and offer surveillance, risk-reducing surgery, chemoprevention.

## 5.1 DNA, RNA and protein synthesis

### DNA structure

- **DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid)** carries the genetic code.
- Double helix (Watson and Crick, 1953): two antiparallel polynucleotide strands wound around each other.
- Each strand is a chain of nucleotides: **sugar (deoxyribose) + phosphate group + nitrogenous base**.
- **Base pairing (complementary):** Adenine (A) pairs with Thymine (T) via 2 hydrogen bonds; Guanine (G) pairs with Cytosine (C) via 3 hydrogen bonds.
- Mnemonic for base pairs: **A–T (Apple Tree) and G–C (Great Couple)**.

```text
DNA double helix (simplified)
5'—A—T—G—C—A—T—G—C—3'
   | | | | | | | |
3'—T—A—C—G—T—A—C—G—5'
Strands run antiparallel (5'→3' and 3'→5')
```

### RNA types

| RNA type | Abbreviation | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Messenger RNA | mRNA | Carries the genetic message from DNA to the ribosome |
| Transfer RNA | tRNA | Brings the correct amino acid to the ribosome; recognises codons via its anticodon |
| Ribosomal RNA | rRNA | Structural and enzymatic component of ribosomes |

### Protein synthesis

- **Transcription:** DNA → mRNA in the nucleus; RNA polymerase reads the DNA template strand (3'→5') and synthesises mRNA (5'→3'); RNA uses Uracil (U) instead of Thymine.
- **Translation:** mRNA → protein at the ribosome in the cytoplasm; codons (triplets of bases on mRNA) are recognised by tRNA anticodons, each carrying a specific amino acid; sequential addition of amino acids builds the polypeptide chain.
- **Genetic code:** 3 bases = 1 codon = 1 amino acid. 64 codons (4³) code for 20 amino acids — the code is **degenerate** (multiple codons for one amino acid). Stop codons (UAA, UAG, UGA) end translation.

## 5.2 Chromosomes and the karyotype

- Humans have **46 chromosomes** in 23 pairs.
  - **22 pairs of autosomes** (non-sex chromosomes): numbered 1–22, largest to smallest.
  - **1 pair of sex chromosomes:** XX (female) or XY (male).
- **Karyotype:** The full set of chromosomes arranged in pairs by size; obtained from cultured cells (blood lymphocytes, amniotic fluid cells, chorion villus); stained with Giemsa (G-banding) to show characteristic banding patterns.
- **Homologous chromosomes:** The two chromosomes of each pair; one from the mother (maternal), one from the father (paternal).

## 5.3 Chromosome abnormalities

### Numerical abnormalities (aneuploidy)

**Cause:** Non-disjunction — failure of homologous chromosomes (or sister chromatids) to separate at meiosis I or II → gametes with 24 or 22 chromosomes → trisomy (47 chromosomes) or monosomy (45 chromosomes) in the offspring.

| Syndrome | Chromosome | Incidence | Key features |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Down syndrome | Trisomy 21 | 1 in 700 live births | Intellectual disability, flat face, upward-slanting eyes, single palmar crease, small ears, hypotonia, congenital heart defects (ASD, VSD, AVSD), hypothyroidism, early Alzheimer's; risk increases with maternal age |
| Edwards syndrome | Trisomy 18 | 1 in 5,000 | Severe intellectual disability, rocker-bottom feet, clenched fists with overlapping fingers, micrognathia, cardiac defects; most die within the first year |
| Patau syndrome | Trisomy 13 | 1 in 10,000 | Severe intellectual disability, holoprosencephaly, cleft lip and palate, polydactyly, cardiac defects; most die within weeks |
| Turner syndrome | 45,X (monosomy X) | 1 in 2,500 females | Short stature, webbed neck, wide-carrying angle (cubitus valgus), primary amenorrhoea, streak ovaries (infertility), coarctation of aorta; intelligence normal |
| Klinefelter syndrome | 47,XXY | 1 in 500–1,000 males | Tall, small firm testes, azoospermia (infertility), gynaecomastia, mild intellectual disability; testosterone replacement needed |

**Mnemonic for trisomy numbers:** "Down (21), Edwards (18), Patau (13)" — decreasing number, decreasing viability.

### Polyploidy

- Triploid (3n = 69 chromosomes) — usually from fertilisation by two sperms; almost always lethal; partial hydatidiform mole.

### Structural abnormalities

Caused by chromosome breakage, with incorrect repair:

| Type | Description | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deletion | Loss of a chromosome segment | Cri-du-chat syndrome (5p deletion) — high-pitched cat-like cry, intellectual disability |
| Duplication | Extra copy of a segment | |
| Inversion | Segment reversed within the chromosome | Pericentric (includes centromere) or paracentric |
| Translocation | Segment moves to another chromosome | Robertsonian translocation (between 2 acrocentric chromosomes, e.g. 14 and 21): carrier has 45 chromosomes but balanced; offspring risk of trisomy 21 (Down syndrome) = 1 in 3 for maternal carrier |
| Ring chromosome | Ends of a chromosome join | |
| Isochromosome | Duplication of one arm, deletion of the other | i(Xq) in some Turner syndrome; i(17q) in medulloblastoma |

## 5.4 Mendelian patterns of inheritance

For single-gene (Mendelian) disorders, the pattern of inheritance depends on whether the gene is on an autosome or the X chromosome, and whether the mutant allele is dominant or recessive.

### Autosomal dominant (AD)

- One abnormal allele is sufficient to cause disease (the mutant allele overrides the normal allele).
- **Pedigree features:** Vertical transmission (every generation affected); each affected parent has a 50% chance of passing the disease to each child; males and females equally affected; a negative family history is possible if the mutation arose de novo.

| Disease | Gene / mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| Huntington's disease | HTT gene; CAG trinucleotide repeat expansion; chromosome 4; neurodegeneration in adulthood |
| Neurofibromatosis type 1 | NF1 gene; café-au-lait spots, cutaneous neurofibromas, Lisch nodules (iris) |
| Marfan syndrome | FBN1 gene (fibrillin); tall stature, arachnodactyly, lens dislocation, aortic aneurysm |
| Familial hypercholesterolaemia | LDL receptor mutation; early atherosclerosis |
| Achondroplasia | FGFR3 mutation; disproportionate short stature; most cases are de novo |

### Autosomal recessive (AR)

- Two abnormal alleles are required to cause disease.
- **Pedigree features:** Horizontal transmission (usually one generation affected; parents appear normal but are carriers); each affected child has a 25% chance of disease; each unaffected sibling has a 2/3 chance of being a carrier; consanguinity increases risk; both parents must be carriers.

| Disease | Mutation / mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| Cystic fibrosis (CF) | CFTR gene; most common serious AR disease in Europeans; thick mucus, lung infections, pancreatic insufficiency |
| Sickle cell disease | HBB gene (Glu→Val at position 6); sickle-shaped RBCs; vaso-occlusion, haemolytic anaemia; common in Africa and India |
| Phenylketonuria (PKU) | PAH gene; phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency; toxic phenylalanine accumulates → intellectual disability; detected by newborn screening |
| Thalassaemia | Alpha or beta globin chain mutations; common in India, the Mediterranean, Southeast Asia |
| Alkaptonuria | Homogentisate 1,2-dioxygenase deficiency; urine darkens on standing |
| Albinism | Tyrosinase deficiency; absence of melanin |

### X-linked recessive (XLR)

- Mutation on the X chromosome; recessive — females are usually carriers (one normal X); males are affected (only one X, so the mutation is expressed).
- **Pedigree features:** Males predominantly affected; females are carriers; no father-to-son transmission (sons receive Y from father); carrier mother has 50% affected sons and 50% carrier daughters; affected father → all daughters are carriers, all sons are unaffected.

| Disease | Gene / features |
| --- | --- |
| Haemophilia A | Factor VIII deficiency; joint and muscle bleeding; treat with recombinant Factor VIII |
| Haemophilia B (Christmas disease) | Factor IX deficiency; similar to A; treat with Factor IX |
| Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) | Dystrophin gene (largest human gene); progressive muscle weakness, Gowers' sign (using hands to walk up the legs from the floor), pseudohypertrophy of calves, wheelchair bound by teens, cardiomyopathy |
| G6PD deficiency | Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase; haemolytic anaemia triggered by oxidant stress (fava beans, primaquine, dapsone); very common in India and Africa |
| Red-green colour blindness | OPN1LW/OPN1MW; most common X-linked condition |

### X-linked dominant (XLD)

- Females are more often affected (some have milder disease due to X-inactivation).
- Affected father passes the condition to all daughters but no sons.
- Example: Vitamin D-resistant rickets (hypophosphataemia), Rett syndrome, Fragile X syndrome (technically XLD with anticipation).

### Mitochondrial inheritance

- Mitochondria have their own circular DNA (16,569 base pairs; 37 genes).
- **All children of an affected mother inherit the condition** (since mitochondria come only from the egg, not the sperm).
- Affected fathers do not pass the condition to any children.
- **Heteroplasmy:** Variable mixture of normal and mutant mitochondrial DNA → variable clinical expression.
- Examples: MELAS (Mitochondrial Encephalomyopathy, Lactic Acidosis, Stroke-like episodes), MERRF (Myoclonic Epilepsy with Ragged Red Fibres), Leber hereditary optic neuropathy (LHON).

## 5.5 Comparison of inheritance patterns

| Feature | Autosomal dominant | Autosomal recessive | X-linked recessive | Mitochondrial |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Generations | Every generation (vertical) | Usually one (horizontal) | Skips generations | Every generation from mother |
| Sex affected | M and F equally | M and F equally | Males predominantly | M and F (from mother) |
| Parents usually | One affected | Both carriers | Mother carrier | Mother affected |
| Recurrence risk (each child) | 50% | 25% | 50% of sons affected | All children of affected mother |
| Consanguinity increase risk? | No | Yes | No | No |

## Remember

- DNA base pairs: A with T, G with C. RNA uses U instead of T.
- 46 chromosomes = 22 pairs of autosomes + 1 pair of sex chromosomes (XX or XY).
- Non-disjunction → aneuploidy. Down = trisomy 21; Edwards = trisomy 18; Patau = trisomy 13; Turner = 45,X; Klinefelter = 47,XXY.
- Robertsonian translocation (14;21) → familial Down syndrome carrier (45 chromosomes, balanced).
- Cri-du-chat = 5p deletion.
- Autosomal dominant: 50% risk per child, one parent affected, vertical pattern.
- Autosomal recessive: 25% affected, 50% carrier; both parents carriers; consanguinity increases risk.
- X-linked recessive: males affected; mothers are carriers; no father-to-son transmission.
- Mitochondrial: all children of an affected mother are affected; father does not transmit.

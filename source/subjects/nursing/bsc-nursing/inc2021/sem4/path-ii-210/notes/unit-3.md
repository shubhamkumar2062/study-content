# Unit 3: Special pathology — breast and central nervous system

This unit covers pathology of the breast (benign and malignant) and the central nervous system (oedema, infections, and tumours). Learn each condition as cause → morphology → features → management → nurse's role.

---

## PART A — BREAST

## 3.1 Anatomy revision

The breast is composed of 15–20 lobes of glandular tissue, ducts, and fatty stroma, all supported by Cooper's ligaments (which attach to the overlying skin). The upper outer quadrant contains the most tissue and is where most breast cancers arise. The main draining lymph nodes are axillary (most important — levels I, II, III).

## 3.2 Fibrocystic change

- Most common benign breast condition in women of reproductive age (25–50 years).
- Not a single disease — an umbrella term for several hormonally driven changes:
  - **Cysts:** fluid-filled cavities; fluctuate with the menstrual cycle.
  - **Fibrosis:** replacement of stroma by dense fibrous tissue.
  - **Adenosis:** increase in the number and size of acini (lobular units).
  - **Epithelial hyperplasia:** increase in ductal or lobular epithelial cells; hyperplasia without atypia — slight increase in cancer risk; atypical hyperplasia — 4–5× increased risk.
- **Features:** Bilateral, often upper outer quadrant, painful, tender nodularity that worsens before menstruation; may discharge from nipple.
- **Nursing:** Reassurance that most fibrocystic change is benign; well-fitting bra; evening primrose oil helps some; any new discrete lump or bloody nipple discharge needs further investigation.

## 3.3 Fibroadenoma

- Most common benign breast **tumour** in young women (15–35 years).
- Benign proliferation of both ductal and stromal elements.
- **Features:** Smooth, well-defined, rubbery, **mobile** ("breast mouse") lump; non-tender; size 1–3 cm; does not change with the cycle (unlike fibrocystic change, which fluctuates).
- **Management:** Ultrasound + fine-needle aspiration (triple assessment: clinical + imaging + cytology); if benign, can be observed or excised if large or symptomatic.
- **Nursing:** Reassurance that it is not cancer; explain the triple assessment; teach that any change in size warrants re-review.

## 3.4 Breast carcinoma

The most common cancer in women worldwide and the leading cause of cancer death in women in India.

### Risk factors

| Factor | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| BRCA1 / BRCA2 mutations | Lifetime risk 50–80%; also high ovarian cancer risk |
| Family history | First-degree relative doubles risk |
| Early menarche, late menopause | Prolonged oestrogen exposure |
| Nulliparity / late first pregnancy | No protective pregnancy-related changes |
| Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) | Small increased risk with combined HRT |
| Prior radiation to chest | (e.g. for Hodgkin lymphoma) |
| Obesity (post-menopausal) | Peripheral oestrogen from fat |
| Alcohol | Dose-related risk |

### Pre-invasive lesions

- **Ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS):** Malignant cells fill the ducts; basement membrane intact; not yet invasive. Usually detected on mammography (microcalcifications). Treated by wide local excision ± radiotherapy.
- **Lobular carcinoma in situ (LCIS):** Malignant cells fill lobules; often bilateral; marker of increased risk; not treated by excision but managed by surveillance or risk-reducing surgery.

### Invasive types

| Type | Features |
| --- | --- |
| Invasive ductal carcinoma (IDC, NOS) | Most common (75%); irregular hard mass; may tether skin or cause nipple retraction |
| Invasive lobular carcinoma | 10%; bilateral in 10–20%; "Indian file" cell pattern on microscopy; diffuse growth, may be missed on imaging |
| Medullary carcinoma | Softer, well-circumscribed; lymphocytic infiltrate; paradoxically better prognosis |
| Inflammatory carcinoma | Dermal lymphatic invasion → red, oedematous, warm skin ("peau d'orange"); aggressive; Stage IIIB |

### Staging (TNM)

- T: tumour size; N: nodal status (axillary nodes — most important prognostic factor); M: distant metastases.
- **Stage I–II:** Localised, potentially curable by surgery ± RT.
- **Stage III:** Locally advanced (skin, chest wall, matted nodes).
- **Stage IV:** Distant metastases — bone (most common), liver, lung, brain; not curable but treatable.

### Molecular subtypes and receptors

| Receptor | Significance |
| --- | --- |
| ER (oestrogen receptor) positive | Hormone therapy effective (tamoxifen for pre-menopausal, aromatase inhibitor for post-menopausal) |
| PR (progesterone receptor) positive | Also responds to hormone therapy |
| HER2 (human epidermal growth factor receptor 2) over-expressed | Trastuzumab (Herceptin) is effective; more aggressive without treatment |
| Triple negative (ER−/PR−/HER2−) | No targeted therapy available; chemotherapy only; aggressive |

### Treatment

- **Surgery:** Wide local excision (lumpectomy) + radiotherapy = mastectomy in terms of survival; modified radical mastectomy (removes breast + axillary nodes); sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB — avoids full axillary clearance if no nodal metastasis).
- **Radiotherapy:** After breast-conserving surgery or for locally advanced disease.
- **Chemotherapy:** Pre-operative (neoadjuvant — shrinks tumour) or post-operative (adjuvant); regimens include anthracyclines (doxorubicin) + cyclophosphamide, taxanes.
- **Hormone therapy:** Tamoxifen (selective oestrogen receptor modulator) for pre-menopausal ER+ women; aromatase inhibitors (letrozole, anastrozole) for post-menopausal ER+ women.
- **Targeted therapy:** Trastuzumab (Herceptin) for HER2+ disease.

### Nurse's role

- **Breast self-examination (BSE) teaching:** Monthly self-examination after menstruation; look (in front of a mirror — changes in shape, dimpling, nipple change) and feel (all four quadrants + axilla, in a systematic pattern). Emphasise that BSE detects changes; clinical breast examination and mammography are needed for diagnosis.
- **Screening:** Annual mammography for women ≥40 years (or earlier with family history/BRCA).
- **Post-mastectomy care:** Drain management and monitoring; arm exercises to prevent lymphoedema (start gently on day 1); no BP cuff or venepuncture on the ipsilateral arm; prevent injury, burns, infections on that arm; teach hand and arm hygiene.
- **Lymphoedema management:** Elevation, compression sleeves, gentle massage, weight management.
- **Psychological support:** Body image issues after mastectomy; prosthesis fitting and information on breast reconstruction; support groups; partner communication.
- **Chemotherapy side effects:** Nausea (antiemetics), alopecia (hair will regrow), neutropenia (infection precautions), mucositis (oral care).

## PART B — CENTRAL NERVOUS SYSTEM

## 3.5 Cerebral oedema and raised intracranial pressure (ICP)

### Types of cerebral oedema

| Type | Mechanism | Causes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vasogenic | Blood-brain barrier breakdown → plasma leaks into extracellular space | Tumours, abscess, contusion, haemorrhage |
| Cytotoxic | Cellular swelling (Na/K pump failure) | Ischaemia, hypoxia |
| Interstitial | CSF leaks out through ependyma | Obstructive hydrocephalus |

### Raised intracranial pressure (ICP)

- Normal ICP: 7–15 mmHg.
- **Causes:** Brain tumour, cerebral oedema, hydrocephalus, subdural or extradural haematoma, cerebral venous sinus thrombosis.
- **Effects:** Headache (worse in the morning and on straining), vomiting (projectile, without nausea), papilloedema (blurring of optic disc margin on fundoscopy — a critical sign), altered consciousness.
- **Herniation:** Uncal herniation (transtentorial) → compression of CN III (dilated, fixed pupil ipsilateral to the lesion) → "blown pupil" → compression of brainstem → death. **A unilateral dilated fixed pupil is a neurosurgical emergency.**
- **Cushing's triad (late sign):** Hypertension + bradycardia + irregular respirations.
- **Management:** Head elevation 30°; mannitol (osmotic diuretic); dexamethasone (for tumour-related oedema); hyperventilation (↓ CO₂ → cerebral vasoconstriction); surgical decompression.
- **Nursing:** Neurological observations hourly (GCS, pupils, limb movements, vital signs); head elevated 30°; keep patient calm (stimulation raises ICP); prevent straining (stool softeners); maintain airway; report any sudden deterioration immediately.

## 3.6 Meningitis

Inflammation of the leptomeninges (pia and arachnoid), usually from infection.

| Type | Common cause | Key features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bacterial | Neisseria meningitidis (meningococcal), Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae (children), Listeria (neonates and elderly) | Sudden high fever, severe headache, neck stiffness (meningism), photophobia; petechial or purpuric rash (meningococcal — non-blanching = emergency); CSF — turbid, high protein, very low glucose, neutrophils |
| Viral (aseptic) | Enteroviruses (most common), herpes simplex, mumps | Milder; CSF — clear, slightly raised protein, normal or mildly low glucose, lymphocytes; self-limiting in most |
| Tuberculous (TB) | Mycobacterium tuberculosis | Gradual onset; basal meningitis (cranial nerve palsies — CN VI, CN III); CSF — clear/xanthochromic, very high protein, very low glucose, lymphocytes, AFB; fibrinous exudate at base; treat with 4-drug anti-TB regimen |

**Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome:** Overwhelming meningococcal septicaemia → bilateral adrenal haemorrhage → adrenal crisis. Fulminant course with purpuric rash, DIC, hypotension; fatal without immediate treatment.

**Nursing care in meningitis:**
- Isolate bacterial meningitis (droplet precautions until 24 hours of antibiotics).
- Dim lighting, quiet environment (photophobia and phonophobia).
- IV antibiotics (ceftriaxone) as an emergency — time to antibiotics is critical.
- IV dexamethasone to reduce inflammation and hearing loss.
- Monitor for complications: seizures (padded cot sides, suction ready), raised ICP, hydrocephalus, hearing loss.
- Observe petechiae progress; non-blanching rash spreading rapidly is life-threatening.
- Prophylaxis: rifampicin for household contacts of meningococcal meningitis.

## 3.7 Encephalitis

- Inflammation of the brain parenchyma itself.
- **Herpes simplex encephalitis (HSE):** Most common sporadic viral encephalitis in adults; HSV-1 reactivation; involves the **temporal lobes** (and frontal lobes) → fever, headache, personality change, memory loss, aphasia, seizures; MRI shows temporal lobe signal change; treat promptly with **IV aciclovir** (delays = higher mortality/disability).
- **Japanese encephalitis:** Most common viral encephalitis in India; flavivirus transmitted by Culex mosquito; children most affected; vaccine available.
- **Nursing:** Seizure precautions; antiviral as ordered; supportive care; screen contacts for mosquito-borne encephalitis (Japanese encephalitis epidemic management).

## 3.8 Brain abscess

- Collection of pus within the brain parenchyma; surrounded by a capsule.
- **Sources:** Direct spread (mastoiditis → temporal lobe; sinusitis → frontal lobe), haematogenous (lung abscess, cyanotic heart disease, dental extraction — via right-to-left shunt), penetrating head injury.
- **Features:** Raised ICP (headache, vomiting), focal neurological deficits (depending on location), fever.
- **Diagnosis:** CT/MRI — ring-enhancing lesion with central hypodensity (pus), surrounding oedema.
- **Treatment:** Surgical drainage + prolonged IV antibiotics; treat the primary source.

## 3.9 Central nervous system tumours

### Classification

| Category | Examples | Age | Features |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gliomas (from glial cells) | Astrocytoma (grades I–IV), oligodendroglioma, ependymoma | Adults mostly; medulloblastoma in children | Infiltrate brain; most are malignant; WHO grade IV = glioblastoma |
| Meningioma | Meningioma | Middle-aged adults; women > men | Extra-axial (outside brain); compresses brain; benign (grade I most); surgically curable |
| Nerve sheath | Acoustic neuroma (schwannoma of CN VIII) | Adults | Unilateral hearing loss, tinnitus, vertigo; at cerebellopontine angle |
| Embryonal | Medulloblastoma | Children (most common malignant childhood brain tumour) | Posterior fossa (cerebellum); presents with raised ICP, ataxia; radiosensitive |
| Metastatic | From lung, breast, melanoma, colon, kidney | Adults | Most common intracranial tumours overall; often multiple; at grey-white junction |

### Glioblastoma (grade IV astrocytoma)

- Most aggressive primary brain tumour; mean survival under 15 months despite treatment.
- **Morphology:** Marked cellular atypia, necrosis, pseudopalisading cells around necrosis, endothelial proliferation.
- **Features:** Rapidly progressive headache, raised ICP, focal deficits, seizures.
- **Treatment:** Maximum safe surgical resection + radiotherapy + temozolomide.

### Common features of CNS tumours (all types)

1. **Raised ICP:** Headache (worst in the morning), projectile vomiting, papilloedema.
2. **Focal deficits:** Depend on location — frontal (personality change, hemiparesis), temporal (memory, aphasia), parietal (sensory, spatial neglect), occipital (visual), cerebellum (ataxia, nystagmus), spinal cord (paraplegia, sensory level).
3. **Seizures:** Partial or generalised; first adult-onset seizure needs brain imaging.

**Nursing care for CNS tumours:**
- Neurological observations (GCS, pupils, power in all limbs).
- Dexamethasone as ordered for perioeudema; proton pump inhibitor (gastric protection from steroids).
- Seizure precautions (padded cot sides, suction, oxygen).
- Pre-operative craniotomy care: consent, hair removal, IV access, bowel preparation.
- Post-operative craniotomy care: hourly GCS and pupils; head-of-bed elevation 30°; watch for CSF leak (clear fluid from nose or ear — do NOT block it, inform doctor); wound care; physiotherapy for deficits.
- Palliative care planning for high-grade gliomas; communicate honestly with the family; pain and symptom management; emotional and spiritual support.

## Remember

- Fibroadenoma = most common benign breast tumour; young women; mobile; not cancer.
- Fibrocystic change = most common benign breast condition; reproductive age; cyclic pain; benign unless atypical hyperplasia.
- Breast carcinoma: BRCA1/2 mutation + other oestrogen-related risk factors; ER/PR status guides hormone therapy; HER2 status guides trastuzumab.
- Mastectomy nursing: no BP or venepuncture ipsilateral arm; arm exercises for lymphoedema; BSE teaching.
- Raised ICP = headache + vomiting + papilloedema; unilateral fixed dilated pupil = herniation emergency.
- Meningococcal meningitis: non-blanching petechial rash + fever + neck stiffness = life-threatening emergency; IV ceftriaxone immediately.
- TB meningitis: basal meningitis, CSF has very low glucose and high protein, lymphocytes; 4-drug anti-TB.
- HSV encephalitis: temporal lobe; treat with IV aciclovir urgently.
- Glioblastoma = most malignant primary brain tumour; medulloblastoma = most common malignant paediatric brain tumour.
- Metastases = most common intracranial tumours overall; from lung and breast most often.

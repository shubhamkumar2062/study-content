# Unit 5: Emergency drugs, deaddiction, IV fluids, poisoning management, vitamins and minerals

This unit covers drugs used in emergencies, management of poisoning and overdose, and essential nutritional agents. Know mechanism, dose (where given), and the nurse's specific role for each group.

---

## 5.1 Emergency drugs

### Adrenaline (Epinephrine)

**Mechanism:** Stimulates both alpha (α) and beta (β) adrenoceptors — α1 constricts blood vessels (raises BP), β1 increases heart rate and force, β2 dilates bronchi.

**Uses and doses:**
- **Anaphylaxis** — 0.5 mg (500 mcg) IM into the outer thigh; repeat after 5 minutes if no improvement; self-injectable auto-injector (EpiPen) for community use
- **Cardiac arrest** — 1 mg IV every 3–5 minutes during CPR (in asystole and pulseless electrical activity; every other loop in VF/pVT after first shock)
- Added to local anaesthetics to slow absorption and prolong effect, and to reduce bleeding

**Adverse effects:** Tachycardia, hypertension, anxiety, tremor, pallor, headache.

**Nurse's responsibilities:** Inject IM into the outer mid-thigh (through clothing if necessary), not IV except in cardiac arrest under supervision; note time of injection and repeat if needed; monitor pulse, blood pressure and respiratory rate; position patient supine with legs raised (unless respiratory distress); prepare IV access; antihistamines and corticosteroids are secondary — adrenaline is always first.

---

### Atropine

**Mechanism:** Blocks muscarinic acetylcholine receptors → increases heart rate, reduces secretions, dilates pupils, reduces bowel and bladder activity.

**Uses:**
- **Bradycardia** — 0.5 mg IV, repeat to max 3 mg
- **Organophosphate poisoning** — large doses IV to block excessive cholinergic effects (bradycardia, bronchospasm, secretions); often needs 2–4 mg repeated every 5–10 minutes until secretions dry
- **Pre-medication before anaesthesia** — reduce saliva, bronchial secretions and vagal reflex
- Antidote to beta-blocker overdose (less often)

**Adverse effects:** Tachycardia, dry mouth, urinary retention, constipation, blurred vision, confusion in elderly.

---

### Dopamine and Dobutamine

| Drug | Receptor | Effect | Main use | Nurse's role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dopamine (low dose <5 mcg/kg/min) | Dopaminergic | Renal and mesenteric vasodilation | Shock with oliguria | IV infusion pump; titrate to urine output and BP |
| Dopamine (medium 5–10 mcg/kg/min) | β1 | Inotropic (increases cardiac output) | Cardiogenic shock | Continuous cardiac monitoring |
| Dopamine (high >10 mcg/kg/min) | α1 | Vasoconstriction | Septic shock unresponsive to fluids | Monitor for ischaemia; extravasation causes necrosis — central line preferred |
| Dobutamine | β1 mainly | Increases cardiac output | Acute heart failure, cardiogenic shock | Titrate to cardiac output/BP; monitor heart rate |

---

### Noradrenaline (Norepinephrine)

Potent α1 agonist → vasoconstriction → raises mean arterial pressure. Used as a vasopressor in **septic shock** after adequate fluid resuscitation. Must be given via central venous catheter (extravasation causes tissue necrosis). Continuous arterial blood pressure monitoring required.

---

### Adenosine

**Use:** Conversion of **supraventricular tachycardia (SVT)** to sinus rhythm.
**Dose:** 6 mg IV rapid bolus (over 1–2 seconds) followed immediately by a 20 mL saline flush; repeat 12 mg twice if no effect.
**Mechanism:** Transiently blocks AV node conduction.
**Adverse effects:** Facial flushing, chest tightness, dyspnoea, brief asystole (< 10 seconds) — warn the patient, continuous ECG monitoring essential.
**Nurse's responsibilities:** 12-lead ECG before; administer into a large antecubital vein with a fast flush; have crash trolley available; document rhythm change.

---

### Amiodarone

**Use:** Treatment and prevention of ventricular tachycardia (VT) and ventricular fibrillation (VF), broad-spectrum antiarrhythmic (class III).

**Adverse effects (long-term):** Pulmonary toxicity (fibrosis — most dangerous), thyroid dysfunction (both hypothyroid and hyperthyroid), liver toxicity, corneal micro-deposits (reversible), peripheral neuropathy, photosensitivity (blue-grey skin discolouration).

**Monitoring:** Annual chest X-ray, thyroid function tests (TFTs), liver function tests (LFTs), lung function tests if symptomatic.

**Nurse's responsibilities:** Advise sunscreen and protective clothing; warn about long half-life (many months); many drug interactions (increases warfarin effect, digoxin toxicity); check TFTs and LFTs at follow-up.

---

### Digoxin

**Uses:** Rate control in **atrial fibrillation (AF)**; also used in heart failure (now less commonly).

**Mechanism:** Inhibits Na+/K+-ATPase pump → increases intracellular calcium → positive inotropy. Also stimulates vagus → slows AV node conduction (rate control in AF).

**Narrow therapeutic index** — therapeutic range 0.8–2 ng/mL; toxic effects common.

**Digoxin toxicity signs:** Nausea and vomiting (early), visual disturbances (yellow-green haloes, blurred vision), bradycardia, any arrhythmia.

**Factors increasing toxicity risk:** Hypokalaemia (most important — potassium depletion sensitises the heart to digoxin), hypomagnesaemia, hypercalcaemia, renal failure, hypothyroidism, amiodarone and verapamil interactions.

**Antidote:** Digoxin-specific antibody fragments (Digibind / DigiFab) — for life-threatening toxicity.

**Nurse's responsibilities:** Check apical pulse for 1 minute before giving — hold if <60 bpm (or as ordered); monitor ECG, electrolytes especially potassium; recognise early toxicity signs; ensure patient receives prescribed potassium supplements.

---

### Calcium gluconate

**Uses:**
- **Hypocalcaemia** (tetany, carpopedal spasm, post-thyroidectomy)
- **Hypermagnesaemia** (antidote to magnesium sulfate toxicity)
- **Hyperkalaemia** — does not lower potassium but stabilises the cardiac membrane (cardiac membrane stabilisation)
- **Calcium channel blocker overdose**

**Dose:** 10 mL of 10% calcium gluconate IV slowly over 10 minutes with cardiac monitoring.

**Nurse's responsibilities:** IV extravasation causes tissue necrosis — ensure good IV access; monitor ECG; have at bedside whenever MgSO4 is running.

---

### Sodium bicarbonate

**Uses:**
- **Metabolic acidosis** — severe (pH < 7.1 with bicarbonate depletion); also used in cardiac arrest with prolonged resuscitation
- **Tricyclic antidepressant (TCA) overdose** — alkalinises the urine, reverses sodium channel block in the myocardium; IV sodium bicarbonate is the specific treatment for TCA-induced arrhythmias
- **Salicylate poisoning** (urine alkalinisation enhances renal excretion)

**Adverse effects:** Hypokalaemia, metabolic alkalosis, hypernatraemia, CO2 generation.

## 5.2 Deaddiction

### Alcohol dependence

**Acute withdrawal:** Seizures, delirium tremens (DTs) — agitation, confusion, hallucinations, autonomic instability. Prophylaxis and treatment: **chlordiazepoxide** (long-acting benzodiazepine) in a symptom-triggered reducing regime (CIWA-Ar scale).

**Wernicke's encephalopathy** (confusion, ophthalmoplegia, ataxia) from thiamine deficiency — give **thiamine IV (Pabrinex)** before any glucose infusion.

| Drug | Use in alcohol dependence |
| --- | --- |
| Chlordiazepoxide / diazepam | Acute detox (CIWA protocol) |
| Thiamine (B1) | Prevent/treat Wernicke's — IV first, then oral |
| Disulfiram (Antabuse) | Deterrent — inhibits aldehyde dehydrogenase; alcohol intake causes flushing, vomiting, palpitations; requires patient motivation and informed consent |
| Naltrexone | Reduces craving; opioid antagonist; monitor liver enzymes |
| Acamprosate | Reduces protracted withdrawal symptoms and craving; renally excreted |

---

### Opioid dependence

| Drug | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Methadone (oral, supervised) | Opioid substitution/maintenance therapy — reduces illicit drug use, stabilises life; long half-life — once-daily |
| Buprenorphine/naloxone (Suboxone) | Partial opioid agonist + naloxone (deters injection); sublingual; safer than methadone in overdose |
| Naltrexone | Relapse prevention after detox — blocks all opioid effect; patient must be opioid-free for 7–10 days first |
| Naloxone (Narcan) | Emergency reversal of opioid overdose — IV/IM/intranasal; short-acting (may need repeat doses) |

---

### Tobacco cessation

| Approach | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Nicotine replacement therapy (NRT) | Patches, gum, lozenges, inhalers — reduces withdrawal; available OTC |
| Bupropion (Zyban) | Antidepressant; reduces craving and withdrawal; CI: seizure history |
| Varenicline (Champix) | Partial nicotinic receptor agonist; most effective pharmacotherapy; monitor for mood changes and suicidal ideation |

## 5.3 Intravenous fluids

### Crystalloids

| Fluid | Main components | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.9% Sodium chloride (normal saline) | Na+ 154 mmol/L, Cl− 154 mmol/L | Fluid resuscitation, hypotension, drug dilution; watch for hyperchloraemic acidosis |
| Ringer's lactate (Hartmann's solution) | Na+ 130, K+ 4, Ca2+ 2.7, Cl− 109, lactate 28 mmol/L | Balanced crystalloid; more physiological; preferred in surgery and trauma |
| 5% Dextrose (glucose) | Glucose 50 g/L (278 mmol/L) | Hypoglycaemia, maintenance fluid, drug dilution; does NOT provide electrolytes |
| 5% Dextrose in 0.45% NaCl | Mixed | Maintenance fluid in children and postoperative care |
| 10% Dextrose | High glucose | Severe hypoglycaemia, neonates, TPN adjunct |

### Colloids

Higher molecular weight particles that stay in the intravascular space longer than crystalloids.

| Colloid | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Albumin 4.5% / 20% | Natural; used in hypoalbuminaemia, cirrhosis, plasma exchange |
| Gelatin solutions (Haemaccel, Gelofusine) | Semi-synthetic; used in fluid resuscitation |
| Hydroxyethyl starches (HES) | Largely abandoned in critical care due to renal failure risk |

**When to prefer colloids:** Severe hypoalbuminaemia, when large volumes of crystalloid would cause pulmonary oedema.

**Monitoring IV fluids:** Respiratory rate and oxygen saturation (oedema), urine output, weight, oedema, electrolytes, JVP/CVP.

## 5.4 Poisoning management

### General approach (ABCDE first, then specific)

1. **Airway** — protect, suction, intubate if unconscious
2. **Breathing** — oxygen
3. **Circulation** — IV access, fluids, monitor ECG
4. **Disability** — GCS, blood sugar, temperature
5. **Exposure** — look for tablets, injection marks, smell; take history from witnesses

**Gastric decontamination:**
- **Activated charcoal** (50 g oral or via NG tube) — given within 1 hour if patient is conscious and airway is protected, and the drug adsorbs to charcoal (most organic drugs); NOT for corrosives, alcohols, metals, lithium
- **Gastric lavage** — now rarely used; only within 1 hour, with a protected airway, for life-threatening ingestion; CI: corrosives, unprotected airway

**Enhanced elimination:**
- **Urinary alkalinisation** (sodium bicarbonate IV) — salicylate, methotrexate, phenobarbitone
- **Haemodialysis** — lithium, salicylates, ethylene glycol, methanol
- **Haemoperfusion** — theophylline, some sedatives

---

### Specific antidotes

| Poison | Antidote | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paracetamol (acetaminophen) | N-acetylcysteine (NAC) IV | Use Rumack–Matthew nomogram to decide treatment; most effective within 8 hours; treat up to 24 h if in doubt |
| Opioids (heroin, morphine, codeine) | Naloxone 0.4–2 mg IV/IM/intranasal | Short-acting (60–90 min) — repeat doses or infusion needed; watch for re-narcotisation |
| Benzodiazepines | Flumazenil 0.2 mg IV, repeat to max 1 mg | Use with caution — may precipitate seizures in dependent patients or TCA co-ingestion; short-acting |
| Organophosphates (pesticides, nerve agents) | Atropine (large doses) + Pralidoxime (2-PAM) | Atropine reverses muscarinic effects; pralidoxime reactivates acetylcholinesterase before ageing; use early |
| Iron | Desferrioxamine (deferoxamine) IM/IV | Chelates free iron; urine turns pink (vin rosé) with treatment |
| Tricyclic antidepressants (TCA) | Sodium bicarbonate IV + supportive | Bicarbonate reverses Na+ channel block; treat arrhythmias; avoid flumazenil, physostigmine |
| Cyanide | Hydroxocobalamin IV (or sodium nitrite + sodium thiosulfate) | Hydroxocobalamin is the preferred agent; binds cyanide to form cyanocobalamin |
| Digoxin | Digoxin-specific Fab fragments (Digibind) | Binds and inactivates digoxin; for life-threatening toxicity |
| Warfarin overdose | Vitamin K1 (phytomenadione) + fresh frozen plasma | Vitamin K reverses slowly over hours; FFP gives immediate clotting factors |
| Methanol / ethylene glycol | Fomepizole (or ethanol) + haemodialysis | Inhibits alcohol dehydrogenase; prevents toxic metabolite formation |
| Beta-blockers | Glucagon + atropine + IV calcium | Glucagon bypasses beta-receptor |
| Paracetamol (paeds) | NAC oral or IV | Same nomogram principle |

## 5.5 Vitamins

### Fat-soluble (A, D, E, K) — stored in body; toxicity possible in excess

| Vitamin | Deficiency disease | Toxicity (excess) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A (retinol) | Night blindness → xerophthalmia → corneal ulceration → blindness; Bitot's spots | Hypervitaminosis A — liver damage, intracranial hypertension, teratogenic |
| D (cholecalciferol) | Rickets (children — bowed legs, soft skull), osteomalacia (adults — bone pain) | Hypercalcaemia, nephrocalcinosis, metastatic calcification |
| E (tocopherol) | Haemolytic anaemia (premature infants), peripheral neuropathy (rare in adults) | Interferes with vitamin K — increased bleeding tendency |
| K (phylloquinone/menaquinone) | Haemorrhagic disease of the newborn; bleeding; raised PT | Haemolytic anaemia in G6PD deficiency (menadione form) |

---

### Water-soluble (B group, C) — not stored; daily intake needed; toxicity rare except B6

| Vitamin | Deficiency disease | Key notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B1 (Thiamine) | Wet beriberi (high-output cardiac failure), dry beriberi (peripheral neuropathy), Wernicke's encephalopathy, Korsakoff's syndrome | Give IV thiamine before glucose in suspected deficiency |
| B2 (Riboflavin) | Angular stomatitis, glossitis, seborrhoeic dermatitis, photophobia | |
| B3 (Niacin) | Pellagra — the "4 Ds": Dermatitis, Diarrhoea, Dementia, Death | Niacin (high dose) used to raise HDL cholesterol; flushing is a side effect |
| B6 (Pyridoxine) | Peripheral neuropathy, sideroblastic anaemia, seborrhoeic dermatitis | Toxicity: sensory neuropathy with mega-doses; prophylactic with isoniazid (INH) which antagonises B6 |
| B12 (Cyanocobalamin) | Megaloblastic anaemia, subacute combined degeneration of the spinal cord (SACD — posterior and lateral columns), glossitis | Requires intrinsic factor for absorption (absent in pernicious anaemia — give IM injections) |
| Folic acid (B9) | Megaloblastic anaemia; neural tube defects (NTD) in the foetus | Supplement pre-conception and in first 12 weeks; folate deficiency in pregnancy — highest clinical importance |
| C (Ascorbic acid) | Scurvy — bleeding gums, perifollicular haemorrhages, poor wound healing, corkscrew hairs | Enhances iron absorption; antioxidant |

## 5.6 Minerals

### Iron

**Deficiency:** Iron-deficiency anaemia (IDA) — the commonest nutritional deficiency in India.

**Oral iron:** Ferrous sulfate, ferrous fumarate. Take on empty stomach with vitamin C. Side effects: nausea, constipation, dark stool. Warn patients — dark stool is harmless.

**IV iron:** Iron sucrose, ferric carboxymaltose — used when oral iron is not tolerated, in inflammatory bowel disease, in dialysis patients, in second/third trimester when rapid correction needed. Risk of anaphylaxis — test dose (some formulations), monitoring for 30 minutes, resuscitation facilities available.

**Interactions:** Tea, coffee, calcium, antacids and tetracyclines reduce oral iron absorption — take 2 hours apart.

---

### Other minerals

| Mineral | Deficiency | Supplementation use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zinc | Growth faltering, diarrhoea, impaired immunity, delayed wound healing | Added to ORS in childhood diarrhoea (WHO protocol); wound care |
| Calcium | Osteoporosis, rickets/osteomalacia, tetany | Post-menopausal women; antacid; antidote to hyperkalaemia (cardiac membrane); given with vitamin D |
| Iodine | Simple goitre, cretinism (congenital hypothyroidism if mother deficient), neonatal hypothyroidism | Salt iodisation programme (India — National Iodine Deficiency Disorders Control Programme) |
| Magnesium | Neuromuscular irritability, arrhythmias | IV in eclampsia, Torsades de pointes; oral in deficiency |
| Potassium | Hypokalaemia — muscular weakness, constipation, arrhythmias; worsens digoxin toxicity | Oral/IV replacement; cardiac monitoring during rapid IV replacement |

## Remember

- Adrenaline for anaphylaxis: 0.5 mg IM outer thigh; repeat after 5 min; never IV unless cardiac arrest.
- Atropine for organophosphate poisoning: large IV doses until secretions dry up; followed by pralidoxime.
- Adenosine for SVT: 6 mg IV rapid bolus + 20 mL saline flush; warn about brief asystole.
- Amiodarone long-term: thyroid, lung and liver toxicity; sun protection; long half-life.
- Digoxin toxicity signs: nausea → visual haloes → bradycardia/arrhythmia; hypokalaemia increases risk; antidote Digibind.
- Calcium gluconate 10 mL 10% IV: antidote to hyperkalaemia (membrane stabilisation) and MgSO4 toxicity.
- TCA overdose: sodium bicarbonate IV (reverses Na-channel block); DO NOT give flumazenil.
- Paracetamol overdose: NAC within 8 hours; use Rumack–Matthew nomogram.
- Organophosphate antidote: atropine (block muscarinic) + pralidoxime (reactivate cholinesterase).
- Thiamine IV before glucose in any patient with suspected alcohol dependence or malnutrition.
- B12 deficiency: megaloblastic anaemia + SACD; pernicious anaemia needs IM B12 (no intrinsic factor).
- Folic acid 400 mcg before conception: prevents NTDs.
- IV iron — have resuscitation facilities ready; monitor 30 minutes post-infusion.

# Unit 6: Vaccines and sera, anticancer drugs, immunomodulators and alternative systems of medicine

This unit covers biological medicines — vaccines, immunoglobulins and antitoxins — and immunological drugs used in cancer and autoimmune disease. It also introduces alternative medicine systems. The National Immunisation Schedule and chemotherapy nursing are high-probability examination topics.

---

## 6.1 Vaccines

### Types of vaccines

| Type | Examples | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Live attenuated** | BCG, MMR, OPV, Rotavirus, Varicella, Yellow fever | Replicates to give strong, long-lasting immunity; contraindicated in pregnancy and immunocompromise; cold chain critical |
| **Inactivated (killed)** | IPV (injectable polio), Hepatitis A, Influenza (most formulations), Rabies post-exposure | Killed organisms; safer in immunocompromise; multiple doses needed; more stable than live vaccines |
| **Toxoid** | Tetanus toxoid (TT), Diphtheria (DT/Td) | Inactivated toxin; stimulates antitoxin production |
| **Subunit / conjugate** | Hepatitis B, Hib (Haemophilus influenzae b), Meningococcal, Pneumococcal (PCV), HPV | Purified antigen only; very safe; may need adjuvant; conjugate = polysaccharide linked to carrier protein (better in infants) |
| **mRNA** | COVID-19 (Pfizer-BioNTech, Moderna) | mRNA encodes spike protein; rapidly developed; no live virus; cold chain required |

---

### National Immunisation Schedule of India (key highlights)

India's Universal Immunisation Programme (UIP) provides the following schedule free of cost:

| Age | Vaccines |
| --- | --- |
| Birth | BCG (intradermal, left upper arm), OPV-0, Hepatitis B-1 |
| 6 weeks | OPV-1, Penta-1 (DTP+Hib+HepB), PCV-1, Rotavirus-1 (where included) |
| 10 weeks | OPV-2, Penta-2, PCV-2, Rotavirus-2 |
| 14 weeks | OPV-3, Penta-3, PCV-3, Rotavirus-3, IPV (fractional dose intradermal) |
| 9–12 months | MR (measles-rubella) or MMR, PCV booster |
| 16–24 months | DTP booster, OPV booster, MR second dose |
| 5–6 years | DTP second booster |
| 10 years | Td (tetanus-diphtheria adult) |
| 16 years | Td |
| Pregnant women | TT-1 and TT-2 (or Td), if previously immunised give booster |

**Nurse's role in immunisation:**
- **Cold chain maintenance:** vaccines stored 2–8°C (OPV at −15 to −25°C); check temperature log daily; discard if freeze-indicator is triggered for inactivated vaccines
- **Documentation:** immunisation card, HMIS register, AEFI (adverse event following immunisation) register
- **Consent:** obtain before vaccination; explain procedure and common side effects
- **Injection technique:** BCG — intradermal 0.05 mL left deltoid; OPV — 2 drops oral; IM vaccines — anterolateral thigh in infants (<1 year), deltoid in children and adults; correct needle size
- **AEFI recognition and management:** fever (paracetamol), swelling at site (cold compress), severe systemic reactions (adrenaline); report within 24 hours for serious AEFI
- **Tracking defaulters:** home visits, health education, record defaulter status

## 6.2 Antisera and antitoxins

### Anti-tetanus serum (ATS)

Horse-derived immunoglobulin; provides passive immunity against tetanus toxin.

**Precaution:** Horse serum can cause **serum sickness** (immune complex disease — fever, rash, joint pain, 7–14 days after) or acute anaphylaxis. **Always do a skin test** before giving: 0.1 mL diluted ATS intradermal; wait 15–20 minutes; a wheal >5 mm is positive — use tetanus immunoglobulin (human) instead if available.

Modern standard: use **human tetanus immunoglobulin (HTIG)** where available — no anaphylaxis risk. In a wound, give HTIG on the **opposite limb** from the tetanus toxoid.

---

### Rabies post-exposure prophylaxis

The **Essen regimen** (most common): rabies vaccine IM days 0, 3, 7, 14 and 28.

For severe bites (category III — transdermal bites, licks on mucosa/open wounds): rabies vaccine **plus** rabies immunoglobulin (RIG) on day 0, infiltrated around the wound.

Nurse: clean the wound thoroughly with soap and water (most important first step), refer immediately, explain that the schedule must be completed.

---

### Anti-snake venom (polyvalent ASV)

**Signs of significant envenomation:** Local — severe swelling, blistering, necrosis; systemic — coagulopathy (non-clotting 20-minute whole-blood clotting test — WBCT), neurotoxicity (ptosis, dysarthria, respiratory paralysis), haematuria.

**Dose:** IV infusion (starting 10 mL/hour, increasing if tolerated); 10 vials initially; more if coagulopathy persists.

**Risk:** Anaphylaxis — adrenaline at the bedside; promethazine and hydrocortisone given before ASV as prophylaxis by some authorities (controversial — adrenaline always first if anaphylaxis occurs).

**Nurse's responsibilities:** IV access; watch for anaphylaxis during infusion; monitor 20-min WBCT to track response; watch for respiratory paralysis in neurotoxic bites (cobra/krait) — respiratory support ready; never incise, suck or apply tourniquet.

## 6.3 Anticancer drugs

### Classification

| Class | Examples | Mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Alkylating agents** | Cyclophosphamide, ifosfamide, chlorambucil, nitrogen mustard | Cross-link DNA strands → prevent replication; cell-cycle non-specific |
| **Antimetabolites** | Methotrexate (MTX), 5-fluorouracil (5-FU), cytarabine, gemcitabine | Mimic normal metabolites → interfere with DNA/RNA synthesis; S-phase specific |
| **Cytotoxic antibiotics** | Doxorubicin (adriamycin), daunorubicin, bleomycin, mitomycin-C | Intercalate DNA or generate free radicals; cell-cycle non-specific |
| **Vinca alkaloids** | Vincristine, vinblastine | Bind tubulin → spindle damage → arrest in mitosis; M-phase specific |
| **Taxanes** | Paclitaxel, docetaxel | Stabilise microtubules → cell cannot divide; M-phase specific |
| **Targeted therapy** | Imatinib (Gleevec — CML: BCR-ABL inhibitor), trastuzumab (Herceptin — HER2+ breast cancer), gefitinib (EGFR inhibitor) | Targeted at specific molecular abnormalities; less toxicity to normal cells |
| **Hormonal therapy** | Tamoxifen (oestrogen receptor antagonist in breast cancer), anastrozole/letrozole (aromatase inhibitors), flutamide (androgen blocker in prostate cancer) | Block hormone-sensitive tumour growth |
| **Corticosteroids** | Prednisolone, dexamethasone | Cytotoxic to lymphoid cells; anti-inflammatory; reduce cerebral oedema; antiemetic component |

---

### Shared toxicities of cytotoxic drugs

| Toxicity | Mechanism | Nursing management |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bone marrow suppression (neutropenia, anaemia, thrombocytopenia) | Rapidly dividing marrow cells killed | Monitor full blood count; neutropenic precautions (ANC < 500/µL); fever in neutropenia = emergency; blood/platelet transfusions as ordered |
| Nausea and vomiting | Central (chemoreceptor trigger zone) + local | Antiemetics before, during and after (ondansetron, dexamethasone, metoclopramide); small frequent meals; hydration |
| Alopecia (hair loss) | Hair follicle cells rapidly dividing | Counsel before treatment; wig/head covering; reversible (usually) |
| Mucositis (mouth sores) | GIT lining rapidly dividing | Regular mouth care; saline/bicarbonate rinses; oral antifungals/antivirals as needed; soft diet |
| Peripheral neuropathy | Vinca alkaloids, taxanes, platinum | Report tingling/numbness; protect from injury |
| Gonadal toxicity | Germline cells | Counsel about infertility; sperm/embryo cryopreservation before treatment |

---

### Drug-specific toxicities

| Drug | Specific toxicity | Nursing/antidote |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cyclophosphamide | Haemorrhagic cystitis (acrolein metabolite) | Give MESNA (mercaptoethanesulfonate) IV with high-dose therapy; hydrate well; monitor urine for blood |
| Methotrexate (high dose) | Mucositis, hepatotoxicity, nephrotoxicity | Folinic acid (leucovorin) rescue; ensure adequate hydration; monitor levels |
| Doxorubicin | Cardiomyopathy (dilated; cumulative dose-related) | Echocardiography before and during treatment; total lifetime dose limit (~450 mg/m²) |
| Bleomycin | Pulmonary fibrosis | Baseline and periodic pulmonary function tests; oxygen toxicity worsens it — minimise supplemental O2 |
| Vincristine | Peripheral neuropathy; inappropriate ADH secretion | Avoid constipation; monitor neurological signs |
| Cisplatin / carboplatin | Nephrotoxicity (cisplatin), ototoxicity, hypomagnesaemia | Aggressive IV hydration before and after cisplatin; monitor creatinine and urine output; audiometry |
| Taxanes | Peripheral neuropathy, hypersensitivity reactions | Premedicate with dexamethasone + antihistamine; monitor during infusion |

---

### Nurse's role in chemotherapy

1. **Verify order:** 5 rights; check body surface area calculation; two-nurse sign-off on cytotoxic doses in many hospitals
2. **PPE for safe handling:** gloves, gown, face shield when preparing or giving; dispose in purple/cytotoxic waste bins; spillage kit
3. **IV extravasation protocol:**
   - Vesicants (cause tissue necrosis if extravasated): doxorubicin, vincristine, mechlorethamine — STOP immediately; do not remove cannula; aspirate; apply cooling (most) or warmth (vinca alkaloids); specific antidote if available; document and photograph
   - Irritants: cause pain but not necrosis — slow the infusion, dilute
4. **Antiemetic premedication:** give 30–60 minutes before chemotherapy
5. **Neutropenic precautions (ANC < 500 — febrile neutropenia is an emergency):** single room, hand hygiene, no live plants/flowers in room, cooked food only, avoid unwell visitors, promptly report temperature ≥38°C (take blood cultures, start antibiotics within 1 hour)
6. **Patient and family education:** expected side effects, when to seek help, infection signs, care of central lines

## 6.4 Immunomodulators

### Immunosuppressants

Used to prevent transplant rejection and treat autoimmune diseases.

| Drug | Use | Monitoring | Key caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cyclosporine | Transplant rejection, psoriasis, RA, nephrotic syndrome | Blood levels, renal function, BP, blood glucose | Nephrotoxic; hypertensive; gingival hyperplasia; hirsutism; avoid live vaccines; many drug interactions (through CYP3A4) |
| Tacrolimus (FK506) | Transplant rejection (more potent than cyclosporine) | Blood levels, renal function, glucose (diabetes risk) | Similar to cyclosporine but more diabetogenic; avoid grapefruit |
| Methotrexate (low dose) | RA, psoriasis, IBD | FBC, LFTs, renal function (at least annually) | Folate supplementation needed; hepatotoxic; teratogenic — reliable contraception |
| Mycophenolate mofetil | Transplant rejection, lupus nephritis | FBC (myelosuppression) | GI side effects; teratogenic |
| Azathioprine | Transplant, autoimmune diseases | FBC | Myelosuppression; check TPMT (enzyme) activity before starting |
| Corticosteroids | Multiple uses (anti-inflammatory, immunosuppression) | Blood sugar, BP, bone density, weight | Osteoporosis; hyperglycaemia; Cushing's; infection; never stop suddenly (Addisonian crisis) |

**All immunosuppressants:** Increased risk of infection (especially opportunistic: fungal, viral — CMV, PCP); screen for TB, hepatitis B and C before starting; avoid live vaccines.

---

### Biologics (targeted immunotherapy)

| Drug | Class | Use | Key concern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Infliximab, adalimumab (anti-TNF) | TNF-alpha inhibitors | RA, Crohn's, ulcerative colitis, psoriasis, ankylosing spondylitis | Screen for latent TB before starting (TB reactivation risk); increased infection; demyelination (rare) |
| Rituximab | Anti-CD20 (B-cell depleter) | RA, lymphoma, leukaemia, vasculitis | Infusion reactions; progressive multifocal leucoencephalopathy (PML); hepatitis B reactivation |
| Tocilizumab | Anti-IL-6 receptor | RA, giant cell arteritis, cytokine storm (COVID-19) | Infection risk; raised lipids; bowel perforation |
| Pembrolizumab, nivolumab | PD-1 checkpoint inhibitors | Various cancers (melanoma, lung, etc.) | Immune-related adverse events (irAEs): colitis, pneumonitis, hepatitis, endocrinopathies, dermatitis — treat with corticosteroids |

---

### IV immunoglobulin (IVIG)

Pooled human immunoglobulin from thousands of donors.

**Uses:** Primary immune deficiency, Kawasaki disease (prevents coronary aneurysms — give within 10 days), Guillain–Barré syndrome (GBS), idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP), neonatal haemolytic disease.

**Adverse effects:** Headache, fever, chills, flushing (slow the infusion); aseptic meningitis; rare: thrombosis, haemolysis, renal failure.

**Nurse:** Pre-medicate with paracetamol and antihistamine; slow infusion rate initially (monitor for 20 minutes); document batch numbers (blood product safety).

## 6.5 Alternative systems of medicine (AYUSH)

India's Ministry of AYUSH (Ayurveda, Yoga and Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, Homeopathy) oversees traditional and complementary medicine.

| System | Origin | Key features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ayurveda** | Ancient India | Tridosha theory (vata, pitta, kapha); plant, mineral and animal products; Panchakarma (purification therapies) |
| **Yoga and Naturopathy** | India | Physical and mental wellness; asanas, pranayama; non-pharmacological |
| **Unani** | Greco-Arabic | Four humours; plant and mineral medicines; common in Muslim communities |
| **Siddha** | South India (Tamil tradition) | Similar to Ayurveda; heavy metals used (require careful preparation) |
| **Homeopathy** | Samuel Hahnemann (Germany, 18th century) | "Like cures like," extreme dilutions; widely used in India |

**WHO traditional medicine strategy:** Recognises the importance of traditional medicine; promotes safety, quality and rational use; encourages member states to integrate it appropriately into health systems.

**Nurse's role:**
- **Ask routinely** about concurrent use of traditional or herbal medicines — many patients use both without disclosing
- Herb–drug interactions can be clinically significant:
  - **St John's Wort (Hypericum):** potent enzyme inducer (CYP3A4, P-glycoprotein) → reduces levels of antiretrovirals, warfarin, oral contraceptives, digoxin, immunosuppressants
  - **Ginkgo biloba:** antiplatelet effect → increased bleeding risk with anticoagulants/antiplatelets
  - **Garlic, ginger, fish oil:** antiplatelet → bleeding risk
  - **Kava:** hepatotoxicity
  - **Liquorice:** hypokalaemia, hypertension
- Advise patients not to substitute proven treatments with traditional remedies for serious conditions
- Treat patients' beliefs with respect and provide evidence-based information without dismissal

## Remember

- Vaccine types: live attenuated (BCG, MMR, OPV, varicella) — CI in pregnancy and immunocompromise; inactivated (IPV, hepatitis A); toxoid (TT, DT); subunit (HBV, HPV, Hib); mRNA (COVID-19).
- National schedule: BCG at birth; Penta (DTP+Hib+HepB) at 6, 10, 14 weeks; MR at 9–12 months; TT/Td for pregnant women.
- Cold chain: 2–8°C (OPV −20°C); AEFI register and reporting.
- ATS precaution: skin test before giving; human HTIG preferred; give on opposite limb from tetanus toxoid.
- Anticancer toxicities: cyclophosphamide → haemorrhagic cystitis/mesna; MTX → mucositis/leucovorin; doxorubicin → cardiomyopathy; bleomycin → pulmonary fibrosis; vincristine → peripheral neuropathy; cisplatin → nephrotoxicity/hydration; paclitaxel → hypersensitivity/premedicate.
- Vesicant extravasation: STOP infusion immediately; aspirate; specific antidote; document.
- Febrile neutropenia (ANC < 500 + fever ≥ 38°C): blood cultures then antibiotics within 1 hour.
- Anti-TNF biologics: screen for latent TB before starting.
- St John's Wort: induces CYP3A4 → reduces drug levels of contraceptives, warfarin, antiretrovirals, digoxin.

## Four principles of bioethics (Beauchamp and Childress)

| Principle | Definition | Nursing example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Autonomy** | Respect for the patient's right to make informed, voluntary decisions | Informed consent; advance directive; respecting refusal |
| **Non-maleficence** | Do not inflict harm or unacceptable risk | Medication safety; fall prevention; infection control |
| **Beneficence** | Positive duty to promote the patient's well-being | Evidence-based wound care; pain management |
| **Justice** | Fair, equitable distribution of benefits and burdens | Organ allocation; equitable access to care |

## Four ethical theories

| Theory | Key thinker | What makes an act right? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Utilitarianism** | Bentham, Mill | Greatest good for greatest number |
| **Deontology** | Kant | Duty; universal rules; rights regardless of outcomes |
| **Virtue ethics** | Aristotle, Nightingale | Good character (compassion, honesty, courage, prudence) |
| **Care ethics** | Gilligan, Noddings | Caring relationships; responding to individual need |

## Informed consent — five elements

1. **Disclosure** — adequate information given
2. **Comprehension** — patient understands
3. **Voluntariness** — free from coercion
4. **Competence (capacity)** — able to understand and decide
5. **Authorisation** — patient explicitly agrees

**Nurse's role:** Witness the signature; verify conditions; raise concerns before the procedure.

---

## ICN Code of Ethics — four elements

1. **Nurses and People** — primary responsibility to patients; promote health; respect rights; confidentiality
2. **Nurses and Practice** — maintain competence; professional standards; safe practice; report unethical behaviour
3. **Nurses and the Profession** — develop knowledge; support professional organisations
4. **Nurses and Co-workers** — collaborative practice; protect individuals from unethical co-worker acts

## Key Indian legislation

| Act / Judgment | Year | Key provision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mental Healthcare Act | 2017 | Right to mental health care; advance directive; nominated representative; involuntary admission criteria; least restrictive care |
| POCSO Act | 2012 | Mandatory reporting of child sexual abuse (under 18); every person including nurses must report to police; non-reporting is an offence |
| Common Cause v Union of India | 2018 | Advance directives (living wills) legally valid in India; right to die with dignity recognised |
| Aruna Shanbaug case | 2011 | Conditional permission for passive euthanasia in permanent vegetative state; High Court approval required |
| Transplantation of Human Organs Act | 1994 (amended 2011) | Brain death criteria; opt-in consent; living donation (near relative); NOTTO for national coordination |

## Euthanasia — types and legal status

| Type | Meaning | Legal in India? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Active euthanasia | Deliberate act to end life | **Illegal** |
| Passive euthanasia | Withholding/withdrawing life-sustaining treatment | **Conditionally legal** (advance directive or court order) |
| Physician-assisted dying | Doctor prescribes lethal dose for patient to self-administer | **Illegal** |

## Confidentiality — exceptions

Confidentiality may be broken (or is legally required) in these situations:
- Serious danger to the patient or identifiable third party
- Mandatory reportable disease notification
- Court order
- POCSO Act — mandatory reporting of child sexual abuse

## DNR — key points

- Written order not to attempt CPR if the heart or breathing stops
- After discussion with patient (if capable) and/or family; signed by senior doctor
- **Does NOT mean stop all care** — nursing care, comfort, nutrition and all other treatments continue
- Document clearly; communicate at every handover
- If a nurse is uncertain about a DNR order and the patient arrests — start CPR and clarify immediately

## MORAL decision-making model

| Letter | Step |
| --- | --- |
| **M** | Massage the dilemma — gather facts; identify the issue |
| **O** | Outline the options — all possible courses of action |
| **R** | Review criteria and resolve — apply principles |
| **A** | Affirm position and act |
| **L** | Look back and evaluate |

## Professional boundaries

- No personal/romantic relationships with patients while in a professional capacity
- Do not accept significant gifts
- No patient images on social media — ever
- Social media: do not accept patient friend requests (blurs boundaries)

---

## Whistleblowing

- Professional and ethical duty to report unsafe practices
- Steps: document → report to supervisor → escalate → report to INC / NHRC if internal process fails
- Never ignore a patient safety concern

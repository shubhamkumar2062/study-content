# Unit 1 — Professionalism, professional conduct and regulatory bodies

This unit asks a simple question with a long answer: what makes nursing a *profession*, and what does it mean to *behave* like a professional at the bedside? You learn the criteria of a profession, the attributes of professionalism, the day-to-day challenges that test it, the rules of professional conduct, and the bodies that keep the profession in order.

## 1.1 Profession

**Profession.** An occupation that needs long, specialised education, is based on a defined body of knowledge, serves society, and governs itself through a code of conduct. Members are accountable to the public, not only to an employer.

An *occupation* is any job done for a living. A *profession* is a special kind of occupation. The difference is education, a knowledge base, service to people, and self-regulation.

### Criteria of a profession

Different writers list these slightly differently, but the usual criteria are:

| Criterion | What it means for nursing |
| --- | --- |
| Specialised body of knowledge | Nursing science, built on biological, behavioural and social sciences |
| Long, formal education | Diploma, degree and higher programmes in recognised institutions |
| Service to society | The main aim is the welfare of patients and the community, not personal gain |
| Code of ethics | A written code guides conduct (INC and ICN codes) |
| Autonomy | The profession decides its own standards and practice |
| Self-regulation | A statutory council controls entry, education and discipline |
| Professional organisation | Members join bodies that speak for the profession |
| Accountability | Each member answers for his or her own practice |
| Lifelong learning | Knowledge is kept up to date throughout the career |

### Nursing as a profession

Nursing meets these criteria: it has its own knowledge base and theories, structured education recognised by the Indian Nursing Council (INC), a code of ethics, a statutory council that regulates it, professional organisations such as the Trained Nurses' Association of India (TNAI), and a clear commitment to public service. Nursing began as a calling and a service and has grown into a full profession with its own research, specialities and leadership roles.

## 1.2 Professionalism

**Professionalism.** The conduct, qualities and judgement expected of a member of a profession — doing the work with knowledge, skill, honesty and respect, and putting the patient's welfare first.

A person can hold a nursing qualification yet not *act* professionally. Professionalism is how the knowledge is used every day: on time, well groomed, kind, accountable, and safe.

### Characteristics and attributes of professionalism

- **Competence** — sound knowledge and skill, kept current.
- **Accountability and responsibility** — owning one's actions and errors.
- **Integrity and honesty** — truthful records, no short cuts.
- **Respect** — for patients, families and colleagues, whoever they are.
- **Caring and compassion** — concern for the person, not only the disease.
- **Communication** — clear, polite, two-way.
- **Teamwork** — working with the health team for one goal.
- **Confidentiality** — guarding what the patient shares.
- **Appearance and punctuality** — tidy uniform, reliable timekeeping.
- **Commitment to learning** — continuing education and reflection.

**Indicators** are the visible signs an observer can tick off: arrives on time, wears the correct uniform, keeps patient information private, documents truthfully, reports an error honestly, speaks respectfully, and seeks help when unsure.

## 1.3 Challenges of professionalism

Several everyday pressures make professional behaviour hard. The syllabus names four.

**Personal identity vs professional identity.** Who you are off duty (your beliefs, likes and habits) may clash with the role you must play on duty. A nurse may personally disagree with a patient's choice but must still give respectful, non-judgemental care. Learning to separate the personal self from the professional role, without becoming cold, is part of growing into the profession.

**Preservation of self-integrity.** Integrity means acting in line with your values and the profession's code. It is *threatened* when a nurse is pressured to do something wrong — to cut corners, to cover a mistake, or to go along with an unsafe practice. Two common threats:

- **Deceiving the patient — withholding information:** keeping back facts the patient has a right to know (for example, hiding a diagnosis or a complication). This breaks honesty and the right to information.
- **Falsifying records:** writing that a drug or observation was done when it was not, or changing an entry to hide an error. This is dishonest, unsafe and can be illegal.

A nurse preserves integrity by refusing such acts, reporting honestly, and speaking up through the proper channel.

**Communication and relationship with team members.** Patient safety depends on open, respectful communication across the team. Blaming, hiding information, or staying silent about a concern puts the patient at risk. Good ethical decisions need every member's relevant input, so the nurse must share concerns clearly and listen to others.

**Relationship with patients and society.** The nurse must keep the patient's trust and also meet society's expectations of honesty and fairness, while respecting professional limits. Favouritism, accepting large gifts, or over-involvement all damage this relationship.

## 1.4 Professional conduct

**Professional conduct** is the set of behaviours a nurse is expected to follow at work. It rests on:

**Following ethical principles.** Acting for the patient's good (beneficence), avoiding harm (non-maleficence), being fair (justice) and respecting choice (autonomy) — covered in detail in Units 3 and 4.

**Adhering to policies, rules and regulations of the institution.** Following hospital protocols, infection-control rules, duty rosters, documentation standards and the law of the land.

**Professional etiquette and behaviour.** Courtesy, punctuality, polite greetings, introducing yourself, calling patients by name, knocking before entering, and treating support staff with respect.

**Professional grooming — uniform and dress code.** A clean, correct uniform with the identity badge; hair tied back; short clean nails; minimal jewellery; closed comfortable footwear. Good grooming is not vanity — it builds patient confidence and controls infection.

**Professional boundaries.** The limits that keep the nurse–patient relationship therapeutic and safe. The relationship exists for the patient's benefit, not the nurse's. Boundaries are crossed by over-familiarity, sharing one's own problems with patients, accepting valuable gifts, lending or borrowing money, or any romantic or financial involvement. Clear boundaries protect both the patient and the nurse and apply to caregivers and team members too.

```text
Professional boundary
  under-involved          THERAPEUTIC ZONE          over-involved
  (cold, neglectful) <----- respectful, helpful -----> (too close,

```

## 1.5 Regulatory bodies

A **regulatory body** is a statutory authority that controls who may practise and how, to protect the public.

### Indian Nursing Council (INC)

- A statutory body set up under the **Indian Nursing Council Act, 1947**, with its office in New Delhi.
- **Roles:** to set and maintain uniform standards of nursing education across the country; to approve and recognise nursing programmes and institutions; to prescribe the curriculum and the syllabus; to maintain the Indian Nurses Register; and to give guidance and a code of conduct for the profession.
- INC works at the national level; it does not itself register individual nurses — that is done by the state councils.

### State Nursing Council (SNC)

- Each state has its own nursing council, created under a state Act.
- **Roles:** to register qualified nurses, midwives and auxiliaries of that state and issue their licence; to maintain the state register; to recognise institutions and (in many states) conduct examinations; and to take disciplinary action, including removing a name from the register for professional misconduct.
- A nurse must be registered with a State Nursing Council to practise in that state.

| Point | INC | State Nursing Council |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Level | National | State |
| Set up under | INC Act, 1947 | A state Act |
| Main work | Standards, curriculum, recognition | Registration, licensing, discipline |
| Register | Indian Nurses Register | State register |

## 1.6 Professional organisations

A **professional organisation** is a voluntary body of members that promotes the profession, protects members' interests, and improves standards. Membership is by choice (unlike registration, which is compulsory).

| Organisation | Level | Role in brief |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trained Nurses' Association of India (TNAI) | National | The main professional body for Indian nurses; founded 1908; promotes education, welfare, standards and the image of nursing; represents nurses nationally |
| Student Nurses' Association (SNA) | National (students) | A unit of TNAI (1929) for student nurses; develops leadership, professional and social skills through activities, competitions and conferences |
| Nurses' League of the Christian Medical Association of India (CMAI) | National | The nursing section of CMAI; supports nursing education and service in Christian mission and other hospitals |
| International Council of Nurses (ICN) | International | A federation of national nurses' associations; founded 1899; headquarters in Geneva; speaks for nursing worldwide and issues the ICN Code of Ethics for Nurses |
| International Confederation of Midwives (ICM) | International | The global body for midwives' associations; strengthens midwifery and sets global standards for midwifery practice and education |

TNAI represents India in the ICN. These bodies **advance the profession**: they run continuing education, publish journals, set standards, defend members' welfare, and give nurses a collective voice in policy.

## 1.7 Nursing application

- You behave professionally the moment you reach the ward: correct uniform, badge, punctuality, a respectful greeting, and a truthful handover.
- If asked to sign for care you did not give, or to hide an error, that is falsifying records — refuse and report through the proper channel.
- Keep the relationship therapeutic: friendly and helpful, but never over-involved; decline valuable gifts politely and explain why.
- Register with your State Nursing Council before you practise, and keep the registration current.
- Join TNAI/SNA to keep learning and to add your voice to the profession.

## Remember

- **Profession** = specialised knowledge + long education + service + code of ethics + self-regulation; nursing meets all of these.
- **Professionalism** = using that knowledge daily with competence, honesty, respect and caring, putting the patient first.
- Four **challenges**: personal vs professional identity, self-integrity (withholding information, falsifying records), team communication, relationship with patients and society.
- **Conduct** = ethical principles + institutional rules + etiquette + grooming (clean uniform, badge, short nails) + professional boundaries.
- **INC** (1947 Act, national) sets standards and curriculum; **State Nursing Council** registers and licenses nurses.
- Professional organisations (voluntary): **TNAI** (1908, national), **SNA** (student unit), **CMAI Nurses' League**, **ICN** (1899, Geneva), **ICM** (midwives).

# Unit 2 — Professional values in nursing

Values are the beliefs that guide what we think is right, important and worth doing. This unit defines values, shows how a person becomes clear about their own values and takes on the values of the profession, and then works through the values a nurse is expected to live by: caring, compassion, respect for the person, privacy, honesty, trust and advocacy.

## 2.1 Values

**Value.** A belief or standard that a person holds about what is good, right, worthwhile or desirable, and which guides behaviour and choices.

Values are learned early from family, culture, religion, school and experience, and they tend to stay fairly stable. They act as an inner compass: they tell us how to act even when no one is watching.

### Characteristics of values

- **Learned**, not inborn — picked up from family, society and experience.
- **Enduring but not fixed** — they last, yet can change with new experience and maturity.
- **Personal** — each person's set is a little different.
- **Guide behaviour and choices** — they shape decisions and priorities.
- **Ranked** — people hold some values above others (a *value system*).
- **Emotion-linked** — acting against a value feels wrong; acting for it feels right.
- **Shown in action** — a true value shows in what a person chooses and repeats, not just what they say.

## 2.2 Value clarification

**Value clarification** is the process of examining your own beliefs, deciding which values you truly hold, and acting on them consistently. It makes hidden values conscious so that choices become deliberate rather than automatic.

A commonly taught model has three stages with seven steps:

```text
CHOOSING  1. freely
          2. from alternatives
          3. after thinking about the results
PRIZING   4. being happy with the choice
          5. willing to state it to others
ACTING    6. acting on it
          7. acting on it repeatedly, as a pattern of life
```

**Why it matters in nursing:** a nurse meets patients whose values differ from her own (about diet, family, death, treatment). Being clear about her own values helps her avoid imposing them on patients and helps her respect the patient's values during care.

## 2.3 Personal and professional values; professional socialisation

**Personal values** are the beliefs you hold as an individual — shaped by your upbringing, religion and culture.

**Professional values** are the standards the profession expects of its members — set out in nursing codes of ethics (for example caring, respect, honesty, autonomy, justice, advocacy).

**Professional socialisation** is the process by which a student takes on the knowledge, skills, attitudes and *values* of the profession and makes them part of her own identity. It happens through education, role models (teachers and senior nurses), clinical experience and reflection. The aim is the **integration** of professional values with personal values, so that the nurse acts professionally not because a rule forces her, but because the values have become her own.

```text
Personal values  +  Professional values
   (home,            (code of ethics,
    culture)          role models, practice)
        \               /
         \  professional socialisation
          \           /
       integrated nurse: professional values
       become part of personal identity
```

## 2.4 Importance of professional values in nursing

Professional values matter because they:

- Keep care **patient-centred and safe**.
- Guide **decisions and priorities** when situations are difficult.
- Build **trust** between nurse, patient, family and team.
- Protect the patient's **dignity and rights**.
- Give the nurse **job satisfaction and a professional identity**.
- Maintain the **good name and standards** of the profession.

The INC syllabus lists the core professional values below.

## 2.5 Caring

**Caring** is the central value of nursing: a genuine concern for the well-being of the patient, shown through attentive, skilled and kind actions.

Caring has a **feeling** side (concern, warmth) and a **doing** side (competent, safe actions). Both are needed — kindness without skill is not safe, and skill without kindness is not nursing.

**The caring process** (the steps caring takes in practice):

1. **Knowing** the patient — their condition, needs and what matters to them.
2. **Being with** — being present, attentive and emotionally available.
3. **Doing for** — giving competent physical care the patient cannot do alone.
4. **Enabling** — helping the patient and family to cope and to care for themselves.
5. **Maintaining belief** — holding hope and faith in the patient's ability to get through.

## 2.6 Compassion

**Compassion** is awareness of another's suffering together with the wish and effort to relieve it. It turns feeling into helpful action.

### Sympathy vs empathy

| Point | Sympathy | Empathy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning | Feeling *for* the patient (pity, sorrow) | Understanding and feeling *with* the patient, from their point of view |
| Stance | Observer, a step removed | Alongside the patient |
| Risk | Can become pity, which patients dislike | Keeps understanding without losing objectivity |
| Value in care | Limited | The more useful response in nursing |

**Altruism** is unselfish concern for the welfare of others — being ready to act for the patient's good even at some cost to oneself. It is the attitude behind compassion.

## 2.7 Other professional values

**Conscientiousness.** Being careful, thorough and reliable; doing each task correctly and completely, checking details, following protocol, and not leaving work half done.

**Dedication / devotion to work.** Commitment to the job and to patients — reliable attendance, extra effort when needed, and loyalty to the goals of good care.

**Respect for the person — human dignity.** Treating every patient as a worthwhile human being, whatever their illness, age, caste, religion, income or behaviour. In practice: address patients by name, explain before you act, cover them during procedures, listen to them, and never humiliate or ignore.

**Privacy and confidentiality.** *Privacy* is the patient's right to keep their body and information from unwanted exposure (screens during a procedure, modesty while bathing). *Confidentiality* is the duty to keep what the patient tells you, and their records, secret and shared only with those who need it for care.

- **Incidental disclosure** is the accidental sharing of patient information — talking about a patient in a lift or corridor, leaving charts open, discussing cases where visitors can hear, or unlocked screens. The nurse must prevent it by lowering her voice, closing files, and discussing patients only in private and only with the team.

**Honesty and integrity.** Being truthful and acting in line with values and the code. **Truth-telling** means giving the patient honest information, not deceiving them, and admitting and reporting one's own errors. (Honest records were covered in Unit 1 under self-integrity.)

**Trust and credibility.** Patients must be able to rely on the nurse.

- **Fidelity** — keeping promises and commitments to the patient ("I will come back in ten minutes" — and you do).
- **Loyalty** — standing by the patient's interests and by the team, honestly.

Trust, once broken, is very hard to rebuild, so credibility is guarded in every small interaction.

## 2.8 Advocacy

**Advocacy** means speaking and acting for the patient's rights, wishes and welfare, especially when the patient cannot do so alone.

The nurse is the health-team member closest to the patient and is well placed to advocate. Advocacy works at several levels:

| Level | Example |
| --- | --- |
| For the patient | Making sure the patient understands before consenting; raising a patient's unaddressed pain with the doctor; protecting a confused patient from harm |
| For the work environment | Speaking up for safe staffing, supplies and safe practices |
| For nursing education and practice | Supporting better training and evidence-based standards |
| For advancing the profession | Promoting the role, image and growth of nursing through organisations and policy |

```text
ADVOCACY
  patient's voice -> nurse -> health team / managers / policy
  "I speak for the patient's rights and wishes."
```

## 2.9 Nursing application

- Do a quick value check before you judge a patient: the patient's values guide *their* care, not yours.
- Show caring as both warmth *and* safe, skilled work — one without the other is not enough.
- Prefer empathy to pity; be *with* the patient, not above them.
- Guard privacy actively: screens, covers, lowered voice, closed files; never discuss patients where others can hear (incidental disclosure).
- Keep small promises (fidelity) — this is how trust is built.
- Advocate: when a patient does not understand, is in pain, or is at risk, speak up for them.

## Remember

- **Values** are learned, enduring, ranked beliefs that guide behaviour; **value clarification** = choosing freely, prizing and acting repeatedly.
- **Professional socialisation** integrates professional values with personal values through education and role models.
- **Caring** is the core value (knowing, being with, doing for, enabling, maintaining belief); **compassion** adds the wish to relieve suffering.
- **Empathy** (feel *with*) beats **sympathy** (feel *for*); **altruism** = unselfish concern.
- Key values: conscientiousness, dedication, respect/human dignity, privacy and confidentiality (beware **incidental disclosure**), honesty/truth-telling, trust (**fidelity**, **loyalty**).
- **Advocacy** = speaking for the patient's rights and wishes, and for the work environment, education and the profession.

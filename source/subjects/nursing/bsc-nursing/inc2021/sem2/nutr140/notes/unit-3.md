# Unit 3: Vitamins and minerals

This notes file joins **INC Unit V Vitamins** (3 hours) and **INC Unit VI Minerals** (3 hours). These are the micronutrients — needed in small amounts but essential. For each one learn the functions, dietary sources and requirement. Deficiency diseases are only listed here; their causes, signs, management and the nurse's role are in Unit 5.

**Requirement figures** in this file are the ICMR-NIN 2020 RDA for a **sedentary adult man (65 kg) and woman (55 kg)**, taken from the official ICMR-NIN 2020 brief note. B-vitamin needs rise with energy intake, so moderate and heavy workers, adolescents, pregnant and lactating women have different (mostly higher) values in the full report. Where the brief note gives no figure, no number is quoted here — look it up in the ICMR-NIN 2020 RDA report.

## 3.1 Vitamins — definition and classification

**Vitamins** are organic compounds needed in very small amounts for normal growth, metabolism and health. The body cannot make them (or makes too little), so they must come from food. They give no energy but many work as coenzymes in energy-releasing reactions.

| Fat-soluble vitamins | Water-soluble vitamins |
| --- | --- |
| A, D, E, K | B complex (B1 thiamine, B2 riboflavin, niacin, B6 pyridoxine, pantothenic acid, folic acid, B12, biotin) and C (ascorbic acid) |
| Absorbed with fat, need bile | Absorbed directly into blood |
| Stored in the liver and fat tissue | Little storage (except B12 in the liver) |
| Deficiency develops slowly | Deficiency develops quickly |
| Excess can be toxic (especially A and D from supplements) | Excess usually passed in urine |
| Not lost much in cooking water | Lost by washing, boiling and throwing water away, and by heat (C, B1) |
| Need not be eaten daily | Need regular intake |

## 3.2 Fat-soluble vitamins

### Vitamin A (retinol)

- **Forms**: preformed vitamin A (retinol) in animal foods; **provitamin A carotenoids (beta-carotene)** in plant foods, converted to retinol in the intestine.
- **Functions**: (1) **vision in dim light** — forms rhodopsin (visual purple) in the rods of the retina; (2) keeps the **epithelium** of the eye, skin, respiratory, gut and urinary tracts healthy and moist; (3) **immunity** — reduces severity of measles and diarrhoea; (4) growth and bone development; (5) reproduction; (6) beta-carotene is an antioxidant.
- **Sources**: animal — liver, fish liver oils, egg yolk, milk, butter, ghee; plant (carotene) — green leafy vegetables (spinach, amaranth, drumstick leaves, coriander, methi), yellow-orange vegetables and fruits (carrot, pumpkin, papaya, mango).
- **RDA (2020)**: man **1000 µg**, woman **840 µg** per day.
- **Deficiency**: night blindness, xerophthalmia (conjunctival and corneal xerosis, Bitot's spots, keratomalacia), dry rough skin, more infections (Unit 5). **Excess** (mostly from supplements): headache, vomiting, liver damage; can harm the unborn baby — a reason supplements are given only as prescribed.

### Vitamin D (calciferol)

- **Forms**: D3 (cholecalciferol) made in the skin by sunlight (ultraviolet B) and found in animal foods; D2 (ergocalciferol) from plants and yeast. Activated in the liver and then the kidney to calcitriol.
- **Functions**: increases **absorption of calcium and phosphorus** from the gut; helps mineralisation of bones and teeth; maintains blood calcium with parathyroid hormone; supports muscle function and immunity.
- **Sources**: **sunlight on bare skin** (main source); fish liver oils, oily fish, egg yolk, liver, fortified milk and oil.
- **RDA (2020)**: **600 IU** per day for adult men and women.
- **Deficiency**: **rickets** in children, **osteomalacia** in adults, contributes to osteoporosis. **Excess**: high blood calcium, kidney stones, calcification of soft tissues.

### Vitamin E (tocopherols)

- **Functions**: main **antioxidant** in cell membranes — protects PUFA and red cell membranes from damage by free radicals; supports immunity and nerve function.
- **Sources**: vegetable oils (sunflower, safflower, wheat germ), nuts and seeds, green leafy vegetables, whole grains.
- **Requirement**: linked to PUFA intake; see the full ICMR-NIN 2020 report.
- **Deficiency**: rare; haemolytic anaemia in premature infants; nerve and muscle problems in fat malabsorption.

### Vitamin K (phylloquinone, menaquinone)

- **Functions**: needed by the liver to make **clotting factors** (prothrombin, factors VII, IX, X); also for bone proteins.
- **Sources**: green leafy vegetables (spinach, cabbage, broccoli), vegetable oils; **gut bacteria** also make it.
- **Requirement**: see the full ICMR-NIN 2020 report.
- **Deficiency**: bleeding — **haemorrhagic disease of the newborn** (newborns have sterile guts and breast milk is low in vitamin K, which is why newborns receive vitamin K prophylaxis by protocol); bleeding in obstructive jaundice and long antibiotic use.

## 3.3 Water-soluble vitamins

| Vitamin | Main functions | Good sources | ICMR-NIN 2020 RDA (man / woman, sedentary) | Deficiency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Thiamine (B1)** | Coenzyme in carbohydrate metabolism; nerve function | Whole grains, unpolished/parboiled rice, pulses, nuts, oilseeds, pork, yeast | 1.8 / 1.7 mg | **Beriberi** (dry — nerves; wet — heart failure, oedema), Wernicke's encephalopathy in alcohol use |
| **Riboflavin (B2)** | Coenzyme (FAD, FMN) in energy release; healthy skin, eyes | Milk, curd, egg, liver, green leafy vegetables, pulses | 2.5 / 2.4 mg | **Angular stomatitis**, cheilosis, glossitis (magenta tongue), seborrhoeic dermatitis |
| **Niacin (nicotinic acid)** | Coenzyme (NAD, NADP) in energy release; can be made from tryptophan | Groundnut, meat, fish, liver, whole cereals, pulses | 18 / 14 mg | **Pellagra** — the 3 D's: dermatitis, diarrhoea, dementia (death); seen with diets based on maize or jowar |
| **Pyridoxine (B6)** | Coenzyme in amino acid metabolism; haemoglobin and neurotransmitter synthesis | Cereals, pulses, meat, fish, vegetables | 2.4 / 1.9 mg | Peripheral neuritis, convulsions in infants, anaemia; deficiency can occur with long isoniazid use |
| **Pantothenic acid** | Part of coenzyme A — energy metabolism, fatty acid and hormone synthesis | Widespread in foods ("pantothen" = everywhere): liver, egg, whole grains, pulses | See full report | Very rare; "burning feet" syndrome |
| **Folic acid (folate)** | DNA synthesis and cell division; red cell formation; prevents **neural tube defects** | Green leafy vegetables, pulses, liver, groundnut | 300 / 220 µg (dietary folate equivalents) | **Megaloblastic anaemia**; neural tube defects in the baby if low before and in early pregnancy |
| **Vitamin B12 (cobalamin)** | DNA synthesis, red cell formation, myelin of nerves; needs intrinsic factor from the stomach for absorption | **Only animal foods**: milk, curd, egg, meat, fish, liver | 2.2 / 2.2 µg | **Megaloblastic anaemia**, pernicious anaemia, nerve damage; risk in strict vegetarians and the elderly |
| **Vitamin C (ascorbic acid)** | Collagen formation (wound healing, healthy gums and vessels); **increases iron absorption**; antioxidant; immunity | Amla (richest common source), guava, citrus fruits, tomato, green leafy vegetables, sprouted pulses | 80 / 65 mg | **Scurvy** — bleeding swollen gums, small skin bleeds, poor wound healing, bone pain |

Biotin is also a B vitamin (fat and glucose metabolism; sources egg yolk, liver, nuts; deficiency rare, linked with eating many raw egg whites).

**Losses in cooking**: vitamin C and thiamine are destroyed by heat, air and alkali (adding soda); all water-soluble vitamins leach into washing and cooking water. Unit 7 gives the measures to save them.

## 3.4 Minerals — definition and classification

**Minerals** are inorganic elements that remain as ash when food is burnt. They form about 4% of body weight.

| Major minerals (macrominerals) — needed more than 100 mg/day | Trace elements (microminerals) — needed in very small amounts |
| --- | --- |
| **Calcium, phosphorus, sodium, potassium, magnesium** (also chloride and sulphur) | Iron, iodine, zinc, copper, fluorine, selenium, manganese, chromium, cobalt (as part of B12), molybdenum |

General functions of minerals: (1) **structure** — bones and teeth (Ca, P, Mg, F); (2) parts of **body compounds** — haemoglobin (Fe), thyroxine (I), insulin (Zn), B12 (Co); (3) **fluid and acid–base balance** (Na, K, Cl); (4) **nerve and muscle** excitability (Ca, Na, K, Mg); (5) **enzyme activators** (Mg, Zn, Cu, Mn, Se).

## 3.5 Major minerals

### Calcium

- **Functions**: forms and keeps **bones and teeth** strong (99% of body calcium is in bone); **blood clotting**; **muscle contraction** including the heart; nerve transmission; enzyme activation; cell membrane permeability.
- **Regulation**: vitamin D, parathyroid hormone (raises blood calcium) and calcitonin (lowers it).
- **Absorption** is helped by vitamin D, lactose, protein and an acidic medium; it is reduced by **phytates** (cereal bran), **oxalates** (spinach, amaranth), excess fibre and excess phosphate.
- **Sources**: **milk and milk products** (best source), **ragi** (richest cereal source), green leafy vegetables, sesame (til), small fish eaten with bones, pulses.
- **RDA (2020)**: **1000 mg** per day for adult men and women.
- **Deficiency**: rickets, osteomalacia, **osteoporosis** (especially after menopause), tetany (low blood calcium).

### Phosphorus

- **Functions**: with calcium forms bones and teeth; part of **ATP** (energy), DNA and RNA, phospholipids of cell membranes; phosphate buffer in acid–base balance.
- **Sources**: widely present — milk, meat, fish, egg, cereals, pulses, nuts. Deficiency in normal diets is rare.

### Sodium

- **Functions**: main positive ion of extracellular fluid — controls **fluid volume and blood pressure**, osmotic pressure, acid–base balance, nerve impulses and muscle contraction; helps absorption of glucose and amino acids.
- **Sources**: **common salt** (sodium chloride) is the main source; pickles, papads, sauces, chutneys, chips, processed and bakery foods (baking soda), salted butter, cheese.
- **Intake advice**: the need is small; most Indians eat far more salt than required. WHO advises adults to keep salt below **5 g a day** (about one level teaspoon from all sources). High intake raises blood pressure.
- **Deficiency (hyponatraemia)**: from heavy sweating, vomiting, diarrhoea — cramps, weakness, confusion. Oral rehydration solution replaces it in diarrhoea.

### Potassium

- **Functions**: main positive ion **inside cells**; nerve and **muscle (including heart) function**, fluid balance, acid–base balance; a diet rich in potassium helps lower blood pressure.
- **Sources**: fruits (banana, orange, coconut water, dried fruits), vegetables (potato, tomato, green leafy vegetables), pulses, nuts, meat.
- **Deficiency (hypokalaemia)**: diarrhoea, vomiting, some diuretics — muscle weakness, cramps, irregular heartbeat. **Excess (hyperkalaemia)**: in kidney failure — dangerous heart rhythm problems; such patients limit high-potassium foods (Unit 6).

### Magnesium

- **Functions**: part of bones; activates many enzymes (energy metabolism, ATP); nerve and muscle function; protein synthesis.
- **Sources**: whole grains, pulses, nuts, green leafy vegetables (chlorophyll contains magnesium).
- **RDA (2020)**: man **440 mg**, woman **370 mg**.
- **Deficiency**: in chronic diarrhoea, alcohol use, malabsorption — tremors, muscle twitching, irregular heartbeat, low calcium.

## 3.6 Trace elements

### Iron

- **Functions**: part of **haemoglobin** (oxygen carriage in blood) and **myoglobin** (oxygen store in muscle); enzymes of energy release (cytochromes); immunity and brain development.
- **Forms in food**: **haem iron** (meat, liver, fish, poultry) — well absorbed; **non-haem iron** (cereals, pulses, green leafy vegetables, jaggery, dates) — poorly absorbed.
- **Absorption is increased by**: vitamin C (amla, guava, lemon, tomato eaten with the meal), meat/fish factor, need of the body (pregnancy, deficiency), fermentation and germination of grains and pulses.
- **Absorption is reduced by**: **tea and coffee** (tannins) taken with meals, phytates, oxalates, calcium supplements taken at the same time, antacids, worm infestation causes loss.
- **Sources**: liver, meat, fish, egg; green leafy vegetables (amaranth, drumstick leaves, mint, bathua), **garden cress seeds (halim)**, ragi, bajra, pulses, jaggery, dates, dried fruits; iron-fortified (double fortified) salt and fortified rice.
- **RDA (2020)**: man **19 mg**, woman **29 mg** per day (women lose iron in menstruation). Needs rise in pregnancy.
- **Deficiency**: **iron deficiency anaemia** — the most common nutritional deficiency in India (Unit 5).

### Iodine

- **Functions**: needed to make **thyroid hormones** (thyroxine, T3), which control BMR, growth and brain development.
- **Sources**: **iodised salt** (main source in India), sea fish and seafood; plant foods vary with the soil (low in hilly and flood-prone areas).
- **RDA (2020)**: **150 µg** per day for adult men and women.
- **Deficiency**: iodine deficiency disorders — goitre, hypothyroidism, cretinism, intellectual disability, stillbirths and abortions (Unit 5). **Goitrogens** (cabbage, cauliflower, tapioca in large raw amounts) interfere with iodine use.

### Zinc

- **Functions**: part of over 100 enzymes; growth, wound healing, immunity, taste and smell, insulin storage, sexual maturation.
- **Sources**: meat, fish, egg, milk, whole grains, pulses, nuts.
- **RDA (2020)**: man **17 mg**, woman **13 mg**.
- **Deficiency**: growth retardation, delayed puberty, poor wound healing, loss of taste, diarrhoea; zinc is part of diarrhoea management in children (as per protocol).

### Fluorine (fluoride)

- **Functions**: hardens **tooth enamel** and protects against **dental caries**; helps bone mineralisation.
- **Source**: mainly **drinking water**; tea, sea fish.
- **Problems**: too little → dental caries; **too much (high-fluoride water in parts of India) → fluorosis** — mottled teeth (dental fluorosis) and bone and joint changes (skeletal fluorosis). Defluoridation of water is the prevention.

### Other trace elements (short)

- **Copper**: iron use and haemoglobin formation, enzymes; sources: nuts, liver, whole grains.
- **Selenium**: antioxidant enzyme (glutathione peroxidase), thyroid hormone activation; sources: cereals, seafood, meat.
- **Chromium**: helps insulin action. **Manganese**: enzymes. **Cobalt**: part of vitamin B12.

## 3.8 Nursing application

- Advise a **vitamin C source with iron-rich meals** and **no tea or coffee within about an hour of meals** for anaemic patients.
- Encourage **sunlight exposure** of arms and face for vitamin D (elderly, bed-bound, purdah-observing women, indoor workers).
- Teach **use of iodised salt** stored in a closed container away from heat and moisture, added after cooking where possible.
- Promote cheap local sources: ragi for calcium; amaranth, drumstick leaves and garden cress seeds for iron; amla and guava for vitamin C; carrot, papaya and green leafy vegetables for vitamin A.
- Supplements (iron, folic acid, vitamin A, vitamin D, calcium) are given only as prescribed or as per national programme protocols, because some can be harmful in excess.
- Watch potassium and sodium in kidney, heart and liver patients (Unit 6).

## Remember

- Fat-soluble: A, D, E, K (stored, can be toxic); water-soluble: B complex and C (not stored, lost in cooking).
- Vitamin A → rhodopsin, night vision; carotene in green leafy and yellow vegetables; RDA man 1000 µg, woman 840 µg.
- Vitamin D from sunlight; calcium absorption; rickets/osteomalacia; RDA 600 IU.
- Vitamin K → clotting factors; newborn bleeding.
- B1 beriberi; B2 angular stomatitis; niacin pellagra (3 D's); folate and B12 megaloblastic anaemia; C scurvy.
- B12 only in animal foods; amla richest common vitamin C source.
- Major minerals: Ca, P, Na, K, Mg; trace: Fe, I, Zn, Cu, F, Se.
- Calcium RDA 1000 mg; best sources milk and ragi; phytates and oxalates reduce absorption.
- Iron RDA man 19 mg, woman 29 mg; vitamin C helps, tea and coffee hinder.
- Iodine 150 µg; iodised salt; goitre, cretinism. Fluoride: too little caries, too much fluorosis.
- Salt: keep below 5 g a day (WHO).

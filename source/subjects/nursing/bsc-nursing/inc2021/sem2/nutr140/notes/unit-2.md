# Unit 2: Carbohydrates, energy, proteins and fats

This notes file joins three small INC units: **Unit II** Carbohydrates and Energy (3 hours), **Unit III** Proteins (3 hours) and **Unit IV** Fats (2 hours). These are the three energy-yielding macronutrients. For each one learn: composition/classification, functions, dietary sources and requirement (RDA).

Requirement figures below are from the **ICMR-NIN 2020 report "Nutrient Requirements for Indians — Recommended Dietary Allowances (RDA) and Estimated Average Requirements (EAR)"** and its official brief note. Reference adults: man 65 kg, woman 55 kg.

## 2.1 RDA and EAR — two words you need first

- **EAR (Estimated Average Requirement)**: the median requirement of healthy people of a group — the intake that meets the need of half of them.
- **RDA (Recommended Dietary Allowance)**: a higher value, at the upper end of the distribution of requirements, that meets the needs of nearly all healthy people of the group.
- The 2020 committee advises using the **EAR** for judging the adequacy of a population's diet and for diet planning, and the **RDA** as a safe target. It also introduced **tolerable upper limits (TUL)** because many foods are now fortified.
- EAR is roughly 80% of the RDA.

## 2.2 Carbohydrates

**Definition.** Organic compounds of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen (usually H : O = 2 : 1); chemically, polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones and the substances that give them on hydrolysis. They are the cheapest and main source of energy in the Indian diet.

### Composition and classification

| Class | Units | Examples and sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Monosaccharides** (simple sugars) | 1 sugar unit | Glucose (fruits, honey), fructose (fruits, honey), galactose (from milk lactose) |
| **Disaccharides** | 2 units | Sucrose = glucose + fructose (table sugar, jaggery); lactose = glucose + galactose (milk); maltose = glucose + glucose (germinating grains, malt) |
| **Oligosaccharides** | 3–10 units | Raffinose, stachyose in pulses (cause flatulence) |
| **Polysaccharides** | many units | **Starch** (cereals, millets, potato, tubers); **glycogen** (animal starch, stored in liver and muscle); **cellulose** and other fibres (vegetables, fruits, whole grains, bran) |

The INC syllabus describes carbohydrates in food as **starches, sugars and cellulose**:

- **Starch** — the main storage carbohydrate of plants; digested by salivary and pancreatic amylase to maltose and then glucose. Cooking makes starch granules swell and burst so it is digested better.
- **Sugars** — mono- and disaccharides; sweet, quickly absorbed; excess gives "empty calories" and dental caries.
- **Cellulose** — a polysaccharide of glucose that human enzymes cannot digest; part of **dietary fibre**. It adds bulk to stool.

**Dietary fibre** (more in Unit 4): soluble fibre (pectin, gums in fruits, oats, pulses) lowers blood cholesterol and slows glucose absorption; insoluble fibre (cellulose, lignin in bran, whole grains, vegetables) adds bulk and prevents constipation.

### Functions of carbohydrates

1. **Main source of energy** — 4 kcal per gram; glucose is the preferred fuel of the brain and the only fuel of red blood cells.
2. **Protein-sparing action** — enough carbohydrate lets protein be used for growth and repair instead of being burnt for energy.
3. **Prevents ketosis** — some carbohydrate is needed for complete fat oxidation; on starvation or very low-carbohydrate diets ketone bodies accumulate.
4. **Stored as glycogen** in liver (keeps blood glucose normal between meals) and muscle (fuel for exercise).
5. **Fibre** helps bowel movement and lowers cholesterol and the glycaemic response.
6. **Lactose** helps calcium absorption and favours useful gut bacteria.
7. **Structural parts** of compounds such as nucleic acids (ribose), glycoproteins and glycolipids.
8. Gives **taste and variety** to food.

### Dietary sources

- **Cereals and millets**: rice, wheat, maize, jowar, bajra, ragi — about 65–75% carbohydrate.
- **Roots and tubers**: potato, sweet potato, tapioca, yam.
- **Pulses**: about half their weight is carbohydrate besides protein.
- **Sugars**: sugar, jaggery, honey, sweets, soft drinks.
- **Fruits**: banana, mango, dates, grapes; dried fruits.
- **Milk**: lactose.

### Requirement

ICMR-NIN gives requirements for **energy** and protein; carbohydrate makes up the energy left after protein and fat, so it supplies most of the day's calories in the usual Indian diet. ICMR-NIN advises getting carbohydrate mainly from whole grains, millets, pulses, vegetables and fruits and limiting refined sugar; WHO advises keeping free (added) sugars below 10% of daily energy. A diet with too much polished cereal and sugar and too little pulse, vegetable and milk is unbalanced even when it gives enough calories.

## 2.3 Energy

**Unit of energy.** The **kilocalorie (kcal)** — the heat needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of water by 1 °C (from 14.5 to 15.5 °C). The international unit is the **kilojoule**: 1 kcal = 4.184 kJ.

**Energy value of foods (physiological fuel values):**

| Nutrient | kcal per gram |
| --- | --- |
| Carbohydrate | 4 |
| Protein | 4 |
| Fat | 9 |
| Alcohol | 7 (no nutrients — "empty calories") |

Example: a snack with 20 g carbohydrate, 5 g protein and 10 g fat = (20 × 4) + (5 × 4) + (10 × 9) = 80 + 20 + 90 = **190 kcal**.

### Total energy expenditure

The day's energy need = **BMR × PAL** (physical activity level). It has three parts:

1. **Basal metabolic rate** — the largest part (about 60–70% in sedentary people).
2. **Physical activity** — the most variable part.
3. **Thermic effect of food (specific dynamic action)** — energy used to digest, absorb and process food; highest for protein.
4. Plus extra for **growth** in children, **pregnancy** and **lactation**.

### Basal metabolic rate (BMR)

**Definition.** The energy the body uses at complete physical and mental rest to keep vital functions going — heartbeat, breathing, kidney work, nerve activity, body temperature and cell activity. It is measured in the morning, 12–14 hours after the last meal, lying awake and relaxed, at a comfortable room temperature (thermoneutral).

**Measurement**: by indirect calorimetry (oxygen used and carbon dioxide produced), or estimated from equations using weight, age and sex. ICMR-NIN 2020 noted that Indians have lower BMR than predicted from international equations (more body fat, less muscle) and reduced the adult estimate.

### Factors affecting BMR

| Factor | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Body surface area and size** | Larger surface area → higher BMR |
| **Body composition** | More lean (muscle) tissue → higher BMR; more fat → lower |
| **Age** | Highest in infancy and childhood (growth); falls with age |
| **Sex** | Men have higher BMR than women (more lean mass) |
| **Thyroid hormones** | Hyperthyroidism raises BMR markedly; hypothyroidism lowers it |
| **Other hormones** | Adrenaline (stress, fear) raises it; growth hormone, testosterone raise it |
| **Fever** | Each 1 °C rise raises BMR by about 13% |
| **Pregnancy and lactation** | Raised |
| **Climate** | Higher in cold climates |
| **Starvation and under-nutrition** | Lowered (the body saves energy) |
| **Sleep** | About 10% lower during sleep |
| **Drugs and stimulants** | Caffeine and nicotine raise it slightly |
| **Physical training** | Raises it through more muscle |
| **Disease** | Burns, trauma, sepsis raise it greatly |

### Energy requirement (ICMR-NIN 2020)

| Group | Energy (kcal/day) |
| --- | --- |
| Adult man, sedentary / moderate / heavy work | 2110 / 2710 / 3470 |
| Adult woman, sedentary / moderate / heavy work | 1660 / 2130 / 2720 |
| Pregnancy (2nd and 3rd trimester, over the woman's need) | + 350 |
| Lactation 0–6 months / 7–12 months | + 600 / + 520 |
| Infants 0–6 months / 6–12 months | 530 / 660 |

Children's and adolescents' figures are in Unit 4. The 2020 values for adults are lower than the 2010 values because Indians were found to spend less energy for the same activities and to have lower BMR.

**Energy balance**: intake = expenditure → weight steady; intake > expenditure → weight gain (stored as fat); intake < expenditure → weight loss.

## 2.4 Proteins

**Definition.** Complex nitrogen-containing organic compounds made of **amino acids** joined by peptide bonds. They contain carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen (about 16% nitrogen), often sulphur and sometimes phosphorus. The word comes from Greek "proteios" — of first importance.

### Composition

About 20 amino acids are found in body proteins. They are grouped by whether the body can make them.

| Essential (indispensable) | Non-essential (dispensable) |
| --- | --- |
| Must come from food because the body cannot make them in enough amount | The body can make them from other substances |

**The eight essential amino acids** (as listed in the INC syllabus): **isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan, valine**. **Histidine** is also essential for infants (and is now counted as essential for all ages, giving nine); **arginine** is semi-essential (needed during growth).

Memory aid: **"PVT TIM HALL"** — Phenylalanine, Valine, Threonine, Tryptophan, Isoleucine, Methionine, (Histidine, Arginine), Leucine, Lysine.

### Quality of protein

- **Complete protein (high biological value)**: has all essential amino acids in the right proportion — egg (reference protein), milk, meat, fish.
- **Incomplete protein**: short of one or more essential amino acids (the **limiting amino acid**). Cereals are low in **lysine**; pulses are low in **methionine** (sulphur amino acids).
- **Supplementary value (mutual supplementation)**: combining cereals and pulses corrects both shortages — e.g. rice + dal, idli, dosa, khichdi, chapati + dal. Adding a little milk improves it further. ICMR-NIN 2020 advises a cereal : legume : milk ratio of about **3 : 1 : 2.5** for good protein quality.
- Measures of protein quality: biological value (BV), net protein utilisation (NPU), protein efficiency ratio (PER), amino acid score; the 2020 report introduced **DIAAS** (digestible indispensable amino acid score).

### Functions of proteins

1. **Growth and building of new tissue** — infancy, childhood, adolescence, pregnancy, lactation.
2. **Repair and maintenance** — wound healing, recovery from surgery, burns and illness; replacing worn-out cells.
3. **Enzymes** (amylase, pepsin), **hormones** (insulin, growth hormone) and **neurotransmitter** precursors (tryptophan → serotonin).
4. **Antibodies (immunoglobulins)** — defence against infection.
5. **Transport** — haemoglobin (oxygen), transferrin (iron), lipoproteins (fats), albumin (drugs, bilirubin).
6. **Fluid balance** — plasma albumin maintains oncotic pressure; low albumin causes oedema (as in kwashiorkor and liver disease).
7. **Acid–base balance** — proteins act as buffers.
8. **Blood clotting** — fibrinogen, prothrombin.
9. **Structure** — collagen (skin, bone, tendon), keratin (hair, nails), actin and myosin (muscle).
10. **Energy** — 4 kcal/g, used when carbohydrate and fat are not enough (wasteful).

### Dietary sources

| Animal (high quality) | Plant (lower quality alone, good when combined) |
| --- | --- |
| Egg, milk, curd, paneer, cheese | Pulses and legumes (20–25% protein) — dal, rajma, chana, moong |
| Meat, poultry, fish | Soybean (about 40% protein — the richest plant source) |
| | Nuts and oilseeds — groundnut, almonds, sesame |
| | Cereals and millets (about 6–12% protein) — eaten in large amounts, so they give much of the protein in Indian diets |

### Protein requirement (ICMR-NIN 2020)

| Group | EAR | RDA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Healthy adults | 0.66 g/kg/day | **0.83 g/kg/day** |
| Adult man (65 kg) | 42.9 g/day | **54.0 g/day** |
| Adult woman (55 kg) | 36.3 g/day | **45.7 g/day** |
| Pregnancy, 2nd trimester (extra) | + 7.6 g | **+ 9.5 g** |
| Pregnancy, 3rd trimester (extra) | + 17.6 g | **+ 22.0 g** |
| Lactation 0–6 months (extra) | + 13.6 g | **+ 16.9 g** |
| Lactation 6–12 months (extra) | + 10.6 g | **+ 13.2 g** |

The report notes that people eating a cereal-based diet with low-quality protein need about **1 g/kg/day**. The 2010 RDA was 1 g/kg/day.

**Needs rise** in growth, pregnancy, lactation, fever, infection, burns, surgery, injury, fractures and recovery from malnutrition. **Protein is restricted** in some cases — advanced kidney failure not on dialysis and hepatic encephalopathy (Unit 6).

### Deficiency and excess

- Deficiency → poor growth, wasting, oedema, low immunity, poor wound healing, anaemia, fatty liver; in children protein energy malnutrition (Unit 5).
- Excess (very high protein for long periods) adds load on the kidneys and is costly; the extra is burnt for energy or stored as fat.

## 2.5 Fats (lipids)

**Definition.** Fats are esters of **fatty acids** with **glycerol** (triglycerides). Lipids are organic compounds insoluble in water but soluble in organic solvents. Fats are solid at room temperature; oils are liquid.

### Classification

**A. By saturation of fatty acids**

| Type | Bonds | Examples and sources | Effect on health |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Saturated fatty acids (SFA)** | No double bonds | Palmitic, stearic — ghee, butter, coconut oil, palm oil, meat fat | In excess raise LDL cholesterol |
| **Monounsaturated (MUFA)** | One double bond | Oleic acid — groundnut, mustard, rice bran, olive oil | Lower LDL; heart-friendly |
| **Polyunsaturated (PUFA)** | Two or more double bonds | **Linoleic acid (n-6)** — sunflower, safflower, soybean, corn oil; **alpha-linolenic acid (n-3)** — mustard, soybean, flaxseed, green leafy vegetables, fish (EPA, DHA) | Lower cholesterol; essential |
| **Trans fatty acids** | Formed when oils are partially hydrogenated or reheated many times | Vanaspati, bakery and fried snacks | Raise LDL and lower HDL — the most harmful |

**B. Other classifications**: visible fats (oil, ghee, butter added in cooking) and invisible fats (present inside foods — nuts, cereals, pulses, milk, egg, meat); simple lipids (fats, waxes), compound lipids (phospholipids, lipoproteins) and derived lipids (fatty acids, cholesterol, steroids).

**Essential fatty acids (EFA)**: linoleic acid (omega-6) and alpha-linolenic acid (omega-3) cannot be made by the body and must come from food. Their deficiency causes dry, rough, scaly skin (phrynoderma, "toad skin", is linked to it), poor growth and poor wound healing.

### Calorie value

Fat gives **9 kcal per gram** — more than twice carbohydrate or protein. This makes fat useful to raise energy in small volumes (for children and underweight patients) and a problem in obesity.

### Functions of fats

1. Concentrated **source of energy**; stored fat is the body's energy reserve.
2. **Carries fat-soluble vitamins** A, D, E and K and helps their absorption (carotene is absorbed better when cooked with a little oil).
3. Supplies **essential fatty acids** — needed for cell membranes, brain and retina (DHA), and for making prostaglandins.
4. **Insulation** — fat under the skin keeps the body warm.
5. **Protection** — fat pads around kidneys, heart and eyes protect them.
6. **Satiety** — fat slows stomach emptying, so meals keep you full longer.
7. **Taste and texture** of food.
8. **Structural** — phospholipids and cholesterol are part of every cell membrane; cholesterol forms bile acids, vitamin D and steroid hormones.

### Dietary sources

- **Visible fats**: cooking oils (groundnut, mustard, sunflower, rice bran, sesame, coconut, soybean), ghee, butter, vanaspati.
- **Invisible fats**: nuts and oilseeds, whole milk and milk products, egg yolk, meat, fish, and small amounts in cereals and pulses.
- **Cholesterol** is only in animal foods: egg yolk, organ meats, butter, ghee, full-cream milk. Plant oils have no cholesterol.

### Fat requirement (ICMR-NIN 2020)

- **Visible fat (cooking oil and ghee): 20–50 g per person per day** (about 4–10 teaspoons), depending on energy need, physical activity and physiological state. For a 2000 kcal diet, not more than about **27 g** of visible fat a day.
- Enough nuts, oilseeds and diverse foods to supply about **6.6 g of n-6 PUFA and 2.2 g of n-3 PUFA** a day.
- Use a **combination of oils** (e.g. groundnut or rice bran with mustard or soybean) rather than one oil, to balance the fatty acids; avoid reheating oil; limit vanaspati, bakery and fried foods (trans fats), and ghee and butter.

### Effects of excess and deficiency

- Excess: obesity, raised blood cholesterol, atherosclerosis and coronary heart disease, fatty liver, gall stones; some cancers.
- Deficiency: low energy intake and weight loss, deficiency of fat-soluble vitamins, essential fatty acid deficiency (dry rough skin, poor growth).

## 2.6 Comparing the three macronutrients

| Feature | Carbohydrate | Protein | Fat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Elements | C, H, O | C, H, O, N (S, P) | C, H, O |
| Unit | Glucose (monosaccharides) | Amino acids | Fatty acids + glycerol |
| kcal/g | 4 | 4 | 9 |
| Main job | Energy | Growth and repair | Concentrated energy, fat-soluble vitamins |
| Storage in body | Glycogen (small) | No true store (muscle protein used in starvation) | Adipose tissue (large) |
| Key sources | Cereals, tubers, sugar | Pulses, milk, egg, meat, fish | Oils, ghee, nuts |
| Requirement basis | Rest of energy need | 0.83 g/kg/day (RDA) | Visible fat 20–50 g/day |

## 2.7 Nursing application

- Count calories in simple terms for patients: oil and fried snacks add many calories in a small volume.
- For an underweight or post-operative patient, raise energy and protein by adding milk, curd, egg, dal, groundnut and a little oil or ghee to small frequent meals.
- Teach cereal + pulse combinations to vegetarian families to improve protein quality cheaply.
- Fever, burns and sepsis raise BMR — plan more calories and protein and monitor weight.
- Teach heart-friendly fat use: mixed oils, no reheating, less vanaspati and bakery foods.

## Remember

- 1 kcal = 4.184 kJ; carbohydrate 4, protein 4, fat 9, alcohol 7 kcal/g.
- Carbohydrates = starches, sugars, cellulose; glucose is the brain's main fuel; carbohydrate spares protein and prevents ketosis.
- BMR: energy at complete rest; raised by thyroxine, fever (about 13% per °C), growth, pregnancy, cold, lean mass; lowered by starvation, old age, hypothyroidism, sleep.
- ICMR-NIN 2020 energy: man 2110/2710/3470; woman 1660/2130/2720 kcal (sedentary/moderate/heavy); pregnancy +350; lactation +600 then +520.
- Eight essential amino acids: isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan, valine (+ histidine in infants).
- Cereals lack lysine, pulses lack methionine — combine them.
- Protein RDA 0.83 g/kg/day (EAR 0.66): man 54 g, woman 45.7 g; pregnancy +9.5 g (2nd) and +22 g (3rd trimester).
- Visible fat 20–50 g/day; EFAs = linoleic (n-6) and alpha-linolenic (n-3); trans fats are the worst.

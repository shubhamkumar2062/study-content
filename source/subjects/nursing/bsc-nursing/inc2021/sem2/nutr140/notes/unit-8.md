# Unit 8: Nutritional assessment, nutrition education and national nutrition programmes

This notes file joins **INC Unit XI** Nutrition assessment and nutrition education (4 hours) and **INC Unit XII** National nutritional programmes and role of the nurse (3 hours). Both are community-facing: first find out who is malnourished and why, then teach and use the national programmes to correct it.

Programme details change often (names, beneficiary groups, schedules). The descriptions below give the structure that examiners ask about; always check the current guideline on the official site (MoHFW/NHM, Ministry of Women and Child Development, Ministry of Education, FSSAI) before quoting figures.

## 8.1 Nutritional assessment — objectives

**Nutritional assessment** is the measurement of the nutritional status of an individual or community using clinical, anthropometric, biochemical and dietary methods.

**Objectives**

1. To find out the **nutritional status** of individuals and groups.
2. To **detect malnutrition early** (under- and over-nutrition) and identify **at-risk groups**.
3. To find the **causes** (diet, disease, social factors).
4. To **plan nutritional care** or therapeutic diets for individuals.
5. To **plan, monitor and evaluate** nutrition programmes and policies.
6. To provide a **baseline** for research and surveillance (e.g. NFHS).

**Methods — "ABCD"**: **A**nthropometry, **B**iochemical (laboratory) assessment, **C**linical examination, **D**ietary assessment. Community assessment also uses vital statistics (infant and under-five mortality, low birth weight rate) and ecological factors (food production, prices, sanitation).

## 8.2 Clinical examination

A head-to-toe look for signs of nutritional deficiency. It is cheap and quick but signs appear late and many are not specific.

| Site | Sign | Possible deficiency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General | Wasting, oedema, apathy | PEM |
| Hair | Thin, sparse, easily plucked, flag sign, loss of colour | PEM (kwashiorkor) |
| Face | Moon face; old-man face | Kwashiorkor; marasmus |
| Eyes | Night blindness, conjunctival xerosis, Bitot's spots, keratomalacia | Vitamin A |
| Eyes | Pale conjunctiva | Anaemia (iron, folate, B12) |
| Lips | Angular stomatitis, cheilosis | Riboflavin (B2) |
| Tongue | Magenta, sore, smooth, red | B2, niacin, folate, B12 |
| Gums | Swollen, spongy, bleeding | Vitamin C |
| Teeth | Mottled enamel; caries | Excess fluoride; low fluoride |
| Neck | Enlarged thyroid | Iodine |
| Skin | Flaky paint dermatosis; pellagrous dermatitis; follicular hyperkeratosis; petechiae | PEM; niacin; vitamin A/EFA; vitamin C/K |
| Nails | Spoon-shaped (koilonychia) | Iron |
| Skeleton | Frontal bossing, rickety rosary, bow legs, pigeon chest | Vitamin D / calcium |
| Nervous system | Loss of ankle reflexes, calf tenderness, numbness; confusion | Thiamine; B12; niacin |
| Legs | Bilateral pitting oedema | Kwashiorkor, thiamine (wet beriberi) |

## 8.3 Anthropometry

Measurement of body size and composition. Simple, cheap, non-invasive, needs standard methods and calibrated equipment.

| Measure | How | Use and interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Weight** | Infant weighing scale / bathroom or beam scale, minimal clothing, scale at zero | Most sensitive to short-term change; plotted on growth chart (weight-for-age); birth weight below 2.5 kg = low birth weight |
| **Height / length** | Infantometer (lying length under 2 years), stadiometer (standing height, heels, buttocks, shoulders and back of head touching, looking straight ahead) | Height-for-age shows chronic malnutrition (stunting) |
| **Weight-for-height** | Compare with WHO standards | Wasting (acute malnutrition) |
| **Mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC)** | Left arm, midpoint between the tip of the shoulder (acromion) and the elbow (olecranon), arm hanging relaxed; tape snug, not tight | Children 6–59 months: below 11.5 cm = SAM; 11.5–12.5 = MAM; also used in adults and pregnant women |
| **Head circumference** | Largest circumference over the eyebrows and the back of the head | Brain growth in under-twos; small head in severe malnutrition |
| **Chest circumference** | At the nipple level | Head > chest after the first year suggests malnutrition |
| **Skinfold thickness** | Calipers at triceps, biceps, subscapular, suprailiac sites | Body fat stores |
| **BMI** | Weight (kg) ÷ height (m)² | Adults (Asian/Indian cut-offs): below 18.5 underweight; 18.5–22.9 normal; 23–24.9 overweight; 25 and above obese |
| **Waist circumference / waist–hip ratio** | Midway between the lowest rib and the iliac crest | Abdominal obesity; risk at 90 cm or more in men, 80 cm or more in women (Indian guidance) |

**Growth monitoring**: monthly weighing and plotting on the **WHO growth chart** in the Mother and Child Protection (MCP) card. A rising line parallel to the reference = good growth; a flat line = growth faltering; a falling line = danger.

**Worked BMI example**: weight 68 kg, height 1.60 m → 68 ÷ (1.60 × 1.60) = 68 ÷ 2.56 = **26.6 kg/m²** → obese by Asian/Indian cut-offs.

## 8.4 Laboratory and biochemical assessment

Detects deficiency **before** clinical signs appear (sub-clinical).

| Test | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Haemoglobin** | Anaemia (pregnant women and young children below 11 g/dL) |
| Peripheral smear, serum ferritin | Type of anaemia, iron stores |
| **Serum albumin, total protein** | Protein status (low in kwashiorkor, liver disease) |
| Serum retinol | Vitamin A |
| Serum calcium, phosphorus, alkaline phosphatase, vitamin D level | Rickets/osteomalacia |
| **Urinary iodine**, thyroid tests (TSH) | Iodine status |
| Blood glucose, HbA1c, lipid profile | Over-nutrition-related disease |
| Serum folate, B12 | Megaloblastic anaemia |
| Stool examination | Worm infestation (cause of anaemia) |
| Urine for albumin | Kidney disease, pre-eclampsia |

Limitations: costly, needs laboratories and trained staff, invasive (blood), results affected by infection and recent diet.

## 8.5 Assessment of dietary intake

| Method | How it works | Advantages | Limitations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **24-hour dietary recall** | Person recalls everything eaten and drunk in the previous 24 hours; amounts estimated with household measures; nutrients calculated using food composition tables | Quick, cheap, low burden, good for groups | Depends on memory; one day may not be typical; under- or over-reporting |
| **Food frequency questionnaire (FFQ)** | A list of foods with how often each is eaten (daily, weekly, monthly, rarely), sometimes with usual portion size | Shows **usual long-term pattern**; cheap; can be self-filled; links diet to disease | Gives approximate amounts only; food list must suit the local diet; memory-dependent |
| **Food diary / diet record** | Person writes all food and drink as eaten for 3–7 days | Accurate, no memory needed | Needs literacy and motivation; people may change their diet while recording |
| **Weighment method** | Raw foods used by a household (or an individual's portions) are weighed for a few days | Most accurate | Time-consuming, intrusive, needs trained staff |
| **Diet history** | Detailed interview on usual meal pattern | Gives habits | Long, needs skilled interviewer |
| **Food balance sheet / household budget survey** | National food availability per head | Population trends | Not individual intake |

**Steps for an FFQ in a nursing assessment**: list the main food groups and local foods; ask how often each is eaten and the usual portion; note cooking methods, snacks, beverages, junk food; identify gaps (no green vegetables, no milk) and excesses (fried foods, sugar); record.

### Writing a nutritional assessment report (lab exercise)

Include: identification data (age, sex, occupation, socio-economic status); anthropometry with interpretation; clinical signs; available lab values; 24-hour recall and FFQ findings with calculated energy and protein compared with RDA; food habits, beliefs and budget; nutritional diagnosis (e.g. "underweight with iron deficiency anaemia related to inadequate iron intake and tea with meals"); plan — diet modification, education, referral, follow-up.

## 8.6 Nutrition education

**Definition**: a process of teaching people about food and nutrition so that they **change their food habits** voluntarily and choose, prepare and eat a healthy diet within their resources.

**Purposes**

- Create **awareness** of the role of nutrition in health.
- **Change faulty food habits**, beliefs and taboos.
- Promote **breastfeeding and correct complementary feeding**.
- Teach **low-cost nutritious foods** and correct cooking methods.
- Prevent deficiency diseases and diet-related chronic diseases.
- Promote **use of services** — anganwadi, growth monitoring, IFA, vitamin A, iodised salt.
- Teach food hygiene and safety.

**Principles**

1. **Know the community** — its food habits, beliefs, foods available, income, literacy.
2. Start from **what people already know and do**; build on good practices.
3. **Simple, clear, practical messages** in the local language; few messages at a time.
4. Use **locally available, affordable foods**.
5. **Involve** the people (mothers, mothers-in-law, fathers, community leaders) — participation, not lecturing.
6. **Demonstrate** — "seeing and doing" works better than hearing.
7. **Repetition and reinforcement** through several channels.
8. Respect culture and religion; don't condemn.
9. **Motivate** by showing benefit (e.g. the child's weight gain on the growth chart).
10. **Evaluate** whether behaviour changed and follow up.

**Methods**

| Approach | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| **Individual** | Counselling at the bedside, antenatal clinic, home visits; growth chart counselling |
| **Group** | Talks and discussions in mothers' meetings, anganwadi, schools; **cooking demonstrations** of low-cost recipes; role play; Poshan Maah activities; Village Health, Sanitation and Nutrition Days (VHSND) |
| **Mass** | Radio, television, posters, wall paintings, newspapers, social media, campaigns (e.g. Eat Right India, Poshan Abhiyaan "Jan Andolan") |
| **Aids** | Charts, flash cards, flannel graphs, models of foods, food displays, puppet shows, leaflets, videos |

## 8.7 Nutritional problems in India

1. **Protein energy malnutrition / child undernutrition** — stunting, wasting, underweight (NFHS-5: about 35%, 19% and 32% of under-fives).
2. **Low birth weight** — linked to maternal undernutrition and anaemia.
3. **Nutritional anaemia** — the most widespread problem (about two-thirds of young children and over half of women of reproductive age in NFHS-5).
4. **Iodine deficiency disorders** — much reduced by iodised salt but not eliminated.
5. **Vitamin A deficiency** — sub-clinical deficiency persists.
6. **Vitamin D and calcium deficiency**, B12 and folate deficiency.
7. **Overweight, obesity and diet-related non-communicable diseases** — diabetes, hypertension, heart disease — rising in all groups (the **double burden**).
8. **Food adulteration and food safety** problems; **fluorosis** in high-fluoride areas; **lathyrism** (khesari dal) in pockets.

## 8.8 National Nutrition Policy

- **National Nutrition Policy, 1993** was adopted by the Government of India (Department of Women and Child Development) to tackle malnutrition through **direct interventions** (supplementary feeding, micronutrient supplementation, nutrition education) and **indirect, long-term measures** (food security, income, agriculture, health care, water and sanitation, women's empowerment), with action across many ministries.
- It was followed by the **National Plan of Action on Nutrition (1995)**, and in recent years by the **National Nutrition Strategy** (NITI Aayog) and **POSHAN Abhiyaan** (2018) with the goal of a "Kuposhan Mukt Bharat" (malnutrition-free India).
- The **National Food Security Act, 2013** gives a legal right to subsidised food grains through the Public Distribution System and supports ICDS and school meals.

## 8.9 National nutrition programmes

### Vitamin A supplementation (prophylaxis programme)

- Started in 1970 as the **National Prophylaxis Programme against Nutritional Blindness**; now part of child health services under the National Health Mission.
- **Children aged 9 months to 5 years** receive **periodic oral vitamin A doses** — the first with measles–rubella vaccination at 9 months, then every six months — as per the national schedule (doses set by the programme; follow the current NHM guideline).
- Supported by bi-annual supplementation rounds, promotion of breastfeeding and vitamin A-rich foods, treatment of xerophthalmia, measles immunisation.
- **Role of nurse**: give the dose as per schedule and record it in the MCP card; check eyes for Bitot's spots; teach mothers about green leafy and yellow vegetables; avoid duplicate dosing (excess is harmful).

### Anemia Mukt Bharat (AMB)

- Launched in **2018** under the National Health Mission (built on the earlier National Nutritional Anaemia Prophylaxis Programme of 1970, the National Iron Plus Initiative and WIFS).
- Uses a **6 × 6 × 6 strategy**: **six beneficiary groups, six interventions, six institutional mechanisms**.
- **Six beneficiary groups**: children 6–59 months; children 5–9 years; adolescents 10–19 years; pregnant women; lactating mothers; women of reproductive age (15–49 years).
- **Six interventions**: (1) **prophylactic iron-folic acid supplementation** for each group with age-specific products and schedules; (2) **periodic deworming**; (3) **intensified year-round behaviour change communication** (including delayed cord clamping and iron-rich diet); (4) **testing and treatment** of anaemia with digital haemoglobin meters ("Test, Treat and Talk"); (5) **iron- and folic acid-fortified foods** in government programmes (ICDS, PM POSHAN, public distribution); (6) addressing **non-nutritional causes** of anaemia in endemic areas (malaria, haemoglobinopathies, fluorosis).
- **Institutional mechanisms** include intra-ministerial coordination, a national AMB unit, a national centre of excellence on anaemia control, convergence with other ministries, supply chain strengthening, and an AMB dashboard/digital portal for monitoring.
- **Role of nurse**: screen haemoglobin; distribute IFA as per schedule and counsel on taking it; deworming; diet counselling; treat or refer per protocol; record and report on the AMB portal; school and community campaigns.

### Weekly Iron Folic Acid Supplementation (WIFS)

- Launched in **2012** by MoHFW for **adolescents (10–19 years)** — boys and girls in government and aided schools, and out-of-school girls through anganwadi centres.
- Components: **supervised weekly IFA tablet**, **biannual deworming**, screening and referral of anaemic adolescents, and information on improving diet and preventing worm infestation. It is now part of AMB's adolescent component.
- **Role of nurse / school health nurse**: organise the fixed weekly day, give the tablet after a meal under supervision, record compliance, manage minor side effects, deworm, educate.

### Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS)

- Started on **2 October 1975** under the Ministry of Women and Child Development; delivered through **anganwadi centres** by the **anganwadi worker (AWW)** and helper. Now run under **Mission Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0**.
- **Beneficiaries**: children 0–6 years, pregnant women and lactating mothers, adolescent girls (Scheme for Adolescent Girls in specified areas).
- **Six services**:

| Service | Beneficiaries | Provided by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. **Supplementary nutrition** (take-home ration for children 6 months–3 years and mothers; hot cooked meal and morning snack for 3–6 years) | Children 6 months–6 years, pregnant and lactating women | AWW |
| 2. **Pre-school non-formal education** | Children 3–6 years | AWW |
| 3. **Nutrition and health education** | Women 15–45 years | AWW, ANM |
| 4. **Immunisation** | Children, pregnant women | Health system (ANM) |
| 5. **Health check-up** | Children, pregnant and lactating women | ANM, Medical Officer |
| 6. **Referral services** | Sick and severely malnourished children, high-risk mothers | AWW, ANM, Medical Officer |

- **Role of nurse (ANM / community health nurse)**: immunisation, health check-ups and referral at the anganwadi; growth monitoring and identification of SAM; antenatal and postnatal care; training and supporting AWWs; nutrition education and Village Health Sanitation and Nutrition Days.

### Mid-day Meal Scheme (MDMS) → PM POSHAN

- Started nationally on 15 August 1995 as the National Programme of Nutritional Support to Primary Education; renamed **PM POSHAN (Pradhan Mantri Poshan Shakti Nirman)** in **2021** under the Ministry of Education and extended to pre-primary classes (balvatikas).
- Provides a **free hot cooked meal** on school days to children in **government and government-aided schools** (classes 1–8 and pre-primary). Nutritional norms (energy and protein per meal) are fixed by the scheme — check the current guideline.
- **Objectives**: improve nutritional status of school children, increase **enrolment, attendance and retention**, improve learning, promote social equity (children of all castes eating together), give nutrition support in drought and disasters. School nutrition gardens are encouraged.
- **Role of nurse / school health nurse**: check hygiene of the kitchen and cooks, safe water, food quality and tasting; health check-ups, deworming, IFA (WIFS), and vitamin A where applicable; health education to children; report food poisoning.

### National Iodine Deficiency Disorders Control Programme (NIDDCP)

- Started as the **National Goitre Control Programme in 1962**; renamed **NIDDCP in 1992** to cover the whole range of iodine deficiency disorders; now under NHM.
- **Objectives**: survey to assess the magnitude of IDD; **supply of iodised salt** in place of common salt (universal salt iodisation); resurvey to assess impact; **laboratory monitoring** of iodised salt and urinary iodine excretion; health education and publicity.
- Sale of non-iodised salt for direct human consumption is banned under food safety rules (with some exceptions). Iodine content standards are set at production and at consumer level (FSSAI) — check the current figures.
- Salt testing kits are used by ASHAs and health workers to check household salt.
- **Role of nurse**: teach use and correct storage of iodised salt; test household salt with a kit; identify goitre and refer; educate on IDD; promote **Global Iodine Deficiency Disorders Prevention Day (21 October)** activities.

| Programme | Key points |
| --- | --- |
| **POSHAN Abhiyaan (2018)** — now part of Mission Poshan 2.0 | Overarching national nutrition mission; convergence of ministries; real-time monitoring by Poshan Tracker app; Jan Andolan (people's movement); **Poshan Maah** (September) and Poshan Pakhwada |
| **National Deworming Day** | Albendazole tablets to children and adolescents (1–19 years) in schools and anganwadis, twice a year, as per the national schedule |
| **Mothers' Absolute Affection (MAA)** | Promotion of breastfeeding through health facilities and community |
| **Nutrition Rehabilitation Centres (NRCs)** | Facility-based care of SAM children under NHM |
| **Home-Based Newborn Care / Home-Based Young Child Care** | ASHA home visits including feeding counselling |
| **Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana (PMMVY)** | Cash incentive to pregnant and lactating women to compensate for wage loss and improve nutrition |
| **Food fortification** | Iodised and double-fortified salt, fortified rice in PDS/ICDS/PM POSHAN, fortified wheat flour, oil and milk; FSSAI "+F" logo |

| **Eat Right India (FSSAI)** | Safe, healthy and sustainable food; less salt, sugar and fat; trans-fat reduction |

## 8.10 Role of the nurse in national nutrition programmes — common points

1. **Identify** beneficiaries and at-risk groups through surveys and home visits.
2. **Assess** — growth monitoring, MUAC, Hb, clinical signs.
3. **Deliver services** — IFA, vitamin A, deworming, immunisation, referral of SAM to NRC.
4. **Educate and counsel** — breastfeeding, complementary feeding, iron-rich diets, iodised salt, food hygiene.
5. **Coordinate** with AWW, ASHA, teachers, panchayat members (convergence).
6. **Supervise and train** grassroots workers.
7. **Maintain records and reports**; use digital portals (MCP card, Poshan Tracker, AMB dashboard).
8. **Evaluate** outcomes and give feedback.
9. **Advocate** for vulnerable families to access food security schemes.

## Remember

- Assessment = ABCD: anthropometry, biochemical, clinical, dietary.
- MUAC midpoint of left upper arm; below 11.5 cm = SAM (6–59 months).
- Indian BMI cut-offs: normal 18.5–22.9; overweight 23–24.9; obese ≥ 25.
- FFQ = how often foods are eaten → usual pattern; 24-hour recall = yesterday's intake.
- Nutrition education: know the people, simple local messages, demonstrations, participation, evaluation.
- National Nutrition Policy 1993; NFSA 2013; POSHAN Abhiyaan 2018.
- AMB 2018: 6 × 6 × 6 (beneficiaries, interventions, institutional mechanisms).
- WIFS 2012: adolescents 10–19, weekly supervised IFA + biannual deworming.
- ICDS 2 Oct 1975: six services through anganwadi.
- MDM 1995 → PM POSHAN 2021: hot cooked meal in government and aided schools.
- NGCP 1962 → NIDDCP 1992: universal iodised salt.
- Vitamin A supplementation: 9 months to 5 years, first with measles–rubella vaccine, then six-monthly.

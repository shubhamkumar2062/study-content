# How to use this guide

Applied Nutrition and Dietetics is a second-semester subject of the INC 2021 B.Sc Nursing curriculum (3 credits, 60 hours: 45 hours theory and 15 hours lab). It is examined in a **combined university paper with Applied Biochemistry**: Applied Biochemistry is **Section A (25 marks)** and Applied Nutrition and Dietetics is **Section B (50 marks)** of one 3-hour, 75-mark paper. Nutrition carries two-thirds of that paper, so give it about two-thirds of your preparation time for the combined exam.

**How the units are arranged.** The INC syllabus has thirteen units, several of them only 2–3 hours. They are joined into eight notes files:

| Notes file | INC unit(s) | Hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit 1 Introduction to nutrition, nutrients and foods | I | 2 |
| Unit 2 Carbohydrates, energy, proteins and fats | II + III + IV | 3 + 3 + 2 |
| Unit 3 Vitamins and minerals | V + VI | 3 + 3 |
| Unit 4 Balanced diet and nutrition across the life cycle | VII | 7 + 8 lab |
| Unit 5 Nutritional deficiency disorders | VIII | 6 |
| Unit 6 Therapeutic diets | IX | 4 + 7 lab |
| Unit 7 Cookery rules, preservation of nutrients and food safety | X + XIII | 3 + 2 |
| Unit 8 Nutritional assessment, nutrition education and national programmes | XI + XII | 4 + 3 |

Study one notes file a day: read it, write the "Remember" list from memory, then answer the questions and MCQs for that unit without looking.

**Section B: Applied Nutrition and Dietetics (50 marks)** — as given in INC Regulations 2020, Appendix 3. There is no internal choice, so every question must be answered. Your university may print it differently; confirm with your university.

| Part | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MCQ | 8 multiple-choice questions | 8 × 1 = 8 |
| Essay | 1 long answer | 1 × 10 = 10 |
| Short | 4 short answers | 4 × 5 = 20 |
| Very short | 6 very short answers | 6 × 2 = 12 |
| **Total** | | **50** |

Section B takes about two-thirds of the 3-hour paper, so the mock test here is timed at 120 minutes. The university exam is 75 marks (Biochemistry 25 + Nutrition 50) with 25 internal marks. You must pass theory with at least 50%. The 15 lab hours (meal planning, balanced and therapeutic diets, low-cost dishes) are assessed internally; they are not a separate practical exam.

**About the numbers in these notes.** Energy, protein and micronutrient requirements are the **ICMR-NIN 2020** values (Nutrient Requirements for Indians — RDA and EAR). Many older textbooks still give the 2010 values (e.g. protein 1 g/kg, iron 17 and 21 mg); write the 2020 values but you may mention that they were revised. Where an official figure could not be confirmed, the notes describe the point without a number and tell you where to check. No drug doses are given; supplements in national programmes follow the current MoHFW/NHM schedule.

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — a favourite; comes up in most papers in some form. Learn the full answer with a table or flow diagram.
- **Medium** — appears every few papers or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — less common, but useful for MCQs and very short answers.

These ratings are estimates from syllabus weight and the usual style of nursing nutrition papers. They are not a promise of what will be asked.

**Where the marks usually come from**

| Notes file | Topics that keep coming back |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Definitions (nutrition, nutrient, malnutrition, balanced diet), classification of nutrients and foods, factors affecting nutrition |
| 2 | Functions and sources of carbohydrates, proteins and fats; essential amino acids; BMR and its factors; calorie values; essential fatty acids |
| 3 | Vitamin A, D, C, B1 — functions, sources, deficiency; iron, calcium, iodine; fat- vs water-soluble vitamins |
| 4 | Balanced diet and its principles; RDA uses and limitations; diet in pregnancy and lactation; anaemia in pregnancy; IYCF and complementary feeding; food exchange; dietary fibre |
| 5 | PEM (kwashiorkor vs marasmus, SAM criteria, ten steps), vitamin A deficiency (xerophthalmia grades), anaemia, IDD, rickets |
| 6 | Therapeutic diet definition and types; diet in diabetes, hypertension/CVD, renal failure, liver disease, diarrhoea, post-operative; NG feeding |
| 7 | Methods of cooking; preventing nutrient loss; adulteration and tests; PFA/FSSA; five keys to safer food; food preservation |
| 8 | ABCD of nutritional assessment; anthropometry (MUAC, BMI); nutrition education; ICDS; AMB; NIDDCP; PM POSHAN; WIFS; role of the nurse |

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. **Use tables** for comparisons — kwashiorkor vs marasmus, fat- vs water-soluble vitamins, cooking methods, ICDS services. A neat table earns marks quickly.
2. **For every nutrient answer use the same order**: definition → classification → functions → sources → requirement (ICMR-NIN 2020) → deficiency → excess. Examiners look for this structure.
3. **For every disease-diet answer use**: objectives → principles (what to give more, what to restrict) → foods allowed → foods to avoid → a short sample day menu → nurse's role.
4. **Give Indian, low-cost foods** — ragi, bajra, amla, guava, drumstick leaves, garden cress seeds, groundnut, sprouts, jaggery. Examiners like local examples.
5. **Learn the key numbers**: 4/4/9 kcal per gram; man 2110 and woman 1660 kcal (sedentary); protein 0.83 g/kg; pregnancy +350 kcal; lactation +600 kcal; SAM MUAC below 11.5 cm; anaemia in pregnancy Hb below 11 g/dL; Indian BMI obese at 25.
6. **Always end with the nurse's role** in deficiency and programme questions — assessment, delivery of services, education, referral, records.
7. **Very short answers (2 marks)**: one-line definition plus one example or fact.
8. **For programme questions** give: year started, ministry, beneficiaries, components/services, nurse's role. Learn ICDS's six services and AMB's 6 × 6 × 6.
9. MCQs come from small facts — read every "Remember" list the night before.

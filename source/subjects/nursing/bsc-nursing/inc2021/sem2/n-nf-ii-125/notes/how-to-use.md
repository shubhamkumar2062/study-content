# How to use this guide

Nursing Foundation II (including the 40-hour Health Assessment Module) is the second-semester core nursing course of the INC 2021 B.Sc Nursing curriculum: 6 credits (120 hours) of theory, 3 credits (120 hours) of skill lab and 4 credits (320 hours) of clinical practice in medical and surgical wards. It turns the basics of Nursing Foundation I into bedside skills — assessment, the nursing process, feeding, hygiene, elimination, oxygen, fluids, medicines, care of the unconscious and dying, and psychosocial care.

**One paper for Foundation I and II.** Nursing Foundation I (first semester) has no university exam of its own. At the end of the second semester you sit **one 3-hour, 75-mark university paper, "Nursing Foundations (I & II)"**, listed in the INC tables as "Nursing Foundations II including First Aid". Questions may come from **both** courses. The internal assessment (25 marks) is the average of the Foundation I and Foundation II internal marks. This pack covers the whole Foundation II syllabus and adds the **First Aid unit of Foundation I** (Unit 10 here), because the paper names first aid. Revise the other Foundation I topics — health and illness, history of nursing, communication, documentation, vital signs, infection control, comfort, pain, sleep, admission and discharge, mobility and patient education — from the Nursing Foundation I pack.

**How the units are arranged.** The INC syllabus for Foundation II has sixteen units. Small units are joined so that each notes file is a useful size:

Procedures are written as the steps you learn in the skill lab, each with its reason. Medication administration is taught as principles — the rights, routes, sites and the calculation method. The calculation examples use invented drugs and numbers for practice only; no drug doses are given anywhere in this pack.

**The university paper (75 marks, 3 hours)** — the pattern given in INC Regulations 2020, Appendix 3, for a full 75-mark paper. There is no internal choice: answer every question. Your university may print it differently; confirm with your university.

| Part | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MCQ | 12 multiple-choice questions | 12 × 1 = 12 |
| Essay / situation type | 2 long answers | 2 × 15 = 30 |
| Short | 5 short answers | 5 × 5 = 25 |
| Very short | 4 very short answers | 4 × 2 = 8 |
| **Total** | | **75** |

Theory total is 100 (75 university + 25 internal); you need at least 50% in theory. The practical exam (Foundation I & II together) is separate.

**Mock tests in this pack**

- **University paper (75 marks, 3 hours)** in the pattern above, drawn from the Foundation II questions and the first-aid questions here. For full practice, also answer a few Foundation I questions from that pack.
- **Quick MCQ test**: 20 questions in 20 minutes, for daily revision.

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — a favourite; comes up in most papers in some form. Learn the full answer.
- **Medium** — appears every few papers or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — less common, but useful for MCQs and very short answers.

These ratings are estimates from syllabus hours and the usual style of B.Sc Nursing fundamentals papers. They are not a promise of what will be asked.

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. **Write procedures as numbered steps with a reason for each** — "Position the patient in left lateral — the rectum and sigmoid colon lie on the left, so fluid flows in by gravity." Examiners give marks for the rationale as much as for the step.
2. Organise long procedure answers under: **definition → purposes/indications → contraindications → equipment → preparation of patient → steps with rationale → after-care → complications → recording and reporting**.
3. For **situation-type essays** ("Mr X, 65 years, admitted with…"), use the nursing process: assessment findings, two or three nursing diagnoses in PES form, goals, interventions with rationale, evaluation.
4. Learn the numbers examiners like: GCS 3–15 (≤ 8 coma); Braden 6–23 (≤ 18 at risk); Norton 5–20 (≤ 14 at risk); SpO2 95–100%; Na+ 135–145 and K+ 3.5–5.0 mEq/L; pH 7.35–7.45; urine 1–2 L a day; injection angles ID 10–15°, SC 45°, IM 90°; CPR 100–120/min, 5–6 cm, 30:2; burns cooled for 20 minutes.
5. **Show every calculation** with the formula, units and answer — even a correct number without working may lose marks.
6. Use tables for comparisons: medical vs nursing diagnosis, types of interventions, IV fluid types, transfusion reactions, stages of grief, IM sites.
7. Very short answers (2 marks): one clear definition plus one example or fact.
8. MCQs come from small facts and safety rules; read every "Remember" list the night before.

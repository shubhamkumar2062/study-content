# Unit 1: Health assessment

INC Unit I (20 hours theory + 20 hours skill lab, taught with the mandatory 40-hour Health Assessment Module): interview and observation techniques, purposes and process of health assessment, health history, methods of physical examination, preparing the patient and the unit, general and system-wise assessment, and documenting the findings.

## 1.1 What health assessment is

**Health assessment** is the systematic collection of information about a person's health status — physical, psychological, social and spiritual — through a health history and a physical examination. It is the first and most important step of the nursing process: every nursing diagnosis, plan and evaluation depends on how carefully the nurse assessed.

**Purposes**

1. To collect baseline data against which later changes are compared.
2. To identify actual and potential health problems and the patient's strengths.
3. To make nursing diagnoses and plan individual care.
4. To evaluate the response to care and treatment.
5. To detect early warning signs (for example a falling level of consciousness) and report them.
6. To screen healthy people (school health, antenatal, occupational check-ups) and promote health.
7. To build a trusting nurse–patient relationship through the interview.

**Types of assessment**

| Type | When | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comprehensive (initial) | On admission | Full history and head-to-toe examination |
| Focused (problem-oriented) | For one complaint | Chest assessment in a breathless patient |
| Ongoing (follow-up) | At set intervals | Hourly vital signs and wound check after surgery |
| Emergency | Life threat | Rapid airway, breathing, circulation check |

## 1.2 Interview techniques

The **interview** is a planned, purposeful conversation to obtain the health history.

**Phases of the interview**

1. **Preparatory (pre-interaction)**: read the records, plan the questions, choose a quiet private place and a time when the patient is comfortable.
2. **Introduction (orientation)**: greet the patient by name, introduce yourself, explain the purpose and how long it will take, assure confidentiality, get consent.
3. **Working phase**: collect the history using the techniques below.
4. **Termination**: summarise, ask "Is there anything else you want to tell me?", thank the patient and explain what happens next.

**Communication techniques used**

| Technique | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Open-ended questions | "Tell me about your pain." — gives a full picture in the patient's words |
| Closed questions | "Do you smoke?" — quick facts, yes/no answers |
| Clarification | "What do you mean by 'giddy'?" |
| Reflection | Repeating the feeling back: "You seem worried about the operation." |
| Paraphrasing and summarising | Checks that you have understood |
| Silence | Gives time to think and express feelings |
| Active listening | Eye contact (as culture allows), nodding, not interrupting |

Avoid leading questions ("You don't drink, do you?"), medical jargon, rushing, and giving false reassurance. Use an interpreter when the language differs, and take history from a relative (secondary source) when the patient cannot speak.

## 1.3 Observation techniques

**Observation** means gathering data with all the senses — looking, listening, touching and smelling — from the first contact. Observe:

- **General appearance**: build, posture, gait, grooming, facial expression, distress.
- **Behaviour and mood**: anxious, restless, withdrawn, confused.
- **Environment**: bedside articles, drains, IV lines, oxygen, smell in the room.
- **Non-verbal cues**: grimacing, guarding a part, clutching the chest.

Good observation is objective: describe what you saw ("patient holding the right lower abdomen, knees drawn up") rather than a judgement ("patient is dramatic").

## 1.4 The process of health assessment

```text
Prepare → Health history (subjective data) → Physical examination (objective data)
→ Review of records and test results → Organise and validate → Document and report
```

- **Subjective data**: what the patient says (symptoms such as pain, nausea, worry). Recorded in the patient's words where possible.
- **Objective data**: what the nurse observes or measures (pulse 110/min, wound discharge, Hb 9 g/dL).

## 1.5 The health history

| Part | What it includes |
| --- | --- |
| Biographic (demographic) data | Name, age, sex, address, religion, education, occupation, income, marital status, informant |
| Chief complaint | Main reason for seeking care, in the patient's words, with duration |
| History of present illness | Onset, location, duration, character, aggravating and relieving factors, associated symptoms, treatment taken (memory aid: OLDCART or PQRST for pain) |
| Past history | Earlier illnesses, operations, injuries, hospital stays, allergies, immunisations, blood transfusions |
| Family history | Diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, cancer, tuberculosis, mental illness, genetic disorders (a genogram helps) |
| Personal and lifestyle history | Diet, sleep, elimination, exercise, tobacco, alcohol and other substance use, hygiene |
| Socio-economic and environmental | Housing, water supply, sanitation, family support |
| Psychological and spiritual | Coping, stress, beliefs and practices that affect care |
| Menstrual and obstetric (women) | Menarche, cycle, LMP, gravida, para |
| Review of systems | Questions system by system from head to toe |

**PQRST for a symptom such as pain**: **P**rovoking/palliating factors, **Q**uality, **R**egion and radiation, **S**everity (0–10 scale), **T**ime (onset, duration, frequency).

## 1.6 Methods of physical examination

### Inspection
Systematic looking: colour, shape, size, symmetry, movement, swelling, lesions. Done first, in good daylight or bright white light, with the part exposed and compared with the other side.

### Palpation
Using the hands to feel texture, temperature, moisture, tenderness, masses, pulsations and organ size.

- **Light palpation** (about 1 cm depth): surface tenderness, muscle guarding.
- **Deep palpation** (about 4 cm, can be bimanual): organs and masses.
- Fingertips feel fine detail; the back (dorsum) of the hand feels temperature; the palm (ulnar surface) feels vibration (for example vocal fremitus).
- Warm the hands, keep nails short, and palpate tender areas last.

### Percussion
Tapping the body surface to produce sound and judge the density of tissue underneath.

- **Direct**: tapping the surface with a finger (for example over the sinuses).
- **Indirect (mediate)**: the middle finger of the non-dominant hand is placed firmly on the skin and its middle phalanx is struck with the tip of the middle finger of the other hand, with a loose wrist movement.

| Percussion note | Over | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resonant | Normal lung | Air-filled tissue |
| Hyper-resonant | Emphysema, pneumothorax | Too much air |
| Tympanic | Stomach, gas-filled bowel | Hollow organ with air |
| Dull | Liver, full bladder, consolidated lung | Solid or fluid-filled |
| Flat | Muscle, bone, large effusion | Very dense tissue |

### Auscultation
Listening with a stethoscope to heart, lung and bowel sounds and to blood vessels (bruits).

- **Diaphragm** (flat side): high-pitched sounds — normal breath sounds, bowel sounds, normal heart sounds S1 and S2. Press firmly.
- **Bell** (cup side): low-pitched sounds — extra heart sounds (S3, S4), murmurs, bruits. Rest lightly.
- Listen directly on the skin, in a quiet room, with warm chest piece.

### Olfaction
Smell can give clues:

| Odour | Possible cause |
| --- | --- |
| Fruity (acetone) breath | Diabetic ketoacidosis |
| Ammonia-like breath or skin | Kidney failure (uraemia) |
| Musty breath | Liver failure |
| Foul-smelling wound | Infection, anaerobes |
| Alcohol | Recent drinking |
| Faecal-smelling vomitus | Intestinal obstruction |

## 1.7 Preparation for examination

**Preparation of the unit (environment)**: warm, well-lit, private room or screened bed; examination table or bed at working height; hand-washing facility.

**Equipment tray**: thermometer, sphygmomanometer, stethoscope, watch with seconds hand, weighing machine and height scale, measuring tape, torch (penlight), tongue depressor, otoscope and ophthalmoscope (where used), tuning fork, percussion (patellar) hammer, Snellen chart, cotton wisp and safety pin for sensation, gloves, lubricant, specimen containers, drapes, kidney tray, record forms.

**Preparation of the patient**

1. Explain the procedure and get consent — reduces anxiety and gains co-operation.
2. Ask the patient to empty the bladder (and give a urine sample if required) — a full bladder is uncomfortable and interferes with abdominal examination.
3. Provide a gown and expose only the part being examined — maintains dignity and warmth.
4. A female chaperone is present when a male examines a female patient (and as per policy) — protects patient and examiner.
5. Position according to the area examined.

| Position | Used for |
| --- | --- |
| Sitting | Head, neck, back, chest, upper limbs |
| Supine | Abdomen, front of chest, breasts, peripheral pulses |
| Dorsal recumbent (supine, knees bent) | Abdomen (relaxes muscles), perineum |
| Lithotomy | Vaginal and perineal examination |
| Sims' (left lateral, upper knee drawn up) | Rectal and vaginal examination, enema |
| Prone | Back and spine, hip movement |
| Knee–chest (genupectoral) | Rectal examination |
| Standing | Gait, posture, hernia, varicose veins |

## 1.8 General assessment (general survey)

1. **Appearance**: apparent age compared with real age, distress, consciousness, nutrition, hygiene, dress.
2. **Body build and posture**; gait.
3. **Height, weight, BMI**, mid-upper-arm circumference if needed.
4. **Vital signs**: temperature, pulse, respiration, blood pressure, oxygen saturation and pain score.
5. **Signs of common general findings** — remember "**PICCLE**": **P**allor (lower palpebral conjunctiva, nail beds, palms), **I**cterus (sclera, under the tongue), **C**yanosis (central — tongue, lips; peripheral — nail beds), **C**lubbing (loss of the angle at the nail bed), **L**ymphadenopathy (neck, axilla, groin), **E**dema (pitting over the shin and ankle; sacrum in bed-bound patients).
6. **Mental status**: orientation to time, place and person, speech, mood, memory.

## 1.9 Assessment of each body system (head to toe)

### Skin, hair and nails
- Colour (pallor, cyanosis, jaundice, flushing), temperature, moisture, texture, turgor (pinch the skin over the sternum or forearm; slow return suggests dehydration), lesions, scars, pressure areas.
- Hair: distribution, lice, dandruff, hair loss. Nails: colour, clubbing, koilonychia (spoon nails in iron deficiency).

### Head and neck
- Skull shape, scalp, face symmetry (facial palsy).
- **Eyes**: eyelids, conjunctiva (pallor, redness), sclera (jaundice), pupils — size, shape, equality and reaction to light (**PERRLA**: pupils equal, round, reactive to light and accommodation), eye movements, visual acuity with a Snellen chart.
- **Ears**: pinna, discharge, wax, hearing (whisper test; Rinne and Weber tests with a tuning fork).
- **Nose**: deviated septum, discharge, patency of each nostril, sinus tenderness.
- **Mouth and throat**: lips, gums, teeth, dentures, tongue (coating, dryness), tonsils, breath odour.
- **Neck**: range of movement, thyroid enlargement, lymph nodes, position of trachea (central), jugular venous distension.

### Chest and lungs
- **Inspection**: shape (barrel chest, pigeon chest), symmetry of movement, rate, rhythm and depth of breathing, use of accessory muscles.
- **Palpation**: chest expansion, tenderness, tactile (vocal) fremitus.
- **Percussion**: resonant over normal lung; dull over fluid or consolidation.
- **Auscultation**: vesicular (normal, over most of the lung), bronchial (over the trachea), bronchovesicular; added sounds — crackles (fluid in small airways), wheeze (narrowed airways), rhonchi, pleural rub.

### Heart and blood vessels
- Apex beat: normally in the left 5th intercostal space at the midclavicular line.
- Auscultation areas:

```text
Aortic area      2nd right intercostal space, beside the sternum
Pulmonary area   2nd left intercostal space, beside the sternum
Erb's point      3rd left intercostal space, beside the sternum
Tricuspid area   4th–5th left intercostal space, lower left sternal border
Mitral area      5th left intercostal space, midclavicular line (apex)
```

- S1 ("lub") — closure of the mitral and tricuspid valves; S2 ("dub") — closure of the aortic and pulmonary valves. Murmurs and extra sounds are reported.
- Peripheral pulses (radial, brachial, carotid, femoral, popliteal, dorsalis pedis, posterior tibial), capillary refill (normally under about 2 seconds), varicose veins, calf tenderness.

### Breasts and axillae
Inspect for symmetry, skin dimpling, nipple retraction or discharge; palpate each quadrant and the axillary tail; teach breast self-examination.

### Abdomen
Order is changed: **inspection → auscultation → percussion → palpation**, because touching or tapping first can change bowel sounds.

- Inspection: contour (flat, distended), umbilicus, scars, visible peristalsis, veins.
- Auscultation: bowel sounds in all four quadrants (normally about 5–30 irregular gurgles per minute; listen for up to five minutes before calling them absent); bruits over the aorta and renal arteries.
- Percussion: tympany over bowel, dullness over liver and full bladder; shifting dullness for fluid (ascites).
- Palpation: light, then deep; liver, spleen and kidneys; tenderness, rebound tenderness, masses, bladder distension.

```text
         Right Upper | Left Upper
        ------------ + ------------   (umbilicus at the centre)
         Right Lower | Left Lower
```

### Genito-urinary and rectal
Done with privacy, chaperone and consent; look for discharge, swelling, lesions, haemorrhoids; ask about urine and bowel habits.

### Musculoskeletal
Posture, gait, joint swelling, deformity, range of motion, muscle strength (graded 0–5), muscle wasting.

### Neurological
- Level of consciousness (Glasgow Coma Scale — see Unit 8), orientation, memory, speech.
- Cranial nerves (simple tests such as smell, vision, eye movements, facial movement, hearing, swallowing, tongue movement).
- Motor: power, tone, coordination (finger–nose test).
- Sensory: touch, pain, temperature.
- Reflexes: biceps, triceps, knee jerk, ankle jerk, plantar response.

## 1.10 Documenting health assessment findings

- Record **promptly**, in ink or the electronic record, in the approved format (head-to-toe or system-wise).
- Write **facts, not opinions**; use measurements ("wound 3 cm × 2 cm, serous discharge").
- Use the patient's own words for subjective data, in quotation marks.
- Use only approved abbreviations; date, time and sign every entry.
- Record normal as well as abnormal findings ("breath sounds vesicular, no added sounds").
- Report abnormal findings (falling GCS, low SpO2, new chest pain) to the nurse in charge and doctor at once, and record that you reported.

## 1.11 Nursing points

- A good assessment is **systematic** (the same order each time), **complete**, **accurate**, **objective** and **respectful**.
- Always compare right and left sides.
- Be culturally sensitive: some patients prefer a nurse of the same sex.
- In the elderly allow more time, check hearing and vision first, and look carefully at skin and nutrition.
- In children, examine the least distressing parts first and leave the throat and ears till last.

## Remember

- Health assessment = health history (subjective) + physical examination (objective).
- Methods: inspection, palpation, percussion, auscultation, olfaction.
- Abdomen: inspection, auscultation, percussion, palpation.
- Percussion notes: resonant (lung), hyper-resonant (excess air), tympanic (stomach), dull (liver), flat (muscle).
- Diaphragm of stethoscope for high-pitched sounds; bell for low-pitched sounds.
- Apex beat: left 5th intercostal space, midclavicular line.
- PERRLA for pupils; PQRST for symptoms; PICCLE for general findings.
- Empty bladder, privacy, chaperone, warm hands, expose one part at a time.
- Document facts, measurements and the patient's words; report abnormal findings at once.

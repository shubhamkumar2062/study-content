# Unit 3: Nutritional needs and hygiene

INC Unit III (5 hours theory + 5 hours skill lab) — nutritional needs, special and therapeutic diets, care in dysphagia, anorexia, nausea and vomiting, and oral, enteral and parenteral feeding — joined with INC Unit IV (5 hours theory + 15 hours skill lab) — hygiene, care of skin, pressure points and pressure ulcers (Braden and Norton scales), perineal, oral, eye, ear and nose care and assistive devices.

## Part A: Nutritional needs

## 3.1 Importance of nutrition in patient care

Good nutrition promotes healing of wounds, resistance to infection, recovery of strength and normal growth. Poor nutrition delays healing, causes pressure ulcers, muscle wasting, anaemia and lowered immunity, and lengthens hospital stay. Many patients lose weight in hospital because of illness, fasting for tests, poor appetite and unappetising food — the nurse watches for this.

## 3.2 Factors affecting nutritional needs

| Factor | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Age and growth | Infants, adolescents, pregnant and lactating women need more per kg |
| Activity and occupation | Heavy workers need more energy |
| Illness | Fever, burns, infection and surgery raise needs; illness lowers appetite |
| Medicines | Some reduce appetite or cause nausea; some alter absorption |
| Socio-economic status | Poverty limits food choice |
| Culture and religion | Vegetarianism, fasting days, food taboos |
| Psychological state | Depression and anxiety reduce or increase intake |
| Oral and swallowing problems | Missing teeth, ill-fitting dentures, sore mouth, dysphagia |
| Alcohol and tobacco | Poor intake, vitamin deficiency |

## 3.3 Assessment of nutritional status

1. **Diet history**: 24-hour recall, food frequency, likes and dislikes, food allergies, appetite, chewing and swallowing.
2. **Anthropometry**: weight, height, **BMI** = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². For Asian Indian adults the cut-offs commonly used are: under 18.5 underweight, 18.5–22.9 normal, 23–24.9 overweight, 25 or more obese. Mid-upper-arm circumference, skinfold thickness and waist circumference also help.
3. **Clinical signs**: hair (dry, easily pulled), eyes (Bitot's spots in vitamin A deficiency), lips and tongue (angular stomatitis, glossitis in B-vitamin deficiency), gums (bleeding), nails (spoon-shaped in iron deficiency), skin, oedema, muscle wasting.
4. **Biochemical**: haemoglobin, serum albumin, total protein, blood glucose, lipid profile.
5. **Screening tools**: for example the Mini Nutritional Assessment for older people and the Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool (MUST).

## 3.4 Review of special and therapeutic diets

**Hospital diets by consistency**

| Diet | Contents | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clear liquid | Water, clear soups, strained fruit juice, coconut water, tea without milk | First feeds after surgery, before some tests |
| Full liquid | Clear liquids plus milk, curd drinks, custard, strained thin porridge | Progression after clear liquids, mouth problems |
| Soft | Easy to chew: khichdi, dalia, idli, mashed vegetables, eggs, curd | After surgery, chewing difficulty, elderly |
| Regular (normal) | Normal balanced diet | Patients with no restriction |

**Therapeutic diets (review from Nutrition)**: diabetic (controlled carbohydrate, high fibre), low-salt (hypertension, heart failure, oedema), low-fat (gall bladder, heart disease), high-protein high-calorie (burns, tuberculosis, malnutrition), low-protein (some kidney failures), high-fibre (constipation), low-residue (before bowel surgery), bland (peptic ulcer), purine-restricted (gout).

## 3.5 Care of patients with feeding problems

**Dysphagia (difficulty in swallowing)**
- Get a swallowing assessment before giving oral food after a stroke.
- Sit the patient upright (90° if possible) during and for 30 minutes after meals — gravity helps food go down and reduces aspiration.
- Give food of the advised texture (thickened fluids, soft pureed food), small amounts, with the chin slightly tucked down while swallowing.
- Allow enough time; check the mouth for pocketed food after meals; keep suction ready.
- Watch for coughing, choking, wet voice — signs of aspiration.

**Anorexia (loss of appetite)**
- Find the cause (pain, nausea, depression, medicines, smell).
- Give small frequent meals, favourite foods (within the diet), attractive service.
- Oral care before meals; a clean, odour-free environment; avoid procedures at meal time.

**Nausea and vomiting**
- Position on the side or sitting up to prevent aspiration; keep a kidney tray and tissues ready.
- Remove smells; give mouth wash after vomiting.
- Give sips of clear fluids, then dry foods such as toast or biscuits.
- Record the amount, colour and content of vomitus (blood, bile, coffee-ground material) and intake–output; watch for dehydration.
- Give anti-emetics as prescribed.

## 3.6 Meeting nutritional needs

### Oral feeding (assisting a patient to eat)
1. Offer bedpan or urinal and hand-washing before meals.
2. Provide oral care, dentures, glasses and hearing aid.
3. Position upright; protect clothing with a napkin.
4. Check the tray matches the prescribed diet.
5. Sit at eye level; let the patient choose the order; give small amounts and do not hurry.
6. For a visually impaired person use the **clock method** to describe where food is on the plate.
7. Record the amount eaten and fluids taken.

### Enteral feeding: nasogastric (or orogastric) tube

**Indications**: unconscious patients, dysphagia, oral or neck surgery, severe weakness, anorexia, premature babies (orogastric). Also used for gastric decompression and lavage.

**Equipment**: NG tube (Ryles tube) of suitable size, water-soluble lubricant, gloves, 50 mL syringe, pH indicator strips, adhesive tape, stethoscope, glass of water, kidney tray, feed at room temperature, measuring jug, towel.

**Procedure for insertion (main steps with reasons)**

1. Check the order, identify the patient, explain and get consent — co-operation makes passage easier.
2. Position in high Fowler's with the head slightly flexed — opens the oesophagus and closes the airway.
3. Measure the length: tip of the **nose → earlobe → xiphisternum (NEX)** and mark it — places the tip in the stomach.
4. Lubricate the tip with water-soluble jelly — reduces friction; oil-based lubricant could cause lipid pneumonia if aspirated.
5. Insert through the clearer nostril along the floor of the nose; ask the patient to swallow (sips of water if allowed) as the tube passes the pharynx.
6. Stop and withdraw if the patient coughs, chokes, becomes cyanosed or cannot speak — the tube may be in the larynx.
7. **Check position**: aspirate stomach contents and test with pH paper (a pH of 5.5 or below suggests the stomach); chest X-ray confirms when aspirate cannot be obtained or as per policy. The air-whoosh test and bubbling in water are not reliable.
8. Secure the tube with tape to the nose; note the mark at the nostril.

**Procedure for feeding**

1. Position at least 30–45° upright; keep it during feeding and for 30–60 minutes after — prevents reflux and aspiration.
2. Check tube position before every feed.
3. Aspirate and note the residual volume; act as per policy if large.
4. Flush with water (about 30 mL for adults) before and after the feed and after medicines — keeps the tube patent.
5. Pour the feed into a barrel (without plunger) held about 30–45 cm above the stomach and let it flow by gravity slowly; never force.
6. Pinch the tube when refilling — prevents air entering.
7. Give oral and nasal care; record type and amount of feed.

**Complications**: aspiration, tube blockage or displacement, diarrhoea, nasal erosion, nausea, fluid and electrolyte imbalance.

### Other enteral feeds
- **Types of feeds**: blenderised home-made feeds (milk, dal, rice water, juices strained) and commercial formulas (standard, high-protein, disease-specific).
- **Methods**: bolus (intermittent by syringe), intermittent by gravity, continuous by pump.
- **Gastrostomy** (tube into the stomach through the abdominal wall, often by endoscopy — PEG) and **jejunostomy** (into the jejunum): for long-term feeding, oesophageal obstruction or high aspiration risk. Care: clean the stoma daily, rotate the external disc if advised, check for leakage and infection, flush before and after use.

### Parenteral nutrition: Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN)
- **Meaning**: giving all nutrients (glucose, amino acids, lipids, electrolytes, vitamins, trace elements) directly into a vein, usually a central venous catheter because the solution is concentrated.
- **Indications**: non-functioning gut (obstruction, severe pancreatitis, short bowel), severe burns, prolonged inability to eat.
- **Nursing care**: strict aseptic technique for the line; infuse by pump at a constant rate; do not use the TPN line for other drugs or blood; monitor blood glucose regularly (high sugar is common), weight, intake and output, electrolytes and signs of infection; never stop TPN suddenly (rebound low sugar) — follow orders.

## Part B: Hygiene

## 3.7 Factors influencing hygienic practice

Culture and religion, socio-economic status, personal preference, knowledge, body image, developmental stage, physical condition (weakness, paralysis, pain), mental state, and availability of water and facilities.

## 3.8 Hygienic care: indications, purposes and effects of neglect

**Indications**: dependent patients — bedridden, unconscious, paralysed, post-operative, elderly, very ill.

**Purposes**: cleanliness, removal of sweat and micro-organisms, stimulation of circulation, comfort and relaxation, prevention of pressure ulcers and infection, observation of the skin, self-esteem.

**Effects of neglected care**: body odour, skin infections, pressure ulcers, pediculosis, dental caries, gum disease, parotitis, eye and ear infections, urinary infection, poor self-image and social withdrawal.

## 3.9 Care of the skin

**Bed bath (sponge bath)** — main steps

1. Explain, provide privacy, close windows, room warm; bring all articles (basins, warm water, soap, towels, washcloths, clean linen and clothes, comb, lotion, laundry bag).
2. Offer bedpan or urinal first.
3. Keep the patient covered with a bath blanket; expose only the part being washed.
4. Wash, rinse and dry in order from **clean to dirty**: eyes (plain water, inner to outer canthus), face, neck, arms (long strokes from wrist towards the axilla — helps venous return), hands, chest, abdomen, legs, feet, then back, and lastly the perineum (with fresh water and gloves).
5. Change water when it is cool or dirty.
6. Give back massage, dress the patient, change linen, comb hair, trim nails if needed.
7. Observe and record skin condition (redness, rashes, pressure areas).

**Feet and nail care**: soak, clean between toes, dry well, cut fingernails curved and toenails straight across. People with diabetes and poor circulation need special care — no cutting of corns, no hot water bottles.

**Hair care**: daily combing; hair wash in bed with a Kelly's pad or improvised trough; protect eyes and ears with cotton.

**Pediculosis (head lice) treatment**: identify lice and nits; wear gloves and protective apron; apply the prescribed medicated lotion as directed; comb with a fine-toothed comb; repeat as directed; wash or boil linen and combs; examine contacts.

## 3.10 Care of pressure points and pressure ulcers

A **pressure ulcer (pressure injury)** is localised damage to the skin and underlying tissue, usually over a bony prominence, caused by pressure, or pressure combined with shear.

**Pressure points**: in supine — occiput, scapulae, elbows, sacrum and coccyx, heels; in lateral — ear, shoulder, greater trochanter, knee, malleolus; in sitting — ischial tuberosities.

**Causes and risk factors**: unrelieved pressure, shear (sliding down in bed), friction, moisture (sweat, incontinence), immobility, poor nutrition, anaemia, low albumin, oedema, reduced sensation, old age, unconsciousness.

**Stages (NPIAP classification)**

| Stage | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Stage 1 | Intact skin with non-blanchable redness |
| Stage 2 | Partial-thickness skin loss with exposed dermis; pink wound bed or intact/ruptured blister |
| Stage 3 | Full-thickness skin loss; fat visible; slough may be present |
| Stage 4 | Full-thickness skin and tissue loss with exposed fascia, muscle, tendon or bone |
| Unstageable | Base covered by slough or eschar, depth cannot be seen |
| Deep tissue pressure injury | Intact or non-intact skin with persistent deep red, maroon or purple discolouration |

**Assessment scales**

| Scale | Items | Score range | At risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Braden** | Sensory perception, moisture, activity, mobility, nutrition (each 1–4), friction and shear (1–3) | 6–23 | 18 or less; lower score = higher risk (12 or less = high risk) |
| **Norton** | Physical condition, mental condition, activity, mobility, incontinence (each 1–4) | 5–20 | 14 or less; lower score = higher risk |

**Prevention**

1. Assess risk on admission and regularly.
2. Change position at least 2-hourly (more often if at high risk); 30° tilted side-lying; keep the head of the bed as low as the condition allows to reduce shear.
3. Use pressure-relieving devices: alternating-pressure air mattress, water or foam mattress, pillows to keep heels off the bed. Avoid ring (donut) cushions — they press on the circulation around.
4. Keep skin clean and dry; manage incontinence; use barrier cream.
5. Lift, do not drag — use draw sheet.
6. Keep linen dry and free of wrinkles and crumbs.
7. Good nutrition and hydration with enough protein.
8. Gentle back care; do not massage reddened bony areas (it damages the tissue further).

**Care of an existing pressure ulcer**: relieve pressure on it completely; clean with normal saline; dress as prescribed (moist wound healing); remove dead tissue as ordered; treat infection; improve nutrition; measure and document size, depth, stage and discharge regularly.

## 3.11 Perineal and meatal care

Done for patients with catheters, after delivery or surgery, and incontinence.

- Use gloves, warm water or prescribed solution, cotton swabs.
- **Female**: separate the labia; clean from front to back (pubis to anus), one swab per stroke, centre first then sides — prevents carrying bowel organisms to the urethra.
- **Male**: retract the foreskin (if not circumcised), clean the glans in circular movement from the meatus outward, then **replace the foreskin** — prevents paraphimosis.
- **Meatal (catheter) care**: clean the meatus and the first few centimetres of the catheter away from the meatus.

## 3.12 Oral care

**Purposes**: keep the mouth clean and moist, prevent infection (stomatitis, parotitis), remove bad breath, improve appetite.

**Self-care patients**: brush twice daily, floss, rinse.

**Special oral care (dependent or unconscious patients)**
1. Position on the side with the head turned (or low semi-Fowler's if conscious) — fluids run out, preventing aspiration.
2. Place a towel and kidney tray under the chin.
3. Use a padded tongue depressor or bite block to keep the mouth open; never put your fingers between the teeth.
4. Clean teeth, gums, palate and tongue with moist swabs held in an artery forceps, one swab per stroke, from the back forward.
5. Use only small amounts of fluid; suction if needed; never pour water into an unconscious patient's mouth.
6. Apply lip moisturiser; inspect for sores, bleeding, thrush; record.

**Denture care**: remove (upper first) with gauze; clean over a basin lined with a towel or water (prevents breaking if dropped); store in a labelled container of water when out of the mouth.

## 3.13 Care of eyes, ears and nose and assistive devices

- **Eyes**: clean with sterile normal saline swabs, inner to outer canthus, one swab per wipe. In the unconscious, keep eyelids closed and moist (as prescribed) to prevent corneal damage.
- **Eye glasses**: clean with water and a soft cloth, store in a labelled case.
- **Contact lenses**: wash hands, remove (soft lenses pinched off the eye, hard lenses with the lid technique), store in the right solution in a labelled case. Never leave them in an unconscious patient's eyes.
- **Ears**: clean the outer ear with a washcloth; do not insert buds or pins into the canal.
- **Hearing aid**: switch off when removing, clean with a dry cloth, store in a labelled box, check the battery; keep dry.
- **Nose**: clean crusts with moist cotton swabs; in a patient with an NG tube, clean around the tube and change tape to prevent pressure sores.

## Remember

- BMI = kg ÷ m²; Asian cut-offs: 18.5–22.9 normal, 23–24.9 overweight, 25+ obese.
- Diets by consistency: clear liquid → full liquid → soft → regular.
- NG tube length = Nose–Earlobe–Xiphisternum; confirm by aspirate pH 5.5 or below (or X-ray), never the "whoosh" test.
- Feed upright (30–45°), flush before and after, keep upright 30–60 minutes after.
- TPN: central line, aseptic, pump, monitor glucose, do not stop suddenly.
- Bed bath: clean to dirty; long strokes towards the heart.
- Braden 6–23 (≤18 at risk); Norton 5–20 (≤14 at risk); low score = high risk.
- Turn 2-hourly; no ring cushions; do not massage reddened bony points.
- Perineal care front to back; replace the foreskin.
- Unconscious oral care: side-lying, moist swabs, no pouring of water.

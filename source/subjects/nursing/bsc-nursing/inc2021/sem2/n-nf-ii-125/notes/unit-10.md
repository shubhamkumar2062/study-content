# Unit 10: First aid and emergencies

This unit is **Unit XIV of Nursing Foundation I** (First Aid, 20 hours theory + 20 hours skill lab, taught with the mandatory First Aid module of NDMA / Indian Red Cross Society). It is included here because the university paper "Nursing Foundations II including First Aid" examines Nursing Foundation I and II together. Steps follow standard first-aid principles as taught by the Indian Red Cross Society and international first-aid guidelines; local protocols may differ slightly — follow your institution's current version. First aid here never involves giving medicines; the first aider's job is to keep the person safe until medical help takes over.

## 10.1 Definition, aims, principles, scope and rules

**First aid** is the immediate help given to a sick or injured person, with materials at hand, until professional medical help arrives.

**Aims (the 3 Ps)**
1. **Preserve life**.
2. **Prevent** the condition from getting worse.
3. **Promote** recovery.

**Basic principles**
1. Ensure **safety** — of yourself, bystanders and the casualty (danger first).
2. Assess quickly and systematically; treat life-threatening problems first (**primary survey**).
3. Call for help early — national emergency **112**, ambulance **108** (in most states).
4. Do no further harm; work within your training.
5. Reassure the casualty; keep them warm and comfortable.
6. Arrange transport to hospital; hand over clearly.

**Scope**: injuries at home, road, school, workplace; sudden illness (fainting, seizures, heart attack, stroke, asthma); poisoning, bites, burns; disasters and community emergencies.

**Rules (golden rules)**
- Do first things first, quickly but calmly.
- Get consent if the person is conscious; act in their interest if unconscious.
- Use standard precautions (gloves or a barrier) for blood and body fluids.
- Do not give anything by mouth to an unconscious person, a person likely to need surgery, or a person who cannot swallow.
- Do not move a casualty unnecessarily, especially if spinal injury is possible.
- Do not remove embedded objects.
- Record what happened and what you did; hand over to the professional.

## 10.2 Primary survey: DR CAB

```text
D — Danger      Is the scene safe? Remove the danger or the casualty.
R — Response    Tap the shoulders and shout "Are you all right?"
                No response → shout for help; call 112/108; ask for an AED.
C — Circulation If not breathing normally → start chest compressions.
A — Airway      Head-tilt chin-lift (jaw thrust if spinal injury suspected).
B — Breathing   Look, listen and feel for no more than 10 seconds.
                Gasping is NOT normal breathing.
```

If the casualty is breathing normally but unconscious, place in the **recovery position** and keep checking breathing. Then do a **secondary survey** (head-to-toe check, history — what happened, symptoms, allergies, medicines, past illness, last meal).

## 10.3 Respiratory emergencies and basic CPR

### Adult basic life support (cardiopulmonary resuscitation)
1. Scene safe → unresponsive → call for help, call 112/108, send for an AED.
2. Open the airway and check for normal breathing (no more than 10 seconds).
3. Not breathing normally → **chest compressions**: heel of one hand on the **centre of the chest (lower half of the sternum)**, other hand on top, arms straight, shoulders over hands.
4. Compress **5–6 cm** deep, at **100–120 per minute**; allow the chest to recoil fully; minimise interruptions.
5. If trained and willing, give **30 compressions : 2 rescue breaths** (each breath over about 1 second, enough to make the chest rise). If not trained or unwilling, give **continuous chest compressions (hands-only CPR)**.
6. **AED**: switch on and follow the voice prompts; pads on bare chest (below the right collarbone and on the left side below the armpit); no one touches the casualty during analysis and shock; resume CPR at once after the shock.
7. Continue until the person breathes normally, help takes over, you are exhausted, or the scene becomes unsafe.

**Children and infants (trained rescuer)**: start with 5 rescue breaths if trained; compress at least one-third of the chest depth (about 4 cm in infants with two fingers or two thumbs; about 5 cm in children with one or two hands); ratio 30:2 (or 15:2 for trained health workers working in pairs, as per protocol).

### Choking (foreign body in the airway)

| Situation | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Mild (can cough, speak, breathe) | Encourage coughing; do not slap the back; stay with them |
| Severe adult or child over 1 year (cannot speak, breathe or cough effectively) | Up to **5 back blows** between the shoulder blades with the heel of the hand while the person leans forward; then up to **5 abdominal thrusts** (Heimlich manoeuvre) — fist above the navel below the breastbone, pull sharply inward and upward; alternate 5 and 5 |
| Pregnant or very obese | Use **chest thrusts** instead of abdominal thrusts |
| Infant under 1 year | Face-down along your forearm, head lower than body: **5 back blows**, then turn face-up: **5 chest thrusts** with two fingers; no abdominal thrusts |
| Becomes unconscious | Lower to the ground, call for help, start CPR; look in the mouth before breaths and remove a visible object; no blind finger sweeps |

### Other respiratory emergencies
- **Asthma attack**: sit upright leaning slightly forward, keep calm, help the person use their own reliever inhaler (as prescribed for them), loosen tight clothing, call for help if no improvement.
- **Drowning**: get the person out safely (reach or throw, do not go in unless trained); start with rescue breaths if trained, then CPR; keep warm.
- **Suffocation and strangulation**: remove the cause, open the airway, CPR if needed.
- **Smoke inhalation**: move to fresh air, check breathing, get help.

## 10.4 Wounds and haemorrhage

**Types of wounds**: abrasion (graze), incised (clean cut), laceration (torn, irregular), puncture (narrow, deep — nail, knife), avulsion (tissue torn away), amputation, crush, gunshot.

**Bleeding**: arterial (bright red, spurting), venous (dark red, steady flow), capillary (oozing). External or internal (suspect internal bleeding after blunt injury with signs of shock).

**First aid for external bleeding**
1. Wear gloves or use a barrier.
2. Apply **firm, direct pressure** over the wound with a clean pad or cloth (or with the hand if nothing else).
3. Keep pressing — if blood soaks through, place another pad on top and press harder; do not remove the first pad.
4. Apply a bandage firm enough to keep pressure, but check circulation beyond it.
5. For life-threatening bleeding from a limb not controlled by direct pressure, a **tourniquet** (commercial or improvised wide band) is applied above the wound by those trained; note the time.
6. Lay the casualty down, treat for shock, call 112/108.

**Special points**: **embedded object** — do not remove; press around it and pad on both sides. **Nosebleed** — sit up, lean forward, pinch the soft part of the nose for about 10 minutes, breathe through the mouth; do not tilt the head back. **Amputated part** — wrap in a clean moist cloth, put in a plastic bag, keep the bag on ice (do not put the part directly on ice), send with the casualty.

**Minor wounds**: wash with clean running water, cover with a clean dressing, advise tetanus protection.

## 10.5 Shock

**Shock** is a life-threatening failure of circulation in which tissues do not get enough oxygen. Causes: severe bleeding, fluid loss (burns, diarrhoea), heart failure, severe infection, severe allergy (anaphylaxis), spinal injury.

**Signs**: pale, cold, clammy skin; rapid weak pulse; rapid shallow breathing; thirst; anxiety, restlessness, then drowsiness; low blood pressure; reduced urine.

**First aid**
1. Treat the cause (control bleeding).
2. Lay the casualty flat; raise the legs if there is no injury to the legs, hips or spine (helps blood return to the vital organs).
3. Loosen tight clothing; keep warm with a blanket (do not overheat).
4. Nothing by mouth; reassure.
5. Monitor breathing and response; call 112/108; be ready to start CPR.

**Severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis)**: swelling of face and throat, wheeze, rash, collapse — call for help immediately, help the person use their own prescribed adrenaline auto-injector if they carry one, keep them sitting (breathing difficulty) or lying with legs raised (faint), and be ready for CPR.

## 10.6 Musculoskeletal injuries

**Fracture** — a break in a bone. Types: closed (simple), open (compound — bone breaks the skin), complicated (with damage to nerves, vessels or organs); greenstick (children), comminuted, impacted.

**Signs**: pain, swelling, bruising, deformity, abnormal movement, crepitus, loss of function, shortening.

**First aid for fractures**
1. Keep the casualty still; support the injured part in the position found.
2. Control bleeding of an open fracture with pressure **around** the wound and cover it with a sterile dressing; do not push bone ends back.
3. **Immobilise** — the joint above and the joint below — using splints (padded boards, rolled newspaper), slings or by bandaging to the body (arm to chest, leg to the other leg), with padding in hollows.
4. Check circulation beyond the bandage (colour, warmth, pulse).
5. Treat for shock; arrange transport.
6. Do not try to straighten the limb unless trained and circulation is lost.

**Dislocation**: a bone displaced from its joint — severe pain, deformity, loss of movement. Support in a comfortable position, do not try to put it back, apply a cold pack, immobilise and get medical help.

**Muscle injuries (sprains, strains)**: **RICE** — **R**est, **I**ce or cold pack wrapped in cloth (about 20 minutes at a time), **C**ompression with a crepe bandage, **E**levation; seek medical advice if severe.

**Head and spinal injuries**: suspect spinal injury after falls from a height, diving, road accidents. Keep the head and neck still in line with the body (manual in-line support), do not move unless in danger, call for help, monitor breathing. If the airway is at risk, use the jaw thrust and, if the casualty must be turned, a **log roll** with several helpers.

## 10.7 Transportation of injured persons

**Principles**: transport only after life-threatening conditions are dealt with and injuries are immobilised; choose the method by the injury, the distance and the number of helpers; keep the casualty under observation; move smoothly; protect yourself (bend knees, keep the back straight, lift with the legs).

| Method | When used |
| --- | --- |
| Human crutch | Casualty can walk with support (minor leg injury) |
| Cradle carry | Children and light adults |
| Pick-a-back | Conscious light casualty who can hold on |
| Fireman's lift | Unconscious light casualty, single rescuer (not with spinal injuries) |
| Drag (blanket or shoulder drag) | Removing quickly from danger (fire, traffic) |
| Two-handed and four-handed seat | Conscious casualty, two rescuers |
| Fore-and-aft carry | Two rescuers, casualty unable to help |
| Chair carry | Through narrow passages or stairs |
| Stretcher (improvised with blanket and poles) | Serious injuries, longer distances; spinal board for spinal injury |

Carry a stretcher feet first normally; head first up stairs and hills (keeps the head higher).

## 10.8 Unconsciousness

1. DR CAB; if breathing normally, put in the **recovery position**:

```text
Kneel beside the casualty → near arm at a right angle →
far hand against the near cheek → bend the far knee →
pull on the knee to roll the casualty towards you →
tilt the head back to keep the airway open → upper leg at right angles
```

2. Check for injuries, medical bracelets; loosen tight clothing; keep warm.
3. Nothing by mouth; monitor breathing until help arrives.

**Fainting (syncope)**: lay the person down and raise the legs; loosen clothing; fresh air; recovers in a minute or so — if not, treat as unconscious.

**Seizures (fits)**: protect from injury (move hard objects away, cushion the head); do not restrain; **put nothing in the mouth**; note the time; after the jerking stops, place in the recovery position; call for help if the fit lasts more than 5 minutes, repeats, or it is the first fit, or the person is injured or pregnant.

**Low blood sugar in a known diabetic who is conscious and can swallow**: give sugar, a sweet drink or glucose; if unconscious, nothing by mouth — recovery position and call for help.

**Stroke**: recognise with **FAST** — **F**ace drooping, **A**rm weakness, **S**peech difficulty, **T**ime to call for help. Note the time symptoms started.

**Heart attack**: chest pain spreading to the arm, jaw or back, sweating, breathlessness — sit comfortably (half-sitting), keep calm, call for help immediately, be ready for CPR.

## 10.9 Foreign bodies

| Site | First aid |
| --- | --- |
| **Skin** (splinter, thorn) | Remove a small superficial splinter with clean tweezers in line with entry, clean and cover; leave deep or large objects in place and pad around them |
| **Eye** | Do not rub; for loose particles, blink, wash with clean water from the inner corner outward, or lift with a moist cloth corner from the white of the eye; for embedded objects or chemicals — do not remove the object; cover the eye lightly (both eyes if possible to limit movement) and get help; chemicals — irrigate with plenty of clean water for a long time |
| **Ear** | Do not probe; tilt the head to let the object fall out; a live insect may come out if the ear is turned towards a light or with gentle flooding of lukewarm water only if the eardrum is known to be intact — otherwise seek help |
| **Nose** | Breathe through the mouth; close the unaffected nostril and blow gently; do not probe; seek help |
| **Throat** | See choking (10.3); for a fish bone, do not try to push it down — seek medical help |
| **Stomach (swallowed objects)** | Most small smooth objects pass; do not induce vomiting; seek medical advice — urgent for button batteries, magnets, sharp objects, or if pain, vomiting or drooling occurs |

## 10.10 Burns and scalds

**Burns** are caused by dry heat, flames, chemicals, electricity, radiation or friction; **scalds** by hot liquids or steam.

**Depth**: superficial (epidermis — red, painful, no blisters), partial thickness (blisters, very painful), full thickness (white, brown or charred, leathery, may be painless).

**Extent — Rule of Nines (adult)**: head and neck 9%, each upper limb 9%, front of trunk 18%, back of trunk 18%, each lower limb 18%, perineum 1%. The casualty's palm with fingers is about 1% of body surface (useful for small, scattered burns). Children have proportionally larger heads, so a different chart is used.

**First aid for heat burns and scalds**
1. **Stop the burning**: "Stop, drop and roll" for clothes on fire; smother with a blanket; remove from the source.
2. **Cool the burn with cool running water for at least 20 minutes** as soon as possible — reduces pain, depth and swelling. Keep the rest of the body warm (risk of hypothermia, especially in children).
3. Remove rings, watches and tight clothing before swelling starts — but not clothing stuck to the burn.
4. **Cover** loosely with cling film (laid lengthwise, not wrapped tightly round a limb) or a clean, non-fluffy cloth.
5. Do **not** apply ice, toothpaste, butter, oil, ointments or turmeric, and do not burst blisters.
6. Seek medical help for burns larger than the casualty's palm, deep burns, burns of the face, hands, feet, genitals or joints, burns in children and the elderly, chemical and electrical burns, and any breathing difficulty (airway burns from smoke).

**Chemical burns**: protect yourself; brush off dry powder; flood with plenty of running water for a long time; remove contaminated clothing.

**Electrical burns**: **switch off the current** before touching the casualty (or push away with a dry wooden object while standing on dry material); check breathing and start CPR if needed; cool entry and exit burns; always send to hospital (internal damage and heart rhythm problems).

## 10.11 Poisoning, bites and stings

### Poisoning
Poisons can be swallowed, inhaled, injected or absorbed through the skin.

1. Ensure your safety (gas, chemicals).
2. Find out what, how much and when; keep the container, label or vomit to show the doctor.
3. **Do not induce vomiting** — corrosives burn twice, and the person may inhale vomit.
4. For corrosives with a conscious casualty, rinse the mouth; do not try to neutralise.
5. Inhaled poisons (smoke, gas): move to fresh air if safe; open windows; no flames.
6. Skin contamination: remove clothes, wash with plenty of water. Eyes: irrigate.
7. If unconscious: recovery position, monitor breathing, CPR if needed (use a barrier — avoid mouth-to-mouth if the poison is on the lips).
8. Call 112/108 and take the casualty to hospital; poison information centres can advise.
9. Suspected pesticide (organophosphate) poisoning is common in India — remove contaminated clothes, wash the skin, keep the airway clear and transport quickly.

### Snake bite
**Do**: reassure (most bites are by non-venomous snakes, and calm reduces spread), keep the casualty still, **immobilise the bitten limb like a fracture** with a splint, remove rings, bangles and tight clothes, keep the limb at about heart level, note the time and the snake's appearance (do not try to catch or kill it), and **take the person to hospital quickly** where anti-snake venom is available.

**Do not**: tie a tight tourniquet, cut or suck the wound, apply ice, chemicals or herbal pastes, give alcohol, or waste time on traditional treatments.

(A memory aid used in Indian training: **Do it R.I.G.H.T.** — **R**eassure, **I**mmobilise, **G**o to hospital, **T**ell the doctor about symptoms on the way.)

### Dog and other animal bites
Wash the wound **immediately and thoroughly with soap and running water for about 15 minutes** — this greatly reduces the risk of rabies; do not apply chillies, oil or herbs; do not suture or tightly bandage; go to a health facility the same day for **anti-rabies vaccination** (and immunoglobulin when advised) and tetanus protection; observe the animal if possible.

### Insect stings (bees, wasps) and scorpion bites
- Bee sting: scrape the sting out sideways with a fingernail or card (squeezing injects more venom); wash; apply a cold pack; watch for anaphylaxis (multiple stings or stings in the mouth are dangerous).
- Scorpion sting: reassure, keep still, cold compress, take to hospital promptly (scorpion stings can be serious in children).

## 10.12 Frostbite and effects of heat

**Frostbite** (freezing of tissue — fingers, toes, ears, nose; seen in high-altitude regions): move to a warm place; remove wet clothing and rings; rewarm the part in warm (not hot) water, about body temperature, or by skin-to-skin contact; **do not rub** (damages tissue); do not rewarm if there is a chance of refreezing; cover loosely with dry dressings; get medical help. Treat **hypothermia** first if present — warm the whole body gradually with blankets and warm sweet drinks if conscious.

**Effects of heat**

| Condition | Signs | First aid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Heat cramps** | Painful muscle cramps after exertion in heat | Rest in shade, ORS or salted drinks, gentle stretching |
| **Heat exhaustion** | Heavy sweating, pale clammy skin, headache, dizziness, nausea, weak rapid pulse | Move to a cool place, lie down with legs raised, loosen clothing, sips of ORS or water, sponge with cool water; seek help if not better soon |
| **Heat stroke** (emergency) | Very high body temperature, hot dry (or sweaty) skin, confusion, seizures, unconsciousness | **Call 112/108**; move to shade; remove outer clothing; **cool rapidly** — wet the body with cool water and fan, or cold wet cloths/ice packs in the neck, armpits and groin; nothing by mouth if confused; recovery position if unconscious |

Prevention (as in national heat action plans): drink enough water, avoid strenuous work in the hottest hours, wear light loose clothing, check on the elderly and children.

## 10.13 Community emergencies

Floods, earthquakes, cyclones, landslides, fires, building collapse, stampedes, mass road accidents, industrial (chemical) accidents and epidemics.

**Role of the first aider and nurse**
1. **Preparedness**: know the disaster plan, emergency numbers, evacuation routes; keep a first-aid kit and emergency supplies; take part in mock drills (NDMA guidance).
2. **Scene safety and alerting**: do not rush in; report to 112/108, police, fire (101) and the disaster response team.
3. **Triage** — sorting casualties by urgency so that the most lives are saved. A common colour system:

| Colour | Category | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Red | Immediate — life-threatening but treatable | Airway problem, severe bleeding, shock |
| Yellow | Delayed — serious but stable | Fractures without shock |
| Green | Minor — "walking wounded" | Small cuts, bruises |
| Black | Dead or not expected to survive with available resources | No breathing after airway opened |

4. **First aid** for the injured, psychological first aid (listen, comfort, link to help), protection of vulnerable groups.
5. **Fire**: raise the alarm, evacuate (crawl low under smoke), use the stairs not the lift, use the correct extinguisher (PASS — Pull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep) only if safe; "stop, drop and roll" if clothes catch fire.
6. **Earthquake**: "drop, cover and hold on" indoors; stay away from windows; after shaking stops, move out carefully to an open area.
7. **Floods**: move to higher ground; avoid walking or driving through flood water; safe drinking water and food; prevent water-borne disease.
8. Record and hand over; support community recovery and rehabilitation.

## 10.14 The first-aid box (contents)

Sterile dressings and gauze pads, adhesive dressings of various sizes, roller and crepe bandages, triangular bandages, adhesive tape, cotton wool, safety pins, scissors, tweezers, disposable gloves, a face shield for rescue breaths, a thermometer, a torch, a clean cloth or cling film for burns, an eye pad, a cold pack, ORS packets, a notebook and pencil, and a list of emergency numbers. (Medicines are kept only as per the institution's policy and given by authorised persons.)

## Remember

- Aims: preserve life, prevent worsening, promote recovery; danger first; call 112 / 108.
- DR CAB: Danger, Response, Circulation (compressions), Airway, Breathing — check breathing for no more than 10 seconds.
- CPR: centre of chest, 5–6 cm, 100–120/min, full recoil, 30:2 or hands-only; AED as soon as available.
- Choking (adult): 5 back blows + 5 abdominal thrusts; infant: 5 back blows + 5 chest thrusts.
- Bleeding: firm direct pressure; tourniquet only for life-threatening limb bleeding.
- Shock: lie flat, legs up (if no injury), keep warm, nothing by mouth.
- Fracture: immobilise the joint above and below; RICE for sprains.
- Burns: cool running water for at least 20 minutes; cover with cling film; no ice, oil or toothpaste; Rule of Nines.
- Poisoning: do not induce vomiting; keep the container.
- Snake bite: reassure, immobilise, rush to hospital; no tourniquet, cutting or sucking. Dog bite: wash 15 minutes with soap and water, get anti-rabies vaccine.
- Heat stroke: rapid cooling and emergency help; frostbite: warm water, never rub.
- Triage: red immediate, yellow delayed, green minor, black dead or expectant.

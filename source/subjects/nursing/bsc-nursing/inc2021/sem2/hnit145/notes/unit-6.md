# Unit 6: eHealth, public health informatics and using information in healthcare management

This notes file joins two INC units: **Unit VII, eHealth: Patients and the Internet** (3 hours theory) and **Unit VIII, Using Information in Healthcare Management** (3 hours theory, 5 hours lab). The first half covers how information and communication technology (ICT) improves personal and public health care; the second covers the nursing information system and how health care data are evaluated, analysed and presented to inform management decisions.

## Part A: eHealth — patients and the internet

## 6.1 Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **eHealth** | The use of information and communication technologies for health (WHO's usage): records, telehealth, mobile health, e-learning, online information |
| **Telehealth** | Delivery of health services, education and information at a distance using ICT; broader than telemedicine |
| **Telemedicine** | Clinical care (consultation, diagnosis, treatment advice) at a distance |
| **Telenursing** | Nursing care at a distance: telephone triage, follow-up calls, home monitoring, counselling |
| **mHealth** | Health services and information delivered through mobile phones and other wireless devices |
| **Digital health** | Umbrella term covering eHealth, mHealth, wearables, data analytics and related technologies |

## 6.2 Using ICT to improve personal health care

| Technology | Examples of use |
| --- | --- |
| **Telemedicine/teleconsultation** | Video, audio or text consultation; specialist advice to a rural centre |
| **Remote patient monitoring** | Home BP, glucose, oxygen saturation sent to the care team; heart failure and diabetes follow-up |
| **Mobile apps** | Medicine reminders, diet and exercise logs, pregnancy trackers, mental health apps |
| **Wearables** | Step counters, heart-rate and rhythm monitors, fall detectors |
| **Patient portals and PHR apps** | View lab reports, discharge summaries, book appointments (ABHA-linked PHR apps) |
| **SMS and voice messages** | Appointment reminders; health messages to pregnant women and mothers |
| **Online health information** | Disease information, self-care videos, support groups |
| **Social media** | Health campaigns, peer support (with risk of misinformation) |

**Modes of telemedicine**

```text
  Synchronous (real time)      → video or audio call, live consultation
  Asynchronous (store-and-forward) → photos, ECG, reports sent and reviewed later
  Remote monitoring            → continuous or periodic data from devices
```

## 6.3 Using ICT to improve public health care

- **Disease surveillance**: early warning of outbreaks.
- **Health education campaigns** via mass SMS, social media and websites.
- **Programme monitoring**: immunisation, maternal and child health, TB, non-communicable diseases.
- **Supply chain**: tracking vaccines and medicines.
- **Training health workers** through e-learning and tele-mentoring.
- **Geographic information systems (GIS)**: mapping disease cases, health facilities and high-risk areas.

| Initiative | Purpose |

| **HMIS portal** | Facility-wise monthly reporting of service data under NHM |

## 6.5 Benefits and challenges of eHealth

| Benefits | Challenges |
| --- | --- |
| Access for rural and remote people | **Digital divide**: no phone, internet or skills, especially among the elderly and the poor |
| Saves travel time and cost | Low **e-health literacy**; misinformation online |
| Continuity of care and follow-up | Limited physical examination in teleconsultation |
| Patient empowerment and self-care | Privacy and security of data |
| Faster specialist opinion | Network and power problems |
| Data for planning | Legal and ethical questions, unclear accountability |

**e-health literacy** is the ability to seek, find, understand and appraise health information from electronic sources and apply it. Nurses should teach patients to check the source and date of information and to discuss it with a health professional.

## 6.6 Public health informatics and the role of nurses

**Public health informatics** is the systematic application of information science and technology to public health practice, research and learning, focusing on **populations** rather than individual patients.

**Functions**: surveillance and outbreak detection; disease and immunisation registries; programme monitoring and evaluation; resource planning; health promotion; emergency response.

**Role of nurses (especially community health nurses, ANMs, CHOs)**

- Collect accurate population data (household surveys, eligible-couple registers, immunisation records) and enter it in national portals.
- Report notifiable diseases and unusual clusters promptly.
- Use data for local action: identify drop-outs from immunisation or ANC, high-risk mothers, TB patients missing treatment.
- Provide teleconsultation support at health and wellness centres.
- Teach the community to use health apps and ABHA safely.
- Protect the confidentiality of community data.

## Part B: Using information in healthcare management

## 6.7 Nursing information system (NIS)

A **nursing information system** is the part of the hospital information system that supports nursing practice, nursing administration, education and research by managing nursing data.

**Components**

| Component | Functions |
| --- | --- |
| **Clinical (patient care) component** | Admission assessment, nursing history, vital signs and charts, nursing diagnoses, care plans, nursing notes, eMAR and barcode administration, intake–output, risk scores (falls, pressure injury), patient education records, handover, discharge planning |
| **Administrative (management) component** | Staffing and scheduling, patient classification and **acuity/workload** measurement, leave and attendance, bed management, quality indicators, incident reports, supplies and inventory, budgets |
| **Education component** | Staff training records, competency checks, e-learning, policies and procedures |
| **Research component** | Data retrieval for audits and studies, nursing-sensitive outcome data |

**Characteristics of a good NIS**: easy to use, fits the nursing process, uses standard nursing terms, available at the bedside, integrated with other modules, secure, gives useful reports.

**Benefits**: less time on paperwork, legible and complete notes, fewer errors, better communication, objective staffing decisions, data showing nursing's contribution to outcomes.

## 6.8 Healthcare data for management decisions

Managers turn data into decisions through a cycle:

```text
  Collect ─► Validate/clean ─► Analyse ─► Present ─► Interpret ─► Decide ─► Act ─► Evaluate
     ▲                                                                              │
     └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Evaluation of data quality

Before using data, check that it is **accurate, complete, timely, consistent, valid and reliable**. Look for missing entries, impossible values (pulse 750) and double counting.

### Common indicators and how they are worked out

| Indicator | Formula | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bed occupancy rate** | (Inpatient days in a period ÷ (available beds × days in the period)) × 100 | Planning beds and staff |
| **Average length of stay (ALOS)** | Inpatient days ÷ number of discharges (including deaths) | Efficiency |
| **Bed turnover rate** | Number of discharges ÷ number of beds | How often a bed is reused |
| **Nurse–patient ratio** | Nurses on duty : patients | Staffing |
| **Nursing hours per patient day** | Total nursing hours ÷ patient days | Workload |
| **Fall rate** | (Number of falls ÷ patient days) × 1000 | Safety |
| **Pressure injury incidence** | New pressure injuries ÷ patients at risk × 100 | Quality of nursing care |
| **Health care-associated infection rate** | e.g. catheter-associated UTIs per 1000 catheter days | Infection control |
| **Medication error rate** | Errors reported per 1000 doses or patient days | Safety |
| **Patient satisfaction score** | From surveys | Quality |

**Worked example**: a 30-bed ward had 810 inpatient days in a 30-day month. Bed occupancy = 810 ÷ (30 × 30) × 100 = **90%**. If there were 162 discharges, ALOS = 810 ÷ 162 = **5 days**.

### Analysis

- **Descriptive**: counts, percentages, rates, averages, trends over time.
- **Comparative**: this month vs last month, ward vs ward, our hospital vs a benchmark.
- **Statistical process control**: run charts and control charts show whether a change is real or just random variation.

### Presentation

| Method | Best for |
| --- | --- |
| Table | Exact numbers |
| Bar chart | Comparing categories (falls per ward) |
| Line graph / run chart | Trends over time (infection rate by month) |
| Pie chart | Parts of a whole (patients by diagnosis) |
| Dashboard | Several key indicators on one screen with colour codes (green/amber/red) |
| Balanced scorecard | Indicators from four views: finance, patients, internal processes, learning and growth |

### Decisions informed by data

- Staffing and rostering by acuity and census.
- Opening or closing beds; planning equipment and supplies.
- Targeting quality improvement (e.g. a rise in falls on night shift leads to more rounds and bed alarms).
- Budgeting and purchasing.
- Reporting to accreditation bodies (NABH) and government programmes.

## 6.9 Nurse manager's role

- Make sure staff enter complete, accurate data.
- Review dashboards and reports regularly and share them with staff.
- Use evidence from data, not guesswork, to justify staffing and resources.
- Protect confidentiality when presenting data (no patient names in reports).

## Remember

- eHealth = ICT for health; telemedicine = clinical care at a distance; mHealth = mobile devices.
- Telemedicine modes: synchronous, store-and-forward, remote monitoring.
- eSanjeevani = national telemedicine; Tele-MANAS = tele-mental health (14416); IHIP = surveillance; ANMOL = app for ANMs.
- Digital divide and e-health literacy are major challenges.
- Public health informatics focuses on populations; nurses collect, report and use community data.
- NIS components: clinical, administrative, education, research.
- Bed occupancy = inpatient days ÷ (beds × days) × 100; ALOS = inpatient days ÷ discharges.
- Fall rate is expressed per 1000 patient days; dashboards show key indicators at a glance.

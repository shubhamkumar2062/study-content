# How to use this guide

Health/Nursing Informatics and Technology is a second-semester course of the INC 2021 B.Sc Nursing curriculum (2 credits, 40 hours of theory, plus 1 credit, 40 hours of lab). It teaches you to use computers and information systems in patient care, to understand electronic health records and the standards behind them, and to handle health information safely, legally and ethically.

**It is a college exam, not a university exam.** INC lists this course among the courses examined by the college itself; the result is sent to the university for inclusion in the mark sheet and counts towards your aggregate. The INC table gives **25 internal marks and 25 end-semester college exam marks (total 50)**; the college paper is set for 50 marks over 2 hours and counted as 25. **The minimum pass is 50%.**

**How the units are arranged.** The INC syllabus has ten units. Unit I is large and has its own notes file; small units are joined so that each file is a useful size:

| Notes file | INC unit(s) | Hours (theory) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit 1 Computer applications in patient care and nursing practice | I | 10 |
| Unit 2 Principles of health informatics and information systems | II + III | 4 + 3 |
| Unit 3 Shared care and electronic health records | IV | 4 |
| Unit 4 Patient safety and clinical risk | V | 3 |
| Unit 5 Clinical knowledge, decision making and standardised terminologies | VI | 3 |
| Unit 6 eHealth, public health informatics and using information in management | VII + VIII | 3 + 3 |
| Unit 7 Information law and governance, quality and evidence-based practice | IX + X | 4 + 3 |

Study one notes file a day: read it, redraw the text diagrams, then answer that unit's questions and MCQs without looking. The lab hours (MS Office, EHR practice, statistics, visits to the health informatics department) are best learned by doing; practise Word, Excel and PowerPoint on a computer, not only from notes.

## Exam pattern used for the mock paper

INC Appendix 3 gives a 50-mark college exam pattern with no internal choice. Your college may set it differently; confirm with your college.

| Part | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MCQ | 8 multiple-choice questions | 8 × 1 = 8 |
| Essay | 1 long answer (essay or situation type) | 1 × 10 = 10 |
| Short | 4 short answers | 4 × 5 = 20 |
| Very short | 6 very short answers | 6 × 2 = 12 |
| **Total** | 2 hours | **50** |

Internal assessment (25 marks) comes from assignments, visit reports, lab skill checklists and sessional tests.

## Probability ratings

- **High** — a favourite; comes up in most papers in some form. Learn a full answer.
- **Medium** — appears every few papers or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — less common, but useful for MCQs and 2-mark answers.

These ratings are estimates from the weight of each topic in the INC syllabus and the usual style of college papers. They are not a promise of what will be asked.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. **Define first.** Start every answer with a clear definition (health informatics, EHR, interoperability, telemedicine, EBP). Examiners look for it.
2. **Use tables for comparisons**: EMR vs EHR vs PHR, HIS vs CIS, data vs information, privacy vs confidentiality vs security, RCA vs FMEA, tacit vs explicit knowledge.
3. **Draw simple diagrams**: input–process–output, DIKW pyramid, HMIS modules, ABDM consent flow, risk management cycle, Swiss cheese model, EBP triad.
4. **Always add the nurse's role.** Every unit ends with nursing implications; two or three points can lift an answer by a mark or two.
5. **Give Indian examples**: ABDM/ABHA, eSanjeevani, e-Hospital, IHIP, HMIS portal, ANMOL, Tele-MANAS, the DPDP Act. They show that you know the Indian setting.
6. **Learn the few exact facts examiners like**: ABHA = 14 digits; ABDM launched 27 September 2021; EHR Standards 2013, revised 2016; DPDP Act assent 11 August 2023; Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 25 March 2020; ICD-11 from 1 January 2022; NANDA-I 13 domains; Omaha System 4 domains.
7. **Work the formulas** for bed occupancy, average length of stay and fall rate; a short numerical example earns full marks quickly.
8. For **2-mark answers**, write a one-line definition plus one example or one fact.
9. Government programmes and laws change; the notes say where to check current facts. In the exam, write what the notes give.

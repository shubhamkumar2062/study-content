# How to use this guide

Applied Biochemistry is a second-semester subject of the INC 2021 B.Sc Nursing curriculum (2 credits, 40 hours, including a few laboratory observation hours). It is examined in a **combined university paper with Applied Nutrition and Dietetics**: Applied Biochemistry is **Section A (25 marks)** and Applied Nutrition and Dietetics is **Section B (50 marks)** of one 3-hour, 75-mark paper. Biochemistry explains what happens to food after it is eaten, so study it alongside the Nutrition pack — carbohydrates, lipids and proteins appear in both.

**How the units are arranged.** The INC syllabus has eight units. Units VI and VII are small and closely linked (bilirubin is part of the liver function tests), so they share one notes file:

| Notes file | INC unit(s) | Hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit 1 Carbohydrates, blood glucose and diabetes | I | 8 |
| Unit 2 Lipids, lipoproteins and atherosclerosis | II | 8 |
| Unit 3 Amino acids, proteins and plasma proteins | III | 9 |
| Unit 4 Clinical enzymology | IV | 4 |
| Unit 5 Acid–base balance | V | 3 |
| Unit 6 Heme catabolism, jaundice and organ function tests | VI + VII | 2 + 3 |
| Unit 7 Immunochemistry | VIII | 3 |

Study one notes file a day: read it, write out the pathways and tables once from memory, then answer the questions and MCQs for that unit without looking. Unit 1 is long because it carries the most hours and the most questions.

**Section A: Applied Biochemistry (25 marks)** — as given in INC Regulations 2020, Appendix 3. There is **no essay** in this section and **no internal choice**, so every question must be answered. Your university may print it differently; confirm with your university.

| Part | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MCQ | 4 multiple-choice questions | 4 × 1 = 4 |
| Short | 3 short answers | 3 × 5 = 15 |
| Very short | 3 very short answers | 3 × 2 = 6 |
| **Total** | | **25** |

Section A is one third of the 3-hour paper, so the mock test here is timed at 60 minutes. The university exam is 75 marks (Biochemistry 25 + Nutrition 50) with 25 internal marks. You must pass theory with at least 50%. Because the paper is combined, a weak Biochemistry section can pull down the whole result — do not leave it for the last week.

**About the long questions in the bank.** INC lists "essay" as an assessment method for Units I–IV, so long (10-mark) questions are included for practice and for internal/sessional tests. In the university paper, expect the same topics to come as 5-mark short answers; a long answer cut to its main points makes a good short answer.

**Laboratory values.** Normal values in these notes are approximate ("about") and are the figures most Indian textbooks use. Every laboratory prints its own reference range, which depends on its method; in the exam, write "about" or give the range your teacher taught.

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — a favourite; comes up in most papers in some form. Learn the full answer with a table or pathway.
- **Medium** — appears every few papers or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — less common, but useful for MCQs and very short answers.

These ratings are estimates from syllabus weight and the usual style of nursing biochemistry papers. They are not a promise of what will be asked.

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. **Use tables and flow charts.** Comparisons (type 1 vs type 2 diabetes, three types of jaundice, lipoproteins, immunoglobulin classes, acid–base disorders) score well as tables. Pathways (haem breakdown, urea cycle outline, fat digestion) should be written as arrow chains.
2. **Quote normal values with units and the word "about"**: fasting glucose 70–100 mg/dL, pH 7.35–7.45, PaCO2 35–45 mmHg, HCO3⁻ 22–26 mmol/L, bilirubin 0.2–1.2 mg/dL, total protein 6–8 g/dL, albumin 3.5–5 g/dL, urea 15–40 mg/dL, creatinine 0.6–1.2 mg/dL, total cholesterol below 200 mg/dL.
3. **Draw** the immunoglobulin Y structure, GTT curves, the electrophoresis pattern and the time-course of cardiac markers; a neat labelled figure earns marks quickly.
4. Every answer should end with **one or two nursing points** — sample collection (fluoride tube, avoid haemolysis, fresh urine), patient preparation (OGTT, lipid profile), and what to observe (hypoglycaemia, Kussmaul breathing, jaundice in newborns).
5. For 2-mark answers write a definition plus one example or one value. For 5-mark answers write 6–8 clear points or a table with a short introduction.
6. MCQs come from small facts: enzyme names, which test detects what, which immunoglobulin crosses the placenta. Read the "Remember" list of each notes file the night before.
7. With only 25 marks in 60 minutes, spend about 1–2 minutes on each MCQ, 10–12 minutes on each short answer and 4–5 minutes on each very short answer.

# Unit 3: Amino acids, proteins and plasma proteins

INC Unit III (9 hours): classification of amino acids by nutritional need and metabolic fate; digestion, absorption and metabolism of proteins and related disorders; biologically important compounds made from amino acids (names only); inborn errors of aromatic amino acid metabolism (in brief); plasma proteins — types, functions and normal values; causes of proteinuria, hypoproteinaemia and hypergammaglobulinaemia; principle of electrophoresis with normal and abnormal patterns (in brief).

> Normal values here are approximate ("about"); each laboratory prints its own range.

## 3.1 Amino acids and proteins

**Amino acids** are the building blocks of proteins. Each has an amino group (–NH2), a carboxyl group (–COOH), a hydrogen and a side chain (R group) attached to one carbon. Twenty amino acids are used to build human proteins. They are joined by **peptide bonds** to form peptides and proteins.

**Proteins** do almost everything in the body: enzymes, structure (collagen, keratin), transport (haemoglobin, albumin), defence (antibodies), hormones (insulin), clotting (fibrinogen), muscle contraction (actin, myosin) and buffering. Protein gives 4 kcal per gram. Protein is the only major food that supplies **nitrogen**.

## 3.2 Classification of amino acids

### By nutritional requirement

| Group | Meaning | Amino acids |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Essential** | Cannot be made in the body; must come from food | Valine, leucine, isoleucine, threonine, methionine, phenylalanine, tryptophan, lysine |
| **Semi-essential** (conditionally essential) | Body makes them, but not enough during growth | **Arginine, histidine** (needed in the diet of growing children) |
| **Non-essential** | Body can make them | Glycine, alanine, serine, aspartate, glutamate, asparagine, glutamine, proline, tyrosine, cysteine |

Memory aid for the ten (essential + semi-essential): **"PVT TIM HALL"** — Phenylalanine, Valine, Threonine, Tryptophan, Isoleucine, Methionine, Histidine, Arginine, Leucine, Lysine.

A protein containing all essential amino acids in good proportion (egg, milk, meat, fish) is a **complete / first-class protein**. Cereals are low in lysine and pulses are low in methionine; eating them together (dal + rice, khichdi) makes up the gap — the **supplementary value of proteins**.

### By metabolic fate

The carbon skeleton left after removing the amino group can become glucose, ketone bodies, or both.

| Group | What the skeleton forms | Amino acids |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Purely ketogenic** | Acetyl-CoA / acetoacetate (ketone bodies, fat) | Leucine, lysine |
| **Both ketogenic and glucogenic** | Both | Isoleucine, phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan (threonine is placed here in some books) |
| **Purely glucogenic** | Glucose (by gluconeogenesis) | All the rest — e.g. alanine, glycine, serine, glutamate, aspartate, methionine, valine, histidine, arginine |

Other useful groups: aromatic (phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan), sulphur-containing (methionine, cysteine), branched-chain (valine, leucine, isoleucine), acidic (aspartate, glutamate), basic (lysine, arginine, histidine).

## 3.3 Digestion of proteins

| Site | Enzyme (inactive form → active) | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stomach | Pepsinogen → **pepsin** (activated by HCl) | Breaks proteins into large peptides; HCl also denatures protein |
| Stomach (infants) | **Rennin** | Clots milk protein (casein) so it stays longer in the stomach |
| Small intestine (pancreatic juice) | Trypsinogen → **trypsin** (by enteropeptidase/enterokinase of the gut wall); trypsin then activates the others | Trypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase cut inside the chain (endopeptidases); carboxypeptidase cuts from the carboxyl end |
| Small intestine (brush border) | Aminopeptidases, dipeptidases, tripeptidases | Cut small peptides into free amino acids |

Enzymes are released as **inactive precursors (zymogens)** so they do not digest the cells that make them. Early activation of trypsin inside the pancreas causes acute pancreatitis.

## 3.4 Absorption

- Free amino acids are absorbed in the small intestine by **sodium-dependent active transport** (several carriers for different groups); di- and tripeptides are absorbed too and split inside the cell.
- A minor route, the **γ-glutamyl cycle** (glutathione-dependent), also carries amino acids into cells.
- Amino acids reach the **liver by the portal vein**.
- Newborns can absorb some whole proteins (antibodies in colostrum) for a short time after birth — the start of passive immunity; this is also how some food allergies may start.

## 3.5 Metabolism of amino acids and proteins

```text
          Dietary protein        Body protein breakdown
                 ↘                   ↙
                AMINO ACID POOL ──────→ Body proteins, enzymes, hormones,
                       │                 special products (3.7)
                       ↓
            Removal of the amino group
     (transamination + oxidative deamination)
          ↙                          ↘
   AMMONIA (NH3)               CARBON SKELETON
       ↓  liver                  → glucose, ketone bodies, energy (TCA cycle)
   UREA CYCLE → urea → kidney → urine
```

**Transamination**: the amino group is moved from an amino acid to α-ketoglutarate, forming glutamate and a new keto acid. Enzymes are **aminotransferases (transaminases)**, which need **pyridoxal phosphate (vitamin B6)**. Two of them are important in diagnosis: **ALT** (alanine aminotransferase) and **AST** (aspartate aminotransferase) — see Unit 4.

**Oxidative deamination**: glutamate dehydrogenase removes the amino group from glutamate as free **ammonia**, mainly in the liver.

**Transport of ammonia**: ammonia is toxic to the brain. It travels in blood mainly as **glutamine** (from most tissues) and **alanine** (from muscle) to the liver and kidney.

**Urea cycle (Krebs–Henseleit cycle)**: occurs **only in the liver**, partly in mitochondria and partly in the cytoplasm. Ammonia + CO2 + the amino group of aspartate → **urea**, which is carried in blood to the kidney and excreted. It uses energy (3 ATP per urea). Key enzymes: carbamoyl phosphate synthetase-I, ornithine transcarbamoylase, argininosuccinate synthetase, argininosuccinate lyase, arginase.

```text
NH3 + CO2 → carbamoyl phosphate → (with ornithine) citrulline → (+ aspartate)
argininosuccinate → arginine (+ fumarate) → ARGININE splits → UREA + ornithine
(ornithine goes round again)
```

Urea is the main nitrogen-containing waste in urine. Blood urea rises in kidney failure and falls in severe liver failure.

**Nitrogen balance**: positive in growth, pregnancy and recovery (intake > loss); negative in starvation, fever, burns, surgery, uncontrolled diabetes and cancer wasting (loss > intake).

## 3.6 Disorders related to protein metabolism

| Disorder | Cause | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Protein–energy malnutrition** | Low protein/energy intake | **Kwashiorkor** (oedema due to low albumin, fatty liver, skin and hair changes) and **marasmus** (severe wasting, no oedema) |
| **Hyperammonaemia** | Liver failure or urea cycle enzyme defects | Vomiting, irritability, lethargy, flapping tremor, coma — ammonia poisons the brain; **hepatic encephalopathy** in cirrhosis |
| **Inborn errors of amino acid metabolism** | Missing enzyme | Phenylketonuria, alkaptonuria, albinism etc. (3.8) |
| **Gout** | High uric acid (end product of purine — not protein — breakdown, but often taught here) | Painful joints, tophi, kidney stones |
| **Cystinuria, Hartnup disease** | Defective amino acid transport in kidney/gut | Stones (cystine); pellagra-like rash (Hartnup) |
| **Proteinuria, hypoproteinaemia** | See 3.10 | |

## 3.7 Biologically important compounds made from amino acids (names only)

| Amino acid | Compounds formed |
| --- | --- |
| **Glycine** | Haem (with succinyl-CoA), creatine, glutathione, purines, bile salts (glycocholic acid) |
| **Tyrosine** (from phenylalanine) | Dopamine, noradrenaline, adrenaline (catecholamines); thyroxine (T4) and T3; melanin |
| **Tryptophan** | Serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine), melatonin, niacin (vitamin B3) |
| **Histidine** | Histamine |
| **Arginine** | Nitric oxide, creatine, urea, ornithine (→ polyamines) |
| **Methionine** | S-adenosylmethionine (methyl donor), cysteine, creatine (methyl group) |
| **Cysteine** | Taurine (bile salts), glutathione |
| **Glutamate** | GABA (inhibitory brain transmitter), glutathione |
| **Aspartate, glutamine** | Purines and pyrimidines (DNA, RNA) |
| **Serine** | Ethanolamine, choline, sphingosine (phospholipids) |

## 3.8 Inborn errors of aromatic amino acid metabolism (in brief)

```text
Phenylalanine ──(phenylalanine hydroxylase)──→ Tyrosine ──→ Homogentisic acid ──(homogentisate oxidase)──→ ... → fumarate + acetoacetate
      ✗ PKU                                     │      ✗ tyrosinaemia                ✗ alkaptonuria
                                                ├──(tyrosinase)──→ Melanin   ✗ albinism
                                                ├──→ Dopamine → noradrenaline → adrenaline
                                                └──→ Thyroxine
```

| Disease | Missing enzyme | Main features | Management in brief |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Phenylketonuria (PKU)** | Phenylalanine hydroxylase | Phenylalanine and phenylketones build up → **intellectual disability**, fits, hyperactivity, **mousy (musty) body odour**, fair skin and hair (less melanin), eczema; mother with uncontrolled PKU harms the foetus | **Newborn screening** (Guthrie test or modern blood spot methods); lifelong **low-phenylalanine diet** started early; avoid aspartame-sweetened products |
| **Alkaptonuria** | Homogentisate oxidase | Homogentisic acid in urine → **urine turns black on standing** (alkaline/air oxidation); dark pigment in cartilage, sclera and ears (**ochronosis**); arthritis in adult life | Usually mild in childhood; supportive treatment |
| **Albinism** | Tyrosinase (in melanocytes) | No melanin: white hair, pale skin, pink/pale irises, **photophobia**, poor vision, nystagmus, high risk of skin cancer and sunburn | Protect from sun (clothing, sunscreen, dark glasses); eye care |
| **Tyrosinaemia** (type I) | Fumarylacetoacetate hydrolase (later step) | Liver failure, kidney tubular defects, cabbage-like odour | Special diet and specific drug under specialist care |

Tryptophan: **Hartnup disease** (defective absorption of tryptophan) gives a pellagra-like skin rash, because less tryptophan is available for niacin.

## 3.9 Plasma proteins — types, functions and normal values

Plasma proteins are separated into **albumin, globulins (α1, α2, β, γ) and fibrinogen**. Most are made in the **liver**; immunoglobulins (γ-globulins) are made by **plasma cells** (B lymphocytes).

**Normal values (serum, adults, about)**

| Protein | About |
| --- | --- |
| Total protein | 6.0–8.0 g/dL |
| Albumin | 3.5–5.0 g/dL |
| Globulin | 2.0–3.5 g/dL |
| Albumin : globulin (A:G) ratio | normally above 1; commonly about 1.5–2 : 1 (falls below 1, "reversed", in cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome and myeloma) |
| Fibrinogen (plasma) | 200–400 mg/dL |

Serum = plasma minus clotting proteins (fibrinogen).

**Types and functions**

| Protein | Examples | Functions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Albumin** (largest share, smallest molecule) | — | Maintains **colloid osmotic (oncotic) pressure** (about 80% of it) and so holds water in vessels; transports bilirubin, free fatty acids, calcium, hormones (thyroxine), many drugs; buffer; source of amino acids in starvation |
| **α1-globulins** | α1-antitrypsin, α1-acid glycoprotein, α1-lipoprotein (HDL) | α1-antitrypsin protects the lung from enzyme damage (its deficiency → emphysema, liver disease) |
| **α2-globulins** | Haptoglobin, ceruloplasmin, α2-macroglobulin | Haptoglobin binds free haemoglobin (falls in haemolysis); ceruloplasmin carries copper (low in Wilson's disease) |
| **β-globulins** | Transferrin, β-lipoprotein (LDL), complement C3, haemopexin | Transferrin carries iron (rises in iron deficiency) |
| **γ-globulins** | Immunoglobulins IgG, IgA, IgM, IgD, IgE | Antibodies — defence (Unit 7) |
| **Fibrinogen** and other clotting factors | Factor I, prothrombin | Blood clotting |
| **Acute phase proteins** | C-reactive protein (CRP), fibrinogen, haptoglobin, ceruloplasmin, α1-antitrypsin rise; albumin and transferrin fall | Rise within hours of infection, injury or inflammation — CRP is a common marker of inflammation |

**Albumin and oedema**: when albumin falls below about 2.5 g/dL the oncotic pressure falls, fluid leaks from capillaries into tissues → **oedema** (puffy face, ankles) and ascites (nephrotic syndrome, cirrhosis, kwashiorkor).

## 3.10 Proteinuria, hypoproteinaemia and hypergammaglobulinaemia

### Proteinuria

Normal urine contains very little protein (less than about **150 mg per day**). More than this is **proteinuria**. **Microalbuminuria** (albumin about 30–300 mg/day) is an early sign of kidney damage in diabetes and hypertension.

| Type | Causes |
| --- | --- |
| Physiological / functional (temporary) | Heavy exercise, fever, cold exposure, emotional stress, **orthostatic (postural) proteinuria** in young people on standing, pregnancy |
| **Glomerular** (filtration barrier damaged — mostly albumin) | Nephrotic syndrome, glomerulonephritis, **diabetic nephropathy**, hypertension, **pre-eclampsia**, SLE |
| **Tubular** (small proteins not reabsorbed) | Tubular damage from heavy metals, some drugs, pyelonephritis |
| **Overflow (pre-renal)** | Small proteins in excess in blood: **Bence Jones protein** (light chains) in multiple myeloma, haemoglobinuria (intravascular haemolysis), myoglobinuria (crush injury) |
| **Post-renal** | Urinary tract infection, stones, tumours of the bladder/urethra, blood in urine |

Tests: dipstick, heat and acetic acid test, sulphosalicylic acid test, 24-hour urine protein, urine albumin : creatinine ratio. Bence Jones protein precipitates at about 40–60 °C, redissolves near boiling and reappears on cooling.

### Hypoproteinaemia (low total plasma protein, mostly low albumin)

| Mechanism | Causes |
| --- | --- |
| Low intake | Starvation, protein–energy malnutrition (kwashiorkor) |
| Poor digestion/absorption | Malabsorption, chronic pancreatitis |
| Low synthesis | **Liver disease** (cirrhosis, chronic hepatitis) |
| Increased loss | **Nephrotic syndrome** (urine), **burns** (skin), protein-losing enteropathy (gut), haemorrhage, repeated tapping of ascites |
| Increased breakdown | Fever, sepsis, cancer, thyrotoxicosis, after major surgery |
| Dilution | Over-hydration, pregnancy (mild, physiological) |

### Hypergammaglobulinaemia (raised γ-globulins)

| Type | Pattern | Causes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Polyclonal** | Broad rise of many antibodies | Chronic infections (tuberculosis, kala-azar, chronic osteomyelitis, HIV), **chronic liver disease (cirrhosis)**, autoimmune disease (SLE, rheumatoid arthritis), sarcoidosis |
| **Monoclonal** (paraproteinaemia) | One clone of plasma cells makes one protein → sharp narrow **M band** | **Multiple myeloma**, Waldenström macroglobulinaemia (IgM), monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance |

## 3.11 Electrophoresis (in brief)

**Principle**: charged particles move in an electric field. At an alkaline pH (about 8.6, barbital buffer) plasma proteins carry a **negative charge** and move towards the **anode (+)**. Each protein moves at a speed that depends on its net charge, size and shape, so proteins separate into bands.

**Method in short**: a small drop of serum is applied on a strip (cellulose acetate or agarose gel) soaked in buffer → current passed for a set time → strip stained (e.g. amido black or Ponceau S) → bands seen and measured by a densitometer as peaks.

**Normal pattern** (from anode to cathode — fastest to slowest):

```text
 (+) Anode                                              Cathode (−)
     │ ALBUMIN │  α1  │   α2   │   β   │      γ       │ origin
     │  tall,  │ faint│ moderate│moderate│  broad,     │ (serum applied)
     │  sharp  │      │         │        │  diffuse    │
     5 bands in serum; plasma shows an extra fibrinogen band between β and γ
```

**Abnormal patterns**

| Condition | Pattern |
| --- | --- |
| **Nephrotic syndrome** | Albumin markedly ↓, **α2 ↑** (α2-macroglobulin retained), γ ↓ or normal |
| **Cirrhosis of liver** | Albumin ↓, broad polyclonal γ ↑, with **β–γ bridging** (no gap between β and γ) |
| **Multiple myeloma** | A tall, narrow **M band (spike)** usually in the γ region; other γ often ↓ |
| **Chronic infection / inflammation** | Broad polyclonal **γ ↑** (sometimes α2 ↑) |
| **Acute inflammation** | α1 and α2 ↑ (acute phase proteins) |
| **α1-antitrypsin deficiency** | α1 band very faint or absent |
| **Agammaglobulinaemia** | γ band absent or very faint |

## 3.12 Application in nursing

- Check urine for protein (dipstick) in antenatal visits — proteinuria with high blood pressure suggests pre-eclampsia.
- Collect a correct 24-hour urine: discard the first morning sample, then collect every sample for 24 hours including the next morning's first sample; keep cool; record start and end times.
- Weigh daily and look for oedema in patients with low albumin (nephrotic syndrome, cirrhosis, malnutrition); give the diet advised.
- In liver failure, watch for confusion and flapping tremor (rising ammonia); protein intake is adjusted by the doctor/dietitian.
- Support newborn screening; counsel parents of a child with PKU about the lifelong diet.
- Advise albino patients and families about sun protection and eye care.

## Remember

- Essential amino acids (+ 2 semi-essential): PVT TIM HALL; arginine and histidine are semi-essential.
- Purely ketogenic: leucine and lysine.
- Pepsin (stomach, activated by HCl); trypsin activated by enteropeptidase; rennin clots milk in infants.
- Transamination needs vitamin B6; urea is made only in the liver via the urea cycle.
- Glycine → haem, creatine, glutathione; tyrosine → catecholamines, thyroxine, melanin; tryptophan → serotonin, melatonin, niacin; histidine → histamine.
- PKU = phenylalanine hydroxylase defect (mousy odour, intellectual disability); alkaptonuria = urine darkens on standing; albinism = tyrosinase defect.
- Serum (about): total protein 6–8, albumin 3.5–5, globulin 2–3.5 g/dL; A:G ratio normally above 1 (about 1.5–2 : 1).
- Albumin keeps oncotic pressure; low albumin → oedema.
- Proteinuria > about 150 mg/day; Bence Jones protein in multiple myeloma.
- Electrophoresis at pH 8.6: albumin fastest towards the anode; M band = myeloma; β–γ bridging = cirrhosis; α2 ↑ with albumin ↓ = nephrotic syndrome.

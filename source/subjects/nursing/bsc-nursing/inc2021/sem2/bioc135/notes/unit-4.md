# Unit 4: Clinical enzymology

INC Unit IV (4 hours): isoenzymes — definition and properties; enzymes of diagnostic importance in liver diseases (ALT, AST, ALP, GGT), myocardial infarction (CK, cardiac troponins, AST, LDH), muscle diseases (CK, aldolase), bone diseases (ALP) and prostate cancer (PSA, ACP).

> Enzyme reference ranges depend heavily on the method, temperature and units used by each laboratory, so these notes describe *which* enzyme rises and *how much*, and do not give fixed normal values except where a figure is well established (marked "about").

## 4.1 Enzymes — a quick recap

**Enzymes** are biological catalysts, mostly proteins, that speed up chemical reactions in the body without being used up. The substance acted on is the **substrate**. Many need a non-protein helper: a **coenzyme** (often a B-vitamin derivative, e.g. pyridoxal phosphate for transaminases) or a metal ion **cofactor** (Mg²⁺ for kinases, Zn²⁺).

Factors affecting enzyme activity: substrate concentration, enzyme concentration, temperature (optimum about 37 °C; high heat denatures), pH (each enzyme has an optimum — pepsin about 2, trypsin about 8), activators and inhibitors. Activity is reported in **units per litre (U/L or IU/L)**.

**Why plasma enzymes help diagnosis**

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Functional plasma enzymes** | Act in plasma; normally present in high amounts | Clotting factors (thrombin), lipoprotein lipase |
| **Non-functional plasma enzymes** | Work **inside cells**; only small amounts leak into plasma during normal cell turnover | ALT, AST, CK, LDH, ALP, GGT, amylase |

When cells are damaged or die, or their production is increased, their enzymes spill into the blood. The **rise** points to the **organ damaged**, and the **pattern over time** helps judge when damage happened and how much.

Other clinically useful enzymes outside this syllabus list: **amylase and lipase** (acute pancreatitis), **cholinesterase** (low in liver disease and organophosphate poisoning). Enzymes are also used as **drugs** (streptokinase, asparaginase) and as **reagents** in tests (glucose oxidase in glucose test strips, enzymes in ELISA).

## 4.2 Isoenzymes

**Definition**: isoenzymes (isozymes) are **different molecular forms of the same enzyme** that catalyse the **same reaction**, but differ in their structure and physical, chemical and immunological properties. They are made by different genes or by different combinations of sub-units, and are usually found in different tissues.

**Properties (ways in which isoenzymes differ)**

1. **Amino acid composition and structure** (different sub-units, e.g. H and M sub-units of LDH).
2. **Electrophoretic mobility** — they move different distances in electrophoresis; this is the commonest way to separate them.
3. **Tissue (organ) distribution** — each tissue has a typical isoenzyme; this gives **organ specificity** to a test.
4. **Kinetic properties** — different affinity for the substrate (Km) and maximum speed.
5. **Heat stability** — e.g. placental ALP is heat-stable, bone ALP is heat-labile.
6. **Response to inhibitors** — e.g. prostatic acid phosphatase is inhibited by tartrate.
7. **Immunological properties** — antibodies can tell them apart (used to measure CK-MB mass, troponins).
8. Sub-cellular location (cytoplasmic and mitochondrial forms of AST).

**Clinical value**: total enzyme activity tells you that cells are damaged somewhere; the isoenzyme tells you **which organ**.

### Important isoenzymes

**Creatine kinase (CK)** — a dimer of M (muscle) and B (brain) sub-units:

| Isoenzyme | Sub-units | Main tissue | Raised in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CK-1 (CK-BB) | BB | Brain, smooth muscle | Brain injury, stroke (rarely measured) |
| CK-2 (**CK-MB**) | MB | **Heart muscle** (heart has the highest share) | **Myocardial infarction**, myocarditis, after cardiac surgery |
| CK-3 (CK-MM) | MM | Skeletal muscle (most of normal serum CK) | Muscle injury, muscular dystrophy, IM injections, exercise |

**Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH)** — a tetramer of H (heart) and M (muscle) sub-units; five isoenzymes:

| Isoenzyme | Sub-units | Main tissue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LDH-1 | H4 | Heart, RBCs, kidney |
| LDH-2 | H3M | Heart, RBCs (normally the largest fraction in serum) |
| LDH-3 | H2M2 | Lungs, spleen, other tissues |
| LDH-4 | HM3 | Liver, skeletal muscle |
| LDH-5 | M4 | **Liver**, skeletal muscle |

Normally LDH-2 > LDH-1. After MI, LDH-1 rises above LDH-2 — the "**flipped LDH pattern**". LDH-5 rises in liver disease. LDH also rises in haemolysis (from RBCs), megaloblastic anaemia, many cancers; a haemolysed sample gives a falsely high LDH.

**Alkaline phosphatase (ALP)** isoenzymes: liver, bone, intestinal and placental (heat-stable; a similar heat-stable "Regan" isoenzyme appears in some cancers).

## 4.3 Enzymes in liver diseases — ALT, AST, ALP, GGT

| Enzyme | Where it comes from | Raised in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ALT** (alanine aminotransferase; old name SGPT) | Mainly **liver** cytoplasm → the **most liver-specific** of the four | Hepatocellular damage: acute **viral hepatitis** (very high — often more than 10 times the upper limit), drug- or toxin-induced hepatitis, fatty liver (mild), cirrhosis (mild or normal) |
| **AST** (aspartate aminotransferase; old name SGOT) | Liver, heart, skeletal muscle, kidney, RBCs (cytoplasm and mitochondria) — **less specific** | Liver damage (as ALT), **myocardial infarction**, muscle injury, haemolysis |
| **ALP** (alkaline phosphatase) | Lining of the **bile canaliculi** (liver), bone (osteoblasts), placenta, intestine | **Obstructive (cholestatic) jaundice** — gallstone in the bile duct, cancer of the head of the pancreas, intrahepatic cholestasis, liver secondaries — marked rise; only mild rise in hepatitis |
| **GGT** (γ-glutamyl transferase) | Liver (bile ducts), kidney, pancreas | **Alcohol** intake (sensitive marker of drinking), cholestasis (rises with ALP), drugs that induce liver enzymes (phenytoin, phenobarbital), fatty liver |

**Reading the pattern**

```text
Hepatocellular pattern (hepatitis):  ALT ↑↑↑, AST ↑↑↑, ALP mild ↑, GGT mild ↑
                                      ALT > AST in viral hepatitis
Cholestatic pattern (obstruction):   ALP ↑↑↑, GGT ↑↑↑, ALT/AST mild ↑, conjugated bilirubin ↑
Alcoholic liver disease:             AST : ALT ratio more than 2, GGT ↑↑
```

**Why GGT with ALP?** ALP rises in both bone and biliary disease. If GGT is also raised, the ALP is from the **liver**; if GGT is normal, think of **bone**.

## 4.4 Enzymes in myocardial infarction — CK, troponins, AST, LDH

When heart muscle dies, its proteins leak into blood in a typical **time sequence**. Today **cardiac troponins** are the preferred markers; AST and LDH are mostly of historical/teaching value but are still asked in exams.

| Marker | Starts to rise | Peaks | Returns to normal | Comment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cardiac troponin I / T** | about 3–6 h (earlier with high-sensitivity tests) | about 12–24 h | **I: about 7–10 days; T: up to about 14 days** | **Most specific and sensitive** for heart muscle damage; the marker of choice; also detects a late-presenting MI |
| **CK-MB** | about 3–6 h | about 12–24 h | about 2–3 days | Good for detecting **re-infarction** (it returns to normal quickly) |
| Total CK | about 4–6 h | about 24 h | about 3–4 days | Not specific — also from skeletal muscle |
| **AST** | about 6–8 h | about 24–48 h | about 4–6 days | Not specific (liver, muscle) |
| **LDH** | about 12–24 h | about 2–3 days | about 8–14 days | Late marker; **LDH-1 > LDH-2 (flipped pattern)** |

```text
Height
of rise   Troponin ───────────────────────────────── (stays 7–14 days)
  ↑        CK-MB  ──────╲
  │       AST         ─────────╲
  │      LDH                ─────────────────╲
  └──────────────────────────────────────────→ time after chest pain
    0     6 h   12 h  24 h  2 d   3 d   5 d   7 d   14 d
  Order of rise:  troponin ≈ CK-MB → AST → LDH
  Order of fall:  CK-MB → AST → LDH and troponin (last)
```

**Troponins** are not enzymes; they are regulatory proteins of the heart's contraction apparatus (troponin T binds tropomyosin, troponin I inhibits contraction, troponin C binds calcium). Cardiac forms differ from skeletal forms, so tests are heart-specific. They are listed with enzymes because they are used the same way. Troponin can also rise in myocarditis, heart failure, pulmonary embolism, severe sepsis and kidney failure, so it is read with the ECG and symptoms. **Myoglobin** rises earliest (1–3 h) but is not heart-specific.

## 4.5 Enzymes in muscle diseases — CK, aldolase

| Enzyme | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **CK (mainly CK-MM)** | The best enzyme for skeletal muscle damage. **Very high in Duchenne muscular dystrophy** (raised from early childhood, even before weakness; often raised in female carriers), polymyositis/dermatomyositis, crush injury and **rhabdomyolysis**, severe exercise, prolonged fits, IM injections, hypothyroidism. Normal in purely neurogenic weakness (e.g. myasthenia gravis, nerve disease) |
| **Aldolase** | A glycolytic enzyme; raised in muscular dystrophy and inflammatory muscle disease; less specific (also liver, RBCs) and now less used |

In rhabdomyolysis, myoglobin from damaged muscle can block kidney tubules → acute kidney injury; urine looks dark brown ("cola-coloured").

## 4.6 Enzymes in bone diseases — ALP

**Bone ALP** comes from **osteoblasts** (bone-forming cells), so it rises whenever bone formation is increased.

| Raised ALP — bone causes | Physiological rise |
| --- | --- |
| **Rickets** (children) and **osteomalacia** (adults) — vitamin D deficiency | **Growing children and adolescents** (growth spurt) |
| **Paget's disease of bone** — very high values | **Pregnancy** (placental ALP, third trimester) |
| **Bone secondaries** that form new bone (e.g. from prostate cancer) | Healing fractures |
| Hyperparathyroidism, osteogenic sarcoma | |

ALP is usually normal in osteoporosis and in multiple myeloma (bone destruction without new bone formation). Use GGT to tell bone ALP from liver ALP (4.3).

## 4.7 Enzymes in prostate cancer — PSA, ACP

| Marker | What it is | Clinical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PSA (prostate-specific antigen)** | A protease (serine protease) made by prostate gland cells; keeps semen liquid | **Main marker** for screening (with digital rectal examination), diagnosis and follow-up of prostate cancer. Commonly a value above about **4 ng/mL** needs further evaluation (cut-offs vary with age). It is prostate-specific but **not cancer-specific**: also rises in benign prostatic hyperplasia, prostatitis, urinary retention, after catheterisation, cystoscopy, prostate biopsy, digital rectal examination and ejaculation |
| **ACP (acid phosphatase)** | Phosphatase that works at acid pH; prostate is the richest source; also in RBCs, bone, platelets | **Prostatic ACP** (inhibited by **tartrate** — "tartrate-labile") rises when prostate cancer has **spread beyond the capsule, especially to bone**. Largely replaced by PSA. It is also found in semen (used in forensic work after alleged sexual assault) |

## 4.8 Collecting enzyme samples — nursing points

1. **Avoid haemolysis**: use a proper needle size, avoid prolonged tourniquet, do not shake the tube, send promptly. RBCs contain LDH, AST, ACP and potassium, so haemolysis gives falsely high values.
2. **Timing matters in suspected MI**: record the **time of onset of chest pain** and the time of each sample; troponin is often repeated after a few hours.
3. **Before PSA**: collect blood **before** a digital rectal examination, catheterisation or prostate procedure, or as advised (these raise PSA); note recent ejaculation or urinary retention.
4. **CK**: note recent IM injections, falls, fits, vigorous exercise or surgery — all raise CK.
5. Use the tube type the lab specifies, label with name, date and time, and send without delay; enzymes lose activity if left at room temperature.
6. Interpret results with clinical signs; tell the doctor at once about a rising troponin.

## Remember

- Non-functional plasma enzymes (ALT, AST, CK, LDH, ALP) act inside cells; a rise means cell damage or increased production.
- Isoenzymes: different forms of the same enzyme, same reaction; differ in structure, electrophoretic mobility, tissue distribution, Km, heat stability, inhibitors.
- CK: BB brain, MB heart, MM skeletal muscle. LDH: 5 isoenzymes; LDH-1 heart and RBCs, LDH-5 liver and muscle; flipped LDH-1 > LDH-2 after MI.
- Liver: ALT most liver-specific; ALT > AST in viral hepatitis; AST : ALT > 2 in alcoholic liver disease; ALP + GGT ↑↑ in obstructive jaundice; GGT ↑ with alcohol.
- MI: troponin is the marker of choice (rises 3–6 h, stays up to 7–14 days); CK-MB normal in 2–3 days (re-infarction); AST next; LDH last (normal by 8–14 days).
- Muscle disease: CK (very high in Duchenne dystrophy), aldolase.
- Bone: ALP ↑ in rickets, osteomalacia, Paget's, osteoblastic secondaries; normally high in children and pregnancy.
- Prostate: PSA (above about 4 ng/mL needs work-up; also raised in BPH, after rectal examination); prostatic ACP is tartrate-labile, raised in spread to bone.
- Avoid haemolysis — falsely high LDH, AST, ACP and potassium.

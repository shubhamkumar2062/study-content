# Unit 6: Heme catabolism, jaundice and organ function tests

This notes file joins two small INC units:

- **INC Unit VI — Heme catabolism (2 hours)**: haem degradation pathway; jaundice — types, causes, urine and blood investigations (van den Bergh test).
- **INC Unit VII — Organ function tests (3 hours)**: renal, liver and thyroid function tests — biochemical parameters and normal values only.

They are joined because bilirubin is the link: the liver function tests begin with serum bilirubin, and jaundice is read from the same tests. Both units are assessed by short and very short answers.

> All normal values here are approximate ("about") adult values. Each laboratory prints its own reference range, which depends on its method and units; always read the report against that range.

## 6.1 Haem and its breakdown — why it matters

**Haem** is an iron-containing porphyrin ring (protoporphyrin + Fe²⁺). It is part of haemoglobin, myoglobin, cytochromes and catalase. About 80–85% of haem broken down each day comes from **old red blood cells** (life span about **120 days**); the rest from immature RBCs in the marrow and other haem proteins.

## 6.2 Haem degradation pathway

```text
OLD RBCs (about 120 days) — taken up by macrophages of the reticuloendothelial system
                            (mainly spleen; also liver Kupffer cells, bone marrow)
        │
   Haemoglobin → Globin (→ amino acids, reused) + HAEM
        │
        │  haem oxygenase (needs O2, NADPH)
        ↓
   BILIVERDIN (green)  +  Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ (→ stored as ferritin / reused)  +  CO (breathed out)
        │
        │  biliverdin reductase (NADPH)
        ↓
   BILIRUBIN (orange-yellow) = UNCONJUGATED bilirubin
        │   insoluble in water; carried in blood bound to ALBUMIN
        │   (so it is NOT filtered by the kidney and not found in urine)
        ↓
   LIVER CELL: uptake → CONJUGATION with glucuronic acid
        │   enzyme: UDP-glucuronyl transferase
        ↓
   BILIRUBIN DIGLUCURONIDE = CONJUGATED bilirubin (water-soluble)
        │   secreted actively into bile canaliculi (rate-limiting step)
        ↓
   BILE → INTESTINE
        │   bacteria remove glucuronic acid and reduce bilirubin
        ↓
   UROBILINOGEN (colourless)
        ├─ most → oxidised to STERCOBILINOGEN → STERCOBILIN (brown colour of stool)
        └─ some → reabsorbed into portal blood → most re-excreted by liver in bile
                  (enterohepatic urobilinogen cycle); a small amount → kidney →
                  URINE UROBILINOGEN → oxidised to UROBILIN (yellow colour of urine)
```

**Key differences between the two forms**

| Feature | Unconjugated (indirect) | Conjugated (direct) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solubility | Fat-soluble, not water-soluble | Water-soluble |
| Transport in blood | Bound to albumin | Loosely bound/free |
| In urine | **Never** (too tightly bound, not filtered) | Appears when its blood level rises |
| Toxicity | Can enter the brain of newborns → **kernicterus** | Not toxic to brain |
| van den Bergh | **Indirect** reaction (needs alcohol) | **Direct** reaction |

**Normal serum bilirubin (about)**: total **0.2–1.2 mg/dL** (often quoted 0.3–1.0); conjugated (direct) **up to about 0.3 mg/dL**; unconjugated (indirect) = total − direct.

## 6.3 Jaundice

**Definition**: yellow colour of the skin, sclera and mucous membranes due to a raised serum bilirubin. Jaundice is usually **visible when total bilirubin is above about 2–3 mg/dL** (first seen in the sclera, in daylight). A level above normal but below this, with no visible yellow colour, is **latent (subclinical) jaundice**.

### Types and causes

| Type | Basic problem | Causes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pre-hepatic (haemolytic)** | Too much bilirubin made — more than the normal liver can conjugate | Haemolytic anaemias: hereditary spherocytosis, thalassaemia, sickle cell disease, **G6PD deficiency**, malaria, **mismatched blood transfusion**, **Rh/ABO haemolytic disease of the newborn**, large haematoma breakdown |
| **Hepatic (hepatocellular)** | Liver cells damaged — both uptake/conjugation and excretion are faulty | **Viral hepatitis** (A, B, C, E), alcoholic hepatitis, cirrhosis, drug-induced hepatitis (e.g. some anti-TB drugs, paracetamol overdose), leptospirosis, liver cancer; inherited conjugation defects — **Gilbert's syndrome** (mild), Crigler–Najjar syndrome (severe), Dubin–Johnson and Rotor syndromes (excretion defects) |
| **Post-hepatic (obstructive / cholestatic)** | Bile cannot flow from liver to gut; conjugated bilirubin backs up into blood | **Gallstone in the common bile duct**, **carcinoma of the head of the pancreas**, cholangiocarcinoma, stricture of the bile duct, enlarged nodes at the porta hepatis, biliary atresia in infants, intrahepatic cholestasis (drugs, pregnancy) |

**Physiological jaundice of the newborn**: appears after 24 hours, usually on days 2–3, and fades within about 1–2 weeks. Causes: high RBC breakdown (shorter RBC life, high RBC count at birth) and immature liver conjugating enzyme (UDP-glucuronyl transferase). It is unconjugated. Jaundice in the **first 24 hours**, rising rapidly, or lasting beyond 2 weeks is **pathological** and needs investigation. Very high unconjugated bilirubin can cause **kernicterus** (brain damage); **phototherapy** changes bilirubin into water-soluble forms that can be excreted.

### Laboratory findings in the three types

| Test | Pre-hepatic (haemolytic) | Hepatic | Post-hepatic (obstructive) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Serum unconjugated bilirubin | **↑↑** | ↑ | Normal or slight ↑ |
| Serum conjugated bilirubin | Normal | ↑ | **↑↑** |
| van den Bergh reaction | **Indirect** positive | **Biphasic** | **Direct** positive |
| **Urine bilirubin** (bile pigment) | **Absent** ("acholuric" jaundice) | Present | **Present** (dark, tea-coloured urine) |
| **Urine urobilinogen** | **↑↑** | Variable (↑ early, may ↓ at peak cholestasis) | **Absent** (no bile reaches the gut) |
| Urine bile salts | Absent | May be present | **Present** |
| Stool colour | Dark (more stercobilin) | Normal or pale | **Clay-coloured, pale** |
| ALT / AST | Normal | **Markedly ↑** | Mild ↑ |
| ALP / GGT | Normal | Mild ↑ | **Markedly ↑** |
| Other | Anaemia, ↑ reticulocytes, ↑ LDH, ↓ haptoglobin | Prothrombin time may be prolonged | Itching (bile salts in skin), slow pulse, fat malabsorption, vitamin K deficiency (prolonged PT corrected by vitamin K) |

## 6.4 Investigations

### Blood: van den Bergh test

**Principle**: bilirubin reacts with **diazotised sulphanilic acid (diazo reagent)** to form a **reddish-purple azobilirubin**, whose colour intensity is measured.

| Reaction | What happens | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Direct** | Colour appears **at once** in watery solution (no alcohol needed) | **Conjugated** (water-soluble) bilirubin — obstructive jaundice |
| **Indirect** | Colour appears **only after adding alcohol (methanol)**, which dissolves unconjugated bilirubin | **Unconjugated** bilirubin — haemolytic jaundice |
| **Biphasic** | Some colour at once, deepening after alcohol | Both raised — hepatic jaundice |

In modern labs the same diazo principle is used on analysers to report **total and direct bilirubin**; indirect = total − direct.

### Urine tests

| Substance | Test | Positive result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bile pigments (bilirubin)** | **Fouchet's test** (barium chloride + Fouchet's reagent: ferric chloride in trichloroacetic acid); also dipstick | **Green/blue** colour |
| | Gmelin's test (nitric acid) | Play of colours (rings) |
| **Bile salts** | **Hay's test** — sprinkle fine **sulphur powder** on the urine | Sulphur **sinks** (bile salts lower the surface tension); normally it floats |
| **Urobilinogen** | **Ehrlich's test** (Ehrlich's aldehyde reagent — p-dimethylaminobenzaldehyde); use **fresh** urine | **Cherry-red** colour (increased) |

**Nursing tips for urine tests**: test **fresh** urine (urobilinogen oxidises to urobilin and bilirubin breaks down in light); protect the sample from light; note the colour of urine and stool.

## 6.5 Organ function tests — renal function tests (RFT)

The kidneys excrete nitrogenous wastes, keep water, electrolytes and acid–base balance, and make erythropoietin, renin and active vitamin D.

**Blood parameters (about)**

| Parameter | Normal (about) | Rises in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Blood urea** | 15–40 mg/dL (BUN about 7–20 mg/dL) | Kidney failure; also dehydration, high-protein diet, GI bleeding, catabolic states (pre-renal); falls in liver failure, pregnancy |
| **Serum creatinine** | about 0.6–1.2 mg/dL (slightly lower in women) | **Kidney failure** — a better index of GFR than urea, as it is little affected by diet; also with large muscle mass |
| **Serum uric acid** | about 3.5–7 mg/dL (men), 2.5–6 mg/dL (women) | Gout, kidney failure, leukaemia and chemotherapy (cell breakdown), pre-eclampsia |
| Serum sodium | 135–145 mmol/L | — |
| Serum potassium | 3.5–5.0 mmol/L | **Hyperkalaemia in kidney failure** (dangerous to the heart) |
| Serum chloride | about 96–106 mmol/L | — |
| Serum bicarbonate | 22–26 mmol/L | Falls in kidney failure (metabolic acidosis) |
| Serum calcium (total) | about 8.5–10.5 mg/dL | Falls in chronic kidney disease |
| Serum phosphate | about 2.5–4.5 mg/dL | Rises in chronic kidney disease |
| BUN : creatinine ratio | about 10–20 : 1 | > 20 suggests pre-renal cause (dehydration, bleeding) |

**Clearance and GFR (about)**

| Test | Normal (about) |
| --- | --- |
| **Glomerular filtration rate (GFR)** | about 120 mL/min (90–120 mL/min/1.73 m² is normal; it falls with age) |
| **Creatinine clearance** | about 90–130 mL/min (uses timed urine + serum creatinine: C = U × V / P) |
| **eGFR** | Calculated from serum creatinine, age and sex; above 90 mL/min/1.73 m² is normal; below 60 for 3 months or more suggests chronic kidney disease |
| Urea clearance | about 75 mL/min (maximum clearance) — older test, rarely used |
| Inulin clearance | Gold standard for GFR (research use) |

**Urine parameters (about)**

| Parameter | Normal (about) |
| --- | --- |
| Volume | 1–2 litres per day (polyuria above about 3 L/day; oliguria below about 400 mL/day; anuria below about 100 mL/day) |
| Specific gravity | 1.003–1.030 (concentrating ability: early-morning sample usually above 1.020) |
| pH | about 4.5–8 (average about 6) |
| Protein | less than about 150 mg/day; albumin less than about 30 mg/day |
| Glucose, ketones, bilirubin, blood | Absent |
| Deposits | A few epithelial cells; occasional RBCs and pus cells; casts and crystals may indicate disease |

**Formula for clearance**

```text
Clearance (mL/min) = (U × V) / P
U = urine concentration, V = urine flow (mL/min), P = plasma concentration
Example: urine creatinine 100 mg/dL, urine flow 1.2 mL/min, plasma creatinine 1.0 mg/dL
         → clearance = (100 × 1.2) / 1.0 = 120 mL/min
```

## 6.6 Liver function tests (LFT)

| Group | Parameter | Normal (about) | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Excretory function | **Serum bilirubin** — total / direct | 0.2–1.2 / up to 0.3 mg/dL | Jaundice and its type |
| | Urine bilirubin, urobilinogen, bile salts | Bilirubin absent; urobilinogen trace | Type of jaundice (6.3) |
| Liver cell damage | **ALT** (SGPT) | about 7–40 U/L* | Hepatocellular damage |
| | **AST** (SGOT) | about 8–40 U/L* | Hepatocellular damage (less specific) |
| Cholestasis | **ALP** | about 40–130 U/L* in adults (higher in children, pregnancy) | Obstruction, bone disease |
| | **GGT** | about 10–50 U/L* (higher in men) | Cholestasis, alcohol |
| Synthetic function | **Total serum protein** | 6–8 g/dL | — |
| | **Albumin** | 3.5–5.0 g/dL | Falls in chronic liver disease (long half-life, about 20 days) |
| | Globulin, A:G ratio | 2–3.5 g/dL; ratio above 1 | Reversed A:G in cirrhosis |
| | **Prothrombin time (PT) / INR** | PT about 11–14 s; INR about 1 | Prolonged in liver failure (clotting factors made in liver; short half-life — earliest sign of failing synthesis) |
| Others | Blood ammonia | Low; method-dependent | Rises in liver failure / hepatic encephalopathy |

\*Enzyme ranges differ the most between laboratories — always use the printed range.

**Patterns**: hepatocellular damage → ALT/AST ↑↑; cholestasis → ALP/GGT ↑↑ and direct bilirubin ↑; chronic liver disease (cirrhosis) → albumin ↓, globulin ↑, PT prolonged.

## 6.7 Thyroid function tests (TFT)

```text
Hypothalamus → TRH → Anterior pituitary → TSH → Thyroid → T4 (mostly) and T3
       ↑_______________ negative feedback by T4/T3 __________________|
```

Most T4 and T3 in blood is bound to proteins (mainly thyroxine-binding globulin); only the small **free** fraction is active, so **free T4 and free T3** are better tests than total levels.

| Parameter | Normal (about) |
| --- | --- |
| **TSH** | about 0.4–4.5 mIU/L (some labs 0.5–5) |
| **Total T4** | about 5–12 µg/dL |
| **Free T4 (FT4)** | about 0.8–1.8 ng/dL |
| **Total T3** | about 80–200 ng/dL |
| **Free T3 (FT3)** | about 2.3–4.2 pg/mL |
| Anti-TPO antibodies | Negative / low (raised in autoimmune thyroid disease — Hashimoto's) |

Values in pregnancy and in newborns differ; labs give trimester- and age-specific ranges.

**Interpretation**

| Condition | TSH | Free T4 / T3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary hypothyroidism** (thyroid fails — iodine deficiency, Hashimoto's, after surgery/radio-iodine) | **↑** | **↓** |
| Subclinical hypothyroidism | ↑ | Normal |
| **Primary hyperthyroidism** (Graves' disease, toxic nodule) | **↓** (suppressed) | **↑** |
| Secondary hypothyroidism (pituitary failure) | ↓ or inappropriately normal | ↓ |
| T3 toxicosis | ↓ | T4 normal, T3 ↑ |

**TSH is the best single screening test** for thyroid function (the pituitary responds to small changes in thyroid hormone). **Newborn screening** for congenital hypothyroidism (heel-prick TSH or T4) prevents intellectual disability (cretinism) because treatment started early works.

## 6.8 Application in nursing

- Inspect the sclera in daylight for jaundice; note urine (dark) and stool (pale) colour.
- In newborns, watch for jaundice in the first 24 hours; during phototherapy cover the eyes and genitals, turn the baby, keep hydration and temperature, check bilirubin as ordered.
- Relieve itching in obstructive jaundice (cool bath, trimmed nails, prescribed lotions); watch for bleeding (vitamin K deficiency) and check PT before procedures such as liver biopsy.
- Collect correct samples: fresh, light-protected urine for bilirubin/urobilinogen; 24-hour urine for creatinine clearance (discard first void, collect all for 24 h including the last, keep cool); fasting is not needed for most RFT/LFT but follow lab rules.
- Monitor intake–output, weight and potassium in kidney failure; report a rising creatinine or potassium.
- Thyroid tests: note drugs (thyroxine, steroids, amiodarone) and pregnancy on the request form.

## Remember

- RBC life about 120 days; haem → biliverdin (haem oxygenase) → bilirubin (biliverdin reductase); iron reused, CO breathed out.
- Unconjugated bilirubin travels on albumin, never appears in urine; conjugated in liver by UDP-glucuronyl transferase (bilirubin diglucuronide).
- Gut bacteria → urobilinogen → stercobilin (stool colour) and urine urobilin.
- Serum bilirubin about 0.2–1.2 mg/dL; direct up to about 0.3; jaundice visible above about 2–3 mg/dL.
- Haemolytic: indirect ↑, urine bilirubin absent, urobilinogen ↑. Obstructive: direct ↑, urine bilirubin present, urobilinogen absent, clay stools, ALP ↑↑. Hepatic: biphasic, ALT/AST ↑↑.
- van den Bergh: diazo reagent → purple azobilirubin; direct = conjugated; indirect needs alcohol.
- Urine tests: Fouchet's (bile pigments), Hay's sulphur test (bile salts), Ehrlich's (urobilinogen).
- RFT (about): urea 15–40 mg/dL, creatinine 0.6–1.2 mg/dL, uric acid 3.5–7 mg/dL (men), GFR about 120 mL/min, urine 1–2 L/day, specific gravity 1.003–1.030.
- LFT: bilirubin, ALT, AST, ALP, GGT, total protein, albumin 3.5–5 g/dL, PT/INR.
- TFT: TSH about 0.4–4.5 mIU/L; primary hypothyroidism TSH ↑ T4 ↓; hyperthyroidism TSH ↓ T4/T3 ↑; TSH is the best screening test.

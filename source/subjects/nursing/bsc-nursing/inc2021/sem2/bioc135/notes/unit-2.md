# Unit 2: Lipids, lipoproteins and atherosclerosis

INC Unit II (8 hours): fatty acids (definition, classification); MUFA, PUFA, essential and trans fatty acids; digestion, absorption and metabolism of lipids and related disorders; compounds formed from cholesterol; ketone bodies (names, types and significance only); lipoproteins (types and functions — their metabolism is not required); lipid profile; atherosclerosis in brief.

> Lipid values in these notes are approximate ("about") and follow commonly used adult targets. Laboratories and cardiology guidelines differ; read every report against its own reference range.

## 2.1 What lipids are

Lipids are a mixed group of compounds that are insoluble in water and soluble in fat solvents (ether, chloroform), and are used or stored by living cells.

| Group | Examples | Main role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple lipids | Triacylglycerols (fats and oils), waxes | Stored energy (9 kcal per gram) |
| Compound lipids | Phospholipids (lecithin), glycolipids, lipoproteins | Cell membranes, nerve tissue, transport |
| Derived lipids | Fatty acids, cholesterol, steroid hormones, fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) | Fuel, hormones, vitamins |

Fat also insulates the body, cushions organs and carries fat-soluble vitamins.

## 2.2 Fatty acids

**Definition**: fatty acids are long hydrocarbon chains with a carboxyl group (–COOH) at one end. In nature they usually have an even number of carbon atoms and are found mostly as esters (in triacylglycerols and phospholipids), not free.

**Classification**

| Basis | Groups | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chain length (approximate) | Short chain (up to 6 C), medium chain (8–12 C), long chain (14 C and more) | Butyric (butter); caprylic (coconut oil); palmitic, stearic |
| Saturation | **Saturated** — no double bond | Palmitic (16 C), stearic (18 C) |
| | **Monounsaturated (MUFA)** — one double bond | Oleic acid (18 C, ω-9) |
| | **Polyunsaturated (PUFA)** — two or more double bonds | Linoleic (ω-6), α-linolenic (ω-3), arachidonic (ω-6), EPA and DHA (ω-3) |
| Nutritional need | **Essential** — must come from diet | Linoleic, α-linolenic (arachidonic becomes essential if linoleic is lacking) |
| | Non-essential — body can make them | Palmitic, oleic |
| Shape at the double bond | **Cis** (natural form, chain bends) or **trans** (chain straight) | Most natural oils are cis; trans fats come mainly from partially hydrogenated oil |

The "ω" (omega) number tells where the first double bond is, counted from the methyl (far) end.

**Clinical significance**

| Type | Main sources | Significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **MUFA** | Olive oil, groundnut oil, mustard oil, rice-bran oil, nuts | Lower LDL cholesterol without lowering HDL; protective for the heart |
| **PUFA (ω-6)** | Sunflower, safflower, soyabean, corn oils | Lower LDL; very high intake of ω-6 alone is not advised |
| **PUFA (ω-3)** | Fish and fish oil (EPA, DHA), flaxseed, mustard oil, walnuts | Lower triglycerides, reduce clotting and inflammation; DHA important for brain and retina of the foetus and infant |
| **Essential fatty acids** | Vegetable oils | Needed for cell membranes, skin barrier, and to make eicosanoids (prostaglandins, leukotrienes, thromboxanes); deficiency causes dry scaly skin (phrynoderma, "toad skin"), poor growth, poor wound healing, fatty liver |
| **Trans fatty acids** | Vanaspati, some margarines, bakery and deep-fried foods, re-used frying oil | **Raise LDL and lower HDL** — the most harmful dietary fat; increase coronary heart disease risk. India's food regulator (FSSAI) limits trans fat in edible oils and fats — check the current permitted level |

Saturated fats (ghee, butter, coconut and palm oil, red meat) raise LDL cholesterol when eaten in excess.

## 2.3 Digestion and absorption of lipids

```text
MOUTH/STOMACH  Lingual and gastric lipase — minor (more important in infants: milk fat)
DUODENUM       Bile salts (from liver via gall bladder) EMULSIFY fat into small droplets
               Pancreatic lipase (+ colipase) → triacylglycerol → 2-monoacylglycerol + 2 fatty acids
               Cholesterol esterase → cholesterol esters → cholesterol + fatty acid
               Phospholipase A2 → phospholipids → lysophospholipid + fatty acid
JEJUNUM        Products + bile salts form MICELLES → reach the brush border → enter cells
INSIDE CELL    Long-chain fatty acids re-made into triacylglycerol + cholesterol esters
               + apoprotein B-48 → CHYLOMICRONS → lacteals (lymph) → thoracic duct → blood
               Short- and medium-chain fatty acids → straight into portal blood
BILE SALTS     Reabsorbed in the ileum and returned to the liver (enterohepatic circulation)
```

**Clinical points**

- No bile (obstructive jaundice) or no pancreatic lipase (chronic pancreatitis, cystic fibrosis) → fat is not absorbed → **steatorrhoea** (bulky, pale, greasy, foul-smelling stools that float) and deficiency of vitamins A, D, E and K (bleeding from vitamin K deficiency).
- Medium-chain triglyceride (MCT) oil is used in diets for fat malabsorption because it needs little bile or lipase and goes directly to portal blood.

## 2.4 Metabolism of lipids

| Process | Site | Summary | Key points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Lipolysis** | Adipose tissue | Stored triacylglycerol → glycerol + free fatty acids | Hormone-sensitive lipase; activated by adrenaline, glucagon, cortisol; inhibited by insulin |
| **β-oxidation of fatty acids** | Mitochondria (liver, muscle, heart) | Fatty acid broken two carbons at a time → acetyl-CoA, NADH, FADH2 | Long-chain fatty acids enter mitochondria with **carnitine**; high energy yield (palmitate gives about 106 ATP); brain and RBCs cannot use fatty acids |
| **Fatty acid synthesis** | Cytoplasm (liver, adipose, lactating breast) | Acetyl-CoA → fatty acids | Needs NADPH (from HMP shunt) and biotin; stimulated by insulin and a carbohydrate-rich diet |
| **Triacylglycerol synthesis** | Liver, adipose | Glycerol-3-phosphate + fatty acids | Liver exports it as VLDL |
| **Cholesterol synthesis** | Liver mainly (all nucleated cells can) | Acetyl-CoA → HMG-CoA → mevalonate → … → cholesterol | Rate-limiting enzyme **HMG-CoA reductase**, the target of statin drugs |
| **Ketogenesis** | Liver mitochondria only | Excess acetyl-CoA → ketone bodies | See 2.6 |
| **Phospholipid metabolism** | Most tissues | Lecithin, sphingomyelin etc. | Lung surfactant is mainly dipalmitoyl lecithin |

Glycerol from fat can be made into glucose (gluconeogenesis), but fatty acids cannot be converted into glucose.

## 2.5 Disorders of lipid metabolism

| Disorder | Basis | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Obesity** | Energy intake more than use → fat storage | Risk of diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, fatty liver |
| **Fatty liver** | Fat made or brought to the liver faster than exported as VLDL | Alcohol, obesity, diabetes, protein deficiency (kwashiorkor) and lack of choline/methionine (**lipotropic factors**); can progress to cirrhosis |
| **Hyperlipoproteinaemias / dyslipidaemias** | Raised cholesterol and/or triglycerides (primary inherited types I–V, or secondary to diabetes, hypothyroidism, nephrotic syndrome, alcohol) | Premature atherosclerosis, xanthomas; very high triglycerides cause acute pancreatitis |
| **Familial hypercholesterolaemia** | Defective LDL receptors | Very high LDL, tendon xanthomas, heart attacks at a young age |
| **Ketosis / ketoacidosis** | Excess ketone bodies | Starvation, uncontrolled diabetes (see 2.6) |
| **Lipid storage diseases (sphingolipidoses)** | Inherited lysosomal enzyme defects | Tay-Sachs disease (nerve degeneration, cherry-red spot), Gaucher's disease (large spleen, bone pain), Niemann-Pick disease |
| **Steatorrhoea** | Poor digestion/absorption | See 2.3 |
| **Respiratory distress syndrome of the newborn** | Lack of lung surfactant in preterm babies | Alveoli collapse |

## 2.6 Ketone bodies (names, types and significance)

**Names**: three compounds made in liver mitochondria from acetyl-CoA:

| Ketone body | Type | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Acetoacetate** | Primary ketone body (a keto acid) | The first one formed |
| **β-hydroxybutyrate** | Formed from acetoacetate by reduction (strictly a hydroxy acid, not a ketone) | Main ketone body in blood in ketoacidosis |
| **Acetone** | Formed by loss of CO2 from acetoacetate; volatile | Breathed out — gives the **fruity breath** in ketoacidosis |

**Significance**

1. **Normal fuel**: heart muscle, skeletal muscle and kidney use ketone bodies; in prolonged starvation the **brain** adapts to use them, sparing body protein. The liver makes them but cannot use them.
2. **Ketosis**: increased ketone bodies in blood (ketonaemia) and urine (ketonuria) when fat breakdown is high and carbohydrate is unavailable — **starvation, prolonged fasting or vomiting, uncontrolled diabetes**, very low-carbohydrate diets, alcohol excess.
3. **Ketoacidosis**: acetoacetate and β-hydroxybutyrate are acids; in excess they use up bicarbonate → metabolic acidosis with high anion gap (diabetic ketoacidosis). Features: vomiting, dehydration, Kussmaul breathing, fruity breath, drowsiness, coma.
4. **Detection**: **Rothera's test** (sodium nitroprusside) and urine dipsticks give a purple colour with acetoacetate and acetone; they **do not detect β-hydroxybutyrate**, so a urine test can underestimate ketosis.

## 2.7 Compounds formed from cholesterol

Cholesterol is a steroid alcohol found only in animal foods (egg yolk, meat, organ meats, butter, ghee). The body also makes it in the liver.

```text
                       ┌─→ Bile acids and bile salts (cholic, chenodeoxycholic acid) — fat digestion
                       │       (main route of cholesterol removal from the body)
                       ├─→ Steroid hormones:
CHOLESTEROL ───────────┤     • Glucocorticoids (cortisol)       — adrenal cortex
                       │     • Mineralocorticoids (aldosterone) — adrenal cortex
                       │     • Sex hormones: progesterone, testosterone, oestrogens
                       ├─→ Vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol) — 7-dehydrocholesterol in skin + sunlight (UV)
                       └─→ Part of cell membranes and myelin; cholesterol esters for storage
```

## 2.8 Lipoproteins — types and functions

Lipids do not dissolve in plasma, so they travel as **lipoproteins**: a core of triacylglycerol and cholesterol esters, covered by a shell of phospholipid, free cholesterol and proteins called **apolipoproteins** (apo B-48, B-100, A-I, C-II, E).

| Lipoprotein | Made in | Main lipid carried | Function | Density |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Chylomicrons** | Intestine | Dietary triacylglycerol | Carry dietary fat from gut to tissues (muscle, adipose); remnants to liver | Lowest density, largest |
| **VLDL** (very low density) | Liver | Endogenous triacylglycerol | Carry fat made in the liver to tissues | Very low |
| **IDL** (intermediate) | From VLDL | TG and cholesterol | Short-lived; becomes LDL | Low |
| **LDL** (low density) | From VLDL/IDL in blood | **Cholesterol** (richest in cholesterol) | Carry cholesterol to tissues; excess enters artery walls → atherosclerosis ("**bad cholesterol**") | Low |
| **HDL** (high density) | Liver, intestine | Protein-rich; collects cholesterol | **Reverse cholesterol transport** — takes excess cholesterol from tissues back to the liver; protective ("**good cholesterol**") | Highest density, smallest |

On electrophoresis, HDL moves as α-lipoprotein, LDL as β-lipoprotein, VLDL as pre-β; chylomicrons stay at the origin. **Lipoprotein (a)** is an LDL-like particle; a high level is an inherited risk for heart disease.

## 2.9 Lipid profile

**Preparation**: classically a 10–12 hour overnight fast (water allowed), because triacylglycerol rises after a meal. Many labs now accept non-fasting samples for cholesterol; follow the lab instruction. Avoid alcohol and heavy fatty meals the day before.

| Parameter | Desirable / normal (adults, about) |
| --- | --- |
| Total cholesterol | below 200 mg/dL |
| LDL cholesterol | below 100 mg/dL (optimal); 160 and above is high |
| HDL cholesterol | above 40 mg/dL in men and above 50 mg/dL in women; 60 and above is protective |
| Triacylglycerols (triglycerides) | below 150 mg/dL |
| VLDL cholesterol | about 5–40 mg/dL (calculated as TG ÷ 5) |
| Total cholesterol : HDL ratio | below about 4.5–5 |

**Friedewald formula** (all in mg/dL; valid when TG is below about 400):

```text
LDL cholesterol = Total cholesterol − HDL cholesterol − (Triglycerides ÷ 5)

Example: TC 220, HDL 40, TG 150 → LDL = 220 − 40 − 30 = 150 mg/dL (high)
```

People with diabetes or known heart disease are given lower LDL targets by their doctors.

## 2.10 Atherosclerosis (in brief)

**Definition**: a disease of large and medium arteries in which lipid-rich plaques (atheromas) form in the inner wall (intima), narrowing the lumen and making the wall hard and fragile.

**Risk factors**

- Cannot change: increasing age, male sex, family history, genetic dyslipidaemia
- Can change: high LDL, low HDL, high triglycerides, smoking, hypertension, diabetes, obesity, physical inactivity, diet rich in saturated and trans fat, stress

**How it develops**

```text
Damage to the endothelium (high BP, smoking, high glucose, high LDL)
        ↓
LDL enters the intima and is oxidised
        ↓
Monocytes enter → become macrophages → swallow oxidised LDL → FOAM CELLS
        ↓
Fatty streak (earliest visible lesion, even in young people)
        ↓
Smooth muscle cells move in, make collagen → FIBROUS PLAQUE (fibrous cap over a lipid core)
        ↓
Calcification, narrowing; cap may RUPTURE → platelet clot (thrombus) → sudden blockage
```

**Results**: angina and myocardial infarction (coronary arteries), stroke (cerebral/carotid arteries), peripheral vascular disease and gangrene (leg arteries), aneurysm (aorta), renal artery stenosis.

**Prevention and nursing role**: teach a diet low in saturated and trans fat and rich in fibre, fruits and vegetables and MUFA/PUFA oils; regular exercise; weight control; stopping smoking; control of blood pressure, diabetes and lipids (statins as prescribed); regular lipid profile checks.

## 2.11 Application in nursing

- Prepare patients correctly for a lipid profile (fasting if the lab asks; note recent illness, since values fall after an acute illness or MI).
- Check urine for ketones in diabetic, vomiting or starving patients; remember the dipstick misses β-hydroxybutyrate.
- In obstructive jaundice and pancreatic disease, watch for steatorrhoea and bleeding (vitamin K).
- Diet counselling: limit vanaspati, re-used frying oil and bakery products (trans fat); use a mix of oils; include fish or other ω-3 sources.
- Preterm babies may lack surfactant (a phospholipid); antenatal steroids given to the mother help the lungs mature.

## Remember

- Fatty acid = hydrocarbon chain with –COOH; MUFA has one double bond (oleic), PUFA two or more (linoleic, linolenic, arachidonic).
- Essential fatty acids: linoleic and α-linolenic; deficiency → phrynoderma.
- Trans fats raise LDL and lower HDL — the worst dietary fat.
- Fat digestion needs bile salts (emulsify) and pancreatic lipase; long-chain fats travel as chylomicrons in lymph.
- β-oxidation is in mitochondria and needs carnitine; HMG-CoA reductase controls cholesterol synthesis.
- Ketone bodies: acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate, acetone; made in liver, used by heart, muscle, kidney and (in starvation) brain; Rothera's test misses β-hydroxybutyrate.
- From cholesterol: bile acids, steroid hormones, vitamin D3.
- Chylomicrons — dietary fat; VLDL — liver fat; LDL — cholesterol to tissues (bad); HDL — reverse transport (good).
- Desirable (about): total cholesterol <200, LDL <100, HDL >40 (men)/>50 (women), TG <150 mg/dL; LDL = TC − HDL − TG/5.
- Atherosclerosis: endothelial injury → oxidised LDL → foam cells → fatty streak → fibrous plaque → rupture and clot.

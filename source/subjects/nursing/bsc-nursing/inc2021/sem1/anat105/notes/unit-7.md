# Unit 7: The renal and reproductive systems

This notes file joins **INC Unit VIII, the renal system** (5 hours: kidney, ureters, bladder, urethra) and **INC Unit IX, the reproductive system** (5 hours: male organs, female organs, breast). Urine formation, the menstrual cycle and hormones are in Applied Physiology.

## Part A — The renal (urinary) system

```text
 Kidneys (make urine) → Ureters (carry) → Urinary bladder (stores) → Urethra (passes out)
```

## 7.1 Kidneys: position and relations

- Two bean-shaped organs, each about **11 cm long, 6 cm wide, 3 cm thick**, weighing about 150 g.
- Lie on the posterior abdominal wall, **behind the peritoneum (retroperitoneal)**, on either side of the vertebral column, roughly from **T12 to L3**.
- The **right kidney is a little lower** than the left because the liver lies above it.
- Upper poles touch the diaphragm; the **adrenal glands** sit on top. The lower ribs (11th and 12th) cross the back of the kidneys.
- Relations in front: right kidney — liver, duodenum, hepatic flexure of colon; left kidney — stomach, spleen, pancreas, splenic flexure, jejunum.

**Coverings (inside to outside)**

1. **Renal (fibrous) capsule** — thin, tough, close to the surface.
2. **Perirenal fat (adipose capsule)** — cushions the kidney.
3. **Renal fascia** — holds the kidney and adrenal gland to the posterior wall.
4. Pararenal fat outside the fascia.

## 7.2 Internal structure of the kidney

A cut (coronal) section shows:

```text
       Capsule
  ┌──────────────────────────┐
  │ CORTEX (outer, granular) │
  │   Renal columns dip in   │
  │  ▼ ▼ MEDULLA: pyramids ▼ │
  │   papilla → minor calyx  │
  │   → major calyx          │
  │   → RENAL PELVIS ────────┼──► Ureter
  │   Hilum: renal artery in,│
  │   renal vein out         │
  └──────────────────────────┘
```

- **Cortex**: outer reddish layer containing glomeruli and convoluted tubules.
- **Medulla**: 8–18 cone-shaped **renal pyramids** (striped because of loops of Henle and collecting ducts); their tips (papillae) open into **minor calyces** → **major calyces** (2–3) → **renal pelvis** → ureter.
- **Renal columns**: extensions of cortex between pyramids.
- **Hilum**: notch on the medial border where the renal artery enters and the renal vein, ureter (pelvis), nerves and lymphatics pass.

## 7.3 The nephron

The **nephron** is the structural and functional unit of the kidney; each kidney has about one million.

```text
Afferent arteriole → GLOMERULUS (capillary tuft) → Efferent arteriole → peritubular capillaries
                        inside Bowman's capsule
RENAL CORPUSCLE = Glomerulus + Bowman's (glomerular) capsule
   ↓
Proximal convoluted tubule (cortex; cuboidal cells with brush border)
   ↓
Loop of Henle: descending limb → hairpin bend → ascending limb (dips into medulla)
   ↓
Distal convoluted tubule (cortex; touches its own afferent arteriole at the
                          juxtaglomerular apparatus)
   ↓
Collecting duct (shared by many nephrons) → papilla → minor calyx
```

- **Cortical nephrons** (most) have short loops; **juxtamedullary nephrons** have long loops reaching deep into the medulla (concentrating urine).
- **Juxtaglomerular apparatus**: juxtaglomerular cells in the afferent arteriole wall (secrete renin) plus the macula densa of the distal tubule.
- The **filtration membrane**: glomerular capillary endothelium (with pores), basement membrane, and the podocytes of the inner layer of Bowman's capsule.

**Blood supply**: renal artery (from the abdominal aorta) → segmental → interlobar → arcuate → interlobular arteries → afferent arterioles → glomeruli → efferent arterioles → peritubular capillaries and vasa recta → veins → renal vein → inferior vena cava. The kidneys receive about one-fifth of the cardiac output.

## 7.4 Ureters

- Two muscular tubes about **25–30 cm** long and 3 mm wide, carrying urine from the renal pelvis to the bladder by **peristalsis**.
- Run down on the posterior abdominal wall (on the psoas muscle), cross the pelvic brim near the bifurcation of the common iliac artery, and enter the back of the bladder obliquely.
- Wall: transitional epithelium (mucosa), smooth muscle, fibrous outer coat.
- **Three narrow points**: pelviureteric junction, crossing of the pelvic brim, and entry into the bladder. Kidney stones commonly lodge here and cause colicky pain radiating from loin to groin.
- The oblique passage through the bladder wall acts as a valve preventing backflow.

## 7.5 Urinary bladder

- Hollow muscular sac in the pelvis, behind the symphysis pubis. In females it lies in front of the uterus and vagina; in males in front of the rectum, above the prostate.
- When full it rises into the abdomen and can be felt above the symphysis pubis (a distended bladder in retention).
- Capacity: usually about 400–600 ml in adults; the urge to pass urine is felt at a few hundred ml (figures vary).
- **Wall**: transitional epithelium in folds (rugae) when empty; the **detrusor muscle** (three layers of smooth muscle); outer serous/adventitial coat.
- **Trigone**: smooth triangular area on the inside of the base between the two ureteric openings and the internal urethral opening; it does not stretch. Infection often starts here.
- **Sphincters**: internal urethral sphincter (smooth muscle at the bladder neck, involuntary) and external urethral sphincter (skeletal muscle in the pelvic floor, voluntary).

## 7.6 Urethra

| Feature | Female | Male |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Length | About **4 cm** | About **18–20 cm** |
| Course | Straight, behind the symphysis pubis; opens in the vestibule between the clitoris and vaginal opening | S-shaped; passes through the prostate, pelvic floor and penis |
| Parts | — | Prostatic (about 3 cm, ejaculatory ducts open), membranous (shortest and narrowest, through the external sphincter), spongy/penile (longest, in the corpus spongiosum) |
| Function | Urine only | Urine and semen |

The short female urethra close to the anus explains why urinary tract infections are much more common in women.

## Part B — The reproductive system

## 7.7 Male reproductive organs

```text
 Testis (sperm + testosterone) → Epididymis (stores, matures sperm)
 → Vas (ductus) deferens → joins the duct of the seminal vesicle → Ejaculatory duct
 → through the prostate → Urethra → out through the penis
```

| Organ | Structure |
| --- | --- |
| **Scrotum** | Pouch of skin and dartos muscle outside the body; keeps the testes about 2–3 °C below body temperature, needed for sperm production |
| **Testes** | Two oval glands (about 4–5 cm); covered by the **tunica vaginalis** (outside) and the tough **tunica albuginea**; divided into about 250 lobules containing coiled **seminiferous tubules** (sperm form here from spermatogonia, supported by **Sertoli cells**); **interstitial (Leydig) cells** between the tubules make **testosterone** |
| **Epididymis** | Highly coiled tube (about 6 m uncoiled) on the back of the testis — head, body, tail; sperm mature and are stored |
| **Vas (ductus) deferens** | Muscular tube about 45 cm; travels in the **spermatic cord** (with testicular artery, pampiniform plexus of veins, nerves and lymphatics) through the **inguinal canal** into the pelvis; this is the tube cut in vasectomy |
| **Seminal vesicles** | Two coiled glands behind the bladder; their alkaline fluid with fructose forms most of the semen |
| **Ejaculatory ducts** | Formed by the vas and the seminal vesicle duct; pass through the prostate into the urethra |
| **Prostate gland** | Chestnut-sized gland below the bladder surrounding the first part of the urethra; adds a thin milky fluid; enlarges in older men (benign enlargement) and can obstruct urine flow; felt through the rectum |
| **Bulbourethral (Cowper's) glands** | Pea-sized, below the prostate; mucus for lubrication |
| **Penis** | Root, body and glans; three columns of erectile tissue — two **corpora cavernosa** (upper) and one **corpus spongiosum** (lower, contains the urethra, expands as the glans); foreskin (prepuce) covers the glans |

The testes develop in the abdomen and descend through the inguinal canal into the scrotum before birth; an undescended testis needs attention in the newborn check.

## 7.8 Female reproductive organs

**External genitalia (vulva)**: mons pubis, labia majora, labia minora, clitoris (erectile tissue), vestibule (with urethral and vaginal openings), greater vestibular (Bartholin's) glands, and the perineum (area between the vaginal opening and anus, which may tear in childbirth).

**Internal organs**

```text
         Uterine tube   Fundus   Uterine tube
   Ovary  (fimbriae)  ┌───────┐  (fimbriae)  Ovary
      ○ ~~~~~~~~~~~~~ │ Body  │ ~~~~~~~~~~~~~ ○
                      │Cavity │
                       \ Isthmus /
                        │Cervix│
                        │      │ ← internal os / external os
                        │Vagina│
```

| Organ | Structure |
| --- | --- |
| **Vagina** | Fibromuscular tube about 8–10 cm, from the vestibule up and back to the cervix; lined with stratified squamous non-keratinised epithelium (no glands; acidic secretions protect against infection); the spaces around the cervix are the **fornices** (posterior one deepest) |
| **Uterus** | Hollow, pear-shaped muscular organ, about 7.5 cm long, 5 cm wide, 2.5 cm thick (non-pregnant), between the bladder and rectum; usually **anteverted and anteflexed** (tilted forward). Parts: **fundus** (above the tubes), **body**, **isthmus**, **cervix** (with internal os and external os) |
| Uterine wall | **Endometrium** (mucous lining; functional layer shed in menstruation, basal layer regenerates it), **myometrium** (thick smooth muscle in interlacing layers; contracts in labour and compresses blood vessels after delivery), **perimetrium** (peritoneum) |
| Supports of the uterus | Pelvic floor (levator ani), **transverse cervical (cardinal) ligaments**, uterosacral ligaments, round ligaments (keep it anteverted), broad ligaments (folds of peritoneum, carrying the tubes and vessels); weakness leads to prolapse |
| **Uterine (fallopian) tubes** | About 10 cm each; parts: interstitial (in the uterine wall), isthmus (narrow), **ampulla** (widest; **usual site of fertilisation** and of ectopic pregnancy), infundibulum with finger-like **fimbriae** near the ovary; lined by ciliated columnar epithelium; cilia and peristalsis move the ovum |
| **Ovaries** | Two almond-shaped glands (about 3 × 2 × 1 cm) on each side of the uterus, attached to the broad ligament (mesovarium) and to the uterus (ovarian ligament); outer **cortex** with follicles at various stages and the **corpus luteum**, inner **medulla** with vessels; produce ova, oestrogen and progesterone |

**Blood supply**: uterine artery (from the internal iliac; crosses above the ureter near the cervix — "water under the bridge", important in hysterectomy) and ovarian artery (from the abdominal aorta).

## 7.9 Breast (mammary gland)

- Modified sweat glands lying in the superficial fascia over the **pectoralis major**, from about the 2nd to the 6th rib, from the sternal edge to the mid-axillary line. The **axillary tail** extends into the axilla.
- **Nipple** (with openings of 15–20 lactiferous ducts) surrounded by the pigmented **areola**, which has Montgomery's glands (sebaceous, lubricate during feeding).
- **Glandular tissue**: 15–20 **lobes** arranged like the spokes of a wheel; each lobe has lobules of milk-secreting **alveoli** → ducts → **lactiferous duct**, which widens under the areola (lactiferous sinus) before opening on the nipple.
- **Supporting tissue**: fat (gives size and shape) and fibrous bands — **suspensory (Cooper's) ligaments**. Cancer pulling on these ligaments causes dimpling of the skin.
- **Lymphatic drainage**: about three-quarters to the **axillary lymph nodes**, the rest mainly to internal mammary (parasternal) nodes — so breast cancer often spreads to the axilla first.
- Male breast is rudimentary.

```text
  Chest wall │ Pectoralis major │ fat + lobes ──► ducts ──► sinus ──► nipple
             │                  │   Cooper's ligaments to skin
```

## 7.10 Application and implication in nursing

- **Catheterisation**: the female urethra is short and opens just above the vagina; in males the S-shaped urethra is straightened by holding the penis upward during insertion. Strict asepsis is essential, especially in females.
- **Bladder assessment**: a palpable bladder above the pubis suggests retention; record intake and output.
- **Renal angle**: tenderness at the angle between the 12th rib and the spine (loin) suggests kidney infection.
- **Kidney biopsy and nephrostomy** depend on the kidney's position behind the lower ribs.
- **Perineal care** from front to back reduces urinary infection in females.
- **Breast self-examination** covers the whole breast including the axillary tail; women should know their normal breast and report lumps, dimpling or nipple changes.
- **Antenatal and postnatal care** depend on knowing the uterus (fundal height), cervix and pelvic floor; pelvic floor exercises reduce prolapse and incontinence.
- **Testicular self-examination** helps detect lumps early in young men.

## Remember

- Kidneys: retroperitoneal, T12–L3, right lower than left; capsule, perirenal fat, renal fascia.
- Cortex (glomeruli, convoluted tubules); medulla (pyramids).
- Nephron: glomerulus + Bowman's capsule → PCT → loop of Henle → DCT → collecting duct.
- Ureter 25–30 cm, three narrow points; bladder trigone; detrusor muscle.
- Urethra: female 4 cm; male 18–20 cm (prostatic, membranous, spongy).
- Testis: seminiferous tubules (sperm), Leydig cells (testosterone).
- Path of sperm: testis → epididymis → vas deferens → ejaculatory duct → urethra.
- Uterus: fundus, body, isthmus, cervix; endometrium, myometrium, perimetrium; anteverted, anteflexed.
- Fertilisation usually in the ampulla of the uterine tube.
- Breast: 15–20 lobes, lactiferous ducts, Cooper's ligaments; most lymph to axillary nodes.

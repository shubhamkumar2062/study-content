# Unit 8: The nervous system

INC Unit X (6 hours): review of the neuron, the divisions of the nervous system (CNS, ANS, PNS), structure of the brain, spinal cord, cranial nerves, spinal and peripheral nerves, functional areas of the cerebral cortex, and the ventricular system (formation, circulation and drainage of cerebrospinal fluid). The physiology of nerve impulses, reflexes and pain is in Applied Physiology.

## 8.1 Divisions of the nervous system

```text
NERVOUS SYSTEM
├── Central nervous system (CNS): brain + spinal cord
└── Peripheral nervous system (PNS): 12 pairs cranial nerves + 31 pairs spinal nerves
     ├── Sensory (afferent) division: carries information to the CNS
     └── Motor (efferent) division
          ├── Somatic: to skeletal muscle (voluntary)
          └── Autonomic (ANS): to smooth muscle, cardiac muscle, glands (involuntary)
               ├── Sympathetic (thoracolumbar)
               └── Parasympathetic (craniosacral)
```

## 8.2 Structure of the neuron (review)

```text
 Dendrites → Cell body (nucleus, Nissl bodies) → Axon hillock → Axon
    → (myelin sheath with gaps = nodes of Ranvier) → Axon terminals → Synapse
```

| Part | Feature |
| --- | --- |
| Cell body (soma) | Nucleus and Nissl bodies (rough ER); found in grey matter and ganglia |
| Dendrites | Short branched processes carrying impulses towards the cell body |
| Axon | Single long process carrying impulses away; may be myelinated |
| Myelin sheath | Fatty insulating layer made by **Schwann cells** in the PNS and **oligodendrocytes** in the CNS; gaps are **nodes of Ranvier**; speeds conduction |
| Synapse | Junction between neurons, where a chemical transmitter crosses the gap |

**Types by structure**: multipolar (most; motor neurons), bipolar (retina, olfactory epithelium), unipolar/pseudounipolar (sensory neurons with cell bodies in dorsal root ganglia).
**Types by function**: sensory (afferent), motor (efferent), interneurons (connector, within the CNS).

**Neuroglia (supporting cells)**: astrocytes (support, help form the blood–brain barrier), oligodendrocytes (CNS myelin), microglia (phagocytes), ependymal cells (line the ventricles and central canal; help form CSF), Schwann cells and satellite cells (PNS).

**Grey matter** = cell bodies and dendrites; **white matter** = myelinated axons (the myelin looks white). A **nerve** is a bundle of axons outside the CNS; a **tract** is a bundle inside the CNS; a **ganglion** is a group of cell bodies outside the CNS.

## 8.3 Coverings of the CNS: meninges

| Layer | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Dura mater** (outer) | Tough fibrous layer; in the skull it has two layers that separate to form the **dural venous sinuses**; folds include the falx cerebri (between hemispheres) and tentorium cerebelli (between cerebrum and cerebellum) |
| **Arachnoid mater** (middle) | Thin, web-like; separated from the pia by the **subarachnoid space** containing CSF and large vessels; **arachnoid villi (granulations)** project into the venous sinuses |
| **Pia mater** (inner) | Delicate, vascular, closely follows every fold of the brain and cord |

Spaces: **extradural (epidural)** — between bone and dura (in the vertebral canal it contains fat and veins; used for epidural anaesthesia); **subdural** — potential space between dura and arachnoid; **subarachnoid** — contains CSF (lumbar puncture samples this).

## 8.4 The brain

The adult brain weighs about 1.3–1.4 kg.

```text
 BRAIN
 ├── Forebrain: Cerebrum (two hemispheres) + Diencephalon (thalamus, hypothalamus)
 ├── Midbrain
 └── Hindbrain: Pons, Medulla oblongata, Cerebellum
 Brainstem = Midbrain + Pons + Medulla oblongata
```

### Cerebrum

- The largest part; two **cerebral hemispheres** separated by the longitudinal fissure and joined by the **corpus callosum** (a large band of white matter).
- Surface folded into ridges (**gyri**) and grooves (**sulci**), which increase the area of grey matter.
- **Cerebral cortex**: outer grey matter, a few millimetres thick. **White matter** below it, with **basal nuclei (ganglia)** deep inside (help control movement; damaged in Parkinson's disease).
- **Lobes** (named after the overlying bones), separated by major sulci:
  - **Frontal** — in front of the central sulcus.
  - **Parietal** — behind the central sulcus.
  - **Temporal** — below the lateral sulcus.
  - **Occipital** — at the back.
  - (Insula — hidden deep in the lateral sulcus.)

### Functional areas of the cerebral cortex

| Area | Location | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary motor area** | Precentral gyrus (frontal lobe, in front of the central sulcus) | Voluntary movement of the **opposite** side of the body; body parts are mapped upside-down (motor homunculus) |
| Premotor area | In front of the motor area | Plans skilled, learned movements |
| **Broca's (motor speech) area** | Inferior frontal gyrus, usually of the **left** hemisphere | Producing speech; damage → expressive aphasia |
| Prefrontal area | Front of the frontal lobe | Personality, judgement, behaviour, planning |
| **Primary somatosensory area** | Postcentral gyrus (parietal lobe) | Touch, pain, temperature, position from the opposite side |
| **Primary visual area** | Occipital lobe | Vision |
| **Primary auditory area** | Superior temporal gyrus | Hearing |
| **Wernicke's (sensory speech) area** | Back of the superior temporal gyrus, usually left | Understanding speech; damage → receptive aphasia |
| Gustatory area | Near the lower end of the postcentral gyrus (and insula) | Taste |
| Olfactory area | Medial temporal lobe | Smell |

```text
             Central sulcus
   FRONTAL     |     PARIETAL
  (motor,      |   (sensory)
  Broca's)     |
 ──────────── Lateral sulcus ──────
   TEMPORAL (hearing, Wernicke's)   OCCIPITAL (vision)
```

### Diencephalon

- **Thalamus**: paired oval masses of grey matter on each side of the third ventricle; relay station for sensations going to the cortex.
- **Hypothalamus**: below the thalamus; controls the ANS, temperature, hunger, thirst and the pituitary (see Unit 5).

### Brainstem

| Part | Structure and function |
| --- | --- |
| **Midbrain** | Between diencephalon and pons; contains the **cerebral aqueduct**; nuclei of cranial nerves III and IV; reflex centres for sight and hearing |
| **Pons** | Bridge of fibres in front of the cerebellum; nuclei of V–VIII; helps regulate breathing |
| **Medulla oblongata** | Continuous with the spinal cord at the foramen magnum; **vital centres** — cardiac, respiratory and vasomotor; nuclei of IX–XII; motor tracts cross here (**decussation of the pyramids**), which is why each side of the brain controls the opposite side of the body |

### Cerebellum

- Lies behind the pons and medulla, under the occipital lobes, separated by the tentorium cerebelli.
- Two hemispheres joined by the vermis; outer grey cortex in fine folds (folia), white matter inside with a tree-like pattern (arbor vitae).
- Coordinates movement, balance and posture. Damage → unsteady gait (ataxia), intention tremor.

## 8.5 The spinal cord

- Cylindrical cord about **45 cm** long in adults, from the **foramen magnum** to about the **L1–L2** vertebra, where it tapers as the **conus medullaris**. A thread of pia, the **filum terminale**, anchors it to the coccyx.
- Two enlargements: cervical (for the upper limbs) and lumbar (for the lower limbs).
- Below L2 the vertebral canal contains the nerve roots of the lower spinal nerves — the **cauda equina** ("horse's tail"). This is why lumbar puncture below L2 does not injure the cord.
- **Cross-section**: central **H- (butterfly-) shaped grey matter** around the central canal; **white matter** around it in columns carrying ascending (sensory) and descending (motor) tracts.

```text
           Dorsal (posterior) root with ganglion — sensory in
                   │
     ┌─────────────┼─────────────┐
     │  white   ┌──┴──┐  white   │
     │  matter  │ GREY│  matter  │   dorsal horn = sensory
     │       ───┤  •  ├───       │   • = central canal
     │          └──┬──┘          │   ventral horn = motor
     └─────────────┼─────────────┘   lateral horn (T1–L2) = sympathetic
           Ventral (anterior) root — motor out
       Dorsal root + Ventral root → Spinal nerve
```

## 8.6 Spinal nerves and plexuses

- **31 pairs**: 8 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral, 1 coccygeal. (There are 8 cervical nerves but only 7 cervical vertebrae.)
- Each forms from a **dorsal (sensory) root** with its ganglion and a **ventral (motor) root**, so every spinal nerve is **mixed**. It exits through the intervertebral foramen and divides into dorsal and ventral rami.
- **Dermatome**: the area of skin supplied by one spinal nerve (e.g. the umbilicus is around T10), used to test the level of a spinal injury and of spinal anaesthesia.
- The thoracic ventral rami run as **intercostal nerves**; the others join to form **plexuses**:

| Plexus | Roots | Important nerves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cervical | C1–C4 | **Phrenic nerve (C3–C5)** to the diaphragm |
| Brachial | C5–T1 | Axillary (deltoid), musculocutaneous (biceps), **radial** (back of arm and forearm; damage → wrist drop), **median** (front of forearm, thumb muscles; carpal tunnel), **ulnar** ("funny bone" at the elbow; small hand muscles) |
| Lumbar | L1–L4 | Femoral (front of thigh, quadriceps), obturator (adductors) |
| Sacral | L4–S4 | **Sciatic nerve** (largest nerve of the body; back of the thigh, divides into tibial and common peroneal nerves), pudendal (perineum, external sphincters) |

## 8.7 Cranial nerves

Twelve pairs arising from the brain (mostly the brainstem).

| No. | Name | Type | Main function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | Olfactory | Sensory | Smell |
| II | Optic | Sensory | Vision |
| III | Oculomotor | Motor (and parasympathetic) | Most eye movements, raises the eyelid, constricts the pupil |
| IV | Trochlear | Motor | Superior oblique muscle of eye |
| V | Trigeminal | Mixed | Sensation of face, scalp, cornea, teeth; muscles of chewing |
| VI | Abducens | Motor | Lateral rectus (turns eye outward) |
| VII | Facial | Mixed | Facial expression; taste from anterior 2/3 of tongue; lacrimal and salivary glands |
| VIII | Vestibulocochlear | Sensory | Hearing and balance |
| IX | Glossopharyngeal | Mixed | Taste from posterior 1/3 of tongue; swallowing; parotid gland |
| X | Vagus | Mixed | Pharynx, larynx; parasympathetic to the heart, lungs and gut |
| XI | Accessory | Motor | Sternocleidomastoid and trapezius |
| XII | Hypoglossal | Motor | Muscles of the tongue |

Memory aid for names: "Oh Oh Oh To Touch And Feel Very Good Velvet, Ah Heaven". For type (sensory/motor/both): "Some Say Marry Money, But My Brother Says Big Brains Matter More".

## 8.8 The autonomic nervous system (structure)

The ANS uses a **two-neuron pathway**: a preganglionic neuron from the CNS to a ganglion, and a postganglionic neuron from the ganglion to the organ.

| Feature | Sympathetic | Parasympathetic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outflow | Thoracolumbar (lateral horns T1–L2) | Craniosacral (cranial nerves III, VII, IX, X and sacral segments S2–S4) |
| Ganglia | Close to the spinal cord, in the **sympathetic chain (trunk)** on each side of the vertebral column, and prevertebral ganglia (coeliac, mesenteric) | Close to or within the organ |
| Fibres | Short preganglionic, long postganglionic | Long preganglionic, short postganglionic |
| Main transmitter at organ | Noradrenaline (mostly) | Acetylcholine |
| General effect | "Fight or flight": raises heart rate, widens pupils and airways, reduces gut activity | "Rest and digest": slows heart, constricts pupils, increases gut activity and secretions |

## 8.9 Ventricular system and cerebrospinal fluid

**Ventricles** are cavities in the brain lined by ependyma and filled with CSF.

| Ventricle | Location |
| --- | --- |
| Two **lateral ventricles** | One in each cerebral hemisphere (C-shaped) |
| **Third ventricle** | Narrow slit between the two thalami |
| **Fourth ventricle** | Between the pons/medulla in front and the cerebellum behind |

**Formation**: CSF is made mainly by the **choroid plexuses** (tufts of capillaries covered by ependyma) in the ventricles — mostly in the lateral ventricles. About 500 ml is made per day; the total volume at any time is about 120–150 ml (textbook figures vary slightly).

**Circulation**:

```text
Lateral ventricles
   → Interventricular foramina (of Monro)
      → Third ventricle
         → Cerebral aqueduct (of Sylvius, in the midbrain)
            → Fourth ventricle
               → Median aperture (Magendie) + two lateral apertures (Luschka)
                  → Subarachnoid space (around brain and spinal cord; some into the central canal)
```

**Drainage (absorption)**: through the **arachnoid villi (granulations)** into the **superior sagittal sinus** and other dural venous sinuses, and so into the internal jugular veins.

**Functions in brief**: cushions the brain and cord, keeps the brain buoyant, removes wastes, keeps pressure steady.

**Hydrocephalus**: blockage of flow (often at the narrow cerebral aqueduct) or poor absorption makes CSF collect in the ventricles. In infants the head enlarges because the skull sutures are not fused.

## 8.10 Application and implication in nursing

- **Lumbar puncture**: done at L3–L4 or L4–L5 (below the end of the cord) with the patient curled in the lateral position; the needle passes through skin, ligaments, epidural space, dura and arachnoid into the subarachnoid space. After the procedure observe for headache and CSF leak.
- **Neurological observation**: level of consciousness, pupil size and reaction (oculomotor nerve), limb movement (motor cortex and tracts), vital signs (medulla). A unilateral dilated pupil after head injury is a warning sign.
- **Stroke**: damage in one hemisphere causes weakness on the **opposite** side; left-sided damage often affects speech.
- **Nerve injuries from positioning or injections**: sciatic nerve (dorsogluteal injection), radial nerve (pressure on the back of the arm, crutches), ulnar nerve (pressure at the elbow), common peroneal nerve (pressure at the side of the knee → foot drop). Pad these areas.
- **Spinal injury**: injury above C3–C5 stops the diaphragm; careful log-rolling keeps the spine in line.
- **Cranial nerve tests** (e.g. gag reflex IX/X before oral feeding after a stroke) help assess safety to swallow.

## Remember

- CNS = brain + cord; PNS = 12 cranial + 31 spinal nerve pairs.
- Myelin: Schwann cells (PNS), oligodendrocytes (CNS).
- Meninges: dura, arachnoid, pia; CSF in the subarachnoid space.
- Brainstem = midbrain, pons, medulla; vital centres in the medulla; pyramids cross in the medulla.
- Precentral gyrus = motor; postcentral = sensory; Broca's = speech output; Wernicke's = understanding; occipital = vision.
- Cerebellum = coordination and balance.
- Spinal cord ends at L1–L2; cauda equina below.
- Spinal nerves: 8 C, 12 T, 5 L, 5 S, 1 Co; all are mixed.
- Sympathetic = thoracolumbar; parasympathetic = craniosacral.
- CSF: choroid plexus → lateral → foramen of Monro → 3rd → aqueduct → 4th → Magendie and Luschka → subarachnoid space → arachnoid villi → venous sinuses.

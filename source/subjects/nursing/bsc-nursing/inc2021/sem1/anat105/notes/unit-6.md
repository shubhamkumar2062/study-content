# Unit 6: The musculoskeletal system

INC Unit VII (10 hours — the biggest unit): the skeletal system (anatomical positions; bones — types, structure, growth and ossification; axial and appendicular skeleton; joints) and the muscular system (types and structure of muscle, muscle groups, principal muscles, and the muscles used in nursing procedures such as injections).

## Part A — The skeletal system

## 6.1 Functions of the skeleton (in brief)

Support and shape, protection of organs (skull, ribs, pelvis), movement (levers for muscles), blood cell formation in red marrow, and storage of calcium and phosphate (and fat in yellow marrow).

## 6.2 Types of bones by shape

| Type | Description | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long | Longer than wide; shaft and two ends | Femur, humerus, radius, tibia, phalanges |
| Short | Cube-shaped | Carpals, tarsals |
| Flat | Thin, plate-like, often curved | Skull vault bones, sternum, ribs, scapula |
| Irregular | Complex shapes | Vertebrae, facial bones, hip bone |
| Sesamoid | Develop inside a tendon | Patella (largest), pisiform |
| Sutural (Wormian) | Small extra bones in skull sutures | Variable |

## 6.3 Structure of a long bone

```text
        Proximal epiphysis (spongy bone + red marrow, articular cartilage on top)
        ─── epiphyseal plate (growth plate) / line in adults ───
        Metaphysis
        Diaphysis (shaft):  compact bone wall
                            medullary cavity with yellow marrow
                            endosteum lines the cavity
                            periosteum covers the outside
                            nutrient artery enters through the nutrient foramen
        Metaphysis
        Distal epiphysis
```

- **Periosteum**: tough fibrous outer layer with an inner bone-forming (osteogenic) layer; rich in nerves and blood vessels — this is why bone injuries are painful and why the periosteum is needed for healing. It is not present over articular cartilage.
- **Articular cartilage**: hyaline cartilage on joint surfaces.

**Microscopic structure**

- **Compact bone** is built of **osteons (Haversian systems)**: a central (Haversian) canal with vessels and nerves, surrounded by rings (lamellae) of bone matrix; bone cells (**osteocytes**) live in small spaces (lacunae) joined by tiny canals (canaliculi). Volkmann's canals run across, linking the central canals.
- **Spongy (cancellous) bone**: a lattice of thin plates (trabeculae) with red marrow in the spaces; found at bone ends and inside flat and irregular bones.
- **Bone cells**: osteoblasts (build bone), osteocytes (maintain it), osteoclasts (large cells that break bone down and reshape it).
- **Matrix**: about one-third organic (mainly collagen — gives flexibility) and two-thirds inorganic calcium phosphate salts (hydroxyapatite — gives hardness).

## 6.4 Growth and ossification

**Ossification** is the formation of bone.

| Type | Process | Bones |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intramembranous | Bone forms directly in fibrous membrane | Flat bones of the skull vault, most facial bones, clavicle (partly) |
| Endochondral (intracartilaginous) | A hyaline cartilage model is gradually replaced by bone | Most bones: long bones, vertebrae, base of skull |

**Endochondral ossification of a long bone**

1. A cartilage model of the bone forms in the embryo.
2. A **primary ossification centre** appears in the middle of the shaft; a periosteal collar forms around it.
3. Blood vessels invade; bone replaces cartilage; the medullary cavity forms.
4. **Secondary ossification centres** appear in the epiphyses, mostly after birth.
5. Between the shaft and each epiphysis a plate of cartilage, the **epiphyseal (growth) plate**, keeps growing in length; the shaft side turns into bone.
6. After puberty the plate ossifies completely (the **epiphyseal line**) and growth in length stops.

- **Growth in length**: at the epiphyseal plates.
- **Growth in thickness (width)**: by osteoblasts under the periosteum (appositional growth) while osteoclasts widen the medullary cavity.
- Growth is affected by growth hormone, thyroid hormones, sex hormones, vitamin D, calcium, and weight-bearing exercise. A fracture through the growth plate in a child can affect limb growth.
- **Fontanelles**: membranous gaps in the newborn skull; the **anterior fontanelle** (diamond-shaped) usually closes by about 18 months and the **posterior fontanelle** (triangular) by about 2–3 months (ranges vary).

## 6.5 The skeleton: axial and appendicular

The adult skeleton has **206 bones**: 80 axial and 126 appendicular.

```text
AXIAL (80)                              APPENDICULAR (126)
Skull           22 (8 cranial, 14 facial)  Pectoral girdles  4 (2 clavicles, 2 scapulae)
Ear ossicles     6                         Upper limbs      60 (30 each)
Hyoid            1                         Pelvic girdle     2 (hip bones)
Vertebral column 26                        Lower limbs      60 (30 each)
Thoracic cage   25 (24 ribs + sternum)
```

### Skull

- **Cranial bones (8)**: frontal, 2 parietal, 2 temporal, occipital, sphenoid, ethmoid.
- **Facial bones (14)**: 2 nasal, 2 maxillae, 2 zygomatic, mandible (only movable skull bone), 2 lacrimal, 2 palatine, 2 inferior nasal conchae, vomer.
- **Sutures**: coronal (frontal–parietal), sagittal (between parietals), lambdoid (parietal–occipital), squamous (temporal–parietal).
- **Foramen magnum** in the occipital bone lets the spinal cord pass.

### Vertebral column

| Region | Number | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cervical | 7 | Small bodies; **foramen transversarium** (for vertebral artery); C1 **atlas** (no body, holds the skull), C2 **axis** (dens/odontoid process); C7 vertebra prominens |
| Thoracic | 12 | Facets for ribs; long downward spines |
| Lumbar | 5 | Largest, heavy bodies; short square spines |
| Sacrum | 1 (5 fused) | Joins the hip bones |
| Coccyx | 1 (4 fused, varies) | Tailbone |

- A typical vertebra: **body** in front, **vertebral arch** (pedicles and laminae) behind enclosing the **vertebral foramen**, spinous process, two transverse processes, articular processes. Intervertebral discs (fibrocartilage, with a soft centre — nucleus pulposus) lie between bodies.
- **Curvatures**: cervical and lumbar curves are concave backward (lordosis, secondary); thoracic and sacral curves are convex backward (kyphosis, primary).
- The spinal cord ends at about the **L1–L2** level in adults, so lumbar puncture is done below this, usually at L3–L4 or L4–L5.

### Thoracic cage

Sternum (manubrium, body, xiphoid process), 12 pairs of ribs (1–7 true, 8–10 false, 11–12 floating) and 12 thoracic vertebrae.

### Appendicular skeleton

| Region | Bones |
| --- | --- |
| Pectoral girdle | Clavicle (S-shaped; most commonly fractured bone), scapula (with acromion, coracoid process, glenoid cavity) |
| Arm | Humerus (head, surgical neck, greater and lesser tubercles, medial and lateral epicondyles) |
| Forearm | Radius (lateral, thumb side), ulna (medial; olecranon forms the point of the elbow) |
| Hand | 8 carpals, 5 metacarpals, 14 phalanges |
| Pelvic girdle | Two hip bones (each = ilium + ischium + pubis fused at the **acetabulum**), joined to the sacrum behind and at the symphysis pubis in front |
| Thigh | Femur (longest and strongest bone; head, neck, greater and lesser trochanters, condyles) |
| Knee | Patella |
| Leg | Tibia (medial, weight-bearing; medial malleolus), fibula (lateral; lateral malleolus) |
| Foot | 7 tarsals (talus, calcaneus — heel, navicular, cuboid, 3 cuneiforms), 5 metatarsals, 14 phalanges |

**Female vs male pelvis**: the female pelvis is wider and shallower, with a round (gynaecoid) inlet, a wider subpubic angle (about 90° or more) and a larger outlet — adapted for childbirth.

## 6.6 Joints (articulations)

### Classification

| By structure | By movement | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fibrous** — bones joined by fibrous tissue | Mostly immovable (synarthrosis) | Skull sutures, tooth sockets (gomphosis), distal tibiofibular joint (syndesmosis, slightly movable) |
| **Cartilaginous** — joined by cartilage | Slightly movable (amphiarthrosis) | Primary (hyaline): epiphyseal plates, 1st rib–sternum. Secondary (fibrocartilage): intervertebral discs, symphysis pubis |
| **Synovial** — joint cavity with fluid | Freely movable (diarthrosis) | Shoulder, elbow, hip, knee |

### Structure of a synovial joint

```text
   Bone ─────────────
   Articular (hyaline) cartilage
   Joint cavity with synovial fluid
   Synovial membrane lining the capsule
   Fibrous capsule, strengthened by ligaments
   Articular cartilage
   Bone ─────────────
   (some joints also have discs/menisci, bursae, fat pads)
```

### Types of synovial joint

| Type | Movement | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ball and socket | All directions, including rotation | Shoulder, hip |
| Hinge | Flexion and extension | Elbow, knee (modified), ankle, interphalangeal |
| Pivot | Rotation around one axis | Atlanto-axial (C1–C2), proximal radio-ulnar |
| Condyloid (ellipsoid) | Flexion–extension, abduction–adduction | Wrist (radiocarpal), metacarpophalangeal |
| Saddle | Like condyloid plus opposition | Carpometacarpal joint of the thumb |
| Gliding (plane) | Sliding | Intercarpal, between vertebral articular processes |

### Major joints

- **Shoulder (glenohumeral)**: ball and socket between the head of the humerus and the shallow glenoid cavity, deepened by the glenoid labrum. Very mobile but unstable; held mainly by the **rotator cuff** muscles (supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis). Dislocates easily (usually forward and downward).
- **Elbow**: hinge between humerus, ulna and radius.
- **Hip**: ball and socket between the femoral head and acetabulum; deep socket and strong ligaments make it stable. Fracture of the femoral neck is common in older women with osteoporosis.
- **Knee**: largest joint; modified hinge between femoral condyles, tibia and patella. **Menisci** (medial and lateral, fibrocartilage) cushion it; **cruciate ligaments** (anterior and posterior) inside and **collateral ligaments** (medial and lateral) at the sides give stability.
- **Ankle**: hinge between tibia, fibula and talus.

## Part B — The muscular system

## 6.7 Types and structure of muscle

The three types (skeletal, smooth, cardiac) are compared in Unit 1. Here we look at skeletal muscle in more detail.

```text
Muscle (covered by epimysium)
  → Fascicles (bundles, covered by perimysium)
     → Muscle fibres = muscle cells (covered by endomysium; sarcolemma, many nuclei)
        → Myofibrils
           → Sarcomeres (thin actin and thick myosin filaments) → striations
```

- A muscle is attached to bone by a **tendon** (dense regular connective tissue) or a flat sheet (**aponeurosis**). The fixed end is the **origin**; the moving end is the **insertion**.
- **Motor unit**: one motor neuron and all the muscle fibres it supplies; the **neuromuscular junction** is where the nerve meets the fibre.
- Muscles work in groups: prime mover (agonist), opposing (antagonist), helpers (synergists) and stabilisers (fixators).

## 6.8 Muscle groups

| Region | Important muscles | Main action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Head (face and jaw) | Frontalis, orbicularis oculi (closes eye), orbicularis oris (closes lips), buccinator (cheek), masseter and temporalis (chewing) | Facial expression, chewing |
| Neck | **Sternocleidomastoid** (turns head to the opposite side; accessory muscle of breathing), trapezius (upper part) | Moves head, shrugs shoulders |
| Thorax | External and internal intercostals, diaphragm, pectoralis major and minor, serratus anterior | Breathing, arm movements |
| Abdomen | Rectus abdominis, external oblique, internal oblique, transversus abdominis | Support viscera, flex trunk, forced expiration, raise abdominal pressure (coughing, defaecation, labour) |
| Back | Trapezius, latissimus dorsi, erector spinae | Posture, arm extension |
| Pelvis | Pelvic floor: **levator ani** and coccygeus | Support pelvic organs, control of continence |
| Upper limb | **Deltoid**, biceps brachii, triceps brachii, forearm flexors and extensors | Arm and hand movement |
| Lower limb | **Gluteus maximus, medius and minimus**, quadriceps femoris (**vastus lateralis**, vastus medialis, vastus intermedius, rectus femoris), hamstrings, gastrocnemius and soleus (calf), tibialis anterior | Standing, walking, posture |

## 6.9 Principal muscles

| Muscle | Origin → insertion (simplified) | Action | Nerve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deltoid | Clavicle, acromion and spine of scapula → deltoid tuberosity of humerus | Abducts arm (also flexes and extends) | Axillary nerve |
| Biceps brachii | Scapula (two heads) → radial tuberosity | Flexes elbow, supinates forearm | Musculocutaneous nerve |
| Triceps brachii | Scapula and humerus (three heads) → olecranon of ulna | Extends elbow | Radial nerve |
| Diaphragm and intercostals | See Unit 2 | Breathing | Phrenic; intercostal nerves |
| Rectus abdominis | Pubis → xiphoid and costal cartilages 5–7 | Flexes trunk, compresses abdomen | Lower intercostal nerves |
| Levator ani (pelvic floor) | Pubis, fascia, ischial spine → coccyx and midline | Supports pelvic organs, helps continence | Sacral nerves (S2–S4) |
| Gluteus maximus | Ilium, sacrum, coccyx → gluteal tuberosity of femur, iliotibial tract | Extends hip (climbing, rising from sitting) | Inferior gluteal nerve |
| Gluteus medius and minimus | Outer surface of ilium → greater trochanter | Abduct hip; keep pelvis level when walking | Superior gluteal nerve |
| Vastus lateralis | Femur (greater trochanter, linea aspera) → patella and tibial tuberosity (via patellar tendon) | Extends knee | Femoral nerve |

## 6.10 Major muscles involved in nursing procedures (intramuscular injection sites)

| Site | Muscle | How to locate (outline) | Main structure to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deltoid | Deltoid | About 2–3 fingerbreadths (roughly 2.5–5 cm) below the acromion, in the middle of the muscle, above the armpit level | Radial nerve and brachial artery lower down; axillary nerve |
| Ventrogluteal | Gluteus medius and minimus | Heel of the hand on the greater trochanter, index finger on the ASIS, middle finger spread along the iliac crest; inject in the centre of the V between the two fingers | Few large nerves or vessels — often preferred |
| Dorsogluteal | Gluteus maximus | Upper outer quadrant of the buttock (divide the buttock into four) | **Sciatic nerve** and superior gluteal vessels — many guidelines now prefer the ventrogluteal site |
| Vastus lateralis | Vastus lateralis | Middle third of the outer front thigh, between the greater trochanter and the knee | Femoral vessels and nerve on the inner thigh |
| Rectus femoris | Rectus femoris | Middle third of the front of the thigh | Used mainly for self-injection |

**Why the vastus lateralis is the usual site in infants**: the gluteal muscles are poorly developed until a child walks, and the sciatic nerve is relatively large and close; the vastus lateralis is thick and away from major vessels and nerves.

**Other procedures using muscle anatomy**

- **Pelvic floor (Kegel) exercises** strengthen the levator ani after childbirth and for stress incontinence.
- **Correct lifting**: use the strong quadriceps and gluteal muscles (bend the knees, keep the back straight) to protect the erector spinae and the lumbar discs.
- **Range-of-motion exercises** move each joint through its normal movements to prevent contractures.
- Turning and positioning protect the skin over bony prominences: sacrum, greater trochanter, heels, elbows, occiput, scapulae, ischial tuberosities.

## 6.11 Application and implication in nursing

- **Fractures**: immobilise the joint above and below; check circulation, sensation and movement distal to plasters and splints (compartment syndrome warning signs).
- **Older adults** lose bone mass (osteoporosis) — fall prevention protects the hip and wrist.
- **Lumbar puncture** positioning (lateral, knees to chest, back arched) opens the spaces between lumbar spines.
- **Fontanelles** are checked in infants: a bulging fontanelle may mean raised intracranial pressure; a sunken one, dehydration.
- **Body mechanics** reduce back injury in nurses.
- **Immobility** causes muscle wasting, joint stiffness and bone loss; early mobilisation and ROM exercises prevent them.

## Remember

- 206 bones: axial 80, appendicular 126.
- Long bone: diaphysis, epiphyses, epiphyseal plate, periosteum, medullary cavity.
- Haversian system = osteon; osteoblasts build, osteoclasts break down.
- Length growth at the epiphyseal plate; width growth under the periosteum.
- Vertebrae: 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, sacrum, coccyx; spinal cord ends at L1–L2.
- Hip bone = ilium + ischium + pubis; femur is the longest bone; clavicle is the most fractured.
- Joints: fibrous, cartilaginous, synovial; shoulder and hip are ball and socket; knee is a modified hinge.
- Deltoid — axillary nerve; abducts arm.
- Ventrogluteal site uses gluteus medius; dorsogluteal risks the sciatic nerve; vastus lateralis is the infant site.

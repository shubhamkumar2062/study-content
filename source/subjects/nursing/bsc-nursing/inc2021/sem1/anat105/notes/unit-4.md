# Unit 4: The circulatory and lymphatic system

INC Unit IV (6 hours): blood components, arterial and venous systems, the heart (position, chambers, layers, valves, coronary arteries, nerve and blood supply), lymphatic tissue and the veins used for IV injections. Heart function, blood pressure and blood clotting are in Applied Physiology.

## 4.1 Blood components (structure)

Blood is a fluid connective tissue, about 5 litres in an adult (roughly 7–8% of body weight).

| Component | Share of blood | Structure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plasma | About 55% | Pale yellow fluid: about 90% water with proteins (albumin, globulins, fibrinogen), salts, nutrients, hormones, wastes |
| Red blood cells (erythrocytes) | About 45% of the volume (haematocrit) | Biconcave discs about 7–8 µm across, **no nucleus**, packed with haemoglobin; flexible enough to squeeze through capillaries |
| White blood cells (leucocytes) | Under 1% | Nucleated cells of five kinds (below) |
| Platelets (thrombocytes) | Under 1% | Small cell fragments without a nucleus, from megakaryocytes in the bone marrow |

| White cell | Group | Nucleus | Main role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Neutrophil (most common) | Granulocyte | 3–5 lobes | Phagocytosis of bacteria |
| Eosinophil | Granulocyte | 2 lobes, red granules | Allergy, parasites |
| Basophil (least common) | Granulocyte | S-shaped, dark blue granules | Releases histamine and heparin |
| Lymphocyte | Agranulocyte | Large round nucleus filling the cell | Immunity (B and T cells) |
| Monocyte (largest) | Agranulocyte | Kidney- or horseshoe-shaped | Becomes a macrophage in tissues |

All blood cells are made in the **red bone marrow** (in adults: flat bones, vertebrae, ribs, sternum, pelvis, ends of long bones).

## 4.2 Blood vessels: structure

All vessels except capillaries have three coats (tunicae).

| Coat | Structure |
| --- | --- |
| Tunica intima (inner) | Endothelium (simple squamous) on a thin connective tissue layer |
| Tunica media (middle) | Smooth muscle and elastic fibres |
| Tunica adventitia / externa (outer) | Connective tissue with small vessels (vasa vasorum) and nerves |

| Feature | Artery | Vein | Capillary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Direction | Away from the heart | Towards the heart | Between arterioles and venules |
| Wall | Thick, muscular and elastic | Thin, less muscle | One layer of endothelium only |
| Lumen | Narrow, round | Wide, often collapsed | About the width of one red cell |
| Valves | None (except at the heart outflow) | Present in limb veins | None |
| Pressure | High; pulsatile | Low | Low |
| Bleeding when cut | Spurting bright red | Steady flow, darker | Oozing |

- **Elastic (conducting) arteries**: aorta and its main branches; stretch and recoil.
- **Muscular (distributing) arteries**: radial, femoral; control flow to organs.
- **Arterioles**: control blood pressure by narrowing and widening.
- **Venous valves** are folds of intima; they stop backflow. When they fail, varicose veins develop.

## 4.3 The arterial system

```text
Left ventricle → Ascending aorta (gives right and left coronary arteries)
   → Arch of aorta: 1. Brachiocephalic trunk → right common carotid + right subclavian
                    2. Left common carotid
                    3. Left subclavian
   → Descending thoracic aorta (intercostal, bronchial, oesophageal branches)
   → Abdominal aorta (from T12): coeliac trunk, superior mesenteric, renal, gonadal,
                                  inferior mesenteric, lumbar arteries
   → divides at L4 into right and left common iliac arteries
        → internal iliac (pelvis) + external iliac → femoral artery (lower limb)
```

- **Common carotid** → external carotid (face, scalp) and internal carotid (brain, eye).
- **Subclavian** → axillary → **brachial** (in front of the elbow) → **radial** and **ulnar** (forearm and hand). The subclavian also gives the **vertebral artery** to the brain.
- **Femoral** → popliteal (behind knee) → anterior tibial (→ **dorsalis pedis**) and posterior tibial.
- **Circle of Willis**: ring of arteries at the base of the brain joining the internal carotid and vertebral (basilar) systems, so blockage of one vessel can be partly compensated.

**Pulse points**: temporal, carotid (side of the neck, beside the trachea), brachial (medial to the biceps tendon at the elbow), radial (lateral wrist, thumb side), femoral (groin, midway between ASIS and symphysis pubis), popliteal, posterior tibial (behind the medial malleolus), dorsalis pedis (top of the foot), apical (5th left intercostal space, midclavicular line).

## 4.4 The venous system

- **Systemic veins** return blood to the right atrium through the **superior vena cava** (head, neck, upper limbs, thorax) and **inferior vena cava** (abdomen, pelvis, lower limbs), and the coronary sinus (heart wall).
- Head: internal jugular vein (from the brain via the dural venous sinuses) and external jugular vein; internal jugular + subclavian = brachiocephalic vein; right and left brachiocephalic veins = superior vena cava.
- Limbs have **superficial veins** (in the subcutaneous tissue, visible, used for injections) and **deep veins** (running with arteries, sharing their names).
- **Hepatic portal system**: veins from the stomach, intestines, pancreas and spleen join to form the portal vein, which carries blood to the liver before it reaches the inferior vena cava.

## 4.5 Veins used for IV injections

**Upper limb (first choice in adults)**

```text
   Hand: dorsal venous network (back of hand)
          |                         |
    Cephalic vein (lateral,     Basilic vein (medial,
    thumb side)                 little-finger side)
          \                         /
           \__ Median cubital vein _/   ← in front of the elbow (cubital fossa)
```

| Vein | Where | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dorsal metacarpal veins / dorsal venous network | Back of hand | Peripheral cannulation |
| Cephalic vein | Lateral (thumb side) of forearm and arm | Cannulation; large and straight |
| Basilic vein | Medial side of forearm and arm | Cannulation; PICC lines |
| Median cubital vein | Front of the elbow | Most common for **venepuncture** (blood samples) |
| Median antebrachial vein | Front of forearm | Alternative site |

**Other sites**

- Lower limb: **great saphenous vein** (in front of the medial malleolus, runs up the medial leg) and dorsal venous arch of the foot — used when arm veins are not available (higher risk of thrombosis in adults).
- Neck: **external jugular vein** (emergency).
- Central veins: internal jugular, subclavian and femoral veins for central venous catheters (placed by trained staff).
- Infants: scalp veins and hand/foot veins.

Choose distal sites first so the vein above remains usable later, avoid joints when possible, avoid the arm with a fistula or on the side of a mastectomy with lymph node removal, and avoid veins that are hard, inflamed or near an infected area.

## 4.6 The heart: position and relations

- A hollow muscular organ about the size of the owner's closed fist (about 250–350 g), lying in the **middle mediastinum** between the lungs, behind the sternum.
- About two-thirds of it lies to the left of the midline.
- **Apex**: points down, forward and to the left; lies at the **left 5th intercostal space, just medial to the midclavicular line** (about 9 cm from the midline in adults) — the apex beat.
- **Base**: faces backward and upward (mainly the left atrium), at about the level of T5–T8 in the lying position; the great vessels attach here.

| Relation | Structure |
| --- | --- |
| In front | Sternum, costal cartilages, thymus remnant |
| Behind | Oesophagus, descending aorta, vertebrae (T5–T8) |
| Sides | Lungs and pleura; phrenic nerves |
| Below | Diaphragm (central tendon) |
| Above | Great vessels: aorta, pulmonary trunk, superior vena cava |

## 4.7 Layers of the heart

```text
 Outside → inside
 Fibrous pericardium      (tough outer sac, attached to the diaphragm)
 Serous pericardium:
    Parietal layer        (lines the fibrous sac)
    — Pericardial cavity with a little serous fluid —
    Visceral layer = Epicardium (on the heart surface)
 Myocardium               (cardiac muscle; thickest in the left ventricle)
 Endocardium              (endothelium lining the chambers and covering the valves)
```

- Fluid between the layers of serous pericardium reduces friction. Fluid or blood collecting here can squeeze the heart (cardiac tamponade).
- The myocardium of the left ventricle is about three times thicker than that of the right, because it pumps blood around the whole body.

## 4.8 Chambers of the heart

Four chambers: two atria (receiving) and two ventricles (pumping). The right and left sides are separated by the **interatrial septum** (has the fossa ovalis, remnant of the fetal foramen ovale) and **interventricular septum**.

| Chamber | Receives from | Sends to | Features |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Right atrium | Superior and inferior venae cavae, coronary sinus | Right ventricle | Contains the SA node; has an ear-like auricle |
| Right ventricle | Right atrium | Pulmonary trunk → lungs | Trabeculae carneae, papillary muscles, chordae tendineae |
| Left atrium | Four pulmonary veins | Left ventricle | Forms most of the base |
| Left ventricle | Left atrium | Aorta → body | Thickest wall; forms the apex |

```text
 Body → SVC/IVC → RA → tricuspid → RV → pulmonary valve → pulmonary arteries → lungs
 Lungs → 4 pulmonary veins → LA → mitral → LV → aortic valve → aorta → body
```

## 4.9 Heart valves

| Valve | Position | Cusps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tricuspid (right atrioventricular) | Right atrium → right ventricle | 3 |
| Mitral (bicuspid, left atrioventricular) | Left atrium → left ventricle | 2 |
| Pulmonary (semilunar) | Right ventricle → pulmonary trunk | 3 half-moon cusps |
| Aortic (semilunar) | Left ventricle → aorta | 3 half-moon cusps; coronary arteries arise just above two of them |

- Atrioventricular valves are held by **chordae tendineae** attached to **papillary muscles**, which stop the cusps turning inside out when the ventricles contract.
- Closure of the AV valves gives the first heart sound ("lub"); closure of the semilunar valves gives the second ("dub").
- Auscultation areas (where each valve is best heard, not where it lies): aortic — right 2nd intercostal space at the sternal edge; pulmonary — left 2nd intercostal space; tricuspid — lower left sternal edge (4th–5th space); mitral — apex (left 5th intercostal space, midclavicular line).

## 4.10 Coronary arteries (blood supply of the heart)

The heart muscle gets its own blood from the **right and left coronary arteries**, which arise from the ascending aorta just above the aortic valve. They fill mainly when the ventricles relax (diastole).

| Artery | Branches | Supplies (usual pattern) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Right coronary artery (RCA) | Marginal branch, posterior interventricular (descending) branch in most people | Right atrium, right ventricle, back of the septum, SA node (in most) and AV node (in most) |
| Left coronary artery (LCA) | **Left anterior descending (anterior interventricular)** and **circumflex** | Left atrium, most of the left ventricle, front two-thirds of the septum |

Venous blood from the heart wall drains mainly through cardiac veins into the **coronary sinus**, which opens into the right atrium. Blocking a coronary artery causes myocardial infarction (heart attack) in the area it supplies; the left anterior descending artery is commonly involved.

## 4.11 Nerve supply and conducting system

- **Sympathetic** nerves (from the upper thoracic segments) increase heart rate and force.
- **Parasympathetic** supply through the **vagus nerve** slows the heart.
- These act through the cardiac plexus; they adjust, but do not start, the heartbeat.
- **Conducting system** (specialised cardiac muscle that starts and spreads the impulse):

```text
SA node (right atrium wall, near SVC opening — the pacemaker)
   → atrial muscle → AV node (lower interatrial septum)
   → Bundle of His → right and left bundle branches (in the septum)
   → Purkinje fibres (ventricle walls)
```

## 4.12 The lymphatic system

**Lymph** is tissue fluid that has entered lymph vessels; it is clear, or milky (chyle) after a fatty meal from the gut.

- **Lymph capillaries**: blind-ended, very permeable; absent from the CNS, bone marrow and cartilage.
- **Lymph vessels**: like thin veins with many valves; carry lymph through lymph nodes.
- **Lymph ducts**:
  - **Thoracic duct** (largest, about 40 cm): begins at the **cisterna chyli** in the abdomen, drains the lower limbs, abdomen, left side of the thorax, left arm and left side of the head and neck; empties into the junction of the **left subclavian and left internal jugular veins**.
  - **Right lymphatic duct**: drains the right arm and right side of the head, neck and thorax into the right subclavian/internal jugular junction.

**Lymphatic tissue and organs**

| Organ | Structure | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lymph nodes | Bean-shaped, capsule, cortex with follicles (B cells), paracortex (T cells), medulla; several afferent vessels in, one efferent vessel out at the hilum | Filter lymph; groups in neck (cervical), axilla, groin (inguinal), around abdominal vessels |
| Spleen | Largest lymphatic organ, left hypochondrium behind ribs 9–11; white pulp (lymphoid) and red pulp (blood-filled sinusoids) | Removes old red cells; stores platelets; immune responses. Injury causes heavy bleeding |
| Thymus | Behind the sternum in the upper mediastinum; large in children, shrinks after puberty | T-lymphocytes mature here |
| Tonsils | Palatine, pharyngeal (adenoids), lingual — a ring of lymphoid tissue in the pharynx | Guard the entry to the respiratory and digestive tracts |
| MALT | Lymphoid tissue in mucosa, e.g. Peyer's patches, appendix | Local immunity |

## 4.13 Application and implication in nursing

- IV cannulation and venepuncture depend on knowing the dorsal venous network, cephalic, basilic and median cubital veins; avoid arteries (the brachial artery lies deep to the median cubital region).
- Pulse is felt where an artery crosses a bone near the surface; the radial pulse is routine, the carotid is used in collapse.
- Chest compressions over the lower half of the sternum squeeze the heart between the sternum and vertebral column.
- Valves in leg veins plus muscle pumping return blood; immobility allows stasis and deep vein thrombosis, so early ambulation and leg exercises are taught.
- Lymph nodes are examined for infection and cancer spread; after axillary node removal the arm on that side may swell (lymphoedema), so it is not used for BP, injections or cannulation unless policy allows.
- Splenic injury from trauma to the left lower ribs can cause internal bleeding — watch vital signs.

## Remember

- RBC: biconcave, no nucleus; neutrophils most, basophils fewest; monocyte largest.
- Arteries thick muscular wall; veins have valves; capillaries are one cell thick.
- Arch of aorta: brachiocephalic, left common carotid, left subclavian.
- Median cubital vein = venepuncture; cephalic lateral, basilic medial.
- Apex beat: left 5th intercostal space, midclavicular line.
- Layers: pericardium, myocardium, endocardium.
- Mitral = bicuspid (left); tricuspid on the right.
- Coronary arteries arise from the ascending aorta; drain to the coronary sinus.
- SA node is the pacemaker; vagus slows the heart.
- Thoracic duct drains most of the body into the left venous angle.

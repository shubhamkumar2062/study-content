# Unit 1: Anatomical terms, cell, tissues, membranes and glands

This unit covers INC Unit I (8 hours). It gives you the language of anatomy and the building blocks — cells, tissues, membranes, glands, cartilage and muscle — that every later system is made of.

## 1.1 The anatomical position

All descriptions in anatomy assume the body is in the **anatomical position**:

- standing upright, head and eyes looking straight ahead
- arms by the sides with the **palms facing forward** (thumbs pointing away from the body)
- feet flat on the ground, close together, toes pointing forward

Even when a patient lies in bed, "left" and "right" always mean the **patient's** left and right, not the nurse's. Mixing these up is a real source of wrong-site errors, so nurses always say "the patient's right arm".

## 1.2 Terms of position and direction

| Term | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anterior (ventral) | Towards the front | The sternum is anterior to the heart |
| Posterior (dorsal) | Towards the back | The vertebral column is posterior to the oesophagus |
| Superior (cranial) | Towards the head, upper | The head is superior to the neck |
| Inferior (caudal) | Towards the feet, lower | The stomach is inferior to the diaphragm |
| Median | On the midline | The nose lies in the median plane |
| Medial | Nearer the midline | The ulna is medial to the radius |
| Lateral | Farther from the midline | The ear is lateral to the eye |
| Proximal | Nearer the trunk or point of attachment (limbs) | The elbow is proximal to the wrist |
| Distal | Farther from the trunk or point of attachment | The fingers are distal to the wrist |
| Superficial | Nearer the surface | The skin is superficial to muscle |
| Deep | Farther from the surface | Bone is deep to muscle |
| Prone | Lying face down | Position for some back procedures |
| Supine | Lying on the back, face up | Usual examination position |
| Palmar | The front (palm) of the hand | Palmar creases |
| Plantar | The sole of the foot | Plantar reflex |

Proximal and distal are used mainly for limbs and tubes (for example "proximal end of the urethra").

## 1.3 Anatomical planes

A plane is an imaginary flat surface used to cut (section) the body.

| Plane | Other names | Divides the body into |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sagittal | Vertical (anteroposterior) plane | Right and left parts. The **mid-sagittal (median)** plane gives two equal halves; a parasagittal plane gives unequal halves |
| Coronal | Frontal plane | Front (anterior) and back (posterior) parts |
| Transverse | Axial or horizontal plane | Upper (superior) and lower (inferior) parts |
| Oblique | — | Any slanting cut between the above |

```text
          Sagittal          Coronal           Transverse
            |                 ___                ___
          ( | )              ( | )              (___)
          / | \     front -> /=|=\ <- back      /===\  <- cut across
            |                 | |                | |
     left | right       front | back      upper / lower
```

CT and MRI images are usually displayed as transverse (axial), sagittal and coronal sections, so these words appear on every scan report.

## 1.4 Terms of movement

| Movement | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flexion | Bending; the angle between two parts decreases | Bending the elbow |
| Extension | Straightening; angle increases | Straightening the knee |
| Abduction | Movement away from the midline | Raising the arm sideways |
| Adduction | Movement towards the midline | Bringing the arm back to the side |
| Medial rotation | Turning the front of a limb towards the midline | Turning the thigh inward |
| Lateral rotation | Turning the front of a limb away from the midline | Turning the thigh outward |
| Inversion | Turning the sole of the foot inward | Happens at subtalar joint |
| Eversion | Turning the sole outward | Happens at subtalar joint |
| Supination | Turning the forearm so the palm faces forward (or up) | Holding a bowl of soup |
| Pronation | Turning the forearm so the palm faces back (or down) | Typing on a keyboard |
| Plantar flexion | Pointing the foot downward | Standing on tiptoe |
| Dorsiflexion (dorsal flexion) | Pulling the foot upward | Walking on heels |
| Circumduction | Circular movement combining flexion, extension, abduction and adduction | Swinging the arm in a circle |

These are the words used in range-of-motion (ROM) exercises, positioning and physiotherapy charts.

## 1.5 Organization of the human body

Levels of organization, from simple to complex:

```text
Chemical (atoms, molecules) → Cell → Tissue → Organ → System → Organism
```

**Body cavities**

- **Dorsal cavity**: cranial cavity (brain) and vertebral canal (spinal cord).
- **Ventral cavity**: thoracic cavity (lungs, heart, great vessels, oesophagus) separated by the diaphragm from the abdominopelvic cavity (abdomen: stomach, liver, intestines, kidneys; pelvis: bladder, rectum, internal reproductive organs).

**Regions of the abdomen (nine regions)** — formed by two vertical (midclavicular) lines and two horizontal lines (subcostal and transtubercular):

```text
 Right hypochondriac | Epigastric  | Left hypochondriac
 Right lumbar        | Umbilical   | Left lumbar
 Right iliac (inguinal)| Hypogastric (suprapubic) | Left iliac (inguinal)
```

A simpler clinical method uses **four quadrants** (right upper, left upper, right lower, left lower) divided through the umbilicus. Appendicitis pain settles in the right lower quadrant; gallbladder pain in the right upper quadrant.

**Surface and bony landmarks** a nurse uses every day:

| Landmark | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Sternal angle (angle of Louis) | Level of the 2nd rib; counting ribs and intercostal spaces |
| Suprasternal (jugular) notch | Checking tracheal position |
| Xiphoid process | Hand position for chest compression is above it, on the lower half of the sternum |
| Midclavicular line, 5th intercostal space | Apex beat |
| Acromion process | Locating the deltoid injection site |
| Anterior superior iliac spine (ASIS), iliac crest | Ventrogluteal site; McBurney's point |
| Greater trochanter of femur | Ventrogluteal and hip landmarks |
| Umbilicus | Abdominal quadrants |
| Symphysis pubis | Measuring fundal height, bladder palpation |
| Vertebra prominens (C7) | Counting vertebrae |
| Line joining highest points of iliac crests (about L4) | Lumbar puncture level |

## 1.6 Structure of the cell

The **cell** is the smallest unit of life. A typical human cell has three parts: plasma membrane, cytoplasm with organelles, and nucleus.

```text
         ┌──────────── Plasma membrane ────────────┐
         │  Mitochondria   Ribosomes   Lysosome      │
         │     ┌──────── Nucleus ────────┐           │
         │     │  Nucleolus  Chromatin   │  Golgi    │
         │     │  Nuclear envelope       │  apparatus│
         │     └─────────────────────────┘           │
         │  Rough ER   Smooth ER   Centrioles        │
         └───────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

| Part | Structure | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plasma (cell) membrane | Phospholipid bilayer with proteins, cholesterol and carbohydrate chains (fluid mosaic) | Encloses the cell, controls what enters and leaves, carries receptors |
| Cytoplasm | Jelly-like cytosol containing organelles | Site of many chemical reactions |
| Nucleus | Double nuclear envelope with pores, chromatin (DNA), nucleolus | Holds the genes; controls cell activity |
| Nucleolus | Dense area in the nucleus | Makes ribosomal RNA |
| Mitochondria | Double membrane; inner membrane folded into cristae | Aerobic respiration, ATP production ("powerhouse") |
| Ribosomes | RNA and protein granules, free or on ER | Protein synthesis |
| Rough endoplasmic reticulum | Membrane channels studded with ribosomes | Makes proteins for export |
| Smooth endoplasmic reticulum | Membrane channels without ribosomes | Lipid and steroid synthesis, detoxification |
| Golgi apparatus | Stacks of flattened sacs | Packages and modifies proteins into vesicles |
| Lysosomes | Membrane sacs of digestive enzymes | Break down worn-out parts and bacteria |
| Centrosome (centrioles) | Pair of small cylinders | Forms the spindle in cell division |
| Cytoskeleton | Microfilaments, microtubules | Shape and movement; cilia and flagella |

The human body cell has **46 chromosomes (23 pairs)**; gametes (ovum, sperm) have 23.

## 1.7 Cell division

**Mitosis** — division of body (somatic) cells; one cell gives two identical daughter cells with 46 chromosomes each. Used for growth and repair.

| Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Interphase (not strictly a phase of mitosis) | DNA replicates; cell grows |
| Prophase | Chromatin condenses into visible chromosomes (two chromatids); nuclear membrane breaks down; spindle forms |
| Metaphase | Chromosomes line up at the equator of the spindle |
| Anaphase | Chromatids separate and move to opposite poles |
| Telophase | Nuclear membranes re-form; cytoplasm divides (cytokinesis) |

**Meiosis** — two divisions in the testis and ovary; one cell gives four cells with 23 chromosomes each. Crossing over in meiosis I mixes genes, so each gamete is different.

## 1.8 Tissues

A **tissue** is a group of similar cells (with intercellular material) that work together for one function. There are four basic types.

### Epithelial tissue

Covers surfaces and lines cavities and tubes; forms glands. Cells sit on a basement membrane, have little intercellular substance and no blood vessels.

| Type | Shape and layers | Location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple squamous | One layer of flat cells | Alveoli, lining of blood vessels (endothelium), serous membranes |
| Simple cuboidal | One layer of cube cells | Kidney tubules, thyroid follicles, gland ducts |
| Simple columnar | One layer of tall cells, may have goblet cells and microvilli | Stomach, intestines |
| Ciliated columnar | Columnar with cilia | Uterine tubes |
| Pseudostratified ciliated columnar | Looks layered but all cells touch the basement membrane; with goblet cells | Trachea, bronchi (respiratory epithelium) |
| Stratified squamous keratinised | Many layers, surface cells dead and filled with keratin | Skin |
| Stratified squamous non-keratinised | Many layers, surface cells living and moist | Mouth, oesophagus, vagina, cornea |
| Transitional | Many layers that flatten when stretched | Urinary bladder, ureters |

### Connective tissue

Supports and binds other tissues. Cells are scattered in plenty of matrix (ground substance plus fibres — collagen, elastic, reticular). Most types have a good blood supply (cartilage is the exception).

- **Loose (areolar)** — under the skin, around organs.
- **Adipose** — fat cells; stores energy, insulates, cushions.
- **Dense regular** — tendons and ligaments.
- **Dense irregular** — dermis, capsules of organs.
- **Elastic** — walls of large arteries.
- **Reticular** — framework of lymph nodes, spleen, bone marrow.
- **Cartilage**, **bone** and **blood** are specialised connective tissues.

### Muscle tissue

Contracts to produce movement (see 1.11).

### Nervous tissue

Neurons (conduct impulses) and neuroglia (support cells). Found in the brain, spinal cord and nerves.

## 1.9 Membranes

A membrane is a thin sheet of tissue that covers, lines or separates.

| Membrane | Structure | Where | Function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mucous membrane | Epithelium on loose connective tissue (lamina propria); goblet cells secrete mucus | Tubes opening to the outside: respiratory, digestive, urinary, reproductive tracts | Protects, lubricates, traps microbes, absorbs |
| Serous membrane | Two layers of simple squamous epithelium (parietal lining the cavity, visceral covering the organ) with serous fluid between | Pleura (lungs), pericardium (heart), peritoneum (abdomen) | Lets organs move without friction |
| Synovial membrane | Connective tissue, no epithelium | Lines joint cavities, bursae, tendon sheaths | Secretes synovial fluid that lubricates joints |
| Cutaneous membrane | The skin | Body surface | Protection |
| Meninges | Dura, arachnoid, pia mater | Around brain and spinal cord | Protect the CNS |

Fluid in a serous cavity is normally a thin film. When it collects in excess we see pleural effusion, pericardial effusion or ascites.

## 1.10 Glands

A gland is epithelial tissue that makes and releases a secretion.

| Basis | Types |
| --- | --- |
| Where secretion goes | **Exocrine** — through a duct onto a surface (sweat, salivary, sebaceous). **Endocrine** — ductless; hormones into the blood (thyroid, adrenal). **Mixed** — both (pancreas, gonads) |
| Number of cells | Unicellular (goblet cell) and multicellular |
| Shape | Tubular, alveolar (acinar), tubulo-alveolar; simple (one duct) or compound (branched duct) |
| Way of secreting | **Merocrine** — by exocytosis, cell intact (most sweat glands, salivary glands). **Apocrine** — tip of the cell is shed (axillary sweat glands, mammary gland). **Holocrine** — whole cell breaks down (sebaceous glands) |

## 1.11 Cartilage

Cartilage is a firm, flexible connective tissue. Its cells (chondrocytes) sit in spaces (lacunae) in the matrix. It has **no blood vessels**; it is fed by diffusion from the surrounding perichondrium, so it heals slowly.

| Type | Matrix | Location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hyaline | Glassy, fine collagen fibres | Articular surfaces of joints, costal cartilages, trachea and bronchi, nose, larynx (most), fetal skeleton, growth plates |
| Fibrocartilage | Thick bundles of collagen fibres; very strong | Intervertebral discs, symphysis pubis, menisci of the knee |
| Elastic | Many elastic fibres; springy | Pinna (external ear), epiglottis, auditory tube |

## 1.12 Skeletal, smooth and cardiac muscle compared

| Feature | Skeletal | Smooth | Cardiac |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Location | Attached to bones | Walls of hollow organs and vessels, iris | Heart wall (myocardium) |
| Cell shape | Long cylindrical fibres | Spindle-shaped | Short branched cells |
| Nuclei | Many, at the edge | One, central | One (sometimes two), central |
| Striations | Present | Absent | Present |
| Intercalated discs | Absent | Absent | Present (join cells) |
| Control | Voluntary (somatic nerves) | Involuntary (autonomic nerves, hormones) | Involuntary; own pacemaker |
| Speed of contraction | Fast, tires easily | Slow, sustained | Rhythmic, does not tire |

## 1.13 Application and implication in nursing

- Correct directional terms make documentation exact: "a 3 cm laceration on the lateral aspect of the left forearm, 4 cm proximal to the wrist".
- Landmarks guide procedures: the acromion for deltoid injection, ASIS and greater trochanter for ventrogluteal injection, the sternum for chest compressions, the iliac crest level for lumbar puncture.
- Prone, supine and lateral positions are used to prevent pressure injury and to prepare for procedures.
- Skin and mucous membranes are the first barrier against infection; keeping them intact (oral care, perineal care) prevents infection.
- Cartilage has no blood supply, so joint cartilage injuries heal poorly; early safe mobility protects joints.
- Cancer is uncontrolled mitosis; this explains why anticancer treatment affects fast-dividing tissues (hair, gut lining, bone marrow).

## Remember

- Anatomical position: palms forward; "right" means the patient's right.
- Sagittal = right/left; coronal = front/back; transverse = upper/lower.
- Four tissues: epithelial, connective, muscle, nervous.
- Mitosis → 2 identical cells (46); meiosis → 4 different cells (23).
- Transitional epithelium lines the bladder; pseudostratified ciliated columnar lines the trachea.
- Serous membranes: pleura, pericardium, peritoneum. Mucous membranes line tubes open to the outside.
- Sebaceous glands are holocrine; salivary glands are merocrine.
- Fibrocartilage = intervertebral discs, symphysis pubis, menisci; elastic cartilage = pinna, epiglottis.
- Only cardiac muscle has intercalated discs.

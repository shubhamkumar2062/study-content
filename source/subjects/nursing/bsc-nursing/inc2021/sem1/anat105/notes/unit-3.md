# Unit 3: The digestive system

INC Unit III (6 hours): structure of the alimentary canal and the accessory organs of digestion, with nursing applications. Digestive juices and absorption are covered in Applied Physiology.

## 3.1 Overview

```text
ALIMENTARY CANAL (a continuous tube; lengths quoted in textbooks vary with muscle tone)
Mouth → Pharynx → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum)
      → Large intestine (caecum + appendix, colon, rectum) → Anal canal → Anus

ACCESSORY ORGANS
Teeth, tongue, salivary glands, liver, gallbladder (biliary tract), pancreas
```

## 3.2 General structure of the wall of the alimentary canal

From the oesophagus to the anal canal the wall has four layers.

| Layer (inside → out) | Structure | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mucosa | Epithelium, lamina propria (loose connective tissue with lymphoid tissue), thin muscularis mucosae | Stratified squamous in mouth, oesophagus and anal canal (wear and tear); simple columnar from stomach to rectum (secretion and absorption) |
| Submucosa | Loose connective tissue with blood vessels, lymphatics and the **submucosal (Meissner's) nerve plexus** | Controls secretion |
| Muscularis externa | Inner circular and outer longitudinal smooth muscle with the **myenteric (Auerbach's) plexus** between them | Peristalsis; the stomach has an extra inner oblique layer |
| Serosa / adventitia | Visceral peritoneum (serosa) in the abdomen; loose connective tissue (adventitia) where there is no peritoneum, as in most of the oesophagus | Reduces friction |

**Sphincters** are thickened rings of circular muscle: upper and lower oesophageal, pyloric, ileocaecal, internal anal (smooth, involuntary) and external anal (skeletal, voluntary).

**Peritoneum**: the large serous membrane of the abdomen. Parietal peritoneum lines the wall; visceral peritoneum covers organs. Folds include the **mesentery** (holds the small intestine, carries its vessels), the **greater omentum** (apron hanging from the stomach over the intestines, stores fat and walls off infection) and the **lesser omentum** (stomach to liver). Organs behind the peritoneum are **retroperitoneal** — kidneys, pancreas (mostly), most of the duodenum, ascending and descending colon.

## 3.3 The mouth (oral cavity)

- Bounded by the lips in front, cheeks at the sides, **hard palate** (maxilla and palatine bones) and **soft palate** above, tongue and floor below; opens behind into the oropharynx. The **uvula** hangs from the soft palate; during swallowing the soft palate rises to close the nasopharynx.
- **Tongue**: skeletal muscle covered with mucous membrane; attached to the hyoid bone and floor of the mouth by the frenulum. The upper surface has papillae (filiform, fungiform, circumvallate) carrying taste buds. Motor nerve: hypoglossal (XII).
- **Teeth**: two sets.

| Set | Number | Per quadrant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deciduous (milk) teeth | 20 | 2 incisors, 1 canine, 2 molars |
| Permanent teeth | 32 | 2 incisors, 1 canine, 2 premolars, 3 molars |

A tooth has a **crown** (above the gum), **neck** and **root** (in the socket). Layers: **enamel** (hardest substance in the body) over the crown, **dentine** under it, **pulp cavity** with vessels and nerves in the centre, and **cementum** covering the root. The periodontal ligament holds the tooth in the socket.

## 3.4 Salivary glands

| Gland | Location | Duct | Secretion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Parotid (largest) | In front of and below the ear | **Stensen's (parotid) duct**, opens opposite the upper second molar | Watery (serous) |
| Submandibular | Under the lower jaw | **Wharton's duct**, opens beside the frenulum of the tongue | Mixed |
| Sublingual (smallest) | Under the tongue in the floor of mouth | Many small ducts | Mainly mucous |

Mumps is a viral infection of the parotid glands.

## 3.5 Oesophagus

- Muscular tube about **25 cm long**, from the pharynx at C6 through the thorax (behind the trachea and heart), passing through the diaphragm at **T10**, to the stomach (cardiac orifice at about T11).
- Muscle: skeletal in the upper third, mixed in the middle, smooth in the lower third.
- Lining: stratified squamous non-keratinised epithelium.
- The **lower oesophageal (cardiac) sphincter** prevents reflux of stomach acid; weakness leads to heartburn (GERD).
- Normally narrowed at four places (start, crossing of aortic arch, crossing of left bronchus, passing through diaphragm); swallowed objects and tubes may catch here.

## 3.6 Stomach

J-shaped, widest part of the canal, in the epigastric, left hypochondriac and umbilical regions, under the diaphragm.

```text
            Fundus
           ______
  Cardiac /      \
 orifice |        \   Greater curvature
  Lesser |  Body   |
curvature \         \
           \_Antrum__\__ Pylorus → Pyloric sphincter → Duodenum
```

- **Parts**: cardia, fundus (above the level of the cardiac orifice; often contains gas), body, pyloric antrum, pyloric canal and **pyloric sphincter**.
- **Curvatures**: lesser (right/upper, lesser omentum attached) and greater (left/lower, greater omentum attached).
- **Muscle**: three layers — outer longitudinal, middle circular, **inner oblique** (for churning).
- **Rugae**: folds of mucosa when empty; flatten as the stomach fills (capacity about 1.5 litres in an adult, varies).
- **Gastric glands** (in gastric pits): **mucous cells** (mucus), **chief (peptic) cells** (pepsinogen), **parietal (oxyntic) cells** (hydrochloric acid and intrinsic factor, needed for vitamin B12 absorption), **G cells** in the antrum (gastrin).

## 3.7 Small intestine

About 5–6 m long (often quoted; varies), from the pyloric sphincter to the ileocaecal valve. Main site of digestion and absorption.

| Part | Length (approximate) | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duodenum | 25 cm | C-shaped around the head of the pancreas; mostly retroperitoneal; the common bile duct and main pancreatic duct open together at the **hepatopancreatic ampulla (ampulla of Vater)** on the major duodenal papilla, guarded by the **sphincter of Oddi**; **Brunner's glands** secrete alkaline mucus |
| Jejunum | About 2/5 of the rest | Thicker wall, wider, more vascular, prominent circular folds |
| Ileum | About 3/5 of the rest | Thinner wall, fewer folds, many **Peyer's patches** (lymphoid tissue); ends at the ileocaecal valve |

**Adaptations for absorption** (increase surface area):

1. **Circular folds (plicae circulares)** of mucosa and submucosa.
2. **Villi**: finger-like projections, each with a capillary network and a central lymph vessel (**lacteal**) that absorbs fat.
3. **Microvilli** on the epithelial cells (brush border).
4. **Intestinal glands (crypts of Lieberkühn)** between villi secrete intestinal juice.

```text
   Villus (section)
      /\        epithelium with microvilli
     /  \       goblet cells
    | ┃  |      ┃ = lacteal (fat → lymph)
    |~┃~ |      ~ = capillary network (sugars, amino acids → portal blood)
   _|_┃__|_
     crypt
```

## 3.8 Large intestine

About 1.5 m long, from the ileocaecal valve to the anus; wider than the small intestine.

- **Caecum**: blind pouch in the right iliac fossa; the **vermiform appendix** (about 8–9 cm, rich in lymphoid tissue) hangs from it. McBurney's point (one-third of the way from the right ASIS to the umbilicus) marks the usual base of the appendix.
- **Colon**: ascending (right side, up to the hepatic flexure), transverse (across the abdomen, to the splenic flexure), descending (left side), sigmoid (S-shaped, in the pelvis).
- **Rectum**: about 12–13 cm, in front of the sacrum.
- **Anal canal**: about 4 cm; internal sphincter (smooth, involuntary) and external sphincter (skeletal, voluntary, pudendal nerve).
- **Special features of the colon**: longitudinal muscle in three bands (**taeniae coli**), which pucker the wall into pouches (**haustra**); small fat tags (**appendices epiploicae**). No villi; many goblet cells.

## 3.9 Liver

- Largest gland of the body, about **1.5 kg** in adults; lies mainly in the right hypochondriac and epigastric regions under the diaphragm, protected by the lower ribs.
- **Lobes**: large right and smaller left (separated in front by the falciform ligament), plus the small caudate and quadrate lobes on the under (visceral) surface.
- **Porta hepatis** (on the under surface): entry of the **hepatic artery** (oxygenated blood, about one-quarter of the supply) and **portal vein** (nutrient-rich blood from the gut and spleen, about three-quarters); exit of the right and left hepatic ducts. Blood leaves through the **hepatic veins** into the inferior vena cava.
- Covered by a fibrous capsule; most of it by peritoneum.

**Microscopic structure — the hepatic lobule**: a roughly hexagonal unit with a **central vein** in the middle. Plates of liver cells (hepatocytes) radiate from it, separated by **sinusoids** (lined by Kupffer cells — macrophages). At the corners are **portal triads**: a branch of the hepatic artery, a branch of the portal vein and a bile ductule. Blood flows from the corners to the central vein; bile flows the opposite way, through canaliculi to the bile ducts.

## 3.10 Gallbladder and biliary tract

- **Gallbladder**: pear-shaped sac (fundus, body, neck) under the right lobe of the liver; stores and concentrates bile (capacity about 30–50 ml). Its fundus lies near the tip of the right 9th costal cartilage.
- **Ducts**:

```text
Right hepatic duct + Left hepatic duct → Common hepatic duct
Common hepatic duct + Cystic duct (from gallbladder) → Common bile duct
Common bile duct + Main pancreatic duct → Ampulla of Vater → Duodenum (2nd part)
```

A stone blocking the common bile duct stops bile reaching the gut, causing jaundice and pale stools.

## 3.11 Pancreas

- Soft, lobulated gland about 12–15 cm long, lying across the back of the upper abdomen behind the stomach (retroperitoneal). Parts: **head** (in the curve of the duodenum), neck, **body** and **tail** (reaching the spleen).
- **Exocrine part** (about 98–99%): acini secrete digestive enzymes into the main pancreatic duct (duct of Wirsung), which joins the common bile duct; an accessory duct (of Santorini) may also open into the duodenum.
- **Endocrine part**: the **islets of Langerhans** (see Unit 5) secrete insulin and glucagon into the blood.

## 3.12 Blood and nerve supply in brief

- Arteries from the abdominal aorta: **coeliac trunk** (stomach, liver, spleen, pancreas, upper duodenum), **superior mesenteric artery** (small intestine to the first two-thirds of the transverse colon), **inferior mesenteric artery** (rest of the colon and upper rectum).
- Veins from the gut and spleen join to form the **hepatic portal vein**, so absorbed food and drugs pass through the liver before reaching the general circulation (first-pass effect).
- Nerves: parasympathetic (vagus nerve and pelvic splanchnic nerves) increases movement and secretion; sympathetic reduces them.

## 3.13 Application and implication in nursing

- **Nasogastric tube**: measured from the nose tip to the earlobe and then to the xiphisternum (NEX measurement); passes the natural narrowings of the oesophagus. Confirm position before every feed as per hospital policy.
- **Oral care**: the mouth harbours many microbes; good oral hygiene prevents infection of the parotid gland (parotitis) and chest infection in unconscious patients.
- **Abdominal assessment** uses the regions and quadrants: RLQ tenderness at McBurney's point suggests appendicitis; RUQ pain may be from the gallbladder or liver.
- **Enema and suppository** insertion follow the direction of the rectum and anal canal; the patient lies in the left lateral position, which follows the curve of the sigmoid colon and rectum.
- **Liver biopsy and paracentesis** require knowledge of liver position and the peritoneal cavity.
- The ileum absorbs vitamin B12 and bile salts; patients who lose the ileum or lack intrinsic factor need monitoring for B12 deficiency.
- Stoma care: a colostomy (from colon) gives formed stool; an ileostomy gives liquid, irritant output that needs careful skin protection.

## Remember

- Four layers of the gut wall: mucosa, submucosa, muscularis externa, serosa/adventitia.
- Permanent teeth 32, deciduous 20; enamel is the hardest substance.
- Parotid duct opens opposite the upper second molar.
- Oesophagus 25 cm, passes the diaphragm at T10; stomach has an extra oblique muscle layer.
- Parietal cells: HCl and intrinsic factor; chief cells: pepsinogen.
- Duodenum: ampulla of Vater, sphincter of Oddi, Brunner's glands. Ileum: Peyer's patches.
- Villi with lacteals absorb fat.
- Colon: taeniae coli, haustra, appendices epiploicae.
- Liver: hepatic artery + portal vein in, hepatic veins out; lobule with central vein and portal triads.
- Cystic duct + common hepatic duct = common bile duct.
- Pancreas: exocrine acini + endocrine islets.

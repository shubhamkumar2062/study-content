# How to use this guide

Applied Anatomy is a first-semester subject of the INC 2021 B.Sc Nursing curriculum (3 credits, 60 hours of theory). It is examined in a **combined university paper with Applied Physiology**: Applied Anatomy is **Section A (37 marks)** and Applied Physiology is **Section B (38 marks)** of one 3-hour, 75-mark paper. Anatomy here means structure; the matching functions are in the Physiology pack, so study the two side by side, system by system.

**How the units are arranged.** The INC syllabus has ten units. To keep each notes file a useful size, two pairs of small units are joined:

| Notes file | INC unit(s) | Hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit 1 Anatomical terms, cell, tissues | I | 8 |
| Unit 2 Respiratory system | II | 6 |
| Unit 3 Digestive system | III | 6 |
| Unit 4 Circulatory and lymphatic system | IV | 6 |
| Unit 5 Endocrine system and sensory organs | V + VI | 4 + 4 |
| Unit 6 Musculoskeletal system | VII | 10 |
| Unit 7 Renal and reproductive systems | VIII + IX | 5 + 5 |
| Unit 8 Nervous system | X | 6 |

Study one notes file a day: read it, draw every diagram once from memory, then answer the questions and MCQs for that unit without looking.

**Section A: Applied Anatomy (37 marks)** — as given in INC Regulations 2020, Appendix 3. There is no internal choice, so every question must be answered. Your university may print it differently; confirm with your university.

| Part | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MCQ | 6 multiple-choice questions | 6 × 1 = 6 |
| Essay | 1 long answer | 1 × 10 = 10 |
| Short | 3 short answers | 3 × 5 = 15 |
| Very short | 3 very short answers | 3 × 2 = 6 |
| **Total** | | **37** |

Section A takes about half of the 3-hour paper, so in the mock test here it is timed at 90 minutes. The university exam is 75 marks (Anatomy 37 + Physiology 38) with 25 internal marks. You must pass theory with at least 50%.

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — a favourite; comes up in most papers in some form. Learn the full answer with a labelled diagram.
- **Medium** — appears every few papers or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — less common, but useful for MCQs and very short answers.

These ratings are estimates from syllabus weight and the usual style of nursing anatomy papers. They are not a promise of what will be asked.

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. **Draw a labelled diagram in every essay and in most short answers.** Use a pencil, label on one side with straight lines, and give a title. A clear diagram of the heart, kidney, nephron, eye, ear, synovial joint or skin often earns as many marks as the text.
2. Start structure questions with **position and relations**, then **external features**, then **internal structure (gross and microscopic)**, then **blood and nerve supply**, and finish with **applied nursing points**.
3. Learn the numbers that examiners like: 206 bones, 31 pairs of spinal nerves, 12 cranial nerves, 4 parathyroids, 32 permanent teeth, female urethra 4 cm and male 18–20 cm, ureter 25–30 cm, oesophagus 25 cm, diaphragm openings at T8, T10, T12, spinal cord ends at L1–L2.
4. Use tables for comparisons: right vs left lung, artery vs vein, skeletal vs smooth vs cardiac muscle, male vs female pelvis, sympathetic vs parasympathetic.
5. Every unit in the INC syllabus says "application and implication in nursing". Add two or three nursing points to each long answer — injection sites, positioning, catheterisation, lumbar puncture, chest drain, breast self-examination.
6. For very short answers (2 marks), write a one-line definition plus one example or one fact. Don't write a page.
7. MCQs come from small facts; read the "Remember" list of each notes file the night before.

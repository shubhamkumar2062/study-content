# Unit 5: The endocrine system and the sensory organs

This notes file joins two small INC units: **Unit V, the endocrine system** (4 hours: structure of the hypothalamus, pineal, pituitary, thyroid, parathyroid, thymus, pancreas and adrenal glands) and **Unit VI, the sensory organs** (4 hours: structure of the skin, eye, ear, nose and tongue). Hormone actions and the physiology of vision and hearing belong to Applied Physiology; only the hormone names are listed here so the structure makes sense.

## Part A — The endocrine system

## 5.1 Endocrine glands in general

Endocrine glands are **ductless**; they release hormones directly into the blood, so they have a very rich capillary supply.

```text
             Pineal gland (brain)
             Hypothalamus + Pituitary (base of brain)
             Thyroid + Parathyroids (neck)
             Thymus (upper mediastinum)
             Adrenal glands (on top of kidneys)
             Pancreatic islets (abdomen)
             Ovaries / Testes (pelvis / scrotum)
```

## 5.2 Hypothalamus

- Part of the diencephalon, below the thalamus, forming the floor and lower side walls of the third ventricle.
- Connected to the pituitary by the **infundibulum (pituitary stalk)**.
- Contains groups of nerve cells (nuclei), e.g. supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei, which make ADH and oxytocin.
- Links the nervous and endocrine systems through:
  - **Nerve fibres** (hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract) to the posterior pituitary.
  - **Hypophyseal portal veins** carrying releasing and inhibiting hormones to the anterior pituitary.

## 5.3 Pituitary gland (hypophysis)

- Pea-sized (about 1 cm, 0.5 g), lies in the **sella turcica (hypophyseal fossa) of the sphenoid bone**, below the optic chiasma. Called the "master gland".

| Lobe | Origin and structure | Hormones released |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anterior (adenohypophysis) | Glandular epithelium from the roof of the embryonic mouth; receives hypothalamic hormones by the portal veins | GH, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, prolactin |
| Posterior (neurohypophysis) | Nervous tissue, an extension of the hypothalamus; nerve fibres and pituicytes | Stores and releases ADH and oxytocin (made in the hypothalamus) |

Because the optic chiasma lies just above, a pituitary tumour can press on it and cause loss of the outer halves of the visual fields.

## 5.4 Pineal gland

- Small cone-shaped body (about 8 mm) attached to the roof of the third ventricle, behind the hypothalamus.
- Made of pinealocytes and neuroglia; often contains calcium deposits ("brain sand") in adults, visible on X-ray.
- Secretes **melatonin**, linked to the sleep–wake cycle.

## 5.5 Thyroid gland

- Butterfly-shaped gland in the front of the neck, at the level of C5–T1, weighing about 25 g.
- **Two lateral lobes** on either side of the trachea and larynx, joined by an **isthmus** across the 2nd–4th tracheal rings; sometimes a pyramidal lobe rises from the isthmus.
- Enclosed in a capsule; moves up with swallowing because it is attached to the larynx.
- **Microscopic structure**: many **follicles** lined by cuboidal follicular cells, filled with colloid (thyroglobulin). Follicular cells make **T3 and T4**. Between follicles are **parafollicular (C) cells**, which make **calcitonin**.
- Blood supply: superior thyroid artery (external carotid) and inferior thyroid artery (thyrocervical trunk of subclavian). The **recurrent laryngeal nerves** run close behind the gland.
- Enlargement of the gland is a goitre.

## 5.6 Parathyroid glands

- Usually **four** tiny glands (about the size of a rice grain), two on the back of each thyroid lobe (superior and inferior pairs).
- Chief cells secrete **parathormone (PTH)**, which raises blood calcium.
- They can be removed or damaged by accident in thyroid surgery, causing low calcium (tingling, tetany). Nurses watch for this after thyroidectomy.

## 5.7 Thymus

- Two-lobed lymphoid organ behind the sternum in the upper (superior) mediastinum, in front of the great vessels.
- Large in childhood; after puberty it is gradually replaced by fat (involution).
- Each lobule has an outer **cortex** packed with developing T-lymphocytes and an inner **medulla** with **Hassall's corpuscles**.
- Hormone: **thymosin**, which helps T-cell maturation.

## 5.8 Pancreas (endocrine part)

- The pancreas (structure in Unit 3) has about one million **islets of Langerhans** scattered among the exocrine acini, more in the tail.

| Cell | Proportion | Hormone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alpha (α) | About 20% | Glucagon |
| Beta (β) | About 70% (the majority) | Insulin |
| Delta (δ) | Small | Somatostatin |
| PP (F) cells | Few | Pancreatic polypeptide |

## 5.9 Adrenal (suprarenal) glands

- Two glands, one on the upper pole of each kidney, inside the renal fascia. The right is triangular (pyramid-like); the left is crescent (semilunar) shaped.
- Each has an outer **cortex** and an inner **medulla**, which are really two different glands.

```text
 Capsule
 Cortex: Zona glomerulosa    → mineralocorticoids (aldosterone)      "Salt"
         Zona fasciculata    → glucocorticoids (cortisol)            "Sugar"
         Zona reticularis    → sex hormones (androgens)              "Sex"
 Medulla (chromaffin cells, modified sympathetic nerve cells)
                             → adrenaline and noradrenaline
```

(Memory: "GFR — Salt, Sugar, Sex" from outside in.)

- The medulla is supplied by sympathetic preganglionic fibres; it acts like a sympathetic ganglion.

## Part B — The sensory organs

## 5.10 Skin

The skin is the largest organ (about 1.5–2 m² in adults). Two main layers sit on the subcutaneous tissue.

**Epidermis** — stratified squamous keratinised epithelium, **no blood vessels**. Layers from deep to surface:

| Layer | Features |
| --- | --- |
| Stratum basale (germinativum) | One layer of dividing cells; melanocytes (pigment) present here |
| Stratum spinosum | Several layers joined by desmosomes ("prickle cells") |
| Stratum granulosum | Cells with keratin granules, starting to die |
| Stratum lucidum | Clear layer, only in thick skin (palms, soles) |
| Stratum corneum | Flat dead keratin-filled cells that flake off |

**Dermis** — dense connective tissue with collagen and elastic fibres, blood vessels, lymphatics, nerves and sensory receptors, hair follicles, sweat and sebaceous glands.

- **Papillary layer** (upper): dermal papillae (fingerprints), Meissner's corpuscles (light touch).
- **Reticular layer** (deeper): strong fibres; Pacinian corpuscles (pressure, vibration) are in the deep dermis and subcutaneous tissue.

**Appendages**: hair (shaft, root, follicle, arrector pili muscle), nails, **sebaceous glands** (open into hair follicles; sebum), **sweat glands** — eccrine (all over, open on the skin surface) and apocrine (axilla, groin; open into hair follicles).

**Subcutaneous tissue (hypodermis)**: loose areolar and adipose tissue — the layer used for subcutaneous injections.

```text
 Surface ────────────────── stratum corneum
 EPIDERMIS  (no vessels)    ...stratum basale
 ─────────── dermal papillae ───────────
 DERMIS: vessels, nerves, sweat gland, sebaceous gland, hair follicle
 ────────────────────────────────────────
 SUBCUTANEOUS: fat, larger vessels
```

## 5.11 Eye

The eyeball (about 2.5 cm across) lies in the bony **orbit**, cushioned by fat. Accessory structures: eyebrows, eyelids, conjunctiva, lacrimal apparatus, six extrinsic eye muscles.

**Three coats of the eyeball**

| Coat | Parts | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outer fibrous | **Sclera** (white, back 5/6) and **cornea** (transparent, front 1/6) | Cornea has no blood vessels; bends light most; very sensitive (corneal reflex) |
| Middle vascular (uvea) | **Choroid**, **ciliary body**, **iris** | Choroid is pigmented and vascular; ciliary body has ciliary muscle (changes lens shape) and makes aqueous humour; iris has a central opening, the **pupil**, with sphincter and dilator pupillae muscles |
| Inner nervous | **Retina** | Rods (dim light) and cones (colour, detail); **macula lutea** with the **fovea centralis** (sharpest vision, cones only); **optic disc** (blind spot, no receptors) where the optic nerve leaves |

**Contents**

- **Lens**: transparent, biconvex, held by suspensory ligaments (zonules) from the ciliary body. Clouding = cataract.
- **Aqueous humour**: watery fluid in the front of the eye (anterior and posterior chambers), made by the ciliary body, drained through the canal of Schlemm. Poor drainage raises pressure (glaucoma).
- **Vitreous humour (body)**: jelly behind the lens, keeps the eyeball shape.

```text
         Cornea
   Anterior chamber (aqueous)
    Iris ─ pupil ─ Iris
         Lens
   Vitreous body
   Retina  ── Fovea ── Optic disc → Optic nerve
   (Choroid and sclera outside)
```

**Accessory structures**

- **Conjunctiva**: thin mucous membrane lining the eyelids and covering the white of the eye (not the cornea).
- **Lacrimal apparatus**: lacrimal gland (upper outer part of the orbit) → tears flow across the eye → puncta at the inner corner → canaliculi → lacrimal sac → **nasolacrimal duct** → inferior meatus of the nose.
- Extrinsic muscles: four recti (superior, inferior, medial, lateral) and two obliques. Nerves: lateral rectus — abducens (VI); superior oblique — trochlear (IV); all others — oculomotor (III).

## 5.12 Ear

```text
 EXTERNAL EAR         | MIDDLE EAR                    | INNER EAR
 Pinna → External     | Tympanic cavity with ossicles | Cochlea (hearing)
 acoustic meatus →    | Malleus → Incus → Stapes →    | Vestibule + semicircular
 Tympanic membrane    | oval window                   | canals (balance)
                      | Auditory tube → nasopharynx   | → Vestibulocochlear nerve (VIII)
```

- **External ear**: pinna (elastic cartilage covered with skin) and the **external acoustic meatus** (about 2.5 cm, S-shaped; outer third cartilage, inner two-thirds bone; ceruminous glands make wax). Ends at the **tympanic membrane (eardrum)**, a thin, pearly grey membrane.
- **Middle ear (tympanic cavity)**: air-filled space in the temporal bone with the three smallest bones of the body — **malleus** (attached to the eardrum), **incus**, **stapes** (footplate in the oval window). The **auditory (Eustachian) tube** connects it to the nasopharynx and equalises pressure. It also connects behind with the mastoid air cells.
- **Inner ear (labyrinth)**: a bony labyrinth filled with perilymph, containing a membranous labyrinth filled with endolymph.
  - **Cochlea**: snail-shaped tube (about 2¾ turns) containing the **organ of Corti** — the hearing receptor.
  - **Vestibule** (utricle and saccule) and three **semicircular canals** — balance and head position.

## 5.13 Nose (organ of smell)

- The olfactory area is in the roof of the nasal cavity, on the upper septum and superior concha.
- **Olfactory epithelium**: olfactory receptor cells (bipolar neurons with hair-like cilia in the mucus), supporting cells and basal cells.
- The axons pass through the **cribriform plate of the ethmoid** to the **olfactory bulb** — the olfactory nerve (I). A fracture here can cause loss of smell and leakage of cerebrospinal fluid from the nose.

## 5.14 Tongue (organ of taste)

- Muscular organ covered with mucous membrane; front two-thirds in the mouth, back one-third in the oropharynx, separated by a V-shaped groove (sulcus terminalis).
- **Papillae**: filiform (most numerous, no taste buds), fungiform (on the tip and sides, have taste buds), circumvallate (8–12 large ones in a V in front of the sulcus, many taste buds), foliate (sides).
- **Taste buds**: barrel-shaped clusters of gustatory (receptor) and supporting cells opening by a taste pore.
- Nerves of taste: anterior two-thirds — facial nerve (VII) via the chorda tympani; posterior one-third — glossopharyngeal (IX). General sensation, anterior two-thirds — lingual branch of the trigeminal (V). Movement — hypoglossal (XII).

## 5.15 Application and implication in nursing

- **Thyroid surgery**: watch the voice (recurrent laryngeal nerve) and calcium level (parathyroids); keep tracheostomy equipment ready as per policy.
- **Insulin injections** go into the subcutaneous tissue; rotate sites to prevent fatty lumps.
- **Skin**: the epidermis has no blood supply; pressure over bony points cuts off the dermal circulation and causes pressure injury, so reposition patients regularly. Intradermal tests (e.g. Mantoux) go into the dermis.
- **Eye care**: instil drops into the lower conjunctival sac, not on the cornea; press on the inner corner (puncta) after some drops to reduce drainage into the nose.
- **Ear drops and irrigation**: straighten the canal — pull the pinna **up and back in adults**, **down and back in young children**.
- Children's shorter, more horizontal Eustachian tubes explain frequent middle-ear infection.
- Loss of smell or clear nasal discharge after head injury may mean a fracture of the anterior cranial base.

## Remember

- Pituitary sits in the sella turcica; anterior lobe = glandular, posterior lobe = nerve tissue storing ADH and oxytocin.
- Thyroid: 2 lobes + isthmus over tracheal rings 2–4; follicles (T3, T4) and C cells (calcitonin).
- Four parathyroids behind the thyroid.
- Adrenal cortex: glomerulosa, fasciculata, reticularis (salt, sugar, sex); medulla: adrenaline.
- Islets: beta cells (insulin) are the majority.
- Epidermis layers: basale, spinosum, granulosum, lucidum, corneum.
- Eye coats: fibrous (sclera, cornea), vascular (choroid, ciliary body, iris), nervous (retina). Fovea = sharpest vision; optic disc = blind spot.
- Ossicles: malleus, incus, stapes; organ of Corti in the cochlea.
- Taste: VII (anterior 2/3), IX (posterior 1/3).

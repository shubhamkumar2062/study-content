# Unit 7: Admission and discharge, mobility and immobility, patient education

INC Unit XI (6 hours theory + 2 skill lab): hospital admission and discharge. INC Unit XII (8 hours theory + 10 skill lab): mobility and immobility. INC Unit XIII (4 hours theory + 2 skill lab): patient education. They are joined because together they follow a patient from arrival, through care in bed and getting moving again, to going home well prepared.

## 7.1 Admission to the hospital

**Admission** is accepting a patient into a hospital ward for observation, diagnosis or treatment. It may be **routine (planned / elective)**, for example for planned surgery, or **emergency**, through the casualty department.

The first impression matters: a warm, organised admission reduces anxiety and builds trust.

**Preparation of the unit**

1. Get information about the incoming patient (diagnosis, condition, age, sex, any special needs).
2. Prepare the **admission bed**: a clean **closed bed** converted to an **open bed** when the patient arrives (or a post-operative bed or other special bed if needed); mackintosh and draw sheet as needed.
3. Check the unit: locker, over-bed table, chair, call bell, light, oxygen and suction points working.
4. Keep ready the admission tray: thermometer, BP apparatus, stethoscope, weighing machine, measuring tape, urine specimen container, identity band, admission forms, patient gown.

**Admission procedure**

| Step | Rationale |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Greet the patient and family by name; introduce yourself | Reduces anxiety; shows respect |
| 2. Check identity and the admission papers; put on an **identification band** with at least two identifiers (name and hospital number) | Prevents errors throughout the stay |
| 3. Take the patient to the bed; help change into a hospital gown if required; give privacy | Comfort and dignity |
| 4. **Orient** the patient and family to the ward: bed controls, call bell, toilet, visiting hours, meal times, rules, the nurse in charge | Reduces fear of the unfamiliar; promotes safety |
| 5. Record **vital signs, height and weight** and collect any ordered specimens | Baseline values for comparison |
| 6. Take the **nursing history** and do an initial assessment (including allergies, current medicines, fall risk, pressure ulcer risk, pain) | Basis for the nursing care plan |
| 7. Take care of **valuables and belongings**: list them, give to relatives or to the hospital locker against a signed receipt | Prevents loss and disputes |
| 8. Inform the doctor; carry out admission orders | Starts treatment promptly |
| 9. Explain the plan, tests and what to expect; answer questions | Knowledge reduces anxiety |
| 10. **Record** the admission: date, time, mode (walking, wheelchair, stretcher), condition, vital signs, belongings, care given | Legal record and communication |

**Medico-legal issues at admission**

- **Medico-legal cases (MLC)**: accidents, assault, burns, poisoning, suspected suicide or homicide, sexual offences, unknown or unconscious patients brought by police — the doctor registers an MLC and informs the police as per law; the nurse preserves evidence (clothes, specimens, vomitus) in labelled, sealed containers, records exact times and facts, and maintains the chain of custody.
- **Consent**: general consent for admission and treatment; **informed consent** (written) for procedures and surgery, signed by the patient or, if the patient is a minor or cannot decide, by the legal guardian.
- **Emergency care** must not be delayed for paperwork.
- Accurate identification, honest records, privacy and confidentiality.

**Roles and responsibilities of the nurse in admission**: welcoming and orienting, safe identification, baseline assessment, safe custody of belongings, informing the doctor, starting the care plan, emotional support, health teaching from day one, and complete documentation.

## 7.2 Discharge, referral and transfer

**Discharge** is the release of a patient from the hospital.

| Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Planned discharge** | The doctor decides the patient is fit to go; prepared in advance |
| **LAMA (Leave Against Medical Advice)** / DAMA | Patient or family decides to leave although the doctor advises staying. The nurse informs the doctor, the doctor explains the risks, and the patient or guardian signs a LAMA form; record everything. The patient cannot be kept by force (except under specific legal provisions) |
| **Abscond** | Patient leaves without informing anyone. Search the premises, inform the nurse in charge, the doctor and security; inform the family and, in medico-legal cases, the police; record the time last seen and the facts |
| **Referral** | Sent to another specialist or hospital for care not available here |
| **Transfer** | Moved to another ward, unit or hospital (internal or external) |
| **Death** | Discharge by death — last offices and handing over the body as per policy |

**Discharge planning**

Discharge planning **starts on the day of admission**. It aims to make the move home safe and to prevent readmission. It includes:

1. Assessing what the patient and family will need at home (self-care ability, support, home conditions, money).
2. Teaching medicines, diet, wound care, exercises, warning signs, and when to come back.
3. Arranging equipment (walker, wheelchair), home care or community nurse follow-up.
4. Co-ordinating with the doctor, physiotherapist, dietitian and social worker.
5. Giving written instructions in a language the patient understands.

A common memory aid is **METHOD**: **M**edications, **E**nvironment (home safety), **T**reatment (wound care, exercises), **H**ealth teaching, **O**utpatient follow-up, **D**iet.

**Discharge procedure**

1. Check the written discharge order.
2. Inform the patient and family in advance; plan transport.
3. Complete teaching; check understanding (ask the patient to explain back).
4. Give the discharge summary, prescriptions, investigation reports, follow-up date and instructions.
5. Return the patient's valuables and medicines brought from home against signature.
6. Remove IV lines or other devices as ordered.
7. Complete billing formalities as per hospital procedure.
8. Take the patient to the vehicle by wheelchair if needed.
9. Record the date, time, condition, mode of leaving, who accompanied, and the instructions given.
10. Inform the admission office; prepare the unit.

**Transfer procedure**: check the order; inform the receiving unit and family; prepare the patient; give a transfer report (condition, vital signs, medicines last given, allergies, pending tests); send records and belongings; accompany a seriously ill patient; record.

**Medico-legal issues at discharge**: LAMA forms, discharge of MLC patients only with proper documentation and police intimation where required, correct handing over of minors to their guardians, accurate discharge summaries, and confidentiality of records.

**Care of the unit after discharge (terminal cleaning)**: strip the bed, send linen to the laundry (infected linen separately), clean and disinfect the mattress, bed, locker and equipment as per the infection control policy (more thoroughly after infectious patients), air the room, make a closed bed, and replace equipment.

## 7.3 Mobility: elements of normal movement

**Mobility** is the ability to move freely, easily and purposefully. It depends on:

| Element | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Body alignment and posture** | Body parts in correct relation to one another; the line of gravity passes through the base of support |
| **Joint mobility** | Range of motion (ROM) of each joint |
| **Balance** | Keeping the body stable; the centre of gravity stays over the base of support |
| **Coordinated movement** | Smooth, purposeful movement through nerves, muscles and bones working together |

## 7.4 Principles of body mechanics

**Body mechanics** is the efficient, safe use of the body during activity. It protects nurses from back injury and patients from harm.

1. **Wide base of support** (feet apart, one slightly forward) — more stability.
2. **Lower the centre of gravity** (bend the knees and hips, not the back).
3. **Keep the object or patient close** to your body — less strain on the back.
4. **Use the large leg and arm muscles**, not the back.
5. **Face the direction of movement**; turn by pivoting the feet, **do not twist the spine**.
6. **Push, pull or roll rather than lift** where possible; slide on a smooth surface (draw sheet, slide sheet).
7. **Work at a comfortable height** — raise the bed.
8. **Tighten the abdominal and gluteal muscles** before moving — supports the spine.
9. **Get help** or use mechanical aids (hoists) for heavy or dependent patients; move on a count so everyone acts together.

## 7.5 Factors affecting body alignment and activity

Age and development, health status (pain, fractures, paralysis, arthritis, heart and lung disease), nutrition (obesity, malnutrition), emotions (depression, fear of falling), lifestyle and occupation, medicines (sedatives), pregnancy, and environment (stairs, slippery floors).

## 7.6 Exercise: types and benefits

| Type | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Isotonic (dynamic) | Muscle shortens and moves a joint | Walking, swimming, ROM exercises |
| Isometric (static) | Muscle tightens without moving the joint | Quadriceps setting, gluteal setting in bed |
| Isokinetic | Movement against a constant resistance on a machine | Physiotherapy equipment |
| Aerobic | Uses oxygen; continuous large-muscle activity | Brisk walking, cycling |
| Anaerobic | Short bursts without enough oxygen | Weight lifting, sprinting |

**ROM exercises**: **active** (patient does them independently), **active-assisted** (patient does with help), **passive** (nurse or therapist moves the joint for a patient who cannot).

**Benefits of exercise**: stronger heart and better circulation, better lung expansion, maintained muscle strength and joint flexibility, stronger bones, better digestion and bowel movement, weight control, better sleep, better mood and self-esteem, lower risk of diabetes and high BP.

## 7.7 Effects of immobility

| System | Effects |
| --- | --- |
| Skin | **Pressure ulcers** (over the sacrum, heels, hips, elbows, back of head) |
| Musculoskeletal | Muscle wasting (atrophy) and weakness, **contractures** (foot drop, hip and knee flexion), joint stiffness, **osteoporosis** (calcium loss from bone) |
| Cardiovascular | **Orthostatic hypotension**, more work for the heart, **deep vein thrombosis** (slow venous flow) and pulmonary embolism, dependent oedema |
| Respiratory | Shallow breathing, collection of secretions, **hypostatic pneumonia**, atelectasis |
| Gastrointestinal | Loss of appetite, **constipation**, indigestion |
| Urinary | Urinary stasis, **infection**, **kidney stones** (from calcium loss), retention |
| Metabolic | Lower metabolism, negative nitrogen balance, calcium loss |
| Psychological | Boredom, depression, loss of self-esteem, sleep disturbance, confusion (especially in the elderly) |

## 7.8 Maintaining body alignment and activity: nursing interventions

**Assessment**: posture and alignment, gait, ROM, muscle strength, balance, activity tolerance, pain, skin, and risk scores for falls and pressure ulcers.

**Maintaining alignment (positions)**: support each position with pillows and devices — head in line with the spine, hips and knees slightly flexed, feet at a right angle against a footboard, trochanter roll to prevent outward rotation of the hip, hand roll for the fingers; change position at least every 2 hours.

**Moving a patient up in bed (two nurses)**: explain; bed flat and raised, brakes on; remove the pillow and place it at the head of the bed (protects the head); patient flexes the knees and pushes with the feet if able; nurses on each side hold the draw (lift) sheet close to the patient; wide stance facing the head of the bed; on a count of three, slide the patient up (do not drag on the skin — causes friction injury); replace the pillow and make comfortable.

**Turning to the side (log rolling** for spinal injury: two or three nurses turn the head, shoulders and hips together, keeping the spine straight).

**Assisting to sit at the edge of the bed (dangling)**: raise the head of the bed, swing the legs over the side while supporting the shoulders, let the patient sit for a minute and check for dizziness (orthostatic hypotension) before standing.

**Transferring from bed to wheelchair**

| Step | Rationale |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Place the wheelchair at an angle of about 45° to the bed on the patient's **stronger side**; lock the brakes; lift the footrests away | Short pivot; stability; the strong side leads |
| 2. Lower the bed; help the patient sit on the edge; put on non-skid footwear | Feet flat on the floor; prevents slipping |
| 3. Stand in front with a wide base and bent knees; use a gait belt if available | Body mechanics; secure hold |
| 4. On a count, help the patient stand, pivot on the strong foot and sit down in the chair | Coordinated movement; less strain |
| 5. Position the feet on the footrests, cover the legs, give the call bell | Comfort and safety |

**Bed to stretcher**: bring the stretcher alongside at the same height, lock both, use a slide sheet or roller board, at least three people (more for heavy patients), move on a count, side rails up, cover the patient.

**Lifting**: use mechanical hoists for dependent patients; avoid manual lifting of the full body weight where possible.

## 7.9 Walking and assisting with ambulation

**Ambulation** is walking. Early ambulation after surgery or illness prevents many effects of immobility.

- Check vital signs and dizziness before walking; let the patient sit first.
- Non-skid footwear, clear path, good light.
- Walk at the patient's **weaker side**, holding a gait belt or with one arm around the waist, so you can support them.
- If the patient faints or starts to fall, **ease them gently to the floor** while protecting the head — do not try to hold them up.

**Walking aids**

| Aid | Guidelines |
| --- | --- |
| **Cane (walking stick)** | Held in the hand on the **stronger** side; top at the level of the hip (greater trochanter); elbow slightly bent. Move the cane and the weak leg together, then the strong leg |
| **Walker** | Lift (or roll) the walker forward, step into it with the weak leg, then the strong leg; do not walk too close to the front bar |
| **Crutches (axillary)** | Top of the crutch about 3 finger-widths (a few centimetres) below the axilla; elbows slightly bent; **weight is taken on the hands, never on the armpits** (pressure damages the axillary nerves and vessels — crutch palsy) |

**Crutch gaits**: four-point (slowest, most stable: right crutch, left foot, left crutch, right foot), three-point (both crutches with the injured leg, then the good leg — when one leg cannot bear weight), two-point (crutch and opposite foot together — faster), swing-to and swing-through (both legs swing to or past the crutches — for paraplegics with braces). On stairs: "up with the good, down with the bad" (going up, the good leg leads; going down, the crutches and injured leg lead).

## 7.10 Care of patients with immobility using the nursing process

| Step | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Assessment | Mobility level, skin condition, muscle strength, ROM, breathing, bowel and bladder, mood, risk scores |
| Nursing diagnosis | Impaired physical mobility related to a fracture; risk for impaired skin integrity related to immobility; risk for constipation |
| Planning | Patient will keep skin intact; will do ROM exercises twice a day; will walk with a walker within one week |
| Implementation | Turning every 2 hours, pressure-relieving mattress, skin care, ROM and breathing exercises, coughing, fluids and fibre, early mobilisation, diversion, teaching |
| Evaluation | Skin intact, no contractures, chest clear, regular bowel movement, walking distance increasing |

## 7.11 Care of patients with casts and splints

A **cast** is a rigid casing (plaster of Paris or fibreglass) that keeps a fractured bone still. A **splint** is a support, often removable, used for immobilisation and first aid.

**Care of a patient with a cast**

| Care | Rationale |
| --- | --- |
| Support a wet plaster cast on pillows; handle with the **palms**, not fingertips; leave it uncovered to dry (plaster takes a day or more to dry fully; fibreglass sets quickly) | Fingertips make dents that press on the skin; covering slows drying |
| Elevate the limb above heart level for the first days | Reduces swelling |
| Do **neurovascular checks** often (especially the first 24–48 hours): the **5 Ps — pain, pallor, pulselessness, paraesthesia (tingling, numbness), paralysis** — plus colour, warmth, capillary refill and swelling of fingers or toes | Detects compartment syndrome and pressure from a tight cast early |
| **Pain that is not relieved by prescribed analgesia, or increases on passive stretching of the fingers or toes, must be reported at once** | It may be compartment syndrome — an emergency |
| Check the cast edges; petal them with tape if rough | Prevents skin abrasion |
| Never insert objects inside the cast to scratch; report a bad smell, drainage or hot spots | Prevents skin injury; signs of infection or pressure ulcer |
| Keep the cast dry (cover with plastic when bathing) | A wet plaster cast softens |
| Exercise unaffected joints; isometric exercises of muscles inside the cast as advised | Prevents stiffness and wasting |
| Teach the patient and family these points before discharge | Safe home care |

**Splints**: check that they are well padded and not too tight, check skin and circulation, and teach correct application if removable.

## 7.12 Patient education

**Patient teaching** is a planned process of helping patients and families gain the knowledge, attitudes and skills they need to maintain or improve health. It is a legal and professional responsibility of the nurse.

**Importance and purposes**: promote health and prevent illness, help patients cope with illness and treatment, improve self-care and adherence to treatment, shorten hospital stay and prevent readmission, reduce anxiety and complications, and involve families in care.

**Domains of learning** (Bloom)

| Domain | What is learnt | Teaching method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cognitive | Knowledge and understanding | Explanation, discussion, leaflets |
| Affective | Attitudes, values, feelings | Discussion, role play, sharing experiences |
| Psychomotor | Skills | **Demonstration and return demonstration** (e.g. using an inhaler, checking blood sugar) |

**Principles of teaching**: assess readiness and motivation; start from what the patient already knows; teach from simple to complex; use the patient's language and simple words; short sessions; involve the patient actively; use more than one sense (see, hear, do); repeat key points; give written material; reinforce with praise; involve the family; choose a time free of pain and fatigue.

**Process of patient teaching (integrating the nursing process)**

| Step | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Assessment | Learning needs, readiness, literacy, language, culture, physical and emotional state, support at home |
| Nursing diagnosis | Deficient knowledge related to a new diagnosis of diabetes |
| Planning | Set measurable learning objectives with the patient (e.g. "will demonstrate insulin pen use correctly before discharge"); choose content, method, materials, time |
| Implementation | Teach, demonstrate, involve the family, give leaflets |
| Evaluation | Ask the patient to explain in their own words (**teach-back**) or do a return demonstration; re-teach what was missed; document what was taught and the response |

**Barriers to learning**: pain, anxiety, fatigue, low literacy, language difference, hearing or vision problems, cultural beliefs, denial, a noisy environment, and lack of time.

## Remember

- Admission: welcome, ID band (two identifiers), orient, baseline vital signs and weight, history, valuables listed and signed, inform doctor, record.
- MLC: preserve evidence, record facts and times, police informed by the doctor as per law.
- Discharge planning starts at admission; METHOD = Medications, Environment, Treatment, Health teaching, Outpatient follow-up, Diet.
- LAMA: inform doctor, explain risks, signed LAMA form, record. Abscond: search, inform, record.
- Terminal cleaning of the unit after discharge.
- Body mechanics: wide base, low centre of gravity, load close, use legs, pivot don't twist, push rather than lift.
- Isotonic = moves joint; isometric = tightens without moving.
- Immobility: pressure ulcers, contractures, osteoporosis, DVT, orthostatic hypotension, hypostatic pneumonia, constipation, UTI and stones, depression.
- Wheelchair at 45° on the strong side, brakes locked. Walk on the weak side. Cane in the strong hand.
- Crutches: weight on the hands, not the axillae; up with the good, down with the bad.
- Cast: handle with palms, elevate, 5 Ps, report unrelieved pain (compartment syndrome).
- Teaching: cognitive, affective, psychomotor; return demonstration and teach-back.

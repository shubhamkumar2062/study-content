# Unit 1: Health, illness and health care delivery

INC Unit I (5 hours): introduction to health and illness. INC Unit II (5 hours): health care delivery systems — levels of prevention, levels of care, health care agencies, hospitals and the health team. The two small units are joined here because the second builds directly on the first.

## 1.1 Concept of health

**WHO definition (1948).** Health is a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being, and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity. The definition is positive (it describes well-being, not only freedom from disease) and wide (it covers mind and social life, not only the body). Its weakness is the word "complete": almost nobody is completely well in every area, so it is seen as an ideal to work towards.

Later ideas added that health should let a person lead a **socially and economically productive life**, and many writers add a **spiritual** dimension.

**Other ways of looking at health**

| View | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Biomedical | Health = absence of disease; the body is like a machine |
| Ecological | Health = balance between people and their environment |
| Psychosocial | Health is shaped by social, cultural and psychological factors |
| Holistic | All of the above together; the whole person in their setting |

Nursing follows the **holistic** view: we care for the person, not only the disease.

## 1.2 Dimensions of health

| Dimension | What it means | Nursing example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical | Body working well: normal vital signs, sleep, nutrition, mobility | Monitoring vital signs, helping a patient walk |
| Mental | Thinking clearly, coping with stress, a sense of control | Reducing anxiety before surgery |
| Social | Good relationships, a role in family and society | Encouraging family visits |
| Emotional | Recognising and expressing feelings appropriately | Allowing a patient to talk about fear |
| Spiritual | Meaning, purpose, faith or values | Arranging prayer or a religious visit if wished |
| Intellectual | Learning, curiosity, decision-making | Teaching about a new illness |
| Vocational / occupational | Satisfaction from work | Discussing return to work after illness |
| Environmental | Safe water, air, housing, surroundings | Teaching about clean drinking water |

The dimensions affect each other. A man with a fracture (physical) may lose his job (vocational), worry about money (mental) and stop meeting friends (social).

## 1.3 Maslow's hierarchy of needs

Abraham Maslow arranged human needs in five levels. Lower needs must be met, at least partly, before a person can attend to higher ones.

```text
              /\
             /  \        5. Self-actualisation  (reaching one's potential)
            /----\
           /      \      4. Esteem              (self-respect, recognition)
          /--------\
         /          \    3. Love and belonging  (family, friends, acceptance)
        /------------\
       /              \  2. Safety and security (protection, freedom from fear)
      /----------------\
     /                  \1. Physiological       (air, water, food, sleep,
    /--------------------\                       elimination, temperature)
```

**Use in nursing — setting priorities**

1. Physiological needs come first: a clear airway, breathing, circulation, then fluids, food, elimination, rest. A breathless patient cannot listen to health teaching.
2. Safety next: side rails, preventing falls and infection, explaining procedures to reduce fear.
3. Love and belonging: involve family, be warm and accepting.
4. Esteem: call patients by name, ask permission, protect privacy and dignity, let them do what they can for themselves.
5. Self-actualisation: support patients to set their own goals, for example a stroke patient learning to write again.

Limitations: the order is not fixed for everyone (some people put faith or family above food), and it was not based on patients. Still, it is the most common framework for prioritising care in exams.

## 1.4 Health–illness continuum

Health and illness are not two boxes; they are two ends of a line. A person moves along it from day to day.

```text
Premature                                                           High-level
death  ←─ disability ─ symptoms ─ signs ─│─ awareness ─ education ─ growth → wellness
          (illness side)             neutral point          (wellness side)
```

- **Travis's illness–wellness continuum**: treatment can bring a person back to the neutral point, but moving towards high-level wellness needs awareness, education and growth.
- **Dunn's high-level wellness grid**: two axes — health (poor to good) and environment (unfavourable to favourable) — give four quadrants: high-level wellness in a favourable environment, emergent high-level wellness in an unfavourable one, protected poor health, and poor health in an unfavourable environment.

Nursing point: even a person with a chronic illness (diabetes, a disability) can be well in many dimensions. Our goal is to move each patient as far towards wellness as they can go.

## 1.5 Factors influencing health

| Group | Factors |
| --- | --- |
| Internal | Genetic make-up, age, sex, developmental stage, intellect, emotions, beliefs, spirituality |
| Lifestyle | Diet, exercise, sleep, smoking, alcohol, drugs, sexual behaviour, stress |
| Environmental | Water, sanitation, housing, air pollution, occupation, climate |
| Socio-economic | Income, education, employment, social class, caste and gender discrimination |
| Cultural | Food habits, health beliefs, use of traditional remedies, family pattern |
| Health services | Availability, cost, distance, quality of care, health insurance |

## 1.6 Causes and risk factors for illness

**Causes** of illness can be biological (micro-organisms, genetic defects), physical (injury, heat, radiation), chemical (poisons, drugs), nutritional (deficiency or excess) and psychological (stress).

A **risk factor** is anything that increases the chance of getting an illness. It does not cause the disease by itself.

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Genetic and physiological | Family history of diabetes, obesity, pregnancy |
| Age | Infants and the elderly for infections; older people for heart disease |
| Lifestyle | Smoking (lung cancer, heart disease), sedentary life, high-salt diet |
| Environment | Overcrowding (tuberculosis), stagnant water (malaria), industrial dust |
| Modifiable vs non-modifiable | Smoking and weight can be changed; age, sex and heredity cannot |

Nurses teach people to reduce modifiable risk factors — this is health promotion.

## 1.7 Illness: types and illness behaviour

**Disease** is a change in body structure or function that can be measured. **Illness** is the person's own experience of feeling unwell. A person may have a disease without feeling ill (early hypertension), or feel ill without a disease being found.

**Types of illness**

| Type | Features | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acute | Sudden onset, short course, usually recovers | Common cold, appendicitis |
| Chronic | Slow onset, lasts six months or more, needs long-term management | Diabetes, arthritis |
| Terminal | Not expected to recover; leads to death | Advanced cancer |
| Communicable | Spreads from person to person | Tuberculosis |
| Non-communicable | Does not spread | Hypertension |

**Illness behaviour** is how a person notices symptoms, decides what they mean and seeks help. Suchman described five stages:

1. **Symptom experience** — the person notices something is wrong, feels concern and decides whether it is serious.
2. **Assumption of the sick role** — accepts being ill, seeks advice from family, may stay away from work.
3. **Medical care contact** — goes to a health worker for diagnosis and treatment.
4. **Dependent patient role** — depends on health workers; follows treatment.
5. **Recovery and rehabilitation** — gives up the sick role and returns to normal duties.

Illness behaviour is affected by age, culture, knowledge, cost, distance to care, past experience and how serious the symptoms seem. Some people delay care; some deny symptoms. The nurse respects the patient's beliefs and gently corrects wrong ideas.

## 1.8 Impact of illness on the patient and family

**On the patient**: changes in body image (amputation, mastectomy), loss of independence, fear and anxiety, loss of income, changes in self-esteem, pain, disturbed sleep, loneliness in hospital.

**On the family**: role changes (a wife becomes the earner), financial burden, emotional stress, disturbed family routine, care-giving load, changes in relationships, worry about the future. Children may feel neglected.

Nursing role: explain the illness, involve the family in planning care, teach care-giving skills, refer to social workers or support groups, and watch for carer fatigue.

## 1.9 Levels of prevention

Prevention means stopping a disease from starting, catching it early, or reducing its effects.

| Level | Aim | Activities | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primordial | Stop risk factors from appearing in a population | Healthy lifestyles from childhood | Teaching school children about diet and exercise |
| Primary | Prevent disease before it occurs | **Health promotion** (education, nutrition, sanitation, exercise) and **specific protection** (immunisation, chemoprophylaxis, use of helmets) | BCG vaccine, iodised salt, hand washing |
| Secondary | Early diagnosis and prompt treatment to halt the disease | Screening, case finding, early treatment | Blood pressure screening, Pap smear, breast self-examination |
| Tertiary | Limit disability and restore function | Disability limitation, rehabilitation | Physiotherapy after a stroke, artificial limbs |

```text
Health ──► Risk factors ──► Early disease ──► Advanced disease ──► Disability
        Primordial      Primary          Secondary           Tertiary
```

## 1.10 Levels of care

| Level | What it provides | In India |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary care | First contact; promotive, preventive and simple curative care close to home | Sub-centres and Health and Wellness Centres, Primary Health Centres (PHC) |
| Secondary care | Specialist care for more complex problems; referral from primary level | Community Health Centres (CHC), sub-district and district hospitals |
| Tertiary care | Highly specialised care, advanced equipment, teaching and research | Medical college hospitals, AIIMS, regional and super-specialty institutes |

Under the Indian Public Health Standards, the usual population norms are: one **sub-centre** for 5,000 people (3,000 in hilly and tribal areas), one **PHC** for 30,000 (20,000 in hilly and tribal areas) and one **CHC** for 1,20,000 (80,000 in hilly and tribal areas). A good **referral system** links the levels, so patients go up for specialist care and come back down for follow-up.

## 1.11 Types of health care agencies and services

| Agency | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Hospital | In-patient and out-patient diagnosis, treatment and care |
| Clinic / dispensary | Out-patient care, often for one specialty or a small area |
| Hospice | Comfort and palliative care for people in the last stage of life, and support for families |
| Rehabilitation centre | Restoring function after injury, stroke or addiction (physiotherapy, occupational therapy) |
| Extended care facility (long-term care, nursing home) | Care for people who need help for a long time but not acute hospital care, often the elderly |
| Home health care | Nursing and therapy in the patient's own home |
| Day care centre | Care during the day, for example chemotherapy or dialysis, with no overnight stay |

## 1.12 Hospitals: types, organisation and functions

**Types of hospitals**

| Basis | Types |
| --- | --- |
| Ownership | Government (central, state, local bodies), private (for-profit), voluntary/trust/charitable |
| Function | General, specialty (cancer, eye, mental health), teaching, research |
| Length of stay | Short-stay (acute) and long-stay (chronic, rehabilitation) |
| Size | Small, medium, large (by number of beds) |
| System of medicine | Modern medicine, AYUSH (Ayurveda, Yoga, Unani, Siddha, Homoeopathy) |

**Organisation** (a typical government hospital)

```text
                       Governing body / Health department
                                      │
                         Medical Superintendent / Director
          ┌───────────────┬──────────────┼──────────────┬──────────────┐
   Medical services  Nursing service  Diagnostic     Support        Administration
   (clinical depts)  (Chief Nursing   (lab, radiology) (pharmacy,    (accounts, records,
                     Officer / Nursing                  dietary,      stores, security)
                     Superintendent)                    laundry, CSSD)
                          │
            Deputy / Assistant Nursing Superintendents
                          │
             Ward sisters / Nursing officers in charge
                          │
              Staff nurses → Nursing students → Support staff
```

**Functions of a hospital**: patient care (diagnosis, treatment, nursing, rehabilitation), prevention and health promotion, education of health workers, and research.

## 1.13 The health care team

| Member | Main role |
| --- | --- |
| Doctor | Diagnosis, medical treatment, prescribing |
| Nurse | Assessment, nursing care 24 hours a day, giving prescribed treatment, teaching, co-ordinating care |
| Pharmacist | Dispensing medicines, drug information |
| Dietitian | Planning therapeutic diets |
| Physiotherapist | Exercise, mobility and breathing therapy |
| Occupational therapist | Helping people regain daily-living and work skills |
| Medical social worker | Financial and social problems, discharge support |
| Laboratory and radiology technologists | Diagnostic tests and imaging |
| Counsellor / psychologist | Emotional support and counselling |
| Multipurpose health workers, ASHA | Community-level care and link with services |
| Support staff (ward attendants, housekeeping) | Cleanliness, transport, help with basic care |

The nurse is with the patient the longest, so she often **co-ordinates** the team: passes information, arranges investigations and makes sure the plan of care is followed.

## Remember

- WHO 1948: complete physical, mental and social well-being, not merely absence of disease.
- Maslow: physiological → safety → love and belonging → esteem → self-actualisation; meet lower needs first.
- Health–illness continuum: health is a moving position on a line, not a fixed state.
- Risk factor raises the chance of illness; modifiable ones (smoking, weight) can be changed.
- Suchman's five stages: symptom experience, sick role, medical care contact, dependent role, recovery.
- Prevention: primary (promotion + specific protection), secondary (early diagnosis), tertiary (disability limitation and rehabilitation).
- Care levels: primary (SC, PHC), secondary (CHC, district hospital), tertiary (medical colleges).
- PHC for 30,000 and CHC for 1,20,000 in plains (20,000 and 80,000 in hilly/tribal areas).
- Hospice = palliative care at end of life; rehabilitation centre = restoring function.
- The nurse co-ordinates the health team because she is with the patient round the clock.

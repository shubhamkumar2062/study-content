# How to use this guide

Nursing Foundation I (including the First Aid module) is a first-semester course of the INC 2021 B.Sc Nursing curriculum: 6 credits of theory (120 hours), with 80 hours of skill lab and 160 hours of clinical practice in medical and surgical wards. It is the base of everything you will do on the wards — vital signs, infection control, bed making, positioning, admission and discharge, moving patients and first aid.

## There is no university exam for this course this semester

**Read this first.** Nursing Foundation I has **no end-semester university examination in the first semester**. In the first semester you earn only **internal marks**: 25 for theory (10 continuous assessment + 15 sessional exams) and 25 for practical. These internal marks are **carried forward** and added to the internal marks of **Nursing Foundation II** in the second semester (the two are averaged).

The **university theory exam is "Nursing Foundations (I & II)" at the end of the second semester**: one 3-hour, 75-mark paper (plus 25 internal marks), which can ask questions from **both** Nursing Foundation I and Nursing Foundation II. So everything in this pack will be examined — just one semester later, together with Nursing Foundation II. Do not let it go cold: keep revising it while you study Part II.

You must get at least 50% in theory and 50% in practical separately, and you must pass the mandatory First Aid module (pass mark 50%).

## The practice paper in this pack

Because there is no paper this semester, the mock test here is a **practice paper** set in the same pattern as the Nursing Foundations (I & II) university paper (INC Regulations 2020, Appendix 3, 75-mark pattern), using only Part I topics. There is **no internal choice** — answer every question. Your university may print the paper differently; confirm with your university.

| Part | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MCQ | 12 multiple-choice questions | 12 × 1 = 12 |
| Essay | 2 long (essay / situation-type) answers | 2 × 15 = 30 |
| Short | 5 short answers | 5 × 5 = 25 |
| Very short | 4 very short answers | 4 × 2 = 8 |
| **Total** | 3 hours | **75** |

There is also a **quick MCQ test** of 20 questions in 20 minutes for daily practice.

Suggested timing for the 3-hour paper: MCQs 10 minutes, each essay 40 minutes, each short answer 12–13 minutes, each very short answer 4 minutes, and 10 minutes to check.

## How the units are arranged

The INC syllabus has fourteen units, several of them very small. To keep each notes file a useful size, small related units are joined:

| Notes file | INC unit(s) | Theory hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit 1 Health, illness and health care delivery | I + II | 5 + 5 |
| Unit 2 History of nursing and nursing as a profession | III | 12 |
| Unit 3 Communication, nurse–patient relationship, documentation and reporting | IV + V | 8 + 4 |
| Unit 4 Vital signs | VI | 15 |
| Unit 5 Equipment and linen, infection control and a safe environment | VII + VIII + X | 3 + 10 + 5 |
| Unit 6 Comfort, rest and sleep, and pain | IX | 15 |
| Unit 7 Admission and discharge, mobility and immobility, patient education | XI + XII + XIII | 6 + 8 + 4 |
| Unit 8 First aid (including basic CPR) | XIV | 20 |

**What is not in this course**: personal hygiene of the patient (bed bath, oral care, hair care), the nursing process in detail, nutrition, elimination, oxygen therapy, and the full administration of medicines (routes, calculation of dosages) are taught in **Nursing Foundation II**. This pack covers the principles of safe medicine use only where Part I needs them — the "rights" of administration and the routes used for pain relief (Unit 6). No doses are given anywhere in this pack; doses are always as prescribed.

Procedures are written as **steps with the reason for each step** — learn the reasons, because examiners and OSCE assessors ask "why".

## Probability ratings

- **High** — a favourite; appears in most papers in some form. Learn the full answer.
- **Medium** — appears every few papers or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — less common, but good for MCQs and very short answers.

These ratings are estimates from the syllabus hours, the competencies INC lists, and the usual style of nursing foundation papers. They are not a promise of what will be asked.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. **Write procedures as numbered steps with a rationale**: "Count respiration with fingers still on the wrist — the patient does not change the breathing pattern." This is what "with rationale" questions want.
2. **Start every essay with a definition**, then a classification or list of types, then the main content in points, then **nursing responsibilities**, and finish with a line on documentation or health teaching.
3. **Use tables for comparisons**: fever vs hyperthermia, medical vs surgical asepsis, acute vs chronic pain, therapeutic vs non-therapeutic communication, types of beds, colour coding of waste.
4. **Draw simple diagrams**: the chain of infection, Maslow's pyramid, the communication process, the health–illness continuum, the rule of nines, pulse sites. Even a neat box diagram earns marks.
5. **Learn the numbers examiners like**: normal temperature, pulse, respiration, BP and SpO2 for adults and newborns; Fowler's angles; CPR rate, depth and ratio; WHO five moments; colour codes of the BMW Rules 2016; Florence Nightingale's dates; INC Act 1947.
6. **Situation-type essays** ("A 60-year-old patient is admitted with high fever…"): write assessment findings, two or three nursing problems, and the nursing care for each, with rationale.
7. **Very short answers (2 marks)**: a one-line definition plus one example or one fact.
8. **First aid answers**: always begin with safety and calling for help, and include a "do not" list.
9. MCQs come from small facts — read the "Remember" list of each notes file and the revision sheet the night before.
10. This course is also tested in the **practical exam and OSCE** — practise every procedure on a mannequin, saying the rationale aloud as you go.

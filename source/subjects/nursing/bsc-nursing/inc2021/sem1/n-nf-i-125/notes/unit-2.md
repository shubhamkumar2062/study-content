# Unit 2: History of nursing and nursing as a profession

INC Unit III (12 hours): history of nursing in the world and in India, the contributions of Florence Nightingale, the meaning, scope and functions of nursing, nursing as a profession, values, and the code of ethics and professional conduct for nurses.

## 2.1 Why study the history of nursing?

History shows how nursing grew from untrained, religious or family care into a profession with its own education, council and ethics. It explains why we follow certain practices (clean hands, fresh air, record keeping), gives pride in the profession, and shows how today's problems were solved before.

## 2.2 History of nursing in the world

| Period | Main features |
| --- | --- |
| Primitive times | Illness blamed on evil spirits; care given by mothers and the family; the medicine man used charms and herbs |
| Ancient civilisations | Egypt: embalming, knowledge of herbs. Greece: Hippocrates (father of medicine) taught observation and natural causes of disease. Rome: military hospitals |
| Early Christian era | Care of the sick seen as a religious duty. Deaconesses (Phoebe is often named as the first visiting nurse). Fabiola, a Roman woman, set up a free hospital in Rome |
| Middle Ages | Religious orders and monasteries ran hospitals. Military nursing orders (Knights Hospitallers) cared for pilgrims and soldiers during the Crusades |
| "Dark period" of nursing (about 1500–1850) | After the Reformation many monasteries closed. Nursing was done by poorly paid, untrained and often uneducated women. Hospitals were dirty and overcrowded. Charles Dickens's character Sairey Gamp became a symbol of this careless nurse |
| Reform period | Pastor Theodor Fliedner started a deaconess institute at Kaiserswerth, Germany (1836), training women in nursing; Florence Nightingale trained there. Modern nursing began with Nightingale's school in 1860 |
| 20th century onwards | Nursing councils and registration, the International Council of Nurses (ICN, founded 1899), university degrees in nursing, specialisation, nursing research and nurse practitioners |

## 2.3 Florence Nightingale (1820–1910): founder of modern nursing

**Life in brief**

- Born on **12 May 1820** in Florence, Italy, to a wealthy English family; she was named after the city. She was well educated in languages, mathematics and philosophy.
- Felt a "call" to serve. Against her family's wishes she trained at **Kaiserswerth** (1851) and later became superintendent of a hospital for gentlewomen in London (1853).
- **Crimean War (1854)**: she went with 38 nurses to the British military hospital at **Scutari**. She found filth, no clean linen, poor food and very high death rates. She organised cleaning, laundry, kitchens, sanitation and records. Death rates fell sharply. She walked the wards at night with a lamp — "**the Lady with the Lamp**".
- After the war the public raised the **Nightingale Fund**, which she used to open the **Nightingale Training School for Nurses at St Thomas' Hospital, London, in 1860** — the first secular, organised school of nursing.
- Wrote **Notes on Nursing** (1859) and **Notes on Hospitals**, and many reports.
- She took great interest in the **health of the army and people of India**, and her reports helped improve sanitation in Indian cantonments.
- First woman to receive the **Order of Merit (1907)**. Died on **13 August 1910**.
- **International Nurses Day** is celebrated on her birthday, **12 May**.

**Her contributions**

1. **Nursing education** — a planned course, a residence for students, selection of good-character candidates, and nurse teachers.
2. **Environment and hygiene** — fresh air, light, warmth, cleanliness, quiet, clean water and drainage. Her environmental theory still guides patient care.
3. **Statistics** — she collected and analysed death rates and showed them in diagrams (the polar area or "coxcomb" diagram) to persuade the government. She is seen as a pioneer of using data in health care.
4. **Hospital planning** — pavilion wards with cross-ventilation and good spacing of beds.
5. **Nursing as a respectable career for women**, with dignity, discipline and ethics.
6. **Observation and records** — she taught nurses to observe patients carefully and record what they saw.
7. **Public health and the army** — reforms in military health and sanitation.

The **Nightingale Pledge**, recited by students at the lamp-lighting ceremony, was written in her honour (in 1893) by a committee in the USA; she did not write it herself.

## 2.4 History of nursing in India

| Time | Event |
| --- | --- |
| Ancient India | The **Charaka Samhita** describes the four pillars of treatment: physician, medicine, attendant (nurse) and patient, and the qualities of a good attendant — knowledge, skill, devotion to the patient and cleanliness. **Sushruta** described surgery and the care of surgical patients. Emperor **Ashoka** (3rd century BC) is said to have built hospitals for people and animals |
| Muslim period | Unani medicine; hospitals (shifakhanas) in large cities |
| 1664 | The East India Company opened a hospital for soldiers at **Fort St George, Madras** — the first of the British-style hospitals |
| 19th century | Training of midwives began in Madras; the **first school of nursing** was started at the **Government General Hospital, Madras, in 1871**. Mission hospitals in many places trained Indian women as nurses. Military nursing services for the British army in India were organised in the late 1800s |
| 1908 | The **Trained Nurses' Association of India (TNAI)** was formed |
| 1926 | The **Madras Registration Council** — the first state body to register nurses |
| 1946 | The Bhore Committee (Health Survey and Development Committee) recommended better nursing education and more nurses. **B.Sc Nursing** began at the College of Nursing, Delhi, and Christian Medical College, Vellore |
| 1947 | The **Indian Nursing Council Act** was passed, creating the **Indian Nursing Council (INC)** to bring uniform standards of nursing education across the country |
| After independence | Post-basic B.Sc, M.Sc and PhD programmes; state nursing councils in every state; nurse practitioner programmes; INC revised the B.Sc curriculum (Regulations 2020, notified 2021) |

**Main nursing bodies in India**

| Body | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Indian Nursing Council (INC) | Statutory body; lays down syllabus and standards, recognises institutions |
| State Nursing and Midwifery Councils | Register nurses and midwives to practise in the state; conduct some exams |
| TNAI | Professional association; welfare, conferences, journal (*The Nursing Journal of India*) |
| Military Nursing Service (MNS) | Nurses in the armed forces |

## 2.5 Nursing: definitions and concepts

**Nurse**: a person who has completed a recognised nursing programme and is registered to practise, who cares for people who are sick or well.

**Nursing — well-known views** (in our words)

- **Florence Nightingale**: nursing is putting the patient in the best possible condition so that nature can do its healing work.
- **Virginia Henderson**: the nurse helps a person, sick or well, to carry out the activities that lead to health, recovery or a peaceful death, which the person would do alone if they had the strength, will or knowledge — and to make them independent as soon as possible.
- **International Council of Nurses**: nursing covers independent and collaborative care of people of all ages, families, groups and communities, sick or well, in all settings. It includes promoting health, preventing illness and caring for the ill, disabled and dying, plus advocacy, research and shaping health policy.

**Central concepts of nursing (the metaparadigm)**

| Concept | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Person | The receiver of care — an individual, family or community, with physical, mental, social and spiritual needs |
| Environment | Internal and external surroundings that affect the person |
| Health | The person's position on the health–illness continuum |
| Nursing | The actions the nurse takes with and for the person |

**Philosophy of nursing**: belief in the worth and dignity of every person, the right of everyone to good health care without discrimination, care of the whole person, and the nurse's duty to keep learning.

**Objectives of nursing**: promote health, prevent illness, restore health, help people cope with disability, and support a peaceful death.

## 2.6 Characteristics, nature and scope of nursing

**Characteristics / nature**

- It is both an **art** (caring, compassion, communication) and a **science** (knowledge from anatomy, physiology, microbiology, psychology).
- It is **holistic** — caring for body, mind and spirit.
- It is **helping and caring**, and it is for people **sick and well**.
- It uses the **nursing process** — a systematic, problem-solving method.
- It is **dynamic**, changing with new knowledge and technology.
- It is a **24-hour service**.

**Scope of nursing**

| Area | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Hospital nursing | Wards, ICU, operation theatre, emergency, dialysis |
| Community health nursing | Sub-centres, PHCs, home visits, school health, national programmes |
| Nursing education | Teachers, principals, clinical instructors |
| Nursing administration | Ward in-charge, nursing superintendent, chief nursing officer |
| Specialised practice | Critical care, oncology, cardiac, mental health, paediatric, midwifery, nurse practitioner |
| Industrial / occupational health | Factories, industries |
| Military nursing | Armed forces |
| Research | Nursing studies to improve care |
| Private and independent practice | Home care, consultation |
| International nursing | WHO, abroad |

## 2.7 Functions of a nurse

| Function | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Independent | Actions the nurse decides and does on her own knowledge | Positioning, bed making, health teaching, comfort measures, hygiene, observation |
| Dependent | Actions done on the written order of a doctor | Giving prescribed medicines, IV fluids, special diets |
| Interdependent (collaborative) | Done jointly with other team members | Planning discharge with the doctor, physiotherapist and social worker |

**Roles of the nurse**: caregiver, communicator, teacher/educator, counsellor, advocate (speaking up for the patient's rights), manager, co-ordinator, change agent, researcher, leader, rehabilitator, comforter.

## 2.8 Qualities of a nurse

- **Personal**: good health, neat appearance, honesty, patience, kindness, emotional stability, sense of humour, punctuality, tact.
- **Professional**: sound knowledge, skill, accuracy, keen observation, good judgement, responsibility, ability to keep confidences, interest in learning.
- **Social and ethical**: respect for all people without discrimination, team spirit, courtesy, loyalty, sympathy and empathy (understanding the patient's feelings without being overwhelmed).

## 2.9 Categories of nursing personnel in India

| By education | By position (typical hospital) |
| --- | --- |
| Auxiliary Nurse Midwife (ANM) | Nursing officer / staff nurse |
| General Nursing and Midwifery (GNM) | Senior nursing officer / ward sister |
| B.Sc Nursing; Post-basic B.Sc Nursing | Assistant / Deputy Nursing Superintendent |
| M.Sc Nursing; Nurse Practitioner programmes | Nursing Superintendent / Chief Nursing Officer |
| M.Phil / PhD in Nursing | In education: tutor, lecturer, professor, principal |

Support workers (nursing assistants, ward attendants) help but are not registered nurses.

## 2.10 Nursing as a profession

A **profession** is an occupation that needs long, special education, has its own body of knowledge, serves society and is controlled by its own code of ethics.

**Criteria of a profession and how nursing meets them**

| Criterion | Nursing |
| --- | --- |
| Specialised body of knowledge | Nursing theories, research, nursing process |
| Education in a university | B.Sc, M.Sc, PhD in nursing |
| Service to society | Care of the sick and promotion of health |
| Code of ethics | ICN and INC codes |
| Professional organisation | INC, state councils, TNAI |
| Autonomy | Independent nursing functions, nurse practitioners |
| Lifelong commitment and career ladder | Continuing education, promotion |
| Licensing / registration | Registration with the state nursing council |

## 2.11 Values

**Values** are the beliefs a person holds about what is right, good and important; they guide behaviour. **Professional values** for nurses include:

- **Human dignity** — respect for every person's worth and uniqueness.
- **Altruism** — concern for the welfare of others.
- **Autonomy** — respecting the patient's right to decide.
- **Integrity** — honesty and acting by the code.
- **Social justice** — fair care for all regardless of caste, religion, money or gender.
- **Caring, accountability and excellence.**

**Why values matter**: they guide decisions in difficult situations, build public trust, help in ethical dilemmas, and make care consistent. A nurse should be aware of her own values so they do not bias care (values clarification).

## 2.12 Ethics, code of ethics and professional conduct

**Ethics** is the study of right and wrong conduct. **Ethical principles** in nursing:

| Principle | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Autonomy | Respect the patient's right to make decisions | Taking informed consent |
| Beneficence | Do good | Giving timely care |
| Non-maleficence | Do no harm | Checking a patient's identity before a procedure |
| Justice | Be fair | Giving care by need, not by status |
| Veracity | Tell the truth | Not giving false hope |
| Fidelity | Keep promises | Returning when you said you would |
| Confidentiality | Keep information private | Not discussing a patient in the canteen |
| Accountability | Answer for your actions | Reporting your own error |

**Code of ethics**: a written set of principles that a profession agrees to follow. The **ICN Code of Ethics for Nurses** (first adopted in 1953 and revised since) groups the nurse's duties under nurses and patients/people, nurses and practice, nurses and the profession, and nurses and global health/co-workers. In India the **INC Code of Ethics and Code of Professional Conduct for Nurses** set out the same ideas for Indian practice; read the current version on the INC website.

Main points of these codes (in our words):

1. Respect the life, dignity and rights of every person, without discrimination.
2. Keep patient information confidential and share it only for care or as the law requires.
3. Practise within your competence and keep your knowledge up to date.
4. Take responsibility and be accountable for your own actions.
5. Protect patients from unsafe or unethical practice and report it.
6. Work co-operatively and respectfully with colleagues.
7. Keep high standards of personal conduct that bring credit to the profession.
8. Take part in improving health care and the profession.

**Professional conduct** is behaving according to these standards: punctuality, proper uniform, polite speech, no acceptance of gifts that may influence care, no misuse of drugs or records, honesty in documentation, and respect for colleagues. **Professional misconduct** (negligence, falsifying records, breaching confidentiality, abusing a patient) can lead to disciplinary action by the nursing council, including removal from the register.

# How to use this subject

Applied Physiology (PHYS110) is a first-semester B.Sc Nursing course of 3 credits (60 hours of theory) under the Indian Nursing Council (INC) Regulations 2020. It explains how each body system works, how the systems keep the internal environment steady, and what changes in disease — the base for every nursing assessment you will do. Study it alongside Applied Anatomy: learn the structure there, then the function here.

## How the notes are arranged

The INC syllabus has 11 units. Some are small, so related units are joined:

| Notes file | INC unit(s) | Hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit 1 General physiology | I | 4 |
| Unit 2 Respiratory system | II | 6 |
| Unit 3 Digestive system | III | 8 |
| Unit 4 Circulatory system and blood | IV (circulatory) + V (blood) | 6 + 5 |
| Unit 5 Endocrine system and sensory organs | VI (endocrine) + VII (sensory organs) | 5 + 4 |
| Unit 6 Musculoskeletal system | VIII | 6 |
| Unit 7 Renal and reproductive systems | IX (renal) + X (reproductive) | 4 + 4 |
| Unit 8 Nervous system | XI | 8 |

Every notes file ends with an "Application and implication in nursing" section, because the INC lists it in every unit and examiners like to see it in essays, and a short "Remember" list for quick revision.

**Suggested routine**: read one notes file a day; draw the flow diagrams (feedback loop, cardiac conduction, clotting cascade, CSF circulation, reflex arc) from memory; learn the normal values in the tables; then write out two or three questions from the bank without looking, and check against the model answer.

## Exam pattern

Applied Anatomy and Applied Physiology are examined together in **one university theory paper of 75 marks and 3 hours**. **Section A is Applied Anatomy (37 marks)** and **Section B is Applied Physiology (38 marks)**. Internal assessment carries 25 marks, so the course total is 100.

**Section B — Applied Physiology (38 marks)** as given in INC Appendix 3:

| Part | Questions | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MCQ | 7 × 1 | 7 |
| Essay | 1 × 10 | 10 |
| Short answer | 3 × 5 | 15 |
| Very short answer | 3 × 2 | 6 |
| **Total** | | **38** |

There is **no internal choice** in the INC pattern — every question must be answered. Plan about 90 minutes of the 3 hours for this section. Universities sometimes add their own instructions (for example separate answer books for the two sections), so confirm the exact paper format with your university.

The mock tests in this subject follow this pattern:

- **Section B paper (38 marks, 90 minutes)** — exactly the physiology half of the combined paper.
- **Quick MCQ test (20 questions, 20 minutes)** — for daily practice.

## How probability ratings work

Each question in the bank is rated **high**, **medium** or **low**:

- **High** — topics with many teaching hours and that appear often in nursing physiology papers (for example cardiac cycle, regulation of blood pressure, clotting mechanism, erythropoiesis, gastric juice, transport of oxygen, menstrual cycle, mechanism of muscle contraction, CSF, reflex arc, pain).
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a larger question.
- **Low** — less common, but useful for MCQs and very short answers.

The ratings are estimates based on the weight of each unit in the syllabus and common patterns in nursing examinations. They are not a promise of what will be asked, so do not skip any unit — with no internal choice, every unit can appear.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. **Start with a definition** and, where possible, a normal value (for example "Cardiac output is the volume of blood pumped by each ventricle per minute — about 5 L/min").
2. **Draw a labelled diagram or flow chart** in every essay: the cardiac cycle phases, conduction system, clotting cascade, oxygen dissociation curve, nephron with filtration forces, CSF pathway, reflex arc, hormonal changes in the menstrual cycle. Flow charts with arrows score well and save time.
3. **Learn the numbers** in the tables (TV 500 mL, CO 5 L/min, GFR 125 mL/min, RBC life 120 days, platelets 1.5–4 lakh/µL, CSF 150 mL). MCQs and very short answers test them often. Write "about" — exact values differ between textbooks and laboratories.
4. **Structure essays** with headings: definition → mechanism/steps → regulation → factors → disorders/alterations → nursing implications.
5. **End every essay with nursing implications** — two or three points linking the physiology to patient care.
6. **Very short answers (2 marks)**: two clear points or a definition plus one example. Do not write a page.
7. **Use the right words**: "depolarisation", "negative feedback", "autoregulation", "Frank–Starling law", "Bohr effect" — examiners look for them.
8. **Manage time**: about 20 minutes for the essay, 10 minutes per short answer, 3–4 minutes per very short answer and a few minutes for the MCQs, leaving time to check.

## Other useful notes

- Keep a separate page of normal values and revise it weekly.
- Compare pairs that are commonly confused: rods vs cones, sympathetic vs parasympathetic, UMN vs LMN, intrinsic vs extrinsic clotting, active vs passive immunity, myopia vs hypermetropia, oogenesis vs spermatogenesis.
- The last-night revision sheet collects the key facts of all eight files on one page.

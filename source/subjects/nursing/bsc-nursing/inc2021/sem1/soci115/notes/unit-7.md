# Unit 7: Social problems, vulnerable groups, rights and welfare

This notes file covers the second part of INC Unit VI (Social organization and disorganization): major social problems, vulnerable groups, fundamental rights of individuals, women and children, the role of the nurse in reducing social problems and enhancing coping, and social welfare programmes in India.

Scheme names, ministries and laws below are as listed on official sites (indiacode.nic.in, wcd.gov.in, socialjustice.gov.in, pib.gov.in). Benefit amounts and eligibility change often, so check the ministry website before quoting figures.

## 7.1 What is a social problem?

A **social problem** is a condition that affects a large number of people in an undesirable way, that society considers harmful, and that it believes can be solved by **collective (group) action**.

Features: it affects many people; it is judged harmful by society's values; it has social causes; problems are inter-linked (poverty → illiteracy → child labour → poor health); and solutions need group effort, policy and law.

## 7.2 Major social problems in India

### Poverty

**Poverty** is the inability to meet basic needs – food, clothing, shelter, health care, education. **Absolute poverty** means falling below a minimum standard (poverty line); **relative poverty** means being poor compared with others in the same society.

- Causes: unemployment and underemployment, low agricultural productivity, large families, illiteracy, inequality in land and assets, caste discrimination, illness and disability, natural disasters, inflation.
- Health effects: malnutrition, anaemia, low birth weight, infections, TB, poor maternal care, untreated chronic disease, less use of services.
- Measures: employment guarantee in rural areas (MGNREGA), subsidised food grains under the National Food Security Act, housing schemes, self-help groups and skill training, health insurance (Ayushman Bharat – PM-JAY), pensions under the National Social Assistance Programme.

### Housing

Problems: shortage of houses, overcrowding, slums and unauthorised settlements, homelessness, poor ventilation, no safe water or toilets.

Health effects: spread of respiratory infections and TB, diarrhoeal diseases, vector-borne diseases (dengue, malaria), accidents and fires, stress and lack of privacy.

Measures: Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana (urban and rural housing), slum improvement, Swachh Bharat Mission (toilets and sanitation), Jal Jeevan Mission (tap water).

### Food supplies

Problems: food insecurity, hunger, uneven distribution, price rise, wastage, adulteration, poor diet quality.

Health effects: protein-energy malnutrition, micronutrient deficiencies (iron, iodine, vitamin A), stunting and wasting in children.

Measures: Public Distribution System under the National Food Security Act; Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0 (supplementary nutrition for children, pregnant and nursing women and adolescent girls); PM POSHAN (school meals); food fortification; Food Safety and Standards Act.

### Illiteracy

Illiteracy is the inability to read and write with understanding. It is higher among women, rural people, and Scheduled Castes and Tribes.

Effects: poverty, unemployment, superstition, early marriage, large families, low use of health services, inability to read labels and instructions.

Measures: the **Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009** (free and compulsory education for children aged 6–14), adult literacy programmes, scholarships, school meals to keep children in school.

### Prostitution and trafficking

**Prostitution** (commercial sex work) is offering sexual services for money; **trafficking** is the recruiting, moving or holding of persons by force, fraud or coercion for exploitation.

- Causes: poverty, unemployment, broken families, desertion, early marriage, trafficking, lack of education, some traditional practices.
- Health effects: sexually transmitted infections and HIV, unwanted pregnancy, violence, substance abuse, depression.
- Law: **Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956** – punishes keeping a brothel, living on the earnings of prostitution, procuring and trafficking; provides rescue and protective homes.
- Measures: rescue and rehabilitation, shelter homes, vocational training, targeted HIV prevention for sex workers.

### Dowry

**Dowry** is money, property or goods given or demanded in connection with a marriage. It is illegal under the **Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961**. A death of a woman linked with dowry harassment within seven years of marriage is "dowry death" under **section 80 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023**.

- Causes: tradition, status display, son preference, greed, materialism, social pressure.
- Effects: debt of the bride's family, harassment, domestic violence, suicide and dowry deaths, female foeticide and neglect of girls.

### Child labour

**Child labour** is work that deprives children of childhood, schooling and health.

- Law: the **Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986** prohibits employment of children in all occupations (except limited family work and the entertainment industry outside school hours, under conditions) and bars adolescents from hazardous work. Article 24 of the Constitution bans employment of children below 14 years in factories, mines and hazardous work.
- Causes: poverty, large families, illiteracy of parents, demand for cheap labour, debt.
- Health effects: injuries, exposure to chemicals and dust, malnutrition, stunted growth, abuse, no education.

### Child abuse

**Child abuse** is any act or failure to act by a parent, caregiver or other person that results in harm to a child.

| Type | Examples | Signs the nurse may notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical | Hitting, burning, shaking | Unexplained bruises, fractures of different ages, burns with a pattern |
| Sexual | Molestation, rape, exploitation | Genital injury, STI, fear of a particular person, sudden change in behaviour |
| Emotional | Threats, constant criticism, rejection | Withdrawal, low self-esteem, anxiety |
| Neglect | Failure to provide food, care, schooling, treatment | Malnutrition, poor hygiene, missed immunization |

Laws: **Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act, 2012** (child-friendly procedures; reporting of offences is mandatory); **Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015** (children in need of care and protection, Child Welfare Committees, adoption). The nurse must report suspected sexual abuse as required by law, give first aid and care, preserve evidence as per protocol, and refer to the Child Welfare Committee or police.

### Juvenile delinquency

**Delinquency** is an offence or anti-social act committed by a child (below 18 years in Indian law). Under the Juvenile Justice Act, 2015 such a child is a "child in conflict with law" and is dealt with by Juvenile Justice Boards, with the aim of reform, not punishment.

Causes: broken or poor families, neglect, bad company, school failure, poverty, substance use, media influence, lack of recreation.

Prevention: family support, school counselling, recreation, observation homes and special homes with education and training, after-care.

### Crime

**Crime** is an act forbidden by law and punishable by the state. The main criminal law is now the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023**, which replaced the Indian Penal Code from July 2024.

Causes: economic (poverty, unemployment), social (broken homes, slums, social disorganization), psychological (personality disorders), substance abuse, political and cultural factors.

Health links: injuries, sexual violence, trauma, prison health problems. Nurses in emergency departments handle medico-legal cases – they record facts accurately, preserve evidence, and inform the police as per hospital policy.

### Substance abuse

**Substance abuse** is the harmful use of psychoactive substances – alcohol, tobacco, cannabis, opioids, sedatives, inhalants – leading to dependence and damage to health and social life.

- Causes: peer pressure, curiosity, stress, family history, easy availability, advertising, unemployment.
- Effects: liver disease, cancers, accidents, violence, HIV and hepatitis through shared needles, family breakdown, crime, loss of work.
- Law: **Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act, 1985** controls narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances and punishes illicit trafficking.
- Measures: de-addiction centres, counselling, awareness drives (Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan), tobacco control law and cessation services, support groups such as Alcoholics Anonymous.

### HIV/AIDS

HIV infection is a health problem with heavy **social** effects: stigma and discrimination, loss of work, rejection by family, orphaned children, and burden on poor households.

- The **HIV and AIDS (Prevention and Control) Act, 2017** protects the rights of people living with or affected by HIV – it prohibits discrimination in employment, health care, education and housing, and requires informed consent and confidentiality for testing and treatment.
- The National AIDS Control Programme provides prevention, testing, counselling and free antiretroviral treatment.
- Nursing role: universal precautions, non-judgemental care, confidentiality, counselling, prevention of parent-to-child transmission, fighting stigma.

### COVID-19

The COVID-19 pandemic was a health crisis that became a **social problem**:

- loss of jobs and income, especially for daily-wage and migrant workers
- mass return migration and food insecurity
- school closures, digital divide, learning loss, rise in child labour and child marriage risk
- increase in domestic violence, anxiety, depression and loneliness
- stigma against patients and health workers
- disruption of routine health services (immunization, TB, maternal care)

Responses included free food grain distribution, relief packages, tele-consultation, vaccination drives, and the work of health workers including nurses on the front line.

## 7.3 Vulnerable groups

A **vulnerable group** is a group that is at higher risk of poverty, exclusion, ill health and exploitation because of age, disability, gender, caste, ethnicity or other circumstances, and has less power to protect itself.

| Group | Main problems | Protection and support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Elderly** | Chronic disease, disability, loneliness, neglect and abuse, financial dependence, breakdown of joint families | Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007; old-age pensions under the National Social Assistance Programme; Atal Vayo Abhyuday Yojana; geriatric care under national health programmes |
| **Persons with disabilities (handicapped)** | Stigma, inaccessible buildings and transport, unemployment, lack of education | **Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016** (gives effect to the UN Convention; equality, accessibility, reservations in education and jobs); disability certificates and aids |
| **Minorities** (religious and linguistic) | Discrimination, fear of insecurity, poverty in some groups | Articles 29 and 30 of the Constitution (protection of culture, right to run educational institutions); welfare schemes |
| **Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes** | Historical discrimination, poverty, isolation, low literacy | Constitutional safeguards, reservations, special laws against atrocities, tribal welfare schemes |
| **Women** | Gender discrimination, violence, low status, maternal ill health | Mission Shakti, One Stop Centres, laws on dowry and domestic violence |
| **Children** | Malnutrition, abuse, labour, trafficking | Mission Vatsalya, Juvenile Justice Act, POCSO Act |
| **Transgender persons** | Exclusion, discrimination, unemployment, violence | **Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019**; SMILE scheme (shelter homes – Garima Greh, skill training, health support) |
| **Other marginal groups** | Migrants, homeless, persons engaged in begging, people with mental illness, people living with HIV, sex workers | SMILE scheme (begging rehabilitation); Mental Healthcare Act, 2017; HIV and AIDS Act, 2017 |

## 7.4 Fundamental rights of individuals, women and children

The Constitution of India (Part III) gives every person Fundamental Rights, enforceable in courts.

| Right | Articles | Meaning in brief |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Right to equality | 14–18 | Equality before law (14); no discrimination on religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth (15); equal opportunity in public employment (16); abolition of untouchability (17); abolition of titles (18) |
| Right to freedom | 19–22 | Six freedoms of speech, assembly, association, movement, residence and profession (19); protection in conviction (20); life and personal liberty (21) – courts have read health, dignity and a clean environment into it; education for children 6–14 (21A); safeguards on arrest (22) |
| Right against exploitation | 23–24 | Ban on trafficking in human beings and forced labour (23); no child below 14 in factories, mines or hazardous work (24) |
| Right to freedom of religion | 25–28 | Freedom of conscience and religion |
| Cultural and educational rights | 29–30 | Protection of minorities' culture and institutions |
| Right to constitutional remedies | 32 | Right to approach the Supreme Court for enforcing rights (writs) |

**Rights especially for women**

- Article 14 and 15 – equality and no discrimination on grounds of sex.
- Article 15(3) – the state may make **special provisions for women and children**.
- Article 16 – equal opportunity in public jobs.
- Article 23 – protection against trafficking.
- Directive Principles: equal pay for equal work, and just and humane conditions of work and **maternity relief** (Article 42).
- Laws: Dowry Prohibition Act, Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, laws against sexual harassment at the workplace, PCPNDT Act against sex selection.

**Rights especially for children**

- Article 21A – free and compulsory education for children aged 6–14 years.
- Article 24 – no child below 14 in hazardous employment.
- Article 15(3) – special provisions for children.
- Directive Principles – protection of children from exploitation and opportunities for healthy development; early childhood care and education for children below 6 (Article 45).
- Fundamental Duty (Article 51A(k)) – parents and guardians must provide opportunities for education to their child aged 6–14.
- India is a party to the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (rights to survival, development, protection and participation).

## 7.5 Role of the nurse in reducing social problems and enhancing coping

1. **Identification** – during home visits, clinics and admissions, spot signs of poverty, malnutrition, abuse, addiction, neglect of elders and violence.
2. **Health education** – teach about nutrition, hygiene, harms of tobacco and alcohol, legal age of marriage, HIV prevention, rights and schemes.
3. **Counselling and support** – listen without judging, help families solve problems and build coping skills (problem solving, relaxation, social support, positive thinking).
4. **Referral and linkage** – connect people to social workers, One Stop Centres, Child Welfare Committees, de-addiction and mental health services, legal aid, NGOs and government schemes.
5. **Advocacy** – speak for patients' rights, confidentiality and dignity; fight stigma against HIV, leprosy, mental illness and disability.
6. **Care and rehabilitation** – care for victims of violence and accidents, help disabled and elderly people regain independence.
7. **Legal duties** – document medico-legal cases accurately, report as the law requires (for example POCSO), preserve evidence.
8. **Community participation** – work with ASHAs, anganwadi workers, self-help groups, panchayats and NGOs.
9. **Research** – study the size and causes of social problems in the community.
10. **Role model** – practise and promote non-discrimination and healthy living.

**Enhancing coping**: coping is the effort a person makes to manage stress. The nurse supports healthy coping by giving information, encouraging expression of feelings, mobilising family and peer support, teaching relaxation and practical problem solving, and discouraging harmful coping such as alcohol or withdrawal.

## 7.6 Social welfare programmes in India

**Social welfare** means organised services to help people, especially the weak and needy, meet their basic needs and improve their quality of life.

| Area | Programmes (names as on official sites) |
| --- | --- |
| Women | **Mission Shakti** – sub-schemes *Sambal* (safety and security: One Stop Centres, also called Sakhi Centres; Women Helpline 181; Beti Bachao Beti Padhao) and *Samarthya* (empowerment) |
| Children | **Mission Vatsalya** (child protection services, institutional and community care); **Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0** (nutrition, early childhood care and education through anganwadis; merges the earlier Anganwadi Services, POSHAN Abhiyaan and the Scheme for Adolescent Girls); PM POSHAN (school meals) |
| Elderly | National Social Assistance Programme (old-age, widow and disability pensions); Atal Vayo Abhyuday Yojana |
| Persons with disabilities | Schemes under the Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities (aids and appliances, scholarships) |
| Marginalised | **SMILE** – Support for Marginalized Individuals for Livelihood and Enterprise (transgender persons and persons engaged in begging) |
| Poor and rural families | MGNREGA (wage employment), National Food Security Act (subsidised grains), housing (PMAY), Ayushman Bharat – PM-JAY (health insurance for poor families) |
| Health-related social programmes | National AIDS Control Programme, Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan, national programmes for the elderly and for mental health |

## Remember

- Social problem = harmful condition affecting many, with social causes, needing collective action.
- Laws: ITPA 1956, Dowry Prohibition 1961, Child and Adolescent Labour 1986, NDPS 1985, RTE 2009, POCSO 2012, JJ 2015, RPWD 2016, HIV and AIDS Act 2017, Mental Healthcare Act 2017, Transgender Persons Act 2019, Senior Citizens Act 2007; BNS 2023 s. 80 dowry death.
- Fundamental Rights: equality 14–18, freedom 19–22 (21A education), exploitation 23–24, religion 25–28, culture 29–30, remedies 32.
- Women: Art 15(3), 42; children: Art 21A, 24, 45, 51A(k).
- MWCD umbrella missions: Shakti, Vatsalya, Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0.
- Nurse: identify, educate, counsel, refer, advocate, rehabilitate, report.

# How to use this subject

Applied Sociology is a theory course of the first semester (3 credits, 60 hours). It is mostly definitions, characteristics, types, differences and the role of the nurse, so it rewards neat, well-structured answers. Study one notes file a day: read it once, close it, write the definitions and the "difference between" tables from memory, then answer the question bank for that unit.

## How the notes follow the INC syllabus

The INC course has seven units. Some are tiny and some are very large, so they have been arranged into eight notes files:

| Notes file | INC unit(s) | Hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Introduction, basic concepts and social groups | Unit I (Introduction) joined with the first part of Unit II (society, community, association, institution, individual and society, personal disorganization, social groups) | 1 + part of 15 |
| 2 Social processes, socialization, social change and communities | Rest of Unit II (social processes, socialization, social change, urban/rural/tribal communities, importance of social structure) | part of 15 |
| 3 Culture | Unit III | 8 |
| 4 Family and marriage | Unit IV | 8 |
| 5 Social stratification | Unit V | 8 |
| 6 Social organization, social control and disorganization | First part of Unit VI | part of 15 |
| 7 Social problems, vulnerable groups, rights and welfare | Second part of Unit VI | part of 15 |
| 8 Clinical sociology | Unit VII | 5 |

Units II and VI carry 15 hours each, so expect the most questions from notes files 1, 2, 6 and 7.

## Exam pattern

Applied Sociology is **Section A (37 marks)** of a combined 3-hour university paper of 75 marks. **Section B (38 marks) is Applied Psychology.** Use about half the paper time (around 90 minutes) for Sociology.

| Part of Section A | Marks |
| --- | --- |
| MCQ | 6 × 1 = 6 |
| Essay (long answer) | 1 × 10 = 10 |
| Short answer | 3 × 5 = 15 |
| Very short answer | 3 × 2 = 6 |
| **Total** | **37** |

The INC pattern (Appendix 3) has no internal choice: answer every question. Internal assessment (continuous assessment and sessional exams) is added to the university marks as your university directs. Confirm the exact pattern and any choice with your university.

The mock tests in this subject:

- **Section A: Applied Sociology (37 marks, 90 minutes)** – the Sociology section of the combined paper.
- **Quick MCQ test** – 20 questions in 20 minutes.

## Probability ratings

- **High** – asked again and again in most university papers; prepare a complete answer with definition, points, a table and the nursing role.
- **Medium** – asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** – less common; useful for MCQs and very short answers.

These ratings are estimates from syllabus weight and common question patterns, not a promise of what will be asked.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. **Begin every answer with a definition**, and name the sociologist where you can (Comte, Tylor, MacIver, Cooley, Ghurye, Sorokin, Caplan).
2. **Use tables for "difference between" questions** – primary vs secondary group, community vs association, culture vs civilization, caste vs class, rural vs urban, competition vs conflict, accommodation vs assimilation. Six to eight rows earn full marks.
3. **Write characteristics as numbered points with a heading word** ("1. Universality – …").
4. **Always end with "Role of the nurse"** or "Application in nursing" – examiners in a nursing paper look for this link, even when the question does not ask for it directly.
5. **Give Indian examples** – joint family, caste, Sanskritization, dowry, tribal communities, national schemes.
6. **For legislation questions**, write the name and year of the Act and two or three main points. Do not invent section numbers or figures.
7. **Draw a simple diagram** where it helps – classification of social processes, forms of marriage, agencies of socialization, phases of crisis.
8. **For the 10-mark essay**, plan a structure: introduction and definition → characteristics or types → causes or factors → effects on health → role of the nurse → conclusion.
9. Very short answers (2 marks): one-line definition plus two points or one example.
10. Keep Sociology to about 90 minutes so that Psychology (Section B) gets its fair share of time.

# Unit 4: Family and marriage

This notes file covers INC Unit IV (Family and Marriage, 8 hours): characteristics, basic needs, types and functions of the family; forms of marriage, social customs and importance of marriage; legislation on Indian marriage and family; and the influence of marriage and family on health and health practices.

## 4.1 Family

The **family** is the basic and universal social group. Burgess and Locke describe it as a group of persons united by ties of marriage, blood or adoption, forming a single household, interacting with each other in their social roles and creating a common culture. MacIver and Page describe it as a group defined by a sex relationship sufficiently precise and enduring to provide for the procreation and upbringing of children.

Simple definition: **a family is a group of people related by marriage, blood or adoption, usually living together, who share economic resources and care for one another and the children.**

## 4.2 Characteristics of the family

1. **Universality** – found in every society and age.
2. **Emotional basis** – love, affection, sympathy, cooperation.
3. **Limited size** – smaller than other groups.
4. **Nuclear position in social structure** – the unit on which other groups are built.
5. **Formative influence** – shapes the child's personality and habits.
6. **Responsibility of members** – members take responsibility for each other's welfare.
7. **Social regulation** – customs and laws regulate it (marriage rules, inheritance).
8. **Permanent and temporary nature** – the family as an institution is permanent; each particular family changes and ends.
9. **A common habitation** – usually a shared home.
10. **Economic provision** – a common budget and sharing of earnings.

## 4.3 Basic needs met by the family

Using Maslow's hierarchy as a guide, a healthy family meets:

| Need | How the family meets it |
| --- | --- |
| Physiological | Food, water, shelter, clothing, rest, regulated sexual needs |
| Safety and security | Protection of children, the sick and the elderly; financial security |
| Love and belonging | Affection, companionship, emotional support |
| Esteem | Respect, recognition, a name and status in society |
| Self-actualization | Education, support to develop talents and careers |

It also meets **health needs** – home care of the sick, nutrition, hygiene and support during pregnancy, childbirth and old age.

## 4.4 Types of family

| Basis | Types | Explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structure (size) | **Nuclear** | Husband, wife and their unmarried children |
| | **Joint** | Two or more generations living together with common property, kitchen and worship, under the eldest male (Karta) |
| | **Extended** | Nuclear family plus other relatives (grandparents, uncles, aunts) |
| Marriage | Monogamous, polygynous, polyandrous | One spouse, several wives, several husbands |
| Descent | **Patrilineal** | Descent and property through the father (most Indian families) |
| | **Matrilineal** | Descent and property through the mother (traditionally the Nairs of Kerala, the Khasi and Garo of Meghalaya) |
| Residence | Patrilocal | Wife lives in the husband's home |
| | Matrilocal | Husband lives in the wife's home |
| | Neolocal | Couple set up a new home |
| Authority | Patriarchal / matriarchal | Authority with the eldest male / female |
| Relationship to the person | Family of **orientation** | The family one is born into |
| | Family of **procreation** | The family one creates by marriage |
| Newer forms | Single-parent, blended (step) family, childless couple, live-in partners, elderly living alone | Growing in cities |

### Joint family – merits and demerits

| Merits | Demerits |
| --- | --- |
| Security for the old, sick, widowed and disabled | Lack of privacy, quarrels |
| Shared work and resources; economies of scale | Domination by the head; women and young people have little say |
| Child care by grandparents | Some members may become idle and dependent |
| Preserves culture and values | Slows individual initiative |
| Support in illness and crisis | Overcrowding can spread infections |

**Changes in the Indian family**: joint families are breaking into nuclear ones because of industrialization, urbanization, migration for jobs, education, women's employment, new laws (property rights of daughters) and individualism. The results include more freedom and equality, but also less support for the elderly, working mothers needing child care, and loneliness.

## 4.5 Functions of the family

MacIver divided functions into **essential** and **non-essential**.

**Essential functions**

1. Stable satisfaction of the sex need
2. Production and rearing of children (reproduction)
3. Provision of a home

**Non-essential (other) functions**

| Function | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Economic | Earning, saving, property, division of labour |
| Educational and socialization | First school of the child |
| Religious | Daily worship, rituals, festivals |
| Recreational | Family outings, games, festivals |
| Protective | Care of children, old, sick, disabled |
| Affectional | Love and emotional security |
| Health | Nutrition, hygiene, home nursing, decisions about treatment |
| Status giving | Name, caste, social position |

Many of these are now shared with outside agencies – schools, hospitals, crèches, insurance, old-age homes – but the family remains the main source of emotional support.

## 4.6 Marriage

**Marriage** is a socially approved union of a man and woman that regulates sexual relations, gives legitimacy to children and creates rights and duties between spouses and families. Westermarck described it as a relation of one or more men to one or more women recognised by custom or law, involving certain rights and duties.

Characteristics: universal; a relationship between man and woman approved by society; regulated by custom or law; creates mutual rights and obligations; usually accompanied by a ceremony; aims at family life and children.

### Forms of marriage

```text
    Monogamy               Polygamy            Group marriage

  by Indian law for most)      |               rare, historical)
                   +-----------+-----------+
                   |                       |

         one man, several wives     one woman, several husbands
                                    - fraternal (husbands are brothers,
                                      e.g. traditionally the Todas)
                                    - non-fraternal
```

**Rules about whom one may marry**

- **Endogamy** – marrying within one's own group (caste, sub-caste, religion, tribe).
- **Exogamy** – marrying outside a group (outside one's gotra or clan; not within prohibited degrees of relationship).
- **Hypergamy (anuloma)** – a woman marries a man of higher caste or status.
- **Hypogamy (pratiloma)** – a woman marries a man of lower caste or status; traditionally disapproved.
- **Cross-cousin marriage** – marriage with mother's brother's or father's sister's child; accepted in parts of South India.
- **Levirate** – a widow marries her dead husband's brother; **sororate** – a widower marries his dead wife's sister.

**Traditional Hindu forms** described in the Dharmashastras: Brahma, Daiva, Arsha, Prajapatya (approved), and Asura, Gandharva, Rakshasa, Paisacha (disapproved). Hindu marriage was seen as a sacrament (**samskara**) – religious, lifelong and indissoluble – though law now allows divorce.

**Muslim marriage (nikah)** is a civil contract: offer and acceptance in the presence of witnesses, consent of both parties, and **mahr** (a sum due to the wife). After divorce or the husband's death the wife observes a waiting period (iddat).

**Ways of acquiring a mate in some tribal societies**: by probation (living together before marriage), by capture, by trial (a test of strength), by purchase (bride price), by service (working for the bride's family), by exchange (sisters exchanged between two families), by mutual consent and elopement, and by intrusion.

### Social customs relating to marriage in India

- arranged marriage decided by elders, with horoscope matching in many communities
- engagement, wedding rituals (saptapadi, kanyadan, nikah, church ceremony)
- dowry (a social evil, banned by law) and bride price in some tribes
- caste and gotra rules, child marriage (illegal), widow remarriage (once restricted, now legal and more accepted)
- inter-caste and love marriages increasing in cities

### Importance of marriage

1. Regulates sexual behaviour and reduces promiscuity
2. Gives legal and social status to children
3. Forms the family and continues society
4. Provides companionship, love and emotional security
5. Creates economic cooperation and division of labour
6. Links two families and builds kinship
7. Passes on culture and property

## 4.7 Legislation on Indian marriage and family

Learn the name, year and one or two main points of each Act. Check the current text on India Code (indiacode.nic.in) if you need sections.

| Act | Main points |
| --- | --- |
| **Special Marriage Act, 1954** | Civil marriage for any two persons in India irrespective of religion, before a Marriage Officer after notice of intended marriage; also provides registration and divorce |
| **Hindu Marriage Act, 1955** | Applies to Hindus (including Buddhists, Jains, Sikhs). Conditions include monogamy, mental capacity, minimum age, not within prohibited degrees or sapinda relationship; registration; void and voidable marriages; judicial separation and divorce, including divorce by mutual consent |
| **Hindu Succession Act, 1956** | Inheritance among Hindus; daughters now have the same coparcenary rights as sons in joint family property |
| **Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956** | Rules of adoption; an adopted child becomes the child of the adoptive parents; duty to maintain wife, children and aged or infirm parents |
| **Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961** | Giving, taking or demanding dowry is an offence; dowry agreements are void; any dowry received must be held for the benefit of the woman |
| **Family Courts Act, 1984** | Family Courts to promote conciliation and speedy settlement of marriage and family disputes |
| **Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005** | Covers physical, sexual, verbal and emotional, and economic abuse within the family; Protection Officers; protection orders; the woman's right to live in the shared household |
| **Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006** | A child is a male under 21 years or a female under 18 years; a child marriage is voidable at the option of the child party; punishment for an adult male who marries a child and for those who perform or promote it; Child Marriage Prohibition Officers |
| **Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007** | Children and relatives must maintain parents and senior citizens who cannot support themselves; claims decided by a Maintenance Tribunal |
| **Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act, 2019** | Declaration of talaq by a husband in the instant form (triple talaq) is void and illegal and a punishable offence; the wife is entitled to a subsistence allowance and custody of minor children |
| **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023** (replaced the Indian Penal Code from July 2024) | Section 80: **dowry death** – death of a woman by burns, injury or in abnormal circumstances within seven years of marriage after cruelty or harassment linked to dowry; Sections 85–86: cruelty by husband or his relatives |

Other personal laws govern marriage and divorce among Muslims, Christians and Parsis. The **Pre-Conception and Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques (PCPNDT) Act** bans sex selection, which is linked to son preference in families.

## 4.8 Influence of marriage and family on health and health practices

**Positive influences**

- Family is the first source of health habits – diet, hygiene, sleep, exercise.
- Married people with good family support generally cope better with illness.
- The family nurses the sick at home, gives medicines, takes them for follow-up.
- Planned, mature marriages allow spacing of births and better maternal and child health.
- Joint families support new mothers and the elderly.

**Negative influences**

| Problem | Health effect |
| --- | --- |
| Child (early) marriage | Early pregnancy, anaemia, obstructed labour, low birth weight, maternal and infant deaths, school dropout |
| Consanguineous marriage | Higher risk of inherited disorders such as thalassaemia and other recessive disease |
| Large families, poor spacing | Malnutrition, maternal depletion, poverty |
| Dowry and domestic violence | Injuries, depression, suicide, dowry deaths |
| Son preference | Neglect of girl children, sex-selective practices, skewed sex ratio |
| Broken families, divorce, desertion | Emotional problems in children, delinquency |
| Family taboos and beliefs | Delay in seeking care, harmful feeding practices |
| Polygamy or multiple partners | Risk of sexually transmitted infections, HIV |

**Role of the nurse**: premarital counselling (age at marriage, genetic counselling, HIV testing), family planning advice, identifying domestic violence and child marriage and referring to Protection Officers, One Stop Centres or police, involving the family in care plans, and supporting families through crises.

## Remember

- Family = group united by marriage, blood or adoption sharing a home and a common culture.
- Types: nuclear, joint, extended; patrilineal and matrilineal (Nair, Khasi, Garo); patrilocal, matrilocal, neolocal.
- MacIver's essential functions: sex need, reproduction and child rearing, a home.
- Marriage forms: monogamy, polygyny, polyandry (fraternal – Todas), group marriage; endogamy, exogamy, hypergamy, hypogamy.
- Child under the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006: male under 21, female under 18.
- Dowry Prohibition Act 1961; dowry death within 7 years of marriage (BNS 2023, s. 80); PWDVA 2005.
- Early marriage and consanguinity harm health; family support improves it.

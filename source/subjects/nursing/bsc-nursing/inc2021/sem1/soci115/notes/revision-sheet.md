## One-line definitions

- **Sociology** – scientific study of society, social relationships, groups and institutions.
- **Society** – web of social relationships.
- **Community** – people in a definite locality with a common life and we-feeling.
- **Association** – group organised for a specific purpose.
- **Institution** – established procedure for meeting a social need (marriage).
- **Social group** – two or more people who interact and share a sense of unity.
- **Socialization** – lifelong learning of culture, norms and roles.
- **Social change** – change in social structure and relationships over time.
- **Culture** – learned, shared, transmitted way of life.
- **Family** – group united by marriage, blood or adoption, sharing a home.
- **Marriage** – socially approved union regulating sex and legitimising children.
- **Social stratification** – ranking of people in layers with unequal rewards.
- **Caste** – hereditary, endogamous group with fixed rank.
- **Class** – group with similar wealth, occupation and education; open.
- **Social mobility** – movement from one social position to another.
- **Social control** – means by which society makes members conform.
- **Social disorganization** – breakdown of harmony and relationships in society.
- **Social problem** – harmful condition affecting many, needing collective action.
- **Clinical sociology** – applied sociology that assesses and changes social situations.
- **Crisis** – acute upset when usual coping fails; time-limited.

## Must-know lists

- **Nature of sociology**: independent, social, categorical, pure, abstract, generalizing, rational and empirical.
- **Social processes**: associative – cooperation, accommodation, assimilation; dissociative – competition, conflict; plus isolation.
- **Agencies of socialization**: family (first), peers, school, religion, community, media, workplace, state.
- **Characteristics of culture**: learned, shared, transmitted, social, adaptive, dynamic, integrated, varies.
- **Leininger**: preservation, accommodation, repatterning.
- **Types of family**: nuclear, joint, extended; patrilineal, matrilineal; patrilocal, matrilocal, neolocal.
- **MacIver's essential functions of family**: sex need, reproduction and child rearing, home.
- **Forms of marriage**: monogamy, polygyny, polyandry (fraternal, non-fraternal), group marriage.
- **Ghurye's caste features**: segmental division, hierarchy, feeding restrictions, disabilities and privileges, hereditary occupation, endogamy.
- **Mobility**: horizontal, vertical (up, down), intergenerational, intragenerational.
- **Race criteria**: skin, hair, cephalic index, nasal index, stature, eyes, blood groups.
- **Social control**: formal (law, police, courts) and informal (family, religion, customs, public opinion).
- **Norms**: folkways (mild) → mores (strong) → laws (formal).
- **Sick role (Parsons)**: excused from duties; not blamed; must want to recover; must seek help.
- **Crisis types**: maturational, situational, adventitious.
- **Crisis steps**: assessment → planning → intervention → resolution → anticipatory planning.

# How to use this guide

Communicative English is a first-semester course of the INC 2021 B.Sc Nursing curriculum (2 credits, 40 hours of theory). It is about using English to do a nurse's work: listening to patients and colleagues, speaking on the ward and on the telephone, reading charts and orders, and writing notes, letters and reports.

**It is a college exam, not a university exam.** INC lists Communicative English among the courses examined by the college itself; the result is sent to the university for the mark sheet. The INC table gives 25 internal marks and 25 end-semester college exam marks (total 50); the college paper is usually set for 50 marks over 2 hours and then counted as 25. **The minimum pass for English is 40%** — lower than the 50% needed in other theory courses — but aim far higher, because this subject is easy to score in with practice.

**How the units are arranged.** One notes file for each of the seven INC units:

| Notes file | INC unit | Hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit 1 Communication and the roles of the four skills | I | 3 |
| Unit 2 Introduction to LSRGW: sounds, stress, intonation, vocabulary, tenses, linkers | II | 5 |
| Unit 3 Attentive listening | III | 5 |
| Unit 4 Speaking: conversation, telephone, handover, presentations | IV | 9 |
| Unit 5 Reading: strategies, abbreviations, visuals, reports, remedial grammar | V | 5 |
| Unit 6 Writing skills: history, notes, letters, reports, journals, abstract, CV | VI | 5 |
| Unit 7 LSRW in combination: critical thinking, instructions, error analysis | VII | 8 |

Language points named in the course competencies — paraphrasing, voice modulation, spelling, pause and silence — are covered in Unit 2.

## Exam pattern used for the mock paper

INC does not print a separate pattern for college exams, so the mock follows the 50-mark pattern of INC Appendix 3 with no internal choice. Your college may set it differently; confirm with your college.

| Part | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MCQ | 8 multiple-choice questions | 8 × 1 = 8 |
| Essay | 1 long answer (often a letter, report or situation) | 1 × 10 = 10 |
| Short | 4 short answers | 4 × 5 = 20 |
| Very short | 6 very short answers | 6 × 2 = 12 |
| **Total** | 2 hours | **50** |

Internal assessment (25 marks) comes from attendance, written assignments, presentations and two sessional exams. Many teachers also test speaking live (role play, handover, presentation), so practise aloud.

## Probability ratings

- **High** — asked in most papers in some form (letters, reports, SBAR handover, barriers to communication, tenses, active listening, telephone etiquette). Learn a full model answer.
- **Medium** — appears every few papers or inside a bigger question.
- **Low** — less common; useful for MCQs and 2-mark answers.

These are estimates from the syllabus hours and the usual style of first-year English papers in nursing colleges. They are not a promise of what will be asked.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. **Format carries marks.** In letters, write every part: sender's address, date, receiver, subject, salutation, body in paragraphs, closing, signature and name. In reports, use headings. Examiners give marks for format even before reading the content.
2. **Use the situation given.** If the question names a ward, a patient or a date, use them. Invent sensible details (names, times) where none are given, but keep them realistic.
3. **Write in clear, correct, simple sentences.** One grammar mistake per line costs more than a short answer. Re-read every answer for tense, subject–verb agreement and spelling.
4. **For definitions, give definition + one example.** For 2-mark questions that is enough.
5. **Use tables for "differentiate"** (formal vs informal letter, hearing vs listening, skimming vs scanning, fact vs opinion).
6. **Learn the acronyms**: 7 Cs, SOLER, SBAR/ISBAR, SQ3R, SOAPIE, DAR, IMRaD, Gibbs' six stages, LSRW.
7. **Write model dialogues** in conversation questions — label speakers ("Nurse:", "Patient:") and show politeness phrases.
8. **Practise aloud.** Pronunciation, stress and intonation are learnt by speaking, not by reading. Record yourself on your phone and listen back.
9. **Keep a personal error log** (Unit 7) through the semester and revise it before the exam.

**Daily plan:** read one notes file, say its "Remember" list aloud, write one long and two short answers from the question bank without looking, then do the MCQs. In the last week, write one letter and one report every day and take the mock paper under time.

## 4.1 Meaning and definition of personality

The word comes from the Latin *persona*, the mask an actor wore on stage to show the character he played. In everyday speech "personality" means charm or appearance; in psychology it means much more.

**Definition (in plain words):** personality is the unique and fairly stable pattern of thinking, feeling and behaving that makes a person different from others and shapes how he or she adjusts to the environment. Gordon Allport described it as the dynamic organisation within the individual of the psychophysical systems that determine his unique adjustment to the environment — learn the key words: *dynamic*, *organisation*, *psychophysical*, *unique*, *adjustment*.

### Characteristics of personality

- **Unique:** no two people are exactly alike.
- **Consistent:** a person behaves in a similar way in similar situations.
- **Dynamic:** it grows and changes with experience.
- **Integrated:** body, mind and behaviour work as a whole.
- **Both inherited and learned.**
- **Goal-directed and adjustive.**
- **Measurable** to some extent through tests.

### Determinants of personality

| Factor | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Biological | Heredity, physique, nervous system, endocrine glands (thyroid, adrenal), health |
| Psychological | Intelligence, emotions, motives, self-concept |
| Family | Parenting style, birth order, relationships, home atmosphere |
| Social | School, peers, community, mass media |
| Cultural | Values, religion, customs, gender roles |
| Life experiences | Success, failure, trauma, illness |

## 4.2 Structure of personality (Freud)

```text
         Conscious       ┐
   ───── Preconscious ── ├─ levels of mind ("iceberg")
         Unconscious     ┘  (largest part, hidden)

   ID      – inborn drives, pleasure principle, "I want it now"
   EGO     – reality principle, balances id and superego, "let's be realistic"
   SUPEREGO– conscience and ideals, "you must not"
```

A healthy personality has a strong ego that satisfies needs in socially acceptable ways. When the ego cannot balance the demands, anxiety results and defence mechanisms are used (Unit 2).

## 4.3 Classification of personality

### Type approaches (place people into categories)

| Classifier | Types | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hippocrates (based on body fluids) | Sanguine, choleric, melancholic, phlegmatic | Cheerful and active; quick-tempered; sad and gloomy; calm and slow |
| Kretschmer (body build) | Pyknic, athletic, asthenic (leptosomic), dysplastic | Short and round – sociable, moody; muscular – energetic; thin and tall – shy, reserved; mixed |
| Sheldon (somatotypes) | Endomorph, mesomorph, ectomorph | Soft, round – relaxed, loves comfort (viscerotonic); muscular – adventurous (somatotonic); thin – sensitive, private (cerebrotonic) |
| Carl Jung | Introvert, extrovert, ambivert | Inward-looking, reserved; outgoing, social; balance of both (most people) |
| Friedman and Rosenman | Type A, Type B | Competitive, impatient, hurried, hostile (linked to heart disease); relaxed, patient, easy-going |

**Limitation of type theories:** people rarely fit one box; they oversimplify.

### Trait approaches (describe how much of each quality a person has)

A **trait** is a lasting quality such as honesty, sociability or anxiety.

- **Allport:** cardinal traits (dominate a whole life, rare), central traits (5–10 main ones that describe a person), secondary traits (appear in some situations).
- **Cattell:** surface traits (visible) and source traits (underlying); 16 source traits measured by the 16PF questionnaire.
- **Eysenck:** three dimensions — extraversion–introversion, neuroticism–stability, psychoticism.
- **Big Five (five-factor model)** — remember **OCEAN**: Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism.

### Other approaches (name only if asked)

- **Psychoanalytic** (Freud, Jung, Adler): unconscious forces and early experience.
- **Humanistic** (Rogers, Maslow): self-concept, growth, self-actualisation.
- **Behavioural / social learning** (Skinner, Bandura): personality is learned behaviour, through reward and imitation.

## 4.4 Measurement and evaluation of personality – introduction

| Method | Description | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interview | Structured or unstructured conversation | Case history interview |
| Observation | Watch behaviour in natural or set situations | Behaviour in ward or play |
| Rating scales | Someone who knows the person rates traits on a scale | Teacher rates punctuality 1–5 |
| Personality inventories (questionnaires) | Many yes/no or agree/disagree items, scored against norms | MMPI, 16PF, Eysenck Personality Questionnaire |
| Projective techniques | Ambiguous stimuli; person "projects" inner feelings into the answer | Rorschach inkblot test, Thematic Apperception Test (TAT), sentence completion, word association, draw-a-person |
| Situational tests | Behaviour observed in a real-life-like task | Group discussion, stress interview |
| Biographical data / autobiography | Life history, diaries | — |

Projective tests need specially trained psychologists to score and interpret; nurses observe and report, they do not interpret these tests.

## 4.5 Alteration in personality

Personality can be altered in two broad ways.

**1. Personality disorders** — long-standing, rigid, unhelpful patterns of inner experience and behaviour that begin in adolescence or early adulthood and cause distress or problems in relationships and work. They are grouped into three clusters:

| Cluster | Description | Types |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A – odd, eccentric | Suspicious, detached, strange beliefs | Paranoid, schizoid, schizotypal |
| B – dramatic, emotional, erratic | Impulsive, attention-seeking, unstable | Antisocial, borderline, histrionic, narcissistic |
| C – anxious, fearful | Worried, dependent, perfectionist | Avoidant, dependent, obsessive-compulsive |

**2. Personality change** from another cause:

- Brain injury or tumour, especially of the frontal lobe (disinhibition, irritability, poor judgement).
- Dementia, stroke, epilepsy.
- Long-term alcohol or drug use.
- Severe mental illness (schizophrenia, severe depression).
- Hormonal disorders (hyperthyroidism — anxious, irritable; hypothyroidism — slow, dull).
- Severe trauma, prolonged stress.

## 4.6 Role of the nurse in identifying personality and improving altered personality

**Identification**

- Observe and record behaviour objectively: how the patient relates to staff, handles frustration, follows advice.
- Take a history from the patient and family: previous personality ("what was he like before?"), recent changes.
- Report sudden personality change at once — it may mean a medical cause (head injury, infection, metabolic problem).
- Help the team with rating scales and checklists as instructed.

**Care and improvement**

- Build a consistent, trusting relationship; same nurse where possible.
- Set clear, firm but kind limits for manipulative or aggressive behaviour; apply rules consistently across staff.
- Reinforce acceptable behaviour with praise.
- Teach social skills, anger management, problem-solving.
- Encourage self-awareness and responsibility for actions.
- Involve family; teach them to respond consistently.
- Treat underlying causes (medical, substance use) and refer to mental health services.
- Understand your own reactions: some personalities provoke staff; stay professional.
- Use personality knowledge in daily care: an introvert may need privacy and written information; an extrovert may prefer group activities; a Type A patient after heart attack needs help to slow down.

## 4.7 Psychological assessment and tests – introduction

**Psychological assessment** is the collection of information about a person's behaviour, abilities, personality and emotions, using interviews, observation, history and tests, to understand the person and help decisions.

A **psychological test** is a standardised, objective measure of a sample of behaviour. "Standardised" means the same instructions, conditions and scoring for everyone, and scores are compared with **norms** (average scores of a large similar group).

### Types of psychological tests

| Basis | Types |
| --- | --- |
| What is measured | Intelligence, aptitude, achievement, personality, interest, attitude, neuropsychological |
| Number tested | Individual tests (one at a time, e.g. Wechsler scales); group tests (many at once) |
| Form | Verbal (language used); non-verbal (pictures, symbols); performance (handling objects, puzzles, block designs) |
| Time | Speed tests (easy items, strict time); power tests (items get harder, more time) |
| Scoring | Objective (fixed key); subjective (examiner judgement, e.g. projective) |
| Standardisation | Standardised vs teacher-made / informal |

### Development of a test (steps)

```text
1. Decide the purpose and what will be measured
2. Write a large pool of items
3. Try out on a small group (pilot); item analysis – keep good items
4. Final form with fixed instructions, time and scoring key
5. Standardise on a large representative group → prepare norms
6. Check reliability and validity
7. Prepare the manual
```

### Characteristics of a good test

| Characteristic | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Reliability | Gives consistent results on repeated use (test–retest, split-half, parallel forms) |
| Validity | Measures what it claims to measure (content, criterion, construct validity) |
| Objectivity | Scores do not depend on who scores it |
| Standardisation | Uniform procedure for giving and scoring |
| Norms | Reference scores of a defined group for comparison |
| Practicability (usability) | Easy to give, score and interpret; reasonable cost and time |
| Discrimination | Separates high and low performers |
| Cultural fairness | Suitable for the person's language and background |

### Principles of psychological testing

1. Only **trained and qualified** persons give and interpret tests.
2. **Informed consent**; explain the purpose.
3. **Confidentiality** of results.
4. Good **rapport**; reduce anxiety, which lowers scores.
5. Suitable **environment**: quiet, well lit, no interruptions.
6. Follow **standard instructions** exactly.
7. Choose a test suited to age, language and culture.
8. A test result is **one piece of information** — never label a person on one score.

### Uses of psychological tests

- **Clinical:** diagnosis (intellectual disability, dementia, depression), planning and evaluating treatment.
- **Educational:** identify learning difficulty, giftedness, guidance.
- **Vocational:** selection and placement, career counselling.
- **Research.**
- **Legal:** fitness to stand trial, disability assessment.
- **Self-understanding.**

### Interpretation

- A **raw score** means little alone; it is converted to a standard form — percentile rank, standard score, IQ — and compared with norms.
- Consider the person's health, mood, motivation, education and culture at the time of testing.
- Interpretation is done by a psychologist; results are discussed with the team and explained to the person sensitively.

## 4.8 Role of the nurse in psychological assessment

- **Prepare the patient:** explain the purpose, reassure, see that they have slept and eaten, and have glasses or hearing aids.
- **Arrange the setting:** quiet room, privacy, time agreed with the psychologist.
- **Observe and record** behaviour on the ward — sleep, appetite, mood, interaction — which supplements test findings.
- **Use simple screening tools** the nurse is trained in, as per hospital policy (for example orientation questions, pain scales, mood or anxiety checklists), and report findings.
- **Maintain confidentiality** of results.
- **Support the patient** after testing, especially if results are worrying.
- **Use results in care planning** — for example simpler teaching for a patient with low cognitive scores.
- **Avoid labelling** or discussing results casually.

## Remember

- Personality = unique, stable, dynamic pattern of thinking, feeling, behaving; from Latin *persona*.
- Freud: id (pleasure), ego (reality), superego (conscience).
- Types: Hippocrates, Kretschmer, Sheldon, Jung, Type A/B. Traits: Allport, Cattell (16PF), Eysenck, Big Five (OCEAN).
- Measurement: interview, observation, rating scale, inventories (MMPI, 16PF), projective (Rorschach, TAT).
- Personality disorders: clusters A (odd), B (dramatic), C (anxious).
- Good test = reliable, valid, objective, standardised, normed, practicable.
- Nurse prepares, observes, records, keeps confidentiality; psychologist interprets.

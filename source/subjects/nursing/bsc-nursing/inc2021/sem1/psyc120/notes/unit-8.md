# Unit 8: Soft skills and self-empowerment

This notes file joins INC Unit IX (Application of soft skill, 10 hours) and INC Unit X (Self-empowerment, 2 hours). Both are about the nurse as a person: how she or he communicates, relates, manages time and stress, presents herself or himself, and grows in confidence and helps others grow.

## 8.1 Concept of soft skills

**Soft skills** are personal and interpersonal qualities — communication, attitude, teamwork, time management, emotional control, courtesy — that decide how well a person works with others. **Hard skills** are technical, job-specific abilities that can be measured (giving an injection, recording BP).

| Hard skills | Soft skills |
| --- | --- |
| Technical, task-based | Personal and interpersonal |
| Learned in classroom and skill lab | Learned through experience, reflection, practice |
| Easy to measure | Harder to measure |
| Example: catheterisation | Example: explaining the procedure kindly, gaining consent, keeping privacy |

Patients judge nursing care largely by soft skills: they may not know whether an injection was technically perfect, but they remember whether the nurse was kind, listened and explained.

### Core soft skills for nurses

Communication, empathy, active listening, teamwork, adaptability, problem-solving, critical thinking, time management, stress management, leadership, professional etiquette, cultural sensitivity, conflict resolution, positive attitude, integrity.

## 8.2 Types of soft skill: visual, aural and communication skills

The syllabus groups soft skills by the channel used.

| Type | Meaning | Examples in nursing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visual skills | Using what is seen — observation and visual presentation | Noticing facial pain, pallor, restlessness; using charts, pictures and demonstrations in teaching; neat appearance; body language |
| Aural skills | Using hearing — listening | Active listening to patient complaints, tone of voice, handover reports, listening for alarms and breath sounds |
| Communication skills | Exchanging information and feelings | Clear speech, written records, health teaching, telephone communication, reporting to doctors |

## 8.3 The way of communication

**Communication** is the process of sending and receiving messages — facts, ideas, feelings — so that they are understood.

```text
Sender → encodes Message → Channel (spoken, written, gesture)
      → Receiver decodes → Feedback → back to sender
      (Noise/barriers can distort at any step)
```

### Types (ways) of communication

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Verbal – oral | Talking, explaining, telephone |
| Verbal – written | Nursing notes, reports, leaflets, messages |
| Non-verbal | Facial expression, eye contact, posture, gestures, touch, distance (proxemics), tone of voice (paralanguage), appearance, silence |
| Formal / informal | Official reports vs friendly talk |
| Upward, downward, horizontal | To superiors, to juniors, to colleagues |
| One-way / two-way | Instructions vs discussion with feedback |

Non-verbal messages are powerful; when words and body language disagree, people believe the body language.

### Principles of effective communication (the "7 Cs")

Clear, concise, concrete, correct, coherent, complete and courteous.

### Therapeutic communication techniques

Active listening, open-ended questions ("How are you feeling today?"), restating, reflecting feelings, clarifying, using silence, giving information, summarising, offering self ("I will stay with you").

Avoid: false reassurance ("Don't worry, everything will be fine"), giving advice too quickly, judging, changing the subject, medical jargon, closed or leading questions only.

## 8.4 Building relationship with client and society

- Introduce yourself by name and role; address the patient respectfully.
- Respect privacy, dignity, culture and beliefs.
- Be honest and keep promises.
- Show empathy (understanding the patient's feelings) rather than sympathy (feeling sorry for them).
- Be consistent and reliable.
- Maintain professional boundaries — friendly but not a personal friend; no gifts or personal favours that compromise care.
- In the community: respect local leaders and customs, involve the community in health activities, be a role model of healthy living.

## 8.5 Interpersonal relationships (IPR)

### Definition

An **interpersonal relationship** is a strong, deep or close association between two or more people, based on interaction, mutual influence and some shared purpose or feeling.

### Types

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Family relationships | Parent–child, siblings, spouses |
| Friendship | Peers, classmates |
| Professional / work relationships | Nurse–doctor, nurse–nurse, supervisor–staff |
| Therapeutic relationship | Nurse–patient (purposeful, patient-centred, time-limited) |
| Social / community relationships | Neighbours, community groups |
| Romantic relationships | Partners |

### Purposes

Meeting the need to belong, emotional support, sharing information, cooperation to reach goals, personal growth, and — in nursing — helping the patient recover and cope.

### Phases of the nurse–patient relationship (Hildegard Peplau)

| Phase | Nurse's activity |
| --- | --- |
| Orientation | Meet, introduce, build trust, identify problems, agree on goals |
| Identification | Patient identifies with the nurse as a helper; expresses feelings |
| Exploitation (working) | Patient makes full use of services; nurse helps work on problems |
| Resolution (termination) | Goals met; relationship ends; independence encouraged |

### Interpersonal skills

Active listening, empathy, assertiveness, respect, trust-building, cooperation, conflict resolution, negotiation, giving and receiving feedback, emotional intelligence.

### Barriers to good interpersonal relationships and communication

| Category | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Physical | Noise, distance, poor lighting, interruptions, workload and time pressure |
| Physiological | Pain, hearing or speech problems, fatigue, sedation |
| Psychological | Anxiety, anger, prejudice, low self-esteem, poor listening |
| Language and semantic | Different language, medical jargon, unclear words |
| Cultural | Different norms on eye contact, touch, gender roles |
| Organisational | Hierarchy, rigid rules, poor team culture |
| Attitudinal | Superiority, judgemental attitude, lack of interest |

### Strategies to overcome barriers

- Reduce noise and interruptions; sit at eye level; ensure privacy.
- Use simple language; check understanding by asking the person to repeat back.
- Use an interpreter, pictures or demonstrations where language differs.
- Use aids for hearing or vision problems; write things down.
- Manage own emotions; stay calm and courteous.
- Respect cultural differences; ask rather than assume.
- Give and invite feedback.
- Build team culture: regular meetings, structured handover (for example SBAR — situation, background, assessment, recommendation).
- Resolve conflicts early by talking privately and focusing on the problem, not the person.

## 8.6 Survival strategies

### Managing time

- Set priorities: urgent and important first (ABC of airway, breathing, circulation; ordered treatments due now).
- Plan the shift: list tasks, group activities by patient or location.
- Avoid procrastination; break big tasks into small ones.
- Delegate appropriately and supervise.
- Limit time-wasters: unnecessary chatting, mobile phone use, poor organisation.
- Keep a diary or planner; review at the end of the day.

### Coping with stress

- Recognise early signs (irritability, poor sleep, headache).
- Healthy lifestyle: sleep, diet, exercise, avoiding tobacco and alcohol.
- Relaxation: deep breathing, progressive muscle relaxation, yoga, meditation, music.
- Talk to friends, family, mentors; use counselling services.
- Problem-solving for solvable stressors; acceptance for unsolvable ones.

### Resilience

**Resilience** is the ability to bounce back from adversity, trauma or stress and continue to function well. Built through supportive relationships, realistic optimism, flexible thinking, self-care, a sense of purpose, problem-solving skills and learning from setbacks.

### Work–life balance

A healthy balance between professional duties and personal life — family, rest, hobbies, health. In nursing (shift duty, emotional work) imbalance leads to **burnout**: emotional exhaustion, detachment from patients (depersonalisation) and a sense of reduced accomplishment. Strategies: plan off-duty time, keep boundaries, take breaks, share household tasks, make time for hobbies and exercise, ask for support.

## 8.7 Applying soft skills to workplace and society

| Skill | Key points |
| --- | --- |
| Presentation skills | Know the audience; clear objectives; logical order (introduction, body, summary); simple visual aids; eye contact; clear voice and pace; rehearse; invite questions |
| Social etiquette | Greet politely, punctuality, respect others' time and space, courteous language, thank people, appropriate dress, respectful use of mobile phones |
| Telephone etiquette | Answer promptly; greet, give name, ward and designation; speak clearly; listen and note details; repeat back orders and confirm (as per hospital policy); keep confidentiality — do not give patient information to unknown callers; end politely |
| Motivational skills | Recognise others' efforts, give positive feedback, set clear goals, lead by example, involve people in decisions |
| Teamwork | Shared goals, clear roles, mutual respect, open communication, helping each other, accepting responsibility for team outcomes |
| Leadership and decision-making | Take initiative, guide juniors, make fair decisions |
| Conflict management | Listen to both sides, focus on the problem, find a win–win solution |

## 8.8 Use of soft skills in nursing

- Admission: warm welcome reduces anxiety.
- Procedures: explanation and consent, privacy, gentle handling.
- Health teaching: simple words, demonstration, teach-back.
- Handover and reporting: clear, accurate, structured.
- Breaking bad news (with the team): calm, private, honest, compassionate.
- Difficult situations: angry relatives, aggressive patients — stay calm, listen, de-escalate.
- Team functioning: cooperation with doctors, colleagues, support staff.
- Community work: building trust for vaccination, screening and health programmes.
- Career growth: interviews, presentations, leadership roles.

## 8.9 Self-empowerment

**Empowerment** is the process by which people gain control over decisions and actions that affect their lives. **Self-empowerment** is taking charge of one's own life: recognising one's abilities, setting goals, making decisions and taking responsibility for them.

### Dimensions of self-empowerment

| Dimension | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Psychological | Self-confidence, self-esteem, positive self-image, sense of control |
| Intellectual / educational | Knowledge and skills; continuing education |
| Social | Supportive relationships; participation in groups |
| Economic | Financial independence and security |
| Physical | Health, fitness, safety |
| Political / legal | Awareness of rights and the ability to use them |
| Spiritual | Values, meaning and purpose in life |
| Professional | Competence, autonomy and voice in the workplace |

### Self-empowerment development

1. **Self-awareness:** know your strengths, weaknesses, values (SWOT analysis of self).
2. **Positive self-talk** and challenging negative beliefs.
3. **Goal setting:** SMART goals — specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, time-bound.
4. **Continuous learning:** skills, reading, training programmes.
5. **Assertiveness:** expressing needs and opinions honestly and respectfully; saying "no" when needed.
6. **Decision-making and accepting responsibility.**
7. **Health and self-care.**
8. **Building a support network and mentors.**
9. **Taking small risks** and learning from failures.

### Importance of women's empowerment in society

Most nurses in India are women, and many patients are women; nurses therefore both experience and promote women's empowerment.

- Empowered women make better decisions about their own and their children's health, nutrition, family planning and education.
- Women's education and income are linked with lower maternal and child deaths and smaller, healthier families.
- Empowerment reduces vulnerability to domestic violence, exploitation and discrimination.
- It increases women's participation in economic and political life, benefiting the whole society.
- Government schemes and laws support women's education, health, safety and work; nurses should know the current schemes of their state and refer women to them (check current details with official sources).

### Professional etiquette and personal grooming

**Professional etiquette:**

- Punctuality and reliability.
- Respectful behaviour towards patients, relatives, seniors and juniors.
- Confidentiality of patient information, including on social media.
- Courteous language; no gossip.
- Accepting responsibility and admitting mistakes.
- Following hospital policies and the professional code of conduct.
- Appropriate use of mobile phones on duty.

**Personal grooming:**

- Clean, ironed uniform as per institution; name badge.
- Hair neat and tied; nails short, clean, without polish (infection control).
- Minimal jewellery and no dangling items; comfortable, clean, quiet footwear.
- Personal hygiene; avoid strong perfumes.
- Good posture and pleasant expression.

Grooming is not vanity: it supports infection control, safety and the patient's trust in the nurse.

### Role of the nurse in empowering others

- **Patients:** give information so they can make informed choices; teach self-care (diabetic foot care, insulin technique as taught, colostomy care); involve them in care planning; respect their decisions.
- **Families and caregivers:** teach skills to care at home; link with support groups.
- **Women and adolescent girls:** education on nutrition, menstrual hygiene, family planning, rights and services.
- **Community:** health education, mobilising self-help groups, involving local leaders.
- **Colleagues and students:** mentoring, sharing knowledge, recognising achievements.
- **Vulnerable groups:** advocacy and linking to welfare schemes.

## Remember

- Soft skills = interpersonal qualities; hard skills = technical; patients remember soft skills.
- Types in INC syllabus: visual, aural, communication skills.
- Communication: sender → message → channel → receiver → feedback; 7 Cs; non-verbal is powerful.
- Peplau's phases: orientation, identification, exploitation, resolution.
- Barriers: physical, physiological, psychological, language, cultural, organisational; overcome with simple language, privacy, feedback, teach-back.
- Survival strategies: time management, stress coping, resilience, work–life balance (prevent burnout).
- Self-empowerment: self-awareness, SMART goals, assertiveness, learning, responsibility.
- Professional etiquette and grooming support trust and infection control.

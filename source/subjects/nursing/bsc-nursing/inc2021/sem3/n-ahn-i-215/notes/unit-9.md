# Unit 9: Patients with communicable diseases

INC Unit IX covers infection control principles and the nursing management of common communicable diseases seen in India and globally, including HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis, viral hepatitis, malaria, typhoid and dengue.

---

## 9.1 Principles of infection control

**Chain of infection**: Infectious agent → Reservoir → Portal of exit → Mode of transmission → Portal of entry → Susceptible host.

**Breaking the chain** at any link prevents infection.

### Standard precautions (apply to ALL patients, regardless of diagnosis)
- **Hand hygiene**: 5 moments (before patient contact, before aseptic task, after body fluid exposure, after patient contact, after contact with patient surroundings). WHO 6-step technique with soap and water (20 seconds) or alcohol hand rub (minimum 20 seconds).
- **PPE**: gloves (whenever touching blood/body fluids/mucous membranes/non-intact skin), mask and eye protection/face shield (risk of splash), gown (if clothing likely to be soiled).
- **Safe sharps handling**: never recap needles; use a sharps container; needle-stick protocol if injury occurs.
- **Safe handling of linen and waste**: red bags for infectious waste; yellow bags/sharps containers for sharps.
- **Respiratory hygiene and cough etiquette**: mask patients with respiratory symptoms; cover cough.
- **Safe patient care equipment**: single-use items; decontaminate reusable equipment.

### Transmission-based precautions

| Category | Transmission route | Diseases | Precautions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Contact | Direct or indirect contact | MRSA, C. difficile, scabies, wound infections | Gloves + gown for all contact; dedicated equipment; private room or cohort |
| Droplet | Large droplets, <1 m | Influenza, meningococcal meningitis, mumps, rubella, pertussis | Surgical mask within 1 m; private room or curtain |
| Airborne | Small droplet nuclei, travel >1 m | Pulmonary TB, measles, chickenpox | N95/FFP2 respirator; negative-pressure room if available; private room |

## 9.2 HIV/AIDS

**HIV** (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) infects and destroys CD4+ T-lymphocytes (helper T cells), progressively weakening immunity until opportunistic infections and AIDS develop.

### Transmission
- Sexual intercourse (unprotected; highest risk anal sex).
- Blood and blood products: shared needles (intravenous drug use), blood transfusion (now screened), needle-stick injury (risk ~0.3%).
- Mother to child (MTCT): during pregnancy, delivery or breastfeeding.
- **HIV is NOT transmitted** by: casual contact, coughing, sneezing, sharing food/utensils, mosquito bites, toilet seats.

### Stages (WHO/CDC)

| Stage | CD4 count | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary infection (acute HIV) | Variable (may fall initially) | Flu-like illness 2–4 weeks after infection; seroconversion illness |
| Asymptomatic (clinical latency) | 500–350 cells/µL (falling slowly) | No symptoms; virus replicates; lasts years without treatment |
| Symptomatic HIV | 350–200 cells/µL | Minor opportunistic infections; weight loss, fever, lymphadenopathy, oral candidiasis, shingles |
| AIDS | <200 cells/µL | Major opportunistic infections (see below); AIDS-defining cancers |

### Opportunistic infections

| Organ | Infection | Key features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lungs | Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP) | Dry cough, progressive breathlessness; treated with co-trimoxazole |
| Brain | Toxoplasmosis, Cryptococcal meningitis | Headache, altered consciousness; Cryptococcus: headache, stiff neck, positive India ink stain of CSF |
| Mouth/oesophagus | Candidiasis | White plaques; dysphagia; treated with fluconazole |
| Intestine | Cryptosporidiosis | Profuse watery diarrhoea |
| Skin | Kaposi's sarcoma | Painless purple/red skin lesions; AIDS-defining |
| Eyes | CMV retinitis | Blurred vision; can cause blindness |
| Systemic | Disseminated Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC) | Fever, weight loss, diarrhoea; in very low CD4 counts |

### Diagnosis
- **ELISA** (enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay): screening test; detects antibodies; false negative in window period (3–6 weeks after infection before antibodies form).
- **Western blot**: confirmatory test.
- **Rapid HIV tests**: point-of-care; result in 20 minutes; needs confirmation.
- **Viral load** (HIV RNA PCR): measures amount of virus; used to monitor treatment.
- **CD4 count**: monitors immune status; guides when to start treatment.

The **window period** (infection to detectable antibodies): 3–6 weeks with 4th generation tests; during this time the person can still transmit HIV.

### Antiretroviral therapy (ART)

**Principle**: ART does not cure HIV but suppresses viral load to undetectable, allows CD4 recovery, prevents progression and transmission. **Treatment as prevention**: undetectable = untransmittable (U=U).

**Drug classes**

| Class | Examples | Site of action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs) | Tenofovir (TDF), lamivudine (3TC), zidovudine (AZT) | Block reverse transcriptase (DNA synthesis from viral RNA) |
| Non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NNRTIs) | Efavirenz, nevirapine | Directly inhibit reverse transcriptase |
| Protease inhibitors (PIs) | Lopinavir, darunavir | Block HIV protease (prevents virus maturation) |
| Integrase strand transfer inhibitors (INSTIs) | Dolutegravir, raltegravir | Block integration of viral DNA into host genome |

**India's first-line ART (national programme)**: TDF + 3TC + dolutegravir (TLD) for most adults.

**When to start**: ART is started in ALL HIV-positive individuals regardless of CD4 count (Test and Treat policy).

**PMTCT** (Prevention of Mother-to-Child Transmission): ART given to HIV+ pregnant women throughout pregnancy and breastfeeding; infant prophylaxis with nevirapine; promote safe infant feeding.

### Nursing care for HIV/AIDS

- **Infection prevention**: standard precautions always; treat all blood and body fluids as potentially infectious; needle-stick management (wash site, report, post-exposure prophylaxis within 72 hours).
- **Medication adherence**: ART must be taken every day without interruption — missing doses causes resistance. Counsel on importance, timing, storage, side effects.
- **Nutritional support**: high-protein diet; treat opportunistic infections causing weight loss; nutritional supplements.
- **Psychosocial care**: HIV carries stigma and discrimination; ensure confidentiality; support disclosure to partners; link to support groups.
- **Palliative care**: in advanced AIDS; pain management, spiritual and psychosocial support.
- **Prevention counselling**: condom use, harm reduction for IVDU (needle exchange, opioid substitution therapy), partner notification, PrEP (pre-exposure prophylaxis) for high-risk individuals.
- **Occupational exposure (needle-stick)**: wash thoroughly with soap and water; do not squeeze; report to occupational health; risk assessment; PEP (post-exposure prophylaxis) with ART must start within 72 hours and continue for 28 days.

## 9.3 Tuberculosis (TB)

**TB** is caused by **Mycobacterium tuberculosis**, an acid-fast bacillus (AFB) spread by **airborne** droplet nuclei.

### Pathogenesis
Inhaled droplets → primary complex (Ghon focus — small area of consolidation + hilar lymph nodes; Ghon complex); most cases are contained by immune system; latent TB infection (LTBI); reactivation occurs when immunity falls (HIV, malnutrition, diabetes, old age, immunosuppressants).

**Active pulmonary TB**: cough >3 weeks, blood-stained sputum (haemoptysis), night sweats, fever, weight loss (classic "consumption"), fatigue. Extrapulmonary TB: lymph nodes (most common extrapulmonary), pleura, spine (Pott's disease), meningitis, kidney, intestine, miliary (disseminated).

### Diagnosis
- **Sputum smear microscopy (AFB smear)**: quick, cheap, identifies infectious cases; 3 smears (early morning sputum best).
- **Sputum culture** (Löwenstein–Jensen medium): gold standard but takes 4–8 weeks; needed for drug sensitivity testing.
- **CBNAAT / Xpert MTB/RIF**: rapid PCR — detects TB and rifampicin resistance in 2 hours; preferred initial test.
- **Chest X-ray**: upper-lobe infiltrates, cavitation, pleural effusion.
- **Mantoux (tuberculin skin test)**: inject 5 TU PPD intradermally; read induration at 48–72 h; >10 mm = positive in most patients; indicates infection but not active disease.
- **IGRA (interferon-gamma release assay)**: blood test, more specific than Mantoux.

### Treatment — DOTS (Directly Observed Treatment Short-course)

**Principle**: every dose supervised by a health worker to ensure adherence. **National TB Elimination Programme (NTEP)** in India uses daily regimens under Nikshay portal.

**Standard regimen (DS-TB)**:

| Phase | Duration | Drugs | Mnemonic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intensive phase | 2 months | Isoniazid (H) + Rifampicin (R) + Pyrazinamide (Z) + Ethambutol (E) | HRZE × 2 months |
| Continuation phase | 4 months | Isoniazid (H) + Rifampicin (R) | HR × 4 months |

Total: 6 months (2HRZE/4HR).

**Drug adverse effects**

| Drug | Key adverse effect | Nursing point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Isoniazid (H) | Peripheral neuropathy (give pyridoxine), hepatitis | Give pyridoxine (B6) with isoniazid; check LFT |
| Rifampicin (R) | Orange-red discolouration of urine, tears, sweat; hepatitis; drug interactions | Warn patient about colour change; monitor LFT |
| Pyrazinamide (Z) | Hepatitis, hyperuricaemia, arthralgia | LFT; check uric acid; give with food |
| Ethambutol (E) | Optic neuritis (blurred vision, red–green colour blindness) | Monthly visual acuity; report any visual change |

**Drug-resistant TB**: MDR-TB = resistant to H and R; XDR-TB = MDR + resistant to fluoroquinolones and injectable drugs. Longer, more toxic regimens (18–24 months); bedaquiline-based newer regimens.

### Nursing care for TB
- **Airborne precautions**: isolate in a single room with negative pressure if available; N95 mask for healthcare workers; surgical mask for patient when outside room.
- **DOTS**: directly observe every dose; record on treatment card; encourage adherence (explain that incomplete treatment causes resistance and relapse).
- **Nutritional support**: TB is a wasting disease; high-protein, high-calorie diet; Nikshay Poshan Yojana (nutritional support).
- **Contact tracing**: examine all close contacts (household, workplace) for symptoms and Mantoux; treat LTBI if identified.
- **Educate**: cover cough, dispose sputum safely (sputum cup with lid + disinfectant), hand hygiene, complete 6-month course.
- **Monitor side effects**: monthly check of vision (ethambutol), LFT at baseline and monthly (all drugs).
- **HIV testing**: all TB patients should be offered HIV testing (TB-HIV co-infection is common).

## 9.4 Viral hepatitis

**Hepatitis** is inflammation of the liver. The five main hepatitis viruses differ in route of transmission and outcome.

| Feature | Hepatitis A (HAV) | Hepatitis B (HBV) | Hepatitis C (HCV) | Hepatitis D (HDV) | Hepatitis E (HEV) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Transmission | Faeco-oral | Blood/body fluids, sexual, MTCT | Blood/body fluids | Needs HBV (co-infection or superinfection) | Faeco-oral |
| Incubation | 15–50 days | 45–180 days | 15–150 days | Similar to HBV | 15–60 days |
| Chronicity | No | Yes (~10% adults, ~90% neonates) | Yes (~80%) | Yes (superinfection) | No (except in immunocompromised) |
| Cirrhosis / cancer | No | Yes (long-term) | Yes (long-term) | Accelerated liver disease | Rarely |
| Vaccine | Yes | Yes | No | HBV vaccination protects | Yes (India — vaccine available) |
| Treatment | Supportive | Tenofovir, entecavir | DAAs (sofosbuvir, daclatasvir) — >95% cure | Treat HBV | Supportive; ribavirin in severe HEV |

**Clinical features** (all types): anorexia, nausea, vomiting, low-grade fever, right upper quadrant pain, then jaundice (yellow sclera and skin), dark urine (bilirubin), pale stools (less bile reaching gut), hepatomegaly.

**Severe hepatitis B prevention (3-dose HBV vaccine)**: universal infant vaccination in India (at birth, 6 weeks, 10 weeks, 14 weeks under UIP); healthcare workers must be vaccinated; HBsAg-positive mothers' babies receive HBV vaccine + hepatitis B immunoglobulin within 12 hours of birth.

**Nursing care**
- Bed rest during acute phase; activity as tolerated.
- High-carbohydrate, low-fat diet; small frequent meals; avoid alcohol completely.
- Faeco-oral infections (A, E): hand hygiene, safe water, sanitation, food hygiene.
- Blood-borne infections (B, C): standard precautions, do not share razors, toothbrushes, needles; condom use.
- Monitor for fulminant hepatic failure: altered consciousness, coagulopathy (PT prolonged), hypoglycaemia → refer to ICU.
- Medication precaution: hepatitis patients should avoid hepatotoxic drugs (paracetamol at high dose, NSAIDs, herbal medicines metabolised by the liver).

## 9.5 Malaria

**Malaria** is caused by *Plasmodium* species transmitted by the bite of a female *Anopheles* mosquito.

| Species | Key feature |
| --- | --- |
| P. falciparum | Most dangerous; causes cerebral malaria; can kill |
| P. vivax | Dormant liver stage (hypnozoites); relapses; most common in India |
| P. malariae | Quartan fever (every 72 h); can cause nephrotic syndrome |
| P. ovale | Rare; like vivax, needs radical cure |

**Life cycle (simplified)**:
Mosquito injects sporozoites → liver (pre-erythrocytic stage, 7–30 days) → merozoites released → infect red blood cells → erythrocytic cycle (synchronised rupture of RBCs releases merozoites and toxins → fever spike). Gametocytes taken by mosquito → sexual cycle in mosquito.

**Clinical features**: cyclical fever with chills and rigors (tertian = every 48 h for falciparum/vivax; quartan = every 72 h), headache, myalgia, anaemia (RBC destruction), jaundice, splenomegaly.

**Severe falciparum malaria** (medical emergency): cerebral malaria (impaired consciousness, seizures), severe anaemia, pulmonary oedema, renal failure, hypoglycaemia, blackwater fever (massive haemolysis, haemoglobin in urine).

**Diagnosis**: peripheral blood smear (thick and thin films — gold standard, identifies species and density), Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT — rapid, field use), PCR (research/reference).

**Treatment**

| Species | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Uncomplicated P. falciparum | Artemisinin-based combination therapy (ACT): artesunate + sulphadoxine-pyrimethamine (AS-SP) in India |
| Uncomplicated P. vivax | Chloroquine + primaquine (primaquine for radical cure; contraindicated in G6PD deficiency — test first) |
| Severe malaria | IV artesunate (preferred) or IV quinine; ICU care |

**Nursing care**: tepid sponging for fever, anti-pyretics (paracetamol — not aspirin in children), fluid balance monitoring, blood glucose monitoring (hypoglycaemia in severe falciparum), neurological observations (cerebral malaria), convulsion precautions, insecticide-treated bed net at night, vector control (remove stagnant water), complete antimalarial course.

## 9.6 Typhoid fever

**Typhoid** is caused by **Salmonella typhi** (enteric fever), transmitted via the **faeco-oral route** (contaminated food and water). **Paratyphoid** is caused by S. paratyphi.

**Features**: incubation 10–14 days; stepwise rising fever, bradycardia (relative to fever), headache, malaise; at the end of week 1 → **rose spots** (faint salmon-coloured maculopapular rash on abdomen and chest, transient, appear in crops). Hepatosplenomegaly. Week 2–3: complications.

**Complications**: intestinal perforation (most dangerous — sudden severe abdominal pain, rigid abdomen, shock), intestinal haemorrhage, encephalopathy, myocarditis.

**Diagnosis**: blood culture (best in first week — most sensitive), Widal test (antibodies to O and H antigens; a rising titre is more meaningful than a single test; false positives common), stool culture (second week+), urine culture, TUBEX/Typhidot rapid tests.

**Treatment**: fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin) but resistance is increasing; ceftriaxone IV (for severe cases); azithromycin; chloramphenicol (older, side effects — aplastic anaemia).

**Nursing care**: isolation (enteric precautions — standard precautions + strict hand hygiene after using toilet), complete 10–14 day antibiotic course, high-calorie soft bland diet (semi-solid in recovery), monitor for perforation (sudden worsening of pain, rigid abdomen → emergency laparotomy), fluids, temperature monitoring, bed rest, typhoid vaccine for contacts and high-risk groups (Typbar-TCV for children, Vi-PS for adults), safe drinking water and sanitation.

## 9.7 Dengue fever

**Dengue** is caused by dengue virus (DENV 1–4), transmitted by the bite of **Aedes aegypti** mosquito (day-biting; breeds in clean stagnant water — tyres, flower pots, overhead tanks).

**Features**: sudden high fever, severe headache, retro-orbital pain (pain behind the eyes), severe myalgia and arthralgia ("break-bone fever"), maculopapular rash, mild haemorrhagic manifestations (petechiae, bruising, positive tourniquet test — inflate BP cuff to midpoint between systolic and diastolic for 5 minutes; ≥20 petechiae per square inch = positive).

### Warning signs (progress to severe dengue)
Abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, clinical fluid accumulation (ascites, pleural effusion), mucosal bleeding, lethargy, liver enlargement >2 cm, rising haematocrit with rapid fall in platelet count.

### Classification

| Phase | Features |
| --- | --- |
| Dengue without warning signs | Fever 2–7 days, typical dengue features |
| Dengue with warning signs | Any warning sign above |
| Severe dengue | Severe plasma leakage → dengue shock syndrome; severe bleeding; severe organ impairment (liver, CNS, heart, kidney) |

**Dengue haemorrhagic fever (DHF)**: fever + thrombocytopenia (<100,000/µL) + haemoconcentration (haematocrit rise ≥20%) + plasma leakage evidence. Can progress to **dengue shock syndrome** (DSS): hypotension, narrow pulse pressure, cold extremities.

**Diagnosis**: NS1 antigen (early, days 1–5), IgM/IgG antibodies (from day 5), PCR; CBC (falling platelet, rising haematocrit), liver function tests (elevated AST/ALT).

**Treatment**: supportive (no specific antiviral); paracetamol for fever (**avoid aspirin and NSAIDs** — increase bleeding risk); IV fluids for severe dengue/shock; platelet transfusion if <10,000/µL or active bleeding; monitor hourly in severe dengue.

**Nursing care**: hourly vital signs in severe dengue, urine output, monitor haematocrit and platelet daily, IV access, fluid resuscitation as ordered (avoid over-hydration), watch for bleeding (gums, nose, IV sites, melaena), no IM injections (bleeding risk), vector control education (remove water-holding containers, use insect repellent, long sleeves, mosquito nets), dengue vaccine (Dengvaxia) available in some countries for seropositive individuals.

## 9.8 Other communicable diseases — brief notes

| Disease | Cause | Transmission | Key nursing point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cholera | Vibrio cholerae | Faeco-oral, contaminated water | Profuse "rice-water" stools; rapid dehydration; oral rehydration solution (ORS) or IV fluids; oral cholera vaccine |
| Rabies | Rabies virus | Animal bite (dogs, bats) | Wash wound with soap and water for 15 minutes; rabies immunoglobulin into wound + rabies vaccine post-exposure; 100% fatal once symptoms start |
| Meningococcal meningitis | Neisseria meningitidis | Droplet | Meningeal signs, petechial rash; IV penicillin; droplet precautions; vaccinate contacts |
| Chickenpox (varicella) | Varicella-zoster virus | Airborne | Vesicular rash, all stages simultaneously; aciclovir for severe cases; airborne + contact precautions; varicella vaccine |
| Measles | Paramyxovirus | Airborne | Koplik's spots (oral), maculopapular rash from face downward; vitamin A; MMR vaccine; airborne precautions |

## 9.9 Remember

- Chain of infection: break it at any link (hand hygiene is the simplest and most effective).
- Airborne precautions (N95 mask, single room): TB, measles, chickenpox.
- HIV window period: 3–6 weeks; U=U (undetectable = untransmittable on ART).
- HIV needle-stick: wash, report, PEP within 72 hours.
- TB treatment: 2HRZE/4HR under DOTS; never stop early; orange urine with rifampicin is normal.
- Hepatitis A/E: faeco-oral; B/C: blood/body fluids; B → cirrhosis → cancer; cure HCV with DAAs.
- Malaria diagnosis: blood smear; falciparum = cerebral malaria; radical cure (primaquine) for vivax.
- Typhoid complication to fear: intestinal perforation (rigid abdomen = emergency).
- Dengue: Aedes aegypti; avoid aspirin; platelet fall + haematocrit rise = severe dengue; no IM injections.
- Tourniquet test: ≥20 petechiae per square inch = positive for dengue.

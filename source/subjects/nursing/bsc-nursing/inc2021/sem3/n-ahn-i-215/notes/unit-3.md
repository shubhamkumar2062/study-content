# Unit 3: Nursing management of patients with respiratory problems

This notes file covers INC Unit IV (18 hours). For each disorder learn the same frame: **definition → causes → pathophysiology (brief) → clinical features → diagnostic tests → medical and surgical management → nursing management → patient education**. Drugs are named by class only.

## 3.1 Review of anatomy and physiology

```text
 Nose → pharynx → larynx → trachea → main bronchi → bronchioles
   (upper airway)          (lower airway)          → alveoli (gas exchange)
```

- Right lung has 3 lobes, left lung 2 lobes. Each lung is covered by visceral pleura; the parietal pleura lines the chest wall; between them is the pleural space with a thin film of fluid and negative pressure.
- **Ventilation** (moving air), **diffusion** (O₂ and CO₂ across the alveolar wall) and **perfusion** (blood flow through lungs) are all needed for gas exchange.
- Breathing is controlled by the respiratory centre in the medulla, driven mainly by blood CO₂ level.
- Defences: hair and mucus in the nose, cilia (mucociliary escalator), cough reflex, alveolar macrophages.

## 3.2 Nursing assessment

### History

- Cough (dry or productive, duration, time of day), sputum (amount, colour, smell, blood — haemoptysis), breathlessness (dyspnoea — at rest, on exertion, lying flat = orthopnoea, waking at night), chest pain (pleuritic pain worse on breathing), wheeze, fever, weight loss, night sweats.
- Smoking history (pack-years), occupation (dust, chemicals), exposure to tuberculosis, allergy, past illnesses, family history.

### Physical assessment

| Step | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| **Inspection** | Rate, rhythm and depth of breathing, use of accessory muscles, chest shape (barrel chest), movement, cyanosis, clubbing of fingers, position the patient prefers |
| **Palpation** | Tracheal position, chest expansion, tactile vocal fremitus, tenderness |
| **Percussion** | Resonant (normal), dull (fluid, consolidation), stony dull (pleural effusion), hyper-resonant (pneumothorax, emphysema) |
| **Auscultation** | Breath sounds and added sounds |

### Breath sounds

| Sound | Description | Found in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vesicular | Soft, low-pitched; inspiration longer than expiration | Normal, over most of the lungs |
| Bronchial | Loud, hollow; expiration as long as inspiration | Normal over trachea; abnormal over the lung = consolidation |
| Bronchovesicular | In between | Normal near main bronchi |
| **Crackles (crepitations)** | Short popping sounds, mostly on inspiration | Fluid in alveoli: pneumonia, pulmonary oedema, fibrosis |
| **Wheeze** | Musical, continuous, mostly on expiration | Narrowed airways: asthma, COPD |
| **Rhonchi** | Low-pitched snoring sounds; may clear on coughing | Secretions in larger airways |
| **Pleural rub** | Grating sound | Inflamed pleura |
| **Stridor** | High-pitched sound on inspiration | Upper airway obstruction — emergency |

### Diagnostic tests and nursing responsibilities

| Test | Nursing points |
| --- | --- |
| Chest X-ray, CT scan | Remove metal objects; ask about pregnancy |
| **Sputum examination** (culture, smear for acid-fast bacilli, cytology) | Early-morning deep-cough specimen before mouth wash and before antibiotics, in a sterile container; not saliva |
| **Arterial blood gas (ABG)** | Usually radial artery; check collateral circulation (Allen's test); press the site for at least 5 minutes; send on ice if delay; note oxygen being given |
| **Pulse oximetry** | Normal SpO₂ usually 95–100%; warm finger, no nail polish |
| **Pulmonary function tests (spirometry)** | Explain the breathing effort needed; withhold bronchodilators if ordered |
| **Bronchoscopy** | Nil by mouth before; after: nil by mouth until gag reflex returns; watch for bleeding, breathing difficulty |
| **Thoracentesis (pleural tap)** | Sitting up leaning forward over a table; stay still, do not cough during needle insertion; after: watch for breathlessness (pneumothorax), record amount and colour of fluid |
| Pleural and lung biopsy, mantoux test, CBNAAT (rapid molecular TB test) | Explain and prepare as per test |

Normal ABG values (approximate): pH 7.35–7.45; PaCO₂ 35–45 mmHg; PaO₂ 80–100 mmHg; HCO₃⁻ 22–26 mEq/L.

## 3.3 Upper respiratory tract infections

| Condition | Key points | Care |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Common cold (rhinitis)** | Viral; sneezing, running nose, mild fever | Rest, fluids, steam inhalation, hand hygiene |
| **Sinusitis** | Facial pain, nasal block, purulent discharge | Steam, nasal decongestants, antibiotics if bacterial, as ordered |
| **Pharyngitis, tonsillitis** | Sore throat, fever, enlarged tonsils; streptococcal infection can lead to rheumatic fever | Warm saline gargles, fluids, complete antibiotic course; tonsillectomy for repeated attacks |
| **Laryngitis** | Hoarse voice | Voice rest, steam, no smoking |
| **Epistaxis** (nose bleed) | Often from Little's area | Sit up, lean forward, pinch the soft part of the nose for 10–15 minutes, cold compress; nasal packing if severe |

**After tonsillectomy**: lateral or prone position until awake; watch for bleeding (frequent swallowing is an early sign); cold fluids and soft foods; avoid red-coloured drinks (confused with blood).

## 3.4 Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)

**Definition**: a common, preventable and treatable lung disease with persistent breathlessness and airflow limitation that is not fully reversible. It includes **chronic bronchitis** and **emphysema**. (Bronchial asthma is reversible airway narrowing and is usually discussed with it.)

- **Causes and risks**: smoking (main cause), indoor air pollution from biomass cooking fuels (important in India), occupational dust, outdoor pollution, repeated infections, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency (rare).
- **Pathophysiology**:
  - Chronic bronchitis: irritants → more mucus glands and mucus, damaged cilia → airway narrowing and productive cough (cough with sputum for at least 3 months a year for 2 years in a row).
  - Emphysema: destruction of alveolar walls → large air spaces, loss of elastic recoil → air trapping, barrel chest.
  - Long-standing low oxygen → narrowing of lung vessels → pulmonary hypertension → right heart failure (cor pulmonale).

| Feature | Chronic bronchitis ("blue bloater") | Emphysema ("pink puffer") |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cough and sputum | Marked | Little |
| Cyanosis, oedema | Common | Less |
| Breathlessness | Later | Early and marked; pursed-lip breathing |
| Body build | Often heavy | Thin, barrel chest |

These are classic descriptions; most patients have features of both.

- **Diagnostic tests**: spirometry (reduced FEV₁/FVC ratio after bronchodilator confirms airflow limitation), chest X-ray (hyperinflation), ABG, sputum culture, ECG and echo (cor pulmonale), CBC (polycythaemia).
- **Medical management (classes)**: bronchodilators (beta-2 agonists, anticholinergics), inhaled corticosteroids in some, mucolytics, antibiotics in infective attacks, **controlled oxygen** therapy, vaccines (influenza, pneumococcal), pulmonary rehabilitation. Severe disease: long-term oxygen therapy, non-invasive ventilation; surgery (bullectomy, lung volume reduction) in selected patients.
- **Important**: some COPD patients retain CO₂ and depend on low oxygen to drive breathing, so oxygen is given in the **controlled concentration ordered**, often by Venturi mask, aiming at the target saturation set by the doctor (commonly 88–92%), and the patient is watched for drowsiness.

**Nursing diagnoses and interventions**

| Diagnosis | Interventions (with rationale) |
| --- | --- |
| Ineffective airway clearance related to thick secretions | High-Fowler's or tripod position (lungs expand); fluids if not restricted (thin secretions); steam/nebulisation as ordered; controlled and huff coughing; chest physiotherapy and postural drainage; suction if needed |
| Impaired gas exchange related to airflow limitation | Controlled oxygen as ordered; monitor SpO₂, ABG, level of consciousness; pursed-lip and diaphragmatic breathing (keeps airways open, empties lungs) |
| Activity intolerance related to breathlessness | Plan rest between activities; graded exercise; energy-saving techniques |
| Imbalanced nutrition, less than body requirement | Small frequent high-calorie, high-protein meals; rest before meals; mouth care after inhalers |
| Anxiety related to breathlessness | Stay with the patient, calm voice, teach breathing control |

**Patient education**: stop smoking (most important); avoid smoke and dust, use cleaner cooking fuel and ventilation; correct inhaler and spacer technique; rinse mouth after steroid inhalers; recognise early signs of infection (change in sputum colour, more breathlessness); vaccination; breathing exercises; safe home oxygen use (no smoking or fire near oxygen).

### Bronchial asthma (brief)

- Chronic inflammation of airways with episodes of reversible narrowing (bronchospasm, oedema, mucus).
- Triggers: allergens (dust mite, pollen, animal dander), smoke, cold air, exercise, infection, some drugs.
- Features: episodes of wheeze, breathlessness, chest tightness, cough worse at night or early morning.
- Management (classes): relievers (short-acting beta-2 agonists), controllers (inhaled corticosteroids, long-acting beta-2 agonists), leukotriene modifiers.
- **Acute severe asthma**: high-Fowler's position, oxygen, nebulised bronchodilators and systemic corticosteroids as ordered; danger signs — cannot speak a sentence, silent chest, drowsiness, cyanosis.
- Teaching: avoid triggers, peak-flow monitoring, written action plan, inhaler technique.

## 3.5 Pleural effusion and empyema

**Pleural effusion**: abnormal collection of fluid in the pleural space.

- **Transudate** (low protein): heart failure, cirrhosis, kidney disease (nephrotic syndrome).
- **Exudate** (high protein): tuberculosis (common in India), pneumonia, cancer, pulmonary embolism.
- **Features**: breathlessness, pleuritic chest pain, reduced chest movement on the affected side, **stony dull percussion**, reduced or absent breath sounds, trachea pushed to the opposite side in large effusions.
- **Tests**: chest X-ray (blunting of costophrenic angle), ultrasound, thoracentesis with fluid analysis.
- **Management**: treat the cause; thoracentesis; intercostal chest drain for large effusions; **pleurodesis** (sealing the pleural space with an agent) for recurrent effusions.

**Empyema**: pus in the pleural space, usually after pneumonia, lung abscess or chest surgery. Features: high fever, chills, chest pain, toxic look. Management: antibiotics, chest drain, and if the pus is thick or loculated, surgery (decortication).

### Care of a patient with an intercostal chest drain (underwater seal)

1. Keep the drainage bottle **below chest level** at all times. — *Prevents fluid flowing back into the chest.*
2. Keep the tube end under the water (about 2 cm). — *The water acts as a one-way seal so air cannot enter the chest.*
3. Check **tidaling** (water level rising and falling with breathing) — shows the tube is open; stopped tidaling may mean blockage or re-expanded lung.
4. **Bubbling**: continuous bubbling may mean an air leak in the system; intermittent bubbling on coughing is expected with pneumothorax.
5. Keep tubes free from kinks and loops; secure connections with tape.
6. Keep two clamps at the bedside, but **do not clamp routinely** (risk of tension pneumothorax); clamp only briefly when changing bottles or if the system disconnects, as per policy.
7. Record amount, colour and nature of drainage; report sudden increase (bleeding) or more than the limit set.
8. Encourage deep breathing, coughing and arm and shoulder exercises.
9. If the tube comes out of the chest, cover the site at once with a sterile dressing and inform the doctor.

## 3.6 Bronchiectasis

- **Definition**: permanent abnormal widening of bronchi with destruction of their walls.
- **Causes**: severe childhood infections (measles, whooping cough), tuberculosis, cystic fibrosis, obstruction by tumour or foreign body, immune defects.
- **Pathophysiology**: infection and inflammation damage the bronchial wall → bronchi widen → mucus pools → repeated infection (vicious circle).
- **Features**: chronic cough with **large amounts of foul-smelling purulent sputum**, especially in the morning and on changing position; haemoptysis; clubbing; coarse crackles.
- **Tests**: high-resolution CT (the test of choice), sputum culture, spirometry.
- **Management**: postural drainage and chest physiotherapy (main nursing measure), antibiotics during attacks, bronchodilators, mucolytics; surgical removal (lobectomy) for localised disease.
- **Nursing**: postural drainage two to three times a day before meals, sputum cup with lid and record of daily amount, good mouth care, high-protein diet, fluids, teach the family chest physiotherapy.

## 3.7 Pneumonia

- **Definition**: acute inflammation of lung tissue (alveoli) usually caused by infection, with filling of alveoli by exudate (consolidation).
- **Classification**: community-acquired, hospital-acquired (including ventilator-associated), aspiration pneumonia, pneumonia in immunocompromised patients; anatomically lobar or bronchopneumonia.
- **Organisms**: bacteria (commonly *Streptococcus pneumoniae*), viruses, fungi, atypical organisms.
- **Risk factors**: old age, smoking, alcohol, COPD, diabetes, immobility, reduced consciousness, poor swallowing, ventilation.
- **Pathophysiology (lobar)**: congestion → red hepatisation → grey hepatisation → resolution.
- **Features**: fever with chills, cough with rusty or purulent sputum, pleuritic chest pain, rapid breathing, crackles, bronchial breath sounds and dullness over consolidation; confusion in the elderly.
- **Tests**: chest X-ray, sputum Gram stain and culture, blood culture, CBC (raised white cells), ABG, SpO₂.
- **Management**: antibiotics as ordered (after cultures are sent), oxygen, fluids, antipyretics; ventilation in severe cases.

**Nursing care**: semi- or high-Fowler's position; oxygen; deep breathing, coughing, incentive spirometry; fluids 2–3 litres/day if not restricted; tepid sponging for fever; rest; mouth care; turning two-hourly; monitor temperature, respiration, SpO₂.

**Prevention of hospital-acquired and ventilator-associated pneumonia**: hand hygiene, head end raised 30–45°, oral care, early mobilisation, aspiration precautions, aseptic suctioning.

## 3.8 Lung abscess

- Localised area of pus formation with destruction of lung tissue (cavity).
- Causes: aspiration (alcoholism, unconsciousness, poor dental hygiene), pneumonia, obstruction by a tumour.
- Features: fever, cough with large amounts of foul, sometimes blood-stained sputum, chest pain, weight loss, clubbing.
- Tests: chest X-ray and CT (cavity with air–fluid level), sputum culture.
- Management: long course of antibiotics, postural drainage; surgery (lobectomy) if not resolving.
- Nursing: as for bronchiectasis; good oral hygiene; nutrition.

## 3.9 Cysts and tumours of the lung

- **Lung cysts** (e.g. hydatid cyst from dog tapeworm, congenital cysts, bullae): may be silent or cause cough, pain, infection; surgical removal when needed.
- **Lung cancer (bronchogenic carcinoma)**:
  - Risk factors: smoking (main), passive smoking, air pollution, occupational exposure (asbestos, radon).
  - Types: non-small-cell (adenocarcinoma, squamous cell, large cell) and small-cell.
  - Features: persistent cough, haemoptysis, chest pain, breathlessness, hoarseness, weight loss, recurrent pneumonia; signs of spread.
  - Tests: chest X-ray, CT, bronchoscopy with biopsy, sputum cytology, PET scan for staging.
  - Management: surgery (lobectomy, pneumonectomy), radiotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy and immunotherapy; palliative care.

**After lung surgery**: chest drain care (no drain or drain as per surgeon after pneumonectomy); position as advised (after pneumonectomy usually on the back or partly turned towards the operated side, not on the unoperated side, so the remaining lung can expand — follow the surgeon's order); pain relief to allow deep breathing; arm exercises on the operated side; early ambulation.

## 3.10 Chest injuries

| Injury | Features | Management |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rib fracture** | Pain on breathing, tenderness | Pain relief to allow deep breathing; do not strap the chest |
| **Flail chest** (several ribs broken in two places) | **Paradoxical movement** — the segment moves in on inspiration | Oxygen, analgesia, ventilation in severe cases |
| **Pneumothorax** (air in pleural space) | Sudden chest pain, breathlessness, hyper-resonance, absent breath sounds | Oxygen, needle aspiration or chest drain |
| **Tension pneumothorax** | Severe breathlessness, trachea shifted to opposite side, distended neck veins, low BP | Emergency needle decompression, then chest drain |
| **Open (sucking) chest wound** | Air sucked in through the wound | Cover with a dressing taped on three sides (acts as a flutter valve); chest drain |
| **Haemothorax** (blood in pleural space) | Dullness, shock | Chest drain, blood replacement; surgery if heavy bleeding |
| Cardiac tamponade | See Unit 5 | Pericardiocentesis |

First aid in chest injury: airway, breathing, circulation; oxygen; position upright if spine is not injured; cover open wounds; shift to hospital.

## 3.11 Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS)

- **Definition**: sudden, severe inflammatory lung injury causing leaky capillaries, fluid-filled alveoli (non-cardiac pulmonary oedema) and severe hypoxaemia not corrected by ordinary oxygen.
- **Causes**: sepsis (commonest), pneumonia, aspiration, major trauma, shock, multiple transfusions, pancreatitis, drowning, burns.
- **Pathophysiology**: injury → inflammation → damaged alveolar–capillary membrane → protein-rich fluid floods alveoli → surfactant lost → alveoli collapse → stiff lungs → severe hypoxaemia → later fibrosis.
- **Features**: increasing breathlessness, rapid breathing, cyanosis, falling SpO₂ despite oxygen, crackles; chest X-ray shows "white-out" on both sides.
- **Management**: treat the cause; mechanical ventilation with lung-protective settings and PEEP (positive end-expiratory pressure) to keep alveoli open; prone positioning in severe cases; careful fluids; sedation.
- **Nursing**: ICU monitoring, ventilator care, suctioning, positioning, pressure-area care, nutrition, prevention of infection, family support.

## 3.12 Pulmonary embolism

- **Definition**: blockage of a pulmonary artery or its branch, usually by a blood clot that has travelled from a deep vein of the leg or pelvis.
- **Risk factors (Virchow's triad)**: slowing of blood flow (immobility, long journeys, surgery), damage to vessel wall (trauma, surgery), increased clotting (pregnancy, oral contraceptives, cancer, dehydration).
- **Features**: sudden breathlessness, pleuritic chest pain, rapid breathing and pulse, anxiety, cough, haemoptysis; large embolism → collapse, shock, death.
- **Tests**: CT pulmonary angiography (main test), D-dimer, ECG, ABG, ventilation-perfusion scan, leg Doppler ultrasound.
- **Management**: oxygen, anticoagulants as ordered, thrombolytics in massive embolism, rarely embolectomy; vena cava filter in selected cases.
- **Nursing**: high-Fowler's position, oxygen, monitoring; watch for bleeding during anticoagulant therapy (gums, urine, stools, injection sites); bleeding precautions (soft toothbrush, electric shaver).
- **Prevention**: early ambulation after surgery, leg exercises, graduated compression stockings and intermittent pneumatic compression as ordered, adequate fluids, preventive anticoagulants in high-risk patients as ordered.

## 3.13 Common respiratory nursing procedures

- **Oxygen therapy**: by nasal prongs (low flow), simple face mask, Venturi mask (fixed accurate concentration), non-rebreather mask (high concentration). Safety: no smoking or open flame, check humidifier, check skin under mask and prongs, give only the flow ordered, monitor SpO₂.
- **Nebulisation**: sitting position, slow deep breaths through mouthpiece or mask, mouth rinse after steroid nebulisation.
- **Chest physiotherapy**: percussion (cupped hands), vibration, postural drainage — before meals, not over spine, breasts or kidneys; stop if the patient becomes breathless or cyanosed.
- **Incentive spirometry**: slow deep breath held 3–5 seconds, 10 times an hour while awake.
- **Suctioning**: sterile technique for tracheal suction; pre-oxygenate; suction only while withdrawing; each pass no longer than about 10–15 seconds.

## 3.14 Health behaviours to prevent respiratory illness

- Do not smoke; avoid second-hand smoke; tobacco cessation help (quit lines and cessation centres).
- Use cleaner cooking fuel (LPG), smokeless stoves and good kitchen ventilation.
- Masks and protective equipment at dusty workplaces.
- Cover coughs and sneezes (respiratory hygiene), hand hygiene, masks during epidemics.
- Vaccinations as advised (influenza, pneumococcal, BCG in childhood).
- Early treatment of respiratory infections; complete the full course of TB treatment.
- Healthy diet, exercise, avoid overcrowding, good house ventilation.

## Remember

- Breath sounds: crackles = fluid in alveoli; wheeze = narrowed airways; stridor = upper airway obstruction (emergency).
- Stony dull percussion = pleural effusion; hyper-resonance = pneumothorax.
- Sputum: early morning, deep cough, sterile container, before antibiotics.
- COPD = chronic bronchitis + emphysema; smoking and biomass smoke; give oxygen only as ordered, controlled.
- Pursed-lip breathing keeps airways open.
- Chest drain bottle below the chest; do not clamp routinely; tidaling shows the tube is open.
- Bronchiectasis: large amounts of foul sputum; postural drainage.
- Flail chest = paradoxical movement; tension pneumothorax = trachea shift + distended neck veins + low BP.
- Open chest wound: dressing taped on three sides.
- ARDS: severe hypoxaemia not corrected by oxygen; ventilation with PEEP; prone position.
- Pulmonary embolism usually comes from a leg DVT; prevent with early ambulation.

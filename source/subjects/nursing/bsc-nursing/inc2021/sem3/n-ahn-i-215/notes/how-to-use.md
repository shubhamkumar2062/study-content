# How to use this guide

Adult Health Nursing I is the largest clinical nursing subject in Semester 3 of the INC 2021 B.Sc Nursing programme. It integrates pathophysiology, medical management and nursing care across nine body systems and runs through approximately **115 theory hours plus extensive clinical hours**. Everything you learn here directly supports your clinical postings in medical and surgical wards.

## Exam pattern

**Standard INC 75-mark end-semester theory paper, 3 hours.** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 20 objective / MCQ questions | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — answer 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — answer 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

AHN-I also carries **50 marks of internal assessment** (sessional, assignments, clinical log) which are added to the university marks. The practical examination is separate.

## How to study this subject

AHN-I is wide; you cannot memorise everything the week before the exam. Use this strategy:

1. **Read the unit notes** while you have that posting — clinical experience makes the notes memorable.
2. **Learn the nursing care plan frame** for every condition: assessment → nursing diagnosis → goal → interventions → evaluation. Every long and short essay benefits from this structure.
3. **Learn procedures and investigations** in the clinical area: IV cannulation, dressings, catheterisation, oxygen delivery, ECG. The viva and practical exams test these directly.
4. **Answer the questions in the question bank** after each unit; check the full answer structure rather than just the main points.
5. **Run the MCQ bank** before class tests and again before the exam.

## Probability ratings

- **High** — asked in most years; often as a long essay or a short answer; learn the full nursing care plan.
- **Medium** — appears every few years or as part of a broader question.
- **Low** — less common, but tested in MCQs and very short answers.

These ratings are estimates from the typical pattern of INC nursing papers; they are not a promise.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. **Start every answer with a nursing assessment.** The nursing process always begins with assessment, and examiners reward it. A one-line definition + assessment + relevant history and physical findings sets up a strong answer.

2. **Use the nursing process frame explicitly.** Structure every nursing care answer as: Assessment → Nursing diagnoses → Goals/outcomes → Nursing interventions → Evaluation. This applies to both long essays and short answers.

3. **Classify before you describe.** For fractures, anaemia, hepatitis, malaria, shock — give the classification table first. Classification alone is worth 2–3 marks in a 10-mark essay.

4. **Write nursing interventions with rationale.** For example: "Elevate the limb (to reduce swelling and improve venous return)." A bare list of interventions scores less than a list with reasons.

5. **Quote normal values when relevant.** Hb normal ranges, SpO2 targets, BP targets, urine output targets (0.5 mL/kg/h), blood sugar ranges. These show clinical reasoning.

6. **For BCLS questions**, write the exact sequence: Check safety → Unresponsive? → Shout for help → Call emergency (activate code) → Check pulse (<10 seconds) → Start compressions 30:2, rate 100–120/min, depth 5–6 cm → AED as soon as available. Never say "give CPR" without the sequence.

7. **Tables score well for comparisons.** RA vs OA, gastric vs duodenal ulcer, types of hepatitis, types of shock, degrees of burns — write comparisons as tables; they are easy to read and mark.

8. **Health education and discharge teaching** are frequently asked. Always include in a nursing care essay.

9. **For very short answers (2 marks)**, give a one-line definition or classification plus one nursing point. Do not write a paragraph.

# Unit 7: Patients with disorders of the integumentary system

INC Unit VII covers skin anatomy and physiology, assessment, and nursing management of common skin conditions including eczema, psoriasis, urticaria, acne, infections, pressure ulcers and burns.

---

## 7.1 Anatomy and physiology of the skin

The skin (integument) is the largest organ of the body. It has three layers.

```text
SKIN LAYERS (cross-section)
─────────────────────────────────────────
Epidermis   │ outermost, avascular, 5 sub-layers
            │ Stratum corneum (dead keratinocytes)
            │ Stratum granulosum
            │ Stratum spinosum
            │ Stratum basale (mitosis, melanocytes)
─────────────────────────────────────────
Dermis      │ collagen + elastin fibres
            │ blood vessels, lymphatics
            │ hair follicles, sweat glands, sebaceous glands
            │ nerve endings (pain, touch, temperature)
─────────────────────────────────────────
Hypodermis  │ subcutaneous fat
(Subcutis)  │ insulation, shock absorption, energy store
─────────────────────────────────────────
```

**Functions of the skin**

| Function | How |
| --- | --- |
| Protection | Physical barrier against microorganisms, UV, chemicals |
| Thermoregulation | Sweating (cooling), vasoconstriction/dilation, fat insulation |
| Sensation | Receptors for pain, pressure, touch, temperature, vibration |
| Excretion | Salt and water via sweat |
| Vitamin D synthesis | UV converts 7-dehydrocholesterol to previtamin D3 |
| Immunity | Langerhans cells present antigens |
| Fluid balance | Prevents water loss from internal tissues |

**Skin appendages**: hair follicles (cover and protection), sebaceous glands (secrete sebum, lubricate skin), sweat glands — eccrine (all over body, thermoregulation) and apocrine (axilla, groin, body odour after puberty), nails (protect tips of digits).

## 7.2 Nursing assessment of the skin

### History
Ask about: onset and duration of lesion, spread, itching or pain, previous skin disease, family history (psoriasis, eczema, atopy), allergies (food, drugs, latex, metals), occupation, cosmetics, home remedies, systemic diseases (diabetes, thyroid, autoimmune), medications, recent infections or travel.

### Physical assessment

Examine in good natural or bright light. Assess:
- **Colour**: normal, pallor (anaemia), cyanosis, jaundice (yellow), erythema (redness), pigmentation changes, vitiligo (depigmentation).
- **Temperature**: warm (infection, inflammation), cold (ischaemia).
- **Moisture**: dry, oily, sweaty.
- **Texture and turgor**: smooth vs rough; pinch the skin — slow return indicates dehydration.
- **Lesions**: describe using the correct terminology below.

**Primary lesions** (arise from normal skin):

| Lesion | Description | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Macule | Flat, colour change, <1 cm | Freckle, vitiligo |
| Patch | Flat, colour change, >1 cm | Port-wine stain |
| Papule | Raised, solid, <1 cm | Acne lesion, wart |
| Plaque | Raised, flat-topped, >1 cm | Psoriasis |
| Nodule | Raised, solid, >1 cm, deeper | Lipoma, rheumatoid nodule |
| Vesicle | Fluid-filled blister, <1 cm | Chickenpox, herpes |
| Bulla | Fluid-filled blister, >1 cm | Pemphigus, second-degree burn |
| Pustule | Pus-filled | Acne, folliculitis |
| Wheal (urtica) | Raised, transient, irregular edge | Urticaria |
| Cyst | Encapsulated, fluid/semisolid | Sebaceous cyst |

**Secondary lesions** (follow primary lesions or treatment): scales (psoriasis), crusts (impetigo), erosion, ulcer, fissure, scar, lichenification (thickened skin from chronic scratching).

### Diagnostic tests
Skin biopsy (histology), scraping for microscopy and culture (fungal/bacterial), patch test (contact allergy), Tzanck smear (viral), Wood's lamp (fluorescence in tinea/pityriasis), allergy scratch/prick test, CBC and immunology if systemic disease suspected.

## 7.3 Eczema (Dermatitis)

**Eczema** is a chronic, itching, inflammatory skin condition. It is not contagious.

**Types**

| Type | Trigger / feature | Key nursing point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Atopic eczema | Genetic; associated with asthma, hay fever; common in children | Avoid triggers, emollients, avoid scratching |
| Contact dermatitis (allergic) | Allergy to nickel, latex, cosmetics, plants | Identify and remove allergen; patch test |
| Contact dermatitis (irritant) | Soap, detergents, frequent hand-washing | Barrier cream, wear gloves |
| Seborrhoeic dermatitis | Yeast (Malassezia); affects scalp, face, chest | Antifungal shampoo, mild steroid cream |
| Nummular (discoid) eczema | Coin-shaped patches; dry skin, cold weather | Emollients, mild steroids |
| Stasis (varicose) eczema | Venous insufficiency; lower legs | Compression bandaging, treat venous disease |

**Features**: intense itching (pruritus), redness, vesicles (which weep and crust), lichenification from chronic scratching.

**Management**
- Identify and remove triggers.
- Emollients (moisturisers) — apply liberally and frequently; never use soap.
- Topical corticosteroids (mild for face/skin folds, moderate/strong for body) during flares; apply thinly.
- Topical calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus, pimecrolimus) for sensitive areas.
- Antihistamines for sleep disturbance.
- Systemic steroids or immunosuppressants (cyclosporin, methotrexate) for severe cases.
- Wet dressings to soothe acute weeping lesions.

**Nursing care**: keep nails short and clean, cool the environment, cotton clothing, avoid known triggers, teach proper application of emollients and steroids (finger-tip unit), avoid abrupt steroid withdrawal, monitor for secondary bacterial infection (Staphylococcus aureus, impetigo).

## 7.4 Psoriasis

**Psoriasis** is a chronic, immune-mediated disorder with accelerated keratinocyte turnover (normal 28 days → 3–4 days) causing thick silvery-white scales on erythematous plaques.

**Features**
- Well-demarcated erythematous plaques with silvery scales; most common on elbows, knees, scalp and lower back.
- **Auspitz sign**: tiny bleeding points when scale is lifted.
- **Koebner phenomenon**: new plaques appear at sites of skin injury (scratch, tattoo, surgery).
- **Nail changes**: pitting, onycholysis (nail lifting), oil-drop sign.
- **Psoriatic arthritis** in 20–30%: affects joints, can cause deformity.

**Types**: plaque (most common), guttate (drop-like after Strep throat, common in children), inverse (skin folds), erythrodermic (whole body, emergency), pustular.

**Management**
- **Topical**: coal tar, dithranol, calcipotriol (vitamin D analogue), topical steroids, retinoids.
- **Phototherapy**: narrowband UVB (NB-UVB), PUVA (psoralen + UVA).
- **Systemic**: methotrexate, cyclosporin, acitretin (retinoid).
- **Biologics**: TNF inhibitors (etanercept, adalimumab), IL-17 inhibitors (secukinumab) for severe disease.

**Nursing care**: apply thick emollients to soften scales before treatment, teach trigger avoidance (stress, infections, certain drugs — beta-blockers, lithium, NSAIDs, antimalarials), reassure that it is not contagious, support with psychosocial care (poor body image is common), monitor for side effects of methotrexate (liver, bone marrow — regular LFT and CBC).

## 7.5 Urticaria (Hives)

**Urticaria** is a superficial skin oedema from mast-cell histamine release, producing transient wheals (hives) and surrounding flare that disappear within 24 hours. **Angioedema** is deeper swelling of dermis/subcutis, commonly around eyes and lips; involvement of the larynx is life-threatening.

**Causes**: foods (nuts, shellfish, eggs, strawberries), drugs (penicillin, aspirin, NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors), latex, insect stings, infections, cold, pressure, exercise, idiopathic (most chronic urticaria).

**Management**
- Identify and avoid trigger.
- Non-sedating antihistamines (cetirizine, levocetirizine, fexofenadine) — first line, taken regularly not just when itchy.
- Sedating antihistamines (chlorpheniramine) for night-time relief.
- Short course of systemic corticosteroids for severe acute episodes.
- **Anaphylaxis / severe angioedema** (laryngeal oedema, bronchospasm, hypotension): adrenaline (epinephrine) 0.5 mg IM immediately (outer thigh), call emergency, oxygen, IV antihistamine, IV corticosteroid, monitor airway.

**Nursing**: teach patient to carry an adrenaline auto-injector (EpiPen) if at risk of anaphylaxis, wear a MedicAlert bracelet, recognise early warning signs.

## 7.6 Acne Vulgaris

**Acne** is a chronic disorder of the pilosebaceous unit (hair follicle + sebaceous gland), most common in adolescents.

**Pathogenesis**: androgen stimulation → increased sebum → Propionibacterium acnes (Cutibacterium acnes) colonises → inflammatory response → open comedones (blackheads, pore is open, sebum oxidises black), closed comedones (whiteheads), papules, pustules, nodules, cysts → scarring.

**Management** (stepwise by severity)

| Severity | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Mild (comedones, few papules) | Topical benzoyl peroxide, topical retinoids (adapalene), topical clindamycin |
| Moderate (papules, pustules) | As above + oral antibiotics (doxycycline, erythromycin 6–8 weeks) |
| Severe/nodular/cystic | Oral isotretinoin (retinoid — very effective, teratogenic: mandatory contraception in females, monthly LFT and lipids) |
| In females with hormonal acne | Combined oral contraceptive pill, anti-androgens (spironolactone) |

**Nursing care**: wash face gently twice daily with a mild soap-free cleanser, do not squeeze lesions (drives infection deeper, causes scars), apply topical treatment as a thin layer over the whole area not just on spots, sun protection with isotretinoin and topical retinoids, support self-image in adolescents.

## 7.7 Skin infections

**Bacterial**
- **Impetigo**: very contagious superficial infection (Staphylococcus, Streptococcus); honey-coloured crusts around mouth in children; topical mupirocin or oral antibiotics (flucloxacillin); separate towels and clothing.
- **Cellulitis**: spreading infection of dermis and subcutaneous tissue (Streptococcus, Staphylococcus); red, warm, swollen, tender skin, often on a leg; fever, lymphangitis; IV penicillin or cephalosporin; mark the edge to monitor spread; elevate the limb.
- **Erysipelas**: superficial cellulitis with well-defined raised edge; treated the same way.
- **Folliculitis**: infection of hair follicles; furuncle (boil) is a deep infection; carbuncle is confluent boils; topical antiseptic or antibiotics; warm compresses; incision and drainage for fluctuant boils; avoid shaving over affected area.

**Fungal (Tinea / Dermatophytosis)**
- Tinea pedis (athlete's foot), tinea corporis (ringworm), tinea capitis (scalp), tinea unguium (onychomycosis).
- Diagnosis: Wood's lamp (some species fluoresce), KOH scraping (fungal hyphae).
- Treatment: topical antifungals (clotrimazole, miconazole) for body/foot; oral antifungals (terbinafine, fluconazole) for scalp or nails; keep skin dry, cotton socks, shared footwear avoided.

**Viral**
- **Herpes zoster (shingles)**: reactivation of varicella-zoster virus in a dermatomal strip (unilateral); burning pain, then vesicular rash; oral aciclovir within 72 h reduces severity; analgesics; post-herpetic neuralgia; contact precautions.
- **Warts (verrucae)**: HPV; treated with salicylic acid, cryotherapy, electrocautery; will often clear spontaneously.
- **Molluscum contagiosum**: poxvirus; pearly, umbilicated papules; resolves without treatment.

**Infestations**
- **Scabies**: Sarcoptes scabiei mite burrowing into skin; intense itching worse at night; linear burrows between fingers, wrists, genitalia; treat the whole family simultaneously with permethrin 5% cream head to toe for 8–12 hours; wash all clothes and bedding at 60 °C; treat close contacts.
- **Pediculosis (lice)**: head, body, pubic; malathion or permethrin shampoo; fine comb; treat household contacts.

## 7.8 Pressure ulcers (Pressure injuries)

A **pressure ulcer** (decubitus ulcer, bed sore) is localised injury to skin and underlying tissue, usually over a bony prominence, from sustained pressure or pressure combined with shear/friction.

**Risk factors**: immobility, poor nutrition (low protein), incontinence (moisture), altered sensation (spinal injury, neuropathy), low blood pressure, old age, oedema.

**Assessment tool — Braden Scale** (6 categories each scored 1–4; lowest score = highest risk):
Sensory perception, Moisture, Activity, Mobility, Nutrition, Friction and shear. Score ≤18 = at risk; ≤12 = very high risk.

**Common sites**: sacrum, heels, ischial tuberosities, trochanters, occiput, medial malleoli, elbows.

**Staging (NPUAP/EPUAP)**

| Stage | Features |
| --- | --- |
| Stage 1 | Non-blanchable erythema; skin intact; may be warmer/firmer |
| Stage 2 | Partial-thickness loss of dermis; shallow open ulcer; pink wound bed, blister or serosanguineous wound |
| Stage 3 | Full-thickness skin loss into subcutaneous tissue; fat may be visible; no bone, tendon or muscle exposed |
| Stage 4 | Full-thickness loss with exposed bone, tendon or muscle; slough or eschar may be present |
| Unstageable | Full-thickness loss; slough or eschar obscures depth |
| Deep tissue injury | Purple/maroon localised area; intact or open blister; damaged by intense pressure/shear |

**Prevention** — the 5 Ps:
1. **Position change** every 2 hours; use a turning schedule; head of bed <30° unless contraindicated.
2. **Pressure-relieving surfaces**: special foam mattress, alternating-pressure mattress, heel offloading.
3. **Proper skin care**: keep skin dry but not dry enough to crack; barrier cream; moisture-wicking pads for incontinence.
4. **Protein and nutrition**: adequate protein (1.2–1.5 g/kg/day), vitamin C and zinc for healing; dietitian referral.
5. **Patient and family education**: the causes, signs and reporting.

**Treatment**
- Remove pressure completely.
- Wound assessment: dimensions, bed colour (red = healthy granulation; yellow = slough; black = eschar), odour, exudate, undermining.
- Wound cleaning: normal saline or clean running water; avoid antiseptics (cytotoxic to healing tissue).
- Dressings: hydrocolloid (moist healing, Stage 2), foam (exudate absorption), alginate (heavy exudate), silver dressings (infection), vacuum-assisted closure (Stage 3–4).
- Debridement of slough/eschar: sharp, enzymatic or autolytic.
- Treat infection: systemic antibiotics if signs of cellulitis/osteomyelitis.
- Nutritional support.
- Surgical closure (flap) for large deep ulcers.

## 7.9 Burns

### Classification of burns

**By depth**

| Depth | Old name | Features | Healing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Superficial (epidermal) | First degree | Red, dry, painful; epidermis only | 3–5 days; no scar |
| Superficial partial-thickness | Second degree (superficial) | Red/pink, moist, blistered, very painful | 10–14 days; minimal scar |
| Deep partial-thickness | Second degree (deep) | Pale/white/mottled, less painful (nerve damage), dry/wet | 3–8 weeks; scarring |
| Full-thickness | Third degree | White/grey/leathery/charred; painless (nerves destroyed) | Needs grafting; scar |
| Full-thickness + underlying tissues | Fourth degree | Bone, tendon, muscle involved | Needs reconstruction |

**By extent — Rule of Nines (adults)**

```text
              HEAD & NECK = 9%
           ┌──────────────────┐
           │      9%          │
      ─────┤                  ├─────
ARM 9% │  │   TRUNK          │  │ ARM 9%
       │  │  Front 18%       │  │
       │  │  Back  18%       │  │
      ─────┤                  ├─────
           │                  │
           │  PERINEUM = 1%   │
      ─────┴──────────────────┴─────
   LEG 18%                    LEG 18%
  (front 9%                 (front 9%
   back 9%)                  back 9%)
─────────────────────────────────────
Total body surface area (TBSA) = 100%
```

Children use the **Lund–Browder chart** (more accurate because head is proportionally larger in children).

**Palm method**: patient's palm (fingers together) = approximately 1% TBSA; useful for scattered burns.

### Burn first aid
1. Remove from source; stop, drop and roll for flames.
2. Cool the burn: running cool (not cold/iced) water for 20 minutes; do not apply butter, toothpaste or ice.
3. Remove jewellery and loose clothing near the burn (not stuck clothing).
4. Cover with clean cling film or a non-fluffy dressing.
5. Keep the patient warm (hypothermia risk).
6. Refer to burns unit if: >10% TBSA (adults), >5% TBSA (children/elderly), full-thickness, face/hands/feet/genitalia/major joints, electrical/chemical/inhalation burns.

### Acute burn management (the 4 Rs)

**1. Resuscitation**
- **Parkland formula**: 4 mL × weight (kg) × %TBSA burned of Hartmann's (Ringer's lactate) in the first 24 hours; give half in the first 8 hours (counted from time of burn), the rest over the next 16 hours.
- Monitor urine output (target 0.5–1 mL/kg/h via catheter); adjust fluids accordingly.
- Nil by mouth initially (ileus), then nasogastric tube feeding.

**2. Resuscitation of the airway**
- Suspect inhalation injury: singed nasal hairs, hoarse voice, stridor, carbonaceous sputum, burns inside mouth.
- Airway must be secured early — oedema worsens in first 6–8 hours; intubation before the airway closes.
- 100% oxygen for suspected carbon monoxide poisoning; Carboxyhaemoglobin test.

**3. Wound care**
- Clean gently with chlorhexidine or mild soap and water; remove blisters (deep ones only).
- Topical antimicrobials: silver sulfadiazine 1% cream, silver-containing dressings (moist wound healing, reduce infection).
- **Escharotomy**: incision through eschar of circumferential full-thickness burns of limbs or chest to relieve compartment syndrome and allow chest movement.
- **Skin grafting**: split-thickness skin graft (STSG) — donor site taken with a dermatome; graft fixed on debrided wound; cultured skin or artificial dermis (Integra) for large burns.

**4. Rehabilitation**
- Early splinting and positioning to prevent contractures.
- Physiotherapy and occupational therapy.
- Pressure garments (worn 23 h/day for 1–2 years) to minimise hypertrophic scarring.
- Psychological support — burns cause major body image change, PTSD, depression.
- Nutritional support: high protein, high calorie (burns are hypermetabolic).

### Nursing care plan for a burn patient

| Problem | Nursing goal | Interventions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Impaired skin integrity | Wound healing; prevent infection | Aseptic wound care, topical antimicrobials, secure dressings, monitor for wound infection (fever, purulent discharge, cellulitis) |
| Risk for fluid volume deficit | Adequate hydration | Maintain Parkland resuscitation, accurate intake-output, hourly urine output, daily weight, IV access (large-bore) |
| Risk for infection | Prevent sepsis | Strict asepsis, barrier nursing, barrier cream, monitor wound and blood cultures, antibiotics as ordered |
| Acute pain | Pain control | Analgesia (opioids IV for major burns), pre-medicate before dressing changes, position of comfort, diversion |
| Impaired nutrition | Adequate calories and protein | NG tube feeding, dietitian input, high-protein high-calorie diet, vitamin C and zinc supplementation |
| Risk for contracture | Maintain range of movement | Splinting and positioning (see physiotherapy), early ROM exercises, pressure garments |
| Body image disturbance | Positive self-concept | Acknowledge feelings, involve in care, refer to support group and counsellor, gradual mirror exposure |

## 7.10 Skin cancers

| Type | Key features | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basal cell carcinoma (BCC) | Commonest; pearly nodule with rolled edges, telangiectasia; sun-exposed areas; local invasion only, rarely metastasises | Surgery, radiotherapy, topical imiquimod or 5-FU for superficial type |
| Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) | Second commonest; arises in sun-damaged/scarred skin or actinic keratosis; can metastasise | Surgery, radiotherapy, Mohs for facial lesions |
| Malignant melanoma | Most dangerous; arises from melanocytes; ABCDEs: Asymmetry, irregular Border, Colour variation, Diameter >6 mm, Evolution; spreads early via lymph and blood | Wide local excision + sentinel lymph node biopsy, immunotherapy (nivolumab), targeted therapy (BRAF inhibitors) |

**Sun protection teaching**: SPF 30+, broad-spectrum sunscreen, protective clothing, avoid midday sun, examine skin monthly (ABCDE), report changes to a doctor.

## 7.11 Remember

- Skin layers: epidermis (avascular) → dermis (vascular, nerves, glands) → hypodermis (fat).
- Pressure ulcer prevention: 2-hourly turns, pressure-relieving mattress, adequate protein.
- Burns rule of nines: head 9%, each arm 9%, trunk front 18%, trunk back 18%, each leg 18%, perineum 1%.
- Parkland formula: 4 mL × kg × %TBSA burned of Hartmann's in 24 hours; half in first 8 hours.
- Full-thickness burn: painless (nerve ends destroyed), waxy/leathery/charred; needs grafting.
- Anaphylaxis: adrenaline 0.5 mg IM first, then antihistamine + steroid.
- Psoriasis: Koebner phenomenon, Auspitz sign, silvery plaque; not infectious.
- Atopic eczema: itchy rash in flexures; associated with asthma and hay fever.
- Scabies: itching worst at night; treat whole family and wash all bedding.

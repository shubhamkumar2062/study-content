# Last-night revision sheet

Adult Health Nursing I is a 75-mark end-semester paper (20 MCQ + 2 × 10 long essays + 7 × 5 short answers). If you have one hour, work through the comparison tables and procedure sequences below.

---

**Wound healing phases**

| Phase | Timing | Key events |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Haemostasis | Minutes | Vasoconstriction → platelet plug → clot |
| Inflammatory | Days 1–5 | Vasodilation, WBC migration, wound debridement; red, warm, swollen |
| Proliferative | Days 6 – weeks 3 | Granulation tissue, fibroplasia, angiogenesis, wound contraction, epithelialisation |
| Remodelling | 3 weeks – 2 years | Collagen remodelling, scar matures; tensile strength peaks at ~80% of normal |

First intention (sutured clean wound) → thin scar. Second intention (open wound) → granulation → larger scar.

---

**Burns**

Rule of nines (adult): head + neck = 9%, each arm = 9%, anterior trunk = 18%, posterior trunk = 18%, each leg = 18%, perineum = 1%. Children: use Lund–Browder (head is larger).

| Depth | Features | Healing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Superficial (1st degree) | Red, dry, painful | 3–5 days, no scar |
| Superficial partial (2nd, superficial) | Pink, blistered, very painful | 10–14 days |
| Deep partial (2nd, deep) | Pale/white, less painful | 3–8 weeks, scarring |
| Full thickness (3rd degree) | Waxy/leathery/charred, **painless** | Needs skin graft |

**Parkland formula**: 4 mL × kg × %TBSA of Hartmann's in 24 h. Half in first 8 hours (from time of burn), half over next 16 hours. Target urine output: 0.5–1 mL/kg/h.

**Pressure ulcer stages (NPUAP)**

| Stage | Features |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Non-blanchable erythema, skin intact |
| 2 | Partial-thickness; shallow open ulcer / blister |
| 3 | Full-thickness into subcutaneous fat; no exposed bone |
| 4 | Full-thickness with exposed bone, tendon or muscle |

Prevention — 5 Ps: Position (2-hourly turns), Pressure-relieving mattress, Proper skin care, Protein (nutrition), Patient education. Braden score ≤18 = at risk.

**Fracture healing stages**

```text
Haematoma (hours) → Soft callus (1–2 weeks) → Hard callus (3–6 weeks) → Remodelling (months–years)
```

Compartment syndrome: pain unrelieved by analgesia + tense limb + paraesthesia = **emergency** → bivalve cast → surgeon → fasciotomy.

5 Ps neurovascular check: **P**ain, **P**ulse, **P**allor, **P**araesthesia, **P**aralysis. Check every 1–2 hours post-fracture/cast.

**RA vs OA**

| Feature | Rheumatoid arthritis | Osteoarthritis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanism | Autoimmune synovitis | Cartilage degeneration |
| Joints | Symmetric; small joints (MCP, PIP, wrists) | Asymmetric; weight-bearing (knee, hip) |
| Morning stiffness | >1 hour | <30 minutes |
| Systemic features | Yes (fatigue, anaemia, nodules) | No |
| RF | Positive (~70%) | Negative |
| Treatment | DMARDs (methotrexate) | NSAIDs, physiotherapy, weight loss |

**HIV stages**

| Stage | CD4 | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary infection | Variable | Seroconversion illness |
| Asymptomatic | >500 → falling | No symptoms; contagious |
| Symptomatic | 350–200 | Oral candida, shingles, weight loss |
| AIDS | <200 | PCP, cryptococcal meningitis, Kaposi's sarcoma |

ART drug classes: NRTIs (tenofovir/3TC), NNRTIs (efavirenz), PIs (lopinavir), INSTIs (dolutegravir). India first line: TLD (tenofovir + lamivudine + dolutegravir). U = U (undetectable = untransmittable).

Needle-stick: wash + report + PEP within **72 hours**.

**TB reminders**

Organism: *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, AFB, airborne transmission.
Diagnosis: sputum AFB smear × 3, Xpert MTB/RIF, chest X-ray (upper lobe cavitation), Mantoux (>10 mm = positive for most).
Regimen: **2HRZE/4HR** under DOTS. Rifampicin → orange urine/tears (normal, warn patient). Isoniazid → give pyridoxine to prevent neuropathy. Ethambutol → monthly vision check.
Isolation: airborne precautions (N95 for staff, surgical mask on patient).

**Hepatitis comparison**

| Type | Route | Chronic? | Vaccine? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Faeco-oral | No | Yes |
| B | Blood, sexual, MTCT | Yes (~10% adults) | Yes (3-dose) |
| C | Blood | Yes (~80%) | No (treat with DAAs) |
| D | Blood (needs HBV) | Yes | HBV vaccine covers D |
| E | Faeco-oral | No (usually) | Yes (India) |

HBV vaccine: birth, 6 w, 10 w, 14 w in India. HBsAg+ mother: baby gets HBV vaccine + HBIG within 12 hours.

**BCLS sequence**

```text
1. Check safety
2. Unresponsive? Shout for help
3. Call emergency services / activate code
4. Check pulse (<10 seconds)
5. 30 compressions (rate 100–120/min, depth 5–6 cm, full recoil)
6. 2 rescue breaths (or compression-only if untrained)
7. AED as soon as available → analyse → shock if advised
Continue until ROSC, AED takes over, or told to stop
```

**MONA for MI**: Morphine (pain), Oxygen (SpO2 <94%), Nitrates (sublingual), Aspirin (300 mg stat).

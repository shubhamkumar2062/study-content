# Unit 2: Intraoperative care and anaesthesia

This notes file covers INC Unit II (Intraoperative care, 15 hours). It deals with the operation theatre (OT), the OT team, positioning and draping, instruments and sutures, sterilisation, scrubbing, monitoring, safety, anaesthesia and legal aspects.

## 2.1 Organisation and physical set-up of the operation theatre

### Classification of operation theatres

- **By type of surgery**: general, orthopaedic, cardiothoracic, neurosurgical, obstetric and gynaecological, eye, ENT, emergency (trauma) theatre, day-care theatre.
- **By cleanliness of procedures**: clean (e.g. hernia repair), clean-contaminated (e.g. gall bladder, bowel opened under control), contaminated (e.g. fresh trauma, spillage from bowel), dirty/infected (e.g. abscess, perforated bowel). Dirty cases are often listed last or done in a separate theatre.
- **By design**: single-theatre unit or a theatre complex with several theatres sharing common areas.

### OT design — the zones

The OT complex is planned so that people and articles move from clean to cleaner areas and never back the wrong way.

| Zone | Areas | Who and what |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Protective (outer) zone** | Reception, waiting, changing rooms, stores, patient transfer area | Street clothes allowed up to the changing room |
| **Clean zone** | Pre-operative (holding) area, recovery room, staff rest room, corridors | Theatre clothes required |
| **Aseptic (sterile) zone** | Operating rooms, scrub area, sterile stores, set-up room | Theatre clothes, cap, mask; scrubbed staff only near the sterile field |
| **Disposal zone** | Dirty utility, sluice, waste and linen collection | Used items leave by a separate route |

**Features of the operating room**

- Away from main traffic; near surgical wards, ICU, blood bank, central sterile supply department (CSSD), radiology.
- Smooth, washable, non-porous walls and floors with rounded corners; anti-static flooring.
- Positive-pressure ventilation with filtered air (HEPA filters in many theatres) and frequent air changes; air flows from clean areas out to less clean areas.
- Temperature about 20–24 °C and relative humidity about 50–60% (check your hospital standard).
- Shadowless operating lamp; emergency power supply.
- Doors kept closed; minimum movement of staff.

### Staffing and members of the OT team

| Sterile (scrubbed) members | Non-sterile (unscrubbed) members |
| --- | --- |
| Surgeon | Anaesthetist (anaesthesiologist) |
| Assistant surgeon | Circulating nurse |
| Scrub nurse (instrument nurse) | Anaesthesia technician, OT technician, perfusionist (cardiac surgery), radiographer, attendants, housekeeping |

OT nursing staff include the OT in-charge (nursing sister), scrub nurses, circulating nurses, recovery-room nurses and CSSD staff.

## 2.2 Duties and responsibilities of the nurse in the OT

### Scrub nurse

1. Prepares the instrument trolley and sterile field before the case.
2. Scrubs, gowns and gloves; helps the surgeon to gown and glove.
3. Counts swabs (sponges), needles and instruments with the circulating nurse **before the first incision, before closing a cavity, before skin closure and at the end**.
4. Passes instruments, sutures and swabs to the surgeon in the correct way (handle first, ready for use).
5. Anticipates the surgeon's needs and keeps the field tidy and sterile.
6. Takes care of specimens handed over.
7. Applies the dressing at the end and removes used instruments safely.

### Circulating nurse

1. Checks the OT (cleaning, equipment, suction, diathermy, light) before the case.
2. Receives the patient and checks identity, consent, site marking, nil-by-mouth status, allergies, investigations, prosthesis and jewellery removed.
3. Helps the anaesthetist and helps to position the patient.
4. Opens sterile packs for the scrub nurse; ties gowns.
5. Counts with the scrub nurse and records the counts.
6. Labels and sends specimens.
7. Watches for breaks in asepsis; controls traffic and noise.
8. Records the intra-operative notes and leads parts of the surgical safety checklist.
9. Transfers the patient to the recovery room with a handover.

### Scrub nurse vs circulating nurse

| Point | Scrub nurse | Circulating nurse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sterility | Sterile (scrubbed) | Not sterile |
| Works within | Sterile field | Whole room, outside the sterile field |
| Main task | Instruments, sutures, swabs to surgeon | Supplies, patient safety, records, coordination |
| Counts | Shares counts | Shares counts and records them |

## 2.3 WHO surgical safety checklist

The checklist has three parts:

| Phase | When | Main checks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sign in** | Before induction of anaesthesia | Identity, site, procedure and consent confirmed; site marked; anaesthesia machine and medicines checked; pulse oximeter on; known allergy; difficult airway or aspiration risk; risk of blood loss |
| **Time out** | Before skin incision | Team members introduce themselves; confirm patient, site and procedure; antibiotic given in time if needed; critical events reviewed; essential imaging displayed |
| **Sign out** | Before the patient leaves the theatre | Name of procedure recorded; instrument, swab and needle counts correct; specimen labelled; equipment problems noted; key concerns for recovery handed over |

The nurse documents and signs the checklist.

## 2.4 Positions and draping for common surgical procedures

| Position | How | Used for | Nursing precautions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Supine (dorsal recumbent)** | On the back, arms on arm boards (less than 90°) or at the side | Abdominal, chest, many others | Pad heels and back of head; arms not beyond 90° (brachial plexus injury) |
| **Trendelenburg** | Supine, head lower than feet | Pelvic and lower abdominal surgery | Watch breathing (abdominal organs press on diaphragm) |
| **Reverse Trendelenburg** | Head higher than feet | Upper abdominal, head and neck surgery | Footboard to stop sliding |
| **Lithotomy** | Supine, legs raised and abducted in stirrups | Perineal, vaginal, rectal, cystoscopy | Raise and lower **both legs together** slowly; pad the knees (peroneal nerve); watch BP when lowering |
| **Prone** | Face down, chest supported | Spine, back of legs, buttocks | Protect eyes, face and breasts; chest rolls for breathing; arms carefully placed |
| **Lateral (Sims' / kidney)** | On the side; kidney bridge raised | Kidney, chest (thoracotomy), hip | Pillow between legs; axillary roll; secure with straps |
| **Sitting / Fowler's** | Sitting up | Some neck, shoulder and brain surgery | Watch for low BP and air embolism |
| **Jack-knife (Kraske)** | Prone, hips raised, head and feet down | Anal and rectal surgery (piles, fissure) | Pad pressure points; watch breathing |

General rules: enough staff to move the patient; keep body alignment; pad bony points; avoid pressure on nerves and eyes; keep limbs secure; expose only the area needed; keep the patient warm.

**Draping**: sterile drapes cover the patient leaving only the operation site exposed. The scrub nurse hands drapes folded so they can be opened without touching unsterile areas; a drape once placed is not moved towards the incision.

## 2.5 Instruments, sutures and equipment

### Basic instrument groups

| Group | Examples | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cutting | Scalpel (Bard-Parker handle with blades), scissors (Mayo, Metzenbaum) | Cut tissue and sutures |
| Grasping and holding | Tissue forceps (toothed and non-toothed), Allis, Babcock, Kocher's | Hold tissue |
| Clamping (haemostats) | Artery forceps (straight and curved, mosquito) | Stop bleeding |
| Retracting | Langenbeck, Deaver, Morris, self-retaining retractors | Hold wound open |
| Suturing | Needle holder, needles | Sew tissue |
| Suction | Yankauer, Poole suction tips | Remove blood and fluid |
| Others | Sponge-holding forceps, towel clips, probes, dilators | Various |

### Sutures and suture materials

| Classification | Examples | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Absorbable** | Catgut (now rarely used), polyglactin (Vicryl), polyglycolic acid, polydioxanone (PDS) | Broken down in the body; used for deeper layers and bowel |
| **Non-absorbable** | Silk, nylon, polypropylene (Prolene), stainless steel wire | Stay permanently or are removed (skin) |
| Monofilament | Nylon, polypropylene | Less tissue drag, less infection |
| Multifilament (braided) | Silk, polyglactin | Easy to handle, knot well |

Other closure methods: staples, skin adhesives, adhesive strips.

**Needles**: round-bodied (for soft tissue like bowel), cutting and reverse-cutting (for skin and tough tissue); straight or curved; eyed or eyeless (swaged, suture fused to needle).

Suture size: the more zeros, the finer the thread (e.g. 6-0 is finer than 2-0).

### Equipment

Operating table, shadowless lamp, anaesthesia machine, suction machine, electrosurgical (diathermy) unit, monitors (ECG, pulse oximetry, BP, capnography), defibrillator, warming blanket, tourniquet, image intensifier.

**Diathermy safety**: place the patient (return) plate on a clean, dry, hairless, fleshy area close to the site; patient must not touch metal; no pooled spirit-based skin preparation under the patient (fire risk).

## 2.6 Disinfection and sterilisation of equipment

- **Sterilisation**: kills all micro-organisms including spores.
- **Disinfection**: kills most micro-organisms but not necessarily spores.
- **Spaulding classification**: critical items (enter sterile tissue — instruments) need sterilisation; semi-critical (touch mucous membrane — endoscopes) need high-level disinfection at least; non-critical (touch intact skin — BP cuff) need cleaning and low-level disinfection.

| Method | Examples / notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Steam under pressure (autoclave)** | Most common for instruments, linen, gloves; common cycles are 121 °C for about 15–30 minutes or 134 °C for a shorter time, as per the machine's validated cycle |
| Dry heat (hot-air oven) | Glassware, powders, oils |
| Ethylene oxide gas | Heat-sensitive items (plastics); items must be aerated afterwards |
| Hydrogen peroxide plasma | Heat-sensitive items; no toxic residue |
| Chemical (liquid) | Glutaraldehyde, ortho-phthalaldehyde (high-level disinfection of scopes) |
| Radiation (gamma rays) | Used by manufacturers for disposable items |

**Steps before sterilisation**: decontaminate → clean thoroughly (dirt protects organisms) → dry → inspect → pack → sterilise → store.

**Indicators**: physical (time, temperature, pressure gauges), chemical (autoclave tape and strips change colour), biological (spores, the most reliable test).

**Cleaning the OT**: damp dusting before the first case; cleaning between cases; terminal cleaning at the end of the day; weekly thorough cleaning; fumigation or other approved methods as per hospital policy.

## 2.7 Preparation of sets for common surgical procedures

A **set** (tray) is a pack of instruments needed for one procedure, wrapped, labelled and sterilised.

| Set | Typical contents |
| --- | --- |
| Basic minor set | Scalpel handle and blades, scissors, toothed and non-toothed forceps, artery forceps, needle holder, sponge holders, towel clips, gallipots, swabs |
| Laparotomy set | Basic set plus long instruments, abdominal retractors (Deaver, Morris, self-retaining), intestinal clamps, Babcock and Allis forceps, suction tip, large swabs (mops) |
| Appendicectomy, hernia sets | Basic set plus appropriate retractors and clamps |

Every set has a list inside so that counting and checking are easy.

## 2.8 Scrubbing, gowning, masking and gloving

### Surgical hand scrub (steps with rationale)

1. Put on cap (all hair covered) and mask (over nose and mouth) before scrubbing. — *Hair and droplets carry bacteria.*
2. Remove rings and watch; nails short, no nail polish or artificial nails. — *Organisms collect under rings and nails.*
3. Adjust water; wet hands and forearms, keeping **hands higher than elbows**. — *Water runs from the cleanest area (hands) to less clean (elbows).*
4. Clean under nails with a nail cleaner (first scrub of the day).
5. Scrub with antiseptic solution (e.g. chlorhexidine or povidone-iodine) each finger (four sides), palm, back of hand, forearm up to 5 cm above the elbow, for the time set by hospital policy (commonly about 3–5 minutes). Alcohol-based surgical hand rub is an approved alternative in many hospitals. — *Removes dirt and reduces skin flora.*
6. Rinse from fingertips to elbow without touching the tap or sink.
7. Enter the theatre backing through the door with hands held up in front, away from the body.
8. Dry with a sterile towel: one end for one hand and arm from fingers to elbow, the other end for the other arm.

### Gowning

Pick up the folded gown by the inside neckline, let it unfold away from the body, slip both arms into the sleeves; the circulating nurse ties the back. Only the front from chest to waist and the sleeves to about 5 cm above the elbow are considered sterile.

### Gloving

- **Closed gloving**: hands stay inside the gown cuffs while the gloves are put on — preferred, as bare hands never touch the outside of the gown or gloves.
- **Open gloving**: used for procedures like catheterisation or when changing a glove; the bare hand touches only the inside fold of the glove.

## 2.9 Monitoring the patient during the procedure

- Heart rate and ECG, blood pressure, oxygen saturation, end-tidal carbon dioxide (capnography), temperature.
- Breathing, airway pressure (ventilated patients).
- Blood loss: weigh swabs, measure suction contents.
- Urine output (if catheterised).
- Fluid and blood given.
- Position and pressure areas.
- Level of anaesthesia (by anaesthetist).

## 2.10 Maintaining a therapeutic environment in the OT

- Correct temperature and humidity; keep the patient warm (warm blankets, warmed fluids) to prevent hypothermia.
- Limit traffic; keep doors closed; minimise talking.
- Good lighting; quiet; respect for the unconscious patient's dignity.
- Proper waste disposal and cleaning between cases.

## 2.11 Assisting in major and minor operations; handling specimens

- Know the steps of the operation in order to anticipate needs.
- Pass instruments firmly into the surgeon's palm, in position of use.
- Keep sharps in a separate container; use a neutral (no-touch) zone for passing sharps.
- **Specimens**: receive in a container, label at once with name, identification number, specimen type and site, date; add the correct fixative (usually 10% formalin for histology), but send for culture or frozen section **fresh, without fixative**; fill request form; record in specimen register; hand over and get signature.

## 2.12 Prevention of accidents and hazards in the OT

| Hazard | Prevention |
| --- | --- |
| Wrong patient, wrong site, wrong procedure | Identification, site marking, surgical safety checklist |
| Retained swab or instrument | Counts, radio-opaque swabs |
| Burns (diathermy, fire) | Correct plate placement, let skin preparation dry, careful oxygen use |
| Falls from table | Safety straps, side supports, enough staff |
| Nerve injury, pressure injury | Proper positioning and padding |
| Electric shock | Earthing, equipment checks |
| Infection | Asepsis, sterilisation, traffic control |
| Needle-stick injuries to staff | No recapping, sharps containers, hands-free passing |
| Exposure to anaesthetic gases | Scavenging system |
| Radiation | Lead aprons, distance, thyroid shields |
| Hypothermia | Warming |
| Allergy (latex, drugs) | History, latex-free equipment |

## 2.13 Anaesthesia

**Anaesthesia** means loss of sensation, with or without loss of consciousness.

### Types and methods

| Type | Method | Examples / use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **General anaesthesia** | Unconsciousness, amnesia, analgesia, muscle relaxation | Inhalation agents, intravenous induction agents; airway by endotracheal tube or laryngeal mask airway |
| **Regional — spinal** | Local anaesthetic into the subarachnoid space (CSF) in the lumbar region | Lower abdomen, perineum, lower limbs, caesarean section |
| **Regional — epidural** | Into the epidural space; catheter allows top-ups | Labour analgesia, post-operative pain relief |
| **Regional — nerve block** | Around a nerve or plexus | Brachial plexus block for arm surgery |
| **Local infiltration** | Into the tissue at the site | Minor procedures, suturing |
| **Topical** | Spray, gel, drops on mucous membrane or eye | Endoscopy, eye procedures |
| **Monitored sedation (conscious sedation)** | Sedatives and analgesics, patient can respond | Endoscopy, minor procedures |

### Phases and stages of general anaesthesia

- **Phases**: induction → maintenance → emergence (recovery).
- **Classical stages (Guedel)**, seen most clearly with older inhalation agents:

| Stage | Name | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Analgesia | From induction to loss of consciousness; drowsy |
| II | Excitement (delirium) | Irregular breathing, struggling, vomiting possible — avoid stimulation |
| III | Surgical anaesthesia | Regular breathing, muscles relaxed, reflexes lost; surgery done in this stage |
| IV | Medullary paralysis (overdose) | Breathing stops, circulation fails — emergency |

### Equipment and drugs (classes only)

- **Equipment**: anaesthesia machine (gas supply, flowmeters, vaporiser), breathing circuits, face masks, laryngoscope, endotracheal tubes, laryngeal mask airways, suction, monitors, defibrillator, emergency trolley.
- **Drug classes**: pre-medication (sedatives, anticholinergics to dry secretions, antiemetics, antacids); intravenous induction agents; inhalation agents; opioid analgesics; neuromuscular blocking agents (muscle relaxants) and their reversal agents; local anaesthetics; emergency drugs (vasopressors, drugs for arrhythmias).

### Complications and nursing considerations

| Type | Complications | Nursing considerations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General | Airway obstruction, aspiration, laryngospasm, hypoxia, low BP, arrhythmias, nausea and vomiting, sore throat, hypothermia, malignant hyperthermia (rare: high fever, muscle rigidity) | Nil by mouth before surgery, airway care, monitor until awake, lateral position, warmth |
| Spinal | Fall in BP, headache after dural puncture, urinary retention, high block affecting breathing | Monitor BP; keep flat as per local practice; fluids; check return of sensation and movement; check bladder |
| Epidural | Low BP, catheter migration, infection | Monitor BP and level of block; care of catheter site |
| Local | Allergy, toxicity if absorbed in excess (tingling around mouth, ringing in ears, convulsions) | Observe and report |

## 2.14 Legal aspects of OT nursing

- **Informed consent** before the procedure (Unit 1); consent is specific to the procedure.
- Correct identification and site — failure is negligence.
- Retained foreign bodies, burns, falls and wrong-site surgery lead to legal action; counts and checklists protect patients and staff.
- Accurate and timely documentation: counts, specimens, times, drugs, implants, events.
- Specimen handling: correct labelling and chain of custody.
- Confidentiality and dignity of the unconscious patient.
- Incident reporting of any error or near miss.
- Following biomedical waste management rules for OT waste.

## Remember

- OT zones: protective → clean → aseptic → disposal; movement always from clean to cleaner.
- Positive-pressure filtered ventilation; doors closed; limited traffic.
- Scrub nurse is sterile and passes instruments; circulating nurse is not sterile and coordinates.
- Counts: before incision, before closing a cavity, before skin closure, at the end.
- WHO checklist: sign in, time out, sign out.
- Lithotomy: raise and lower both legs together.
- Scrub with hands above elbows; closed gloving preferred.
- Autoclave is the commonest method of sterilisation; biological indicators are the most reliable test.
- Histology specimens in formalin; culture specimens fresh.
- Guedel stage III = surgical anaesthesia.
- Spinal anaesthesia: watch for low BP, headache and urinary retention.

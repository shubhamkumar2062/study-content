# Unit 3: Pathogenic organisms: bacteria, viruses, fungi, parasites, rodents and vectors

Section A (Applied Microbiology), INC Unit III. For each group: characteristics, source, portal of entry, transmission and how the organism is identified. Learn the tables — short answers and MCQs come straight from them.

## 3.1 How pathogens cause disease (overview)

| Step | What happens | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry | Through respiratory tract, gut, skin breaks, genital tract, blood, placenta | Droplets, food, needle, sexual contact |
| Attachment and colonisation | Pili, capsule, adhesins | *E. coli* in the urinary tract |
| Invasion and spread | Enzymes (coagulase, hyaluronidase, streptokinase) | *Streptococcus pyogenes* spreads in cellulitis |
| Damage | Exotoxins, endotoxin, cell destruction, immune reaction | Tetanus toxin, cholera toxin, septic shock |
| Exit | Secretions, faeces, blood, lesions | Cough in TB, faeces in typhoid |

## 3.2 Gram-positive cocci

| Organism | Characteristics | Source and transmission | Diseases | Identification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ***Staphylococcus aureus*** | Cocci in grape-like clusters; non-motile; golden colonies; coagulase and catalase positive; tolerates salt and drying | Nose and skin of carriers (many people); hands of staff; contact, fomites | Boils, carbuncles, abscess, wound and surgical site infection, impetigo, osteomyelitis, pneumonia, septicaemia, food poisoning (toxin in food), toxic shock syndrome. **MRSA** = methicillin-resistant strains, a major hospital pathogen | Gram stain of pus, culture on blood agar (beta haemolysis), coagulase test, sensitivity test |
| *Staphylococcus epidermidis* | Coagulase negative; normal skin flora | Patient's own skin | Infections of IV catheters, prosthetic valves and joints | Culture of blood (more than one bottle positive) |
| ***Streptococcus pyogenes*** (group A, beta-haemolytic) | Cocci in chains; clear (beta) haemolysis on blood agar | Throat and skin; droplets, contact | Sore throat (pharyngitis), tonsillitis, scarlet fever, impetigo, cellulitis, erysipelas, puerperal sepsis. Late non-suppurative complications: **rheumatic fever** and **acute glomerulonephritis** | Throat swab culture; **ASO** titre for past infection |
| ***Streptococcus pneumoniae*** (pneumococcus) | Lancet-shaped Gram-positive diplococci; capsule; alpha (green) haemolysis | Upper respiratory tract; droplets | Lobar pneumonia, otitis media, sinusitis, meningitis | Sputum Gram stain and culture; urinary antigen; preventable by pneumococcal vaccine |
| *Streptococcus viridans* group | Alpha-haemolytic; mouth flora | Mouth (after dental procedures) | Infective endocarditis on damaged valves | Blood culture |
| *Enterococcus* | Gram-positive cocci in pairs/short chains; gut flora; hardy | Gut; hands, equipment | Urinary tract infection, wound infection, endocarditis; vancomycin-resistant enterococci (VRE) in hospitals | Culture |

## 3.3 Gram-negative cocci

| Organism | Characteristics | Transmission | Diseases | Identification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ***Neisseria meningitidis*** (meningococcus) | Kidney-shaped Gram-negative diplococci, often inside pus cells; capsule | Droplets from nasopharyngeal carriers; crowding | Meningococcal meningitis, septicaemia with purpuric rash | CSF Gram stain and culture on chocolate agar in CO2; antigen tests |
| ***Neisseria gonorrhoeae*** (gonococcus) | Kidney-shaped diplococci inside pus cells | Sexual contact; mother to baby during birth | Gonorrhoea (urethritis, cervicitis), pelvic inflammatory disease, **ophthalmia neonatorum** | Gram stain of urethral pus; culture on selective media |

## 3.4 Gram-positive bacilli

| Organism | Characteristics | Source and transmission | Disease | Identification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ***Corynebacterium diphtheriae*** | Club-shaped rods in "Chinese letter" pattern; metachromatic granules | Droplets from cases and carriers | **Diphtheria**: grey pseudomembrane in throat that can block the airway; exotoxin damages heart and nerves | Throat swab; Albert's stain; culture on Loeffler's serum slope and tellurite medium; toxin test |
| ***Clostridium tetani*** | Anaerobic, spore-forming; "drumstick" (terminal round spore) | Spores in soil, dust, animal dung; enter through wounds, the umbilical stump (unclean cord care) | **Tetanus**: exotoxin causes muscle spasm, lockjaw, risus sardonicus; neonatal tetanus | Mainly clinical diagnosis; prevented by tetanus toxoid (Td) and clean delivery |
| *Clostridium perfringens* | Anaerobic, spore-bearing, capsulate, non-motile | Soil, gut; deep crushed wounds | **Gas gangrene** (spreading muscle necrosis with gas); food poisoning | Gram stain of exudate; anaerobic culture |
| *Clostridium botulinum* | Anaerobic, spore-bearing | Toxin in badly preserved canned food | Botulism — paralysis | Toxin detection |
| ***Clostridioides difficile*** | Anaerobic, spore-bearing | Hospital environment, hands; spores survive alcohol rub | Antibiotic-associated diarrhoea and pseudomembranous colitis | Toxin test of stool; **soap-and-water hand washing** needed |
| *Bacillus anthracis* | Large rods in chains ("bamboo stick"); spores; capsule | Animals and their products (hides, wool) | Anthrax (skin, lung, gut) | Smear and culture |
| ***Mycobacterium tuberculosis*** | Slender acid-fast rods; aerobic; slow-growing; resists drying | Airborne droplet nuclei from open pulmonary cases; rarely unpasteurised milk (*M. bovis*) | **Tuberculosis** — lungs and any organ | Sputum ZN smear, cartridge-based molecular test (also detects rifampicin resistance), culture on Lowenstein–Jensen or liquid media (weeks), chest X-ray; Mantoux test (infection, not disease) |
| ***Mycobacterium leprae*** | Acid-fast; cannot be grown in artificial media | Prolonged close contact, nasal discharge of untreated cases | **Leprosy**: skin patches with loss of sensation, nerve thickening | Slit-skin smear (ZN with 5% H2SO4); clinical diagnosis |

## 3.5 Gram-negative bacilli

| Organism | Characteristics | Source and transmission | Diseases | Identification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ***Escherichia coli*** | Motile; lactose fermenter (pink on MacConkey); normal gut flora | Own gut flora; contaminated food and water | **Urinary tract infection** (most common cause), diarrhoea (travellers' and infant), neonatal meningitis, wound infection, septicaemia | Urine culture with colony count; stool culture |
| ***Klebsiella pneumoniae*** | Non-motile; large mucoid capsule; lactose fermenter | Gut and respiratory tract; hospital environment, hands | Pneumonia, UTI, neonatal sepsis; many hospital strains are multidrug resistant (ESBL, carbapenem-resistant) | Culture and sensitivity |
| ***Salmonella* Typhi and Paratyphi** | Motile; non-lactose fermenter | Faeco-oral — food and water contaminated by patients or carriers (gall bladder carriers); flies | **Enteric (typhoid) fever** — step-ladder fever, relative bradycardia, rose spots; complications: intestinal perforation and bleeding | **Blood culture** (best in the first week), stool and urine culture (later weeks), **Widal test** (antibodies, after the first week; interpret with care) |
| Other *Salmonella* | — | Poultry, eggs, meat | Food poisoning (gastroenteritis) | Stool culture |
| ***Shigella*** | Non-motile; non-lactose fermenter | Faeco-oral; very small infective dose; flies, fingers | **Bacillary dysentery** — frequent small stools with blood and mucus | Stool culture |
| ***Vibrio cholerae*** | Curved comma-shaped; very actively motile (darting); grows in alkaline media | Contaminated water and food | **Cholera** — profuse "rice-water" stools, severe dehydration | Hanging drop; stool culture in alkaline peptone water and TCBS agar; rapid tests |
| ***Pseudomonas aeruginosa*** | Motile; non-fermenter; blue-green pigment, grape-like smell; survives in moist places and some disinfectants | Sinks, humidifiers, wet equipment, hands | Burns and wound infection, ventilator-associated pneumonia, UTI with catheters; naturally resistant to many antibiotics | Culture |
| *Proteus* | Swarming motility; urease positive | Gut | UTI, kidney stones | Culture |
| *Acinetobacter baumannii* | Coccobacilli; survives long on dry surfaces | ICU environment, equipment, hands | Ventilator pneumonia, bloodstream and wound infections; often extensively drug-resistant | Culture |
| *Haemophilus influenzae* | Small coccobacilli; needs factors in blood (grows on chocolate agar) | Droplets | Meningitis and pneumonia in young children (type b, prevented by Hib in the pentavalent vaccine), epiglottitis | CSF culture |
| *Bordetella pertussis* | Small coccobacilli | Droplets | Whooping cough | Nasopharyngeal swab culture, PCR |
| *Helicobacter pylori* | Curved, microaerophilic | Faeco-oral / oral–oral | Gastritis, peptic ulcer | Urea breath test, biopsy |

## 3.6 Viruses

**Characteristics**

- Smallest infectious agents (about 20–300 nm); seen only with the electron microscope.
- Contain **only one kind of nucleic acid** — DNA or RNA — in a protein coat (**capsid**); some have an outer lipid **envelope** (HIV, influenza, hepatitis B). Enveloped viruses are more easily destroyed by alcohol, soap and detergents.
- **Obligate intracellular parasites** — multiply only inside living cells using the host cell's machinery.
- Not affected by antibacterial antibiotics; some are treated with antiviral drugs; many are prevented by vaccines.

**Multiplication (replication) of a virus**

```text
 1 Adsorption (attachment to receptor) → 2 Penetration → 3 Uncoating
 → 4 Synthesis of viral nucleic acid and proteins → 5 Assembly (maturation)
 → 6 Release (by cell lysis or budding)
```

**Cultivation**: in laboratory animals, embryonated hen's eggs, and cell (tissue) cultures. **Diagnosis**: antigen detection (rapid tests, ELISA), antibody detection (IgM = recent infection), nucleic acid tests (PCR, RT-PCR), electron microscopy, virus isolation.

**Important viral diseases**

| Route | Virus — disease | Key points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Respiratory (droplet/airborne) | Influenza; SARS-CoV-2 — COVID-19; measles; mumps; rubella; varicella-zoster (chickenpox, shingles) | Measles and varicella spread by air (airborne precautions); rubella in early pregnancy causes congenital defects |
| Faeco-oral | Poliovirus — poliomyelitis; hepatitis A and E — infective hepatitis; rotavirus — infant diarrhoea | Hepatitis E is dangerous in pregnancy; polio and rotavirus prevented by vaccines in the national schedule |
| Blood, sexual, mother to child | **HIV** — AIDS; **hepatitis B, C** (and D with B) | Main risk to nurses through needle-stick injury; hepatitis B vaccine protects |
| Vector-borne | Dengue, chikungunya, Zika (*Aedes*); Japanese encephalitis (*Culex*); Kyasanur Forest disease (ticks) | Mosquito control and personal protection |
| Animal bite | Rabies (dog bite mainly) | Wound washing with soap and running water for 15 minutes, then post-exposure vaccination as advised; nearly always fatal once symptoms begin |
| Skin/mucosa contact | Herpes simplex; human papillomavirus (warts, cervical cancer) | HPV vaccine prevents cervical cancer |

## 3.7 Fungi

**Characteristics**: eukaryotic; cell wall of chitin; no chlorophyll. Forms: **yeasts** (single round cells that bud — *Cryptococcus*), **yeast-like** (*Candida* forms buds and pseudohyphae), **moulds** (filaments called hyphae forming a mycelium — *Aspergillus*, *Mucor*, dermatophytes), **dimorphic** (mould in soil at room temperature, yeast in the body at 37 °C — *Histoplasma*).

**Classification of mycoses (fungal infections)**

| Type | Where | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Superficial** | Outermost layer of skin, hair shaft; little or no reaction | Pityriasis versicolor (*Malassezia* — scaly patches), black piedra, tinea nigra |
| Cutaneous (often taught with superficial) | Skin, hair and nails (keratin) | **Dermatophytes** (*Trichophyton*, *Microsporum*, *Epidermophyton*) — ringworm: tinea capitis, corporis, cruris, pedis, unguium; cutaneous candidiasis |
| Subcutaneous | Below the skin after a thorn prick or injury | Mycetoma (Madura foot), sporotrichosis, chromoblastomycosis |
| **Deep (systemic)** | Internal organs; spread from lungs | Histoplasmosis, blastomycosis; cryptococcal meningitis |
| Opportunistic | In people with low immunity (diabetes, cancer, AIDS, steroids, ICU) | Candidiasis (oral thrush, vaginitis, candidaemia from central lines), aspergillosis, **mucormycosis** (seen after COVID-19 with uncontrolled diabetes and steroid use) |

**Identification**: KOH mount of skin, hair, nail scrapings; Gram stain (Candida = large Gram-positive budding cells); India ink for *Cryptococcus* in CSF; culture on Sabouraud's dextrose agar; LPCB mount of the growth; antigen tests.

Nursing: keep skin folds dry; oral care in sick and immunocompromised patients; care of central lines; avoid sharing combs and towels (ringworm).

## 3.8 Parasites

A **parasite** lives on or in a host and gets food from it. **Protozoa** are single-celled; **helminths** are worms.

| Parasite | Disease | Transmission | Identification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Entamoeba histolytica* | Amoebic dysentery; amoebic liver abscess | Faeco-oral — cysts in water and food | Fresh stool for trophozoites (with ingested red cells) and cysts; serology for liver abscess |
| *Giardia lamblia* | Diarrhoea, malabsorption | Faeco-oral cysts | Stool microscopy |
| ***Plasmodium*** (*vivax*, *falciparum*, *malariae*, *ovale*) | **Malaria** — cyclical fever with chills; *P. falciparum* causes severe/cerebral malaria | Bite of infected female ***Anopheles*** mosquito; also blood transfusion, mother to fetus | **Peripheral blood smear** (thick for detection, thin for species); rapid diagnostic tests (antigen) |
| *Leishmania donovani* | **Kala-azar** (visceral leishmaniasis) — fever, big spleen, darkening of skin | Sandfly (*Phlebotomus*) | Bone marrow or spleen aspirate; rapid antibody test |
| *Trichomonas vaginalis* | Vaginitis with frothy discharge | Sexual | Wet mount |
| *Toxoplasma gondii* | Congenital infection; brain infection in AIDS | Cat faeces, undercooked meat | Serology |
| ***Ascaris lumbricoides*** (roundworm) | Intestinal obstruction, malnutrition | Eggs in soil-contaminated food, unwashed hands | Eggs in stool |
| **Hookworm** (*Ancylostoma duodenale*, *Necator americanus*) | **Iron-deficiency anaemia** | Larvae in soil **penetrate bare skin** of feet | Eggs in stool |
| *Enterobius vermicularis* (pinworm) | Itching around the anus at night, especially children | Eggs on fingers, bedding; self-reinfection | Cellophane (adhesive) tape test of perianal skin in the morning |
| *Taenia solium* (pork tapeworm) | Intestinal taeniasis; **neurocysticercosis** (seizures) when eggs are swallowed | Undercooked pork; eggs via faeco-oral route | Stool, CT/MRI brain, serology |
| *Echinococcus granulosus* | Hydatid cyst (liver, lung) | Eggs from dog faeces | Imaging, serology |
| *Wuchereria bancrofti* | **Lymphatic filariasis** — lymphoedema, elephantiasis, hydrocele | Bite of ***Culex*** mosquito | **Night blood smear** (microfilariae circulate at night), antigen card test |

Prevention of intestinal parasites: safe drinking water, sanitary latrines, hand washing, wearing footwear, cooking meat well, periodic deworming as per the national programme.

## 3.9 Rodents and vectors

A **vector** is an arthropod (insect or tick) that carries an infection from one host to another. Transmission is **mechanical** (carried on legs or body, e.g. housefly) or **biological** (the organism multiplies or develops inside the vector, e.g. malaria in *Anopheles*).

| Vector | Diseases |
| --- | --- |
| *Anopheles* mosquito (female) | Malaria |
| *Aedes aegypti* mosquito (day-biting, breeds in clean stored water, coolers, tyres) | Dengue, chikungunya, Zika, yellow fever |
| *Culex* mosquito (breeds in dirty water, drains) | Lymphatic filariasis (bancroftian), Japanese encephalitis |
| *Mansonia* mosquito | Brugian filariasis |
| Sandfly | Kala-azar |
| Housefly (mechanical) | Typhoid, cholera, dysentery, diarrhoea, trachoma, intestinal worms |
| Human louse | Epidemic typhus, relapsing fever, trench fever |
| Rat flea (*Xenopsylla cheopis*) | Plague, endemic (murine) typhus |
| Mites | Scrub typhus (larval mite), scabies (itch mite) |
| Hard ticks | Kyasanur Forest disease, tick typhus |
| Cockroach (mechanical) | Gut infections in hospital kitchens and wards |

**Rodents (rats, mice)** act as reservoirs: **plague** (via rat fleas), **leptospirosis** (urine), murine typhus, salmonellosis (droppings in food), rat-bite fever, and they damage stores and wiring.

**Control**

1. **Environmental**: remove breeding sites — no stagnant water, cover water tanks, empty coolers weekly, clear drains, proper garbage disposal, rodent-proof buildings and food stores.
2. **Personal protection**: mosquito nets (long-lasting insecticidal nets), repellents, full sleeves, screens.
3. **Chemical**: insecticide spraying (indoor residual spraying, fogging during outbreaks), larvicides; rodenticides and traps for rats.
4. **Biological**: larva-eating fish (*Gambusia*, guppy) in ponds and wells.
5. Health education and community participation.

In hospitals, pest control by an approved agency, screened windows, closed bins and no food left in wards keep vectors and rodents out.

## 3.10 Application and implication in nursing

- Match precautions to the route: **airborne** for TB, measles, chickenpox; **droplet** for influenza, diphtheria, meningococcus, pertussis; **contact** for MRSA, *C. difficile*, scabies, wound infections; **standard** for all, including HIV and hepatitis B (blood-borne).
- Collect the right specimen at the right time: blood culture in the first week of typhoid, night blood for filaria, smear during fever for malaria, fresh warm stool for amoebae, early-morning sputum for TB.
- Educate patients and families on safe water, food hygiene, mosquito control and completing treatment.
- Report notifiable diseases as per local rules (e.g. cholera, plague, diphtheria) and suspected outbreaks to the infection control team.
- Use soap and water (not alcohol rub) for *C. difficile* and norovirus.

## Remember

- *S. aureus*: clusters, coagulase positive, boils and wound infection; MRSA in hospitals.
- *S. pyogenes*: chains, beta haemolysis; rheumatic fever and glomerulonephritis follow.
- Gram-negative diplococci: meningococcus (meningitis), gonococcus (gonorrhoea, ophthalmia neonatorum).
- *C. tetani*: drumstick spore, soil, wounds, cord; prevented by tetanus toxoid.
- *M. tuberculosis*: acid-fast, airborne, LJ medium, slow growth.
- *E. coli* is the commonest cause of UTI; typhoid — blood culture first week, Widal after.
- Cholera: comma-shaped, darting motility, rice-water stools, TCBS.
- Viruses: one nucleic acid, obligate intracellular, not killed by antibacterial antibiotics.
- Fungi: superficial (pityriasis versicolor), cutaneous (ringworm), deep (histoplasmosis), opportunistic (candida, aspergillus, mucor). KOH mount, SDA culture.
- *Anopheles* → malaria; *Aedes* → dengue, chikungunya; *Culex* → filaria, JE; sandfly → kala-azar; rat flea → plague; rat urine → leptospirosis; chigger → scrub typhus.
- Hookworm enters through bare feet and causes anaemia; pinworm — tape test.

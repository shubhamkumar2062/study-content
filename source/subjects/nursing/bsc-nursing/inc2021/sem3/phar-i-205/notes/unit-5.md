# Unit 5: Drugs for anaemia, clotting, lipids and shock

Second part of INC Unit V (4 hours with Unit 4): haematinics and treatment of anaemia, coagulants and anticoagulants, antiplatelets and thrombolytics, hypolipidaemics, plasma expanders and treatment of shock, and drugs used in other blood disorders. Doses are not given; follow the prescriber's order and your hospital protocol.

## 5.1 Haematinics and treatment of anaemia

**Anaemia** is a fall in haemoglobin below the normal level for age and sex. The commonest type in India is **iron-deficiency anaemia** (poor diet, hookworm, blood loss, pregnancy). **Haematinics** are substances needed to form red blood cells: iron, vitamin B₁₂, folic acid, and also proteins, vitamin C and copper.

```text
 Bone marrow + iron (haem) + B₁₂ and folic acid (DNA) + erythropoietin (signal from kidney)
                               ↓
                     normal red blood cells
 Lack of iron → small pale cells (microcytic hypochromic)
 Lack of B₁₂ or folate → large cells (megaloblastic)
```

### Iron

| Form | Examples | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oral | Ferrous sulfate, ferrous fumarate, ferrous gluconate, ferrous ascorbate, carbonyl iron | First choice; ferrous (Fe²⁺) salts are absorbed better than ferric |
| Parenteral | Iron sucrose (IV), ferric carboxymaltose (IV), iron dextran | When oral iron fails, cannot be tolerated or is not absorbed, or anaemia is severe late in pregnancy |

**Absorption** occurs mainly in the duodenum. It is **increased** by vitamin C (ascorbic acid), meat and an empty stomach, and **decreased** by tea, coffee, milk, calcium, antacids, PPIs, phosphates, phytates (cereals) and tetracyclines.

**Side effects of oral iron**: nausea, epigastric pain, constipation or diarrhoea, **black stools** (harmless — warn the patient), metallic taste, staining of teeth with liquid preparations.

**Side effects of IV iron**: allergic reactions (rarely anaphylaxis), low BP, joint and muscle pain, local pain and skin staining if it leaks.

**Nursing responsibilities**
1. Give oral iron between meals for best absorption; if the stomach is upset, give with or after food (as ordered) rather than stopping it.
2. Give with orange juice or another vitamin C source, not with tea, milk or antacids (separate by about 2 hours).
3. Liquid iron through a straw and rinse the mouth afterwards.
4. Explain that stools will be black; teach a diet rich in iron (green leafy vegetables, jaggery, pulses, meat, liver, eggs).
5. Treatment continues for some months after the haemoglobin is normal to fill the iron stores.
6. For IV iron: check the order, give by the prescribed infusion method, observe closely during and after the infusion, keep emergency drugs ready.
7. Keep iron tablets in child-proof containers out of children's reach — they look like sweets and are dangerous if a child swallows many.

**National programme**: under **Anaemia Mukt Bharat** (launched 2018), iron and folic acid supplements, deworming, screening and treatment are given to children, adolescents, pregnant and lactating women and women of reproductive age, with behaviour change communication. Check the current MoHFW guidelines for the target groups and schedules.

### Vitamin B₁₂ and folic acid

- **Vitamin B₁₂** (cyanocobalamin, hydroxocobalamin) is needed for DNA synthesis and for myelin of nerves. Deficiency causes **megaloblastic anaemia** and nerve damage (numbness, poor balance). **Pernicious anaemia** (lack of intrinsic factor) needs lifelong injections because oral B₁₂ is not absorbed.
- **Folic acid** is needed for DNA synthesis. Deficiency (poor diet, pregnancy, alcoholism, some drugs such as phenytoin and methotrexate) causes megaloblastic anaemia. Folic acid before conception and in early pregnancy reduces **neural tube defects**.
- **Important**: folic acid alone in B₁₂ deficiency may correct the blood picture while nerve damage gets worse — the cause must be found first.

### Erythropoietin

Epoetin (recombinant erythropoietin) stimulates the marrow to make red cells. Used in anaemia of chronic kidney disease and some cancer chemotherapy. Side effects: raised BP, clotting (fistula thrombosis), headache. Iron is usually needed alongside.

**General treatment of anaemia**: find and treat the cause (deworming, treatment of bleeding piles or heavy periods), improve diet, give the right haematinic, and blood transfusion for severe anaemia.

## 5.2 Blood clotting in brief

```text
 Vessel injury → platelets stick and clump (platelet plug)
             → clotting cascade → thrombin → fibrinogen → FIBRIN clot
             → later, plasmin dissolves fibrin (fibrinolysis)

 Antiplatelets act on the plug.   Anticoagulants act on the cascade.
 Thrombolytics activate plasmin.  Antifibrinolytics (tranexamic acid) block plasmin.
```

Vitamin K is needed by the liver to make factors II (prothrombin), VII, IX and X.

## 5.3 Coagulants and haemostatics

| Drug | Use | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vitamin K₁ (phytomenadione) | Prevention of haemorrhagic disease of the newborn (given to every newborn at birth); bleeding from warfarin; vitamin K deficiency (obstructive jaundice, malabsorption) | IV injection must be slow — rare severe reactions; action takes hours |
| Fresh frozen plasma, clotting factor concentrates (factor VIII, factor IX), fibrinogen | Haemophilia, bleeding with clotting factor deficiency, massive transfusion | Given as per blood bank protocol |
| Desmopressin | Mild haemophilia A and von Willebrand disease (releases factor VIII) | Watch for low sodium |
| Tranexamic acid | Antifibrinolytic: post-partum haemorrhage, trauma bleeding, heavy menstrual bleeding, dental bleeding in haemophilia | Risk of clotting; avoid in active thrombosis |
| Protamine sulfate | Neutralises heparin | Chemical antagonist; can cause low BP and allergy |
| Local haemostatics | Adrenaline-soaked gauze, gelatin sponge, oxidised cellulose, thrombin | Applied at the bleeding site (epistaxis, surgery) |

## 5.4 Anticoagulants

Anticoagulants prevent new clots and stop existing clots from growing; they do **not** dissolve clots.

**Uses**: deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (treatment and prevention after surgery or immobility), atrial fibrillation (stroke prevention), prosthetic heart valves, acute coronary syndrome, during dialysis and heart–lung bypass, to keep IV lines patent.

### Heparin

| Feature | Unfractionated heparin (UFH) | Low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Examples | Heparin | Enoxaparin, dalteparin |
| Action | Activates antithrombin → inactivates thrombin and factor Xa | Mainly factor Xa |
| Route | IV (bolus and infusion) or SC; **never IM** (haematoma) | SC |
| Onset | Immediate | Quick |
| Monitoring | aPTT (activated partial thromboplastin time) | Usually none routinely; platelet count |
| Reversal | Protamine sulfate | Protamine (partial) |

**Side effects of heparin**: bleeding (most important), **heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT)** — falling platelets with new clots, osteoporosis with long use, hair loss, allergy, raised potassium.

**SC heparin/LMWH technique**: inject into the abdominal fatty tissue, away from the umbilicus and scars; pinch a skin fold and insert the needle at 90° (or as the product directs); do not aspirate; do not rub after injection (bruising); rotate sites; do not expel the air bubble of a prefilled LMWH syringe.

### Oral anticoagulants

**Warfarin** (vitamin K antagonist)
- Blocks the vitamin K–dependent formation of factors II, VII, IX and X.
- Takes several days to work fully; heparin or LMWH is therefore given together at the start and stopped when the INR is in range.
- Monitored by the **prothrombin time / INR**; the target range is set by the doctor for each condition.
- **Many interactions**: effect increased by aspirin and NSAIDs, amiodarone, metronidazole, fluconazole and miconazole, erythromycin, cotrimoxazole, alcohol binges; effect reduced by rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, barbiturates and by foods very rich in vitamin K (green leafy vegetables) in large changing amounts.
- **Teratogenic** — not used in pregnancy (heparin is used instead).
- Reversal: vitamin K; fresh frozen plasma or prothrombin complex concentrate for serious bleeding.

**Direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs)**: dabigatran (thrombin inhibitor), rivaroxaban and apixaban (factor Xa inhibitors). Fixed dosing without routine INR checks; fewer food interactions; dose adjusted in kidney disease.

**Contraindications to anticoagulants**: active bleeding, bleeding disorders, recent brain, eye or spinal surgery, severe uncontrolled hypertension, peptic ulcer with bleeding, severe liver disease; warfarin in pregnancy.

**Nursing responsibilities (bleeding precautions)**
1. Check the latest aPTT, INR and platelet count before giving, and report results outside the ordered range.
2. Observe for bleeding: gums, nose, urine (red or brown), stools (black or bloody), bruises, bleeding from puncture sites, heavy periods, headache or confusion (bleeding in the brain), fall in BP.
3. Avoid IM injections; apply pressure longer after needle pricks.
4. Use a soft toothbrush and an electric razor; prevent falls.
5. Keep protamine and vitamin K available as per protocol.
6. Teach: take warfarin at the same time daily, keep INR appointments, keep a steady diet (do not suddenly add or stop large amounts of green leafy vegetables), avoid aspirin and other over-the-counter painkillers and herbal products without advice, inform dentists and doctors, carry an anticoagulant card, report bleeding at once.

## 5.5 Antiplatelet drugs

| Drug | Action | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aspirin | Irreversibly inhibits cyclo-oxygenase in platelets → less thromboxane A₂ → less clumping (effect lasts the life of the platelet) | Gastric irritation and bleeding (give after food); not in children with viral illness (Reye's syndrome); may trigger asthma |
| Clopidogrel, prasugrel, ticagrelor | Block the ADP (P2Y₁₂) receptor on platelets | With aspirin ("dual antiplatelet therapy") after acute coronary syndrome and stents; bleeding |
| Dipyridamole | Phosphodiesterase inhibition | With aspirin in stroke prevention; headache |
| Glycoprotein IIb/IIIa blockers | Abciximab, eptifibatide, tirofiban (IV) | During coronary angioplasty; bleeding, low platelets |

**Uses**: prevention of myocardial infarction and stroke in people at risk, after MI, unstable angina, coronary stents, transient ischaemic attack and ischaemic stroke.

**Nursing**: give aspirin with or after food; watch for black stools and bruising; before surgery or dental extraction the doctor decides whether to stop them; patients with stents must not stop antiplatelets on their own (stent clotting).

## 5.6 Thrombolytics (fibrinolytics)

**Action**: convert plasminogen to **plasmin**, which breaks down fibrin and dissolves a fresh clot.

**Examples**: streptokinase, urokinase, alteplase, reteplase, tenecteplase.

**Uses**: acute ST-elevation myocardial infarction when angioplasty is not available quickly ("time is muscle" — benefit is greatest when given early), acute ischaemic stroke within the time window after a CT scan rules out bleeding (alteplase, tenecteplase), massive pulmonary embolism, blocked catheters.

**Side effects**: bleeding, including bleeding in the brain; allergy, fever and low BP with streptokinase (it is a bacterial protein — it should not be repeated within months because antibodies form); reperfusion arrhythmias.

**Absolute contraindications** (examples): any previous brain haemorrhage, recent ischaemic stroke (outside the stroke use), active internal bleeding, suspected aortic dissection, recent major head injury or brain surgery, known brain tumour or vascular malformation.

**Nursing**: confirm the checklist of contraindications with the doctor; establish IV lines and take blood samples before starting; avoid arterial and unnecessary needle punctures; monitor vital signs, cardiac rhythm, neurological status and puncture sites closely during and after the infusion; stop and inform at once if there is sudden headache, fall in consciousness or bleeding; tranexamic acid and blood products may be ordered for serious bleeding.

## 5.7 Hypolipidaemic drugs

High LDL cholesterol and triglycerides lead to atherosclerosis, heart attack and stroke. Lifestyle (diet low in saturated fat, exercise, weight control, no smoking) comes first and continues with drugs.

| Group | Examples | Action | Side effects / points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Statins | Atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin | Inhibit HMG-CoA reductase (key enzyme in cholesterol synthesis in the liver) → LDL falls | Muscle pain and weakness (rarely muscle breakdown — dark urine), raised liver enzymes; not in pregnancy; shorter-acting ones best at night; interactions with macrolides, azole antifungals, fibrates, grapefruit juice |
| Fibrates | Fenofibrate, gemfibrozil | Lower triglycerides mainly | GI upset, muscle problems with statins, gallstones |
| Cholesterol absorption inhibitor | Ezetimibe | Blocks cholesterol absorption in the gut | Often added to a statin |
| Bile acid sequestrants | Cholestyramine, colestipol | Bind bile acids in the gut | Constipation, bloating; bind other drugs (give other drugs 1 hour before or several hours after) |
| Nicotinic acid (niacin) | — | Lowers triglycerides and LDL | Flushing, itching, raised blood sugar and uric acid |
| PCSK9 inhibitors | Evolocumab, alirocumab (SC) | Increase LDL removal | Expensive; injection-site reactions |
| Omega-3 fatty acids | Fish oil | Lower triglycerides | Fishy burps |

**Nursing**: take a diet and lifestyle history; teach the patient to report unexplained muscle pain or dark urine; check lipid profile and liver tests as ordered; stress that treatment is long-term.

## 5.8 Plasma expanders and the treatment of shock

**Shock** is a state in which tissues get too little blood flow and oxygen. Types: hypovolaemic (bleeding, dehydration, burns), cardiogenic (heart pump failure), distributive (septic, anaphylactic, neurogenic) and obstructive (pulmonary embolism, cardiac tamponade).

**Intravenous fluids**

| Type | Examples | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crystalloids | Normal saline (0.9% NaCl), Ringer lactate, dextrose solutions | First choice for most fluid resuscitation; cheap; much of the fluid moves out of the vessels |
| Colloids (plasma expanders) | Human albumin, dextrans, gelatin (polygeline), hydroxyethyl starch | Larger molecules stay longer in the vessels; more costly; risk of allergy |
| Blood and products | Whole blood, packed red cells, fresh frozen plasma, platelets | In blood loss and clotting problems |

**Plasma expanders — points to note**
- **Dextran**: can cause allergy, interfere with blood grouping and cross-matching (take the sample before infusing) and affect clotting.
- **Hydroxyethyl starch**: risk of kidney injury and bleeding; its use is restricted, especially in sepsis and critical illness.
- **Gelatin**: allergic reactions.
- **Albumin**: in burns, liver disease with ascites, low protein states.

**Drugs used in shock**

| Shock type | Main drug treatment (besides cause-specific care) |
| --- | --- |
| Hypovolaemic | Fluids and blood; stop the bleeding |
| Septic | Fluids, early antibiotics after cultures, noradrenaline for persistent low BP, corticosteroids in some cases |
| Cardiogenic | Careful fluids; inotropes (dobutamine), vasopressors; treat the cause (reopen the coronary artery) |
| Anaphylactic | **Adrenaline IM** first, then oxygen, fluids, antihistamine and corticosteroid; nebulised bronchodilator for wheeze |
| Neurogenic | Fluids, vasopressors, atropine for slow heart rate |

**Nursing in shock**
1. Position: lie flat with legs raised unless breathless or head injury; maintain airway and give oxygen.
2. Insert large-bore IV cannulas; give fluids at the ordered rate through a pump or with drop counting.
3. Monitor pulse, BP, respiratory rate, SpO₂, temperature, level of consciousness, skin colour and warmth, capillary refill and **hourly urine output** (a good guide to tissue perfusion); CVP if available.
4. Watch for fluid overload (breathlessness, crackles, raised neck veins), especially in elderly and heart patients.
5. Observe for transfusion and allergic reactions.
6. Keep the patient warm, reassure the family, and record everything accurately.

## 5.9 Drugs used to treat other blood disorders

| Disorder | Drugs used | Nursing points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Haemophilia A / B | Factor VIII / factor IX concentrate; desmopressin (mild A); tranexamic acid for mouth bleeding | Avoid IM injections and aspirin; immobilise bleeding joints; teach home factor infusion |
| Sickle cell disease | Hydroxyurea (fewer crises), folic acid, analgesics in crisis, fluids, oxygen, transfusion | Keep well hydrated and warm; early treatment of infection; vaccination |
| Thalassaemia major | Regular blood transfusion with **iron chelation** (deferoxamine by SC or IV infusion; oral deferasirox or deferiprone); folic acid | Watch for iron overload; transfusion reactions; growth monitoring |
| Immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) | Corticosteroids, IV immunoglobulin, platelet transfusion in severe bleeding | Bleeding precautions |
| Neutropenia (e.g. after chemotherapy) | Filgrastim (G-CSF) to raise neutrophils | Infection prevention, bone pain |
| Anaemia of chronic kidney disease | Erythropoietin with iron | Monitor BP |
| Leukaemias and lymphomas | Anticancer drugs (Pharmacology II) | — |

## Remember

- Iron: ferrous salts by mouth first; vitamin C helps, tea, milk and antacids hinder; black stools are expected; keep away from children.
- B₁₂ deficiency → megaloblastic anaemia and nerve damage; pernicious anaemia needs lifelong injections; never give folic acid alone for undiagnosed megaloblastic anaemia.
- Folic acid before and in early pregnancy prevents neural tube defects.
- Vitamin K to every newborn; vitamin K reverses warfarin; protamine reverses heparin.
- Heparin: IV or SC, never IM; aPTT; HIT. Warfarin: INR, many interactions, not in pregnancy, takes days to act.
- Anticoagulants prevent clot growth; thrombolytics dissolve fresh clots (plasmin); tranexamic acid blocks fibrinolysis.
- Aspirin blocks thromboxane A₂ irreversibly; clopidogrel blocks ADP receptors; no aspirin for children with viral illness (Reye's).
- Statins inhibit HMG-CoA reductase; report muscle pain.
- Dextran: take the cross-match sample first. Adrenaline IM first in anaphylaxis; noradrenaline in septic shock.
- Hourly urine output is the bedside guide to perfusion in shock.

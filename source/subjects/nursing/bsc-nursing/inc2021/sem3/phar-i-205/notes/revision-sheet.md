# Last-night revision sheet

Pharmacology I is examined in Semester 4 as **Section A (38 marks)** of the combined 75-mark paper. Work through these summary tables and the MCQ bank tonight. You need classification, mechanism, one key adverse effect, and nursing responsibilities for each drug group.

---

**Pharmacokinetics — ADME key points**

| Step | Key term | Nurse's relevance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Absorption | First-pass effect: oral drugs metabolised in liver/gut wall before reaching circulation; reduces bioavailability; e.g. GTN, morphine → given sublingually or IV | Sublingual route bypasses first pass |
| Distribution | Volume of distribution (Vd): lipid-soluble drugs have large Vd (penetrate tissues); protein binding — only free drug is active | Warfarin is 99% protein-bound; drug interactions displace it → bleeding risk |
| Metabolism | CYP450 enzymes in liver; inducers (rifampicin, phenytoin) speed up metabolism; inhibitors (cimetidine, erythromycin) slow it | TB drugs (rifampicin) reduce effect of oral contraceptives |
| Excretion | Mostly renal; half-life (t½) = time for plasma level to fall by 50%; 5 half-lives to eliminate fully | Renal failure → drug accumulation; reduce dose (e.g. aminoglycosides, digoxin) |

**Autonomic drugs — quick reference**

| Drug type | Receptor | Main effects | Key examples | Nursing point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cholinergic (parasympathomimetic) | Muscarinic / nicotinic | ↓ HR, ↑ GI motility, miosis, bronchoconstriction, salivation | Pilocarpine (eye drops), neostigmine (reversal of neuromuscular block) | SLUDGE: Salivation, Lacrimation, Urination, Defaecation, GI cramps, Emesis (cholinergic excess) |
| Anticholinergic (parasympatholytic) | Muscarinic block | ↑ HR, dry mouth, urinary retention, constipation, blurred vision, dilated pupil | Atropine, hyoscine, glycopyrrolate | Atropine → tachycardia; contraindicated in glaucoma, BPH; antidote for organophosphate poisoning |
| Sympathomimetic (adrenergic) | α, β | α: vasoconstriction, ↑ BP; β1: ↑ HR, ↑ force; β2: bronchodilation | Adrenaline (α+β), noradrenaline (α), salbutamol (β2), dobutamine (β1) | Adrenaline 0.5 mg IM for anaphylaxis; also used in cardiac arrest (IV/IO) |
| Sympatholytic | α block / β block | β-blockers: ↓ HR, ↓ BP, bronchospasm | Propranolol (non-selective), atenolol (β1-selective), prazosin (α1-block) | β-blockers contraindicated in asthma; mask hypoglycaemia symptoms |

**Cardiovascular drug classes**

| Class | Mechanism | Examples | Key adverse effect | Nursing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ACE inhibitors | Block angiotensin-converting enzyme → ↓ angiotensin II → vasodilation, ↓ aldosterone | Ramipril, lisinopril, enalapril | Dry cough (bradykinin accumulation); hyperkalaemia; angioedema (rare — stop drug) | Monitor K+, renal function; avoid in pregnancy |
| ARBs | Block angiotensin II receptor | Losartan, valsartan | No dry cough; hyperkalaemia | Same monitoring as ACE inhibitors |
| Loop diuretics | Inhibit Na/K/2Cl in loop of Henle | Frusemide (furosemide) | Hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, ototoxicity (high IV dose) | Monitor electrolytes; give in morning to avoid nocturnal diuresis |
| Thiazide diuretics | Inhibit NaCl in DCT | Hydrochlorothiazide, indapamide | Hypokalaemia, hyperglycaemia, hyperuricaemia | Give potassium supplement or combine with K-sparing diuretic |
| Potassium-sparing | Block aldosterone (spironolactone) or Na channels (amiloride) | Spironolactone, amiloride | Hyperkalaemia; spironolactone → gynaecomastia | Do not combine with ACE inhibitor without monitoring K+ |
| Calcium channel blockers | Block L-type calcium channels | Amlodipine, nifedipine (dihydropyridines — vasodilation); diltiazem, verapamil (also ↓ HR) | Ankle oedema (dihydropyridines); bradycardia (diltiazem/verapamil) | Verapamil contraindicated with beta-blockers (complete heart block risk) |
| GTN / organic nitrates | Release nitric oxide → venodilation, ↓ preload, coronary vasodilation | GTN sublingual, isosorbide dinitrate | Headache, flushing, postural hypotension; tolerance with long-acting forms | Sublingual GTN: sit/lie down, repeat up to 3 times at 5-min intervals; call ambulance if no relief after 1 tablet |
| Heparin | Activates antithrombin III → inhibits thrombin and Xa | UFH (IV/SC), LMWH (enoxaparin SC) | Bleeding; HIT (heparin-induced thrombocytopenia) | Monitor APTT (UFH); antidote = protamine sulphate; watch for bruising and blood in urine/stool |
| Warfarin | Inhibits vitamin K-dependent clotting factors (II, VII, IX, X) | Warfarin | Bleeding; teratogenic | Monitor INR (target 2–3 for most); antidote = vitamin K; many drug and food interactions (avoid cranberry juice, variable leafy veg intake) |
| Aspirin | Irreversibly inhibits COX-1 → ↓ thromboxane A2 | 75–100 mg for antiplatelet effect | GI bleeding; Reye's syndrome in children | Give with food; avoid in children <16 |

| Drug | Class | Use | Adverse effect | Nursing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Salbutamol (albuterol) | SABA (short-acting β2 agonist) | Acute bronchospasm | Tremor, tachycardia | Demonstrate inhaler technique; spacer in children and elderly |

**Anti-infectives — summary table**

| Class | Mechanism | Key examples | Spectrum | Major adverse effect | Nurse's point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Penicillins | Cell-wall synthesis inhibition | Amoxicillin, flucloxacillin, benzylpenicillin | Gram+ mostly; amoxicillin broader | **Allergy/anaphylaxis** | Ask allergy history; keep adrenaline ready after injection |
| Cephalosporins | Cell-wall (beta-lactam) | Cefalexin (1st gen), ceftriaxone (3rd gen) | Broad; later gens more Gram- | Allergy (cross-react with penicillin ~10%) | Monitor renal function |
| Aminoglycosides | Protein synthesis (30S) | Gentamicin, amikacin, streptomycin | Gram- (aerobic) | **Nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity** | Monitor renal function and hearing; keep well-hydrated |
| Macrolides | Protein synthesis (50S) | Erythromycin, azithromycin, clarithromycin | Gram+ + atypicals (Mycoplasma, Chlamydia, Legionella) | GI upset; CYP inhibition | Give with food |
| Fluoroquinolones | DNA gyrase/topoisomerase IV | Ciprofloxacin, ofloxacin, levofloxacin | Broad Gram- and some Gram+ | Tendon rupture (Achilles), QT prolongation | Avoid in growing children, pregnancy; take 2 h after antacids |
| Tetracyclines | Protein synthesis (30S) | Doxycycline | Broad; Mycoplasma, Chlamydia, rickettsiae, malaria prophylaxis | Dental staining / bone in children; photosensitivity | Avoid in children <8, pregnancy; take with full glass of water, upright |
| Metronidazole | DNA strand breakage | Metronidazole | Anaerobes, protozoa (Giardia, amoebiasis, trichomonas) | Disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol, metallic taste | No alcohol during and 48 h after course |
| Sulfonamides + trimethoprim | Folate synthesis | Co-trimoxazole (TMP-SMX) | Broad; PCP treatment and prophylaxis | Allergy, marrow suppression, hyperkalaemia | Adequate hydration; avoid in G6PD deficiency |

**TB drugs (HRZE)**: Isoniazid (H) — give pyridoxine; Rifampicin (R) — orange urine is normal, monitor LFT; Pyrazinamide (Z) — monitor LFT and uric acid; Ethambutol (E) — monthly vision check.

**Antimalarials**: chloroquine (P. vivax); ACT (artesunate combinations) for P. falciparum; primaquine for radical cure of vivax (check G6PD first); lumefantrine (with artemether) — take with fatty food.

**Insulin types**

| Type | Onset | Peak | Duration | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rapid-acting (aspart, lispro) | 15 min | 1–2 h | 3–5 h | Mealtime; can give just after food |
| Short-acting (regular/soluble) | 30–60 min | 2–4 h | 6–8 h | Pre-meal 30 min; IV infusion for DKA |
| Intermediate (NPH, isophane) | 1–2 h | 4–8 h | 12–18 h | Twice daily basal |
| Long-acting (glargine, detemir) | 1–2 h | Flat / no peak | 20–24 h | Once daily basal; do NOT mix in same syringe |

Hypoglycaemia (BG <70 mg/dL): give 15 g fast glucose (glucose tablets, juice, sugar water) → recheck in 15 min → repeat if still low; if unconscious → IV dextrose 25% or glucagon IM.

---

**Nurse's responsibilities with any drug (5 Rs + 2)**

Right Patient → Right Drug → Right Dose → Right Route → Right Time + Right Documentation + Check Allergy.

Before giving: check allergy, check relevant lab (BG for insulin, INR for warfarin, APTT for heparin, ECG for QT-prolonging drugs), explain the drug and its side effects to the patient.
After giving: document, observe for desired effect and adverse effects.

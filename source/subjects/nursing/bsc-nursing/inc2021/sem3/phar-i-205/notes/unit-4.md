## 4.1 The autonomic nervous system in brief

The heart, blood vessels, bronchi, gut, bladder, eyes and glands are controlled by two systems that usually oppose each other.

| Feature | Sympathetic ("fight or flight") | Parasympathetic ("rest and digest") |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transmitter at the organ | Noradrenaline (adrenaline from the adrenal medulla) | Acetylcholine |
| Receptors | α₁, α₂, β₁, β₂ (adrenergic) | Muscarinic (M); nicotinic at ganglia and skeletal muscle |
| Heart | Rate and force ↑ (β₁) | Rate ↓ (M₂) |
| Blood vessels | Constriction (α₁); dilatation in muscle (β₂) | Little direct effect |
| Bronchi | Dilatation (β₂) | Constriction and secretions (M₃) |
| Eye | Pupil dilates (α₁) | Pupil constricts; focus for near (M₃) |
| Gut | Motility ↓ | Motility and secretion ↑ |
| Bladder | Retention (relaxes wall, closes sphincter) | Emptying (contracts wall) |
| Glands | Sweating (sympathetic but cholinergic) | Salivation, lacrimation |

Drugs act by copying (agonists, "-mimetics") or blocking (antagonists, "-lytics" or blockers) these transmitters.

## 4.2 Cholinergic drugs (parasympathomimetics)

| Type | Examples | Uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct-acting (on muscarinic receptors) | Bethanechol, pilocarpine, carbachol | Bethanechol: post-operative urinary retention and gut atony. Pilocarpine: glaucoma (constricts pupil, opens drainage) |
| Indirect (anticholinesterases — block the enzyme that breaks down acetylcholine) | Neostigmine, pyridostigmine, physostigmine, edrophonium, rivastigmine, donepezil | Myasthenia gravis (neostigmine, pyridostigmine), reversal of muscle relaxants after surgery (neostigmine), post-operative urinary retention and ileus, glaucoma, Alzheimer's disease (donepezil, rivastigmine) |

**Side effects** — too much acetylcholine action. Remember **SLUDGE**: Salivation, Lacrimation, Urination, Defecation, GI cramps, Emesis; plus sweating, bradycardia, low BP, bronchospasm, small pupils and blurred vision.

**Contraindications**: asthma, peptic ulcer, intestinal or urinary obstruction, bradycardia.

**Nursing**: in myasthenia gravis give on time (often before meals to help chewing and swallowing); observe for weakness (too little drug) versus excess salivation, sweating and muscle twitching (too much) and report either; check pulse; keep atropine available as ordered (it blocks muscarinic effects).

## 4.3 Anticholinergic drugs (parasympatholytics)

Atropine, hyoscine (scopolamine), glycopyrrolate, dicyclomine, ipratropium, tiotropium, tropicamide, oxybutynin, benzhexol (trihexyphenidyl).

**Uses**
- Pre-anaesthetic medication: dry secretions and prevent reflex bradycardia (atropine, glycopyrrolate).
- Sinus bradycardia and heart block (atropine IV).
- Eye examination: dilate pupils and paralyse accommodation (tropicamide, atropine eye drops).
- Abdominal colic, irritable bowel, dysmenorrhoea (dicyclomine, hyoscine butylbromide).
- Motion sickness (hyoscine).
- Asthma and COPD (ipratropium, tiotropium).
- Overactive bladder (oxybutynin); drug-induced parkinsonism (benzhexol).
- To counter muscarinic effects of cholinergic drugs.

**Side effects** ("dry as a bone, red as a beet, hot as a hare, blind as a bat, mad as a hatter"): dry mouth, flushed hot dry skin, fever (no sweating), blurred vision and light sensitivity, tachycardia, constipation, urinary retention, confusion and restlessness, especially in the elderly and children.

**Contraindications**: narrow-angle glaucoma, enlarged prostate, intestinal obstruction, tachyarrhythmia; caution in hot weather and in the elderly.

**Nursing**: mouth care and sips of water; monitor urine output (ask about difficulty passing urine); check pulse; warn about blurred vision (no driving after eye drops, dark glasses); keep the patient cool; watch elderly patients for confusion.

## 4.4 Adrenergic drugs (sympathomimetics)

| Drug | Main receptors | Main uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adrenaline (epinephrine) | α and β | **Anaphylaxis (first drug, IM)**, cardiac arrest, with local anaesthetics to prolong action and reduce bleeding |
| Noradrenaline | Mainly α₁ (and β₁) | Low BP in septic shock (IV infusion) |
| Dopamine | Dopamine receptors, β₁, α (depending on rate) | Shock, low cardiac output |
| Dobutamine | β₁ | Cardiogenic shock, acute heart failure (increases force of contraction) |
| Isoprenaline | β₁ and β₂ | Heart block, bradycardia (rarely now) |
| Ephedrine | Indirect and direct α, β | Low BP during spinal anaesthesia |
| Phenylephrine | α₁ | Low BP, nasal decongestant, pupil dilatation |
| Salbutamol, terbutaline | β₂ | Asthma (Unit 3); delay preterm labour |

**Side effects**: palpitations, tachycardia, arrhythmias, raised BP, headache, anxiety, tremor, chest pain; tissue necrosis if noradrenaline or dopamine leaks out of the vein.

**Nursing for vasoactive infusions**
1. Give through an infusion pump, preferably by a central line or a large vein; never as a bolus unless ordered.
2. Check the dilution, rate and line with a second nurse.
3. Monitor BP, heart rate and rhythm continuously, urine output and the colour and warmth of the limbs.
4. Inspect the cannula site often; stop the infusion and inform the doctor at the first sign of leakage (blanching, swelling).
5. Do not stop suddenly — the BP can fall sharply; change syringes quickly.

## 4.5 Antiadrenergic drugs (adrenergic blockers)

**α-blockers**
- Prazosin, terazosin, doxazosin: block α₁ in vessels → vasodilatation; used in hypertension and benign prostatic hyperplasia. **First-dose hypotension** — give the first dose at bedtime and warn the patient to rise slowly.
- Tamsulosin: acts mainly on the prostate (BPH); dizziness, ejaculatory problems.
- Phentolamine: short-acting; used in phaeochromocytoma crises.

**β-blockers**

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Non-selective (β₁ + β₂) | Propranolol, timolol (eye drops) |
| Cardioselective (mainly β₁) | Atenolol, metoprolol, bisoprolol, esmolol (short-acting IV) |
| With α-blocking action | Labetalol, carvedilol |

**Actions**: slow the heart, reduce force of contraction and cardiac output, reduce renin release → lower BP and lower oxygen demand of the heart.

**Uses**: hypertension, angina, after myocardial infarction, arrhythmias (fast AF, SVT), stable heart failure (carvedilol, metoprolol, bisoprolol — started low under supervision), thyrotoxicosis (relieves tremor and palpitations), migraine prevention, performance anxiety, glaucoma (timolol), hypertension in pregnancy (labetalol).

**Side effects**: bradycardia, heart block, low BP, fatigue, cold hands and feet, bronchospasm (non-selective), sleep disturbance, sexual dysfunction; they **mask the warning signs of hypoglycaemia** (tremor, palpitations) in diabetics.

**Contraindications**: asthma (non-selective especially), severe bradycardia or heart block, uncontrolled heart failure, cardiogenic shock.

**Nursing**: count the apical pulse for one full minute and check BP before each dose; withhold and report if the pulse or BP is below the limit set by the prescriber (commonly a pulse below 60/min in adults); teach diabetics to check blood glucose because the usual warning signs may be absent; **never stop suddenly** — rebound tachycardia, angina or myocardial infarction can occur; rise slowly.

## 4.6 Drugs for congestive heart failure (CHF)

In heart failure the heart cannot pump enough blood for the body's needs → breathlessness, oedema, fatigue. Treatment aims to relieve congestion, reduce the workload of the heart and prolong life.

| Group | Examples | How it helps | Important side effects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Loop diuretics | Furosemide, torsemide | Remove excess fluid → relieve breathlessness and oedema | Low potassium, sodium, magnesium; dehydration; low BP; hearing damage with rapid IV injection |
| Aldosterone antagonists | Spironolactone, eplerenone | Diuresis; improve survival | High potassium; gynaecomastia (spironolactone) |
| ACE inhibitors | Enalapril, ramipril, lisinopril, captopril | Dilate vessels, reduce aldosterone; improve survival | Dry cough, angioedema, first-dose hypotension, high potassium, kidney impairment; not in pregnancy |
| Angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs) / ARNI | Losartan, telmisartan, valsartan; sacubitril–valsartan | As ACE inhibitors, without cough | High potassium, low BP; not in pregnancy |
| β-blockers | Carvedilol, metoprolol, bisoprolol | Protect the heart from excess sympathetic drive; improve survival | See 4.5 |
| SGLT2 inhibitors | Dapagliflozin, empagliflozin | Reduce hospital admissions and deaths | Genital infections, dehydration |
| Cardiac glycoside | Digoxin | See below | See below |
| Inotropes (acute) | Dobutamine, dopamine, milrinone | Increase force of contraction | Arrhythmias |
| Vasodilators | Nitrates, hydralazine, nitroprusside | Reduce preload and afterload | Headache, low BP |

**Digoxin**
- **Action**: inhibits the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump in heart muscle → more calcium inside the cell → **stronger contraction (positive inotropic)**; it also slows conduction through the AV node and slows the heart rate (negative chronotropic).
- **Uses**: heart failure with atrial fibrillation; to control the ventricular rate in atrial fibrillation and flutter.
- **Narrow therapeutic index**; it accumulates in kidney failure and in the elderly.
- **Warning signs of digoxin excess to report**: loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, slow or irregular pulse, visual disturbances (blurred or yellow-green vision, halos), confusion.
- **Factors that increase the risk**: low potassium (diuretics), low magnesium, high calcium, kidney failure, old age, hypothyroidism, interacting drugs (amiodarone, verapamil, quinidine raise digoxin levels).

**Nursing for heart failure drugs**
1. Count the apical pulse for a full minute before digoxin; withhold and report if below the set limit (commonly below 60/min in adults, higher limits in children) or if the rhythm has changed.
2. Check serum potassium and kidney function results before giving digoxin or diuretics.
3. Weigh the patient daily at the same time with the same clothes; record intake and output; a sudden weight gain suggests fluid retention.
4. Give diuretics in the morning so sleep is not disturbed.
5. Teach: low-salt diet, potassium-rich foods with potassium-losing diuretics (unless on spironolactone or ACE inhibitors, which raise potassium), rise slowly, report breathlessness, swelling or the warning signs above.

## 4.7 Vasodilators

- **Venodilators** reduce preload (nitrates).
- **Arteriolar dilators** reduce afterload (hydralazine, minoxidil, calcium channel blockers).
- **Mixed** (sodium nitroprusside, ACE inhibitors, α-blockers).

**Hydralazine**: arteriolar dilator; used in hypertension (including in pregnancy) and with nitrates in heart failure. Side effects: headache, reflex tachycardia, fluid retention, lupus-like syndrome with long use.

**Sodium nitroprusside**: very short-acting IV vasodilator for hypertensive emergencies and acute heart failure. Given only by infusion pump with continuous BP monitoring; the solution is protected from light (cover the bottle and line). It is used for short periods because a toxic breakdown product builds up with prolonged use.

## 4.8 Antianginal drugs

Angina is chest pain caused by an imbalance between the oxygen demand of the heart and its supply through narrowed coronary arteries.

**Organic nitrates** — glyceryl trinitrate (GTN, nitroglycerin), isosorbide dinitrate, isosorbide mononitrate.
- **Action**: release nitric oxide → relax vascular smooth muscle, mainly veins → less blood returns to the heart (reduced preload) → less work and oxygen demand; also dilate coronary arteries.
- **Uses**: GTN sublingual tablet or spray to relieve an attack or before exertion; long-acting nitrates for prevention; IV GTN in unstable angina and acute heart failure.
- **Side effects**: throbbing headache (common), flushing, dizziness, postural hypotension, reflex tachycardia; **tolerance** with continuous use — a daily nitrate-free period (usually overnight) is planned.
- **Contraindication**: with sildenafil or tadalafil (severe fall in BP); low BP.
- **Patient teaching for sublingual GTN**: sit down before taking it (dizziness); place under the tongue and do not swallow; a slight burning or tingling may be felt; repeat only as instructed, and if the pain does not settle call emergency help at once — it may be a heart attack; keep tablets in the original airtight dark glass bottle away from heat and moisture and replace as advised after opening; carry them always.

**β-blockers** reduce heart rate and oxygen demand — first choice for preventing exertional angina (see 4.5).

**Calcium channel blockers (CCBs)**

| Group | Examples | Main effect | Side effects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dihydropyridines | Amlodipine, nifedipine, felodipine | Vasodilatation | Headache, flushing, ankle oedema, reflex tachycardia (short-acting nifedipine) |
| Non-dihydropyridines | Verapamil, diltiazem | Slow the heart and AV conduction; some vasodilatation | Bradycardia, heart block, constipation (verapamil) |

Verapamil should not be combined with β-blockers (risk of severe bradycardia and heart block).

**Other antianginals**: nicorandil (potassium channel opener), ranolazine, trimetazidine, ivabradine (slows the heart rate). All patients also need aspirin and a statin unless contraindicated (Unit 5).

## 4.9 Antiarrhythmic drugs

Arrhythmias are abnormal heart rhythms. Drugs are grouped by the **Vaughan Williams classification**:

| Class | Action | Examples | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | Block sodium channels | Ia: quinidine, procainamide; Ib: lignocaine (lidocaine), mexiletine; Ic: flecainide, propafenone | Lignocaine IV for ventricular arrhythmias |
| II | β-blockers | Propranolol, metoprolol, esmolol | Slow AV conduction; prevent arrhythmias after MI |
| III | Block potassium channels (prolong repolarisation) | Amiodarone, sotalol | Amiodarone: wide use in atrial and ventricular arrhythmias |
| IV | Block calcium channels | Verapamil, diltiazem | Rate control in AF; SVT |
| Others | Adenosine, digoxin, atropine, magnesium sulfate | Adenosine: drug of choice to stop SVT; atropine: bradycardia |

**Amiodarone side effects** (long-term): thyroid dysfunction (it contains iodine), lung fibrosis, liver injury, photosensitivity and bluish-grey skin, corneal deposits, bradycardia; many drug interactions (raises digoxin and warfarin effects).

**Adenosine**: given as a rapid IV push followed immediately by a saline flush, under cardiac monitoring; causes brief flushing, chest discomfort, breathlessness and a few seconds of pause in the heart beat — warn the patient; avoid in asthma.

**Important point**: all antiarrhythmics can themselves cause arrhythmias (pro-arrhythmia).

**Nursing**: continuous ECG monitoring for IV drugs; check pulse rate and rhythm, BP; keep the emergency trolley and defibrillator ready; check potassium and magnesium; report dizziness, fainting, palpitations and breathlessness; regular thyroid, lung and liver checks with amiodarone and sun protection.

## 4.10 Antihypertensive drugs

Hypertension is a persistently raised blood pressure; it damages the heart, brain, kidneys and eyes. Treatment combines **lifestyle changes** with drugs.

```text
 BP = Cardiac output × Peripheral resistance
       ↓ by β-blockers,            ↓ by vasodilators, CCBs, ACE inhibitors,
         diuretics (volume)          ARBs, α-blockers, central drugs
```

**Classification**

| Group | Examples | Points to remember |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diuretics (thiazide-type) | Hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone, indapamide | Low potassium and sodium, raised uric acid (gout), raised blood sugar and lipids |
| ACE inhibitors | Enalapril, ramipril, lisinopril | Dry cough, angioedema, high potassium; protect the kidneys in diabetes; not in pregnancy |
| ARBs | Losartan, telmisartan, olmesartan | No cough; not in pregnancy |
| Calcium channel blockers | Amlodipine, nifedipine (long-acting) | Ankle oedema, headache, flushing |
| β-blockers | Atenolol, metoprolol, labetalol | See 4.5 |
| α-blockers | Prazosin | First-dose hypotension; useful with prostate enlargement |
| Central sympatholytics | Clonidine, methyldopa | Clonidine: drowsiness, dry mouth, **rebound hypertension if stopped suddenly**. Methyldopa: safe in pregnancy; drowsiness; positive Coombs' test |
| Direct vasodilators | Hydralazine, minoxidil, sodium nitroprusside | Reflex tachycardia, fluid retention; minoxidil causes hair growth |
| Renin inhibitor | Aliskiren | Rarely used |

**Special situations**
- **Pregnancy**: methyldopa, labetalol and nifedipine are used; ACE inhibitors and ARBs are contraindicated (harm to the fetal kidneys).
- **Diabetes or kidney disease with protein in urine**: ACE inhibitor or ARB preferred.
- **Hypertensive emergency** (very high BP with organ damage): IV labetalol, nitroprusside, nicardipine or GTN in an ICU, lowering the BP gradually.

**Nursing responsibilities**
1. Measure BP with the correct cuff size, after rest, in the same arm and position each time; check lying and standing BP for postural drop.
2. Teach the patient to rise slowly from lying or sitting (dizziness, falls), especially after the first dose and in the elderly.
3. Explain that hypertension is usually silent — the drug must be taken daily even when the patient feels well, and not stopped suddenly.
4. Lifestyle: less salt, more fruits and vegetables, weight control, regular exercise, stopping smoking, limiting alcohol, stress management.
5. Check electrolytes, kidney function and blood sugar as ordered (diuretics, ACE inhibitors).
6. Report cough or facial swelling (ACE inhibitors), ankle swelling (CCBs), slow pulse (β-blockers).
7. Teach home BP monitoring and keeping a record.

## Remember

- Cholinergic excess = SLUDGE + bradycardia + bronchospasm; anticholinergic = dry mouth, hot dry skin, urinary retention, tachycardia, confusion; not in glaucoma or prostate enlargement.
- Neostigmine: myasthenia gravis and reversal of muscle relaxants. Atropine: pre-anaesthetic, bradycardia.
- Adrenaline IM is the first drug in anaphylaxis; noradrenaline in septic shock; dobutamine in cardiogenic shock.
- β-blockers: check apical pulse; mask hypoglycaemia; bronchospasm; never stop suddenly.
- Digoxin: inhibits Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase; check apical pulse for a full minute; low potassium increases the risk; warning signs — nausea, slow or irregular pulse, yellow vision.
- ACE inhibitors: dry cough, angioedema, high potassium; ACE inhibitors and ARBs not in pregnancy.
- GTN: sublingual, sit down, headache, keep in dark glass bottle; never with sildenafil.
- Verapamil + β-blocker = dangerous bradycardia.
- Amiodarone: thyroid, lungs, liver, skin, eyes.
- Pregnancy hypertension: methyldopa, labetalol, nifedipine. Clonidine: rebound hypertension.

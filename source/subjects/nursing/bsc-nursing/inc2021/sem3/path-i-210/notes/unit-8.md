# Unit 8: Haematological tests, blood chemistry and blood bank

INC Unit III: blood tests (haemoglobin, white cell and platelet counts, PCV, ESR); coagulation tests (BT, PT, APTT); blood chemistry; blood bank — grouping and cross-matching, blood components, plasmapheresis and transfusion reactions. The course competencies also ask you to understand collection of blood, body fluids, urine and faeces, so specimen collection is covered first.

**About normal values**: the ranges below are commonly quoted adult reference values. Each laboratory sets its own ranges depending on its method and population; always read a report against the range printed on it.

## 8.1 Collecting specimens correctly

A test result is only as good as the sample. Most laboratory errors happen **before** the sample reaches the analyser (wrong patient, wrong tube, haemolysis, delay).

### General rules

1. Check the doctor's order and explain the procedure; get consent.
2. **Identify the patient** with at least two identifiers (name and hospital number) and label the container **at the bedside** in the patient's presence.
3. Hand hygiene, gloves and standard precautions; use a new sterile needle and dispose of sharps immediately in a puncture-proof container.
4. Use the correct container, volume and preservative; note the time of collection and any drugs (anticoagulants, antibiotics).
5. Send to the laboratory promptly with a completed request form; follow the storage instructions if there is delay.

### Blood collection tubes (common colour code)

| Cap colour | Additive | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lavender / purple | EDTA (prevents clotting by binding calcium) | Complete blood count, ESR (some methods), blood film, HbA1c, blood grouping |
| Light blue | Sodium citrate (exact 9 : 1 blood-to-citrate ratio — fill to the mark) | PT, INR, APTT, other clotting tests |
| Grey | Sodium fluoride + oxalate (stops glycolysis) | Blood glucose |
| Red / yellow (gel) | No anticoagulant or clot activator — serum | Biochemistry (urea, creatinine, electrolytes, LFT, lipids), serology |
| Green | Heparin | Some chemistry, blood gases (heparinised syringe) |
| Blood culture bottles | Broth | Culture — collected first, with strict asepsis, before antibiotics |

Tips to avoid errors:

- Keep the tourniquet on for **less than one minute**; prolonged tourniquet or fist pumping falsely raises potassium and protein.
- Avoid **haemolysis** (it falsely raises potassium and LDH): use a suitable needle, let alcohol dry, do not force blood through a small needle, do not shake tubes — invert gently.
- Never take blood from the arm with a running IV infusion (dilution); if unavoidable, use the other arm.
- Fill citrate tubes exactly; mix EDTA tubes gently 8–10 times.
- Fasting samples (glucose, lipids) — about 8–12 hours without food as instructed.

### Other specimens (summary)

| Specimen | Method |
| --- | --- |
| Urine routine | Fresh, preferably early morning, clean container |
| Urine culture | **Midstream clean-catch** into a sterile container after cleaning the genital area; from a catheter — aspirate from the sampling port with a sterile syringe, never from the bag |
| 24-hour urine | Discard the first morning urine, then collect all urine for 24 hours including the first urine of the next morning; keep cool; add preservative if instructed |
| Stool | Fresh sample, clean dry container, not mixed with urine; warm and quick for amoebae; sterile container for culture |
| Sputum | Early morning, deep cough after rinsing the mouth with water, sterile container |
| Body cavity fluids (pleural, ascitic, CSF, synovial) | Collected by the doctor under asepsis; nurse prepares the tray, positions the patient, labels tubes in order, sends promptly for cell count, protein, glucose, culture and cytology; observe the patient after the procedure |

## 8.2 Haemoglobin (Hb)

- Haemoglobin carries oxygen in red cells. Measured in g/dL.
- **Methods**: Sahli's acid haematin method (simple, older, less accurate), **cyanmethaemoglobin** method (standard reference method), automated haematology analysers; point-of-care haemoglobinometers are used in the field.
- **Common reference values**: adult men about 13–17 g/dL; adult women about 12–15 g/dL; newborns higher.
- **Low (anaemia)**: iron deficiency (the commonest in India — poor diet, hookworm, blood loss, pregnancy), vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, haemolysis, bone marrow failure, chronic disease, blood loss.
- **High (polycythaemia)**: dehydration (relative), high altitude, chronic lung or heart disease (hypoxia), polycythaemia vera.

### Red cell indices (from automated counters)

| Index | Meaning | Common reference | Low suggests | High suggests |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MCV (mean corpuscular volume) | Average size of red cells | 80–100 fL | Microcytic — iron deficiency, thalassaemia | Macrocytic — B12/folate deficiency, alcohol, liver disease |
| MCH | Average Hb per red cell | 27–32 pg | Hypochromic | — |
| MCHC | Hb concentration in red cells | 32–36 g/dL | Hypochromic (iron deficiency) | Spherocytosis |

So iron-deficiency anaemia = **microcytic hypochromic**; vitamin B12 or folate deficiency = **macrocytic** (megaloblastic).

Other red cell tests: **red cell count** (about 4.5–5.5 million/µL in men, 3.8–4.8 million/µL in women); **reticulocyte count** (young red cells, about 0.5–2.5%) — raised after bleeding, in haemolysis and in response to iron or B12 treatment; low in marrow failure; **peripheral smear** for cell shapes, malaria parasites and abnormal cells.

## 8.3 White cell count

### Total leucocyte count (TLC)

- Common adult reference: about **4,000–11,000 cells/µL**.
- **Leucocytosis** (above 11,000): bacterial infection, inflammation, tissue necrosis (MI), stress, steroids, pregnancy, leukaemia (often very high, with immature cells).
- **Leucopenia** (below 4,000): typhoid, some viral infections (dengue), bone marrow suppression (anticancer drugs, radiation), aplastic anaemia, overwhelming sepsis, hypersplenism, HIV.

### Differential leucocyte count (DLC)

| Cell | Approximate share | Raised in | Lowered in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Neutrophils | 40–75% | Bacterial infection, acute inflammation, MI, steroids (neutrophilia) | Typhoid, viral infections, chemotherapy (neutropenia — infection risk) |
| Lymphocytes | 20–45% | Viral infections, TB, whooping cough, chronic lymphocytic leukaemia | HIV, steroids |
| Monocytes | 2–10% | TB, typhoid, infective endocarditis, recovery from infection | — |
| Eosinophils | 1–6% | Allergy, asthma, **worm infestations**, skin diseases | Steroids, stress |
| Basophils | 0–1% | Chronic myeloid leukaemia, allergy | — |

**Absolute neutrophil count** below about 500/µL means a high risk of serious infection — protective (reverse) isolation precautions.

## 8.4 Platelet count

- Common reference: about **1.5–4.0 lakh/µL** (150,000–400,000/µL).
- **Thrombocytopenia** (low): dengue, viral infections, ITP (immune), aplastic anaemia, leukaemia, chemotherapy, DIC, hypersplenism, drugs (heparin), pregnancy complications (HELLP). Spontaneous bleeding (petechiae, gum bleeding, nose bleeds) becomes likely when the count is very low.
- **Thrombocytosis** (high): bleeding, iron deficiency, inflammation, after splenectomy, myeloproliferative disorders — risk of thrombosis.
- Note: clumping of platelets in a poorly mixed EDTA tube can give a falsely low count; check the smear.

## 8.5 Packed cell volume (PCV, haematocrit)

- The percentage of blood volume occupied by red cells after spinning blood in a tube.
- Common reference: men about **40–50%**, women about **36–46%** (roughly three times the Hb value).
- **Raised**: dehydration, burns (plasma loss), **dengue with plasma leakage** (a rising PCV with falling platelets is a warning sign), polycythaemia.
- **Low**: anaemia, haemorrhage (after fluid shift), overhydration, pregnancy (dilution).

## 8.6 Erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR)

- The rate at which red cells settle in a vertical column of anticoagulated blood, read as **mm at the end of the first hour**.
- **Methods**: **Westergren** (recommended — longer tube, citrate anticoagulant) and Wintrobe.
- Common reference: men up to about 15 mm/1st hour, women up to about 20 mm/1st hour; rises with age.
- **Principle**: raised plasma proteins (**fibrinogen**, globulins) reduce the negative charge between red cells, so they stack like coins (**rouleaux**) and settle faster.
- **Raised**: infections (especially TB), inflammation (RA, rheumatic fever), tissue destruction (MI), cancers, **multiple myeloma** (very high), anaemia, pregnancy, old age.
- **Low**: polycythaemia, sickle cell anaemia, heart failure, very high white count.
- ESR is **non-specific** — it does not diagnose a disease but helps to monitor activity (TB, RA). Precautions: test within a few hours, keep the tube vertical and away from vibration and sunlight, at room temperature.

## 8.7 Coagulation tests

| Test | What it checks | Common reference | Prolonged in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Bleeding time (BT)** | Platelet number and function and the vessel wall (formation of the platelet plug) | Depends on method: about 1–5 minutes by Duke (ear lobe prick); about 2–7 minutes by Ivy (forearm, BP cuff at 40 mmHg) | Thrombocytopenia, platelet function defects, aspirin use, von Willebrand disease, scurvy |
| Clotting time (CT) | Overall clotting of whole blood | About 4–10 minutes (Lee–White method) | Severe clotting factor deficiency, heparin |
| **Prothrombin time (PT)** with **INR** | **Extrinsic** (factor VII) and common pathways (X, V, II, fibrinogen) | About 11–16 seconds (compare with the laboratory control); INR about 1 in a healthy person | **Vitamin K deficiency**, **liver disease**, **warfarin therapy** (monitored by INR), DIC, obstructive jaundice |
| **Activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT)** | **Intrinsic** (XII, XI, IX, VIII) and common pathways | About 25–40 seconds (compare with control) | **Haemophilia A (VIII) and B (IX)**, **unfractionated heparin therapy** (monitored by APTT), von Willebrand disease, DIC, liver disease |

Other tests: fibrinogen level, **D-dimer** (fibrin breakdown product — raised in DVT, pulmonary embolism, DIC), thrombin time, platelet aggregation studies, factor assays.

```text
 Patient bleeding — which test is abnormal?
   BT prolonged, PT and APTT normal ......... platelet problem (low count or function)
   PT prolonged alone ......................... warfarin, vitamin K deficiency, early liver disease
   APTT prolonged alone ....................... haemophilia, heparin
   PT + APTT prolonged, platelets low, D-dimer high ... DIC
```

**INR (international normalised ratio)** makes PT results comparable between laboratories; target ranges for patients on warfarin are set by the doctor for each condition.

## 8.8 Blood chemistry

Serum or plasma is analysed for substances that reflect organ function. Common tests and usual adult reference ranges:

| Test group | Tests | Common reference (adult) | Clinical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blood sugar | Fasting, post-prandial, random, OGTT, HbA1c | Fasting about 70–99 mg/dL; HbA1c below 5.7% | Diabetes (see unit 7), hypoglycaemia |
| Kidney function (RFT) | Blood urea, serum creatinine, uric acid, electrolytes | Urea about 15–40 mg/dL; creatinine about 0.6–1.2 mg/dL | Kidney failure, dehydration, shock |
| Electrolytes | Sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, calcium | Na+ 135–145 mmol/L; K+ 3.5–5.0 mmol/L; calcium about 8.5–10.5 mg/dL | Fluid balance, vomiting, diarrhoea, kidney disease, pancreatitis |
| Liver function (LFT) | Bilirubin (total, direct), ALT, AST, ALP, total protein, albumin, PT | Total bilirubin about 0.3–1.2 mg/dL; albumin about 3.5–5.0 g/dL | Hepatitis (ALT ↑↑), obstructive jaundice (ALP ↑↑), cirrhosis (albumin ↓, PT ↑) |
| Lipid profile (fasting) | Total cholesterol, LDL, HDL, triglycerides | Desirable total cholesterol below 200 mg/dL | Atherosclerosis risk |
| Cardiac markers | Troponin I/T, CK-MB | Troponin below the laboratory cut-off | Myocardial infarction |
| Pancreatic enzymes | Amylase, lipase | — | Acute pancreatitis |
| Others | Arterial blood gases, serum proteins and electrophoresis, CRP, thyroid function, iron studies (serum iron, ferritin) | — | Acid–base status, myeloma, inflammation, thyroid disease, anaemia |

Causes of false results: haemolysis (↑ potassium), delay in separating serum (↓ glucose, ↑ potassium), sample from the drip arm, non-fasting lipid samples.

## 8.9 Blood bank

### Blood groups

**ABO system** — based on A and B antigens on red cells; the plasma contains naturally occurring antibodies (mostly IgM) against the antigen the person lacks.

| Group | Antigen on red cells | Antibody in plasma | Can receive red cells from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | A | Anti-B | A, O |
| B | B | Anti-A | B, O |
| AB | A and B | None | AB, A, B, O ("universal recipient" of red cells) |
| O | None | Anti-A and anti-B | O only ("universal donor" of red cells) |

For **plasma**, the rule reverses: AB plasma can be given to all groups.

**Rh (D) system** — Rh positive if the D antigen is present. Rh-negative people have no natural anti-D; they make it after exposure to Rh-positive blood (transfusion or pregnancy). A later exposure can cause a haemolytic transfusion reaction, and in an Rh-negative mother carrying an Rh-positive baby, **haemolytic disease of the newborn** (prevented by giving anti-D immunoglobulin to the mother as prescribed).

### Blood grouping

- **Forward (cell) grouping**: patient's red cells mixed with anti-A, anti-B and anti-D sera; clumping (agglutination) shows the antigen.
- **Reverse (serum) grouping**: patient's serum mixed with known A and B cells; confirms the forward result.

### Cross-matching (compatibility testing)

- **Major cross-match**: **donor red cells + recipient serum** — the most important; detects recipient antibodies that would destroy donor cells.
- **Minor cross-match**: donor serum + recipient cells (less important now that most blood is given as packed cells).
- Done at room temperature, at 37 °C and with the **antihuman globulin (Coombs) test** to detect incomplete antibodies.
- **Antibody screening** of the recipient's serum for unexpected antibodies.
- **Direct Coombs test** detects antibody already coating red cells (haemolytic disease of the newborn, autoimmune haemolysis, transfusion reaction); **indirect Coombs test** detects free antibody in serum (antenatal screening, cross-match).

### Screening of donated blood

In India every unit must be tested for **HIV, hepatitis B, hepatitis C, syphilis and malaria** before issue. Donors are selected by history and examination (age, weight, Hb, recent illness, high-risk behaviour).

### Blood components

Whole blood is separated by centrifugation into components so that each patient receives only what is needed and one donation helps several patients.

| Component | Contents | Storage | Main uses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Whole blood | All | 2–6 °C | Acute massive blood loss (when components are not available) |
| **Packed red cells (PRBC)** | Red cells with little plasma | **2–6 °C** in a blood bank refrigerator; shelf life up to 35 days (CPDA-1) or 42 days (SAGM additive) | Anaemia, blood loss — raises Hb |
| **Platelet concentrate** (random donor) / apheresis platelets (single donor) | Platelets | **20–24 °C** with **continuous gentle agitation**; short shelf life (about 5 days); never refrigerate | Thrombocytopenia with bleeding, before surgery, dengue with bleeding, leukaemia |
| **Fresh frozen plasma (FFP)** | All clotting factors | Frozen (−30 °C or colder in Indian practice); thawed at 30–37 °C before use and given promptly | Multiple factor deficiency — liver disease, DIC, warfarin bleeding, massive transfusion |
| **Cryoprecipitate** | Factor VIII, fibrinogen, von Willebrand factor, factor XIII | Frozen | Low fibrinogen (DIC, obstetric bleeding), haemophilia A and von Willebrand disease (when factor concentrates are not available) |
| Plasma derivatives | Albumin, factor VIII and IX concentrates, immunoglobulins | As per product | Burns and low albumin, haemophilia, immune deficiency |

### Plasmapheresis (therapeutic plasma exchange)

- **Definition**: blood is taken out, the **plasma is separated** by a cell separator (apheresis machine), and the blood cells are returned to the patient with a replacement fluid (albumin or FFP). It removes harmful substances in plasma — antibodies, immune complexes, toxins, abnormal proteins.
- **Uses**: Guillain–Barré syndrome, myasthenia gravis crisis, thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP), hyperviscosity in myeloma, some kidney and autoimmune diseases, some poisonings.
- **Donor apheresis** collects only plasma, platelets or other components from a donor.
- **Complications**: low calcium from the citrate anticoagulant (tingling around the mouth, cramps), low blood pressure, bleeding, infection at the line, allergic reactions to replacement fluid.

### Transfusion reactions

Any unfavourable event during or after transfusion.

| Reaction | Cause | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Acute haemolytic** (most dangerous) | **ABO-incompatible** blood — usually a clerical error (wrong patient or wrong unit) | Within minutes: fever, chills, pain in the back or loin, chest pain, burning at the IV site, low BP, shock, red urine (**haemoglobinuria**), bleeding (DIC), kidney failure |
| Febrile non-haemolytic | Antibodies against donor white cells, cytokines in stored blood | Fever and chills; commonest; reduced by leucodepleted blood |
| Allergic (urticarial) | Reaction to donor plasma proteins | Itching, hives |
| Anaphylactic | Severe allergy (e.g., IgA-deficient recipient) | Bronchospasm, hypotension, collapse |
| TACO (transfusion-associated circulatory overload) | Too much or too fast, especially in elderly, heart disease, severe anaemia | Breathlessness, raised BP, crackles, raised JVP |
| TRALI (transfusion-related acute lung injury) | Donor antibodies damage the recipient's lungs | Breathlessness and low oxygen within 6 hours |
| Bacterial contamination (septic) | Contaminated unit (commonest with platelets stored warm) | High fever, rigors, shock |
| Delayed haemolytic | Antibody from an earlier transfusion or pregnancy rises again | Days later: falling Hb, jaundice, fever |
| Transfusion-transmitted infections | HIV, hepatitis B and C, syphilis, malaria | Weeks to months later |
| **Massive transfusion** problems | Large volumes of stored blood | Low calcium (citrate), high potassium, hypothermia, dilution of clotting factors and platelets |
| Long-term | Repeated transfusion (thalassaemia) | **Iron overload** (haemosiderosis) |

## 8.10 Nurse's role in blood transfusion

**Before**

1. Check the doctor's order and the patient's consent; record baseline temperature, pulse, BP, respiration and SpO2.
2. Collect the grouping and cross-matching sample from the correct patient, label it at the bedside (most fatal reactions start with a wrongly labelled sample).
3. Collect the unit from the blood bank just before use.
4. **Bedside check by two qualified persons**: patient's name, hospital number and blood group on the wristband and request form; unit number, group, Rh, expiry date and cross-match label on the bag; inspect the bag for leaks, clots, abnormal colour or bubbles.

**During**

5. Use a blood administration set with a filter and an adequate IV cannula; use only normal saline in the line — never glucose (causes clumping and haemolysis) or drugs.
6. Start slowly and **stay with the patient for the first 15 minutes** (most severe reactions appear early); then check vitals regularly as per policy.
7. Start the transfusion soon after the unit leaves the refrigerator and complete each red cell unit within about **4 hours**; platelets and FFP are given promptly after issue.

**If a reaction is suspected**

8. **Stop the transfusion immediately**; keep the vein open with normal saline through a new set.
9. Inform the doctor and blood bank; check vitals and treat as ordered.
10. Recheck the patient's identity and the bag label.
11. Send the **blood bag and giving set** with a fresh **blood sample** (from the other arm) and the **first urine sample** to the blood bank/laboratory; fill the transfusion reaction form.
12. Monitor urine output and colour, and document everything.

**After**

13. Record the volume, time started and finished, unit number and any reaction; monitor for delayed reactions.

## Remember

- Label at the bedside with two identifiers; most errors are pre-analytical.
- EDTA (lavender) — CBC; citrate (blue, 9 : 1) — PT/APTT; fluoride (grey) — glucose; plain/gel — chemistry.
- Hb men 13–17, women 12–15 g/dL; iron deficiency = microcytic hypochromic; B12/folate = macrocytic.
- TLC 4,000–11,000/µL; neutrophilia = bacterial; eosinophilia = allergy, worms; typhoid = leucopenia.
- Platelets 1.5–4 lakh/µL; PCV about 3 × Hb; rising PCV + falling platelets in dengue is a warning.
- ESR (Westergren) — non-specific; fibrinogen → rouleaux; very high in myeloma.
- BT = platelets; PT/INR = extrinsic, warfarin, vitamin K, liver; APTT = intrinsic, haemophilia, heparin.
- Group O = universal red cell donor; AB = universal recipient (red cells) and universal plasma donor.
- Major cross-match = donor cells + recipient serum.
- PRBC 2–6 °C; platelets 20–24 °C with agitation; FFP frozen; cryoprecipitate = fibrinogen + factor VIII.
- Plasmapheresis removes plasma (antibodies, toxins); risk of low calcium from citrate.
- Acute haemolytic reaction = ABO mismatch → stop, keep vein open with saline, send bag + blood + urine.
- Stay with the patient for the first 15 minutes; only normal saline in the line.

# How to use this guide

Pathology I is a third-semester subject of the INC 2021 B.Sc Nursing curriculum. The INC gives it **1 credit (20 hours of theory, which include a few laboratory and visit hours)**. It teaches general pathology (how cells, tissues and organs react to disease), the systemic (special) pathology of common diseases, and the laboratory and blood-bank tests a nurse meets every day. Study it alongside your Adult Health Nursing I pathophysiology, because the two explain the same diseases from different angles.

## This is a "Part I" course — no university exam this semester

**There is no end-semester university theory examination for Pathology I in Semester 3.** Only internal (sessional/college) marks are recorded this semester, and they are **carried forward**. Pathology is examined by the university **together with Pathology II and Genetics (PATH(II)210) in Semester 4**, as one written paper.

In that Semester 4 paper, pathology forms a **section of a combined 3-hour, 75-mark paper** shared with Applied Pharmacology II:

| Section | Subject | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Pharmacology II | 38 |
| B | Pathology (Part I + Part II) | 25 |
| C | Genetics | 12 |

So the pathology you learn now is **not wasted or over by the end of this semester** — it comes back in the Section B pathology questions next semester. Revise it again before the Part II exam. Genetics is taught and examined with Pathology II (Section C); it is **not** part of this Part I course, so it is not covered here.

Because the real exam is next semester, the mock test in this pack is called a **"Practice paper (no university exam this semester)."** It is built in the shape of the **Section B pathology** questions you will meet in Semester 4, so you can test yourself in the right style now.

## How the units are arranged

The INC syllabus for Pathology I has only **three units**, but Unit I and Unit II each pack in many separate topics. To keep every notes file a useful size, the three INC units are **split into eight notes files**:

| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit 1 Introduction, cell injury and cellular adaptations | I | Importance and terms of pathology, cell injury, necrosis, gangrene, cellular adaptations, apoptosis |
| Unit 2 Inflammation and wound healing | I | Acute, chronic and granulomatous inflammation; wound healing |
| Unit 3 Neoplasia | I | Nomenclature, normal vs cancer cell, benign vs malignant, carcinoma in situ, metastasis |
| Unit 4 Circulatory disturbances, oedema and body fluids | I | Thrombosis, embolism, shock; oedema; transudate and exudate |
| Unit 5 Special pathology: respiratory and cardiovascular systems | II | Pneumonia, lung abscess, TB, COPD, lung tumours; atherosclerosis, ischaemia/infarction, rheumatic and infective heart disease |
| Unit 6 Special pathology: GIT, liver, gall bladder and pancreas | II | Peptic ulcer, gastritis/H. pylori, oral and GI cancers, typhoid ulcer, IBD; hepatitis, cirrhosis, cholecystitis, pancreatitis |
| Unit 7 Special pathology: skeletal and endocrine systems | II | Bone healing, osteoporosis, osteomyelitis, bone tumours, arthritis; diabetes, goitre, thyroid cancer |

The numbers 1–8 are the StudyDesk unit numbers; the right-hand column shows the matching INC unit. Study one notes file at a sitting: read it, draw each `text` diagram once from memory, then answer that unit's questions and MCQs without looking.

## The practice paper pattern

The practice paper follows the **Section B pathology** style of the Semester 4 combined paper, as given in INC Regulations 2020, Appendix 3. There is **no internal choice** — every question is to be answered. Confirm the exact pattern with your university, as universities print papers differently.

| Part | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MCQ | 4 multiple-choice questions | 4 × 1 = 4 |
| Short | 3 short answers | 3 × 5 = 15 |
| Very short | 3 very short answers | 3 × 2 = 6 |
| **Total** | | **25** |

The pathology section has **no long essay** (the 10-mark essays in that combined paper fall in the pharmacology section), so the practice paper has no essay either. Even so, this pack still gives you long (essay-style) questions in each unit, because learning the full structured answer is the surest way to score in the short answers and in next semester's exam. The practice paper is timed at **60 minutes** (about 2.4 minutes per mark, the usual pace).

A separate **Quick MCQ test (20 questions, 20 minutes)** lets you drill facts fast before a class test or viva.

## Probability ratings

- **High** — a favourite; comes up in most papers in some form. Learn the full answer.
- **Medium** — appears every few papers, or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — less common, but worth knowing for MCQs and very short answers.

These ratings are estimates from the weight of each topic in the INC syllabus and the usual style of nursing pathology papers. They are a guide, not a promise of what will be asked.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. **Lead every answer with a one-line definition.** Pathology examiners reward a crisp definition of the term before you explain it.
2. **Use the cause → mechanism → changes → effects → nursing frame.** For any disease write aetiology (cause), pathogenesis (how it develops), morphology (the changes), clinical and laboratory features, and a couple of nursing points. This frame fits almost every question.
3. **Comparisons are easy marks — answer them as tables.** Dry vs wet gangrene, apoptosis vs necrosis, acute vs chronic inflammation, benign vs malignant, gastric vs duodenal ulcer, rheumatoid vs osteoarthritis, transudate vs exudate.
4. **Learn the normal laboratory values** from Unit 8 (Hb, TLC, platelets, PCV, ESR, BT, PT, APTT, blood sugar). MCQs and very short answers love them.
5. **Add nursing points.** Every INC unit asks you to apply pathology in nursing — specimen collection, pressure-sore prevention, wound care, diabetic foot care, transfusion safety. One or two lines lift a short answer.
6. **For very short answers (2 marks)** give a one-line definition plus one example or one fact. Do not write a paragraph.
7. **The night before,** read the "Remember" list at the end of each notes file and run the Quick MCQ test.

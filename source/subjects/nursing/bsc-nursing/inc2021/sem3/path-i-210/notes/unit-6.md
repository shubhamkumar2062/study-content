# Unit 6: Special pathology — gastrointestinal tract, liver, gall bladder and pancreas

INC Unit II (second part): oral leukoplakia and squamous cell carcinoma; oesophageal cancer; gastritis and *H. pylori*; peptic ulcer; gastric cancer; typhoid ulcer; inflammatory bowel disease; colorectal cancer; hepatitis, amoebic liver abscess and cirrhosis; cholecystitis; pancreatitis; tumours of liver, gall bladder and pancreas.

## 6.1 Oral cavity

### Oral leukoplakia

- **Definition**: a white patch or plaque on the oral mucosa that **cannot be rubbed off** and cannot be explained by any other disease.
- **Causes**: tobacco — chewing (gutkha, khaini, betel quid with tobacco) and smoking (including reverse smoking); areca nut; alcohol; chronic irritation from sharp teeth or ill-fitting dentures.
- **Sites**: buccal mucosa (cheek), floor of the mouth, tongue, lips.
- **Microscopy**: thickened epithelium with keratosis; varying degrees of **dysplasia**.
- **Significance**: a **premalignant lesion** — a proportion turns into squamous cell carcinoma; red patches (**erythroplakia**) carry a higher risk. **Oral submucous fibrosis** (from areca nut) is another premalignant condition common in India.

### Squamous cell carcinoma of the oral cavity

- One of the commonest cancers among men in India, closely linked to **tobacco chewing**, areca nut and alcohol; human papillomavirus causes some oropharyngeal cancers.
- Often arises in leukoplakia; common sites: buccal mucosa and gingivobuccal sulcus (where the quid is held), tongue (lateral border), floor of mouth.
- **Appearance**: non-healing ulcer with raised, hard (indurated), everted edges, or a cauliflower-like growth.
- **Microscopy**: invasive nests of squamous cells, often with **keratin pearls** (well differentiated).
- **Spread**: local invasion (jaw), cervical lymph nodes; distant spread late.
- **Diagnosis**: biopsy of the edge of the ulcer; FNAC of neck nodes.

## 6.2 Oesophageal cancer

| Feature | Squamous cell carcinoma | Adenocarcinoma |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frequency in India | Commonest type | Less common |
| Site | Middle third (also upper and lower) | Lower third, near the stomach |
| Risk factors | Tobacco, alcohol, very hot drinks, nutritional deficiency, achalasia, caustic injury, nitrosamines | **Barrett's oesophagus** from long-standing acid reflux, obesity, smoking |

- **Features**: **progressive dysphagia** (first solids, then liquids), weight loss, pain on swallowing, regurgitation, hoarseness, aspiration pneumonia, fistula into the trachea.
- **Diagnosis**: barium swallow (irregular narrowing), endoscopy with biopsy, CT for staging.
- Prognosis is poor because the oesophagus has no serosa and a rich lymphatic network, so it spreads early.

## 6.3 Gastritis and *Helicobacter pylori*

**Gastritis** is inflammation of the gastric mucosa.

- **Acute gastritis** — causes: NSAIDs and aspirin, alcohol, severe stress (burns, shock, head injury), chemotherapy, uraemia. Features: epigastric pain, nausea, vomiting, sometimes bleeding (haematemesis, melaena).
- **Chronic gastritis** — the commonest cause is ***Helicobacter pylori*** infection; others are autoimmune gastritis (antibodies to parietal cells and intrinsic factor → **pernicious anaemia** due to vitamin B12 deficiency), bile reflux, chemicals.

### *Helicobacter pylori*

- Curved, spiral, gram-negative, flagellated bacillus that lives in the mucus layer of the stomach. Infection is very common, usually acquired in childhood (faeco-oral and oral–oral spread).
- How it survives acid: produces **urease** (splits urea → ammonia, which neutralises acid), moves with flagella, sticks to the epithelium, releases toxins that injure cells.
- Diseases linked to it: chronic gastritis, **peptic ulcer** (especially duodenal), gastric adenocarcinoma, gastric MALT lymphoma.
- **Tests**: urea breath test, stool antigen test, rapid urease test on an endoscopic biopsy, histology, serology (antibodies show past exposure).

## 6.4 Peptic ulcer disease

A **peptic ulcer** is a breach in the mucosa of the stomach or duodenum that extends through the muscularis mucosae, caused by **acid and pepsin**. It occurs where the lining is exposed to acid: first part of the duodenum, stomach (lesser curvature), lower oesophagus, and around a gastrojejunostomy.

### Pathogenesis — imbalance between aggressive and defensive factors

```text
  AGGRESSIVE                                   DEFENSIVE
  Acid and pepsin                              Mucus and bicarbonate layer
  H. pylori                         vs.        Mucosal blood flow
  NSAIDs/aspirin (block prostaglandins)        Prostaglandins
  Smoking, alcohol, stress                      Rapid epithelial renewal
  Zollinger–Ellison syndrome (gastrin tumour)
              When aggression > defence → ULCER
```

### Gastric versus duodenal ulcer

| Feature | Duodenal ulcer | Gastric ulcer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frequency | More common | Less common |
| Age | Younger adults | Older (above 50) |
| Acid level | Usually high | Normal or low |
| *H. pylori* | Present in most | Present in many |
| Site | First part of the duodenum (within 2–3 cm of the pylorus), anterior wall | Lesser curvature, near the antrum |
| Pain | 2–3 hours after meals and at night; **relieved by food** | Soon after meals; **worsened by food** |
| Weight | Often maintained or gained | Weight loss (fear of eating) |
| Malignant change | Almost never | Rare; a gastric ulcer must be biopsied to exclude cancer |

### Morphology

- Usually single, round or oval, **punched-out** with straight walls and a clean base, less than 2 cm; mucosal folds radiate from the ulcer.
- Microscopy shows four zones from surface to base: necrotic debris → inflammatory cells (neutrophils) → granulation tissue → fibrous scar.

### Complications

- **Bleeding** (commonest): haematemesis (vomiting blood — "coffee-ground" when altered) and melaena (black tarry stools); anaemia.
- **Perforation**: sudden severe pain, board-like rigid abdomen, peritonitis, air under the diaphragm on X-ray — a surgical emergency.
- **Pyloric (gastric outlet) obstruction** from scarring: vomiting of stale food, visible gastric peristalsis.
- **Penetration** into the pancreas (back pain).
- Malignant change (gastric ulcer only, rare).

**Lab**: endoscopy and biopsy, *H. pylori* tests, haemoglobin, stool for occult blood; serum gastrin if Zollinger–Ellison syndrome is suspected.

## 6.5 Gastric cancer

- Almost all are **adenocarcinomas**; other tumours are lymphoma and GIST.
- **Risk factors**: *H. pylori*, diet high in smoked, salted and pickled food and nitrates, low fruit and vegetables, smoking, chronic atrophic gastritis and intestinal metaplasia, pernicious anaemia, previous gastric surgery, adenomatous polyps, blood group A, family history.
- **Gross types**: fungating (polypoid), ulcerating (ulcer with raised irregular edges), and diffusely infiltrating — **linitis plastica** ("leather-bottle stomach"), in which the whole wall is thickened and rigid.
- **Microscopic types**: intestinal (gland-forming) and diffuse (scattered **signet-ring cells** with mucin pushing the nucleus aside).
- **Features**: vague indigestion, loss of appetite, early fullness, weight loss, anaemia, vomiting, a mass in the epigastrium; often presents late.
- **Spread**: lymph nodes (including the left supraclavicular **Virchow's node**), liver (portal vein), peritoneum, ovaries (**Krukenberg tumour**), umbilicus (Sister Mary Joseph nodule).
- **Diagnosis**: endoscopy with multiple biopsies; anaemia and occult blood in stool; CT for staging.

## 6.6 Typhoid ulcer

- **Cause**: *Salmonella typhi* (enteric fever), spread through contaminated food and water.
- **Mechanism**: bacilli cross the intestinal wall, enter lymphoid tissue (**Peyer's patches** of the terminal ileum), multiply, enter the blood, reach the gall bladder and return to the gut in bile; the Peyer's patches become swollen, then necrotic, and slough in the 2nd–3rd week.
- **Typhoid ulcers**: **oval, long axis along the length of the bowel** (parallel to the gut, following the Peyer's patch), on the antimesenteric border of the ileum. (Contrast tuberculous ulcers, which are **transverse** — girdle-like — and heal with strictures.) Typhoid ulcers heal without stricture.
- **Complications** (usually in the third week): **intestinal perforation** and **haemorrhage**; also myocarditis, cholecystitis, carrier state (bacilli in the gall bladder).
- **Lab**: **blood culture** is the best test in the first week; stool and urine culture later (2nd–3rd week); bone marrow culture is most sensitive; Widal test shows a rising antibody titre after the first week (interpret with caution); white cell count often low (leucopenia) with relative lymphocytosis.

## 6.7 Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)

Chronic, relapsing inflammatory disease of the intestine due to an abnormal immune response to gut bacteria in genetically susceptible people. Two main forms: **Crohn's disease** and **ulcerative colitis**.

| Feature | Crohn's disease | Ulcerative colitis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Site | Any part, mouth to anus; terminal ileum most common | Colon and rectum only; starts in the rectum |
| Distribution | **Skip lesions** (patchy) | **Continuous** from the rectum upward |
| Depth | **Transmural** (full thickness) | Mucosa and submucosa only |
| Gross | Thick wall, narrow lumen ("string sign" on barium), **cobblestone** mucosa, deep fissures, **fistulae**, strictures | Broad ulcers, **pseudopolyps**, thin wall, loss of folds |
| Microscopy | **Non-caseating granulomas** in many | Crypt abscesses, crypt distortion, no granulomas |
| Bleeding per rectum | Less common | Common (bloody diarrhoea with mucus) |
| Complications | Fistulae, abscesses, strictures, malabsorption (vitamin B12), perianal disease | Toxic megacolon, severe bleeding, higher risk of colorectal cancer |
| Smoking | Worsens it | Seems to protect |
| Extra-intestinal | Both: arthritis, eye inflammation (uveitis), skin lesions (erythema nodosum), liver disease (primary sclerosing cholangitis more with UC) | |

**Lab**: anaemia, raised ESR and CRP, low albumin, **faecal calprotectin** (marker of gut inflammation), stool examination to exclude infections (amoebiasis, TB are common mimics in India), colonoscopy with biopsy.

## 6.8 Colorectal cancer

- Mostly **adenocarcinoma**; most arise from an **adenomatous polyp** (adenoma–carcinoma sequence).
- **Risk factors**: age above 50, diet low in fibre and high in red/processed meat and fat, obesity, inactivity, alcohol, smoking, long-standing ulcerative colitis, familial adenomatous polyposis and hereditary non-polyposis colorectal cancer (Lynch syndrome), family history.
- **Right-sided (caecum, ascending colon)**: bulky polypoid growths; present with **iron-deficiency anaemia**, weakness, a mass, vague pain; obstruction is rare (wide lumen, liquid stool).
- **Left-sided (descending, sigmoid, rectum)**: annular "napkin-ring" growths; present with **change in bowel habit**, blood in stool, tenesmus, ribbon-like stools, **intestinal obstruction**.
- **Spread**: lymph nodes, **liver** (portal vein) — the commonest distant site, lungs, peritoneum.
- **Staging**: TNM (older Dukes' staging A–D).
- **Lab**: stool for occult blood, haemoglobin, colonoscopy and biopsy, **CEA** (carcinoembryonic antigen) for follow-up and recurrence, CT.

## 6.9 Liver

### Hepatitis

**Hepatitis** = inflammation of the liver. Causes: viruses (the main ones are hepatitis A–E), alcohol, drugs (paracetamol overdose, anti-TB drugs), autoimmune disease, other infections (leptospirosis, malaria).

| Virus | Spread | Chronic disease | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HAV (RNA) | Faeco-oral (contaminated food, water) | No | Common in children, usually mild |
| HBV (DNA) | Blood, sexual, mother to child | Yes (more often when infected at birth) | Cirrhosis, liver cancer; vaccine available |
| HCV (RNA) | Blood (transfusion, unsafe injections), less often sexual | Yes (most cases) | Cirrhosis, liver cancer |
| HDV (RNA) | Blood; needs HBV | Yes | Worsens hepatitis B |
| HEV (RNA) | Faeco-oral, waterborne epidemics | Usually no | Dangerous in pregnant women (fulminant hepatitis) |

**Pathology of acute viral hepatitis**: swelling of liver cells (ballooning), apoptotic liver cells (**Councilman bodies**), spotty necrosis, inflammation in the portal tracts and lobules, cholestasis; full recovery in most. **Fulminant hepatitis** — massive necrosis with acute liver failure. **Chronic hepatitis** — inflammation for more than 6 months, may lead to cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma.

**Clinical stages**: incubation → prodromal (loss of appetite, nausea, fever, aversion to smoking) → icteric (jaundice, dark urine, pale stools, itching, tender liver) → recovery.

**Lab**: markedly raised **ALT and AST** (ALT usually higher in viral hepatitis); raised bilirubin (conjugated and unconjugated), bile in urine; raised prothrombin time in severe disease; viral markers — **anti-HAV IgM**, **HBsAg** (infection), **anti-HBc IgM** (acute B), **HBeAg** (high infectivity), **anti-HBs** (immunity), anti-HCV and HCV RNA, anti-HEV IgM.

### Amoebic liver abscess

- **Cause**: *Entamoeba histolytica*; trophozoites from intestinal amoebiasis travel through the **portal vein** to the liver.
- **Site**: usually single, in the **right lobe**, upper and posterior part.
- **Contents**: thick, odourless, chocolate-brown or "**anchovy sauce**" pus (liquefied liver tissue); amoebae are found at the edge (wall) rather than in the pus.
- **Features**: fever, pain in the right upper abdomen or right shoulder tip, tender enlarged liver, intercostal tenderness; more common in adult men, especially with alcohol use.
- **Complications**: rupture into the pleura, lung (coughing up anchovy-sauce sputum), peritoneum or pericardium (dangerous with left-lobe abscess).
- **Lab**: neutrophil leucocytosis, raised alkaline phosphatase, ultrasound (most useful), serology for amoebic antibodies; stool may not show cysts.

### Cirrhosis of liver

- **Definition**: a diffuse, irreversible process in which the normal liver structure is replaced by **regenerative nodules** separated by **bands of fibrosis**, throughout the liver.
- **Causes**: chronic alcohol use, chronic hepatitis B and C, **non-alcoholic fatty liver disease** (obesity, diabetes), biliary obstruction (biliary cirrhosis), haemochromatosis, Wilson's disease, autoimmune hepatitis, drugs; some are unknown (cryptogenic).
- **Mechanism**: repeated liver cell injury → activated **hepatic stellate (Ito) cells** become fibroblast-like and lay down collagen → fibrous bands link portal tracts and central veins → surviving cells regenerate as nodules → blood flow within the liver is disturbed.
- **Types by nodule size**: micronodular (less than 3 mm — typical of alcohol), macronodular (more than 3 mm — after viral hepatitis), mixed.

```text
 Liver cell failure                          Portal hypertension
 ─────────────────                           ──────────────────
 Jaundice                                    Oesophageal varices → haematemesis
 Low albumin → oedema, ascites               Splenomegaly → low platelets and WBC (hypersplenism)
 Low clotting factors → bleeding, ↑ PT       Ascites
 Hepatic encephalopathy (ammonia)            Caput medusae, haemorrhoids
 Spider naevi, palmar erythema,              
 gynaecomastia, testicular atrophy (oestrogen excess)
 Hepatorenal syndrome
```

**Lab**: low albumin, raised prothrombin time (both show reduced liver function), raised bilirubin, AST often higher than ALT (in alcohol), low platelets, anaemia, raised ammonia in encephalopathy; ultrasound (small nodular liver, ascites, splenomegaly), endoscopy for varices, liver biopsy; alpha-fetoprotein to screen for liver cancer.

**Complications**: variceal bleeding, ascites and spontaneous bacterial peritonitis, encephalopathy, kidney failure, **hepatocellular carcinoma**.

### Tumours of the liver

- **Metastases** are the commonest liver tumours (from colon, stomach, pancreas, breast, lung) — multiple nodules.
- **Hepatocellular carcinoma** (hepatoma) — the commonest primary malignant liver tumour. Risk factors: chronic **hepatitis B and C**, cirrhosis (any cause), **aflatoxin** (from *Aspergillus* in damp stored grains and groundnuts), alcohol. Features: worsening of a known cirrhosis, pain, mass, weight loss, ascites. Marker: raised **alpha-fetoprotein**.
- Benign: haemangioma (commonest benign), liver cell adenoma (linked with oral contraceptive use).
- Cholangiocarcinoma — from bile duct epithelium; liver flukes are a risk factor in parts of Asia.

## 6.10 Gall bladder

### Cholecystitis

- **Acute cholecystitis**: in about 90% of cases due to a **gallstone** blocking the cystic duct → bile trapped → chemical irritation → secondary bacterial infection (*E. coli*, *Klebsiella*, enterococci). Without stones (acalculous) in critically ill patients, after surgery, burns or trauma, and in typhoid.
  - Features: pain in the right upper abdomen radiating to the right shoulder, fever, vomiting, **Murphy's sign** (catching of breath on deep inspiration while pressing under the right costal margin).
  - Complications: empyema of the gall bladder, gangrene and perforation, peritonitis.
- **Chronic cholecystitis**: almost always with gallstones; thickened fibrotic wall; repeated attacks of pain and fatty-food intolerance (dyspepsia).
- **Gallstones** (cholelithiasis): cholesterol stones (commonest; risk — "female, fat, forty, fertile", rapid weight loss), pigment stones (haemolytic anaemia such as sickle cell disease), mixed. Complications: cholecystitis, obstructive jaundice (stone in the common bile duct), pancreatitis, gall bladder cancer.
- **Lab**: leucocytosis; raised bilirubin and **alkaline phosphatase** if the duct is blocked; raised amylase/lipase if pancreatitis; **ultrasound** is the investigation of choice.

### Tumours of the gall bladder

- **Carcinoma of the gall bladder** (adenocarcinoma) — common in north and north-east India (Gangetic belt); strongly associated with long-standing **gallstones**; presents late with pain, jaundice, a mass and weight loss; poor prognosis. A "porcelain" calcified gall bladder carries a risk.

## 6.11 Pancreas

### Pancreatitis

- **Acute pancreatitis**: inflammation of the pancreas due to **premature activation of its own enzymes** (trypsin, lipase) inside the gland → autodigestion.
  - Causes: **gallstones** and **alcohol** (together most cases); others — high triglycerides, high calcium, trauma and ERCP, drugs, mumps, scorpion sting, idiopathic.
  - Pathology: oedema, **fat necrosis** (chalky white spots — calcium soaps), haemorrhage and necrosis of the gland (acute haemorrhagic pancreatitis).
  - Features: sudden severe epigastric pain **radiating to the back**, relieved by leaning forward; vomiting; fever; shock in severe cases; bruising in the flanks (Grey Turner's sign) or around the umbilicus (Cullen's sign) in haemorrhagic cases.
  - **Lab**: **serum amylase and lipase** raised (lipase more specific and stays raised longer), leucocytosis, **low calcium** (used up in fat necrosis — a sign of severity), high blood sugar, raised CRP; CT scan.
  - Complications: shock, ARDS, kidney failure, DIC, pancreatic pseudocyst, abscess.
- **Chronic pancreatitis**: repeated inflammation leading to fibrosis and calcification; mostly alcohol (also tropical chronic pancreatitis, seen in south India). Features: recurrent pain, malabsorption with fatty stools (**steatorrhoea**), weight loss, **diabetes**; X-ray shows calcification.

### Tumours of the pancreas

- **Pancreatic adenocarcinoma** (ductal) — commonest; risk factors: smoking, chronic pancreatitis, diabetes, obesity, family history. Most arise in the **head of the pancreas** → **painless progressive obstructive jaundice** with a palpable gall bladder (Courvoisier's law), weight loss, back pain; migratory thrombophlebitis (Trousseau's sign) may occur. Very poor prognosis. Marker: CA 19-9.
- Endocrine (islet cell) tumours — insulinoma (low blood sugar), gastrinoma (Zollinger–Ellison syndrome).

## 6.12 Application in nursing

- **Stool collection**: a fresh sample in a clean, dry, labelled container without urine; for amoebae examine warm and fresh (within an hour); for occult blood follow any diet instructions given by the laboratory; for culture use a sterile container.
- **Upper GI bleeding**: monitor pulse, BP, colour and amount of vomit and stool; save samples for inspection; prepare for blood grouping and cross-matching; keep nil by mouth until assessed.
- **Peptic ulcer**: teach regular meals, avoid smoking, alcohol and NSAIDs without advice, complete *H. pylori* treatment; report sudden severe pain (perforation).
- **Oral cancer prevention**: counsel against tobacco and areca nut; examine the mouth and teach self-examination; refer white or red patches and non-healing ulcers.
- **Typhoid**: safe water and food hygiene, hand washing; watch in the 3rd week for sudden abdominal pain, rigidity or melaena (perforation, bleeding).
- **Hepatitis**: standard precautions and safe injection practice; hepatitis B vaccination for health workers; rest, nutrition, avoid alcohol; observe for confusion and bleeding (liver failure).
- **Cirrhosis**: daily weight and abdominal girth, fluid and sodium restriction as ordered, skin care, bleeding precautions (soft toothbrush, pressure on puncture sites), watch for confusion and flapping tremor.
- **Cholecystitis and pancreatitis**: pain assessment, nil by mouth as ordered, fluid balance, monitor blood sugar and calcium in pancreatitis (tingling, muscle twitching); low-fat diet advice after recovery.
- **Colorectal cancer**: encourage reporting rectal bleeding and change in bowel habit; stoma care education if a colostomy is made.

## Remember

- Leukoplakia: white patch that cannot be scraped off; premalignant; tobacco chewing.
- Oral SCC common in India; keratin pearls; buccal mucosa.
- Oesophagus: SCC middle third (tobacco, alcohol, hot drinks); adenocarcinoma lower third (Barrett's).
- *H. pylori*: urease, gastritis, duodenal ulcer, gastric cancer, MALT lymphoma; urea breath test.
- Duodenal ulcer: pain relieved by food, young, high acid, never malignant. Gastric ulcer: pain after food, biopsy to exclude cancer.
- Peptic ulcer complications: bleeding (commonest), perforation, pyloric obstruction.
- Gastric cancer: linitis plastica, signet-ring cells, Virchow's node, Krukenberg tumour.
- Typhoid ulcer: longitudinal, Peyer's patches, terminal ileum, perforation in week 3; blood culture in week 1.
- Crohn's: skip lesions, transmural, granulomas, fistulae. UC: rectum upward, continuous, mucosal, pseudopolyps, cancer risk.
- Colon cancer: right side anaemia; left side obstruction and altered habit; CEA.
- HAV/HEV faeco-oral; HBV/HCV/HDV blood; HBsAg = infection; anti-HBs = immunity.
- Amoebic liver abscess: right lobe, anchovy-sauce pus.
- Cirrhosis: fibrosis + regenerative nodules; stellate cells; portal hypertension + liver failure; AFP for HCC.
- Cholecystitis: gallstones, Murphy's sign, ultrasound.
- Acute pancreatitis: gallstones and alcohol; lipase and amylase; low calcium; fat necrosis.
- Pancreatic head cancer: painless jaundice, palpable gall bladder.

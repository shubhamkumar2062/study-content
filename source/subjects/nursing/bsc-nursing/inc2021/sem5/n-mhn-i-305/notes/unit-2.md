# Unit 2: Principles and concepts of mental health nursing

This unit covers INC Unit II (8 hours). It builds the core framework every psychiatric nurse needs: the therapeutic relationship, the principles that guide care, the Mental Healthcare Act 2017, and how the nurse promotes mental health and fights stigma.

---

## 2.1 Principles of mental health nursing

Psychiatric nursing practice rests on a set of guiding beliefs that separate it from custodial care.

| Principle | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- |
| Each person is a unique human being with dignity | Never reduce a patient to their diagnosis; call them by name, respect their preferences |
| Every person has worth and deserves respect | Care is non-judgemental regardless of the behaviour or diagnosis |
| People have the right to care in the least restrictive setting | Avoid hospitalisation or restraint unless necessary; prefer outpatient and community care |
| Patients and families are partners in care | Involve them in assessment, goal-setting and treatment decisions |
| Mental health lies on a continuum | People can move towards health with the right support; recovery is possible |
| Illness behaviour is not the whole person | Disturbed behaviour arises from illness, not character; empathy, not blame |
| The nurse-patient relationship is itself therapeutic | Planned, purposeful interaction is a treatment modality |
| Self-awareness is mandatory | The nurse's own feelings, biases and reactions must be monitored to protect the relationship |
| A holistic view is essential | Physical, psychological, social, spiritual and cultural needs all matter |
| Confidentiality and legal rights must be upheld | Follow the Mental Healthcare Act 2017; protect private information |

## 2.2 Mental health–mental illness continuum

Health and illness are not two boxes but the ends of one line. A person moves along it over time:

```text
  POSITIVE MENTAL HEALTH
  ↑ coping, thriving, resilient
  |
  Mild distress (stress, burnout) — still functions, responds to support
  |
  Moderate disorder — symptoms clear; work and relationships impaired
  |
  Severe mental illness — functioning badly disrupted; needs intensive help
  ↓
  SEVERE CHRONIC ILLNESS / CRISIS
```

Implications for nursing:
- Promotion and prevention work best at the healthy end.
- Early intervention at mild and moderate levels stops people reaching severe illness.
- Recovery means moving back up the continuum, not necessarily a "cure."

## 2.3 Therapeutic communication

Communication is the most powerful tool a psychiatric nurse has. **Therapeutic communication** is purposeful, planned interaction that promotes the patient's well-being.

### Principles of therapeutic communication

- Focus on the patient's feelings and needs, not on tasks.
- Use active listening: face the patient, maintain appropriate eye contact, do not interrupt.
- Accept silence — it gives the patient time to think and shows the nurse is comfortable being present.
- Respond to both the content and the feeling in what the patient says.
- Keep language simple, calm and free of jargon.
- Match verbal and non-verbal messages — the patient notices inconsistency.

### Therapeutic communication techniques

| Technique | Description | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Active listening | Full attention, verbal and non-verbal signs of interest | Nodding, leaning forward, "I'm listening" |
| Open-ended questions | Invite the patient to talk freely | "Tell me about how you have been feeling." |
| General leads | Encourage the patient to continue | "Go on." "And then?" |
| Restatement | Repeat the key words back | Patient: "I can't sleep." Nurse: "You can't sleep?" |
| Reflection | Mirror the feeling back | "You sound very frightened." |
| Clarification | Check understanding of unclear statements | "Could you explain what you mean by that?" |
| Focusing | Keep the conversation on one topic | "Let's stay with what you just said about your family." |
| Sharing observations | Describe what the nurse notices | "You seem tense right now." |
| Voicing doubt | Gently question distorted beliefs | "I find it hard to follow that idea." |
| Summarising | Pull together what was said | "So far you have told me … " |
| Offering self | Make the nurse available | "I'll sit with you for a while." |
| Giving information | Share factual, helpful information | Explain a medicine's purpose |

### Non-therapeutic communication (avoid these)

| Block | Why it harms |
| --- | --- |
| Giving advice / "you should …" | Takes over the patient's decisions; reduces autonomy |
| False reassurance / "don't worry, it'll be fine" | Dismisses real distress; breaks trust when things are not fine |
| Changing the subject | Tells the patient their feelings do not matter |
| Agreeing with delusions | Reinforces false beliefs, delays recovery |
| Arguing or challenging beliefs directly | Increases distress; the patient will defend their beliefs more |
| Judgement or moralising | Makes the patient feel ashamed; stops honest sharing |
| Excessive probing or questioning | Feels like interrogation; the patient withdraws |
| Closed questions in quick succession | Only yes/no answers; conversation stops |

## 2.4 The therapeutic nurse-patient relationship

**Hildegard Peplau** described this as a **purposeful, growth-promoting, professional** relationship. It is different from friendship: it has boundaries, a purpose and an end.

### Peplau's four phases

```text
  ORIENTATION → IDENTIFICATION → EXPLOITATION → RESOLUTION
  (meeting,      (patient begins  (patient uses   (goals met,
   assessment,    to identify      all available   relationship
   building        with the nurse   help; nurse     ends; patient
   trust)          as helper)       and patient     moves on)
                                   work together)
```

**Orientation phase:** Patient and nurse meet; the nurse builds trust, assesses needs, explains roles and confidentiality.

**Identification phase:** The patient begins to respond, clarify their problems and use the nurse's help selectively.

**Exploitation phase:** The patient uses services to their fullest; nurse helps with problem-solving and independence.

**Resolution phase:** Goals are achieved; the patient no longer needs care; the relationship is ended positively.

### Nurse roles in Peplau's model

Peplau described six overlapping nursing roles:
- **Stranger** — the initial, respectful meeting
- **Resource person** — giving specific information
- **Teacher** — explaining health and illness
- **Leader** — organising care democratically
- **Surrogate** — taking on a symbolic role the patient assigns
- **Counsellor** — helping the patient understand and integrate experiences

### Characteristics of a therapeutic relationship

- **Trust** — the foundation; built by honesty, consistency and confidentiality
- **Empathy** — understanding and sharing the patient's feelings without losing professional objectivity
- **Respect** — treating the patient as worthy, regardless of behaviour
- **Genuineness** — being real and honest, not performing a role
- **Caring** — taking the patient's well-being seriously
- **Purposeful** — always oriented to the patient's goals

### Professional boundaries

Boundaries protect both patient and nurse. Boundary violations include:
- sharing personal problems with the patient
- giving the patient special gifts or favours
- meeting outside scheduled sessions
- touching beyond comfort and clinical need
- keeping secrets from the team about the patient

The nurse maintains professional distance while remaining warm and caring.

## 2.5 Self-awareness and use of self therapeutically

The nurse's personality, values and feelings are the main instruments of psychiatric nursing. **Use of self therapeutically** means the nurse consciously uses their own thoughts, feelings and behaviour to help the patient.

Requirements:
- Know your own values and beliefs about mental illness.
- Recognise your emotional reactions to patients (counter-transference).
- Monitor personal feelings of fear, disgust, pity or over-involvement.
- Seek supervision when boundaries feel difficult.
- Develop emotional resilience and self-care routines to prevent burnout.

**Counter-transference** — feelings the patient arouses in the nurse — is normal but must be recognised, not acted on.

**Transference** — the patient may project feelings about past relationships onto the nurse (may treat the nurse like a parent or abuser). The nurse does not reinforce this; they explore it gently.

## 2.6 Stigma: causes, effects and the nurse's role

**Stigma** is a mark of shame or discredit applied to a person because of a characteristic that is devalued by society. For mental illness, it has three components:

| Component | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Stereotype | Belief that people with mental illness are dangerous, weak or incompetent |
| Prejudice | Agreeing with the negative belief (the person accepts the stereotype) |
| Discrimination | Acting on the prejudice — refusing employment, excluding from social activities, treating badly in health care |

**Effects of stigma on the patient:**
- delays help-seeking and treatment
- causes shame and self-stigma
- breaks family and social ties
- reduces quality of life and recovery prospects
- can lead to violation of rights

**The nurse's anti-stigma role:**
- Use person-first language ("person with schizophrenia," not "schizophrenic").
- Challenge stigmatising statements from staff, family and the public.
- Provide education about the nature of mental illness as a health condition.
- Model respectful, compassionate care visible to others.
- Advocate for the patient's rights in healthcare and society.

## 2.7 Mental health promotion

Mental health promotion aims to increase protective factors and reduce risk factors for the whole population, not only for people already ill.

**Determinants of mental health (key factors):**

| Protective | Risk |
| --- | --- |
| Secure early attachment and good parenting | Adverse childhood experiences (abuse, neglect) |
| Positive self-esteem and coping skills | Trauma and violence |
| Meaningful work and social roles | Social isolation and poverty |
| Social support network | Substance misuse |
| Access to health services | Chronic physical illness |
| Safe housing and environment | Loss, bereavement, unemployment |

**Mental health promotion strategies:**
- **At individual level:** life skills training, stress management, parenting classes.
- **At community level:** support groups, safe spaces, school mental health programmes, workplace mental health policies.
- **At societal level:** policies against discrimination, social security, media campaigns to reduce stigma.

**The nurse's role in mental health promotion:**
- Teach coping skills and relaxation techniques to patients, families and groups.
- Identify at-risk individuals (new mothers, disaster survivors, unemployed young adults).
- Conduct health education sessions in schools, workplaces and PHCs.
- Refer at-risk individuals for early support before crisis.

## 2.8 Mental Healthcare Act 2017 (India)

The Mental Healthcare Act (MHCA) 2017 replaced the Mental Health Act 1987. It is rights-based: mental illness is treated like any other health condition.

**Key provisions nurses must know:**

| Provision | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Right to access mental health care | Every person can receive mental health care from government services |
| Right to community living | The patient cannot be kept in a hospital against their will if they can live in the community |
| Right to confidentiality | Personal information cannot be shared without consent |
| Right to make an advance directive | A person can write what care they want or refuse before becoming ill |
| Right to have a nominated representative | A chosen person can advocate for the patient if they lose capacity |
| Supported admission (voluntary) | The person admits themselves and can leave at will |
| Independent admission | A person with mental illness but capacity to consent can admit themselves |
| Supported admission (involuntary) | Admission against will only by a Magistrate's order and for limited time, with safeguards |
| Prohibitions | Seclusion, chaining, electro-convulsive therapy without anaesthesia, and sterilisation as a treatment are prohibited |
| Responsibility of establishment | Hospitals and nursing homes must register and meet standards |
| Mental Health Review Boards | Oversight bodies in each district and state |

**Nurse's responsibilities under the MHCA 2017:**
- Obtain informed consent before every procedure and explain right to refuse.
- Maintain strict confidentiality of all patient information.
- Report any rights violation — chaining, neglect, abuse — to the Medical Superintendent and board.
- Respect advance directives and nominated representatives.
- Keep records as required; never falsify documents.

## 2.9 Application and implication in nursing

- Always start by building trust; a patient who does not trust cannot engage with therapy.
- Use therapeutic communication in every interaction — routine nursing moments are therapeutic opportunities.
- End the relationship correctly: a nurse moving to a different ward must prepare the patient in advance so there is no sense of abandonment.
- Mental health promotion is everyone's nursing duty; every patient interaction offers a teaching moment.

## Remember

- The therapeutic relationship is purposeful, professional and time-limited — Peplau's four phases: orientation, identification, exploitation, resolution.
- Therapeutic communication: active listening, open questions, reflection, clarification, offering self.
- Non-therapeutic: false reassurance, advice-giving, agreeing with delusions, changing the subject.
- Boundaries protect both nurse and patient — no self-disclosure of personal problems, no gifts, no outside meetings.
- Stigma has three parts: stereotype, prejudice, discrimination; nurses use person-first language and challenge stigma.
- Mental health promotion works at individual, community and societal levels.
- MHCA 2017: rights-based; advance directive, nominated representative, involuntary admission only by magistrate's order, chaining and ECT without anaesthesia are prohibited.
- Self-awareness is a clinical tool: know your transference and counter-transference; seek supervision.

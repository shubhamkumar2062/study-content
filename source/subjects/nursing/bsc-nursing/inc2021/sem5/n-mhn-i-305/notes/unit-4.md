# Unit 4: Treatment modalities and therapies used in mental disorders

This unit covers INC Unit IV (12 hours). It is the pharmacology and therapies unit of mental health nursing. It covers all the major drug classes used in psychiatry, the main psychotherapies, electro-convulsive therapy (ECT), milieu therapy and rehabilitation. This is a high-scoring unit.

---

## 4.1 Overview of treatment modalities

Mental disorders are treated through a **combination** of approaches, matched to the individual patient's needs.

## 4.2 Pharmacotherapy — antipsychotic drugs

Also called **neuroleptics** or **major tranquillisers**. Used in schizophrenia, other psychoses, mania (adjunct) and delirium.

**Mechanism:** Block **dopamine D2 receptors** in the mesolimbic pathway (reduces positive symptoms). Second-generation agents also block serotonin (5-HT2A) receptors.

| Generation | Examples | Main use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First generation (typical) | Haloperidol, chlorpromazine, trifluoperazine | Positive symptoms; more extrapyramidal side effects |
| Second generation (atypical) | Olanzapine, risperidone, quetiapine, clozapine, aripiprazole | Positive and negative symptoms; fewer extrapyramidal effects |

**Side effects (nurses must know these):**

| Side effect | Description | Onset | Nursing action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS) | Group of movement disorders | Varies | Monitor; report; give prescribed anticholinergic (benztropine) |
| Acute dystonia | Sudden sustained muscle contractions (neck, face, eyes) | Hours to days | Anticholinergic injection (procyclidine/benztropine) |
| Pseudo-parkinsonism | Tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, shuffling gait | Days–weeks | Anticholinergic drugs; adjust dose |
| Akathisia | Restless urge to move; subjective distress | Days–weeks | Beta-blocker (propranolol); dose reduction |
| Tardive dyskinesia | Involuntary repetitive movements of face and limbs; often irreversible | Months–years | Report; dose review; switch drug |
| Sedation | Drowsiness | Early | Safety (falls); do not drive |
| Anticholinergic effects | Dry mouth, blurred vision, constipation, urinary retention | Early | Oral hygiene; fluids; prevent constipation |
| Orthostatic hypotension | Drop in BP on standing | Early | Rise slowly; prevent falls |
| Metabolic effects (atypicals) | Weight gain, raised blood sugar, dyslipidaemia | Weeks–months | Monitor weight, BMI, blood sugar |
| Hyperprolactinaemia | Galactorrhoea, amenorrhoea, sexual dysfunction | Weeks | Monitor; counsel patient |
| Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS) | Hyperthermia, rigidity, altered consciousness, autonomic instability — LIFE-THREATENING | Any time | STOP drug immediately; cooling; IV fluids; call doctor; dantrolene |

**Clozapine:** Very effective for treatment-resistant schizophrenia but causes **agranulocytosis** (1–2%). Weekly/fortnightly WBC monitoring is **mandatory** before each prescription.

**Depot injections:** Long-acting injectable antipsychotics (e.g. fluphenazine decanoate, zuclopenthixol decanoate) given every 2–4 weeks — improve adherence in patients who forget or refuse daily tablets.

## 4.3 Pharmacotherapy — antidepressant drugs

Used in depression, anxiety disorders, OCD, PTSD, panic disorder, chronic pain.

| Class | Examples | Mechanism | Key side effects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Selective serotonin re-uptake inhibitors (SSRIs) | Fluoxetine, sertraline, escitalopram, paroxetine | Block serotonin reuptake | Nausea, insomnia, sexual dysfunction, serotonin syndrome at high doses |
| Serotonin-noradrenaline reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs) | Venlafaxine, duloxetine | Block serotonin and noradrenaline reuptake | As SSRIs + raised BP with venlafaxine |
| Tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs) | Amitriptyline, imipramine, clomipramine | Non-selective reuptake inhibitor + anticholinergic | Anticholinergic effects, cardiac arrhythmia in overdose, weight gain, sedation |
| Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) | Phenelzine, tranylcypromine | Inhibit MAO enzyme | Hypertensive crisis with tyramine-rich foods; cheese, red wine, meat — dietary restriction required |
| Mirtazapine | Mirtazapine | Block alpha2 and 5-HT2/5-HT3 receptors | Sedation, weight gain (useful in underweight) |

**Important nursing points:**
- Antidepressants take **2–4 weeks** for full therapeutic effect; warn patient not to stop early.
- Risk of **suicidal ideation** may increase in the first weeks as energy returns before mood fully lifts; close monitoring is essential.
- **Serotonin syndrome** (agitation, tremor, hyperthermia, diarrhoea, rapid heart rate) is a medical emergency — occurs with overdose or drug combinations; stop drug, supportive care.
- For MAOIs: provide written **dietary restriction** list (tyramine-containing foods to avoid).

## 4.4 Pharmacotherapy — mood stabilisers

Used in bipolar disorder (prevention of both manic and depressive episodes) and sometimes augmenting antidepressants.

| Drug | Key uses | Monitoring |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lithium carbonate** | Bipolar disorder (mania prevention, depression prevention, suicide prevention) | Serum lithium level every 3–6 months (narrow therapeutic range 0.6–1.0 mEq/L for maintenance); thyroid, kidney function every 6–12 months |
| Sodium valproate | Acute mania, bipolar prevention, epilepsy | LFT; teratogenic — not in pregnancy; thrombocytopenia |
| Carbamazepine | Bipolar, epilepsy, trigeminal neuralgia | CBC (aplastic anaemia); LFT; hyponatraemia |
| Lamotrigine | Bipolar depression | Stevens-Johnson syndrome (rash — stop drug); slow dose escalation |

**Lithium toxicity** is a nursing priority (narrow therapeutic window):

```text
  MILD (1.0–1.5 mEq/L): nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, tremor, thirst, polyuria
  MODERATE (1.5–2.0 mEq/L): coarse tremor, confusion, drowsiness, ataxia
  SEVERE (>2.0 mEq/L): seizures, cardiac arrhythmia, coma, LIFE-THREATENING

  NURSING: withhold dose, do not give antidiarrhoeal/diuretic, give IV fluids as ordered,
  report immediately, prepare for haemodialysis if severe.
```

Lithium is excreted by the kidney; dehydration, low-sodium diet and NSAIDs raise levels → toxicity. Teach: drink 2–3 L water/day; never reduce salt; avoid NSAIDs; report early symptoms.

## 4.5 Pharmacotherapy — anxiolytics and hypnotics

| Class | Examples | Use | Key concern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Benzodiazepines | Diazepam, lorazepam, clonazepam, alprazolam | Anxiety, panic, alcohol withdrawal, procedural sedation | Dependence with prolonged use; CNS depression; never combine with alcohol; withdrawal seizures on abrupt stopping |
| Buspirone | Buspirone | Generalised anxiety disorder | No dependence; takes 2–4 weeks to work |
| Propranolol | Propranolol | Situational anxiety, performance anxiety | Mask hypoglycaemia; contraindicated in asthma |
| Hydroxyzine | Hydroxyzine | Short-term anxiety | Sedation; anticholinergic |

**Nursing points:** Benzodiazepines are for short-term use only (risk of dependence, falls, respiratory depression). Never stop abruptly — reduce gradually. Warn against driving.

## 4.6 Psychotherapy

**Psychotherapy** is a planned, structured talking treatment that uses a therapeutic relationship to change thoughts, feelings and behaviours. The nurse may deliver supportive psychotherapy and uses principles of several therapies.

| Therapy | Basis | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supportive psychotherapy | Strengthens existing coping; non-directive | Acute crisis, chronic illness |
| Cognitive behaviour therapy (CBT) | Identify and challenge distorted thoughts (cognitive distortions) and change behaviour | Depression, anxiety, OCD, phobias, eating disorders |
| Behaviour therapy | Learning theory; reinforce desired behaviours | Phobias (desensitisation), OCD (ERP), substance use |
| Systematic desensitisation | Gradual exposure to feared stimulus while relaxed | Phobias, PTSD |
| Aversion therapy | Pair unwanted behaviour with unpleasant stimulus | Substance use, paraphilias |
| Token economy | Reward desired behaviours with tokens (exchangeable for privileges) | Chronic schizophrenia, rehabilitation |
| Psychoanalysis / psychodynamic therapy | Explore unconscious conflicts (Freud) | Long-term characterological problems |
| Interpersonal therapy (IPT) | Focus on relationships and social roles | Depression |
| Family therapy | Work with the whole family system | Schizophrenia, adolescent disorders, eating disorders |
| Group therapy | Therapeutic factors of a group (universality, altruism, cohesion) | Various — psychoeducation, skills, support |
| Play therapy | Using play for assessment and therapy | Children |
| Relaxation techniques | Progressive muscle relaxation, deep breathing, guided imagery | Anxiety, stress |

## 4.7 Electro-convulsive therapy (ECT)

ECT is a physical treatment in which a brief electrical current is passed through the brain to cause a generalised seizure, producing therapeutic effects on mood and psychosis.

### Indications

- **Severe depression** with suicidal risk or refusal to eat (fastest treatment)
- Catatonia
- Severe mania not responding to drugs
- Schizophrenia (catatonic type, or when rapid response needed)
- Drug-resistant cases where delay is dangerous (pregnancy, severe physical illness)

### Contraindications (relative)

- Space-occupying brain lesion (raised intracranial pressure)
- Recent myocardial infarction or stroke
- Unstable cardiac arrhythmia

### Procedure (brief)

Modified ECT is performed under general anaesthesia and muscle relaxant (succinylcholine) to prevent fractures:
1. Fasting for 6–8 hours.
2. Pre-procedure medications as ordered (atropine to reduce secretions; anaesthetic agent).
3. Electrode placement (bilateral or unilateral).
4. Brief electrical pulse → generalised seizure (1–2 minutes).
5. Recovery in recovery area.
Typical course: 6–12 sessions, usually 3 per week.

### Nursing care

**Before ECT:**
- Obtain informed consent (witnessed); explain the procedure to patient and family.
- Ensure nothing by mouth (NBM) for 6–8 hours.
- Remove jewellery, dentures, hearing aids, nail polish, hairpins.
- Empty the bladder.
- Baseline vital signs; remove contact lenses.
- Give prescribed pre-medications.

**During ECT:**
- Assist the anaesthetist; maintain airway.
- Observe and record seizure duration and character.
- Monitor vital signs (pulse oximetry, ECG, BP).

**After ECT (recovery):**
- Position the patient in recovery (lateral) position until conscious.
- Monitor vital signs and level of consciousness; keep airway patent.
- Reorient gently — the patient will be confused and may not remember.
- Assess for headache (common — give prescribed analgesia), nausea, muscle ache.
- Note and document **post-ECT confusion and memory disturbance** (short-term memory loss is the main side effect and usually resolves).
- Do not leave the patient alone until fully oriented.
- Keep nil by mouth until gag reflex has returned.

**Side effects of ECT:**
- Short-term memory loss (usually resolves)
- Headache, muscle aches
- Post-ECT confusion and disorientation
- Cardiovascular stress during the procedure (managed by anaesthesia team)
- Prolonged seizure (rare — have diazepam and succinylcholine ready)

## 4.8 Milieu therapy

**Milieu** means the total environment. **Milieu therapy** is the deliberate structuring of the ward environment to be therapeutic.

**Principles of a therapeutic milieu:**
- **Safety** — physical and emotional safety is maintained at all times
- **Structure** — regular, predictable daily programme (meals, groups, activities, bedtime)
- **Norms** — clear, consistent, fair rules and expectations
- **Limit-setting** — boundaries are set clearly and followed by all staff
- **Open communication** — patients and staff communicate respectfully; patient community meetings
- **Responsibility** — patients take graded responsibility for their own care
- **Participation** — patients actively participate in their treatment plan

**Nurse's role in milieu therapy:**
- Maintain a calm, consistent, structured environment.
- Ensure the ward is physically safe (ligature points removed, access to sharps controlled).
- Role-model healthy communication and behaviour.
- Run ward meetings and psychoeducation groups.
- Apply limits consistently; reinforce positive behaviour.

## 4.9 Activity therapy and occupational therapy

**Activity therapy** uses purposeful activities to improve functioning:
- **Occupational therapy (OT):** work-related activities to restore skills and improve self-esteem.
- **Art therapy:** expressive use of art to process feelings.
- **Music therapy:** use of music for relaxation and emotional expression.
- **Dance/movement therapy:** body movement to integrate emotional experience.
- **Recreational therapy:** games, outings, sports — rebuild social skills and enjoyment.
- **Psychodrama:** role-play of real situations to gain insight.

The nurse encourages participation, notes the patient's response (engagement, withdrawal, agitation) and reports to the team.

## 4.10 Rehabilitation in mental health

**Psychiatric rehabilitation** aims to help people with serious mental illness live satisfying lives in the community with the least support needed.

**Components:**
- **Vocational rehabilitation** — work skills training, sheltered employment, job placement.
- **Social skills training** — specific skills taught (initiating conversation, handling conflict, using public transport).
- **Independent living skills** — cooking, managing money, personal care, using health services.
- **Day care centres** — structured daytime programme without overnight admission.
- **Halfway houses** — supervised community housing as a step between hospital and independent living.
- **Assertive Community Treatment (ACT)** — a mobile team visits the patient at home; avoids repeated hospital admission.
- **Family psychoeducation** — teach family about illness, medication, early warning signs, communication strategies.
- **Self-help groups** — peer support; reduces stigma, shares coping strategies.

**Nurse's role in rehabilitation:**
- Assess functional strengths and deficits (self-care, communication, work, social).
- Set realistic, stepwise goals with the patient.
- Teach skills in small steps; reinforce progress.
- Coordinate with OT, social worker and community team.
- Educate family and reduce expressed emotion (criticism, hostility, over-involvement) which is a major relapse risk.

## Remember

- Antipsychotics block dopamine D2 receptors; atypicals also block serotonin (5-HT2A).
- EPS: acute dystonia (hours) → pseudo-parkinsonism (days) → akathisia (days) → tardive dyskinesia (months–years, may be irreversible).
- NMS: hyperthermia + rigidity + altered consciousness → STOP drug, emergency treatment.
- Clozapine requires weekly WBC monitoring for agranulocytosis.
- Antidepressants take 2–4 weeks to work; monitor for suicidal ideation in early weeks.
- Lithium: therapeutic level 0.6–1.0 mEq/L (maintenance); toxicity = nausea, tremor, then confusion, seizures, coma; hydrate well, avoid NSAIDs, do not reduce salt.
- ECT: before — NBM 6–8 h, consent, remove metal; after — recovery position, reorient, monitor confusion; main side effect is short-term memory loss.
- Milieu therapy: safe, structured, normed therapeutic environment; nurse maintains calm, runs groups, sets limits.
- Rehabilitation: vocational, social skills, independent living, day care, halfway houses, family psychoeducation.

# Unit 7: Nursing management of neurotic, stress-related and somatoform disorders

This unit covers INC Unit VII (10 hours). These disorders are very common in the community and in general hospital settings. The nurse meets them on every ward, not just in a psychiatric unit. Understanding anxiety and somatisation is therefore a core nursing competency.

---

## 7.1 Overview: neurotic, stress-related and somatoform disorders

## 7.2 Normal anxiety versus pathological anxiety

**Anxiety** is an unpleasant emotional state of apprehension and tension, often in response to a vague or unknown threat. Normal anxiety is protective and motivating. Pathological anxiety is:
- excessive or out of proportion to the situation
- persistent and difficult to control
- causing significant distress or impaired functioning

### Levels of anxiety (Peplau)

| Level | Features | Nurse's role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mild (+) | Increased alertness; better learning and problem-solving | Use this — it facilitates coping |
| Moderate (++) | Narrowed focus; selective inattention; some impairment | Redirect attention; stay calm; offer simple guidance |
| Severe (+++) | Greatly narrowed focus; physical symptoms; can focus on only one specific detail | Direct, simple instructions; remain with the patient |
| Panic (++++) | Loss of control; terror; fight or flight; may harm self | Stay with patient; calm environment; medication if ordered; never leave alone |

### Physical manifestations of anxiety

Anxiety activates the **sympathetic nervous system** (fight-or-flight response):

| System | Symptoms |
| --- | --- |
| Cardiovascular | Palpitations, tachycardia, chest pain, raised BP |
| Respiratory | Shortness of breath, hyperventilation, chest tightness |
| Gastrointestinal | Nausea, diarrhoea, dry mouth, difficulty swallowing |
| Musculoskeletal | Trembling, muscle tension, fatigue, restlessness |
| Neurological | Headache, dizziness, tingling, feeling faint |
| Other | Sweating, flushing, frequent urination, sleep disturbance |

## 7.3 Generalised anxiety disorder (GAD)

- **Features:** excessive, uncontrollable worry about multiple topics for ≥6 months; associated with restlessness, fatigue, difficulty concentrating, irritability, muscle tension, sleep disturbance.
- **Aetiology:** genetic predisposition, overactive amygdala, stressful life events, personality.
- **Management:** CBT (most effective long-term), relaxation training, buspirone, SSRI/SNRI.
- **Nursing care:** therapeutic relationship; teach relaxation techniques (progressive muscle relaxation, diaphragmatic breathing, guided imagery); help patient identify worry triggers; support problem-solving.

## 7.4 Panic disorder

- **Panic attack:** a sudden, intense episode of overwhelming fear with physical symptoms (palpitations, shortness of breath, chest pain, dizziness, sweating, trembling, fear of dying or losing control) peaking within 10 minutes.
- **Panic disorder:** recurrent, unexpected panic attacks + persistent concern about further attacks or change in behaviour to avoid them.
- Panic attacks are **not dangerous** (no cardiac events) but are terrifying.
- **Management:** SSRI (first-line long-term); CBT with interoceptive exposure; benzodiazepine short-term for acute attacks.

**Nursing care during a panic attack:**
- Stay with the patient — do not leave.
- Calm, matter-of-fact tone: "You are having a panic attack; it will pass in a few minutes; you are not dying."
- Help the patient breathe more slowly — guide diaphragmatic breathing (breathe in through nose for 4 counts, hold for 2, out through mouth for 4).
- Do not ask many questions; keep the environment quiet.
- After the attack, teach panic management techniques.

## 7.5 Phobias

A **phobia** is a marked, persistent, excessive and unreasonable fear of a specific object, activity or situation that leads to avoidance and causes impaired functioning.

| Type | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Specific phobia | Fear of a specific stimulus: blood, spiders, heights, flying, injections |
| Social phobia (social anxiety disorder) | Intense fear of social situations; embarrassment; avoidance of social contact |
| Agoraphobia | Fear and avoidance of situations where escape is difficult (open spaces, crowds, public transport); often accompanies panic disorder |

**Management:**
- **Systematic desensitisation:** gradual, graded exposure to the feared stimulus while maintaining relaxation (Wolpe).
- **In-vivo exposure/flooding:** exposure to the feared stimulus at full intensity (less comfortable; more rapid results).
- **Cognitive restructuring:** challenge irrational beliefs about the feared stimulus.
- SSRI for social phobia and panic.

**Nursing care:**
- Do not force the patient into the feared situation.
- Teach relaxation; practise before graded exposure.
- Give positive reinforcement for each step in desensitisation.
- Educate about the nature of phobias and that avoidance maintains fear.

## 7.6 Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD)

- **Obsessions:** recurrent, intrusive, unwanted thoughts, images or urges that the patient recognises as their own (unlike thought insertion in schizophrenia) and tries to suppress or neutralise.
- **Compulsions:** repetitive behaviours or mental acts the person feels driven to perform in response to an obsession, to reduce anxiety or prevent a dreaded event. The person knows they are excessive.
- Common obsession–compulsion pairs: contamination (washing); doubt (checking locks, switches); order (arranging symmetrically); harm (checking one has not harmed others); religious or sexual thoughts (praying, counting).

**Nursing care:**
- Do not stop compulsive rituals abruptly — this causes severe anxiety.
- Do not participate in rituals or reassure rituals.
- Structure daily schedule to allow reduced ritual time gradually (ERP programme under psychologist).
- Provide a calm, predictable environment.
- Encourage CBT with **exposure and response prevention (ERP)** — the most effective psychological treatment.
- Clomipramine (TCA) or SSRI (fluoxetine, fluvoxamine, sertraline) are first-line pharmacotherapy.

## 7.7 Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)

- **Cause:** exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury or sexual violence — either directly experienced, witnessed, or learned about happening to a close person.
- **Features:** symptoms in four clusters:

```text
  PTSD FOUR SYMPTOM CLUSTERS:
  1. RE-EXPERIENCING   Flashbacks, nightmares, psychological and physical distress
                       on reminders
  2. AVOIDANCE         Avoiding thoughts, feelings, reminders of trauma
  3. NEGATIVE COGNITIONS/MOOD  Distorted blame, negative emotions, diminished
                               pleasure, detachment
  4. HYPERAROUSAL      Sleep difficulty, irritability/anger outbursts, recklessness,
                       hyper-vigilance, startle response, concentration difficulty
```

- **Duration:** symptoms for more than 1 month after the trauma.
- **Management:** **Trauma-focused CBT** or **EMDR** (Eye Movement Desensitisation and Reprocessing) are first-line; SSRI.

**Nursing care:**
- Build trust slowly; never force disclosure of the traumatic event.
- Provide safety, stability, predictability.
- Teach grounding techniques (focus on the present moment using the five senses) for flashbacks.
- Identify triggers and help patient manage them.
- Educate family about PTSD symptoms; they may misread hypervigilance as aggression.

## 7.8 Somatoform disorders

**Somatoform disorders** are characterised by physical symptoms that cannot be fully explained by a medical condition, substance or another mental disorder, and that are linked to psychological factors or conflicts. The patient genuinely experiences the symptoms.

| Disorder | Key feature |
| --- | --- |
| **Somatisation disorder** | Multiple physical complaints in multiple body systems over years; not explained medically; causes impaired functioning and health-seeking |
| **Conversion disorder (functional neurological symptom disorder)** | Neurological symptoms — paralysis, blindness, seizures, aphonia — without neurological disease; often begins after stress; "la belle indifférence" (apparent calm about disability) |
| **Hypochondriasis (illness anxiety disorder)** | Persistent preoccupation with having a serious illness; misinterprets normal body sensations; not reassured by medical investigation |
| **Pain disorder** | Chronic pain for which psychological factors play a major role in onset, severity or maintenance |
| **Body dysmorphic disorder** | Preoccupation with an imagined or slight defect in physical appearance |

**General nursing principles for somatoform disorders:**
- Accept that the patient's distress and disability are real, even if no organic cause is found.
- Do not dismiss or argue ("it's all in your head").
- Build a stable, trusting therapeutic relationship.
- Schedule regular, brief appointments (not on demand — reduces reinforcement of sick-role behaviour).
- Avoid excessive investigation and procedures; each investigation reinforces preoccupation.
- Gradually shift focus from symptoms to function ("Can we focus on what you can do today?").
- Treat concurrent depression and anxiety (often present).
- Psychoeducation: explain the mind-body connection without dismissal.
- Refer to liaison psychiatry in general hospital settings.

## 7.9 Dissociative disorders

**Dissociation** is a disruption in the integrated functions of consciousness, memory, identity or perception. It is usually a defence response to overwhelming trauma.

| Disorder | Feature |
| --- | --- |
| Dissociative amnesia | Inability to recall important personal information (usually traumatic); not due to brain injury |
| Dissociative fugue | Purposeful travel away from home with amnesia; sometimes assumption of a new identity; rare |
| Depersonalisation/derealisation | Feeling detached from one's own body (depersonalisation) or the surrounding world (derealisation) |
| Dissociative identity disorder (DID) | Presence of two or more distinct personality states, each with its own pattern of perceiving and relating |

**Nursing care:**
- Provide safety and a stable environment; do not challenge the dissociation directly.
- Avoid confrontation about identity states.
- Refer for specialist trauma therapy.
- Manage concurrent suicide risk.

## 7.10 General nursing principles for anxiety and neurotic disorders

| Principle | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Establish therapeutic alliance | Calm, non-judgemental presence; listen first |
| Assess severity and risk | Use anxiety scales; ask about suicide risk |
| Provide psychoeducation | Explain that anxiety is a normal biological response; it will not harm them |
| Teach relaxation techniques | Progressive muscle relaxation, deep breathing, guided imagery — practise with the patient |
| Reinforce healthy coping | Identify what the patient already does that helps |
| Limit environmental stimulation | Quiet room, low lighting, calm voice |
| Involve the family | Teach family not to provide excessive reassurance (reinforces avoidance) |
| Encourage physical activity | Regular exercise reduces anxiety and depression |
| Monitor medication | Benzodiazepines only short-term; warn about dependence |
| Coordinate with therapy | Reinforce CBT homework; support exposure plans |

## 7.11 Application and implication in nursing

- In a general hospital ward, every patient admitted for a physical complaint may have anxiety or somatisation. Screen and refer early.
- Nurses with high expressed empathy but no professional boundary can overinvolve themselves with somatising patients — seek supervision.
- Trauma-informed care applies to all nursing settings, not just psychiatric wards.

## Remember

- Peplau's anxiety levels: mild, moderate, severe, panic — nursing response increases with severity.
- Panic attacks: 10 minutes to peak; not dangerous; stay with patient, guided breathing, reassure.
- Phobias: systematic desensitisation (gradual exposure + relaxation) is the treatment.
- OCD: obsessions are ego-dystonic (patient knows they are excessive); compulsions reduce anxiety; treatment = ERP + SSRI/clomipramine; never stop rituals abruptly.
- PTSD: four clusters — re-experiencing, avoidance, negative cognitions, hyperarousal; >1 month after trauma; treatment = trauma-focused CBT or EMDR.
- Somatoform disorders: physical symptoms not explained by organic cause; accept patient's distress; avoid excessive investigation; focus on function; CBT.
- Conversion disorder: neurological symptoms without neurological disease; "la belle indifférence."
- Dissociation: disruption of consciousness/memory/identity, usually after trauma; provide safety, refer for trauma therapy.
- General anxiety nursing: psychoeducation, relaxation techniques, CBT reinforcement, avoid excessive reassurance, short-term benzodiazepines only.

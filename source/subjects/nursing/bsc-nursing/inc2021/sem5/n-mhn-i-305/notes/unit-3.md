# Unit 3: Mental health assessment and the therapeutic nurse-patient relationship

This unit covers INC Unit III (10 hours). It is one of the most practical units: it teaches how to take a psychiatric history, how to do the Mental Status Examination (MSE) systematically, and how the therapeutic relationship is built and maintained.

---

## 3.1 Purposes of psychiatric assessment

A complete assessment provides the data needed to:
- understand the patient's current problems and functioning
- plan individualised, evidence-based care
- set a baseline against which change can be measured
- identify safety risks (suicide, self-harm, violence to others)
- build a therapeutic alliance from the first contact

Assessment in psychiatric nursing is **ongoing** — not a one-time event but a continuous process revisited at every interaction.

## 3.2 Psychiatric history taking

The psychiatric history is the structured account of the patient's past and present. It differs from a medical history in its depth around psychological, social and developmental factors.

### Components of a psychiatric history

| Component | What to explore |
| --- | --- |
| Identifying data | Name, age, sex, marital status, education, occupation, referral source |
| Chief complaint | The patient's own words about why they have come |
| History of present illness | Onset, duration, course (continuous/episodic), precipitants, severity, impact on functioning, treatment received so far |
| Past psychiatric history | Previous episodes, diagnoses, hospitalisations, treatments and responses, suicidal attempts |
| Medical and surgical history | Physical illnesses, surgeries, medications, allergies; some physical conditions cause psychiatric symptoms |
| Family history | Psychiatric illness in first-degree relatives (genetic risk); family relationships and dynamics |
| Personal history | Prenatal and birth events, developmental milestones, childhood environment (abuse, neglect), schooling, work, relationships, sexual history, substance use, legal history |
| Premorbid personality | How the patient was before the illness — relationships, habits, coping style, social functioning |
| Social history | Support network, housing, finances, occupation and stressors |
| Cultural and spiritual history | Cultural background, religious beliefs; some symptoms are culturally shaped |

**Skills in history-taking:**
- Build rapport before moving to sensitive areas.
- Begin with open questions; use focused questions to clarify.
- Observe non-verbal cues throughout.
- Avoid leading questions that suggest the "right" answer.
- Allow the patient time to think and answer.
- If the patient is unable to give history (psychosis, mutism), obtain a **collateral history** from a family member or carer.

## 3.3 Mental Status Examination (MSE)

The MSE is a **structured, systematic description of the patient's mental functioning at the time of the interview**. It is the psychiatric equivalent of the physical examination. Every domain must be assessed and documented.

```text
MSE DOMAINS (remember as: A B S M A T P C I J)
  Appearance
  Behaviour (Psychomotor activity)
  Speech
  Mood and Affect
  Thought (Form and Content)
  Perception
  Cognition
  Insight
  Judgement
```

### Appearance

Observe: age and stated age (looks older/younger?), dress (appropriate for weather/situation, well-kept or dishevelled), hygiene (clean, odour), nutritional state, posture, facial expression, eye contact. Note anything unusual: self-injury marks, tattoos, medication side-effect signs (extrapyramidal signs, tardive dyskinesia).

### Behaviour (Psychomotor activity)

| Term | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Psychomotor retardation | Slowed movement and speech; seen in depression |
| Psychomotor agitation | Purposeless, restless activity; seen in mania, anxiety, stimulant intoxication |
| Stupor | Aware but unresponsive; seen in severe depression, catatonia |
| Catatonia | Motor disturbance — rigidity, waxy flexibility, purposeless repetitive movements, mutism, posturing; seen in schizophrenia, severe mood disorders |
| Waxy flexibility (cerea flexibilitas) | Limbs can be placed in any posture and held there, like soft wax |
| Echopraxia | Automatic imitation of the interviewer's movements |

### Speech

Assess: **rate** (fast, slow, normal), **volume** (loud, soft), **tone** (monotone, varied), **quantity** (verbose, sparse), **coherence** (easy or hard to follow), **spontaneity**.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Pressured speech | Very fast, difficult to interrupt; seen in mania |
| Poverty of speech | Very little output; long pauses; seen in depression, schizophrenia |
| Mutism | No speech |
| Dysarthria | Unclear articulation (neurological) |
| Echolalia | Repeating the interviewer's words |

### Mood and Affect

**Mood** is the patient's sustained internal feeling state ("how they feel inside"), best described in the patient's own words.

**Affect** is the external, observable expression of feeling — what the examiner sees during the interview.

| Affect descriptor | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Appropriate | Affect matches the content of speech |
| Blunted | Marked reduction in emotional expression |
| Flat | Almost no emotional expression; monotone voice, immobile face |
| Labile | Rapid shifts from one affect to another |
| Incongruent | Affect does not match content (laughing while describing a tragedy) |
| Restricted | Somewhat reduced range |

Assess: quality, range, stability, and whether affect is congruent with the topic.

### Thought

Thought assessment has two parts:

**A. Form of thought (how the patient is thinking):**

| Abnormality | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Flight of ideas | Rapid sequence of thoughts loosely connected by sound (rhyming), pun or superficial association; seen in mania |
| Loosening of associations (derailment) | Thoughts slip off-track so the connection is unclear; seen in schizophrenia |
| Circumstantiality | Excessive detail but eventually reaches the point |
| Tangentiality | Goes off-track and never returns to the point |
| Thought blocking | Sudden cessation; patient says thoughts "stop" |
| Perseveration | Repeating the same word or idea despite different questions |
| Clang associations | Words chosen for sound (rhyme, alliteration), not meaning |
| Word salad (incoherence) | Random words with no logical connection; most severe disorganisation |
| Neologisms | The patient invents new words (e.g. "I feel blurbish") |

**B. Content of thought (what the patient is thinking about):**

| Abnormality | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Delusion | A fixed, false belief held with conviction, not shared by the culture, not corrected by evidence |
| Persecutory delusion | Belief of being watched, followed, poisoned or harmed |
| Grandiose delusion | Belief of special powers, wealth, identity (e.g. "I am God") |
| Reference delusion | Belief that neutral events (news, strangers' talk) are directed at the patient |
| Nihilistic delusion | Belief that self, others or the world no longer exist |
| Somatic delusion | False belief about body function or disease |
| Passivity / control | Belief that thoughts, feelings or actions are controlled by an outside force |
| Thought insertion | Belief that thoughts are put into the mind by an external agent |
| Thought withdrawal | Belief that thoughts are removed from the mind |
| Thought broadcasting | Belief that thoughts can be heard by others |
| Obsession | Recurrent, intrusive thought the patient recognises as their own but cannot suppress |
| Phobia | Excessive, irrational fear of a specific thing or situation |
| Suicidal/homicidal ideation | Must be assessed clearly: ideation, plan, intent, means available |

### Perception

**Perception** is how the brain interprets sensory input.

| Abnormality | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Hallucination | A false sensory perception with no external stimulus; patient believes it is real |
| Illusion | A misinterpretation of a real external stimulus |
| Pseudo-hallucination | Perceived inside the mind, the patient knows it is not external |
| Derealization | The outside world seems unreal, dreamlike |
| Depersonalisation | The person feels detached from their own mind or body |

**Types of hallucinations by sense:**

| Type | Seen mainly in |
| --- | --- |
| Auditory | Schizophrenia (commonest psychiatric hallucination): voices commenting, commanding, two voices arguing |
| Visual | Organic disorders, delirium, substance intoxication |
| Olfactory / gustatory | Temporal lobe epilepsy; also schizophrenia |
| Tactile (somatic) | Substance withdrawal (formication — feeling of insects crawling) |
| Hypnagogic | On falling asleep (normal experience) |
| Hypnopompic | On waking (normal experience) |

### Cognition

Cognition is a clinical assessment of cognitive functions at the time of interview.

| Domain | How to assess |
| --- | --- |
| Consciousness (level of arousal) | Alert, drowsy, stupor, coma |
| Orientation | To time (date, day, month, year), place and person |
| Attention and concentration | Serial 7s (count down from 100 by 7s), spell "WORLD" backwards |
| Memory | Immediate (repeat three words now), recent (what did you eat this morning?), remote (where were you born?) |
| Abstract thinking | Explain a proverb (e.g. "A stitch in time saves nine") |
| General knowledge | Current events, general questions appropriate to education |
| Intelligence | Estimate from education, occupation, vocabulary, reasoning |

Formal cognitive testing uses tools like the **Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE)** or **Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA)** when detailed assessment is needed.

### Insight

Insight is the degree to which the patient understands that they have a mental illness and that treatment is needed.

| Grade | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Grade 1 (complete denial) | Completely denies being ill |
| Grade 2 | Slight awareness; acknowledges being ill but blames others |
| Grade 3 | Aware of illness but blames external factors |
| Grade 4 | Aware illness may be in the mind but attributes it to unknown causes |
| Grade 5 | Aware of illness and mental causation but little practical insight |
| Grade 6 (full insight) | Understands nature of illness, need for treatment, and that beliefs may be false |

Poor insight is a major predictor of **non-compliance** with treatment.

### Judgement

Judgement is the ability to make sound decisions in real-life situations.

Assess with a practical question: "What would you do if you found a stamped envelope on the pavement?" or "What would you do if you smelled smoke in a cinema?"

| Level | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Intact | Appropriate, socially expected answer |
| Impaired | Gives an unsafe or socially inappropriate answer |
| Poor | No understanding of the social consequences of actions |

## 3.4 Risk assessment

Every psychiatric assessment must include a **systematic risk assessment**:

### Suicide risk assessment

```text
IDEATION → PLAN → INTENT → MEANS
  "Do you feel like life is not worth living?"
  "Have you thought about hurting yourself?"
  "Do you have a plan for how you would do it?"
  "Have you taken any steps to put the plan in place?"
```

**High-risk indicators:** previous attempt, specific plan, access to means (medication, weapon), social isolation, hopelessness, male sex, older age, substance use, recent loss, refusal to contract for safety.

### Violence risk assessment

Assess history of violence, current thoughts of harming others, impulsivity, substance use, access to weapons, specific target.

## 3.5 Documentation of the MSE

Document the MSE clearly and factually. Use the patient's own words in quotes. Avoid interpretive language. Record objective observations.

Example format:
- "Speech: rapid, pressured, volume loud, difficult to interrupt."
- "Affect: labile; shifted from laughter to tears within the same topic."
- "Thought content: persecutory delusion — patient states neighbours are poisoning water supply."
- "Insight: Grade 1 — completely denies illness."

## 3.6 Application and implication in nursing

- The MSE is not done once at admission; reassess at every shift and document changes.
- Changes in the MSE (worsening thought disorganisation, new suicidal plan, deteriorating orientation) are clinical emergencies — report at once.
- Use observation, open questions and careful listening; never challenge or argue when recording findings.
- Explain to patients and families what an MSE is; it reduces anxiety and builds cooperation.
- Always ask directly about suicidal thoughts — asking does not plant the idea.

## Remember

- MSE domains (A B S M A T P C I J): Appearance, Behaviour, Speech, Mood/Affect, Thought form/content, Perception, Cognition, Insight, Judgement.
- Mood = patient's subjective feeling; Affect = examiner's objective observation of emotional expression.
- Hallucination = false perception with no stimulus; Illusion = misinterpretation of real stimulus.
- Thought form abnormalities: flight of ideas (mania), loosening of associations (schizophrenia), thought blocking, perseveration.
- Delusions: persecutory, grandiose, reference, nihilistic, somatic, passivity, thought insertion/withdrawal/broadcasting.
- Insight Grade 1 = complete denial; Grade 6 = full insight; poor insight predicts non-compliance.
- Suicide risk: always assess ideation, plan, intent and means; previous attempt is a major risk factor.
- Psychiatric history includes premorbid personality and collateral history from family when needed.
- Peplau's four phases: orientation, identification, exploitation, resolution.

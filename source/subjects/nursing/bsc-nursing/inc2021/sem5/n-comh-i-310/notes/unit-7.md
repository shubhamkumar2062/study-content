# Unit 7: Communicable diseases and national health programmes

India carries a double burden — persistent endemic communicable diseases alongside a rising NCD load. The national health programmes (vertical and integrated) are the main vehicles for controlling communicable disease. The community health nurse is a key implementer of these programmes at the field level.

---

## 7.1 Concepts of communicable disease control

**Communicable disease**: a disease caused by an infectious agent (or its toxins) that can be transmitted directly or indirectly from one person, animal, or the environment to another.

**Chain of infection**:
```text
   Infectious agent → Reservoir → Portal of exit → Mode of transmission →
   Portal of entry → Susceptible host
```

Breaking any link breaks transmission. Nursing interventions target every link (treat the source, improve sanitation, vaccinate, improve immunity).

**Incubation period**: time from exposure to first symptom. Guides quarantine duration.

**Communicability period**: period during which the infected person can transmit the disease. May overlap with or follow the incubation period.

## 7.2 Tuberculosis (TB) and Revised National TB Control Programme / National TB Elimination Programme

**Tuberculosis** is caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*. India accounts for about 26% of global TB burden; the target is to eliminate TB (< 1 case per million population) by 2025.

**Transmission**: airborne droplet nuclei; a person with active pulmonary TB can infect 10–15 people per year if untreated.

**Diagnosis**: sputum smear microscopy (most common), GeneXpert/CBNAAT (rapid molecular diagnosis; detects rifampicin resistance within 2 hours), chest X-ray, tuberculin skin test (Mantoux), IGRA (interferon-gamma release assay for latent TB).

**Treatment categories and regimens** (NTEP 2019 onwards):

| Category | Cases | Regimen | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| New cases | New pulmonary/extrapulmonary (not previously treated) | 2HRZE + 4HR (isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide, ethambutol) | 6 months |
| Previously treated | Retreatment (relapse, failure, default) | 2HRZES + HRZE + 5HRE | 8 months (or individualised per DST) |
| Drug-resistant TB (MDR, XDR) | Resistance to isoniazid + rifampicin (MDR) or more (XDR) | Longer regimen with second-line drugs (bedaquiline, linezolid, delamanid) | 9–24 months |

**DOTS (Directly Observed Treatment, Short-course)**: patient takes medication under direct observation of a treatment supporter (ASHA, ANM, or community volunteer). Ensures adherence; prevents drug resistance.

**NTEP key features**: free diagnosis and treatment; electronic nikshay portal (register all TB patients, monitor treatment); Nikshay Poshan Yojana (₹500/month food support to TB patients); TB-free India by 2025 target.

**Nursing role**: contact tracing (examine household contacts for symptoms; sputum test/X-ray); promote DOTS; manage side effects (isoniazid → peripheral neuropathy → pyridoxine supplementation; rifampicin → orange discolouration of secretions — reassure); ensure nutritional support; address stigma.

## 7.3 Malaria

**Causative agents**: *Plasmodium falciparum* (severe disease, most deaths), *P. vivax* (most common in India, relapses), *P. malariae*, *P. ovale*.

**Vector**: *Anopheles* mosquito (female, bites dusk to dawn).

**Features**: fever with chills and rigors (every 48 hours for P. vivax, irregular for P. falciparum), headache, myalgia, hepatosplenomegaly.

**Severe malaria features** (P. falciparum): cerebral malaria (coma), severe anaemia, renal failure, respiratory failure, hypoglycaemia, blackwater fever (haemoglobinuria). Requires IV artesunate and ICU care.

**Diagnosis**: peripheral blood smear (thick and thin); rapid diagnostic test (RDT); RDT is used at village level by ASHA/MPW.

**Treatment** (NVBDCP — National Vector Borne Disease Control Programme):
- Uncomplicated P. falciparum: Artemisinin-based Combination Therapy (ACT) — Artesunate + Sulphadoxine-Pyrimethamine (AS+SP), 3 days.
- P. vivax: Chloroquine 25 mg/kg over 3 days + Primaquine 0.25 mg/kg/day for 14 days (radical cure — after G6PD test).
- Severe malaria: IV artesunate; referral to hospital.

**NVBDCP prevention**: Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS) with insecticides; Long-Lasting Insecticidal Nets (LLIN); environmental management (elimination of breeding sites); biological control (*Gambusia* fish; Bti larvicide); early diagnosis and prompt treatment (EDPT).

## 7.4 Dengue, Chikungunya, and Zika

**Dengue**: caused by DENV 1–4 (four serotypes); vector is *Aedes aegypti* and *Ae. albopictus* (day-biting). Classic features: sudden high fever, severe headache, retro-orbital pain, myalgia/arthralgia, rash. Dengue haemorrhagic fever (DHF): plasma leakage, thrombocytopenia (< 100,000/µL), haemorrhage. Dengue shock syndrome: shock from severe plasma leakage. Treatment: supportive (paracetamol, avoid aspirin/NSAIDs, IV fluids for DHF); no antiviral.

**Chikungunya**: alphavirus; *Aedes* vector; features: acute joint pain (arthralgia) often persistent months after fever resolves; no specific treatment.

**Zika**: flavivirus; *Aedes* vector; most cases mild; risk in pregnancy: microcephaly and other congenital defects.

**Prevention** (all three): reduce *Aedes* breeding sites — drain, cover, or clean water storage containers weekly; introduce *Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis* (Bti) in containers; use repellents and long sleeves; screen windows; use mosquito nets.

## 7.5 Lymphatic filariasis

**Causative organism**: *Wuchereria bancrofti* (most of India); vector: *Culex quinquefasciatus* (night-biting).

**Features**: acute lymphangitis, lymphadenitis; chronic: lymphoedema (elephantiasis of lower limbs), hydrocele, chyluria.

**National Filaria Control Programme**: Mass Drug Administration (MDA) annually — Diethylcarbamazine (DEC) + Albendazole to the entire at-risk population (not infected, not pregnant, not children < 2 years, not seriously ill); repeat annually for 5 years to interrupt transmission (microfilariae are killed; adult worms outlive DEC but die eventually).

**Morbidity management**: lymphoedema management education (foot hygiene, skin care, exercise, elevation, compression bandaging); hydrocele surgery.

---

## 7.6 Kala-azar (Visceral Leishmaniasis)

**Causative organism**: *Leishmania donovani*; vector: **sandfly** (*Phlebotomus argentipes*), night-biting.

**Features**: prolonged fever, progressive weight loss, hepatosplenomegaly (massive splenomegaly), anaemia, weakness; can be fatal untreated.

**Endemic areas in India**: Bihar, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh.

**Treatment**: Liposomal Amphotericin B (single IV dose under NVBDCP); Miltefosine (oral, 28 days; CI in pregnancy); Sodium Stibogluconate (SSG) — older agent.

**Prevention**: IRS (DDT or synthetic pyrethroids); bed nets (sandfly is small enough to pass through 0.25 mm mesh — fine mesh net or insecticide-treated net needed); avoid sandfly bites (peak biting: sunset to midnight).

**Elimination target**: < 1 case per 10,000 population at block level in India.

## 7.7 HIV/AIDS and National AIDS Control Programme (NACP)

**National AIDS Control Organisation (NACO)**: the nodal government agency for India's response to HIV/AIDS under NACP.

**NACP IV key strategies**:
- **Prevention**: condom promotion, Targeted Interventions (TI) for high-risk groups (sex workers, MSM, IDUs, truckers), prevention of mother-to-child transmission (PMTCT), blood safety.
- **Treatment**: free ART through government ART centres; first-line ART — TDF + 3TC + EFV (or DTG for new patients).
- **Testing**: free voluntary counselling and testing (ICTC — Integrated Counselling and Testing Centre); HIV testing integrated into ANC (opt-out for pregnant women).
- **Community mobilisation**: ASHA, NGOs, peer educators.

**Key indicators**: India has 2.4 million PLHIV (people living with HIV); national adult HIV prevalence ~0.22% (National AIDS Control Organisation estimate); 95-95-95 target (95% know status, 95% on ART, 95% virally suppressed).

## 7.8 Other key communicable diseases

| Disease | Agent | Mode of transmission | Key nursing action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cholera | *Vibrio cholerae* | Faecal-oral (contaminated water/food) | ORS; IV fluids for severe dehydration; notify; safe water and sanitation |
| Typhoid | *Salmonella typhi* | Faecal-oral | Antibiotic treatment (azithromycin, ciprofloxacin); identify carriers; food and water hygiene |
| Hepatitis B | HBV (blood, sexual, perinatal) | Blood, sexual contact, mother-to-child | Vaccination; safe injection practices; PEP with HBIg + vaccine for newborn of HBsAg-positive mother |
| Leptospirosis | *Leptospira* spp. | Contact with water or soil contaminated with animal urine; common after floods | Avoid flood water; protective footwear; doxycycline prophylaxis; notify |
| Rabies | Rabies virus | Animal bite (dog, bat, fox) | Wound wash (soap + water × 15 min); anti-rabies vaccination (Essen regimen or reduced regimen); rabies immunoglobulin for category III exposure |
| Japanese Encephalitis (JE) | Flavivirus | *Culex* mosquito bite | JE vaccination (endemic states); vector control; notify; supportive care |
| Plague | *Yersinia pestis* | Flea bite (bubonic), respiratory (pneumonic), direct contact (septicaemic) | Notify; isolation; doxycycline or streptomycin treatment; flea and rodent control |

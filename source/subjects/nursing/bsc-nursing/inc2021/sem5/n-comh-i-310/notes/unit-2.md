# Unit 2: Environmental science, environmental health and sanitation

The environment is everything outside the human body that can affect health. Community health nurses work at the intersection of people and their environments — assessing hazards, educating communities, and advocating for sanitation and clean water.

---

## 2.1 Concepts of environmental health

**Environmental health** is the branch of public health concerned with all aspects of the natural and built environment that may affect human health, including physical, chemical, biological, and social factors.

**Ecology** is the study of relationships between organisms and their environment. Humans depend on healthy ecosystems for food, water, air, and climate regulation. Environmental degradation harms health directly (pollutants cause disease) and indirectly (climate change shifts disease vectors).

**Components of the environment**:
- **Physical**: climate, temperature, radiation, noise.
- **Chemical**: air pollutants, pesticides, heavy metals, industrial chemicals, water contaminants.
- **Biological**: bacteria, viruses, parasites, insects, rodents.
- **Social**: poverty, housing, education, occupation, cultural practices.

---

## 2.2 Air pollution

**Air pollutants** are substances released into the atmosphere in concentrations that are harmful.

| Pollutant | Main sources | Health effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Particulate matter (PM₂.₅, PM₁₀) | Motor vehicles, industries, construction, biomass burning | Respiratory disease (asthma, COPD), cardiovascular disease, lung cancer |
| Carbon monoxide (CO) | Incomplete combustion (vehicles, stoves) | Binds haemoglobin (COHb) → reduces O₂ carrying capacity; headache, confusion, death at high levels |
| Sulphur dioxide (SO₂) | Burning sulphur-containing fuels (coal, diesel) | Bronchoconstriction; acid rain |
| Nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) | Vehicle exhaust, power plants | Respiratory irritant; photochemical smog |
| Ozone (O₃) | Photochemical reaction of NOₓ + VOCs in sunlight | Lung tissue damage; worsens asthma |
| Lead | Old paint, leaded petrol, battery industries | Neurotoxic (especially children — IQ, learning); anaemia, kidney damage |
| Indoor smoke | Burning biomass (wood, dung, crop residues) in poorly ventilated kitchens | Highest burden in India; ALRI in children, COPD in women |

**Prevention**: vehicle emission standards; catalytic converters; cleaner cooking fuels (LPG, solar cookers — Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana); industrial emission limits; urban tree cover; air quality monitoring.

**Nurse's role**: assess breathing in community surveys; identify households using solid fuels; promote ventilated stoves and clean cooking; recognise occupational exposure (painters, battery workers, miners).

## 2.3 Water pollution and safe water supply

Water is an essential resource and a major vehicle for disease transmission.

### Sources of drinking water

| Source | Characteristics | Safety concern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rainwater (harvested) | Generally safe if collected from clean surfaces | Contamination from roof; storage vessel hygiene |
| Surface water (rivers, lakes) | Easily contaminated by sewage, agricultural runoff | Needs treatment before use |
| Groundwater (wells, boreholes) | Usually safer but can be contaminated by agricultural chemicals, natural arsenic/fluoride | Arsenic (Bangladesh/Bengal), fluoride (fluorosis in India) |
| Piped municipal supply | Treated and chlorinated | Contamination at distribution point; intermittent supply means back-siphonage |
| Hand pump (tube well) | Common in rural India | Arsenic in hand-pump water in some states |

### Water quality parameters

| Parameter | Standard (India BIS 10500) | Significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pH | 6.5–8.5 | Corrosion of pipes; bactericidal effect of chlorine reduced at high pH |
| Turbidity | ≤ 1 NTU (desirable), ≤ 5 NTU (acceptable) | Sediment indicates contamination; reduces chlorine efficacy |
| Total dissolved solids | ≤ 500 mg/L (desirable) | Taste; mineral content |
| Nitrate | ≤ 45 mg/L | Methaemoglobinaemia in infants (blue baby syndrome) |
| Arsenic | ≤ 0.01 mg/L | Skin cancer, neuropathy, cardiovascular disease |
| Fluoride | 0.6–1.5 mg/L (desirable) | Below 0.6: dental caries; above 1.5: dental fluorosis; above 4.0: skeletal fluorosis |
| Faecal coliforms (E. coli) | 0 per 100 mL (drinking water) | Key indicator of faecal contamination |
| Residual chlorine | 0.2–0.5 mg/L at consumer end | Disinfection maintained in distribution |

### Water treatment

```text
Collection → Screening (remove large debris) → Sedimentation → Coagulation-flocculation
  → Sand filtration → Disinfection (chlorination) → Storage and distribution
```

**Disinfection**: chlorine is the most widely used. **Boiling** kills all pathogens (reliable at household level). **Chlorination**: bleaching powder (calcium hypochlorite) commonly used in India; residual chlorine must be maintained. SODIS (Solar Disinfection): filling a clear PET bottle with water and exposing to sunlight for 6+ hours; practical in areas without electricity.

**Point-of-use water treatment**: boiling, chlorine tablets, ceramic filter, UV pen — teach households to use these when water quality is in doubt.

## 2.4 Soil pollution and solid waste management

**Soil pollutants**: agricultural chemicals (pesticides, fertilisers), heavy metals (lead, cadmium), industrial waste, domestic solid waste. Health impacts: contamination of food crops; drinking water contamination; skin absorption; inhalation of dust.

**Solid waste management (SWM)** — the 3 Rs:
- **Reduce**: buy less, avoid single-use plastics, reduce packaging.
- **Reuse**: repair and use again.
- **Recycle**: process into new material.

**Waste categories** (Solid Waste Management Rules 2016):
- **Biodegradable (wet waste)**: kitchen scraps, garden waste — composting.
- **Recyclable (dry waste)**: paper, glass, metal, plastic — segregate at source.
- **Hazardous waste**: batteries, paints, chemicals — special disposal.
- **Biomedical waste**: health facility waste — governed by BMW Management Rules 2016.

**Biomedical waste management (INC-relevant)**:
- Yellow bag: pathological, anatomical, soiled linen → incineration.
- Red bag/container: contaminated sharps, plastic, IV tubing → autoclave → shredder → landfill.
- Blue/white container: glass waste → autoclave → landfill.
- Black bag: general waste.
- Never mix biomedical waste with general waste.

## 2.5 Environmental sanitation

**Sanitation** covers excreta disposal, wastewater disposal, solid waste management, control of insects and rodents, food hygiene, and housing conditions.

### Excreta disposal

**Sanitary latrine**: a facility that prevents excreta from contaminating water, soil and food, and prevents access by insects and rodents. The ideal latrine is:
- Watertight pit or septic tank.
- Fly-proof cover slab.
- Superstructure for privacy.
- Water seal (pit latrine/pour-flush latrine).

**Types of latrines in India**: pit latrine, bore-hole latrine, dug well latrine, aquaprivy, septic tank + soak pit (most effective), sewerage system (urban ideal).

**Swachh Bharat Mission (SBM)**: the Government of India's programme to end open defecation. By 2019, over 100 million toilets were constructed; rural areas declared Open Defecation Free (ODF).

**Faecal-oral route diseases**: typhoid, cholera, hepatitis A, polio, amoebiasis, giardiasis — all prevented by sanitary excreta disposal + safe water + hand hygiene.

### Housing and health

**A healthy house** provides:
- Protection from the elements (rain, cold, heat).
- Personal space (overcrowding spreads airborne diseases — TB, measles).
- Ventilation (removes indoor air pollutants and reduces humidity).
- Safe water supply and sanitation.
- Refuse disposal.
- Lighting — natural and artificial.
- Safe electrical installation.
- Absence of dampness (mould → respiratory disease).

**Overcrowding** is defined as more than 2 persons per sleeping room (WHO). Overcrowding increases transmission of respiratory and skin infections.

## 2.6 Vector control

**Vectors** are organisms that transmit pathogens from one host to another.

| Vector | Disease | Control measures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Anopheles* mosquito | Malaria | Eliminate breeding sites (stagnant water), insecticide-treated bed nets (ITNs), indoor residual spraying (IRS), larviciding (Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis, temephos), adult mosquito control, anti-larval fish (*Gambusia*) |
| *Aedes aegypti* | Dengue, Zika, chikungunya | Same as above; emphasise removal of artificial containers with water (tyres, flower pots, overhead tanks) |
| *Culex* mosquito | Lymphatic filariasis (Bancroftian), Japanese encephalitis | Drain rice fields; larvicide; IRS; bed nets |
| Housefly | Typhoid, cholera, dysentery, amoebiasis | Cover food; dispose refuse; cover latrines; proper sewage; sanitary food handling |
| Cockroach | Typhoid, dysentery | Good kitchen hygiene; insecticides in crevices |
| Rat/flea | Plague, leptospirosis | Rat-proofing buildings; trapping; rodenticides; flea control |
| Sandfly | Kala-azar (visceral leishmaniasis) | IRS with DDT (India); avoid sandfly bites |

**Integrated vector management (IVM)**: a rational, evidence-based approach combining several control methods (biological, chemical, environmental, personal protection) to maximise effect and minimise harm.

## 2.7 Occupational health and environmental hazards

**Occupational health** (nurses' role in the community):
- Assess workplace hazards during home visits or community surveys.
- Identify occupation-related diseases (silicosis, asbestosis, NIHL, occupational asthma).
- Refer to Employees' State Insurance Corporation (ESIC) for workers' health care.
- Advise on personal protective equipment.

**Climate change and health**:
- Increasing temperatures expand the range of *Anopheles* (malaria) and *Aedes* (dengue) mosquitoes.
- Extreme weather events (floods, droughts) damage water and sanitation infrastructure.
- Food insecurity increases with unpredictable rainfall.
- Heat stress is a direct health risk for outdoor workers and older adults.

**Nurse's advocacy role**: participate in local health committees; report environmental hazards to the panchayat or municipal health officer; educate communities about pollution-linked disease; support SBM and clean cooking programmes.

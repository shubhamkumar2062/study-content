# How to use this guide

Community Health Nursing I (N-COMH(I)310) is studied in Semester 5 alongside Child Health Nursing and Mental Health Nursing. It is the first systematic study of public health nursing in the B.Sc Nursing programme, shifting focus from the individual patient in a hospital to the community as the client. The subject covers environmental health, nutrition, health education, epidemiology, communicable disease programmes, and non-communicable disease programmes. Because India's national health programmes are examined directly, this subject requires you to know programme names, targets, key interventions, and implementing agencies alongside theoretical concepts.

## Exam pattern

N-COMH(I)310 is examined at the end of Semester 5 as a **standalone theory paper** following the standard INC 2021 B.Sc Nursing exam format.

**Typical INC B.Sc Nursing end-semester theory paper (75 marks, 3 hours)**

| Section | Type | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 20 objective questions (MCQ or fill-in-the-blank) — attempt all | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 questions | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 questions | 7 × 5 = 35 |
| **Total** | | **75** |

Confirm the exact format with your college; some universities use slightly different section patterns.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. **Know the programme names and their acronyms.** The examiner will award marks for correctly naming programmes: "NVBDCP" scores marks that "vector control programme" does not. Learn at least the full name and acronym of each national programme.

2. **Link disease → route of transmission → national programme → nurse's role.** For any communicable disease question, structure your answer as: causative agent → transmission route → clinical features → diagnosis → treatment → national programme → nursing care. This format reliably scores 7–8 marks on a 10-mark essay.

3. **Epidemiology formulas need to be learned precisely.** The examiner expects the correct formula with the denominator clearly stated. IMR = Deaths under 1 year ÷ Live births × 1,000. Sensitivity = TP ÷ (TP + FN). Write the formula first, then give the clinical meaning.

4. **For NCD questions, include risk factors and primary prevention.** An answer about hypertension or diabetes that only describes clinical management misses the community nursing dimension. Always state: lifestyle interventions (diet, exercise, smoking cessation, salt reduction), screening approach, and adherence support.

5. **BCC models often come as 10-mark or 5-mark questions.** For a Health Belief Model question, name all six constructs with an example for each. For the Transtheoretical Model, name all five stages and the matching communication strategy.

6. **Outbreak investigation steps are listed in order.** The examiner expects the steps in sequence. Memorise: Verify → Confirm → Define case → Count → Descriptive epi → Hypothesise → Test → Control → Communicate → Report.

7. **For food-borne disease questions**: give the organism, incubation period, source, clinical features, and control measures. Know the classic scenario: staphylococcal food poisoning has a very short incubation (2–6 hours) and is caused by pre-formed toxin in food left at room temperature — antibiotics are not indicated.

8. **School health services are regularly asked as a 5-mark or 10-mark question.** Use the five components: health appraisal, health promotion, communicable disease control, healthy environment, and special services (nutrition, mental health).

## Probability ratings

- **High** — asked in most years; commit the full answer to memory.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a broader question.
- **Low** — appears rarely; useful for MCQs and to round off a long essay.

These ratings are estimates based on INC syllabus weightings and common exam patterns. They are a guide, not a guarantee.

# Unit 3: Nutrition assessment and education, food borne diseases and food safety

Malnutrition in India affects both extremes — undernutrition (stunting, wasting, anaemia) and overnutrition (obesity, non-communicable diseases). The community health nurse plays a central role in assessing nutritional status, delivering food and nutrition education, and ensuring food safety.

---

## 3.1 Nutrition concepts

**Nutrition** is the process by which the body takes in and uses food for growth, repair and maintenance. **Malnutrition** includes both undernutrition and overnutrition.

**Nutrients and their roles**:

| Nutrient | Key sources | Functions | Deficiency disease |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Carbohydrates | Rice, wheat, sugar, potato | Main energy source (4 kcal/g) | — |
| Proteins | Pulses, legumes, meat, egg, milk, soy | Growth, repair, enzymes, antibodies | Kwashiorkor (protein deficiency), Marasmus (calorie deficiency) |
| Fats | Oils, nuts, dairy, meat | Energy (9 kcal/g), fat-soluble vitamins, cell membranes | Essential fatty acid deficiency |
| Vitamin A | Liver, egg, dairy, dark-green vegetables, orange/yellow fruits | Vision (rhodopsin), immunity, epithelial integrity | Night blindness, xerophthalmia, Bitot's spots |
| Vitamin D | Sunlight (skin synthesis), fish liver oil, fortified foods | Calcium absorption, bone mineralisation | Rickets (children), osteomalacia (adults) |
| Vitamin B₁₂ | Animal foods only (meat, fish, dairy, eggs) | Myelin synthesis, DNA synthesis | Megaloblastic anaemia, subacute combined degeneration of spinal cord |
| Folate | Green leafy vegetables, legumes, fortified grain | DNA synthesis (esp. neural tube formation) | Megaloblastic anaemia; neural tube defects (NTDs) in pregnancy |
| Vitamin C | Citrus fruits, tomatoes, amla | Collagen synthesis, immunity, iron absorption | Scurvy (perifollicular petechiae, bleeding gums, wound healing failure) |
| Iron | Meat (haem iron), legumes, green leafy vegetables (non-haem iron) | Haemoglobin, myoglobin, enzymes | Iron-deficiency anaemia (most common nutritional problem in India) |
| Iodine | Iodised salt, seafood | Thyroid hormone synthesis | Goitre, hypothyroidism, cretinism (congenital hypothyroidism) |
| Calcium | Dairy, green leafy vegetables, sesame | Bone, teeth, muscle contraction, coagulation | Osteoporosis, rickets |
| Zinc | Meat, legumes, nuts | Growth, immunity, wound healing, taste | Growth retardation, immune suppression, diarrhoea |

## 3.2 Nutritional assessment

**ABCD method** of nutritional assessment:

| Component | Methods |
| --- | --- |
| **A — Anthropometric** | Weight, height, BMI, mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC), skin fold thickness, head circumference (children), waist:hip ratio |
| **B — Biochemical** | Haemoglobin, serum albumin/prealbumin, serum retinol (Vitamin A), TSH/T4 (iodine), urine iodine concentration |
| **C — Clinical** | Physical signs of deficiency (Bitot's spots, pallor, oedema, skin changes, hair changes, angular stomatitis, glossitis, goitre) |
| **D — Dietary** | 24-hour dietary recall, food frequency questionnaire, dietary diversity score |

### Anthropometric indices for children (WHO standards)

| Index | Measure | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weight-for-age (W/A) | Underweight | Below −2 SD = underweight; below −3 SD = severe underweight; global malnutrition indicator |
| Height-for-age (H/A) | Stunting | Below −2 SD = stunted (chronic undernutrition / past malnutrition) |
| Weight-for-height (W/H) | Wasting | Below −2 SD = wasted (acute undernutrition / current crisis) |
| MUAC | Acute malnutrition | < 11.5 cm = severe acute malnutrition (SAM); 11.5–12.5 cm = MAM |

**Mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC)** can be measured with a simple tape; it is used at community level for screening without weighing or measuring height. < 23 cm in pregnant women indicates acute malnutrition.

### Classification of protein-energy malnutrition (PEM) in children

| Grade | W/A (% of median) | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marasmus | < 60% | Severe wasting, muscle loss, old-man face, skin and bone; NO oedema; hungry, alert |
| Kwashiorkor | 60–80% | Oedema (pitting, bilateral), skin lesions, hair discolouration and thinning (flag sign), liver enlargement; anorexia, apathetic |
| Marasmic-kwashiorkor | < 60% with oedema | Both features; most severely ill |

**Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM)** management:
- Phase 1 (stabilisation, 2 weeks): treat infection, correct metabolic complications, cautious re-feeding (F-75 formula, 100 kcal/kg/day).
- Phase 2 (rehabilitation): high-energy formula (F-100, RUTF — Ready to Use Therapeutic Food), catch-up growth.
- Avoid high sodium; risk of re-feeding syndrome (hypokalaemia, hypophosphataemia).

## 3.3 Nutritional programmes in India

| Programme | Target group | Key intervention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS) | Children 0–6 years, pregnant and lactating mothers | Supplementary nutrition, pre-school education, health check-up, immunisation, nutrition counselling, referral services |
| Mid-Day Meal Scheme (MDMS) | School children (Classes I–VIII) | Cooked meal at school; reduces hunger and improves attendance |
| Pradhan Mantri Poshan Shakti Nirman (PM POSHAN) | School children | Renamed from MDMS; focus on nutritional quality and fortification |
| National Anaemia Control Programme / Anaemia Mukt Bharat | Women, children, adolescents | Iron and folic acid supplementation; deworming; dietary diversification |
| National Iodine Deficiency Disorders Control Programme (NIDDCP) | All population | Universal iodisation of salt; monitoring of iodine levels |
| Vitamin A Supplementation Programme | Children 6 months–5 years | High-dose Vitamin A every 6 months; integrated with immunisation rounds |
| Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana (PMMVY) | Pregnant and lactating women | Cash transfer of ₹5,000 for first living child to incentivise early ANC and exclusive breastfeeding |
| Poshan Abhiyaan (National Nutrition Mission) | Women and children | Multi-ministry convergence; targets to reduce stunting, underweight, anaemia, and low birth weight |

## 3.4 Nutrition during special life stages

**Infant feeding**:
- Initiate breastfeeding within 1 hour of birth (colostrum — immunoglobulins, lactoferrin, oligosaccharides).
- **Exclusive breastfeeding** for 6 months (no water, juice, or other foods).
- Complementary feeding from 6 months: soft, nutrient-dense foods; continue breastfeeding up to 2 years.
- Breastfeeding benefits: reduces GI infections, ALRI, SIDS; promotes cognitive development; reduces maternal risk of ovarian and breast cancer.

**Adolescent nutrition**: iron and folate for girls (menstrual losses); calcium for bone density; avoid early marriage (adolescent pregnancy → higher maternal and infant mortality); address eating disorders (anorexia nervosa, bulimia — common in girls).

**Pregnancy**: additional 300 kcal/day in 2nd and 3rd trimester; iron and folic acid from first trimester (400 µg folic acid before conception reduces NTD risk); calcium supplementation; avoid alcohol, tobacco.

**Lactation**: additional 500 kcal/day; increased water, protein, calcium, iodine, and vitamin D needs.

**Older adults**: reduced caloric need but maintained need for protein, calcium, vitamin D, vitamin B₁₂; risk of undernutrition, dehydration, and drug-nutrient interactions.

## 3.5 Food-borne diseases

**Food-borne disease (foodborne illness)** results from consuming contaminated food. Sources of contamination: bacteria, viruses, parasites, chemicals, natural toxins.

### Major food-borne infections

| Disease | Organism | Source | Symptoms | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Typhoid | *Salmonella typhi* | Contaminated water/food (sewage-contaminated) | Stepped fever (rose spots, relative bradycardia), GI symptoms, hepatosplenomegaly; complications: intestinal perforation | Ciprofloxacin or azithromycin; ceftriaxone for severe |
| Cholera | *Vibrio cholerae* | Contaminated water and food | Rice-water diarrhoea, severe dehydration; vomiting; rapid fluid loss | ORS (or IV fluids); doxycycline or azithromycin (shortens illness) |
| Hepatitis A | Hepatitis A virus (HAV) | Faecal-oral route; contaminated water, shellfish | Jaundice, fever, anorexia, malaise; self-limiting | Supportive; HAV vaccine available |
| Staphylococcal food poisoning | *S. aureus* toxin (pre-formed) | Contaminated food left at room temperature (cream cakes, custard) | Sudden onset vomiting and diarrhoea within 2–6 hours; NO fever | Supportive; self-limiting; antibiotics not needed (toxin-mediated) |
| Salmonellosis | *Salmonella* spp. (non-typhoidal) | Poultry, eggs, meat | Diarrhoea, nausea, abdominal cramp, fever; 12–72 hours incubation | Rehydration; antibiotics only for severe or immunocompromised |
| *E. coli* diarrhoea | ETEC, EHEC, etc. | Contaminated water, undercooked meat, raw vegetables | ETEC: watery diarrhoea (traveller's diarrhoea); EHEC (O157:H7): bloody diarrhoea, haemolytic uraemic syndrome (HUS) | Rehydration; avoid antibiotics in EHEC (risk of HUS) |
| Botulism | *Clostridium botulinum* toxin | Home-canned or preserved foods | Descending flaccid paralysis starting from cranial nerves (diplopia, dysphagia), no fever, constipation | Antitoxin (trivalent); ventilatory support; intensive care |
| Brucellosis | *Brucella* spp. | Unpasteurised milk/dairy products, contact with infected animals | Undulant fever, joint pain, hepatosplenomegaly; chronic fatigue | Doxycycline + rifampicin for 6 weeks |

## 3.6 Food safety

**Food safety** involves practices to prevent food-borne illness across the food chain (production, processing, storage, distribution, preparation, consumption).

**Key food safety practices**:
- **Clean**: wash hands before cooking and eating; wash fruits and vegetables with clean water.
- **Separate**: raw meat, poultry and seafood away from ready-to-eat food; separate cutting boards.
- **Cook**: cook food to safe internal temperature (meat: 75°C core temperature); no pink pork or poultry.
- **Chill**: refrigerate perishables within 2 hours; keep cold foods below 5°C; hot foods above 60°C.
- **Do not eat**: foods past expiry date; unlabelled or bulging cans; suspicious street food.

**Food adulteration**: deliberate addition of inferior or harmful substances. Examples: milk adulteration with water or urea; ghee with animal fat; spices with sawdust or colour; pulses with Kesari dal (lathyrism risk).

**Prevention of Food Adulteration Act (PFA) 1954** and **Food Safety and Standards Act (FSSA) 2006**: legal framework for food quality in India. The **Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI)** regulates food safety standards, licensing, and testing.

**Nurse's role in food safety**: food hygiene counselling in antenatal and child health clinics; identify food adulteration or contamination hazards in community surveys; report to local health officers; support community kitchen gardens (nutritional security) and homestead food production.

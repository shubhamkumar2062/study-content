# Unit 5: Family health care, primary care in the community, records and school health services

The family is the basic unit of community health nursing care. Primary health care delivered at community level reaches populations that hospitals cannot. School health services protect children during their most formative years. This unit covers each of these areas.

---

## 5.1 Family health

**Family** is a group of individuals related by blood, marriage, adoption, or mutual interdependence. The family is the basic social unit and the fundamental unit of community health nursing care.

**Family health** encompasses the physical, mental, social, and emotional health of all family members and the functioning of the family as a unit.

### Types of families in India

| Type | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Nuclear family | Couple and their unmarried children |
| Joint (extended) family | Multiple generations or related families living together |
| Three-generation family | Grandparents, parents, and children |
| Single-parent family | One parent and children |
| Reconstituted / blended | Step-parents and step-children |

### Family health assessment

A complete family health assessment includes:
- **Family structure**: type, number, ages, relationships.
- **Family function**: communication, decision-making, roles, support systems.
- **Health status of each member**: illnesses, disabilities, immunisation status.
- **Socioeconomic factors**: income, housing, education, occupation.
- **Environmental factors**: water, sanitation, food security, hazards.
- **Health-seeking behaviour**: use of health services, traditional practices, barriers to care.

**Genogram**: a visual representation of a family structure across generations, showing health conditions and relationships. Used to identify hereditary conditions and family health patterns.

**Family risk assessment**: identify high-risk families for priority visiting — families with infants, pregnant women, elderly, disabilities, communicable disease, domestic violence, poverty.

### Family nursing process

**Assessment → Nursing Diagnosis → Planning → Implementation → Evaluation**

The community health nurse works with the family (not just the individual patient) as the client. The focus is on **family strengths** as well as problems, and on building the family's capacity to care for its members.

## 5.2 Reproductive and child health (RCH) in the community

Reproductive and Child Health services are the core of family health care delivered by ASHA, ANM, and community health nurses.

### Antenatal care (ANC)

**National recommendation** (National Health Mission): minimum 4 ANC visits (earlier 4, now WHO recommends 8+). Components at each visit:
- Blood pressure measurement.
- Weight and fundal height assessment.
- Urinalysis (protein, glucose).
- Haemoglobin.
- Foetal heart rate and position.
- Immunisation (TT/Td vaccination).
- Iron and folic acid supplementation.
- Counselling (diet, rest, danger signs, institutional delivery).

**Danger signs in pregnancy** (refer immediately): bleeding, severe headache with visual disturbance, swelling of face/hands/legs, reduced or absent foetal movements, prolonged labour, fever.

**Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA)**: monthly fixed-day ANC clinics at PHCs and CHCs; doctor-led examination for all pregnant women on the 9th of every month.

### Postnatal care

- Minimum 3 postnatal home visits: within 24 hours, Day 3, and Day 7 (and Day 14 and Day 42 as needed).
- Assess uterine involution, lochia, episiotomy/C-section wound, breastfeeding, immunisation (BCG, OPV₀, Hep B₀ at birth or within 24 hours), mental state (postnatal depression).
- Exclusive breastfeeding support.

### Immunisation schedule (National Immunisation Schedule 2023)

| Age | Vaccines |
| --- | --- |
| Birth | BCG, OPV₀, Hepatitis B (Birth Dose) |
| 6 weeks | OPV₁, Penta₁ (DPT+HepB+Hib), IPV₁, Rotavirus₁, PCV₁, fIPV₁ |
| 10 weeks | OPV₂, Penta₂, Rotavirus₂, PCV₂ |
| 14 weeks | OPV₃, Penta₃, IPV₂, Rotavirus₃, PCV₃ |
| 9–12 months | Measles-Rubella (MR)₁, JE₁ (endemic states), Vitamin A₁ |
| 16–24 months | DPT Booster₁, OPV Booster, MR₂, JE₂, Vitamin A₂ |
| 5–6 years | DPT Booster₂ |
| 10 years | Td |
| 16 years | Td |

**Cold chain**: vaccines must be stored at correct temperatures (most vaccines 2–8°C; OPV −15 to −25°C) throughout the supply chain. Any break in cold chain makes vaccines ineffective. The cold chain equipment includes ILR (Ice-Lined Refrigerator), deep freezers, cold boxes, vaccine carriers, and ice packs.

**Adverse Events Following Immunisation (AEFI)**: any untoward reaction after vaccination. Report to block/district health authorities. Classify as: vaccine-related, programme error, coincidental, injection-related, unknown. BCG lymphadenopathy, local redness/swelling (DPT), febrile seizure (DPT/MMR) are common mild AEFIs. Anaphylaxis is a rare severe AEFI — epinephrine must be available at all immunisation sessions.

## 5.3 Primary health care in India

**Primary Health Care (PHC)** as defined at Alma-Ata (1978): essential health care made universally accessible to individuals and families in the community at a cost the community and country can afford. It is the first level of contact between the community and the health system.

**Eight elements of PHC (Alma-Ata)**: education about health problems and methods of prevention and control; promotion of food supply and proper nutrition; adequate supply of safe water and basic sanitation; maternal and child health care including family planning; immunisation against major infectious diseases; prevention and control of locally endemic diseases; appropriate treatment of common diseases and injuries; provision of essential drugs.

### Rural health infrastructure (India)

| Facility | Population served | Level | Staff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sub-Centre (SC) | 3,000–5,000 | Peripheral | ANM, Male Health Worker (MPW) |
| Primary Health Centre (PHC) | 20,000–30,000 (plain) / 10,000–20,000 (hilly/tribal) | 1st level referral | Medical Officer, ANM, PHN, lab technician |
| Community Health Centre (CHC) | 80,000–1,20,000 | 2nd level referral | 4 specialists (surgery, medicine, obstetrics, paediatrics) |
| District Hospital | District | 3rd level | Multi-speciality |

**Health and Wellness Centre (HWC)**: upgraded Sub-Centres and PHCs under Ayushman Bharat (2018); deliver Comprehensive Primary Health Care (CPHC) including NCD screening, mental health, oral health, and palliative care; staffed by Community Health Officers (CHO) with 6-month bridge course.

**ASHA (Accredited Social Health Activist)**: a community health worker, usually a woman from the same community (1 per 1,000 population in rural areas). Roles include: birth registration, immunisation mobilisation, ANC tracking, institutional delivery promotion, DOTS supporter (TB), condom and OCP distribution, linking community to health system. Paid by performance-based incentives (PBIs).

## 5.4 Community health records

**Health records** are a legal document and an essential tool for planning, monitoring, and evaluating health services.

### Records maintained at community level

| Record | Maintained by | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Family folder / household survey register | ANM/PHN | Demographic details, health status, immunisation records of all family members in a subcentre area |
| Eligible couple register | ANM | Women aged 15–45 in reproductive union; for family planning tracking |
| Antenatal register | ANM | All registered pregnant women; ANC visits, TT, IFA, risk factors |
| Immunisation register | ANM | Child immunisation records; due-list for each session |
| MCH register | ANM | Maternal and child health events (births, deaths, ANC, PNC) |
| BPL register | ANM/ASHA | Below-poverty-line families; priority for social welfare schemes |
| Outreach session diary | ANM | Record of activities conducted at each session site |
| ASHA activity register | ASHA | Home visits, referrals, services delivered |
| Disease surveillance register | PHC | Notifiable diseases reported; feeds into IDSP |

**Integrated Health Management Portal (IHMP)** and **Mother and Child Tracking System (MCTS/RCH Portal)**: online registration of pregnant women and children to track services; generates due-lists automatically for ANMs and ASHAs.

### Reports

| Report | Frequency | Sent from → to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MPW (F) monthly report | Monthly | SC → PHC |
| MO's monthly report | Monthly | PHC → Block / BPMU |
| Form P (vital events) | Monthly | SC → PHC |
| IDSP (Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme) S, P, L forms | Weekly | PHC/CHC → District → State → Centre |

## 5.5 School health services

**School health services** promote the health of school children (6–18 years — approximately 250 million children in India) during the most formative years of physical and mental development.

**Components of a school health programme**:
1. **Health appraisal**: annual health examination; vision, hearing, dental, growth assessment; identify and refer problems.
2. **Health promotion**: health education in curriculum (hygiene, nutrition, life skills, reproductive health for older students); physical exercise.
3. **Prevention and control of communicable diseases**: maintain immunisation records; identify contacts in outbreaks; deworming (Albendazole 400 mg twice yearly under National Deworming Day); manage outbreaks (school closure if necessary).
4. **Healthful school environment**: safe water and sanitation (Wash in Schools — WinS); clean toilets (separate for girls — a reason girls drop out); safe playground; safe food in canteen.
5. **Nutrition services**: Mid-Day Meal; monitoring for malnutrition; anaemia screening and IFA supplementation (weekly IFA for 10–19 years under WIFS — Weekly Iron and Folic Acid Supplementation programme).
6. **Mental health services**: counselling; identify children with learning disabilities, ADHD, anxiety, depression; anti-bullying programmes.
7. **School health records**: individual health cards; vision and hearing records; vaccination status; growth charts.
8. **Training school teachers**: health education, first aid, identification of health problems.

**Adolescent Reproductive and Sexual Health (ARSH) programme**: health education for 10–19-year-olds; Rashtriya Kishor Swasthya Karyakram (RKSK) — covers nutrition, mental health, substance abuse, sexual health, injuries, non-communicable diseases for adolescents.

**National Deworming Day**: 10 February and 10 August (bi-annual); children aged 1–19 years receive Albendazole 400 mg; reduces soil-transmitted helminth burden which causes anaemia, malnutrition, and impaired learning.

**School nurse role**: annual health check; first aid; health education; case referral; liaison with parents and teachers; maintaining records; leading health days (World Health Day, No Tobacco Day).

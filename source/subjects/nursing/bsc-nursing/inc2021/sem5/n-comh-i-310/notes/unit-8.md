# Unit 8: Non-communicable diseases and national health programmes

Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) — cardiovascular disease, cancer, diabetes, and chronic respiratory disease — now account for over 60% of deaths in India. The community health nurse plays a vital role in screening, early detection, lifestyle counselling, and linking people to care. This shift from acute to chronic disease is one of the defining challenges of modern public health.

---

## 8.1 Global and Indian burden of NCDs

NCDs are characterised by:
- Chronic course (lasting months to years).
- Complex, multifactorial causation.
- Largely preventable through behaviour and environment change.
- Requiring long-term management, not a single cure.

**Four main NCDs** (WHO): cardiovascular diseases (CVDs), cancers, chronic respiratory diseases, and diabetes mellitus. Together they account for 71% of global deaths.

**India-specific burden**: cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death; India has the second-highest number of people with diabetes globally; cancer incidence is rising, especially breast, cervical, and oral; COPD and asthma are highly prevalent due to tobacco use and air pollution.

**Common risk factors (shared across NCDs)**:
- Tobacco use.
- Harmful use of alcohol.
- Unhealthy diet (high in salt, sugar, saturated fat; low in fruits and vegetables).
- Physical inactivity.
- Overweight and obesity.
- Air pollution (indoor and outdoor).

## 8.2 Hypertension

**Definition**: Blood pressure ≥ 140/90 mmHg on two separate occasions.

**Classification** (JNC 8 / India):
- Normal: < 120/80.
- Pre-hypertension: 120–139/80–89.
- Stage 1 HTN: 140–159/90–99.
- Stage 2 HTN: ≥ 160/≥ 100.

**Risk factors**: age (> 55 males, > 65 females), family history, salt intake, obesity, alcohol, sedentary lifestyle, tobacco.

**Complications**: stroke, coronary artery disease, heart failure, chronic kidney disease, retinopathy.

**Screening**: CPHC guidelines recommend screening all adults ≥ 30 years at HWCs; take BP in both arms first time; confirm on 2 separate visits.

**Community nursing role**:
- Measure BP accurately (cuff size, sitting, after 5 minutes rest, no caffeine or smoking within 30 minutes).
- Lifestyle counselling: DASH diet (reduce salt to < 5 g/day, increase fruits and vegetables, reduce saturated fat, limit alcohol); weight reduction; physical activity (150 min/week moderate intensity); smoking cessation.
- Medication adherence support; identify side effects (cough with ACE inhibitors, ankle oedema with amlodipine, impotence with β-blockers).
- Home BP monitoring; keep a BP diary.

## 8.3 Diabetes mellitus

**Type 1**: autoimmune destruction of β-cells; insulin-dependent; onset usually in childhood/young adults; thin; 10–15% of all diabetes.

**Type 2**: insulin resistance + progressive β-cell failure; linked to obesity and lifestyle; 85–90% of cases; most adults at community level.

**Gestational diabetes**: onset or first detected during pregnancy; risk of complications for mother and baby; reverts in most cases but high risk of T2DM later.

**Diagnosis criteria** (WHO/IDF):
- FPG ≥ 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) on two occasions, OR
- 2-hour plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL after OGTT, OR
- HbA1c ≥ 6.5%, OR
- Random plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL with symptoms.

**Screening**: CPHC protocol — fasting blood glucose using glucometer at HWCs for all adults ≥ 30 years.

**Management goals**: HbA1c < 7% (< 53 mmol/mol); BP < 130/80; LDL < 2.6 mmol/L.

**Lifestyle management**: dietary advice (consistent carbohydrate intake; limit refined sugar and white rice; increase fibre; portion control); physical activity 150 min/week; weight management; smoking cessation; foot care (inspect feet daily, never walk barefoot, appropriate footwear, nail care, check for numbness — diabetic neuropathy).

**Community nursing role**:
- Screen and refer new diagnoses.
- Medication adherence (oral hypoglycaemic agents: metformin, sulfonylureas; insulin).
- Monitor for complications (retinopathy → annual eye check; nephropathy → urine albumin/creatinine ratio; neuropathy → foot exam; CVD risk).
- Hypoglycaemia recognition and management (< 70 mg/dL: fast-acting glucose, then complex carbohydrate; unconscious: glucagon injection or IV dextrose; teach family members).
- Self-monitoring of blood glucose education.

## 8.4 Cardiovascular diseases

**Coronary artery disease (CAD)**: narrowing of coronary arteries by atherosclerosis → angina, MI. Major modifiable risk factors: hypertension, dyslipidaemia, diabetes, smoking, obesity, physical inactivity, unhealthy diet.

**Stroke**: sudden onset neurological deficit from cerebral infarction (ischaemic, 85%) or haemorrhage (15%). Risk factors: same as CAD plus atrial fibrillation, oral contraceptive use in smokers.

**Rehabilitation after MI/stroke**: community nurse role in home rehabilitation, medication adherence (aspirin, statins, antihypertensives), lifestyle counselling, family education, psychosocial support (depression is common after both), and preventing recurrence.

**Rheumatic fever / Rheumatic heart disease (RHD)**: untreated Group A streptococcal pharyngitis → autoimmune damage to heart valves. India has high RHD burden. Prevention: prompt treatment of strep throat with penicillin; secondary prophylaxis (monthly benzathine penicillin injections for 5–10 years to prevent recurrent rheumatic fever).

## 8.5 Cancer

**Leading cancers in India by incidence** (ICMR 2022): breast (women), cervical (women), oral/lip (men), lung, colorectal, stomach, leukaemia.

**Warning signs of cancer (CAUTION)**:
- Change in bowel or bladder habits.
- A sore that does not heal.
- Unusual bleeding or discharge.
- Thickening or lump in breast or elsewhere.
- Indigestion or difficulty in swallowing.
- Obvious change in a wart or mole.
- Nagging cough or hoarseness.

**Breast cancer screening**: clinical breast examination (CBE) every 3 years for women 25–39, annually from 40; mammography for high-risk women; breast self-examination (BSE) — teach women to examine breasts monthly after menstruation.

**Cervical cancer screening**: Visual Inspection with Acetic Acid (VIA) — cotton swab soaked in 3–5% acetic acid applied to cervix; acetowhite areas indicate HPV-infected or pre-cancerous cells; widely used in India at PHC level by trained ANMs. HPV vaccination: Gardasil/Cervarix for girls 9–14 years; reduces risk of cervical cancer.

**Oral cancer screening**: examine the oral cavity for white patches (leukoplakia), red patches (erythroplakia), non-healing ulcers, abnormal lumps; risk factors: tobacco (smoking and smokeless), alcohol, betel nut. Oral visual examination done by ASHA, ANM, or health worker.

**Community nursing role**: teach cancer warning signs; conduct CBE and oral examination during home visits and outreach camps; promote VIA screening; support newly diagnosed patients and their families; facilitate access to cancer treatment (referral to district cancer care unit or tertiary centre); pain management and palliative care in advanced disease.

## 8.6 Chronic respiratory diseases

**Asthma**: reversible airway obstruction due to inflammation and hyper-reactivity. Management: inhaled bronchodilators (β₂ agonists — salbutamol for acute relief) + inhaled corticosteroids (ICS — for maintenance); avoid triggers.

**COPD (Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease)**: progressive, largely irreversible obstruction; caused mainly by smoking and biomass fuel smoke; characterised by chronic cough, sputum, and dyspnoea; FEV₁/FVC < 0.70 on spirometry. Management: smoking cessation (most important); bronchodilators (long-acting β₂ agonists and/or anticholinergics); ICS for frequent exacerbations; pulmonary rehabilitation; oxygen therapy for severe disease.

**Nurse's role**: spirometry performance; smoking cessation counselling; correct inhaler technique (a common problem — patients use spacer devices incorrectly); exacerbation recognition (increased dyspnoea, purulent sputum → start antibiotics/prednisolone); nutrition support; psychosocial support.

## 8.7 Mental health and national programmes

**Common mental disorders in the community**: depression, anxiety, alcohol and substance use disorders (high prevalence, low treatment rates).

**Suicide**: India accounts for over 17% of global suicides; adolescents and farmers are high-risk groups; underlying mental illness (depression, substance use) in most cases. The nurse's role: safe messaging (do not glamorise); risk assessment; listening without judgement; refer to mental health professional; remove access to means where possible.

**National Mental Health Programme (NMHP)**: District Mental Health Programme (DMHP) — trained mental health teams at district level; integration of mental health into primary health care (mhGAP — Mental Health Gap Action Programme: trained primary care staff to identify and treat common mental disorders with WHO mhGAP guide).

**Alcohol and substance use**: India has high alcohol use disorder burden; tobacco use (smoking and smokeless tobacco); cannabis, opioids (especially in Northeast). Intervention: brief motivational counselling; referral to deaddiction centres; Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyan (National NCD awareness programme for youth).

## 8.8 National NCD programme

**National Programme for Prevention and Control of Cancer, Diabetes, Cardiovascular Disease and Stroke (NPCDCS)**: India's main NCD control programme.

**Key activities under NPCDCS**:
- Population-based screening at HWCs for hypertension, diabetes, three common cancers (oral, breast, cervical) for all persons ≥ 30 years.
- NCD clinics at CHC level.
- Cardiac care units and cancer care units at district level.
- Lifestyle modification counselling (CPHC wellness activities).
- Referral pathways from community to district.

**Community Health Officer (CHO) at HWC**: performs population-level NCD screening; provides basic treatment for common conditions; counsels on lifestyle change; refers complex cases to PHC or higher.

**Tobacco control**: National Tobacco Control Programme (NTCP); COTPA 2003 (Cigarettes and Other Tobacco Products Act) — prohibits tobacco advertisements, mandates graphic warning labels, bans smoking in public places; National Quitline (1800-11-2356); mCessation text-message programme.

**Nurse's advocacy role in NCD prevention**: conduct NCD screening camps; support self-help groups for diabetes and hypertension (peer support improves adherence); promote physical activity in community settings (yoga, walking groups); advocate for smoke-free environments and healthy school canteens; support policy (salt and sugar reduction in food; trans-fat elimination); address the mental health dimension of living with chronic disease.

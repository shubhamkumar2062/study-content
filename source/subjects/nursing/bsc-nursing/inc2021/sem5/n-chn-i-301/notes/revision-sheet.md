## Developmental milestones — key ages (four domains)

| Age | Gross motor | Fine motor | Language | Social |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 months | Head control (held steady) | Follows 180° | Cooing | **Social smile** |
| 6 months | Sits with support; rolls | Palmar grasp; transfers | Babbles | Stranger anxiety |
| 9 months | Sits alone; crawls | Inferior pincer | Dada/mama (non-specific) | Waves bye |
| 12 months | Stands; first steps | **Pincer grip** | 1–2 words | Separation anxiety peak |
| 18 months | Runs | Stacks 2–3 cubes | 10–20 words | Feeds self |
| 2 years | Runs well; stairs (2-feet/step) | Stacks 6; copies line | 2-word phrases | Parallel play |
| 3 years | Stands 1 foot 3 s; tricycle | Copies circle | Sentences; 300 words | Toilet trained day |
| 5 years | Skips | Copies triangle | Counts 10; knows colours | Dresses alone |

**Red flag**: regression (loss of an acquired skill) is ALWAYS abnormal.  
**Red flags**: not social smile by 3 m; not sitting by 9 m; not walking by 18 m; no speech by 18 m.

## Apgar score

| Sign | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Appearance (colour) | Blue/pale all over | Blue extremities | Pink all over |
| Pulse | Absent | < 100/min | ≥ 100/min |
| Grimace (reflex) | None | Grimace | Cry/cough |
| Activity (tone) | Limp | Some flexion | Active |
| Respiration | Absent | Weak/irregular | Strong cry |

Score 7–10 normal; 4–6 moderate; 0–3 severe.

## Neonatal jaundice — key facts

| Feature | Physiological | Pathological |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Onset | > 24 hours | < 24 hours |
| Course | Peaks day 3–5; resolves by day 10–14 | Rising rapidly; persists |
| Causes | High RBC turnover, immature liver | Rh/ABO incompatibility, G6PD deficiency, sepsis |

**Phototherapy**: eye patches, unclothed, increase fluids 10–20%; monitor temperature.  
**Kramer Zone 5** (palms + soles yellow) = bilirubin > 15 mg/dL.

## Paediatric BLS (PBLS) — key differences from adult

| Point | PBLS | Adult BLS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial breaths | **5** rescue breaths | 2 rescue breaths |
| Compression:ventilation ratio | **15:2** (2 rescuers); 30:2 (1 rescuer) | 30:2 |
| Compression depth | **1/3 chest AP** (~4 cm infant, ~5 cm child) | 5–6 cm |
| Compression rate | 100–120/min | 100–120/min |
| Infant technique | 2-thumb encircling | — |
| AED | From **1 year** (paediatric pads) | Any age |

## Emergency drug doses

| Drug | Indication | Dose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adrenaline (cardiac arrest) | HR < 60 despite CPR | 0.01 mg/kg IV/IO; 0.1 mg/kg ETT |
| Adrenaline (anaphylaxis) | Anaphylaxis | 0.01 mg/kg IM anterolateral thigh (max 0.5 mg) |
| Dextrose 10% | Hypoglycaemia | 2 mL/kg IV bolus over 5 min |
| Diazepam PR | Febrile/acute seizure > 5 min | 0.5 mg/kg PR |

| Lorazepam IV | Status epilepticus (1st line) | 0.1 mg/kg IV |

| IV fluid (shock) | Septic shock, severe dehydration | 20 mL/kg 0.9% NaCl bolus; reassess |

## DKA — key management points

1. IV fluids (0.9% NaCl or Ringer's lactate) corrected over **48 hours** (not fast — cerebral oedema risk).
2. Insulin infusion 0.05–0.1 U/kg/h — start **1–2 hours after fluids**.
3. Potassium replacement once urine output confirmed.
4. Monitor neurological status hourly — headache/change in consciousness = cerebral oedema → mannitol 0.25–1 g/kg IV.

## Nutritional deficiencies — one-liners

| Deficiency | Key sign | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IDA (Hb < 11) | Pallor, pica, koilonychia | Ferrous sulfate 3–6 mg/kg/day × 3 months |
| Vitamin A | Night blindness → Bitot's spots → keratomalacia | Mega-dose at 9 months (1 lakh IU), then 2 lakh IU/6 months |
| Iodine | Goitre; cretinism (maternal deficiency) | Iodised salt |
| Vitamin D rickets | Bow legs, Harrison's sulcus, craniotabes | Vitamin D 1,500–5,000 IU/day + calcium |

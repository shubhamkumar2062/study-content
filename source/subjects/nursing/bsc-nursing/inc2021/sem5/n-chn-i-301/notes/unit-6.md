# Unit 6: Integrated Management of Neonatal and Childhood Illness (IMNCI)

This unit covers INC Unit VI: the IMNCI strategy, its assessment and classification framework, management of each illness category, and the nurse's role in community-level IMNCI implementation. IMNCI questions are frequently asked in short-answer and MCQ sections.

---

## 6.1 Background and rationale

**IMNCI** (Integrated Management of Neonatal and Childhood Illness) is a clinical and health systems strategy developed by WHO and UNICEF in the mid-1990s to reduce child mortality from the five leading causes: pneumonia, diarrhoea, malaria, measles, and malnutrition/anaemia.

India implemented IMNCI within the **National Health Mission (NHM)** from 2005. Training is given to frontline workers: ASHAs, Auxiliary Nurse Midwives (ANMs), nurses, and doctors.

**Why IMNCI?**
- Young children often have more than one illness at the same time (a child with pneumonia may also be anaemic and malnourished).
- Seeing each disease separately leads to missed diagnoses and over- or under-treatment.
- IMNCI uses a **single integrated assessment** covering all major causes of death in one visit.

**Target age group**: 0–59 months (0–28 days covered by the **Young Infant** chart; 2–59 months covered by the **Child** chart).

For sick neonates at a facility, **Facility-Based Newborn Care (FBNC)** at district hospitals and special newborn care units (SNCUs) provides a higher level of care.

## 6.2 The IMNCI five-step process

```text
STEP 1: ASK the caretaker about the child's problems
STEP 2: CHECK the child (examine for signs)
STEP 3: CLASSIFY the illness (colour-coded)
STEP 4: IDENTIFY TREATMENT (drug, dose, duration, referral)
STEP 5: TREAT / REFER / COUNSEL / FOLLOW-UP
```

**Who does IMNCI?** At community level: ASHA and AWW (brief version). At sub-centre/PHC: ANM/nurse (full IMNCI). At CHC/district hospital: medical officer (can manage yellow/pink classifications).

## 6.3 Danger signs

Danger signs must be checked **in every sick child** before anything else. A child with **any danger sign** gets a PINK classification and must be referred urgently.

**General danger signs (2–59 months)**:
1. Cannot drink or breastfeed (too weak to suck or drink)
2. Vomits everything
3. Convulsions (now, or in this illness)
4. Lethargic or unconscious
5. Stridor at rest
6. Severe visible malnutrition (marasmus, kwashiorkor)
7. Central cyanosis

## 6.4 Classification colour coding

| Colour | Meaning | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pink** | Severe/urgent | Refer to hospital immediately; give pre-referral treatment (first dose of antibiotics, ORS, glucose) |
| **Yellow** | Needs specific treatment at clinic or home | Give specific treatment (antibiotics, ORS, zinc, antimalarial); advise return |
| **Green** | No specific treatment needed | Home care; counsel caretaker; advise when to return |

## 6.5 Illness categories and classification (2–59 months)

### 1. Cough or difficult breathing

**Fast breathing** thresholds by age (key fact to memorise):

| Age | Fast breathing (per minute) |
| --- | --- |
| < 2 months | ≥ 60 |
| 2–12 months | ≥ 50 |
| 1–5 years | ≥ 40 |

| Classification | Signs | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Severe pneumonia or very severe disease (PINK)** | Fast breathing + danger sign (chest in-drawing, stridor, cyanosis, cannot drink) | Refer; first dose antibiotics (IM benzylpenicillin or amoxicillin) |
| **Pneumonia (YELLOW)** | Fast breathing only (no danger signs) | Oral amoxicillin 40 mg/kg/day × 5 days; soothe throat; follow-up in 2 days |
| **No pneumonia: cough or cold (GREEN)** | No fast breathing, no chest in-drawing | Soothe throat/cough with safe remedies; advise return if worsens |

**Chest in-drawing**: lower chest wall moves inward on **inspiration** (this is different from normal chest rise). It indicates severe respiratory distress.

### 2. Diarrhoea

| Classification | Signs | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Severe dehydration (PINK)** | 2 or more of: lethargic, sunken eyes, unable to drink, skin pinch slow (> 3 s) | Refer; IV Ringer's lactate Plan C (100 mL/kg over 3–6 h) |
| **Some dehydration (YELLOW)** | 2 or more of: restless, sunken eyes, drinks eagerly (thirsty), skin pinch slow (2–3 s) | ORS Plan B (75 mL/kg over 4 h in facility); zinc; continue feeding |
| **No dehydration (GREEN)** | Not enough signs for some dehydration | ORS Plan A (home); zinc 20 mg/day × 10 days; continue breastfeeding; advise return |
| **Persistent diarrhoea (YELLOW)** | ≥ 14 days | Treat dehydration; give vitamin A; refer if severe |
| **Dysentery (YELLOW)** | Blood in stool | Ciprofloxacin (or nalidixic acid) for 3 days; follow-up in 2 days |

**Zinc supplementation**: 10 mg/day (< 6 months) or 20 mg/day (≥ 6 months) for 10 days reduces duration and severity of diarrhoea and prevents subsequent episodes. This is a key IMNCI teaching point.

### 3. Fever

| Classification | Signs | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Very severe febrile disease (PINK)** | Stiff neck, bulging fontanelle, petechiae, lethargic | Refer; first dose IM antibiotics; treat for malaria if RDT/microscopy positive |
| **Malaria (YELLOW)** | Fever + positive RDT or microscopy (or in a high malaria area) | Artemisinin combination therapy (ACT) as per national malaria treatment policy |
| **Measles (YELLOW)** | Fever + maculopapular rash + one of: cough, runny nose, red eyes | Vitamin A (reduces severity); treat complications; notify |
| **Fever only (GREEN)** | No danger signs, no specific diagnosis | Paracetamol for high fever; sponge; advise return |

**Dengue warning signs**: sudden high fever, severe headache, eye pain, petechiae, positive tourniquet test → refer.

### 4. Ear problem

| Classification | Signs | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mastoiditis (PINK)** | Tender swelling behind the ear | Refer |
| **Acute otitis media (YELLOW)** | Ear pain or discharge for < 14 days | Amoxicillin × 5 days; dry the ear wick |
| **Chronic otitis media (YELLOW)** | Discharge for ≥ 14 days | Dry the ear with wicking; refer if no improvement in 5 days |
| **No ear problem (GREEN)** | None | Counsel |

**Ear wicking**: roll a clean, dry piece of cloth into a wick, insert gently into the ear canal (do not push further than you can see), leave for a few seconds to absorb discharge, repeat with a new wick until it comes out dry. Teach the caretaker to do this 3 times daily.

### 5. Malnutrition and anaemia

| Classification | Criteria | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Severe acute malnutrition (SAM) (PINK)** | MUAC < 11.5 cm OR W/H < −3 Z-score OR bilateral pitting oedema | Refer for therapeutic feeding (RUTF — Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food) |
| **Moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) (YELLOW)** | MUAC 11.5–12.5 cm OR W/H −2 to −3 Z-score | Supplementary feeding (SNCU, ICDS take-home ration); nutrition counselling |
| **Severe anaemia (PINK)** | Very pale palms; Hb < 7–8 g/dL | Refer |
| **Anaemia (YELLOW)** | Pale palms; Hb 7–10 g/dL | Iron for 14 days; mebendazole if ≥ 2 years; advise return |
| **Normal (GREEN)** | No pallor | Counsel on feeding |

## 6.6 IMNCI for the young infant (0–2 months)

The young infant chart uses different signs. Assess for:

1. **Very severe disease/local bacterial infection**: convulsions, fast breathing (≥ 60/min), severe chest in-drawing, nasal flaring, grunting, bulging fontanelle, pustules (many/severe), eye discharge with redness spreading to the lid margin, temperature > 37.5°C or < 35.5°C, lethargic.
2. **Diarrhoea** (watery stools; dehydration signs as above but adapted for young infants — sunken eyes, skin pinch, reduced feeding).
3. **Jaundice** — yellow after 24 hours: check if soles are yellow → phototherapy; if yellow before 24 h → refer immediately.
4. **Feeding problem or low weight**: MUAC is not useful; check feeding; measure weight.

**Pre-referral treatment for young infant**: first dose IM ampicillin + gentamicin or IM benzylpenicillin; prevent hypothermia (wrap, KMC); give expressed breast milk if can swallow; refer promptly.

## 6.7 Community workers and IMNCI

**ASHA (Accredited Social Health Activist)**:
- Visits newborns on day 1, 3, 7, 14, 21, 28, 42 (HBNC — Home Based Newborn Care).
- Assesses for IMNCI danger signs, infection, hypothermia, breastfeeding.
- Counsels on IYCF, immunisation, care of the sick child.
- Refers serious cases; accompanies families to the facility.

**AWW (Anganwadi Worker)**:
- Monitors growth and nutrition at the anganwadi.
- Provides supplementary nutrition.
- Tracks immunisation default.
- Refers SAM/MAM to the nearest SNCU/NRC (Nutrition Rehabilitation Centre).

**ANM/Nurse**:
- Runs immunisation sessions and OPD at the sub-centre.
- Provides full IMNCI assessment, classifies, and treats yellow-classified children.
- Refers pink-classified children.
- Records and reports cases; maintains IMNCI registers.

## 6.8 Follow-up visits

IMNCI specifies when to bring the child back for follow-up based on the classification:

| Classification | Follow-up |
| --- | --- |
| Pneumonia | 2 days |
| Some dehydration | After Plan B (4 h); or in 2 days if treated at home |
| Dysentery | 2 days |
| Fever (malaria, dengue) | 2 days |
| Ear infection | 5 days |
| Malnutrition/anaemia | 14–30 days |

**Counsel the caretaker** on when to return immediately (any danger sign: worsening, unable to drink, convulsion, fast breathing, fever, blood in stool, yellow skin or eyes in a young infant).

## 6.9 Recording IMNCI case management

The IMNCI case recording form includes:
- Patient details (age, weight, temperature)
- Danger signs checked
- Each illness category: main signs, classification, treatment given
- Counselling given
- Follow-up date

Completed forms are retained at the facility and aggregated for HMIS (Health Management Information System) reporting, which tracks disease burden and programme performance.

## Remember

- IMNCI: integrated, simplified, 5 steps — Ask, Check, Classify, Identify treatment, Treat/Refer/Counsel/Follow-up.
- Fast breathing thresholds: < 2 months ≥ 60/min; 2–12 months ≥ 50/min; 1–5 years ≥ 40/min.
- Any danger sign (cannot drink, vomits all, convulsions, lethargic, stridor at rest, severe malnutrition) → PINK → refer immediately.
- Zinc 20 mg/day × 10 days with every episode of diarrhoea — reduces duration and future episodes.
- SAM: MUAC < 11.5 cm OR W/H < −3 Z OR bilateral pitting oedema → refer for RUTF/therapeutic feeding.
- Malaria: treat with ACT (artemisinin combination therapy) if positive RDT/microscopy.
- Young infant: fast breathing ≥ 60/min; hypothermia ≤ 35.5°C → very severe disease → refer with first dose IM antibiotics.
- ASHA does HBNC (7 visits in first 42 days); AWW monitors growth at anganwadi; ANM/nurse runs IMNCI sessions.

# How to use this guide

N-CHN(I)301 Child Health Nursing I is a Semester 5 subject of the INC 2021 B.Sc Nursing programme. It introduces the nursing care of children across the full age range from birth to adolescence — covering national policy, immunisation, hospital care, growth and development, neonatal care, the IMNCI framework, respiratory and endocrine diseases, and childhood emergencies. The companion subject N-CHN(II)301 (Semester 6) covers surgical, haematological, and other conditions and continues from this foundation.

## University examination pattern

Child Health Nursing I is examined in an end-semester written examination in Semester 5 of the INC 2021 programme:

| Section | Format | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Section A** | 20 multiple-choice questions × 1 mark | 20 |
| **Section B** | Attempt 2 out of 3 long essays × 10 marks | 20 |
| **Section C** | Attempt 7 out of 9 short answer questions × 5 marks | 35 |
| **Total** | | **75** |

Time: 3 hours. Confirm the exact pattern and internal choice structure with your university, as these may vary.

A separate **Quick MCQ test** (20 questions, 20 minutes) in this pack lets you drill facts quickly before class tests or viva.

Study one notes file at a sitting. Read through the unit, then draw each `text` diagram once from memory. Attempt that unit's questions and MCQs without looking. Use the revision sheet for the night before the examination.

## Probability ratings

- **High** — a consistent favourite in INC B.Sc Nursing examinations; expect it in every paper in some form. Learn the full structured answer.
- **Medium** — appears in every other paper or as part of a larger question.
- **Low** — less frequent; still worth knowing for MCQs and very short answers.

These ratings are estimates based on the topic's weight in the INC syllabus and the emphasis placed on child health nursing in Indian nursing examinations. They are a guide, not a guarantee.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. **Give the age-specific normal value.** An examiner asking about respiratory rate, heart rate, or blood glucose in a child expects the paediatric reference range, not the adult value. Cite units 4–8 normal values tables.

2. **Use the nursing care plan structure for clinical questions.** For any "nursing management of..." question, structure your answer as: Assessment → Nursing diagnoses → Planning → Implementation (medical management, nursing actions, health education) → Evaluation. This scores marks for organisation even if your clinical detail is not perfect.

3. **Answer comparison questions as a table.** Croup vs epiglottitis, physiological vs pathological jaundice, simple vs complex febrile seizure, IMNCI pink vs yellow vs green — tables are faster to write and easier to mark.

4. **Lead clinical answers with a definition.** One line before the main answer ("Febrile seizure is a seizure in a child aged 6 months to 5 years associated with fever > 38°C without evidence of intracranial infection or metabolic cause.") signals to the examiner that you understand the topic.

5. **State the IMNCI framework for sick-child questions.** When asked how to assess or manage a sick child under 5, mentioning IMNCI (assess, classify, treat/refer) shows integration of national guidelines and scores additional marks.

6. **Cite the National Immunisation Schedule accurately.** Examiners notice when students mix up ages, routes, and sites. Learn the schedule table in Unit 2 by heart.

7. **The night before**: read the "Remember" section at the end of each notes file; run the Quick MCQ test; review the revision sheet below.

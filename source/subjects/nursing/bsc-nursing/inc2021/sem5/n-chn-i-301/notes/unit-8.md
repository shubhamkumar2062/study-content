# Unit 8: Childhood emergencies and paediatric life support

This unit covers INC Unit VIII: recognition of the seriously ill child, paediatric basic life support (PBLS), and the management of common paediatric emergencies including febrile seizures, status epilepticus, septic shock, anaphylaxis, hypoglycaemia, DKA, and drowning. This is a high-probability examination topic.

---

## 8.1 Recognising the seriously ill child

### Paediatric Assessment Triangle (PAT)

The PAT is performed in the first 30–60 seconds from the door, before touching the child, using **sight and sound only**.

| Component | What to observe | Abnormal finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Appearance** | Tone, interactiveness, consolability, look/gaze, speech/cry | Limp, unresponsive, inconsolable, abnormal cry |
| **Work of breathing** | Sounds, position, retractions, nasal flaring | Grunting, stridor, wheeze, see-saw respirations, tripod position |
| **Circulation to skin** | Colour of skin, lips | Pallor, mottling, cyanosis |

**PAT result:**
- All normal: stable — may not be acutely ill.
- Abnormal appearance only: CNS problem or metabolic disorder.
- Abnormal work of breathing only: respiratory problem.
- Abnormal circulation only: circulatory shock.
- Multiple abnormal: severe, imminent cardiorespiratory failure.

### ABCDE approach

A systematic hands-on assessment:

```text
A — Airway: open? patent? sounds? snoring, stridor, gurgling?
B — Breathing: RR, SpO₂, auscultation, air entry, work of breathing
C — Circulation: HR, BP (use correct cuff size), capillary refill (CRT < 2 s normal),
     skin colour/temperature, pulses (central vs peripheral)
D — Disability: AVPU (Alert, responds to Voice, responds to Pain, Unresponsive)
     or GCS; pupils (size, symmetry, reaction); blood glucose
E — Exposure: temperature, rash (petechiae = meningococcal emergency),
     injuries, evidence of NAI (non-accidental injury)
```

**Normal vital signs by age**:

| Age | HR (bpm) | RR (/min) | Systolic BP (mmHg) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Neonate (0–28 d) | 100–160 | 40–60 | 50–90 |
| Infant (1–12 m) | 100–160 | 30–40 | 70–90 |
| Toddler (1–3 y) | 90–150 | 25–35 | 80–100 |
| Preschool (3–5 y) | 80–140 | 20–30 | 80–100 |
| School-age (6–12 y) | 70–120 | 18–25 | 90–110 |
| Adolescent (> 12 y) | 60–100 | 12–20 | 100–120 |

## 8.2 Paediatric Basic Life Support (PBLS)

PBLS differs from adult BLS at several points.

| Step | Infant (< 1 year) | Child (1 year – puberty) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Check for danger** | Yes | Yes |
| **Responsiveness** | Tap sole; shout | Tap shoulder; shout |
| **Call for help** | Shout, then give 1 min CPR before leaving if alone | Same |
| **Open airway** | Head-tilt/chin-lift to neutral position | Head-tilt/chin-lift to sniffing position |
| **Initial rescue breaths** | **5 breaths** (mouth-to-mouth-and-nose) | **5 breaths** (mouth-to-mouth) |
| **Check circulation** | Brachial pulse (up to 10 s) | Carotid pulse (up to 10 s) |
| **Compressions if no pulse or HR < 60 with signs of poor perfusion** | 2-thumb encircling technique OR 2-finger technique | Heel of 1 or 2 hands |
| **Compression depth** | ~4 cm (1/3 AP diameter of chest) | ~5 cm (1/3 AP diameter) |
| **Compression rate** | 100–120 /min | 100–120 /min |
| **Ratio** | 15:2 (two rescuers) or 30:2 (single rescuer) | 15:2 (two rescuers) or 30:2 (single rescuer) |
| **AED** | From 1 year with paediatric pads; < 1 year if no alternative | Standard AED |

**Two-thumb encircling technique (preferred for two-rescuer infant CPR)**: both thumbs on the lower third of the sternum; both hands wrap around the chest; generates higher coronary perfusion pressure than the two-finger technique.

### Paediatric early warning scores (PEWS)

PEWS scores combine behaviour, cardiovascular, and respiratory sub-scores to identify children at risk of deterioration. A rising PEWS triggers escalation to senior staff or critical care. Many hospitals use their own modified version; the principle is early identification before arrest.

## 8.3 Febrile seizures

**Definition**: a seizure in a child aged 6 months – 5 years with fever (> 38°C) without evidence of intracranial infection or metabolic cause.

| Feature | Simple | Complex |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duration | < 15 minutes | ≥ 15 minutes |
| Type | Generalised tonic-clonic | Focal; or repeated within 24 hours |
| Recovery | Complete within 1 hour | Prolonged; Todd's paresis possible |

**Incidence**: 2–5% of children; most benign; recurrence in 30%.

**Management**:
1. **During the seizure**: position the child on their side (recovery position) to prevent aspiration; protect from injury; do NOT put anything in the mouth; do NOT restrain.
2. **If seizure lasts > 5 minutes**: diazepam 0.5 mg/kg **per rectum (PR)** or midazolam 0.2–0.3 mg/kg **buccal** (into the cheek).
3. **After the seizure**: give paracetamol for fever; assess for the source of fever; check blood glucose.
4. **Parent education**: demonstrate how to give rectal diazepam or buccal midazolam; reassure that most febrile seizures are benign and do not cause brain damage; explain what to do (recovery position, time the seizure, call an ambulance if > 5 minutes or not improving); do not stop routine immunisation (fever from vaccination can trigger a seizure in predisposed children, but the benefits of vaccination outweigh this risk).

**Indication for lumbar puncture**: if < 12 months or if meningeal signs are present — do not assume fever + seizure is a simple febrile seizure in a very young infant.

## 8.4 Status epilepticus

**Definition**: a seizure lasting ≥ 30 minutes **or** recurrent seizures without full recovery between them (most protocols use a working definition of ≥ 5 minutes to initiate treatment, as seizures lasting 5 minutes are unlikely to stop spontaneously).

**Algorithm**:

```text
0–5 min: Secure airway, O₂, IV/IO access; blood glucose check; correct if low
5–10 min: Lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg IV (max 4 mg) OR diazepam 0.3 mg/kg IV
            If no IV: diazepam PR 0.5 mg/kg OR midazolam buccal 0.2 mg/kg
            If still seizing after 5–10 min → repeat benzodiazepine × 1
15–20 min: Phenytoin 20 mg/kg IV over 20 min (ECG monitoring)
            OR levetiracetam 40–60 mg/kg IV
            OR phenobarbitone 20 mg/kg IV if no phenytoin
30–45 min: RSI (rapid sequence induction) + intubation; thiopentone or propofol
            Admit to ICU
```

**Nurse**: time the seizure; maintain airway; give O₂; draw blood (glucose, electrolytes, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, blood culture); prepare and administer anticonvulsants as prescribed; monitor vital signs, SpO₂, ECG.

## 8.5 Sepsis and septic shock in children

**Paediatric sepsis**: life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection.

**Recognition**: fever + at least one of: altered mental status, tachycardia (or bradycardia in infants), tachypnoea, hypotension (late sign in children — shock is advanced when BP falls), prolonged CRT > 2 s, mottled/cold skin.

**Septic shock**: sepsis with cardiovascular dysfunction requiring vasoactive drugs.

**Management (PALS/WHO Pocket Book)**:

1. Obtain IV/IO access; **blood culture before antibiotics** (do not delay antibiotics for culture if access is difficult).
2. **Fluid resuscitation**: 10–20 mL/kg 0.9% NaCl or Ringer's lactate IV over 10–20 minutes; reassess after each bolus; maximum 40–60 mL/kg in first hour (for resource-limited settings 20 mL/kg cautiously, reassess).
3. **Broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour**: ceftriaxone 100 mg/kg/day IV OR cefotaxime + vancomycin if hospital-acquired.
4. **Source control**: remove infected IV lines, drain collections.
5. **Vasopressors** if unresponsive to fluid: noradrenaline (dopamine is an alternative); initiate via IO or central line.
6. Monitor urine output (target 1 mL/kg/hour in a child).

## 8.6 Anaphylaxis

**Trigger**: food (peanut, milk, egg, fish), insect sting, drug (penicillin, latex).

**Features**: sudden onset after exposure; urticaria, angioedema, bronchospasm (wheeze), stridor (laryngeal oedema), hypotension, collapse.

**Management**:

1. **Adrenaline (epinephrine) 1:1000 IM** into anterolateral thigh — **first line and must not be delayed**:
   - Dose: **0.01 mg/kg IM** (maximum 0.5 mg for child; 0.3 mg child auto-injector, 0.5 mg adult auto-injector).
   - May repeat every 5–15 minutes if no improvement.
2. Supplemental O₂.
3. IV fluid bolus (20 mL/kg) for hypotension.
4. Salbutamol nebulisation for bronchospasm.
5. Chlorpheniramine (anti-H1) and ranitidine (anti-H2) IM or IV — adjuncts only, not first line.
6. Hydrocortisone IV — prevents late-phase reaction; not first line.
7. Observe for 4–6 hours (or 12–24 hours in severe reaction) for biphasic reaction.
8. **On discharge**: adrenaline auto-injector (EpiPen junior 0.15 mg for < 25 kg; EpiPen 0.3 mg for ≥ 25 kg); refer to allergist; MedicAlert bracelet; written anaphylaxis action plan.

## 8.7 Hypoglycaemia

**Definition in a neonate**: blood glucose < 2.6 mmol/L (< 47 mg/dL).  
**Definition in a child**: blood glucose < 3.0 mmol/L (< 54 mg/dL) with symptoms.

**Causes in children**: prolonged fasting, insulin excess (diabetes, insulinoma), Addison's disease, GHD, severe illness (sepsis, liver failure).

**Features**: sweating, tremor, pallor, palpitations, hunger; confusion, seizures, coma if severe.

**Treatment**:
- Conscious: 15 g oral fast-acting carbohydrate (3 glucose tablets, 150 mL orange juice); recheck in 15 minutes; repeat if still low; then eat a snack.
- Unconscious / IV access: **2 mL/kg of 10% dextrose** IV bolus over 5 minutes; check glucose in 15 minutes; infuse 10% dextrose at GIR 6–8 mg/kg/min to maintain.
- If no IV access: glucagon 0.5 mg (< 25 kg) or 1 mg (≥ 25 kg) SC/IM.

## 8.8 Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)

**Features**: vomiting, abdominal pain, Kussmaul breathing (deep, sighing), sweet/acetone breath, dehydration, altered consciousness; blood glucose typically > 11 mmol/L; blood ketones positive; arterial pH < 7.3; bicarbonate < 15 mmol/L.

**Management** (specialist protocol):

```text
1. Assessment: weigh, vital signs, blood gas, glucose, electrolytes, urinalysis
2. IV fluids: Ringer's lactate or 0.9% NaCl; correct dehydration slowly over 48 hours
   (rapid rehydration risks CEREBRAL OEDEMA — the main cause of DKA death in children)
3. Insulin infusion: 0.05–0.1 unit/kg/hour (do NOT give bolus insulin)
   Start insulin 1–2 h after fluids begin
4. Potassium: replace once urine output confirmed (even if K⁺ normal, it will fall)
5. Monitor: blood glucose hourly; electrolytes 2-hourly; neurological status (headache,
   change in consciousness = early cerebral oedema → mannitol and reduce IV rate)
6. Convert to SC insulin when eating and blood glucose < 12 mmol/L
```

**Cerebral oedema**: the most feared complication; occurs in ~1% of paediatric DKA; treat with IV mannitol 0.25–1 g/kg or hypertonic saline 3% (3 mL/kg).

## 8.9 Near-drowning / submersion

- Remove the child from the water.
- Begin PBLS at the scene (5 rescue breaths, then 15:2 compressions:breaths).
- Do not stop resuscitation because of hypothermia — "not dead until warm and dead" (a cold child may survive extended submersion).
- In hospital: active rewarming (warmed IV fluids, warm blankets, ventilator with warm humidified O₂); treat hypoxia, correct hypothermia and electrolyte disturbances; monitor for ARDS (acute respiratory distress syndrome) and brain injury.

## 8.10 Dehydration with shock — Plan C

**Severe dehydration with shock** (pale, cold peripheries, CRT > 3 s, weak rapid pulse, reduced consciousness):

**Ringer's lactate IV bolus**:
- **Infants (< 12 months)**: 30 mL/kg over 1 hour; then 70 mL/kg over 5 hours.
- **Children (1–5 years)**: 30 mL/kg over 30 minutes; then 70 mL/kg over 2.5 hours.

Reassess after each bolus; if improving, continue; if not, repeat bolus up to 3 times then refer to ICU.

## 8.11 Transport of the sick child

Before transfer:
- Stabilise first: secure airway, give O₂, establish IV access and start fluids, give first-dose antibiotics/anticonvulsants as indicated.
- Complete the IMNCI referral note.
- Communicate with the receiving team (by phone) and confirm bed availability.
- Brief the accompanying caretaker (parent) on the reason for transfer.
- Keep the child warm; secure IV lines and NG tubes.
- Bring emergency drugs drawn up: adrenaline, glucose, diazepam.

## Remember

- PAT: Appearance + Work of Breathing + Circulation — bedside assessment in 30 seconds.
- PBLS vs adult BLS: 5 initial rescue breaths (not 2); 15:2 two-rescuer (not 30:2); 2-thumb encircling for infant; AED from 1 year.
- Febrile seizure: recovery position; do NOT restrain; diazepam PR or midazolam buccal if > 5 minutes.
- Status epilepticus: lorazepam IV → phenytoin → intubation if no response.
- Septic shock: blood culture → antibiotics within 1 hour → 20 mL/kg IV fluid bolus; vasopressors if unresponsive.
- Anaphylaxis: adrenaline 0.01 mg/kg IM anterolateral thigh — first and immediately; discharge with auto-injector.
- Hypoglycaemia: 15 g glucose orally (conscious) or 2 mL/kg 10% dextrose IV (unconscious).
- DKA: fluids over 48 h (not fast) + insulin infusion 0.1 U/kg/h + potassium; watch for cerebral oedema (headache, confusion).
- Drowning: begin PBLS at scene; rewarm in hospital; do not stop CPR until warm.
- Plan C (severe dehydration + shock): Ringer's lactate 100 mL/kg (30+70 split) over 3–6 hours.

# Unit 4: The healthy child — growth, development, nutrition and play

This unit covers INC Unit IV: normal growth and development from birth to adolescence, developmental screening, nutrition, common nutritional deficiencies, and the role of play. Growth and development milestones are examined very frequently — learn the table by heart.

---

## 4.1 Growth

**Growth** is an increase in physical size (weight, height, head circumference). It is the best single indicator of a child's health and nutrition.

### Weight

| Age | Expected weight |
| --- | --- |
| Birth | ~3 kg (2.5–3.5 kg) |
| 5 months | Doubles birth weight (~6 kg) |
| 1 year | Trebles birth weight (~9 kg) |
| 2 years | Quadruples birth weight (~12 kg) |
| 2–10 years | Gains ~2–3 kg per year |
| Puberty | Growth spurt (girls 10–14 years; boys 12–16 years) |

**Rule**: birth weight (kg) = age in months ÷ 2 + 4 (approximate, up to about 6 months).

### Length / Height

- Birth: ~50 cm
- 1 year: ~75 cm (adds ~25 cm in first year)
- 2 years: ~87 cm (adds ~12.5 cm in second year)
- 2–12 years: adds ~5–6 cm per year
- Puberty: adds 6–10 cm per year during the growth spurt

### Head circumference

- Birth: ~33–35 cm
- Months 1–3: adds ~2 cm per month
- Months 4–6: adds ~1 cm per month
- 1 year: ~47 cm
- 2 years: ~49–50 cm
- **Macrocephaly** (>+2 SD): hydrocephalus.
- **Microcephaly** (<−2 SD): congenital infection (TORCH), genetic syndromes.

### Fontanelles

- **Anterior fontanelle**: diamond-shaped, at junction of frontal and parietal bones. Closes **12–18 months**. Bulging fontanelle = raised intracranial pressure; sunken = dehydration.
- **Posterior fontanelle**: triangular, closes by **6–8 weeks**.

### Teeth

- 20 primary (deciduous) teeth: first tooth erupts at ~6 months (lower central incisors); all 20 by ~2.5 years.
- Permanent teeth begin at ~6 years.

### WHO child growth charts

WHO Child Growth Standards (released 2006) represent ideal growth under optimal conditions. Use Z-scores (standard deviations from the median):

| Indicator | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Weight-for-age | Underweight (< −2 Z) |
| Length/height-for-age | Stunting (< −2 Z); reflects chronic malnutrition |
| Weight-for-length/height | Wasting (< −2 Z); reflects acute malnutrition |
| BMI-for-age | Overweight (> +2 Z) |
| MUAC < 11.5 cm | Severe acute malnutrition (SAM) |

## 4.2 Developmental domains and milestones

Childhood development is assessed across four domains:

1. **Gross motor** — large muscle movements (rolling, sitting, walking, running)
2. **Fine motor/adaptive** — small muscle movements, hand–eye coordination (reaching, pincer grip, drawing)
3. **Language/speech** — receptive (understanding) and expressive (speaking)
4. **Personal-social** — self-care, social interaction (smiling, feeding, dressing)

### Developmental milestones (birth to 5 years)

| Age | Gross motor | Fine motor | Language | Personal-social |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 month** | Lifts chin prone | Hands fisted | Startles to sound | Regards face |
| **3 months** | Holds head steady; raises chest on arms | Opens hands; follows object 180° | Coos, vocalises | Social smile (key milestone) |
| **6 months** | Sits with support; rolls over | Palmar grasp; transfers objects hand to hand | Babbles; turns to voice | Recognises mother; stranger anxiety beginning |
| **9 months** | Sits without support; creeps/crawls | Inferior pincer grip | "Mama/dada" (non-specific) | Waves bye-bye; plays peek-a-boo |
| **12 months** | Stands alone; first steps | Mature pincer grip (finger + thumb) | 1–2 meaningful words | Stranger anxiety peak; cooperates with dressing |
| **18 months** | Walks well; runs stiffly | Stacks 2–3 cubes; scribbles | 10–20 words; uses cup | Feeds self with spoon; points to wants |
| **2 years** | Runs; walks up/down stairs with both feet per step | Stacks 6 cubes; copies vertical line | 2-word phrases; 50+ words | Parallel play; uses spoon and fork |
| **3 years** | Rides tricycle; stands on one foot for 3 s | Stacks 9–10 cubes; copies circle | 3-word sentences; 300 words; asks "why?" | Toilet trained day; dresses with help |
| **4 years** | Hops on one foot; climbs | Copies cross; holds pencil correctly | Full sentences; tells stories | Cooperative play; buttons; brushes teeth |
| **5 years** | Skips; jumps over rope | Copies triangle; draws person with 6 parts | Counts 10 objects; knows colours | Dresses independently; group play |

**Key milestones to memorise**:
- **Social smile**: 6–8 weeks (2 months)
- **Head control**: 3 months
- **Sitting without support**: 6–8 months
- **Pincer grip**: 9–12 months
- **Walking alone**: 12–15 months
- **Meaningful words**: 12 months
- **2-word phrases**: 2 years
- **Toilet training**: 2–3 years

## 4.3 Developmental red flags

Refer immediately if:
- Not smiling by 3 months
- Not sitting alone by 9 months
- Not walking by 18 months
- No speech by 18 months (not even babble)
- Loss of a previously acquired skill (**regression**) at any age — always abnormal and needs urgent investigation
- Not pointing to objects by 12 months (may indicate autism)
- M-CHAT-R (Modified Checklist for Autism in Toddlers) positive at 16–30 months

## 4.4 Developmental screening tools

| Tool | Age range | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Denver Developmental Screening Test II (DDST-II/DASII)** | 0–6 years | Screens gross motor, fine motor-adaptive, language, personal-social domains; 125 items |
| **Bayley Scales of Infant Development** | 1–42 months | Detailed assessment of motor, cognitive, language |
| **M-CHAT-R** | 16–30 months | Screening for autism; parent-completed questionnaire |
| **RBSK screening tool** | 0–18 years | Screens for 4 Ds at anganwadi and school level |

## 4.5 Developmental theories

| Theorist | Theory | Stages relevant to nursing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Erikson** (psychosocial) | Each stage has a central conflict; resolution builds personality | Infancy: Trust vs Mistrust; Toddler: Autonomy vs Shame/Doubt; Preschool: Initiative vs Guilt; School-age: Industry vs Inferiority; Adolescence: Identity vs Role Confusion |
| **Piaget** (cognitive) | Children build knowledge through interaction with the environment | Sensorimotor (0–2 y): learns through senses; Preoperational (2–7 y): magical thinking, egocentrism; Concrete Operational (7–11 y): logic about concrete objects; Formal Operational (11+ y): abstract thinking |
| **Freud** (psychosexual) | Personality forms through stages of sexual energy | Oral (infant), Anal (toddler), Phallic (3–6 y), Latency (6–12 y), Genital (12+) — less used clinically but useful context |

**Nursing application**: a toddler (Erikson: Autonomy) must be given limited choices during care ("Do you want your medicine now or after washing your face?") to maintain a sense of control.

## 4.6 Infant and young child nutrition

### Breastfeeding (0–6 months)

**Exclusive breastfeeding** means only breast milk — no water, no other milk, no food — for the first 6 months.

**Composition of breast milk**:
- **Foremilk** (first part of feed): watery, quenches thirst, lower fat.
- **Hindmilk** (last part): higher fat content; ensures caloric adequacy.
- **Colostrum** (first 3–5 days): thick, yellow, rich in IgA, lactoferrin, lymphocytes, macrophages; acts as the newborn's first immunisation.

**Immunological factors**: Secretory IgA (protects mucosal surfaces against pathogens), lactoferrin (bacteriostatic; binds iron away from bacteria), lysozyme, oligosaccharides (prebiotics), cytokines, macrophages. No formula replicates these.

**Signs of effective breastfeeding**: 8–12 feeds per day; at least 6–8 wet nappies per day; steady weight gain; baby appears satisfied; mother hears swallowing.

**Common breastfeeding problems**: engorgement (frequent feeding, warm compress), sore nipples (correct latch), insufficient milk (increase frequency, correct positioning), inverted nipple (breast shell).

### Complementary feeding (6–12 months)

- Introduce at 6 months (not before 4 months, not after 6 months).
- Start with soft pureed single-ingredient foods; gradually increase texture and variety.
- Introduce **iron-rich foods early** (fortified cereals, mashed lentils/dal, pureed meat).
- Introduce common allergens (egg, peanut) early (by 6–9 months) — delayed introduction increases allergy risk.
- **Avoid**: honey (risk of infant botulism), salt and sugar, whole nuts (choking), cow's milk as main drink before 1 year, low-fat dairy.

### Nutrition aged 1–5 years

- Family meals; a balanced diet with all food groups.
- Portion sizes are small — a child's portion ≈ one tablespoon per year of age.
- **5-a-day**: encourage five portions of fruit and vegetables daily.
- Iron-rich foods remain important; cow's milk should be limited to 350 mL/day (excessive milk displaces iron-rich foods).
- Calcium and vitamin D for bone growth; sunlight exposure.

## 4.7 Common nutritional deficiencies in India

### Iron-deficiency anaemia (IDA)

- Most common nutritional deficiency in Indian children.
- **Diagnosis**: Hb < 11 g/dL in children under 5 years; microcytic hypochromic red cells on smear; low serum ferritin.
- **Features**: pallor, fatigue, poor concentration, pica, irritability.
- **Treatment**: elemental iron **3–6 mg/kg/day** as ferrous sulfate or fumarate, divided 2–3 times daily, for **3 months** (minimum 6–8 weeks after Hb normalises to replenish stores); give between meals with vitamin C-rich juice (improves absorption); darkens stools — reassure parents.
- **Prevention**: iron-fortified foods, iron supplementation under NHM for children 6 months–5 years, deworming 2-yearly from 1 year.

### Vitamin A deficiency (VAD)

- **Causes**: poor dietary intake of beta-carotene (green leafy vegetables, yellow/orange fruits) or preformed vitamin A (liver, egg, dairy); aggravated by recurrent diarrhoea.
- **Features (xerophthalmia)**:
  - Night blindness (nyctalopia) — earliest sign
  - Bitot's spots (foamy white deposits on temporal conjunctiva)
  - Corneal xerosis → corneal ulceration → keratomalacia (liquefaction of the cornea) → blindness
- **Prevention/treatment**: mega-dose vitamin A supplementation on NIS — 1 lakh IU at 9 months, then 2 lakh IU every 6 months up to 5 years.
- Vitamin A is also given in acute measles and severe malnutrition.

### Iodine deficiency disorders (IDD)

- **Causes**: iodine-poor soil and water in hilly and flood-prone regions.
- **Effects**: in the foetus/neonate (cretinism — severe intellectual disability, deaf-mutism, hypothyroidism if the mother was iodine-deficient); in children and adults (goitre, hypothyroidism, impaired cognition).
- **Prevention**: universal iodisation of salt (India mandates ≥15 ppm iodine at consumer level); nurse's role: educate families to use iodised salt and store it in a covered container away from heat and light.

### Vitamin D deficiency rickets

- **Cause**: inadequate sunlight exposure, low dietary vitamin D, exclusive breastfeeding without D supplementation.
- **Features**: bow legs (genu varum), Harrison's sulcus (groove along lower rib margins), frontal bossing, craniotabes (softening of skull bones), delayed fontanelle closure, painful wrists with widened epiphyses (rachitic rosary), raised alkaline phosphatase, low serum calcium/phosphorus.
- **Treatment**: vitamin D **1,500–5,000 IU/day** orally for 3 months + calcium; then maintenance 400–1000 IU/day.
- **Prevention**: daily vitamin D 400 IU from birth for all breastfed infants; sunlight exposure.

## 4.8 Play

**Play is the primary occupation of childhood.** It promotes physical, cognitive, language, social-emotional, and creative development.

### Types of play (Parten's social categories)

| Type | Age | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Solitary play** | Infant–toddler | Plays alone with no interest in nearby children |
| **Parallel play** | Toddler (2–3 y) | Plays alongside other children with similar toys but no interaction — normal at this age |
| **Associative play** | Preschool (3–4 y) | Plays with others; shares materials but no organised goal |
| **Cooperative play** | School-age (4–6+ y) | Organised play with rules and a common goal |

### Age-appropriate toys

| Age | Suitable toys |
| --- | --- |
| Infant | Mobile over cot, rattles, soft toys, teething rings, face mirrors |
| Toddler | Stacking cups/rings, ride-on toys, push-pull toys, simple puzzles (3–4 pieces) |
| Preschool | Finger paints, play dough, role-play (dress-up, kitchen sets), larger puzzles |
| School-age | Board games, construction kits (Lego), team sports, craft materials, books |
| Adolescent | Team sports, music, art, crafts, age-appropriate video games |

**Safety**: no sharp edges; no small parts for < 3 years (choking risk); check age labels; supervise water and outdoor play.

## Remember

- Weight: doubles at 5 months, trebles at 1 year, quadruples at 2 years.
- Head circumference at 1 year ≈ 47 cm; anterior fontanelle closes 12–18 months.
- Key milestones: social smile 2 months; sits alone 6–8 months; walks alone 12–15 months; 2-word phrases 2 years.
- Red flag: **regression** (loss of a skill) is always abnormal.
- Exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months; complementary feeding from 6 months.
- IDA: Hb < 11 g/dL under 5 years; treatment elemental iron 3–6 mg/kg/day × 3 months.
- Vitamin A: night blindness → Bitot's spots → keratomalacia; NIS mega-dose at 9 months.
- Iodine deficiency → cretinism (maternal), goitre (child); prevent with iodised salt.
- Rickets: bow legs, Harrison's sulcus, raised ALP; treat with vitamin D + calcium.
- Parallel play is normal at 2–3 years; cooperative play from 4–5 years.

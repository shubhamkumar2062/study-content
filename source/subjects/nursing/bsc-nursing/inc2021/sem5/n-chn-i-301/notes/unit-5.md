# Unit 5: Nursing care of the neonate — normal and high-risk

This unit covers INC Unit V: care of the normal newborn, assessment at birth, common conditions in the newborn period, and the special care needs of preterm, low-birth-weight, and other high-risk neonates. The Apgar score and neonatal jaundice management are very commonly examined.

---

## 5.1 The normal newborn — immediate care at birth

### Immediate steps (first 30 seconds)

1. **Warm and dry**: place the baby under a radiant warmer or on the mother's chest; dry thoroughly and remove wet linen (evaporation is the main cause of heat loss in the delivery room).
2. **Stimulate**: gentle drying and rubbing of the back.
3. **Airway**: if the baby is not breathing, suction mouth then nose with a bulb syringe or suction catheter (do not suction routinely).
4. **Assess**: breathing, heart rate, colour.
5. **Delayed cord clamping**: clamp and cut the umbilical cord at 1–3 minutes after birth (unless emergency requires earlier clamping); allows up to 80 mL extra blood to transfuse to the baby, reducing iron-deficiency anaemia risk.
6. **Skin-to-skin (Kangaroo Mother Care — KMC)**: place the naked baby prone on the mother's bare chest immediately; maintains temperature, promotes breastfeeding, stabilises heart rate and breathing.
7. **Initiate breastfeeding within 1 hour** of birth.

### Prophylaxis

- **Vitamin K 1 mg IM** into the anterolateral thigh — prevents haemorrhagic disease of the newborn (HDN; now called Vitamin K Deficiency Bleeding, VKDB).
- **Hepatitis B vaccine** (birth dose) within 24 hours.
- **BCG** and **OPV 0** at or soon after birth.
- **Eye prophylaxis**: 1% tetracycline or 0.5% erythromycin ointment into both eyes prevents gonococcal ophthalmia neonatorum.

## 5.2 Apgar score

Assessed at **1 minute** and **5 minutes** after birth. Each of 5 signs is scored 0, 1, or 2.

| Sign | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A**ppearance (colour) | Blue/pale all over | Blue extremities, pink body | Completely pink |
| **P**ulse (heart rate) | Absent | < 100 /min | ≥ 100 /min |
| **G**rimace (reflex irritability) | No response to stimulation | Grimace | Cry, cough, or sneeze |
| **A**ctivity (muscle tone) | Limp | Some flexion | Active motion |
| **R**espiration | Absent | Weak/irregular | Strong cry |

**Interpretation**:
- **7–10**: normal; routine care.
- **4–6**: moderate depression; stimulate, give supplemental O₂, may need brief PPV.
- **0–3**: severe depression; begin neonatal resuscitation immediately.

The 5-minute Apgar is more prognostically important. A score < 7 at 5 minutes → rescore at 10, 15, and 20 minutes.

## 5.3 Normal newborn assessment

**Vital signs**: Temperature 36.5–37.5°C, HR 120–160/min, RR 40–60/min.

**Weight**: 2.5–4.0 kg; physiological weight loss of 7–10% in first 3–4 days (then regains birth weight by 10–14 days); gain ~20–30 g/day thereafter.

**Systematic examination**: head (fontanelles, caput succedaneum vs cephalhaematoma), eyes (red reflex, discharge), ears (shape, position), mouth (cleft, Epstein pearls), neck (clavicle fracture), chest (breast engorgement — normal), abdomen (cord, organomegaly), genitalia (undescended testes in males, ambiguous genitalia), anus (patency), limbs (talipes, polydactyly, single palmar crease — trisomy 21), spine (neural tube defects), skin (milia, erythema toxicum, Mongolian blue spot — normal).

**Gestational age assessment (Ballard Score)**: combines neuromuscular maturity (posture, square window, arm recoil, popliteal angle, scarf sign, heel-to-ear) and physical maturity (skin, lanugo, plantar creases, breast, eye/ear, genitalia). Score correlates with gestational age in weeks (20–44 weeks).

## 5.4 Common conditions in the newborn

### Neonatal jaundice

**Physiological jaundice** (normal):
- Appears **after 24 hours** of life.
- Peaks at day 3–5.
- Disappears by day 10–14 in term infants (can extend to 3 weeks in breastfed infants).
- Due to: high RBC turnover, immature liver, enterohepatic recirculation.
- Assess by **Kramer's zones** (progressive cephalocaudal spread of jaundice):

```text
Zone 1: face/neck     — Bilirubin ~5 mg/dL
Zone 2: trunk         — ~10 mg/dL
Zone 3: below navel   — ~12 mg/dL
Zone 4: arms/legs     — ~15 mg/dL
Zone 5: palms/soles   — >15 mg/dL
```

Confirm with serum total bilirubin (STB) measurement; use the Bhutani nomogram for gestational age-specific thresholds.

**Pathological jaundice** — suspect if:
- Appears **within 24 hours** of birth.
- Rising bilirubin > 0.5 mg/dL/hour.
- STB exceeds the phototherapy threshold for the baby's gestation and age in hours.
- **Direct (conjugated) bilirubin** > 2 mg/dL.
- Persists beyond 2 weeks (term) or 3 weeks (preterm).

**Causes of pathological jaundice**: Rh incompatibility (anti-D), ABO incompatibility (anti-A, anti-B), G6PD deficiency, sepsis, congenital hypothyroidism, biliary atresia (conjugated).

### Phototherapy

**Indications**: STB at or approaching the phototherapy threshold for gestation and age (use hospital nomogram/NNF/WHO guidelines).

**Mechanism**: blue-green light (420–490 nm) converts bilirubin in the skin to water-soluble photoisomers excreted in bile and urine without conjugation.

**Nursing care during phototherapy**:
- Place baby under phototherapy lights at the correct distance (typically 25–30 cm for conventional fluorescent; manufacturer-specified for LED/blanket types).
- **Cover eyes** with phototherapy eye mask (patches) — prevents retinal damage; check every 2 hours; remove briefly for feeds.
- **Keep genitalia covered** in male infants (theoretical gonadal risk).
- Keep baby unclothed (maximum skin exposure) except for the nappy.
- Increase fluid intake (additional 10–20% of feeds) — phototherapy increases insensible fluid loss.
- Monitor temperature: phototherapy lamps generate heat — check temperature 4-hourly.
- Document light position, eye patch, feeds, urine output, bilirubin levels.
- Rebound bilirubin check 12–24 hours after stopping phototherapy.

**Exchange transfusion** (double volume): indicated when STB exceeds the exchange transfusion threshold or if there is acute bilirubin encephalopathy (poor feeding, high-pitched cry, opisthotonus, seizures). Replaces sensitised/haemolysed red cells and removes bilirubin.

### Neonatal sepsis

- **Early-onset** (< 72 hours): usually maternal organisms (GBS, E. coli, Listeria); risk factors: prolonged rupture of membranes > 18 h, maternal fever, chorioamnionitis, GBS colonisation.
- **Late-onset** (> 72 hours): hospital-acquired organisms (Klebsiella, Staph. aureus, Pseudomonas) or community organisms; risk factors: prematurity, IV lines, TPN.
- **Features**: temperature instability (hypothermia more common than fever in neonates), poor feeding/sucking, lethargy, apnoea, respiratory distress, bulging fontanelle (meningitis), jaundice, abdominal distension.
- **Management**: blood culture before antibiotics, then IV ampicillin + gentamicin (early onset) or IV cefotaxime + vancomycin if hospital-acquired; supportive care.

### Other common newborn conditions

- **Neonatal conjunctivitis (ophthalmia neonatorum)**: mucopurulent discharge within 28 days; gonococcal (1–3 days) is an emergency; chlamydial (5–14 days). Culture and treat; prophylaxis with antibiotic eye ointment.
- **Oral thrush (candidiasis)**: white plaques on buccal mucosa that cannot be wiped off; treat with oral nystatin suspension 100,000 units after feeds, 4 times daily.
- **Umbilical cord care**: keep dry; wipe with clean water; sign of infection — redness, discharge, smell → umbilical sepsis → IV antibiotics; omphalitis can extend to portal vein thrombosis.

## 5.5 High-risk neonate — preterm infant

**Preterm**: < 37 completed weeks gestational age.

### Respiratory distress syndrome (RDS / Hyaline Membrane Disease)

- **Cause**: surfactant deficiency (surfactant is produced from ~24 weeks; adequate amounts by 35–36 weeks).
- **Features**: tachypnoea, grunting, nasal flaring, subcostal/sternal retractions, cyanosis — worsening in the first 24–48 hours; CXR shows "ground glass" opacity with air bronchograms.
- **Management**:
  - Antenatal corticosteroids (betamethasone to mother at 24–34 weeks) accelerate surfactant production.
  - After birth: **surfactant therapy** (intratracheal) reduces severity and mortality; CPAP to maintain alveolar patency; mechanical ventilation if severe.
  - Nurse: monitor SpO₂ and blood gases, titrate O₂ carefully (avoid hyperoxia → retinopathy), maintain temperature, provide expressed breast milk feeds by NG.

### Other major prematurity complications

- **Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP)**: abnormal retinal vascularisation due to oxygen toxicity; screen all infants < 32 weeks or < 1500 g at 4–6 weeks of age; treat severe ROP with laser photocoagulation or anti-VEGF injection.
- **Intraventricular haemorrhage (IVH)**: bleeding into the germinal matrix → ventricles; most common in < 32 weeks; graded I–IV; Grade III–IV → hydrocephalus, neurodevelopmental delay.
- **Necrotising enterocolitis (NEC)**: ischaemic/infectious bowel necrosis; features: abdominal distension, bloody stools, feed intolerance, air in bowel wall (pneumatosis intestinalis on X-ray); treatment: bowel rest (nil by mouth), IV antibiotics, surgical resection if perforation.
- **Apnoea of prematurity**: cessation of breathing > 20 seconds with bradycardia and desaturation; treat with caffeine citrate (stimulates respiratory centre).

### Incubator (isolette) care for preterm infants

- Closed incubator maintains temperature (thermoneutral environment 32–36°C depending on weight/gestation), humidity (60–80%), and reduces infection.
- Minimal handling to avoid desaturation episodes; developmental positioning (facilitated tucking, nesting rolls).
- Expressed breast milk through NG tube; advance feeds slowly to reduce NEC risk.

## 5.6 Low-birth-weight, IUGR and post-term neonates

| Category | Definition | Key concerns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LBW | Birth weight < 2500 g | Hypothermia, hypoglycaemia, infection, poor feeding |
| VLBW | Birth weight < 1500 g | All the above plus RDS, IVH, NEC, ROP |
| IUGR — symmetric | Both weight and length reduced; insult from early pregnancy (TORCH infections, chromosomal) | Neurodevelopmental delay, difficulty with catch-up growth |
| IUGR — asymmetric | Weight reduced, length normal; insult in late pregnancy (placental insufficiency, hypertension) | Polycythaemia, hypoglycaemia, fetal distress in labour |
| Post-term | > 42 weeks | Meconium aspiration syndrome, hypoglycaemia, polycythaemia |

**Kangaroo Mother Care (KMC)** for LBW: skin-to-skin contact between mother and baby 24 hours a day; replaces incubator in stable LBW infants > 1500 g; reduces hypothermia, infection, mortality, promotes breastfeeding.

## 5.7 Birth asphyxia — neonatal resuscitation (NRP)

Approximately 10% of newborns need some assistance at birth; 1% need extensive resuscitation.

```text
NRP Algorithm:
   Baby born → Assess (term? breathing? good tone?)
      YES to all → Routine care (warmth, airway, dry, skin-to-skin)
      NO to any →
         → Warm, dry, stimulate, suction if secretions visible
         → Assess breathing and HR at 30 s
         If HR < 100 or apnoeic → PPV with 21% O₂ (room air)
         → Assess at 60 s
         If HR < 100 → Check technique, consider O₂ increase
         If HR < 60 → Start chest compressions (3:1 with PPV)
         If HR < 60 persists → Intubate, IV/IO adrenaline 0.01 mg/kg
```

**Key points**:
- Start PPV with 21% O₂ (room air); increase only if SpO₂ not improving.
- Compressions: 2-thumb encircling technique; depth 1/3 AP diameter; rate 90 compressions + 30 breaths per minute.
- Adrenaline: 0.01–0.03 mg/kg IV/IO; 0.05–0.1 mg/kg via ETT (less effective).

## 5.8 Neonatal hypoglycaemia and hypothermia

### Hypoglycaemia

- **Definition**: blood glucose < 2.6 mmol/L (< 47 mg/dL) in a neonate.
- **At-risk groups**: LBW, preterm, IDM (infant of diabetic mother — macrosomic), IUGR, stressed infants.
- **Features**: jitteriness, poor feeding, hypotonia, apnoea, seizures; often asymptomatic.
- **Treatment**: if symptomatic or glucose persistently < 2.6 mmol/L — 2 mL/kg of 10% dextrose IV as a bolus over 5 minutes; then infuse GIR 6–8 mg/kg/min; recheck glucose 30 minutes later.
- **Prevention**: early and frequent feeds; monitor blood glucose in at-risk infants at 1, 3, and 6 hours.

### Hypothermia

- **Normal neonatal temperature**: 36.5–37.5°C.
- **Cold stress (mild hypothermia)**: 36.0–36.5°C — increases oxygen consumption and glucose use; increases apnoea.
- **Hypothermia** < 36°C — increases mortality, especially in LBW.
- **Causes**: cold delivery room, delayed drying and dressing, bathing too soon.
- **Chain of warmth**: warm delivery room (> 25°C), immediate drying, skin-to-skin or radiant warmer, delayed first bath (after 24 hours or once temperature stable), warm transport.
- **Thermoneutral environment**: in an incubator, the temperature at which the baby requires minimal oxygen to maintain a normal core temperature.

## 5.9 Congenital abnormalities

| Condition | Key nursing points |
| --- | --- |
| **CHD — cyanotic** (Tetralogy of Fallot, TGA) | SpO₂ monitoring; prostaglandin E₁ to maintain PDA for duct-dependent lesions; prepare for surgery |
| **CHD — acyanotic** (VSD, ASD, PDA) | Monitor for heart failure (poor feeding, tachycardia, hepatomegaly); diuretics; surgery/catheter |
| **Cleft lip/palate** | Special feeding (squeezable bottle, Haberman feeder); surgery for cleft lip at ~3 months, palate at ~9–12 months; speech therapy; support parents |
| **Neural tube defects — spina bifida** | Cover the sac with sterile saline-soaked gauze; prevent infection; catheterise if bladder retention; surgical closure within 24–48 hours |
| **Talipes equinovarus (club foot)** | Ponseti method: serial plaster casting from birth changing every 5–7 days for 6–8 weeks; followed by percutaneous Achilles tenotomy; then foot abduction brace (Mitchell brace) for 3 years |

## Remember

- Immediate care at birth: Warm, Dry, Stimulate, Suction (if needed), Delayed cord clamping 1–3 min.
- Vitamin K 1 mg IM at birth prevents HDN.
- Apgar: score each of 5 signs 0–2 at 1 and 5 minutes; 7–10 normal, 4–6 moderate, 0–3 severe.
- Physiological jaundice: appears > 24 hours, peaks day 3–5, clears by day 10–14.
- Pathological jaundice: appears < 24 hours or bilirubin rising rapidly — investigate.
- Phototherapy: eye patches, unclothed, increase fluids, check temperature.
- Neonatal sepsis: temperature instability, poor feeding, lethargy — blood culture before antibiotics.
- Preterm: RDS (surfactant deficiency), ROP (O₂ toxicity), IVH, NEC, apnoea.
- KMC for stable LBW > 1500 g: 24-hour skin-to-skin, reduces mortality.
- Neonatal hypoglycaemia (< 2.6 mmol/L): early feeds; treat symptomatic with 2 mL/kg 10% dextrose IV.
- Cleft lip surgery ~3 months, cleft palate ~9–12 months.
- Club foot (talipes): Ponseti serial casting then Mitchell brace.

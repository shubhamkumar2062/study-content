# Unit 1: Cosmetics — Indian regulatory provisions

A formulation scientist who designs a cream, shampoo or toothpaste in India must know, before the first batch is made, whether the product is a cosmetic or a drug, who licenses it, what must appear on the label and which ingredients are forbidden. This unit covers the law that answers those questions: the **Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940** (the Act) and the **Cosmetics Rules, 2020** (the Rules) made under it. The B.Pharm course (BP809ET) gave an outline; here the provisions are studied rule by rule, as an M.Pharm examiner expects.

> Rule numbers below follow the Cosmetics Rules, 2020 as notified (G.S.R. 763(E), 15 December 2020). The Rules have been amended since (for example in 2025). Always read the current consolidated text before applying them in practice.

## 1.1 The legal framework

```text
Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940  (Parliament)
   │  Chapter III  – import of drugs and cosmetics
   │  Chapter IV   – manufacture, sale and distribution of drugs and cosmetics
   │
   ├── Drugs Rules, 1945            (drugs; earlier also cosmetics)
   └── Cosmetics Rules, 2020        (all cosmetic matters from 15 Dec 2020)
          ├─ standards → Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) Indian Standards (Ninth Schedule)
          ├─ GMP and premises → Seventh Schedule
          └─ permitted colours → Tenth Schedule
```

**History in brief.** Until December 2020, cosmetics were controlled through parts of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945: manufacturing licences were issued in **Form 32**, factory premises followed **Schedule M-II**, and BIS standards were listed in **Schedule S**. The Cosmetics Rules, 2020 moved everything into one separate set of rules with its own "COS" forms. Licences granted under the old rules stayed valid until they expired, and holders had to convert them to the new form.

**Authorities (Rule 4).**

| Authority | Who | Responsible for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Central Licensing Authority (CLA) | Drugs Controller General of India, Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO) | Import of all categories of cosmetics (registration), permission for new cosmetics, coordination with states |
| State Licensing Authority (SLA) | State drugs controller (by whatever name called) | Licences to manufacture for sale or distribution; control of sale, stock and distribution; approval of testing laboratories |
| Inspectors | Appointed by Central or State Government under section 21 of the Act | Inspection of premises, sampling, seizure |
| Government Analyst | Appointed under section 20 of the Act | Testing samples drawn by inspectors |
| Central Cosmetics Laboratory | Notified by the Central Government | Testing and analysis of cosmetics, including import samples and samples sent by courts |

**Standards body.** The Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) writes the product standards (for example for toothpaste, skin creams, shampoos, soaps, hair dyes). Two BIS standards matter for every formulator:

- **IS 4707 (Part 1)** – dyes, colours and pigments permitted in cosmetics.
- **IS 4707 (Part 2)** – raw materials; its **Annex A** lists materials that must not be added to cosmetics, and other parts give restricted ingredients with limits.
- **IS 4011:2018** – methods of test for safety evaluation of cosmetics (named in the Rules for new cosmetics).

## 1.2 Definition of a cosmetic

**Section 3(aaa) of the Act** defines a cosmetic as any article intended to be **rubbed, poured, sprinkled or sprayed on, or introduced into, or otherwise applied to, the human body or any part of it for cleansing, beautifying, promoting attractiveness, or altering the appearance**, and includes any article intended for use as a **component** of a cosmetic.

Points examiners look for:

1. **Four methods of application** (rubbed, poured, sprinkled, sprayed) plus "introduced into" and "otherwise applied" – so oral products such as toothpaste and mouthwash are covered.
2. **Four purposes** – cleansing, beautifying, promoting attractiveness, altering appearance.
3. **Human body only** – a dog shampoo or a room freshener is not a cosmetic under these Rules.
4. **Components** – raw materials sold for making cosmetics are cosmetics in law.
5. **Intended use decides status** – the same chemical may be a drug or a cosmetic depending on purpose and claims. A product that claims to treat, cure, mitigate or prevent disease, or to affect structure or function of the body, falls within the definition of a **drug** (section 3(b)) and needs drug licensing.

**Other definitions in Rule 3 worth learning**

| Term | Meaning (in plain words) |
| --- | --- |
| New cosmetic | Contains a **novel ingredient** not used anywhere in the world, or not recognised for cosmetic use in any national or international literature |
| Loan licence | A licence that lets a person manufacture his cosmetic at **another licensee's manufacturing site** |
| Use before / date of expiry | The date up to which the cosmetic keeps its characteristics as per standards when stored as stated on the label |
| Import registration certificate | Certificate issued by the CLA (Rule 13) registering a cosmetic for import |
| Legal manufacturer / brand owner | The person who authorises another manufacturer (in India or abroad) to make the cosmetic for him |

**Where do cosmeceuticals stand?** "Cosmeceutical" is a marketing and scientific term, **not a legal category** in India. A cosmeceutical with cosmetic claims (smoother-looking skin, more even-looking tone) is a cosmetic; the moment it claims to treat acne, melasma or alopecia it becomes a drug. India has no "quasi-drug" class like Japan, and no "cosmetic–drug" class like the USA. Ayurvedic and herbal products with medicinal claims are handled under the Ayurveda, Siddha and Unani drug provisions of the Act, not the Cosmetics Rules.

## 1.3 Structure of the Cosmetics Rules, 2020

| Chapter | Content | Key rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Short title, definitions | 1–3 |
| II | Authorities, officers and laboratory | 4–11 |
| III | Import and registration | 12–22 |
| IV | Manufacture for sale or distribution | 23–31 |
| V | Permission for import or manufacture of new cosmetic | 32 |
| VI | Labelling, packing and standards | 33–39 |
| VII | Sampling, seizure, test reports, confiscation | 40–54 |
| VIII | Approval of laboratories for testing cosmetics | 55 onwards |

Schedules referred to in this unit: **Second** (documents for import and manufacture applications), **Third** (fees), **Seventh** (GMP, premises, plant and equipment), **Eighth** (batch manufacturing records), **Ninth** (BIS standards), **Tenth** (permitted colours).

## 1.4 Regulatory requirements for labelling (Rules 33–39)

**Rule 33** forbids sale or distribution of any cosmetic of Indian origin unless it is made by a **licensed manufacturer** and **labelled and packed** as the Rules require.

**Rule 34 – manner of labelling.**

| Item | Where | Details |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name of the cosmetic | Inner and outer label | – |
| Name and complete address of the manufacturer (premises where made) | Inner and outer label | If made in a factory not owned by the manufacturer, name and address of the actual manufacturer, or "Made in (country)". For very small packs (30 g or less of solid/semi-solid, 60 mL or less of liquid), the name and principal place of manufacture with PIN code is enough |
| Use before / date of expiry (month and year) | Inner and outer label | Any of the accepted wordings: use before, use by, expiry date, "expiry XX months from manufacture" |
| Batch number | Inner or outer label | Preceded by "B", "Batch No.", "B. No.", "Batch", "Lot No." or "Lot". Not needed for packs of 10 g or less (solid/semi-solid) or 25 mL or less (liquid). Soaps show month and year of manufacture instead |
| Manufacturing licence number | Inner or outer label | Preceded by "M", "M. L. No." or "Mfg. Lic. No." (may be omitted on imported products if the country of origin does not require it) |
| Net contents | Outer label | Weight for solids, fluid measure for liquids, either for semi-solids, plus count if subdivided. Not needed for perfumes/toilet waters of 60 mL or less or solids/semi-solids of 30 g or less |
| Hazard information | Inner label, where a hazard exists | Directions for safe use; warnings and cautions; names and quantities of hazardous or poisonous ingredients |
| Ingredients | Label | Headed "INGREDIENTS"; those above 1% in **descending order** of weight or volume at the time of addition, then those at 1% or less in any order. Not needed for packs of 60 mL or less (liquid) or 30 g or less (solid/semi-solid) |
| Imported cosmetics | Unit pack | Import registration certificate number preceded by "RC", "RC No" or "Reg. Cert. No", with name and address of the importer / registration holder. India-specific labels may be stickered on in the **bonded warehouse** |

Other labelling points:

- If the pack has only **one label**, it must carry everything required on inner and outer labels (Rule 34(6)).
- Labels must also meet any labelling requirement in the relevant **BIS standard** (Rule 34(8)).
- **Export packs** follow the importing country's law but must still show name, batch/lot, use-before date (if any), manufacturer's name and address, and licence number; a code number approved by the SLA may replace the manufacturer's name if the buyer wants (Rule 34(10)).
- **Rule 35** – no one may alter, obliterate or deface the manufacturer's inscriptions on the container, label or wrapper (except with CLA permission).
- **Rule 36** – no cosmetic may make a **false or misleading claim**.
- **Rule 37 – hair dyes** containing p-phenylenediamine or other dyes must carry a **caution** in English and local languages on inner and outer labels: the product may irritate skin, a **preliminary (patch) test** must be done as directed, and it must not be used to dye **eyelashes or eyebrows** because it may cause blindness. The pack must include instructions for the skin test (apply behind the ear or on the inner forearm, leave 24 hours, look for irritation; repeat before every use).
- **Rule 38 – fluoride toothpaste**: fluoride not more than **1000 ppm**, the content in ppm stated on tube and carton, and the expiry date on both.
- **Rule 39(4) – hexachlorophene** in soap (only up to 1%) requires the warning "Contains hexachlorophene – not to be used on babies".

Packaged cosmetics are also subject to the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011 for net quantity, maximum retail price and consumer care details.

## 1.5 Regulatory provisions relating to import (Chapter III of the Act; Rules 12–22)

**Registration is compulsory (Rule 12).** No cosmetic may be imported unless it is registered by the CLA.

```text
Applicant (foreign manufacturer itself, its authorised Indian agent,
           the importer, or its Indian subsidiary)
      │  Form COS-1 on the online portal + documents (Second Schedule, Part I)
      │  + fee per category per manufacturing site (Third/Fourth Schedule)
      ▼
Central Licensing Authority  ── may require testing at an approved laboratory
      │                         (Fifth Schedule products) and inspection of the
      │                         foreign site at the applicant's cost
      ▼
Import registration certificate – Form COS-2   (decision within 6 months;
                                                appeal to Central Government
                                                within 45 days of rejection)
```

Key provisions:

1. **Authorisation** from the foreign manufacturer to the Indian agent must be authenticated (before a first-class magistrate in India, or the competent authority of the exporting country).
2. One application and one certificate may cover **several cosmetics of the same manufacturer** made at one factory (or factories working as one unit) (Rule 13(4)).
3. **Bulk finished formulation** imported for filling in India needs, in addition, a valid Indian manufacturing licence for filling and details of the brand owner.
4. **New cosmetic** – prior permission in **Form COS-3** (Chapter V) is needed before registration.
5. **Validity (Rule 14)** – the certificate is **valid in perpetuity**, provided a **retention fee** is paid before every five-year period ends. Late payment attracts a late fee of 2% of the retention fee per month for up to 180 days; after that the certificate is deemed cancelled.
6. **Changes (Rule 15)** – a change of constitution needs a fresh registration within 180 days; changes in labelling, composition, testing or specifications must be reported within 15 days with an undertaking of compliance with BIS standards; a change of name or address needs prior approval within 60 days.
7. **Suspension/cancellation (Rule 16)** after show-cause; appeal to the Central Government within 30 days.
8. **Already registered products (Rule 17)** – any other person may import a cosmetic already registered, by applying in **Form COS-4**; an **import registration number** is issued in **Form COS-4A**, valid for **three years**.
9. **Declaration to Customs (Rule 19)** – that the consignment complies with Chapter III of the Act.
10. **Port procedure (Rule 20)** – the port officer may sample a consignment; it can be detained until the Central Cosmetics Laboratory reports. If a defect cannot be remedied, the importer must re-export the goods or hand them over for destruction within two months; a representation may be made within 30 days.
11. **Personal use (Rule 21)** – small quantities in a passenger's baggage, for bona fide personal use, may be imported.
12. **Ports of entry (Rule 22)** – only through the points of entry specified in the Drugs Rules, 1945.

**Prohibition of import of certain cosmetics (Rule 18):**

- a cosmetic whose manufacture, sale or distribution is **prohibited in its country of origin**;
- a cosmetic whose **use-before date is six months or less** from the date of import;
- any cosmetic containing **hexachlorophene**;
- any cosmetic **tested on animals after 12 November 2014**.

**Section 10 of the Act** also prohibits import of cosmetics that are **not of standard quality, misbranded, adulterated or spurious**, and cosmetics containing ingredients that may make them unsafe or harmful; **Section 10A** lets the Central Government prohibit the import of any cosmetic in the public interest.

## 1.6 Misbranded, adulterated and spurious cosmetics

These three terms are defined in Chapter IV of the Act (with matching provisions for imports in Chapter III).

| Term (section) | A cosmetic is deemed to be this if … |
| --- | --- |
| **Misbranded (17C)** | it contains a **colour not prescribed**; or it is **not labelled in the prescribed manner**; or its label or container bears a statement that is **false or misleading** in any particular |
| **Spurious (17D)** | it is made or imported under a **name that belongs to another cosmetic**; or it is an **imitation of, or substitute for**, another cosmetic, or resembles it so as to deceive; or its label names a **fictitious or non-existent** manufacturer; or it has been **substituted** wholly or partly by another cosmetic; or it falsely purports to be the product of a manufacturer of whom it is not |
| **Adulterated (17E)** | it contains **filthy, putrid or decomposed** substance; it was prepared, packed or stored under **insanitary conditions** so it may be contaminated or injurious; its **container** is made of a poisonous or harmful substance; it contains a **non-prescribed colour** for colouring; or it contains a **harmful or toxic substance** that may make it injurious to health |

Memory aid: **misbranded = wrong label**, **spurious = wrong identity**, **adulterated = wrong contents or conditions**.

## 1.7 Regulatory provisions relating to manufacture (Chapter IV of the Rules)

### Conditions for obtaining a licence (Rules 23–31)

```text
Applicant ──► Form COS-5 (licence) or COS-6 (loan licence), online
              + fee (Third Schedule) + documents (Second Schedule, Part II)
              + Form COS-7: self-declaration of GMP, premises, plant and
                equipment as per the Seventh Schedule
              + COS-3 permission from CLA if it is a new cosmetic
                    │
                    ▼
State Licensing Authority: scrutiny → licence within 45 days
   Form COS-8 (licence) / Form COS-9 (loan licence)
                    │
                    ▼
Licence uploaded on the CDSCO website → manufacture may begin
                    │
                    ▼
SLA inspects within 30 days to verify COS-7
 (if not inspected in time, the licence is deemed valid; false
  self-declaration → show cause → cancellation, or stop-work order
  until deficiencies are corrected)
```

- **Separate licence for each premises** (Rule 24).
- **Rejection** – reasons and the inspection report are given; a further application with fee within six months may be considered (Rule 28); appeal to the State Government within 90 days (Rule 29).
- **Validity (Rule 30)** – COS-8 and COS-9 are **valid in perpetuity** subject to a **retention fee** before each five-year period ends (same 2% per month late fee up to 180 days, then deemed cancellation).
- **Inspection (Rule 31)** – licensed premises are inspected at least **once in three years**, or as needed on a **risk-based approach**.

### Conditions of the licence (Rule 26)

1. **Competent technical staff** – manufacture under the direction and personal supervision of at least **one whole-time employee** who holds a **Diploma in Pharmacy** approved by PCI, or is a **registered pharmacist**, or has passed the **Intermediate examination with chemistry** (or equivalent), or holds a **bachelor degree in cosmetic technology**.
2. **Premises** complying with the **Seventh Schedule** (GMP, premises, plant and equipment).
3. **Testing** – either the manufacturer's own staff, premises and laboratory equipment for testing raw materials and products, or an arrangement with a laboratory **approved by the CLA and accredited by NABL**. Each batch of raw material and each batch of finished product must be tested; test records kept for **three years** from manufacture.
4. **Batch records** as in the **Eighth Schedule**, kept for **three years after the batch's expiry**.
5. **Inspectors** may enter with or without notice, inspect and take samples (receipt in **Form COS-10**); an **inspection book (Form COS-11)** is kept for the inspector's remarks.
6. **Changes** in labelling, composition, testing, specification or documentation must be reported to the SLA within **30 days** with an undertaking of compliance with BIS standards.
7. **Change in constitution** of the firm – the existing licence remains valid for at most **six months**, by which time a fresh licence is needed; change of name or address needs approval within 60 days.
8. **Soap** makers are exempt from the testing and documentary clauses; their testing procedure is as approved by the SLA.

### Loan licence

A **loan licence** allows a person who has **no manufacturing facility of his own** (often a brand owner) to manufacture his cosmetic at the **licensed premises of another manufacturer**, using that site's plant, staff and testing set-up, but under his own name and responsibility.

| Point | Licence | Loan licence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Application form | COS-5 | COS-6 |
| Licence form | COS-8 | COS-9 |
| Own premises | Required | Uses another licensee's premises |
| Dependence | Independent | A COS-9 is **deemed cancelled or suspended** if the host site's COS-8 is cancelled or suspended |
| Typical user | Factory owner | Brand owner, marketing company, start-up |

Advantages: low investment, fast market entry, use of established GMP facilities. Responsibilities: the loan licensee is answerable for the quality of his product; the host must have capacity and the right product category.

### Permission for new cosmetics (Rule 32)

Anyone intending to import or manufacture a **new cosmetic** applies to the CLA in **Form COS-12** with fees and **data on safety and effectiveness**. If satisfied, the CLA grants prior permission in **Form COS-3**, which is then attached to the import or manufacture application. Safety evaluation tests must follow **IS 4011:2018**.

## 1.8 Prohibition of manufacture and sale of certain cosmetics

**Section 18 of the Act** forbids anyone to manufacture for sale, sell, stock, exhibit or distribute any cosmetic that is **not of standard quality, misbranded, adulterated or spurious**, or that contains any ingredient which may make it unsafe or harmful under ordinary conditions of use, or to manufacture any cosmetic **without a licence** (section 18(c)). **Section 26A** allows the Central Government to prohibit or restrict the manufacture, sale or distribution of a cosmetic that is harmful, in the public interest.

**Rule 39 – standards and specific prohibitions:**

| Provision | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| 39(1) | Must comply with the BIS standard (Ninth Schedule). If no Indian standard exists, it must meet these Rules and the standards of its country of origin |
| 39(2) | Raw materials in **Annex A of IS 4707 (Part 2)** must not be added |
| 39(3) | Only dyes, colours and pigments permitted by IS 4707 (Parts 1/2) and the **Tenth Schedule**. Permitted synthetic and natural organic colours may contain not more than **2 ppm arsenic** (as As2O3), **20 ppm lead** and **100 ppm** of other heavy metals in total |
| 39(4) | **Hexachlorophene** not to be used, except in soaps up to **1% w/w** with the warning label |
| 39(5) | **Mercury**: eye-area products may contain up to **70 ppm (0.007%)** as a preservative; in all other products, unintentional mercury must not exceed **1 ppm** |
| 39(6) | **Lead and arsenic compounds** must not be used for colouring cosmetics |
| 39(7) | **No person shall use any animal for testing of cosmetics** |

India banned animal testing of cosmetics in 2014 and, as noted above, also bans import of cosmetics tested on animals after 12 November 2014. Safety therefore rests on ingredient data, human-safe testing and validated alternative methods (see Unit 4).

## 1.9 Sampling, testing and offences and penalties

**Sampling and testing (Chapter VII).**

- A **purchaser** may get a cosmetic tested by a Government Analyst (application in **Form COS-13**, report in **Form COS-14**) under section 26 of the Act.
- **Inspectors** draw samples (intimation **Form COS-10**), may order stock not to be disposed of (**Form COS-18**), seize stock and records (**Form COS-15**), and send sealed samples to the Government Analyst (memorandum **Form COS-17**); the analyst reports in **Form COS-19** with test protocols.
- Courts may send samples to the Central Cosmetics Laboratory (Forms COS-20 and COS-21).

**Offences and penalties.**

| Offence | Provision | Punishment (outline) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manufacture for sale, sale, stock or exhibition of a **spurious or adulterated** cosmetic | Section 27A(i) | Imprisonment up to **three years** and fine |
| Any other contravention of Chapter IV or the rules for cosmetics (misbranded cosmetic, sale without licence, labelling breaches, etc.) | Section 27A(ii) | Imprisonment up to **one year**, or fine, or both |
| Import in contravention of Chapter III | Section 13 | Imprisonment and/or fine, graded by the type of contravention; the goods are liable to confiscation |
| After conviction | Section 31 and Rules 53–54 | **Confiscation** of the stock; for misbranded (17C) or adulterated (17E) cosmetics, or manufacture without licence (18(c)), also the implements, machinery, packages and vehicles used. The court refers confiscated goods to the inspector; substandard goods are destroyed under supervision |

Repeat offenders face heavier punishment under the Act. Exact fine amounts have been revised by amendments over the years, so quote them only from the current text.

**Other administrative actions:** suspension or cancellation of import registration or licence after a show-cause notice; stop-work orders; recall of batches.

## 1.10 India and the European Union compared

| Point | India | European Union |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Law | D&C Act, 1940 + Cosmetics Rules, 2020 | Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 |
| Pre-market step | Import registration (COS-2) or state manufacturing licence (COS-8/9) | Safety assessment report, a named responsible person, notification on the EU portal |
| Ingredient lists | BIS IS 4707 (prohibited Annex A, permitted colours), Tenth Schedule | Annexes II (prohibited), III (restricted), IV (colourants), V (preservatives), VI (UV filters) |
| Animal testing | Banned (testing, and import of products tested after Nov 2014) | Banned for products and ingredients, with a marketing ban |
| Claims | No false or misleading claims; disease claims make it a drug | Claims must meet common criteria and be substantiated |

## Remember

- Cosmetic: section 3(aaa) – rubbed, poured, sprinkled, sprayed, introduced or applied to the human body for cleansing, beautifying, promoting attractiveness, altering appearance; includes components.
- Cosmetics Rules, 2020: G.S.R. 763(E), 15 December 2020; CLA (CDSCO) for import and new cosmetics, SLA for manufacture and sale.
- Import: COS-1 → COS-2 (perpetual, retention fee every 5 years); new cosmetic COS-12 → COS-3; already registered product COS-4 → COS-4A (3 years).
- Import bans: banned in country of origin, shelf life ≤ 6 months at import, hexachlorophene, tested on animals after 12 Nov 2014.
- Manufacture: COS-5 (licence) / COS-6 (loan licence) + COS-7 self-declaration → COS-8 / COS-9 within 45 days; inspection within 30 days; perpetual with retention fee; inspection at least once in 3 years.
- Technical staff: D.Pharm / registered pharmacist / Intermediate with chemistry / degree in cosmetic technology, whole-time.
- Records: batch records 3 years after expiry; test records 3 years from manufacture.
- Label: name, manufacturer and address, use before, batch (B.), licence (M.), net contents, ingredients > 1% in descending order, RC number for imports; hair dye caution with patch test; fluoride ≤ 1000 ppm.
- 17C misbranded, 17D spurious, 17E adulterated; section 18 prohibitions; 27A penalties (3 years for spurious/adulterated; 1 year or fine for others).
- Rule 39: IS 4707 Annex A banned; colours from Tenth Schedule (As 2 ppm, Pb 20 ppm, heavy metals 100 ppm); hexachlorophene only in soap ≤ 1%; mercury 70 ppm eye products, 1 ppm unintentional; no animal testing.

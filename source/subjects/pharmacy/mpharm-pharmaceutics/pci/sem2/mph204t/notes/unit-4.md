# Unit 4: Design of cosmeceutical products, with safety and efficacy evaluation

A cosmeceutical is designed in three steps: **understand the biology of the problem** (Unit 2), **choose actives that act on that biology at permitted cosmetic levels**, and **build a vehicle that delivers them, keeps them stable and feels pleasant** (Unit 3). This unit applies that method to sun protection and to the problems named in the syllabus – dry skin, acne, pigmentation, prickly heat, wrinkles, body odour, dandruff, dental cavities, bleeding gums, mouth odour and sensitive teeth – and ends with how such products are tested for safety and efficacy.

> Regulatory reminder: in India a product claiming to **treat** acne, melasma, dandruff disease, gum disease or alopecia is a **drug**. Cosmeceutical claims are worded as care and appearance claims (for example "helps reduce the look of blemishes", "helps reduce plaque"). Actives such as hydroquinone, tretinoin, benzoyl peroxide, ketoconazole, minoxidil and clindamycin are regulated as drugs.

## 4.1 General design principles

| Step | Questions to answer |
| --- | --- |
| Target and claim | Which problem, which skin/hair type, which wording is permitted? |
| Active selection | Mechanism, effective and permitted concentration, pH of stability and activity, solubility, penetration needed (surface, stratum corneum, epidermis, follicle) |
| Vehicle | Emulsion, gel, serum, foam, stick; occlusion level; non-comedogenic for oily skin |
| Delivery | Penetration enhancers (glycols), encapsulation (liposomes, niosomes, nanostructured lipid carriers, microsponges, cyclodextrins) to improve stability, targeting or tolerance |
| Stability | Oxidation (vitamin C, retinol), light (avobenzone), hydrolysis; antioxidants, chelators, airless/opaque packs |
| Safety | Irritation (acids, retinoids), sensitisation, phototoxicity; margin of safety |
| Proof | Instrumental and clinical claim substantiation (section 4.9) |

## 4.2 Sun protection and sunscreens

### UV radiation and its effects

| Band | Wavelength | Effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UVC | 100–280 nm | Absorbed by ozone; does not reach the ground |
| UVB | 280 (290)–320 nm | Sunburn (erythema), DNA damage (thymine dimers), skin cancer, delayed tanning, vitamin D synthesis |
| UVA2 | 320–340 nm | Tanning, photoageing |
| UVA1 | 340–400 nm | Penetrates to the dermis; ROS, MMP activation, photoageing, pigmentation, photosensitivity reactions; passes through glass |
| Visible (especially blue light) and infrared | 400–700 nm and above | Visible light worsens pigmentation (melasma) in darker skin; infrared adds heat and ROS |

### Classification of sunscreens

**1. By mechanism**

| Type | How it works | Examples | Points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Organic (chemical, "UV absorbers") | Conjugated molecules absorb UV and release the energy as heat | See table below | Transparent, elegant; some photounstable or sensitising |
| Inorganic (physical, mineral) | Particles absorb and scatter UV (and some visible light) | Zinc oxide (UVB + UVA1), titanium dioxide (UVB + UVA2) | Photostable, low allergy; whitening unless micronised/nano; coated to prevent photocatalysis |
| Systemic/oral photoprotectives | Antioxidants taken by mouth | Polypodium leucotomos extract, carotenoids | Only supplementary |

**2. By chemical class (organic filters)**

| Class | Examples | Range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| p-Aminobenzoates | PABA, padimate O | UVB (PABA now little used: allergy, staining) |
| Cinnamates | Octinoxate (ethylhexyl methoxycinnamate), cinoxate | UVB |
| Salicylates | Octisalate (ethylhexyl salicylate), homosalate | UVB (weak; also solvents) |
| Others | Octocrylene, ensulizole (phenylbenzimidazole sulphonic acid, water-soluble) | UVB |
| Benzophenones | Oxybenzone (benzophenone-3), sulisobenzone | UVB + UVA2 |
| Dibenzoylmethanes | Avobenzone (butyl methoxydibenzoylmethane) | UVA1 – photounstable; stabilised with octocrylene or bemotrizinol |
| Anthranilates | Meradimate | UVA2 |
| Newer broad-spectrum (largely EU/Asia) | Bemotrizinol (bis-ethylhexyloxyphenol methoxyphenyl triazine), bisoctrizole (methylene bis-benzotriazolyl tetramethylbutylphenol), ecamsule (terephthalylidene dicamphor sulphonic acid), drometrizole trisiloxane, diethylamino hydroxybenzoyl hexyl benzoate | UVB + UVA |

**3. By protection claim:** UVB only (sunburn), broad spectrum (UVB + UVA), water-resistant, products with added visible-light protection (tinted with iron oxides).

### Rating sun protection

- **SPF** = MED (minimal erythema dose) of protected skin ÷ MED of unprotected skin, measured on volunteers with product applied at **2 mg/cm²** (international method **ISO 24444**). SPF 15 blocks about 93% of UVB, SPF 30 about 97%, SPF 50 about 98%.
- **UVA-PF**: in vivo by persistent pigment darkening (PPD) or in vitro (ISO 24443) on roughened plates.
- **Critical wavelength (λc)**: the wavelength below which 90% of the area under the absorbance curve (290–400 nm) lies; λc ≥ 370 nm means broad spectrum (US rule).
- **PA system** (Japan, also used on Asian and many Indian products): PA+ (PPD 2 to < 4), PA++ (4 to < 8), PA+++ (8 to < 16), PA++++ (≥ 16).
- **Water resistance:** SPF re-tested after 40 or 80 minutes of immersion (US labels "water resistant (40 minutes)" or "(80 minutes)").
- **In vitro screening** (Mansur equation) is used in research and practicals, not for label claims.

### Regulatory aspects of sunscreens

| Region | Status and main rules |
| --- | --- |
| **India** | Sunscreens are **cosmetics** (no disease claim). They must meet the Cosmetics Rules, 2020 and BIS requirements on permitted ingredients and labelling; claims must not be false or misleading (Rule 36). Imported sunscreens need registration (COS-2) |
| **European Union** | Cosmetics. UV filters only from **Annex VI** of Regulation 1223/2009 with maximum concentrations. Commission Recommendation 2006/647/EC: UVA-PF at least **one third** of the labelled SPF and λc ≥ 370 nm; SPF bands **low (6–10), medium (15–25), high (30–50), very high (50+)**; no "sunblock" or "100% protection" claims; instructions to reapply |
| **USA** | **OTC drugs** under the FDA sunscreen monograph; limited list of filters; broad-spectrum test (λc ≥ 370 nm) needed to claim protection against skin cancer and early ageing; in 2019 the FDA proposed that only zinc oxide and titanium dioxide had enough data to be generally recognised as safe and effective and asked for more data on other filters |
| **Australia** | Primary sunscreens are therapeutic goods regulated by the TGA |
| **Japan** | Cosmetics or quasi-drugs; PA rating |

### Designing a sunscreen

1. **Filter selection** for broad spectrum: combine UVB and UVA filters (e.g. octinoxate + avobenzone + octocrylene, or bemotrizinol + zinc oxide), respecting permitted limits and avoiding unstable pairs (avobenzone degrades with octinoxate in light; photostabilisers are needed).
2. **SPF booster strategy**: film formers (acrylates copolymers), even distribution, light-scattering particles, synergy between filters.
3. **Vehicle**: o/w emulsions (most elegant), w/o (better water resistance), gels, sprays, sticks; solvent emollients (C12–15 alkyl benzoate) to dissolve crystalline filters and prevent recrystallisation.
4. **Physical filters**: coated (silica, alumina, dimethicone) to block photocatalysis; dispersed well to avoid whitening and clumping; for darker skin, tinting with iron oxides improves appearance and visible-light protection.
5. **Water resistance**: film-forming polymers, w/o or silicone-rich bases.
6. **Testing**: SPF, UVA-PF, λc, photostability, water resistance, phototoxicity and photoallergy screening, stability.
7. **Labelling**: SPF, UVA/PA, directions (apply generously 15–20 minutes before exposure, reapply every 2 hours and after swimming or sweating).

## 4.3 Addressing dry skin

**Goal:** restore water and lipids in the stratum corneum and normalise desquamation.

| Strategy | Actives |
| --- | --- |
| Humectants (pull water into the stratum corneum) | Glycerin (the gold standard), sodium PCA, urea (also keratolytic at higher levels), lactic acid/sodium lactate, hyaluronic acid, sorbitol, panthenol |
| Occlusives (reduce TEWL) | Petrolatum, mineral oil, dimethicone, lanolin, waxes, shea butter |
| Emollients (smooth the surface) | Esters, triglycerides, fatty alcohols |
| Barrier repair | Physiological lipid mixture of ceramides, cholesterol and free fatty acids, niacinamide (increases ceramide synthesis), phytosphingosine |
| Desquamation aid | Low-level AHAs (lactic acid), urea |
| Soothing | Allantoin, bisabolol, oat extract |

**Formulation:** w/o or rich o/w creams for very dry skin, lotions for body; avoid harsh surfactants in the regimen (syndet bar, cream cleanser); fragrance-free for sensitive skin; slightly acidic pH.

## 4.4 Addressing acne-prone skin

| Target | Cosmetic actives / approaches |
| --- | --- |
| Excess sebum | Oil-absorbing powders (silica, kaolin), zinc PCA, niacinamide |
| Follicular blockage (comedones) | Salicylic acid (lipophilic BHA, enters the follicle; exfoliant), glycolic/lactic acid, retinol and retinaldehyde (cosmetic retinoids), enzymes |
| C. acnes | Tea tree oil, zinc salts, azelaic acid (drug at therapeutic strengths in many markets) |
| Inflammation and marks | Niacinamide, licorice extract, allantoin, centella, sunscreen to prevent darkening of marks |

**Vehicle:** light, **non-comedogenic** gels, oil-free lotions, foaming cleansers with mild surfactants; avoid heavy occlusives and known comedogenic esters (isopropyl myristate in high amounts); add a non-greasy sunscreen. Medicated products with benzoyl peroxide, adapalene or antibiotics are drugs.

## 4.5 Addressing pigmentation

| Mechanism | Actives |
| --- | --- |
| Tyrosinase inhibition | Kojic acid, arbutin and alpha-arbutin (hydroquinone glucosides that release little hydroquinone), licorice extract (glabridin), azelaic acid, resorcinol derivatives (4-butylresorcinol), vitamin C (ascorbic acid and stable derivatives) |
| Blocking melanosome transfer | Niacinamide, soy extracts (protease inhibitors) |
| Reducing the inflammatory and UV signal | Antioxidants (vitamin C, vitamin E, ferulic acid), tranexamic acid (used topically in cosmetics in many markets) |
| Removing pigmented cells | AHAs, retinol, enzymes (exfoliation) |
| Prevention | **Broad-spectrum sunscreen plus visible-light protection** (iron oxides) – the single most important step, especially for melasma |

**Design points:** vitamin C (L-ascorbic acid) is stable only at low pH (about 3–3.5) and oxidises; use derivatives (magnesium ascorbyl phosphate, ascorbyl glucoside, ethyl ascorbic acid) or anhydrous/airless packs. Hydroquinone is a drug in India and banned in EU cosmetics; mercury compounds are prohibited except as in Rule 39(5). Claims must not promise "fairness" as a medical change in skin colour.

## 4.6 Addressing prickly heat and wrinkles

**Prickly heat (miliaria rubra):**

| Need | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| Keep skin dry and cool | Prickly-heat powders: talc (asbestos-free), starch, kaolin to absorb sweat |
| Cooling and itch relief | Menthol, camphor (low levels), calamine, zinc oxide |
| Reduce bacteria in ducts | Mild antimicrobial and astringent ingredients such as zinc salts (zinc oxide, zinc stearate); older antibacterials such as triclosan are now restricted in many markets |
| Avoid occlusion | Light lotions instead of heavy creams |

A typical prickly-heat powder: talc or starch base (absorbent, slip) + zinc oxide or calamine (soothing, mildly astringent) + kaolin (absorbs sweat) + menthol (cooling) + perfume. Safety: use cosmetic-grade, asbestos-free talc, and keep loose powders away from babies' faces to avoid inhalation.

**Wrinkles (anti-ageing):**

| Target | Actives |
| --- | --- |
| Prevent photoageing | Daily broad-spectrum sunscreen |
| Stimulate collagen, normalise epidermis | Retinol, retinaldehyde, retinyl esters (cosmetic retinoids; tretinoin is a drug) |
| Antioxidant protection | Vitamin C, vitamin E, ferulic acid, green tea polyphenols, coenzyme Q10, resveratrol |
| Signal and carrier peptides | Palmitoyl pentapeptide-4, copper tripeptide-1 |
| Exfoliation and texture | Glycolic acid, lactic acid, polyhydroxy acids (gluconolactone) |
| Hydration and plumping | Hyaluronic acid (various molecular weights), glycerin |
| Expression lines | Acetyl hexapeptide-8 (claimed to reduce muscle contraction signals) |

**Design points:** retinol is unstable to light, oxygen and heat → encapsulation, antioxidants, opaque airless packs, night use; acids are formulated at controlled pH for a balance of efficacy and irritation; start low and build up to reduce retinoid irritation.

## 4.7 Addressing body odour and dandruff

**Body odour – deodorants and antiperspirants**

| Product | Mechanism | Actives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deodorant | Reduce odour-forming bacteria; neutralise or mask odour | Antimicrobials (ethanol, triethyl citrate – esterase inhibitor, farnesol, zinc salts), odour absorbers (zinc ricinoleate, cyclodextrins), fragrance |
| Antiperspirant | Reduce sweat by forming temporary plugs of aluminium hydroxide gel in sweat ducts | Aluminium chlorohydrate, aluminium zirconium tetrachlorohydrex gly, aluminium chloride (stronger, more irritant) |

Formats: roll-on (aqueous or emulsion), stick (stearyl alcohol + volatile silicone), aerosol (powder in suspension), cream. Points: aluminium salts are acidic and can irritate; avoid use on broken skin; antiperspirants are drugs in the USA but cosmetics in India and the EU.

**Dandruff**

| Target | Actives |
| --- | --- |
| Malassezia (antifungal) | Zinc pyrithione, piroctone olamine, climbazole, selenium sulphide; ketoconazole and ciclopirox are drugs |
| Scaling | Salicylic acid, sulphur |
| Cytostatic | Selenium sulphide, coal tar (drug-type use) |
| Inflammation/itch | Menthol, zinc |

**Design:** anti-dandruff shampoos must **deposit and retain** the active on the scalp – insoluble zinc pyrithione particles of the right size are suspended with acrylate polymers and deposited with cationic polymers; contact time of a few minutes; use alternately with a mild shampoo; conditioners and leave-on tonics complete the regimen.

## 4.8 Addressing oral problems

| Problem | Mechanism targeted | Cosmeceutical actives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dental cavities** | Enamel demineralisation by plaque acids | **Fluoride** – sodium fluoride, sodium monofluorophosphate, stannous fluoride (≤ 1000 ppm fluoride in Indian toothpaste); fluoride forms fluorapatite/calcium fluoride reservoirs that resist acid and promote remineralisation; xylitol (non-fermentable sweetener); calcium phosphate technologies |
| **Bleeding gums** (gingivitis) | Plaque bacteria at the gum line | Antiplaque/antigingivitis agents: stannous fluoride, zinc citrate, cetylpyridinium chloride (mouthwash), essential oils (thymol, eucalyptol, menthol, methyl salicylate), chlorhexidine (a drug-strength mouthwash for short-term use), herbal (neem, clove) |
| **Mouth odour** (halitosis) | Volatile sulphur compounds from tongue bacteria | Zinc salts (bind sulphur), cetylpyridinium chloride, chlorine dioxide, flavours (mint) for masking; tongue cleaners |
| **Sensitive teeth** | Hydrodynamic stimulation of exposed dentine | **Nerve desensitisation**: potassium nitrate (potassium ions depolarise nerves); **tubule occlusion**: stannous fluoride, strontium salts, arginine with calcium carbonate, calcium sodium phosphosilicate (bioactive glass), hydroxyapatite; low-abrasivity pastes |
| **Tartar, stains, whitening** | Mineralised plaque, extrinsic stains | Pyrophosphates, zinc citrate (tartar control); silica abrasives, enzymes, peroxides (whitening within permitted limits) |

**Design points:** fluoride compatibility with abrasives (section 3.7); stannous fluoride needs stabilisation (anhydrous or chelated systems) and can stain; potassium nitrate works over 2–4 weeks of regular use; SLS-free pastes for people with mouth ulcers; flavour and sweetener make or break consumer use.

## 4.9 Evaluation of cosmeceuticals: safety, stability and efficacy

### Safety assessment

Because animal testing of cosmetics is banned in India and the EU, safety rests on **ingredient data + exposure + human-relevant tests**.

```text
Hazard data on each ingredient         Exposure in use
(toxicology literature, alternative    (amount applied, frequency,
 tests, supplier data)                  area, rinse-off/leave-on,
           │                            absorption) → Systemic exposure
           └──────────────┬─────────────dosage (SED)
                          ▼
            Margin of safety (MoS) = NOAEL ÷ SED
            (a MoS of at least 100 is generally taken as adequate)
                          ▼
     Product safety assessment report → human tests if needed
```

| Endpoint | Validated alternative (non-animal) methods |
| --- | --- |
| Skin irritation | Reconstructed human epidermis tests (OECD TG 439) |
| Skin corrosion | Reconstructed epidermis (OECD TG 431) |
| Eye irritation | Bovine corneal opacity and permeability (BCOP, OECD TG 437); reconstructed human cornea-like epithelium (OECD TG 492); HET-CAM (hen's egg chorioallantoic membrane, used for screening) |
| Skin sensitisation | Direct peptide reactivity assay (OECD TG 442C), KeratinoSens (442D), h-CLAT (442E), combined in defined approaches |
| Phototoxicity | 3T3 neutral red uptake phototoxicity test (OECD TG 432) |
| Genotoxicity | Ames test, in vitro micronucleus |
| Skin absorption | In vitro diffusion through human/pig skin (OECD TG 428, Franz cells) |

**Human tests (finished products, after safety is established):** single and repeated **patch tests**, the **human repeat insult patch test (HRIPT)** for sensitisation potential, use tests under dermatological supervision, ophthalmological tests for eye products, non-comedogenicity tests, in-use acceptability. India's **IS 4011:2018** gives methods of test for safety evaluation of cosmetics.

### Stability and microbiological quality

- **Stability:** real-time and accelerated storage (for example 40 °C/75% RH), freeze–thaw cycles, centrifugation, light exposure; checks on appearance, odour, colour, pH, viscosity, droplet/particle size, active content, packaging compatibility.
- **Microbiology:** total aerobic count and absence of specified pathogens per BIS limits; **challenge test** (ISO 11930).

### Efficacy and claim substantiation

| Claim | Measurement |
| --- | --- |
| Moisturisation | Corneometer (capacitance), skin conductance |
| Barrier repair | TEWL (Tewameter/evaporimeter) |
| Oil control | Sebumeter (photometric grease spot) |
| Firmness/elasticity | Cutometer (suction) |
| Wrinkles and texture | Fringe-projection 3D imaging, replica profilometry, standard photography with expert grading |
| Even tone, brightening | Chromameter (L\*a\*b\*), ITA°, Mexameter (melanin and erythema index), VISIA-type imaging |
| Sun protection | SPF (ISO 24444), UVA-PF, critical wavelength, water resistance |
| Anti-dandruff | Adherent scalp flaking score, Malassezia counts |
| Hair | Combing force, tensile strength, shine (goniophotometry), breakage |
| Oral | Plaque and gingival indices, fluoride uptake, Schiff sensitivity score, organoleptic halitosis scores, VSC meters |

Good claim substantiation uses a defined panel, controls (vehicle or untreated site), randomisation and blinding, and appropriate statistics. In India, claims must be truthful and not misleading (Rule 36), and the industry self-regulatory code of the Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) asks for evidence behind advertised claims.

## Remember

- Cosmeceutical design = biology → active (permitted level, pH, stability) → vehicle and delivery → safety → proof. Treatment claims make it a drug.
- UVB 290–320 nm (burn), UVA2 320–340, UVA1 340–400 nm (ageing, pigment); visible light worsens melasma.
- Sunscreens: organic (cinnamates, salicylates, benzophenones, avobenzone, octocrylene, triazines) vs inorganic (ZnO, TiO2); SPF at 2 mg/cm² (ISO 24444); λc ≥ 370 nm; UVA-PF ≥ ⅓ SPF (EU); PA+ to PA++++; India = cosmetic, USA = OTC drug, EU = Annex VI filters.
- Dry skin: humectant + occlusive + emollient + barrier lipids. Acne: salicylic acid, niacinamide, non-comedogenic vehicle. Pigmentation: tyrosinase inhibitors + niacinamide + sunscreen. Wrinkles: retinol, antioxidants, peptides, AHAs, sunscreen.
- Prickly heat: absorbent cooling powders, avoid occlusion. Body odour: deodorant (antimicrobial, fragrance) vs antiperspirant (aluminium salts plug ducts). Dandruff: zinc pyrithione, piroctone olamine, climbazole, selenium sulphide.
- Caries: fluoride (≤ 1000 ppm India); gums: stannous fluoride, zinc, CPC, essential oils; halitosis: zinc, CPC, ClO2; sensitivity: potassium nitrate (nerve) or tubule occluders.
- Safety: MoS = NOAEL/SED ≥ 100; OECD alternatives 439, 431, 437, 492, 442C/D/E, 432, 428; HRIPT; IS 4011:2018. Efficacy: corneometer, TEWL, sebumeter, cutometer, chromameter, profilometry.

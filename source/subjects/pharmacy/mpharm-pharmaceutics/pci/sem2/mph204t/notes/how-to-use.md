# How to use this subject

Cosmetics and Cosmeceuticals (MPH204T) is the second-semester M.Pharm Pharmaceutics course that turns formulation science into consumer products: creams, shampoos, toothpastes, sunscreens and herbal cosmetics. Unlike the B.Pharm elective Cosmetic Science (BP809ET), the M.Pharm paper expects you to **quote the law rule by rule**, **explain the biology behind each skin, hair or oral problem**, **justify every ingredient in a formula**, and **discuss safety, regulation and current controversies** (parabens, formaldehyde releasers, 1,4-dioxane, fragrance allergens, natural standards).

## Units and how they map to the PCI syllabus

The five units follow the five units of the PCI MPH 204T syllabus exactly (60 hours, 12 hours each).

| Unit | PCI syllabus unit | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cosmetics – Regulatory | Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and Cosmetics Rules, 2020: definition, labelling, import, misbranded/spurious (and adulterated), manufacture, loan licence, prohibitions, offences and penalties |
| 2 | Cosmetics – Biological aspects | Skin problems (dry skin, acne, pigmentation, prickly heat, wrinkles, body odour), hair structure and growth cycle, oral problems, care needs of each body region |
| 3 | Formulation building blocks | Surfactants, emollients, rheology modifiers, preservatives and their efficacy, classic product formulas, soaps and syndets, perfumes and EU allergens, controversial ingredients. The largest unit |
| 4 | Design of cosmeceutical products | Sunscreens and their regulation, then product design for each problem. A section on **safety, stability and efficacy evaluation** has been added here because it is needed to answer "design" questions fully and is part of the course objectives (safety, stability and efficacy) |
| 5 | Herbal cosmetics | Herbal ingredients for hair, skin and oral care; COSMOS rules for preservatives, emollients, foaming agents, emulsifiers and rheology modifiers; challenges |

The related B.Pharm pack (BP809ET Cosmetic Science) covers the same basics at a simpler level and has practical guides; use it for quick revision of definitions, SPF and BIS tests.

## Exam pattern

The paper carries **100 marks: 25 internal assessment + 75 end-semester**, and the end-semester examination lasts **3 hours**. PCI does not prescribe a section-wise pattern for M.Pharm, so most universities follow the PCI B.Pharm 75-mark split shown below (used for M.Pharm by, for example, Bahra University, Shimla). Confirm the exact pattern with your university.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ, all compulsory | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays – attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers – attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |
| | Total | 75 |

The question bank has long (10-mark), short (5-mark) and very short (2-mark) questions for every unit. The mock paper uses the pattern above, and a 20-question, 20-minute MCQ test is included for quick practice.

## Probability ratings

- **High** – a frequent question type for this syllabus topic; prepare a full answer with tables and examples.
- **Medium** – appears every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** – rare; useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

The ratings are estimates from syllabus weight and common question patterns in PCI-syllabus pharmacy papers. They are not a promise of what will be asked.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. **Law answers:** always cite the source – "section 3(aaa) of the Act", "Rule 34 of the Cosmetics Rules, 2020", "Form COS-8". Name the authority (CDSCO/CLA vs State Licensing Authority) and give the time limits (45 days, 30 days, six months, five years). A table of "item – requirement – rule" scores well.
2. **Biology answers:** draw the skin cross-section, the acne cascade, the melanin pathway and the hair-growth cycle as labelled text diagrams. Link each to a cosmeceutical approach in one line – examiners at M.Pharm level reward this connection.
3. **Formulation answers:** write a formula table with **ingredient – function – typical %**, then the method (phase temperatures, order of mixing) and evaluation tests. Explain *why* (e.g. why stearic acid gives a pearly vanishing cream, why calcium carbonate pastes use monofluorophosphate).
4. **Comparison tables** make strong short answers: soap vs syndet, cold vs vanishing cream, organic vs inorganic sunscreen, deodorant vs antiperspirant, licence vs loan licence, misbranded vs spurious vs adulterated, intrinsic vs extrinsic ageing, COSMOS ORGANIC vs NATURAL.
5. **Controversy questions:** give hazard, exposure, risk and the current regulatory position (EU limits for parabens; 0.001% "releases formaldehyde" warning; dioxane as an ethoxylation impurity controlled by stripping). End with the formulator's balanced view.
6. **Numbers to remember:** acid mantle pH 4.5–5.5; SPF at 2 mg/cm²; λc ≥ 370 nm; UVA-PF ≥ ⅓ SPF; fluoride ≤ 1000 ppm; TFM 76/70/60%; allergen labelling 0.001%/0.01%; COSMOS 20%/10%, 95% PPAI, 2% petrochemical; mercury 70 ppm/1 ppm; colours As 2 ppm, Pb 20 ppm; MoS ≥ 100.
7. Do not invent rule numbers, fines or dates in the exam. If unsure, describe the provision in words – you lose fewer marks than for a wrong number.

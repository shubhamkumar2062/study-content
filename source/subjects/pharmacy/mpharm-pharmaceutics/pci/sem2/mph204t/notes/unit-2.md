# Unit 2: Biological aspects — skin, hair and oral cavity

Every cosmeceutical is designed backwards from a biological problem: why the skin is dry, why a pore turns into a comedone, why a dark patch forms, why sweat smells, why a tooth decays. This unit gives that biology at the depth needed to choose actives and vehicles in Units 3 and 4. It covers the skin and its common cosmetic problems, the hair and its growth cycle, the oral cavity, and the cleansing and care needs of each body region.

## 2.1 Skin: structure that matters for cosmetics

```text
          Sebum + sweat film ("acid mantle", pH about 4.5–5.5)
 ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │ STRATUM CORNEUM  10–20 layers of corneocytes in lipid        │ ◄ barrier, NMF
 │ stratum granulosum  lamellar bodies release lipids           │
 │ stratum spinosum    desmosomes, Langerhans cells             │
 │ stratum basale      keratinocyte stem cells, melanocytes     │ ◄ pigment
 ├─────────────────────── dermo-epidermal junction ─────────────┤
 │ DERMIS  fibroblasts → collagen I/III, elastin, hyaluronan;    │ ◄ wrinkles
 │         vessels, nerves, sebaceous glands, sweat glands,      │
 │         hair follicles                                        │
 ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ HYPODERMIS  adipocytes                                        │
 └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Epidermal turnover.** Basal keratinocytes divide, move upward, fill with keratin and finally lose their nuclei to become flat **corneocytes**, which shed from the surface (desquamation). The journey takes roughly four weeks in young skin and slows with age.

**The stratum corneum barrier ("bricks and mortar").**

- **Bricks** – corneocytes packed with keratin and surrounded by a tough **cornified envelope** (proteins such as loricrin and involucrin, crosslinked by transglutaminase).
- **Mortar** – intercellular lipid lamellae made of **ceramides, cholesterol and free fatty acids** in roughly equimolar amounts. These lipids are secreted from **lamellar bodies** in the granular layer.
- **Natural moisturising factor (NMF)** – water-soluble, hygroscopic molecules inside corneocytes: free amino acids and their derivatives (from breakdown of the protein **filaggrin**), **pyrrolidone carboxylic acid (PCA)**, urocanic acid, lactate, urea and salts. NMF keeps the stratum corneum hydrated and flexible.
- **Desmosomes / corneodesmosomes** hold corneocytes together; proteases break them in a controlled way so cells shed singly and invisibly.

The barrier is measured as **transepidermal water loss (TEWL)**: the higher the TEWL, the weaker the barrier. Healthy stratum corneum holds about 10–20% water; below about 10% it becomes rough and cracks.

**Acid mantle.** The surface pH of about 4.5–5.5 comes from free fatty acids, lactic acid, urocanic acid and the sodium–hydrogen exchanger. An acidic pH keeps barrier enzymes (that make ceramides) active, keeps desquamation enzymes in check, and favours friendly skin bacteria. Alkaline soaps raise surface pH and disturb this balance.

**Skin microbiome.** Staphylococcus epidermidis, Cutibacterium (formerly Propionibacterium), Corynebacterium and Malassezia live on the skin. They occupy space and help defence, but some species cause acne, body odour and dandruff when conditions change.

**Skin types.** Normal, dry, oily, combination and sensitive; Fitzpatrick phototypes I–VI describe how skin burns or tans. Indian skin is mostly types III–V, which tan easily and are prone to pigmentation problems after inflammation.

## 2.2 Skin structure related to common cosmetic problems

### Dry skin (xerosis)

| Cause | Mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| Low humidity, cold, wind | Water evaporates faster than it diffuses up from the dermis |
| Harsh surfactants, hot water, frequent washing | Surfactants dissolve barrier lipids and denature keratin; NMF is washed out; alkaline pH |
| Ageing | Less lipid synthesis, less NMF, slower turnover |
| Genetic (filaggrin defects), atopic tendency | Less NMF, weaker barrier |
| Sun damage, diabetes, hypothyroidism, some drugs | Altered keratinisation |

**What happens:** corneocytes stay stuck together (desquamation enzymes need water), so scales build up; skin feels tight, looks dull and flaky, may crack and itch. The vicious cycle: low water → abnormal shedding → rough surface → more water loss.

### Acne

Acne vulgaris is a disease of the **pilosebaceous unit** (hair follicle with its sebaceous gland), commonest on the face, chest and back during adolescence.

```text
Androgens ↑ ──► sebaceous gland enlarges ──► excess sebum
                                        │
Abnormal keratinisation of the follicle mouth ──► microcomedone
                                        │            │
                                        ▼            ▼
               closed comedone (whitehead)   open comedone (blackhead,
                                             dark due to oxidised lipid
                                             and melanin)
                                        │
Cutibacterium acnes multiplies in sebum ─► lipases → free fatty acids,
                                        │   immune activation
                                        ▼
         Inflammation ──► papules, pustules, nodules ─► scars, post-inflammatory
                                                        hyperpigmentation
```

Four key factors: **(1) increased sebum, (2) follicular hyperkeratinisation, (3) colonisation by C. acnes, (4) inflammation.** Aggravating factors: comedogenic cosmetics (heavy oils and some esters), occlusion, friction, humidity, stress, some foods in susceptible people. Cosmetic products can reduce oil, unblock pores and avoid comedogenicity; moderate to severe acne needs medical treatment.

### Pigmentation

**Melanogenesis.** Melanocytes in the basal layer make melanin inside **melanosomes** and transfer them through dendrites to about 30–40 surrounding keratinocytes (the "epidermal melanin unit").

```text
L-tyrosine ──tyrosinase──► L-DOPA ──tyrosinase──► dopaquinone
                                                     │
                     no cysteine ◄───────────────────┴──────► with cysteine
                         │                                        │
           DHI / DHICA → EUMELANIN                      PHEOMELANIN
           (brown–black)                                (red–yellow)
```

Triggers: **UV light** (DNA damage → α-MSH from keratinocytes → MC1R receptor on melanocytes → more tyrosinase), visible light, inflammation and hormones.

| Disorder | Features |
| --- | --- |
| Melasma | Symmetric brown patches on cheeks, forehead, upper lip; hormones and sun; common in Indian women; relapses easily |
| Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation | Dark marks after acne, eczema or injury; common in darker skin |
| Solar lentigines | Sun spots on exposed skin in older age |
| Freckles (ephelides) | Small spots that darken in summer |
| Uneven tone, dark circles, tan | Mixed causes |

Cosmeceutical targets: tyrosinase activity, melanosome transfer, turnover of pigmented cells, and sun protection.

### Prickly heat (miliaria)

Miliaria results from **blockage of eccrine sweat ducts** in hot, humid weather, so sweat leaks into the skin.

| Type | Level of blockage | Appearance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Miliaria crystallina | Within or just under the stratum corneum | Tiny clear blisters, no inflammation |
| Miliaria rubra (prickly heat) | Deeper in the epidermis | Itchy, prickling red papules on trunk, neck, skin folds |
| Miliaria profunda | Dermo-epidermal junction | Flesh-coloured papules, after repeated rubra |

Contributing factors: occlusive clothing, heavy creams, swelling of the stratum corneum from sweat, and bacteria (Staphylococcus) that form slime in ducts. Care: keep skin cool and dry, avoid occlusion, use absorbent and cooling powders.

### Wrinkles and skin ageing

| Intrinsic (chronological) ageing | Extrinsic (photo-)ageing |
| --- | --- |
| Genetic, hormonal (falling oestrogen) | Mainly UV (also pollution, smoking) |
| Fine wrinkles, thin dry skin, loss of fat | Coarse deep wrinkles, leathery texture, spots, broken vessels |
| Less collagen synthesis, slower turnover | UV → reactive oxygen species → activation of **matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs)** that break collagen; reduced new collagen; abnormal elastin (solar elastosis) |
| Gradual loss of hyaluronic acid | **Glycation** (sugar crosslinks) stiffens collagen |

Wrinkles form where the dermis loses collagen and elastin and facial muscles fold the skin repeatedly (expression lines). Cosmeceutical approaches: daily sun protection, retinoids, antioxidants, peptides, AHAs, moisturisers.

### Body odour

| Gland | Location | Secretion | Role in odour |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Eccrine | Whole body, many on palms, soles, forehead | Watery, salty | Mainly wetness; odour on feet when bacteria break down keratin |
| Apocrine | Armpits, groin, areola; active from puberty | Oily, protein- and lipid-rich, odourless when fresh | Bacteria (Corynebacterium, Staphylococcus) convert precursors into volatile fatty acids such as 3-methyl-2-hexenoic acid and sulphur compounds (thioalcohols) and steroid derivatives that smell |

So body odour needs **sweat + bacteria + time**. Control strategies: reduce sweat (antiperspirants), reduce bacteria (antimicrobials, low pH), mask or absorb odour (fragrance, zinc ricinoleate), and good hygiene.

## 2.3 Hair: structure, chemistry and growth cycle

**The follicle.** The hair grows from the **bulb**, where matrix cells divide around the **dermal papilla** (the papilla controls the size and cycle of the hair). Melanocytes in the bulb colour the hair. The sebaceous gland and arrector pili muscle attach to the follicle.

**The shaft.**

| Layer | Structure | Cosmetic importance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cuticle | 6–10 layers of flat overlapping scales, tips pointing to the end; outer lipid layer (18-methyleicosanoic acid) makes it water-repellent | Shine, smoothness, combability; damaged by bleaching, alkaline products, heat, friction |
| Cortex | Spindle-shaped cells packed with keratin macrofibrils; holds melanin | Strength and elasticity; site of action of dyes, perms and bleaches |
| Medulla | Loose central core, may be absent | Little cosmetic role |

**Hair chemistry.** Keratin is rich in cystine. Bonds that hold it together:

- **Disulphide bonds** – strong covalent; broken by reducing agents (thioglycolate perms, depilatories) and oxidising bleaches.
- **Hydrogen bonds** – broken by water and reformed on drying (basis of temporary setting).
- **Salt (ionic) bonds** – depend on pH; strongest near the isoelectric point (about pH 3.7).

Hair carries a net **negative charge** at normal pH, more so when damaged, which is why **cationic** conditioners deposit on it.

**Growth cycle.**

| Phase | Events | Approximate duration (scalp) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anagen | Active growth; about 85–90% of scalp hairs | 2–7 years |
| Catagen | Regression; bulb shrinks | 2–3 weeks |
| Telogen | Rest; club hair held loosely; about 10–15% | About 3 months |
| Exogen | Release of the old hair as the new anagen hair grows | – |

Scalp hair grows about 1 cm a month; losing up to about 100 hairs a day is normal.

**Common hair and scalp problems**

| Problem | Biology |
| --- | --- |
| Androgenetic alopecia | Dihydrotestosterone (made by 5α-reductase) progressively miniaturises follicles and shortens anagen |
| Telogen effluvium | Many follicles enter telogen together after fever, childbirth, crash diet or stress; diffuse shedding 2–3 months later |
| Dandruff / seborrhoeic dermatitis | Malassezia yeasts break sebum triglycerides to irritant free fatty acids (e.g. oleic acid) → inflammation and rapid scaling |
| Greying (canities) | Loss of melanocyte activity in the bulb; oxidative stress |
| Split ends, frizz, breakage | Cuticle loss and weathering |

## 2.4 Oral cavity: structure and common problems

```text
            Enamel (≈96% hydroxyapatite, no living cells)
             │
   ┌─────────▼────────┐
   │      Dentine      │ ◄ tubules filled with fluid, linked to pulp nerves
   │   ┌───────────┐   │
   │   │   Pulp    │   │ ◄ nerves, blood vessels
   └───┴───────────┴───┘
      Gingiva covers bone; cementum covers root
```

**Saliva** buffers acids (bicarbonate), supplies calcium and phosphate for remineralisation, washes away food and carries antibacterial proteins. A thin protein film, the **acquired pellicle**, forms on enamel within minutes of cleaning; bacteria attach to it and grow into **plaque (a biofilm)**.

| Problem | Mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| **Dental caries** | Plaque bacteria (Streptococcus mutans, lactobacilli) ferment sugars to acids; when plaque pH falls below the **critical pH of about 5.5**, enamel hydroxyapatite dissolves (demineralisation). Saliva and fluoride drive remineralisation between meals; frequent sugar intake tips the balance toward cavities |
| **Calculus (tartar)** | Plaque mineralises with calcium phosphate from saliva |
| **Gingivitis (bleeding gums)** | Plaque at the gum line → inflammation; red, swollen gums that bleed on brushing; reversible with good hygiene |
| **Periodontitis** | Inflammation spreads to ligament and bone; pockets, loosening teeth; needs dental care |
| **Halitosis (mouth odour)** | Anaerobic bacteria on the back of the tongue and in gum pockets break down proteins to **volatile sulphur compounds** (hydrogen sulphide, methyl mercaptan) |
| **Dentine hypersensitivity (sensitive teeth)** | Exposed dentine (enamel wear, gum recession, acid erosion) with open tubules; cold, heat, sweet or air moves tubule fluid and stimulates pulp nerves (**hydrodynamic theory**) → short sharp pain |
| **Stains** | Extrinsic (tea, coffee, tobacco, chromogenic bacteria) and intrinsic |
| **Erosion** | Acid from diet or reflux dissolves enamel without bacteria |

## 2.5 Cleansing and care needs of different body regions

| Region | Special biology | Cleansing need | Care need | Typical products |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Face** | Many sebaceous glands (T-zone), thin skin on cheeks, sun-exposed | Remove sebum, sweat, pollution, make-up without stripping lipids; pH-balanced mild surfactants | Moisturise, sun protection, oil control or anti-ageing as needed | Face wash, cleansing milk, micellar water, toner, moisturiser, sunscreen |
| **Eyelids** | Thinnest skin of the body, close to the eye, sensitive | Gentle, non-stinging; remove waterproof make-up with oils or biphasic removers | Light non-irritant creams; ophthalmologist-tested | Eye make-up remover, eye cream/gel |
| **Lips** | No sebaceous glands, very thin stratum corneum, no melanin protection to speak of | Water is enough | Occlusive balms, UV protection | Lip balm, lipstick with emollients |
| **Hands** | Frequent washing and detergents, exposed to sun | Mild hand washes | Barrier creams, hand creams with humectants and occlusives | Hand wash, hand cream |
| **Feet** | Thick stratum corneum on soles, many eccrine glands, enclosed in shoes | Wash and dry between toes | Keratolytic softening of heels (urea, salicylic acid), antifungal and deodorant powders | Foot cream, foot powder |
| **Nails** | Hard keratin plate; brittle with detergents and solvents | Gentle cleaning; avoid harsh removers | Moisturise cuticles; nail hardeners | Nail polish, remover, cuticle cream |
| **Scalp** | Dense follicles, high sebum, Malassezia | Shampoo to remove sebum, scales and styling residue | Conditioning, anti-dandruff, scalp tonics | Shampoo, conditioner, hair oil |
| **Neck** | Thin skin, sun-exposed, shows ageing | Like face | Moisturiser, sunscreen | Same as face products |
| **Body** | Fewer sebaceous glands on limbs; legs prone to dryness | Mild bath products, syndet bars | Body lotion, especially after bath | Soap/syndet, body wash, lotion |
| **Under-arm** | Apocrine glands, occlusion, hair, friction | Wash with mild cleanser | Control sweat and odour without irritation | Deodorant, antiperspirant |

## Remember

- Stratum corneum = bricks (corneocytes) and mortar (ceramides, cholesterol, free fatty acids); NMF from filaggrin keeps it hydrated; TEWL measures barrier.
- Acid mantle pH about 4.5–5.5 supports barrier enzymes and friendly microbes.
- Dry skin: lipid and NMF loss → abnormal desquamation → scaling.
- Acne: sebum ↑, follicular hyperkeratinisation, C. acnes, inflammation.
- Melanin: tyrosine → DOPA → dopaquinone (tyrosinase) → eumelanin or pheomelanin; UV → α-MSH → MC1R.
- Miliaria = blocked eccrine ducts: crystallina, rubra (prickly heat), profunda.
- Photoageing: UV → ROS → MMPs → collagen breakdown; glycation stiffens collagen.
- Body odour: apocrine secretion + skin bacteria → volatile fatty acids and thioalcohols.
- Hair: cuticle, cortex, medulla; disulphide, hydrogen and salt bonds; negative charge; anagen 2–7 years, catagen 2–3 weeks, telogen about 3 months.
- Dandruff = Malassezia + sebum; androgenetic alopecia = DHT miniaturisation.
- Caries below critical pH about 5.5; gingivitis from plaque; halitosis from volatile sulphur compounds; sensitivity by the hydrodynamic theory.

# How to use this subject

MPH201T is a **descriptive, comparison-heavy** paper. Almost nothing in it is numerical; marks are won by writing the right classification, the right method of preparation in ordered steps, the right evaluation table and two or three real examples. Study one unit at a time: read the notes, then close them and write out the method of preparation and the evaluation table from memory, then answer the question bank for that unit. Finish with the revision sheet the night before.

**Exam pattern.** The PCI M.Pharm scheme gives each theory course 100 marks — **25 internal assessment + 75 end-semester, 3 hours**. The PCI syllabus does not print a section-wise pattern for M.Pharm, so the mock paper here follows the common 75-mark split used in pharmacy papers (and adopted for M.Pharm by, for example, Bahra University, Shimla). **Confirm the exact split with your own university.**

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / multiple-choice questions | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

The pack also has a 20-question, 20-minute quick MCQ test for daily practice.

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — the topic carries real syllabus weight and this kind of question keeps appearing; prepare the full answer with a diagram, a classification table and examples.
- **Medium** — asked every few years, or as one part of a larger question.
- **Low** — rare as a full question, but worth knowing for the objective section and as a backup choice.

These are estimates based on syllabus weight and common question patterns, not a promise of what will be set.

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. **Open with a definition.** Every carrier question should begin with a one-sentence definition and a labelled text diagram of the structure. Aquasome, liposome, microcapsule vs microsphere and the aerosol container are the four diagrams worth being able to draw in under a minute.
2. **Answer "preparation" as numbered steps**, not as a paragraph — materials, the step sequence, what controls particle size, and the finishing steps (separation of free drug, sizing, sterilisation, freeze-drying).
3. **Keep one evaluation table in your head** — size and PDI (DLS), zeta potential, morphology (TEM/SEM), entrapment efficiency after separating free drug, physical state (DSC, XRD), in-vitro release, stability — and then add the two or three tests that are *special* to that carrier (lamellarity and lyso-PC for liposomes; cascade impactor for inhalers; ciliotoxicity for nasal; gel retardation and nuclease protection for gene systems).
4. **Comparisons earn easy marks.** Be ready with liposome vs niosome, microcapsule vs microsphere, phytosome vs liposome, viral vs non-viral vector, ex-vivo vs in-vivo, antisense vs siRNA, pMDI vs DPI vs nebuliser, and passive vs active targeting.
5. **Always finish an essay with applications and limitations.** Three or four real, named examples (liposomal amphotericin B, PEGylated liposomal doxorubicin, PLGA peptide depots, lactose-carrier DPIs, GalNAc–siRNA conjugates) lift an average answer.
6. **Learn the numbers that recur**: respirable size 1–5 µm; EPR window about 10–200 nm; renal cut-off about 6–8 nm; liver fenestrae about 100 nm; clathrin pits about 120 nm, caveolae about 60–80 nm; endosome pH about 5–6.5, lysosome about 4.5–5; DPPC transition about 41 °C; AAV capacity about 4.7 kb; nasal volume about 25–200 µL, area about 150 cm², clearance about 15–20 min; alveolar area about 70–100 m².
7. **Use the mechanism, not just the name.** Saying "PEGylation gives long circulation" is worth less than saying "the hydrated PEG layer sterically prevents opsonin adsorption, so macrophage recognition falls" — and mentioning the counter-points (ABC phenomenon, the PEG dilemma) marks a postgraduate answer.
8. In the objective section, watch for the standard traps: microcapsule (reservoir) versus microsphere (matrix); niosomes are **non-ionic surfactant**, not phospholipid; a phytosome is a **molecular complex**, not a vesicle; HAT medium selects **hybridomas**, not myeloma cells; adenovirus does **not** integrate; and the fine particle fraction is the mass **below 5 µm**.

**A note on the unit split.** The notes follow the five PCI units exactly, in the order given in the syllabus. Unit 3 is the largest in content (micro systems, monoclonal antibodies and four vesicular systems) and has been kept as one unit to match the syllabus rather than split, so plan extra reading time for it.

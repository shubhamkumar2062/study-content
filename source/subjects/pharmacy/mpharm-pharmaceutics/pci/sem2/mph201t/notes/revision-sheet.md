# Last-night revision sheet

If you have only an hour, learn these six with their diagrams: **the biological events in targeting**, **liposome preparation and active loading**, **the microencapsulation and hybridoma methods**, **the aerosol container with propellants and the 1–5 µm rule**, **the viral vector comparison table**, and **the standard evaluation list**. They cover most 10-mark questions in this paper.

## Sequences to write straight out

**Biological journey of an injected carrier** — protein corona and opsonisation → MPS capture (liver Kupffer cells, spleen) → steric PEG shielding (ABC phenomenon, PEG dilemma) → extravasation (continuous / fenestrated / sinusoidal / leaky tumour vessels) → ligand–receptor binding → endocytosis → endosome acidifies → escape (proton sponge, DOPE fusion) or lysosome → efflux (P-gp) and elimination.

**Coacervation** — three immiscible phases → deposition of coating → rigidisation. Simple (desolvating agent) vs complex (gelatin + acacia, pH about 4).

**Hybridoma** — immunise mouse → spleen B cells + HGPRT-deficient myeloma → PEG fusion → **HAT medium** (only hybridomas survive) → ELISA screening → limiting-dilution cloning → expansion → Protein A purification.

**SELEX** — random library → bind target → wash → elute → PCR amplify → enriched library → repeat 6–15 rounds → sequence and stabilise.

**Aerosol manufacture** — micronise and mix the concentrate → **cold filling or (preferred) pressure filling** → crimp valve → leak test in a 50–55 °C bath → spray test → label.

## Comparison tables the examiner likes

| Pair | One-line difference |
| --- | --- |
| Microcapsule vs microsphere | Reservoir (core + wall) vs matrix (drug dispersed in polymer) |
| Nanosphere vs nanocapsule | Matrix vs oily/aqueous core in a polymer shell |
| SLN vs NLC | Pure solid lipid vs solid + liquid lipid blend; NLC loads more and expels less on storage |
| Liposome vs niosome | Phospholipid vs non-ionic surfactant; niosome cheaper and chemically more stable |
| Liposome vs phytosome | Drug dissolved/dispersed in a vesicle vs phytoconstituent **hydrogen-bonded** to the phospholipid head group |
| Passive vs active targeting | Natural fate + EPR vs ligand–receptor recognition; active mainly improves **retention and uptake**, not total delivery |
| First / second / third order | Organ / cell type / intracellular compartment |
| CFC vs HFA propellant | Ozone-depleting, withdrawn vs HFA-134a and 227ea in current inhalers |
| pMDI vs DPI vs nebuliser | Propellant + coordination vs patient's inspiratory effort + lactose carrier vs tidal breathing over minutes |
| Two-phase vs three-phase aerosol | Solution vs suspension or emulsion (foam) |
| Ex vivo vs in vivo gene therapy | Cells modified outside and returned vs vector given to the patient |
| Retrovirus vs lentivirus | Dividing cells only vs non-dividing cells also |
| Adenovirus vs AAV | High efficiency, transient, immunogenic, 8 kb vs low immunogenicity, long expression, 4.7 kb |
| Viral vs non-viral vector | Efficient but immunogenic and limited in capacity vs safe, scalable, large capacity but inefficient |
| Lipoplex vs ionisable LNP | Permanently cationic (toxic, serum-sensitive) vs charged only at low pH, near-neutral in blood |
| Antisense vs siRNA | Single strand, RNase H / steric block / splice switching vs double strand acting through RISC and Argonaute-2 |
| Aptamer vs antibody | Chemically synthesised, small, non-immunogenic, reversible vs biological, large, immunogenic, better in-vivo stability |
| Cationic vs PEGylated surface | Good cell uptake, fast clearance vs long circulation, poorer uptake |

## Classifications to reproduce

- **Targeting methods**: passive, active (ligand, antibody), physical/stimulus (magnetic, thermo, pH, redox, ultrasound, light), inverse, dual, double, combination, cell-mediated, prodrug (ADEPT, GDEPT).
- **Ligands and their receptors**: folate → folate receptor-α; transferrin → transferrin receptor; galactose → asialoglycoprotein receptor (hepatocyte); mannose → macrophage mannose receptor; RGD → αvβ3 integrin; hyaluronic acid → CD44.
- **Liposomes**: by size/lamellarity (SUV, LUV, GUV, MLV, MVV) and by function (conventional, stealth, immuno-, cationic, pH-sensitive, thermosensitive, archaeosome, virosome, transfersome, ethosome).
- **Microspheres**: biodegradable polymeric, natural polymeric, non-biodegradable, magnetic, floating, radioactive, bioadhesive, immuno-microspheres.
- **mAb suffixes**: -omab murine, -ximab chimeric, -zumab humanised, -umab fully human.
- **Aquasome layers**: hydroxyapatite/ceramic core → polyhydroxy oligomer (cellobiose, trehalose) → adsorbed drug.
- **Aerosol parts**: propellant, container, valve (metering), actuator, product concentrate.
- **Nasal forms**: drops, metered spray, aerosol, gel/in-situ gel, powder, microspheres/nanoparticles, liposomes, inserts.
- **Non-viral methods**: physical (naked DNA, electroporation, gene gun, hydrodynamic, sonoporation, magnetofection) and chemical (cationic lipids, ionisable LNPs, PEI, chitosan, dendrimers, GalNAc conjugates).

## Evaluation checklist (adapt to any carrier)

Size and PDI (DLS) → zeta potential → morphology (TEM/SEM/AFM) → entrapment efficiency **after separating free drug** → drug–carrier interaction and physical state (FTIR, DSC, XRD) → in-vitro release with model fitting → stability on storage → sterility, endotoxin, residual solvent.

**Then add the carrier-specific tests**: lamellarity (³¹P-NMR) and lyso-PC for liposomes; floating lag time for floating microspheres; wash-off test for mucoadhesives; **cascade impactor, MMAD and fine particle fraction** plus delivered-dose uniformity for inhalers; spray pattern, droplet size, gelation temperature and **ciliotoxicity** for nasal; **gel retardation, nuclease protection, dye-exclusion encapsulation and reporter-gene transfection** for nucleic acid systems.

## Ten one-liners to carry into the hall

1. EPR = leaky angiogenic vessels + absent lymphatic drainage; limited in patients by heterogeneity, high interstitial fluid pressure and dense stroma.
2. PEG works by steric prevention of opsonin adsorption; its cost is the PEG dilemma and the ABC phenomenon.
3. Active loading by an ammonium sulfate or pH gradient is how liposomal doxorubicin gets above 90% entrapment.
4. Reverse-phase evaporation gives the highest aqueous entrapment among passive liposome methods.
5. Spray drying is the one-step industrial route to microspheres; ionotropic gelation is the mild aqueous one.
6. In HAT medium, unfused myeloma cells die because they lack HGPRT and aminopterin blocks the de-novo pathway.
7. A particle's aerodynamic diameter, not its geometric size, decides where it lands in the lung.
8. The nose-to-brain route bypasses the blood–brain barrier along olfactory and trigeminal nerve pathways.
9. For DNA the last barrier is the nuclear envelope — which is why mRNA and siRNA, which work in the cytoplasm, are far more efficient.
10. For nucleic acid therapeutics, judge the dose by the pharmacodynamic effect and its duration, not by plasma concentration.

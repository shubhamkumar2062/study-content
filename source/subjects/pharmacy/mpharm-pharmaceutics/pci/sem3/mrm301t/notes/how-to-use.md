# How to use this subject

Research Methodology and Biostatistics (MRM301T) is the one theory course of M.Pharm semester III and is common to every specialization. It runs alongside your dissertation work, journal club and proposal presentation, so study it with your own project in mind: the design, controls, randomization, sample size and statistical tests in your protocol are exactly what Units 1 and 2 teach, and your ethics committee or IAEC application draws on Units 3 to 5.

The course has two halves. **Units 1 and 2** reward method: design reasoning, bias control and numerical problems set out step by step. **Units 3, 4 and 5** reward accurate definitions, lists and guideline points — values of medical ethics, the CPCSEA facility requirements, and the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. If you studied BP801T in B.Pharm, Unit 2 will feel familiar; here the focus moves to choosing the right test, sample size, and interpreting P values and confidence intervals.

## Units and how they map to the PCI syllabus

The five units follow the five units of the PCI MRM 301T syllabus exactly. No unit is split or merged.

| Unit | PCI syllabus unit | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | General research methodology | Research, objectives, requirements, practical difficulties, review of literature, study design and types of studies, bias, controls, randomization, crossover design, placebo, blinding. Measures such as relative risk, odds ratio and NNT are added because study-design questions often ask for them |
| 2 | Biostatistics | The longest unit: sample size and dropouts, tests of significance, parametric and non-parametric tests, null hypothesis, P values, degrees of freedom. Friedman test and Kendall's τ are added as the related-samples and rank-correlation members of the non-parametric family |
| 3 | Medical research (medical ethics) | Follows the syllabus list in order, with Indian law and guidelines (ICMR 2017, New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules 2019, Supreme Court rulings on euthanasia). "Fatality" in the syllabus list is treated as futility of treatment, the standard heading in medical ethics |
| 4 | CPCSEA guidelines for laboratory animal facility | Guideline level: committees, 3Rs, facility, environment, husbandry, records, SOPs, personnel, transport, and the principles of anaesthesia and euthanasia. The committee was renamed CCSEA in 2023; examiners may use either name |
| 5 | Declaration of Helsinki | History and revisions to 2024, introduction, basic principles for all research, additional principles for research combined with care |

## Exam pattern

The course carries **100 marks: 25 internal assessment + 75 end-semester**, and the end-semester examination lasts **3 hours**. In the PCI scheme MRM301T is marked as a **"Non-University Exam"**: your **college conducts and evaluates** this examination itself, with the same marks, instead of the university. PCI does not prescribe a section-wise pattern for M.Pharm, so most colleges follow the PCI B.Pharm 75-mark split shown below. Confirm the exact pattern with your college.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ, all compulsory | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |
| | Total | 75 |

The question bank has long (10-mark), short (5-mark) and very short (2-mark) questions for every unit. The mock paper uses the pattern above, and a 20-question, 20-minute MCQ test is included for quick practice. Because the paper is set within your college, look at your department's previous papers too: some colleges give more numericals, others more theory.

## Probability ratings

- **High** — a frequent question type for this syllabus topic; prepare a full answer, with a worked numerical or a table where one fits.
- **Medium** — appears every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare; useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

The ratings are estimates from syllabus weight and common question patterns in PCI-syllabus pharmacy papers. They are not a promise of what will be asked.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. **Numericals:** write data table → H0 and H1 → formula → substitution → df → table value → decision → conclusion in words. Most marks are for the method; show each step. Carry the common table values: t(0.05) = 2.571 (df 5), 2.447 (df 6), 2.365 (df 7), 2.262 (df 9), 2.228 (df 10), 2.145 (df 14); χ²(0.05) = 3.84 (1 df), 5.99 (2 df); z = 1.96 (α 0.05) and 0.84 (power 80%).
2. **Always add the effect size and its confidence interval** to a significant or non-significant result. Examiners at postgraduate level look for correct interpretation of P values ("not significant" is not "no difference").
3. **Design questions:** answer with a labelled flow diagram (parallel RCT, 2 × 2 crossover, case–control vs cohort) and a comparison table.
4. **Bias questions:** for each bias give what it is, an example from pharmacy research, and the prevention (randomization, concealment, blinding, ITT, registration).
5. **Ethics questions:** structure answers by principle (autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice), add one Indian example (ICMR 2017, NDCT Rules 2019, Supreme Court rulings) and one historical landmark.
6. **CPCSEA questions:** list the headings of the guidelines in order (goals → veterinary care → quarantine → surveillance → hygiene → location → facilities → environment → husbandry → records → SOPs → personnel → transport → anaesthesia and euthanasia) and give two or three points under each; numbers to remember: quarantine about 1 week (small) and 6 weeks (large), 18–29 °C, RH 30–70%.
7. **Helsinki questions:** start with the 1964 adoption and the key revisions (1975, 2000, 2008, 2013, 2024), then the principles under headings. The placebo paragraph and post-trial provisions are favourite short notes.
8. Link theory to your own dissertation in long answers ("in a bioequivalence study of my test formulation…"); it shows understanding and earns credit in a college-set paper.

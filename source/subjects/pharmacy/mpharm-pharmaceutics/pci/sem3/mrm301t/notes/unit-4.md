# Unit 4: CPCSEA guidelines for laboratory animal facility

Many M.Pharm projects need animals — pharmacokinetic studies of a new formulation, in vivo evaluation of a nanocarrier, skin irritation testing of a gel. In India this work is controlled by law and by the guidelines of a national committee. This unit covers the guidelines for the laboratory animal facility at the level an examiner expects: purpose, committees, the facility, the environment, husbandry, records, SOPs, personnel, transport, and the ethical principles behind anaesthesia and euthanasia. Technical procedures (drugs, doses, surgical steps) are learnt under supervision and are not part of these notes.

## 4.1 Legal framework

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Parent law | **Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (PCA) Act, 1960**. Section 15 provides for a committee to control and supervise experiments on animals |
| Committee | **CPCSEA** — Committee for the Purpose of Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals. Renamed **CCSEA** (Committee for Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals) by an order of January 2023; functions and powers unchanged. It works under the Department of Animal Husbandry and Dairying, Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry and Dairying |
| Rules | **Breeding of and Experiments on Animals (Control and Supervision) Rules, 1998** (amended later) |
| Guidelines | **CPCSEA Guidelines for Laboratory Animal Facility** — the subject of this unit — plus SOPs for IAECs, forms and species-specific annexures |
| Objective | To ensure that animals are not subjected to unnecessary pain or suffering before, during or after experiments |
| "Animal" | Under the PCA Act, any living creature other than a human being; in practice the regulation covers vertebrates (invertebrates do not need IAEC approval, although journals may ask for it) |

**Main duties of the committee:**

1. Registration of establishments that breed animals, trade in them or conduct experiments (including contract research).
2. Constituting the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC) of each registered establishment and nominating its outside members.
3. Approving experiments on animals above rodents (dogs, cats, pigs, sheep, goats, cattle, monkeys) on the recommendation of the IAEC.
4. Laying down standards for housing, care and experimentation; inspecting facilities.
5. Promoting alternatives and humane methods; taking action against non-compliance.

## 4.2 The 3Rs and the ethical basis

The ethical basis for all animal work is the **3Rs** proposed by W. M. S. Russell and R. L. Burch (1959):

| R | Meaning | Examples in pharmaceutics research |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Replacement** | Avoid animals where a non-animal method answers the question | Cell lines (Caco-2 for permeability), excised tissue from slaughterhouses, in vitro release and permeation (Franz cell with synthetic membranes), computer models, chick chorioallantoic membrane in some studies |
| **Reduction** | Use the minimum number that gives a valid answer | Proper sample size calculation, good design (crossover, shared controls), pilot studies, avoiding duplicate work by literature review, sharing data and tissues |
| **Refinement** | Minimise pain, distress and lasting harm; improve welfare | Anaesthesia and analgesia, humane end points, training animals for handling, enrichment, skilled staff, less invasive sampling |

Some guidelines add a fourth R: **Responsibility** towards the animal, and in India the committee has also stressed **rehabilitation** (aftercare) of large animals after experiments. Before any study the investigator must justify why animals are necessary, why the chosen species is appropriate, and why the number is the minimum.

## 4.3 Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC)

Every establishment registered for animal experiments has an IAEC, constituted and registered with the committee, generally for five years; at reconstitution at least half the members are replaced.

**Composition (members from the establishment):**

1. A biological scientist.
2. Two scientists from different biological disciplines.
3. A veterinarian involved in the care of animals (mandatory).
4. The scientist in charge of the animal house facility.

The chairperson (preferably the head of the institution or department) and the member secretary are chosen from these members.

**Members nominated by the national committee:** a main nominee, a link nominee, a scientist from outside the institute, and a socially aware nominee.

**Functions of the IAEC:**

- Review and approve research protocols submitted in the prescribed form (**Form B**), checking justification, species, numbers, procedures, pain management, end points and the qualification of the investigators.
- Approve experiments up to the level of rodents (rats, mice, rabbits, birds); for larger animals, recommend and forward the proposal to the national committee.
- Approval is normally for one year, renewed after reviewing progress.
- Inspect the animal house and experiments; ensure compliance; suspend work that violates approval.
- Maintain minutes and records of animal use, which are reported to the national committee.

Where hazardous agents (infectious organisms, radioactive or toxic substances, genetically engineered organisms) are used, the protocol and facilities are reviewed by both the **Institutional Biosafety Committee** and the IAEC.

## 4.4 Goals of the guidelines

The guidelines aim to promote the **humane care of animals used in biomedical and behavioural research and testing**, by providing specifications that:

- enhance animal well-being;
- improve the quality of research, because healthy, unstressed animals give reliable, reproducible data;
- protect the health and safety of personnel;
- support Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) in animal facilities.

## 4.5 Veterinary care

- Adequate veterinary care is the responsibility of a **veterinarian** or a person trained and experienced in laboratory animal science and medicine (full-time, part-time or consultant depending on size).
- Daily observation can be done by trained animal house staff, with a direct channel to report problems to the veterinarian.
- The veterinarian also helps set policies on husbandry, nutrition, sanitation, zoonosis control and occupational health, and reviews protocols.

**Animal procurement:** animals must be acquired **lawfully** from registered breeders or suppliers; each consignment is inspected against specifications; a health surveillance programme screens incoming animals; the method of transport is considered.

## 4.6 Quarantine, stabilisation and separation

| Step | Purpose | Key points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Quarantine** | Separate newly received animals until their health (and microbial status) is known; prevents introduction of disease into the colony | Minimum about **one week for small laboratory animals** and **six weeks for larger animals** (cat, dog, monkey). Non-human primates need strict quarantine because of zoonotic infections (for example tuberculosis) |
| **Stabilisation (acclimatisation)** | Allows physiological, psychological and nutritional recovery from transport stress before use | Length depends on the species, transport and the intended use |
| **Separation** | Prevents cross-species infection, anxiety and behavioural changes | Different species in separate rooms; or cubicles, laminar-flow units, filtered cages, isolators. Two species may share a room only if their pathogen status is similar and they are compatible |

## 4.7 Surveillance, diagnosis, treatment and control of disease

- All animals are observed for illness, injury or abnormal behaviour, as a rule **daily**, and more often after surgery or when ill.
- Methods for surveillance and diagnosis must be in place (clinical observation, haematology and biochemistry reference values, microbiological monitoring, post-mortem examination of unexpected deaths).
- Unexpected deaths and signs of illness or distress are reported promptly to the veterinarian.
- Animals with a contagious disease are **isolated** from healthy ones. If a whole room is exposed to an infectious agent, the group is kept intact and isolated during diagnosis, treatment and control.
- Treatment must be compatible with the experiment; otherwise the animal is withdrawn from the study or humanely killed.

## 4.8 Personal hygiene and health of personnel

- Staff maintain a high standard of personal cleanliness; showers, change rooms and lockers are provided.
- Institution supplies and launders clothing; clothing exposed to hazardous agents is decontaminated in-house. Disposable gloves, masks, caps, coats and shoe covers are used where appropriate.
- **Outer garments worn in animal rooms are not worn outside** the facility.
- **No eating, drinking, smoking or applying cosmetics** in animal rooms; a separate area is provided.
- Regular medical check-up of workers to detect zoonotic infections (and to ensure they are not a source of infection to animals); vaccination, particularly against tetanus and other relevant zoonoses.
- Precautions against laboratory animal allergy and bites.

## 4.9 Location of animal facilities in relation to laboratories

- Animal housing is **separated from personnel areas** (offices, conference rooms, most laboratories) for good husbandry and for human comfort and health.
- Ideally an isolated building away from human habitation and not exposed to dust, smoke, noise, wild rodents, insects and birds.
- Separation may be a separate building, wing, floor or room, placed near laboratories but divided by barriers such as entry locks, corridors or floors.
- Animal rooms should occupy roughly **50–60% of the constructed area**; the rest serves stores, washing, office and staff areas, machine rooms, quarantine and corridors.
- Small-animal experiments are done in a separate area away from where animals are housed. Aseptic surgery on larger animals needs a surgical support area, preparation area, operating room and post-operative intensive care area.

**Functional areas** (according to size): quarantine and isolation; species-separated animal rooms; procedure, surgery, necropsy and diagnostic laboratories; containment areas for hazardous agents; stores for feed, bedding and supplies; cage-washing and sterilisation area with autoclave; separate soiled and clean equipment areas; cage repair; waste storage before incineration; refrigerated storage for carcasses; administration; staff showers and toilets.

## 4.10 Physical facilities

| Feature | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Building materials | Durable, moisture-proof, fire-resistant, seamless, vermin- and pest-resistant |
| Corridors | Wide enough for people and equipment; kept clean |
| Utilities | Pipes and electrical lines reached through service panels or shafts outside animal rooms |
| Doors | Rust-, vermin- and dust-proof, well-fitted, with observation window; rodent barriers in small-animal areas |
| Windows | Not recommended for small-animal rooms (may be needed where power failures are frequent); can be provided for primates |
| Floors | Smooth, moisture-proof, non-absorbent, skid-proof, resistant to wear, acids, solvents and disinfectants, few joints |
| Drains | Not essential in rodent rooms (wet vacuum or mopping); where present, floors slope and traps are kept sealed |
| Walls and ceilings | Free of cracks and gaps; withstand scrubbing, disinfectants and high-pressure water |
| Storage | Separate stores for feed, bedding and cages; separate refrigerated storage for carcasses and tissue waste |
| Sanitising facility | Cage-washing area with adequate water supply |

## 4.11 Environment

The **macro-environment** (room) and **micro-environment** (cage) both affect the animal's physiology and therefore the experimental results; sharp fluctuations must be avoided.

| Parameter | Guideline |
| --- | --- |
| Temperature | Facility should be able to hold temperatures within about **18–29 °C**, covering the ranges recommended for common laboratory species; a comfortable zone of about 18–37 °C for larger animals in extreme summer |
| Relative humidity | Controllable within **30–70%** throughout the year |
| Ventilation | Designed for the animals' primary enclosures; heating, ventilating and air-conditioning with a **standby system**; animal areas ventilated separately from human areas |
| Lighting | Adequate light for staff work and lower intensity for animals; **time-controlled lighting** to keep a regular day–night (diurnal) cycle; emergency power |
| Noise | As noise-free as possible; concrete walls contain noise better than metal or plaster |

## 4.12 Animal husbandry

**Caging (housing):** space adequate for freedom of movement and normal postures (species tables in the annexure), comfortable, escape-proof, easy access to food and water, adequate ventilation, keeps animals dry and clean, allows observation without disturbance. Small laboratory animals are housed in polypropylene, polycarbonate or stainless-steel cages with smooth surfaces and no sharp edges.

**Sheltered or outdoor housing** (larger animals): protection from weather, escape routes for submissive animals, drainage.

**Social environment:** group housing for social species where appropriate; stable groups; attention to density, age, sex and rank. **Activity:** space and devices for species-typical movement (perches and ropes for primates; exercise runs for dogs held long term).

**Food:** palatable, uncontaminated, nutritionally adequate diet given daily unless the protocol requires otherwise; not made or stored with farm feeds or pesticides; stored in clean, vermin-proof areas; free from heavy metals and toxins.

**Bedding:** absorbent, non-toxic, not readily eaten; changed as often as needed to keep animals clean and dry (ideally twice a week for rodents); qualities such as ammonia binding, sterilisability and dust-free nature; nesting material for litters.

**Water:** continuous access to fresh, potable, uncontaminated water; devices checked routinely; microbial quality monitored; bottles replaced rather than topped up.

**Sanitation:** rooms and equipment cleaned with suitable detergents and disinfectants; cleaning tools not moved between rooms; cages sanitised before use and washed regularly (hot-water rinse or chemical disinfection); efficacy checked microbiologically.

**Pest control** and **emergency, weekend and holiday care** by qualified personnel, with posted emergency contacts and a disaster plan covering both people and animals.

**Other welfare rules in the guidelines:** brief physical restraint only with suitable devices, after training the animal, and for the minimum time; multiple surgical procedures on one animal only if specifically approved by the IAEC; an animal should not be used for experiments for more than three years without justification.

## 4.13 Record keeping

The animal house maintains:

1. Animal house plans (floor plan, fixtures).
2. Staff records (technical and non-technical).
3. Health records of staff and animals.
4. All SOPs relevant to the animals.
5. Breeding, stock, purchase and sale records.
6. Minutes of IAEC meetings.
7. Records of experiments with the number of animals used (copy of **Form D**, the record of animal use).
8. Death records.
9. Clinical records of sick animals.
10. Training records of staff.
11. Water analysis reports.

Records support traceability, inspections and GLP compliance, and show that the approved numbers were not exceeded. Records of the disposal of transgenic and euthanised large animals are also kept.

## 4.14 Standard operating procedures (SOPs)

The institute keeps SOPs for husbandry, maintenance, breeding, microbial analysis of the animal house and experimentation records. Each SOP contains:

- name of author, title and date of preparation;
- reference to the previous SOP on the subject (issue number and date);
- location and distribution, with signature of each recipient;
- objectives;
- details of instruments (model, serial number, date of commissioning) and methodology;
- manufacturer of reagents and method of analysis;
- normal values of parameters;
- hazard identification and risk assessment.

## 4.15 Personnel and training

- Careful selection of staff, especially those in animal rooms or transport.
- Protective clothing (masks, aprons, gloves, gumboots) and change facilities provided.
- Regular medical check-ups and vaccination (section 4.8).
- **Initial in-house training** of newly recruited staff for some weeks: handling, cage cleaning, hygiene, disinfection and sterilisation, and recognising normal and sick animals during daily checks.
- Investigators must be trained and competent in the procedures approved by the IAEC; training records are maintained. Continuing education in laboratory animal science is encouraged.

## 4.16 Transport of laboratory animals

Main considerations: **mode of transport, containers, animal density, food and water in transit, protection from infection, injury and stress**.

- Mode (road, rail or air) chosen according to distance, season, climate and species.
- Containers of suitable size and design allowing comfortable movement and protection; only the permissible number of animals per container to avoid overcrowding and fighting.
- Adequate food and especially water (or water-rich food) during transit.
- Transport stress minimised; animals inspected and allowed to stabilise on arrival (section 4.6).

## 4.17 Anaesthesia and euthanasia: guideline principles

**Anaesthesia and analgesia:**

- Procedures likely to cause pain are carried out under **appropriate anaesthesia recommended for the species**, maintained for the full duration so the animal never perceives pain.
- Sedatives, analgesics and anaesthetics are used to control pain and distress unless this would defeat the purpose of the study — and that exception must be justified to and approved by the IAEC.
- **Neuromuscular blocking agents are never used without adequate general anaesthesia.**
- Choice of agent depends on species and procedure and is made with the veterinarian; side effects are prevented and the animal stays under veterinary care until fully recovered from anaesthesia and post-operative stress.
- If the investigator must abandon an experiment, or irreparable injury has occurred, the animal is humanely killed.

**Euthanasia:**

- Used at the end of an experiment or for ethical reasons (humane end points, unrelieved suffering).
- Must be carried out quickly and painlessly in an atmosphere free from fear and anxiety, by a trained person, using a **method approved in the guidelines for that species** and by the IAEC.
- A humane method first depresses the central nervous system to give immediate insensitivity to pain.
- Requirements: death without anxiety, pain or distress with minimum lag time; minimum physiological and psychological disturbance; compatibility with the study purpose; minimum emotional effect on the operator; done in a place separate from animal rooms and free of contaminants.
- Larger species are tranquillised first.
- The investigator confirms that the animal is dead before disposal; disposal (usually incineration) and records follow the guidelines.

## 4.18 Transgenic animals and breeding

- **Transgenic** animals carry foreign genes in the germ line; **knockout** animals have specific genes disrupted. They are housed like others of the species but with extra care (clean rooms or isolators), must never be released into the wild, are euthanised before disposal, and disposal is recorded.
- Breeding stock is obtained from registered breeders with known genetic make-up and health status (conventional, specific pathogen free, gnotobiotic).

## 4.19 Remember

- Law: PCA Act 1960 (Section 15) → Breeding of and Experiments on Animals (Control and Supervision) Rules 1998 → CPCSEA, renamed CCSEA in 2023.
- 3Rs (Russell and Burch, 1959): Replacement, Reduction, Refinement (+ Responsibility).
- IAEC: biological scientist, two scientists of different disciplines, veterinarian (mandatory), scientist in charge of animal house + four nominees (main, link, outside scientist, socially aware). Protocol on Form B; animal use recorded in Form D; IAEC approves up to rodents; larger animals need the national committee.
- Quarantine: about 1 week small animals, 6 weeks larger animals; then stabilisation; species kept separate.
- Environment: 18–29 °C, RH 30–70%, standby HVAC, separate ventilation, time-controlled light cycle, low noise.
- Records: plans, staff, health, SOPs, breeding/stock, IAEC minutes, Form D, deaths, clinical, training, water analysis.
- No eating, drinking or smoking in animal rooms; outer garments stay inside; medical check-ups and vaccination.
- Anaesthesia for painful procedures; never paralyse without anaesthesia; euthanasia only by approved, humane methods by trained people.

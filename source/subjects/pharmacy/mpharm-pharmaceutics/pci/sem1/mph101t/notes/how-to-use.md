# How to use this subject

Modern Pharmaceutical Analytical Techniques (MPH101T) is the analytical foundation of the M.Pharm Pharmaceutics programme. You will use these methods throughout your research: UV for dissolution and assay, FTIR and XRD for drug–excipient compatibility and solid dispersions, HPLC for every stability study, NMR and mass spectrometry for identifying degradation products, and ELISA or bioluminescence for biologics and cell work. Examiners at postgraduate level expect more than a block diagram: explain *why* each component is chosen, write the governing equation, compare related techniques, and end with pharmaceutical (preferably formulation) applications.

## Units and how they map to the PCI syllabus

The six units follow the six units of the PCI MPH 101T syllabus exactly (60 hours: five units of 11 hours and one of 5 hours).

| Unit | PCI syllabus unit | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | UV-visible, IR, spectrofluorimetry, flame emission and AAS | Four techniques in one syllabus unit, so these notes are the longest. Study it as four parts (A to D). |
| 2 | NMR spectroscopy | Theory-heavy; learn chemical shift factors, coupling and FT-NMR thoroughly. |
| 3 | Mass spectrometry | Ionisation sources and fragmentation rules are the high-scoring areas. |
| 4 | Chromatography | Seven techniques plus common theory (parameters, resolution, van Deemter). |
| 5 | Electrophoresis and X-ray crystallography | Two topics joined in one syllabus unit. |
| 6 | Immunological assays (RIA, ELISA, bioluminescence) | Only 5 hours in the syllabus; usually short-answer material. |

Topics such as thermal analysis (DSC, TGA), potentiometry and hyphenated techniques are not in the MPH101T list; they were covered in the B.Pharm courses BP701T and BP811ET. Use those packs if your university's paper strays into them.

## Exam pattern

The paper carries **100 marks: 25 internal assessment + 75 end-semester**, and the end-semester examination lasts **3 hours**. PCI does not prescribe a section-wise pattern for M.Pharm, so most universities follow the PCI B.Pharm 75-mark split shown below (used for M.Pharm by, for example, Bahra University, Shimla). Confirm the exact pattern with your university.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ, all compulsory | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |
| | Total | 75 |

The question bank has long (10-mark), short (5-mark) and very short (2-mark) questions for every unit. The mock paper in this pack draws on the same pattern, and a 20-question, 20-minute MCQ test is included for quick practice.

## Probability ratings

- **High** — a frequent question type for this syllabus topic; prepare a full answer with diagram and equations.
- **Medium** — appears every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare; useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

The ratings are estimates from syllabus weight and common question patterns in PCI-syllabus pharmacy papers. They are not a promise of what will be asked.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. Use one plan for every technique question: **principle (with equation) → instrumentation (block diagram) → working conditions / factors → advantages and limitations → applications**.
2. Write equations with every symbol defined: A = εbc; ν̄ = (1/2πc)√(k/µ); F0/F = 1 + K_SV[Q]; ν0 = γB0/2π; δ = Δν(Hz)/ν(MHz); m/z = B²r²e/2V; t ∝ √(m/z); m* = m2²/m1; Rs = (√N/4)((α − 1)/α)(k/(1 + k)); H = A + B/u + Cu; µ = q/6πηr; nλ = 2d sin θ.
3. Comparison questions are favourite short answers — answer them as tables: FES vs AAS, dispersive vs FTIR, fluorescence vs phosphorescence, CW vs FT-NMR, ¹H vs ¹³C NMR, EI vs CI, ESI vs APCI vs MALDI, quadrupole vs TOF, GC vs HPLC, normal vs reversed phase, SDS-PAGE vs native PAGE, zone vs moving boundary, RIA vs ELISA.
4. Learn the standard numbers: Na 589 nm, K 766.5 nm; Cu Kα 1.5406 Å; TMS 0 ppm, CDCl3 7.26 ppm; 9.4 T = 400 MHz; ¹³C 1.1%; Cl 3:1 and Br 1:1 isotope ratios; EI at 70 eV; Rs ≥ 1.5; TMB read at 450 nm.
5. At M.Pharm level, add one or two **formulation applications** to every answer (dissolution by UV, compatibility by FTIR/DSC/XRD, cyclodextrin inclusion by NMR, nanoparticle drug release by HPLC, polymer MW by MALDI or SEC, protein formulation stability by SDS-PAGE). This lifts an average answer to a good one.
6. Practise the worked calculations in the notes (absorbance to concentration, Hooke's law, Hz ↔ ppm, isotope ratios, metastable ion, N, k, α, Rs, Bragg spacing, mobility).

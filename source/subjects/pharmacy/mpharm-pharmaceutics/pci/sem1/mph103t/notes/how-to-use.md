# How to use this subject

Modern Pharmaceutics (MPH 103T) is the industrial core of the first semester of M.Pharm (Pharmaceutics). It builds on B.Pharm Industrial Pharmacy I (BP502T: preformulation, tablets, parenterals) and Industrial Pharmacy II (BP702T: scale-up, quality management, regulatory). At M.Pharm level examiners expect mechanisms, equations, worked numericals and the guideline behind each practice (ICH Q1A(R2), Q2(R2), Q8–Q10, Schedule M, WHO, US FDA).

## How the units map to the syllabus

The PCI syllabus has five numbered items, with item 1 split into 1(a) and 1(b), each worth 10 hours. These notes follow that split, so there are six units:

| Unit | Syllabus item | Topics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1(a) | Preformulation, drug–excipient interactions, stability kinetics and ICH stability testing, dispersion theory, emulsions, suspensions, SMEDDS, SVP and LVP |
| 2 | 1(b) | Optimization: OFAT vs DoE, factorial, CCD, Box–Behnken, mixture designs, RSM, contour plots, applications |
| 3 | 2 | Validation: VMP, calibration, URS–DQ–IQ–OQ–PQ, types, regulations, dosage-form validation |
| 4 | 3 | cGMP, layout, services, maintenance, production and materials management, inventory, PPC, forecasting, budgets, industrial relations, TQM |
| 5 | 4 | Physics of compression, consolidation, friction, force distribution, compaction profiles, solubility |
| 6 | 5 | Heckel, Kawakita, diffusion, dissolution (f1, f2, Higuchi, Peppas), PK parameters, statistics (SD, t-test, chi-square, ANOVA) |

Study one unit every two days: read the notes, rework every worked example with a calculator without looking, then answer the question bank. Each unit has very short (2-mark), short (5-mark) and long (10-mark) questions.

## Exam pattern

Theory paper: **100 marks = 25 internal assessment + 75 end-semester examination (3 hours)**. PCI does not prescribe a section pattern for M.Pharm papers, so the mock paper here uses the common PCI B.Pharm 75-mark split, which several universities also use for M.Pharm (for example Bahra University, Shimla). Confirm the exact pattern with your university.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

There is also a quick 20-question, 20-minute MCQ test for daily practice.

## Probability ratings

- **High** — appears in most question papers of this kind; prepare a full structured answer with equations, a diagram and a worked number.
- **Medium** — appears every few years or as part of a larger question.
- **Low** — rare; useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from syllabus weight and common question patterns, not a promise of what will be asked.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. Open every essay with a crisp definition (and the guideline it comes from), then a labelled diagram or flow chart, then the equation, then applications.
2. Numericals carry easy marks: write the formula, substitute with units, box the answer, add a one-line interpretation ("f2 = 63.9 → similar"). Practise the eight types in this pack: Arrhenius shelf life, factorial effects, Cpk, EOQ, tensile strength, Heckel Py, f1/f2, and t-test/ANOVA.
3. Learn the fixed numbers exactly: ICH conditions (25/60, 30/65, 40/75; India zone IVb 30/75), t90 = 0.105/k, f2 50–100, f1 0–15, BE limits 80.00–125.00%, Peppas n 0.45/0.89 for cylinders, rotatable α 1.414 (k = 2), BBD 15 runs for 3 factors, Six Sigma 3.4 DPMO, Cpk ≥ 1.33.
4. Draw: DLVO energy curve, V-model of qualification, Heckel plot with A, B, C types, force–displacement curve with work areas, contour plot, layout flow of a tablet block, PDCA cycle.
5. For validation and GMP answers, name the regulation (revised Schedule M, 21 CFR 211, EU Annex 15, WHO TRS, ICH Q7–Q10) — postgraduate answers are expected to be regulation-aware.
6. Link topics across units: DoE (Unit 2) builds the design space that validation (Unit 3) confirms; compaction profiles (Unit 5) give the data for Heckel analysis (Unit 6); f2 and ANOVA (Unit 6) are the statistics behind scale-up and post-approval changes.

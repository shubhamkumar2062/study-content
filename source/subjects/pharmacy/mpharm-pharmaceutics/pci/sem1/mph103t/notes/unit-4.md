# Unit 4: cGMP and industrial management

Syllabus item 3: objectives and policies of current good manufacturing practices; layout of buildings, services, equipment and their maintenance; production management — production organisation, materials management, handling and transportation, inventory management and control, production planning and control, sales forecasting, budget and cost control, industrial and personal relationships; concept of Total Quality Management.

## 4.1 Current good manufacturing practices (cGMP)

**GMP** is that part of quality assurance which ensures that products are consistently produced and controlled to the quality standards appropriate to their intended use and as required by the marketing authorisation. The word **"current"** (cGMP, the US term) stresses that firms must use technologies and systems that are up to date; what was acceptable ten years ago may not be acceptable today.

### Objectives of cGMP

1. Every batch has the right identity, strength, quality and purity.
2. Prevent **mix-ups** (wrong label, wrong material), **cross-contamination** and **contamination** (microbial, particulate, chemical).
3. Prevent errors by clear written procedures, training and records ("if it is not written down, it did not happen").
4. Build quality in through validated processes, qualified equipment and controlled environments.
5. Provide traceability: any batch can be traced from raw material to the patient (for recall).
6. Protect the patient and meet legal requirements of the licence.

### Policies (core GMP principles)

| Principle | Practice |
| --- | --- |
| Quality management | Pharmaceutical quality system (ICH Q10), quality risk management (ICH Q9), management review, product quality review |
| Personnel | Qualified, trained staff; clear responsibilities; health checks; hygiene and gowning |
| Premises and equipment | Designed, located and maintained to suit operations and prevent contamination |
| Documentation | Specifications, master formula, batch manufacturing record, SOPs, logbooks; good documentation practice and data integrity (ALCOA+) |
| Production | Following approved procedures; in-process controls; line clearance; segregation |
| Quality control | Independent QC; sampling, testing, release by authorised person; stability programme; retained samples |
| Complaints and recalls | Written procedure; investigation; recall effectiveness |
| Self-inspection and audits | Internal audits, vendor audits, corrective and preventive action (CAPA) |
| Change control, deviations | Formal evaluation of any change or departure from procedure |

**Regulatory sources:** India — Schedule M of the Drugs Rules, 1945 (revised Schedule M notified December 2023, aligned with WHO GMP); WHO GMP; US — 21 CFR Parts 210 and 211; EU GMP (EudraLex Volume 4); PIC/S GMP guide; ICH Q7 for active pharmaceutical ingredients.

## 4.2 Layout of buildings

### Location and general design

- Away from sources of pollution (open drains, refuse dumps, factories emitting dust or fumes); adequate water and power.
- Building designed for **logical, unidirectional flow** of materials and personnel to avoid mix-ups and cross-contamination: receipt → quarantine → sampling → approved storage → dispensing → manufacture → packing → quarantine of finished goods → release → dispatch.
- Separate areas for: receiving, quarantine, sampling (under an LAF or sampling booth), approved materials, rejected and recalled goods (locked), dispensing, production of each dosage form, packing, QC laboratory (microbiology separate), utilities, change rooms, toilets (not opening directly into production), canteen, and waste.
- Surfaces: smooth, impervious, crack-free, easily cleanable walls, floors and ceilings; coved junctions; no uncleanable ledges; flush light fittings; sealed false ceilings in clean areas.
- **Dedicated and self-contained facilities** for highly sensitising materials (penicillins, cephalosporins), certain hormones, cytotoxics and live biologicals, with separate air handling.
- **Airlocks and pressure cascades:** clean areas kept at higher pressure than surroundings (typically a differential of 10–15 Pa between adjacent classified areas); containment areas for potent dusty products kept at lower pressure or using sink/bubble airlocks.

### Typical tablet block flow

```text
Material entry → Airlock → Dispensing (RLAF/down-flow booth) → Granulation (RMG)
→ Drying (FBD) → Sizing → Blending → Compression cubicles (each closed, with dust
extraction) → Coating → In-process QC → Primary packing (blister/strip)
→ Secondary packing → Finished goods quarantine → Release → Dispatch
Personnel: Entry → change room 1 (street clothes off) → change room 2 (factory
garments, hand wash) → airlock → production corridor
```

### Sterile product facility

Grades A (filling zone, unidirectional airflow), B (background for aseptic preparation and filling), C and D (less critical stages), with stepped gowning rooms, pass boxes, and separate washing, sterilisation, preparation, filling and sealing rooms.

## 4.3 Services (utilities)

| Service | GMP requirements |
| --- | --- |
| **HVAC** | Filtration (pre-filters, fine filters, HEPA for clean areas), temperature and relative humidity control, air changes, pressure differentials, dust extraction, separate AHUs for different blocks; qualified and monitored |
| **Water systems** | Potable water → purified water (RO, EDI, UV, ion-exchange) → water for injections (distillation, or equivalent methods allowed by the pharmacopoeia); stored hot (above about 70–80 °C) or ozonated, continuously recirculated loops, sanitary SS 316L piping, no dead legs; routine TOC, conductivity and microbial testing |
| **Compressed air and nitrogen** | Oil-free, dry, filtered at the point of use where in product contact |
| **Steam** | Plant steam for heating; clean/pure steam for sterilisation, made from purified water |
| **Electricity, lighting** | Adequate lighting (inspection areas especially); emergency power for critical systems |
| **Drainage** | Trapped drains with air breaks; no open drains in classified areas, none in grade A/B |
| **Waste and effluent** | Segregation, effluent treatment plant, compliance with pollution control rules |
| **Pest control and fire safety** | Programme with records; no toxic baits in production areas |

## 4.4 Equipment and its maintenance

**Design:** product-contact parts of SS 316L (or other non-reactive material); smooth, polished, crevice-free; easy to dismantle and clean (clean-in-place or wash-out); lubricants not to contact the product; sized for the batch; qualified (Unit 3).

**Identification and status:** each item has an ID number, status label ("cleaned", "to be cleaned", "under maintenance", "in use with product/batch") and a **logbook** recording use, cleaning and maintenance.

**Types of maintenance:**

| Type | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Breakdown (corrective) | Repair after failure | Replacing a broken punch |
| Preventive (planned) | Scheduled servicing to prevent failure | Lubricating a tablet press every set number of hours, changing filters |
| Predictive (condition-based) | Monitoring condition and acting before failure | Vibration analysis of motors, filter differential pressure trends |
| Total productive maintenance (TPM) | Operators share in routine care; aim of zero breakdowns | Autonomous maintenance by press operators |

**Calibration** of instruments and gauges per the calibration programme; out-of-calibration findings trigger an impact assessment.

## 4.5 Production management

**Production management** plans, organises, directs and controls the manufacturing activities so that products of the right quality are made in the right quantity, at the right time and at the least cost.

### Production organisation

```text
Managing Director / Site Head
  ├─ Production Head ── Section heads (tablets, liquids, sterile) ── supervisors ── operators
  ├─ Quality Head (QA and QC — independent of production)
  ├─ Engineering / Maintenance
  ├─ Materials (purchase, stores, planning)
  ├─ Warehouse and Logistics
  └─ HR, Finance, EHS (environment, health and safety)
```

Types of organisation: **line** (direct chain of command, quick decisions, overload at the top), **line and staff** (specialists such as QA and planning advise the line — most common in pharma), and **functional** (specialist supervisors for each function). The key GMP rule: the heads of production and quality must be independent of each other.

### Materials management

The planning, procurement, storage and control of materials so that the right material, of the right quality and quantity, is available at the right time and place at minimum cost.

Functions: material requirement planning, **purchasing** (vendor selection, vendor approval and audit, purchase orders), receiving and inspection (quarantine and sampling), **storage** (approved conditions: controlled room temperature, cold rooms, flammable stores), issue (FIFO or FEFO — first expired first out), inventory control, disposal of surplus and scrap.

**Selective inventory control techniques:**

| Technique | Basis | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ABC analysis** | Annual consumption value: A items ≈ 10–20% of items but 70–80% of value; C items ≈ 50–70% of items but 5–10% of value | Tight control and frequent review for A items; simple rules for C items |
| **VED** | Criticality: vital, essential, desirable | Spare parts, critical materials kept in stock |
| **FSN** | Movement: fast, slow, non-moving | Identify obsolete stock |
| **HML** | Unit price: high, medium, low | Purchasing policy |
| **SDE** | Availability: scarce, difficult, easy | Long-lead imported materials |

### Material handling and transportation

Principles: minimum handling, unit load (pallets), straight-line flow, mechanisation where economical, gravity where possible, safety, and GMP protection of material (closed containers, cleanable equipment).

Equipment: hand trucks and pallet trucks, forklifts (electric indoors — no exhaust), conveyors (belt, roller, screw), vacuum and pneumatic conveying for powders, bin (IBC) systems with lifting and blending, automated guided vehicles and automated storage and retrieval systems. Outbound **transport** must maintain storage conditions (temperature-controlled vehicles and validated shippers for cold-chain products, as in Good Distribution Practice).

## 4.6 Inventory management and control

**Inventory** is the stock of raw materials, packing materials, work-in-progress and finished goods. Too much ties up capital, needs space and risks expiry; too little stops production and loses sales.

**Costs involved:** ordering cost (per order: paperwork, transport, receiving, sampling and testing), carrying (holding) cost (per unit per year: capital interest, storage, insurance, deterioration, obsolescence), and shortage (stock-out) cost.

**Economic order quantity (EOQ):** EOQ = √(2DS/H), where D = annual demand, S = ordering cost per order, H = holding cost per unit per year. At EOQ, annual ordering cost equals annual holding cost and total cost is lowest.

**Worked example:** annual demand of an excipient 12,000 kg; ordering cost ₹2,000 per order; holding cost ₹25 per kg per year.

EOQ = √(2 × 12,000 × 2,000 / 25) = √1,920,000 = **about 1,386 kg**. Orders per year = 12,000/1,386 ≈ 8.7. Annual ordering cost = annual holding cost ≈ ₹17,320; total ≈ ₹34,640.

**Re-order level (ROL)** = average usage per day × lead time (days) + safety stock. With 300 working days (40 kg/day), lead time 10 days and safety stock 200 kg: ROL = 40 × 10 + 200 = **600 kg**.

Other terms: **maximum stock level, minimum stock level, safety (buffer) stock, lead time**; **just-in-time (JIT)** — materials arrive just when needed, minimal stock (needs reliable suppliers; limited in pharma by quarantine and QC testing time); **kanban** cards; **perpetual inventory** systems and periodic stock-taking; **ERP** software linking sales, planning, stores and finance.

## 4.7 Production planning and control (PPC)

**PPC** plans what, how much, when and where to produce, and then controls production so the plan is followed.

| Function | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Forecasting and master production schedule | How much of each product per month |
| Material requirement planning (MRP) | From the bill of materials, calculate what materials are needed and when |
| **Routing** | The sequence of operations and the equipment each batch follows |
| **Scheduling** | Fixing the start and finish time of each operation (Gantt charts) |
| **Loading** | Assigning work to machines and people within capacity |
| **Dispatching** | Issuing orders to start work: batch manufacturing records, material requisitions |
| **Follow-up (expediting)** | Checking progress, removing hold-ups |
| Inspection and corrective action | In-process checks; replanning if behind schedule |

Pharma specifics: campaign manufacturing (several batches of one product before cleaning and changeover), equipment cleaning and changeover time, QC release time, shelf life of intermediates (hold-time studies), batch-size limits set by validation.

## 4.8 Sales forecasting

**Sales forecasting** estimates future sales in units and value; it drives production plans, purchase, staffing and budgets.

**Qualitative methods:** jury of executive opinion, sales-force composite, Delphi method (experts answer rounds of questionnaires anonymously), market research and test marketing.

**Quantitative methods:**

- **Moving average:** average of the last n periods.
- **Exponential smoothing:** new forecast = old forecast + α × (actual − old forecast).
- **Trend projection (least squares):** fit Y = a + bX to past data.
- **Causal (regression) models:** sales related to drivers such as number of prescribers, disease prevalence, price.

**Worked example:** monthly sales (thousand packs) January–June: 40, 44, 43, 48, 50, 53.

- 3-month moving average forecast for July = (48 + 50 + 53)/3 = **50.3**.
- Least-squares trend with X = 1…6: b = 2.514, a = 37.53; July (X = 7): 37.53 + 2.514 × 7 = **55.1**.
- Exponential smoothing with α = 0.3, June forecast 49, June actual 53: July = 49 + 0.3 × (53 − 49) = **50.2**.

The moving average lags behind a rising trend; trend projection follows it. Forecast error is checked by mean absolute deviation or mean absolute percentage error.

## 4.9 Budget and cost control

A **budget** is a financial plan of income and expenditure for a future period, used for planning, coordination and control.

Types: sales budget, production budget, materials budget, labour budget, overhead budget, capital expenditure budget, cash budget, master budget; **fixed budget** (one activity level) and **flexible budget** (adjusts to actual output); **zero-based budgeting** (each expense justified afresh each year).

**Costs:**

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed | Does not change with output in the short term | Rent, depreciation, salaried staff |
| Variable | Changes in proportion to output | Raw and packing materials, power for machines |
| Semi-variable | Partly fixed | Maintenance, telephone |
| Direct / indirect | Traceable to a product or not | Direct materials vs factory overheads |

**Break-even point (BEP)** = fixed cost ÷ (selling price per unit − variable cost per unit). The difference (price − variable cost) is the **contribution** per unit.

**Worked example:** fixed costs ₹18 lakh per year; selling price ₹60 per pack; variable cost ₹36 per pack. Contribution = ₹24; BEP = 18,00,000/24 = **75,000 packs** (sales of ₹45 lakh).

**Cost control tools:** standard costing and variance analysis (material price and usage variance, labour rate and efficiency variance), budgetary control, value analysis, reduction of waste and rejects (lean manufacturing), energy audits, yield monitoring.

## 4.10 Industrial and personal relationships

**Industrial relations** are the relationships between management, employees and their unions, and the government, concerning conditions of work. **Personnel (human resource) management** covers recruitment, selection, induction, training, appraisal, compensation, welfare, safety and discipline.

Good relations depend on: clear communication, fair wages and incentives, safe working conditions, a grievance-handling procedure, participative management (works committees, quality circles, suggestion schemes), collective bargaining with recognised unions, recognition of good performance, and training and career growth. Poor relations show as absenteeism, high turnover, go-slows, strikes and lock-outs — and in pharma also as GMP errors, since tired or demotivated staff make mistakes.

**Legal framework in India (outline):** the Factories Act, 1948 (health, safety, welfare, working hours) and the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, Trade Unions Act, 1926 and related laws, which are being consolidated into the four Labour Codes (Code on Wages, 2019; Industrial Relations Code, 2020; Code on Social Security, 2020; Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020). Check the current notification status of each code for exam answers.

## 4.11 Total Quality Management (TQM)

**TQM** is a management approach in which the whole organisation — every department and every employee — works continuously to improve processes, products and services so as to meet or exceed customer needs.

**Pioneers:**

- **Deming:** 14 points; the **PDCA (Plan–Do–Check–Act)** cycle; reduce variation by statistical methods.
- **Juran:** quality as "fitness for use"; quality trilogy — quality planning, quality control, quality improvement.
- **Crosby:** "zero defects"; cost of quality = price of conformance + price of non-conformance.
- **Ishikawa:** cause-and-effect (fishbone) diagram; quality circles.

**Principles of TQM:** customer focus; leadership and commitment; involvement of all employees; process approach; system approach; continual improvement; fact-based decisions; mutually beneficial supplier relationships.

**Tools:**

- The **seven basic QC tools:** check sheet, histogram, Pareto chart, cause-and-effect diagram, scatter diagram, control chart, flow chart (or stratification).
- **Kaizen** (small continuous improvements), **5S** (sort, set in order, shine, standardise, sustain), **poka-yoke** (mistake proofing, for example barcode checks at dispensing), **benchmarking**, **quality circles**.
- **Six Sigma:** DMAIC (define, measure, analyse, improve, control); six sigma performance corresponds to about 3.4 defects per million opportunities.
- **Lean manufacturing:** remove waste (overproduction, waiting, transport, over-processing, inventory, motion, defects).
- **Cost of quality:** prevention + appraisal + internal failure + external failure costs.

**TQM in pharma:** delivered through the pharmaceutical quality system (ICH Q10), quality risk management (ICH Q9), CAPA, change management, management review, product quality review and ISO 9001-based systems; QbD extends it into development.

## Remember

- cGMP aims: right identity, strength, quality, purity; no mix-ups or contamination; documented and traceable.
- Layout: unidirectional flow of men and materials; airlocks and pressure cascades; dedicated facilities for penicillins, cytotoxics, some hormones.
- Maintenance: breakdown, preventive, predictive, TPM; every item has a logbook and status label.
- EOQ = √(2DS/H): worked example ≈ 1,386 kg; ROL = usage × lead time + safety stock = 600 kg.
- BEP = fixed cost/(price − variable cost) = 75,000 packs in the example.
- PPC: routing, scheduling, loading, dispatching, follow-up; ABC/VED/FSN for selective control.
- Forecasting: moving average 50.3, trend 55.1, exponential smoothing 50.2 (worked example).
- TQM: PDCA, Juran trilogy, zero defects, 7 QC tools, Kaizen, 5S, Six Sigma 3.4 DPMO; ICH Q10 is TQM for pharma.

# How to use this guide

Regulatory Affairs (MPH104T) is a descriptive, fact-heavy subject. Marks come from knowing **which law, which form, which authority and which timeline** applies, and from presenting procedures as clean flow charts and comparison tables. There is little calculation; the only formula you may be asked is the dissolution similarity factor f2. Study one unit a day: read the notes, close them, redraw every flow chart (ANDA review, NDA steps, foreign registration, global submission, protocol development, ethics committee working, informed consent, safety reporting) from memory, then do the question bank and MCQs for that unit.

**How the notes follow the syllabus.** The PCI syllabus has four units. Unit 1 is very large (part a: documentation and generic development; part b: product approval requirements), so it is split here into two units. The mapping is:

| These notes | PCI syllabus |
| --- | --- |
| Unit 1 Documentation and generic drug product development | Unit 1(a) |
| Unit 2 Regulatory requirements for product approval | Unit 1(b) |
| Unit 3 CMC, post-approval changes, combination products and devices, CTD/eCTD, FDA liaison, ICH, EU/MHRA/TGA/ROW | Unit 2 |
| Unit 4 Non-clinical development, global IND/NDA/ANDA, IMPD, IB | Unit 3 |
| Unit 5 Clinical trials, IRB/IEC, informed consent, HIPAA, pharmacovigilance | Unit 4 |

Nothing has been left out or moved between syllabus units. Indian requirements (CDSCO, NDCT Rules 2019, Medical Devices Rules 2017) are added where they help comparison, because Indian universities often ask "compare with India". Several topics overlap with the B.Pharm courses BP702T (Industrial Pharmacy II) and BP804ET (Pharmaceutical Regulatory Science); the facts match those notes.

**Exam pattern (end-semester theory, 75 marks, 3 hours).** The PCI M.Pharm scheme gives MPH104T 100 marks: 25 internal assessment + 75 end-semester, 3 hours. PCI does not prescribe sections for M.Pharm papers, so this pack uses the common PCI 75-mark split (used for M.Pharm by, for example, Bahra University, Shimla). Confirm the exact pattern with your university.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long answers — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

The question bank has long (10-mark), short (5-mark) and very short (2-mark) questions. Very short ones are quick definitions that also help with MCQs and with internal tests.

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — a standard question in most papers on this syllabus; prepare the full answer with a chart or table.
- **Medium** — asked every few years, or as part of a larger question.
- **Low** — less common, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from syllabus weight and common question patterns. They are not a promise of what will be asked.

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. Open every answer with a one-line definition and the legal basis ("An ANDA is filed under section 505(j) of the FD&C Act; rules in 21 CFR 314.94").
2. Expand every abbreviation the first time (CMC, PMOA, IMPD, IB, SUSAR, PHI, CTIS, RLD, SUPAC, GDUFA).
3. Use flow charts for processes and tables for comparisons (NDA vs ANDA vs BLA; IND vs CTA; IB vs IMPD; IRB vs Indian EC; US device classes vs Indian classes; EU procedures).
4. Learn the fixed numbers exactly: NCE 5 years, new clinical investigation 3 years, orphan 7 years, paediatric 6 months, 180-day exclusivity, 30-month stay, biologic reference exclusivity 12 years; IND 30 days; NDA filing 60 days, review 10/6 months; EU 210 active days + 67 days; 15-day alert reports, quarterly for 3 years then annual, records 10 years; ICH 7/15 days; India SAE 24 hours/14 days, 90/30 working days, EC ≥ 7 members, PSURs 6-monthly × 2 years then yearly × 2 years; HIPAA 18 identifiers; f2 50–100; BE 90% CI 80.00–125.00%.
5. Postgraduate examiners reward depth: after the basic list, add one line on "current trend" (ICH E6(R3), eCTD v4.0, ICH Q12, EU CTR 536/2014) or on the Indian position.
6. Rules change. In an answer it is fine to say "as per the NDCT Rules, 2019" or "as per 21 CFR 312"; in real work always read the current text on cdsco.gov.in, fda.gov, ema.europa.eu or ich.org.

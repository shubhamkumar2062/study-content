# Unit 2: Regulatory requirements for product approval (USA as the model)

This unit covers the second half of PCI Unit 1 (part b): what a regulator needs before it approves an **active pharmaceutical ingredient (API)**, a **biological product** or a **novel therapy**, how an **NDA** and an **ANDA** are obtained, and the "ways and means" by which a company outside the USA (for example an Indian firm) gets its products onto the US market. Indian requirements are added where students are usually asked to compare.

US figures here were checked against FDA pages and Title 21 of the Code of Federal Regulations; rules and fees change every year, so always confirm on fda.gov before using them at work.

## 2.1 The US regulatory framework in one view

```text
Congress → Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act, 1938, amended)
         → Public Health Service Act (PHS Act) — biologics
FDA (under the Department of Health and Human Services)
   ├── CDER: drugs (NDA, ANDA, OTC), most therapeutic proteins and antibodies
   ├── CBER: vaccines, blood, cell and gene therapies, tissues
   ├── CDRH: medical devices
   └── Office of Combination Products (assigns lead centre)
Rules: Title 21 CFR; guidance documents (non-binding, show FDA's current thinking)
User fees: PDUFA (new drugs, biologics), GDUFA (generics), BsUFA (biosimilars),
           MDUFA (devices) — fees in exchange for agreed review goals
```

The key legal milestones: 1906 Pure Food and Drugs Act; 1938 FD&C Act (safety, after the sulfanilamide elixir deaths); 1962 Kefauver–Harris Amendments (proof of efficacy, after thalidomide); 1983 Orphan Drug Act; 1984 Hatch-Waxman Act; 1992 PDUFA; 2009 BPCI Act; 2012 FDASIA; 2016 21st Century Cures Act.

## 2.2 Regulatory requirements for APIs

In the USA an API is **not approved on its own**. Its quality is reviewed as part of each drug application that uses it, usually through a **Type II DMF**. Requirements that an API maker must meet:

| Requirement | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| GMP for APIs | **ICH Q7** (Good Manufacturing Practice Guide for Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients), adopted by FDA, EU, India and many others; applies from the introduction of the "API starting material" onwards |
| Establishment registration and drug listing | Every domestic and foreign site that makes, repacks or relabels drugs for the US market registers with FDA and lists its products (21 CFR Part 207) |
| DMF | Detailed CMC information (route of synthesis, controls, impurities, stability) filed in CTD Module 3.2.S, cited by customers through a Letter of Authorisation |
| Impurity control | ICH Q3A (organic impurities), Q3C (residual solvents), Q3D (elemental impurities), M7 (mutagenic impurities, including nitrosamines) |
| Stability | ICH Q1A(R2) conditions and retest period |
| Inspection | Pre-approval and routine surveillance inspections; foreign sites are inspected too |
| Fees | Generic API DMFs pay a GDUFA DMF fee; API facilities pay a facility fee |

**EU:** the API is described in an ASMF or covered by a **CEP** issued by the EDQM after assessment against the European Pharmacopoeia monograph; non-EU API makers need a "written confirmation" from their own regulator that their GMP is equivalent (EU Directive on falsified medicines).

**India:** API manufacture needs a licence from the State Licensing Authority under the Drugs Rules, 1945 and compliance with Schedule M; a **new** API (bulk drug not yet approved in India) needs permission of the Central Licensing Authority under the NDCT Rules, 2019.

## 2.3 Regulatory requirements for biologics

A **biological product** is a virus, therapeutic serum, toxin, antitoxin, vaccine, blood or blood component, allergenic product, protein or analogous product used to prevent, treat or cure a disease. Biologics are large, complex, made in living systems and sensitive to small process changes, so "the process is the product".

### Biologics License Application (BLA)

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Law | Section **351(a)** of the PHS Act; regulations 21 CFR Parts 600–680 (plus 210/211 and 312) |
| Standard | The product must be **safe, pure and potent**, and the facility must meet standards that assure this |
| Form | FDA 356h (same form as NDA) |
| Centre | CBER (vaccines, blood, cell and gene therapy) or CDER (most monoclonal antibodies and therapeutic proteins) |
| Extra elements | Detailed description of cell bank, viral safety, process validation, comparability protocols, lot release (some products need FDA lot release), establishment description |
| Outcome | A **biologics licence** (US licence number on the label) |

### Biosimilars and interchangeables

- Created by the **Biologics Price Competition and Innovation Act (BPCI Act), 2009**; abbreviated route under section **351(k)** of the PHS Act.
- **Biosimilar:** highly similar to an FDA-licensed reference product (licensed under 351(a)), notwithstanding minor differences in clinically inactive components, with **no clinically meaningful differences** in safety, purity and potency.
- **Interchangeable:** a biosimilar that also meets extra standards so it may be substituted at the pharmacy without the prescriber's intervention (subject to state law).
- Evidence follows a "**totality of evidence**", stepwise approach: analytical comparability (structure, function) is the foundation, then animal data if needed, clinical pharmacology (PK/PD), immunogenicity and any comparative clinical study needed to resolve remaining uncertainty.
- The reference product has **12 years** of exclusivity from first licensure, during which a 351(k) application cannot be approved.
- The **Purple Book** lists licensed biological products with their biosimilar or interchangeable status and exclusivity dates (the biologic equivalent of the Orange Book).

**India:** biologics and similar biologics are approved by CDSCO (as new drugs under the NDCT Rules, 2019), with the **Review Committee on Genetic Manipulation (RCGM)** of the Department of Biotechnology reviewing pre-clinical data for recombinant products, and the **Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee (GEAC)** where living modified organisms are involved. The "Guidelines on Similar Biologics" set the comparability exercise; check the latest revision on cdsco.gov.in.

**EU:** biotechnology products must use the centralised procedure (EMA). The EU approved the first biosimilar in 2006 and has detailed product-class guidelines.

## 2.4 Regulatory requirements for novel therapies

"Novel therapies" include new molecular entities, **cell and gene therapies**, tissue-engineered products, RNA-based medicines, and therapies for rare diseases.

### US programmes that speed up development and review

| Programme | Qualifying criteria | Benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fast track** | Serious condition + potential to meet an unmet medical need (non-clinical or clinical data) | More frequent meetings; **rolling review** (sections submitted as completed) |
| **Breakthrough therapy** | Serious condition + preliminary clinical evidence of substantial improvement on a clinically significant endpoint | All fast-track features + intensive guidance from senior FDA staff |
| **Accelerated approval** | Serious condition, meaningful advantage, effect on a **surrogate endpoint** reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit | Earlier approval; **confirmatory trial** required |
| **Priority review** | Significant improvement in safety or effectiveness for a serious condition | Review goal 6 months instead of 10 |
| **RMAT** (regenerative medicine advanced therapy, 21st Century Cures Act 2016) | Cell therapy, tissue product or certain gene therapies for serious conditions, with preliminary clinical evidence | Breakthrough-like benefits |
| **Orphan drug designation** (Orphan Drug Act 1983) | Disease affecting fewer than 200,000 people in the USA (or no expected cost recovery) | 7-year orphan exclusivity, tax credits, fee waivers |

### Special regulatory points for cell and gene therapies

- Regulated by CBER's Office of Therapeutic Products; filed as INDs then BLAs.
- Long-term follow-up of patients (for some gene therapies, many years) for delayed effects.
- Vector shedding, replication-competent virus testing, potency assays that reflect the mechanism.
- Chain of identity and chain of custody for autologous products.
- Manufacturing comparability when the process changes.

**EU:** advanced therapy medicinal products (ATMPs) go through the centralised procedure with the **Committee for Advanced Therapies (CAT)**; the EU also has **PRIME** (priority medicines) support and conditional marketing authorisation.

**India:** the NDCT Rules, 2019 treat products such as recombinant DNA products, monoclonal antibodies, stem-cell-derived products and gene therapy products as **new drugs** needing CLA permission; trials of such products need appropriate ethics committee and, where relevant, DBT committee review. There is also a provision to waive local trials for drugs approved in specified countries in some situations (orphan drugs, unmet need) — check the current rule and notifications before quoting it.

## 2.5 Obtaining an NDA

### a. Legal routes

| Route | Use | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 505(b)(1) | New molecular entity or any product where the applicant owns or has right of reference to all studies | Full non-clinical and clinical package |
| 505(b)(2) | Changes to an approved drug (new salt, ester, dosage form, strength, route, combination, indication) or a new drug relying partly on literature or FDA's earlier findings | Bridging studies + new studies needed for the change; patent certifications for relied-upon drugs |

### b. Steps

```text
1. Pre-IND meeting → IND (21 CFR 312) → Phase I–III trials
2. Formal meetings with FDA:
     Type A (to resolve a stalled programme),
     Type B (pre-IND, end-of-Phase-II, pre-NDA),
     Type C (other topics) — plus newer types for specific needs
3. NDA in eCTD: Form FDA 356h, user fee (PDUFA), Modules 1–5,
   patent information, financial disclosure (21 CFR 54),
   debarment certification, proposed labelling (PLR format)
4. Filing review — 60 days ("refuse to file" if not reviewable)
5. Day-74 letter: filing communication and review designation
6. Review by disciplines; mid-cycle and late-cycle meetings;
   advisory committee if questions of benefit–risk remain
7. Pre-approval inspections (manufacturing, clinical sites — BIMO)
8. Labelling negotiation; REMS if needed
9. Action: approval letter or complete response letter
10. Post-approval: Phase IV commitments, safety reports, annual reports,
    supplements for changes
```

### c. Labelling

The US prescribing information follows the **Physician Labeling Rule (PLR)** format with "Highlights", a table of contents and the full prescribing information. Patient labelling (Medication Guide, patient package insert) may also be required. The approved label becomes the reference that generics must copy.

## 2.6 ANDA for generic drugs (requirements recap)

An ANDA (section 505(j); 21 CFR 314.94) must show:

1. The **reference listed drug (RLD)** it copies and the **reference standard** used for BE.
2. Same active ingredient, route, dosage form, strength and conditions of use.
3. Labelling the same as the RLD (except permitted differences).
4. **Bioequivalence** (or a justified biowaiver), following the FDA product-specific guidance.
5. Full CMC: drug substance (usually via DMF), drug product composition, pharmaceutical development, manufacturing process, specifications, container closure, **stability** (exhibit batches), and inactive ingredients justified for the route (the FDA Inactive Ingredient Database is checked for acceptable levels).
6. **Patent certifications** and exclusivity statements.
7. Facility information and GMP compliance; GDUFA fees paid.

Complex generics (inhalers, injectables with complex release, topical products, peptides) may need extra studies (in vitro characterisation, comparative clinical endpoint studies, immunogenicity risk assessment). **Pre-ANDA meetings** help resolve such issues before filing.

## 2.7 Ways and means of US registration for foreign drugs

An Indian company can supply the US market in three main ways: by filing its **own ANDA or NDA**, by being the **API/DMF supplier** to a US applicant, or as a **contract manufacturer** named in someone else's application. In every case the steps below apply.

### a. Steps for a foreign firm

```text
1. Obtain a DUNS number (unique business identifier used by FDA)
2. Establishment registration of each foreign site (21 CFR 207),
   naming a US agent; renewed every year; drug listing with NDC codes
3. Appoint a US agent (and usually a US regulatory consultant /
   authorised representative for correspondence)
4. Prepare dossier: ANDA/NDA in eCTD via the FDA electronic
   submissions gateway; DMF for the API
5. Pay user fees (application, facility, DMF fees as applicable)
6. FDA review → pre-approval inspection of the foreign site
   (FDA has overseas offices, including in India)
7. Approval → commercial batches → import into the USA:
   customs entry, FDA admissibility review, possible sampling
8. Post-approval: annual reports, safety reporting, supplements,
   surveillance inspections, product listing updates
```

### b. Key import controls

| Control | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Admissibility review | FDA checks each import entry (registration, listing, approval status, labelling) |
| Refusal of admission | Product refused if it appears adulterated, misbranded or unapproved |
| **Import alert** | Lets FDA detain shipments without physical examination ("detention without physical examination") from a firm with serious GMP problems |
| Form FDA 483 | List of observations given at the end of an inspection |
| Warning letter | Formal notice of significant violations; must be answered |
| Official Action Indicated (OAI) | Inspection classification that can block approvals from that site |

### c. Practical tips for Indian firms

1. Build data integrity into systems from the start (audit trails, Part 11 computer systems); many FDA warning letters to Indian sites concern data integrity.
2. Keep the site inspection-ready at all times; FDA may give short notice abroad.
3. Choose BE CROs with clean inspection histories.
4. Use the US reference product for BE and US-specific labelling.
5. Track US patents and exclusivities to plan Paragraph IV or launch timing.

## 2.8 India: approval of new drugs (for comparison)

| Step | Under the NDCT Rules, 2019 |
| --- | --- |
| Clinical trial permission | Application in Form CT-04; permission in Form CT-06; ethics committee approval and CTRI registration also needed |
| Import of a new drug for sale | Application in Form CT-18 |
| Manufacture of a new drug for sale | Application in Form CT-21 |
| Review | CDSCO with the Subject Expert Committee (SEC) |
| Timelines | Clinical trial application decided within 90 working days; 30 working days for a drug discovered and developed in India (deemed approval if not answered) |
| After approval | State manufacturing licence; PSURs every six months for two years, then yearly for two years |

Applications are submitted online through CDSCO's **SUGAM** portal. The dossier follows the CTD format.

## 2.9 Comparison: NDA, ANDA, BLA and 351(k)

| Point | NDA 505(b)(1) | 505(b)(2) | ANDA 505(j) | BLA 351(a) | Biosimilar 351(k) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product | New drug | Modified drug | Generic | New biologic | Biosimilar |
| Law | FD&C Act | FD&C Act | FD&C Act | PHS Act | PHS Act |
| Clinical efficacy trials | Yes | Only as needed | No | Yes | Usually limited/comparative |
| Key evidence | Safety and efficacy | Bridging + new data | BE | Safe, pure, potent | Analytical similarity + PK/PD + immunogenicity |
| Reference list | Orange Book | Orange Book | Orange Book | Purple Book | Purple Book |
| Exclusivity | NCE 5 y; 3 y | 3 y possible | 180-day, CGT | 12 y reference product | First interchangeable exclusivity |

## Remember

- APIs: no stand-alone US approval; ICH Q7 GMP; DMF Type II; registration and listing (21 CFR 207); impurities Q3A/Q3C/Q3D/M7. EU: ASMF/CEP + written confirmation. India: SLA licence; new API needs CLA.
- Biologics: BLA 351(a), 21 CFR 600–680, "safe, pure and potent"; CBER/CDER. Biosimilar 351(k), BPCI Act 2009; interchangeable; 12-year reference exclusivity; Purple Book. India: CDSCO + RCGM + GEAC; similar biologics guidelines.
- Novel therapies: fast track, breakthrough, accelerated approval (surrogate + confirmatory trial), priority review (6 months), RMAT, orphan (< 200,000 patients; 7 years). EU: CAT, PRIME.
- NDA: 505(b)(1) vs 505(b)(2); Form 356h; meetings Type A/B/C; 60-day filing; PLR labelling; REMS.
- ANDA: RLD, sameness, BE, CMC, patent certification, GDUFA.
- Foreign registration: DUNS, establishment registration + listing with US agent, eCTD, fees, pre-approval inspection, import admissibility; Form 483, warning letter, import alert.
- India: CT-04 → CT-06; CT-18 import, CT-21 manufacture; 90/30 working days; SUGAM.

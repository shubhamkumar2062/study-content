# Sodium Alginate Gel

**Aim:** To prepare and dispense 50 g of Sodium Alginate Gel.

> Gel strength depends on the grade of sodium alginate. Use the concentration given in your lab manual; the formula below is typical.

## Principle

Sodium alginate is a natural polysaccharide (from brown seaweeds). In water it hydrates and swells to form a viscous **gel**. Glycerin wets the powder (preventing lumps) and acts as a humectant; a preservative such as methylparaben prevents microbial growth. Alginate gels are used as lubricants and as bases for topical drugs.

## Formula

| Ingredient | For 100 g | For 50 g |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sodium alginate | 5 g | 2.5 g |
| Glycerin | 10 g | 5 g |
| Methylparaben | 0.2 g | 0.1 g |
| Purified water | to 100 g | to 50 g |

## Requirements

Ingredients, mortar and pestle or beaker with stirrer, water bath, ointment jar or collapsible tube.

## Procedure

1. Dissolve methylparaben in hot purified water and cool.
2. Triturate sodium alginate with glycerin in a mortar to a smooth slurry (this stops lumping).
3. Add the water gradually with continuous trituration.
4. Allow to stand so the gum hydrates fully and air bubbles escape.
5. Make up to weight with water, mix and fill into a jar or tube.

## Observations

Clear to slightly opalescent, smooth, viscous gel.

## Result

50 g of Sodium Alginate Gel was prepared and dispensed.

## Label

Sodium Alginate Gel, 50 g. For external use. Store in a cool place.

## Precautions

1. Wet the alginate with glycerin first, else lumps (fish eyes) form.
2. Avoid calcium salts unless intended; they make alginate set into a firm gel.
3. Allow complete hydration before final weighing.

## Viva questions

1. **Source of alginate?** Brown seaweeds (alginic acid salts).
2. **Role of glycerin?** Dispersing/wetting agent and humectant.
3. **Other gelling agents?** Carbomer, tragacanth, methylcellulose, sodium CMC, gelatin.
4. **Advantages of gels?** Non-greasy, cooling, easily washed off.

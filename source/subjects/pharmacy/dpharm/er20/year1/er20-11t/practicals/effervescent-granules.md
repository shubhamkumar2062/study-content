# Effervescent granules

**Aim:** To prepare and dispense 50 g of effervescent granules by the fusion (heat) method.

> Many manuals use a named product such as effervescent sodium citrotartrate or effervescent potassium citrate granules. Follow the drug and quantities in your manual; the calculation below shows how the acid and base are balanced.

## Principle

Citric acid and tartaric acid react with sodium bicarbonate in water to release **carbon dioxide**, making a fizzy, pleasant drink:

```text
C6H8O7·H2O (citric acid, 210) + 3 NaHCO3 (3 × 84) → Na3C6H5O7 + 3 CO2 + 4 H2O
C4H6O6 (tartaric acid, 150)   + 2 NaHCO3 (2 × 84) → Na2C4H4O6 + 2 CO2 + 2 H2O
```

Citric acid alone gives a sticky mass, tartaric acid alone gives crumbly granules, so they are used together in the ratio **citric : tartaric = 1 : 2**. In the **fusion method**, gentle heat releases the **water of crystallisation of citric acid**, which moistens the powder and acts as the binder.

## Calculation of sodium bicarbonate

```text
1 g citric acid   needs 252 ÷ 210 = 1.2 g NaHCO3
1 g tartaric acid needs 168 ÷ 150 = 1.12 g NaHCO3
For 1 g citric + 2 g tartaric: 1.2 + 2.24 = 3.44 g NaHCO3
So citric acid : tartaric acid : sodium bicarbonate = 1 : 2 : 3.44
```

## Typical formula (for about 50 g)

| Ingredient | Quantity |
| --- | --- |
| Citric acid (monohydrate) | 5 g |
| Tartaric acid | 10 g |
| Sodium bicarbonate | 17.2 g |
| Drug (as per manual) | as directed |
| Sucrose (powdered) | to 50 g |

## Requirements

Ingredients, mortar and pestle, sieves (about no. 10 and no. 20), evaporating dish or tray, oven or water bath, thermometer, airtight bottle.

## Procedure

1. Dry all ingredients and powder them separately; pass through a sieve.
2. Mix the citric acid and tartaric acid with the sucrose and drug, then add the sodium bicarbonate and mix lightly.
3. Spread the powder on a dish and heat in an oven (about 93–104 °C) or on a water bath, turning with a spatula, until the mass becomes damp and coherent.
4. Quickly pass the damp mass through a coarse sieve to form granules.
5. Dry the granules at not more than about 50 °C.
6. Pass through a finer sieve to remove fines and pack at once in an airtight bottle.

## Observations

White, dry granules that effervesce briskly when added to water.

## Result

50 g of effervescent granules were prepared and dispensed.

## Label

Effervescent granules, 50 g. **Dissolve the dose in water and drink during effervescence.** Keep the container tightly closed in a dry place.

## Precautions

1. Use dry ingredients and dry apparatus; moisture starts the reaction early.
2. Do not overheat; work quickly once the mass is damp.
3. Pack immediately in airtight containers.

## Viva questions

1. **Why use both citric and tartaric acid?** Citric acid alone is sticky; tartaric alone gives friable granules.
2. **What acts as binder in the fusion method?** Water of crystallisation of citric acid.
3. **Gas released?** Carbon dioxide.
4. **Why granules rather than powder?** Less hygroscopic, controlled effervescence, better flow.

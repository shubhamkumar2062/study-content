# Cetrimide Cream

**Aim:** To prepare and dispense 50 g of Cetrimide Cream.

## Principle

Cetrimide is a **cationic surfactant** with antiseptic action. In this cream it also acts with **cetostearyl alcohol** as an emulsifying system, giving an **oil-in-water** cream that is non-greasy and water-washable. The oil phase (cetostearyl alcohol and liquid paraffin) and the water phase are heated separately to the same temperature, mixed, and stirred until cool. Used as an antiseptic for cuts, burns and minor skin infections.

## Formula (BPC type)

| Ingredient | For 100 g | For 50 g |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cetrimide | 0.5 g | 0.25 g |
| Cetostearyl alcohol | 5 g | 2.5 g |
| Liquid paraffin | 50 g | 25 g |
| Purified water (freshly boiled and cooled) | to 100 g | to 50 g |

## Requirements

Ingredients, two beakers, water bath, thermometer, glass rod or stirrer, ointment jar.

## Procedure

1. Melt the cetostearyl alcohol with the liquid paraffin on a water bath and keep at about 60–70 °C.
2. Dissolve the cetrimide in the water and heat to the same temperature.
3. Add the aqueous phase to the oily phase gradually with continuous stirring.
4. Remove from heat and stir until the cream cools and thickens.
5. Adjust to 50 g with water if needed, mix and fill into a jar.

## Observations

White, smooth, non-greasy cream; mixes with water (o/w).

## Result

50 g of Cetrimide Cream was prepared and dispensed.

## Label

Cetrimide Cream, 50 g. **For external use only.** Store in a cool place.

## Precautions

1. Both phases must be at the same temperature before mixing.
2. Stir continuously until cool.
3. Do not mix with soaps or anionic substances (they inactivate cetrimide).

## Viva questions

1. **Type of cream?** Oil-in-water (vanishing type).
2. **Nature of cetrimide?** Cationic quaternary ammonium compound; antiseptic.
3. **Incompatible with?** Soaps and anionic surfactants.
4. **Difference between cream and ointment?** Cream is an emulsion, softer and less greasy.

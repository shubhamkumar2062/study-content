# Calcium Gluconate Injection (10% w/v)

**Aim:** To prepare and dispense Calcium Gluconate Injection 10% w/v (for example 5 ampoules of 10 ml).

> Follow the stabiliser quantity and container type given in your lab manual.

## Principle

Calcium gluconate is soluble only to a limited extent in cold water, so a 10% solution is **supersaturated** and is made by dissolving the salt in **hot** Water for Injections. A small amount of **calcium saccharate** is added as a **stabiliser** to stop calcium gluconate crystallising out on storage. The solution is filtered, filled into ampoules and **sterilised by autoclaving**. It is used in hypocalcaemia (tetany) and some cases of hyperkalaemia.

## Formula

| Ingredient | For 100 ml |
| --- | --- |
| Calcium gluconate | 10 g |
| Calcium saccharate (stabiliser) | small quantity, as per manual |
| Water for Injections | to 100 ml |

## Requirements

Calcium gluconate, calcium saccharate, Water for Injections, beaker, water bath, volumetric flask, sintered glass/membrane filter, ampoules, ampoule sealing burner, autoclave.

## Procedure

1. Clean ampoules: wash, rinse with Water for Injections and dry/sterilise.
2. Heat about 80 ml Water for Injections to near boiling.
3. Dissolve the calcium saccharate and then the calcium gluconate in the hot water with stirring.
4. Cool, make up to 100 ml with Water for Injections.
5. Filter until clear.
6. Fill 10 ml (plus a slight excess) into each ampoule and seal by fusion.
7. Sterilise by autoclaving at 121 °C for 15 minutes.
8. Inspect for clarity and perform the leakage test.

## Observations

Clear, colourless solution free from crystals and particles.

## Result

Calcium Gluconate Injection 10% w/v was prepared, sterilised and dispensed.

## Label

Calcium Gluconate Injection IP 10% w/v, 10 ml ampoule. Sterile. For slow intravenous injection. Batch number, manufacturing and expiry dates.

## Precautions

1. Dissolve in hot water; do not let the solution crystallise before filtering.
2. Inspect for crystals after sterilisation and on storage.
3. Seal ampoules properly; check for leaks.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is calcium saccharate added?** As a stabiliser to prevent crystallisation of the supersaturated solution.
2. **Uses?** Hypocalcaemia and tetany; to protect the heart in hyperkalaemia.
3. **Route?** Slow intravenous injection.
4. **Why must injections be particle-free?** Particles can block small blood vessels and cause harm.

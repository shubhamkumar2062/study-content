# Magnesium Hydroxide Mixture

**Aim:** To prepare and dispense 100 ml of Magnesium Hydroxide Mixture.

> The formula below follows the older BP method by double decomposition. Use the preservative and quantities given in your lab manual.

## Principle

Magnesium hydroxide is an insoluble antacid and laxative. It is prepared freshly by **double decomposition** of magnesium sulphate with sodium hydroxide, which gives a very fine, gelatinous precipitate that stays well suspended:

```text
MgSO4 + 2 NaOH → Mg(OH)2 ↓ + Na2SO4
```

The by-product sodium sulphate is removed by washing. Light magnesium oxide is added; it slowly hydrates to more magnesium hydroxide (MgO + H2O → Mg(OH)2).

## Formula

| Ingredient | For 1000 ml (master) | For 100 ml (working) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Magnesium sulphate | 47.5 g | 4.75 g |
| Sodium hydroxide | 15 g | 1.5 g |
| Light magnesium oxide | 52.5 g | 5.25 g |
| Preservative | q.s. | q.s. |
| Purified water | to 1000 ml | to 100 ml |

## Requirements

Magnesium sulphate, sodium hydroxide, light magnesium oxide, purified water, beakers, glass rod, filter/strainer (muslin), measuring cylinder, bottle.

## Procedure

1. Dissolve magnesium sulphate in about 30 ml of water.
2. Dissolve sodium hydroxide in about 30 ml of water (in a separate beaker; this gets warm).
3. Add the magnesium sulphate solution slowly to the sodium hydroxide solution with constant stirring. A white gelatinous precipitate forms.
4. Allow to settle, decant the clear liquid and wash the precipitate with water several times (or on muslin) to remove sodium sulphate.
5. Mix the light magnesium oxide with a little water to a smooth cream and add to the washed precipitate.
6. Add preservative, make up to 100 ml with water and mix well.

## Observations

White, opaque, smooth suspension.

## Result

100 ml of Magnesium Hydroxide Mixture was prepared and dispensed.

## Label

Magnesium Hydroxide Mixture, 100 ml. **Shake well before use.** Store in a cool place; do not freeze.

## Precautions

1. Add magnesium sulphate solution to the alkali slowly with stirring for a fine precipitate.
2. Wash the precipitate well to remove sodium sulphate.
3. Handle sodium hydroxide with care.

## Viva questions

1. **Method of preparation?** Double decomposition (chemical reaction).
2. **Uses?** Antacid (small dose) and saline laxative (larger dose).
3. **Why is sodium sulphate removed?** It is an unwanted by-product with laxative action and affects taste.
4. **Why shake before use?** It is a suspension.

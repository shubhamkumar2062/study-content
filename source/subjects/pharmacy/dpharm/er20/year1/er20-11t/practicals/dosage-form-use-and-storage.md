# Proper use and storage of dosage forms, inhalers, spacers and insulin pens

**Aim:** To learn and demonstrate the correct methods of using and storing common dosage forms, including special devices such as metered-dose inhalers, dry powder inhalers, spacers and insulin pens.

## Principle

A medicine works well only when it is used and stored correctly. Pharmacists must be able to show patients the right technique and explain storage, using placebo or demonstration devices.

## Requirements

Demonstration (placebo) metered-dose inhaler, dry powder inhaler, spacer, insulin pen with needles, empty strips, eye drop bottle, suppository model, patient information leaflets.

## Procedure / technique

**1. Metered-dose inhaler (MDI)**

1. Remove the cap and **shake** the inhaler well.
2. Breathe out fully, away from the mouthpiece.
3. Put the mouthpiece between the lips, seal them.
4. Start breathing in **slowly** and press the canister once at the same time.
5. Continue a slow, deep breath; **hold the breath for about 10 seconds**.
6. Wait about 30–60 seconds before a second puff.
7. After a steroid inhaler, **rinse the mouth** with water and spit (prevents oral thrush).

**2. MDI with spacer**

Fit the inhaler to the spacer, shake, press once into the spacer, then breathe in slowly and deeply (or take several normal breaths through the mouthpiece/mask). Useful for children, elderly and those who cannot coordinate pressing and breathing. Wash the spacer as directed and let it dry in air.

**3. Dry powder inhaler (DPI)**

Load the dose as per the device, breathe out away from the device (never into it), seal lips and breathe in **quickly and deeply**, hold the breath, then breathe out slowly. Keep the device dry.

**4. Insulin pen**

1. Wash hands; check the insulin type and expiry; roll cloudy insulin gently (do not shake hard).
2. Attach a new needle.
3. **Prime** by dialling 2 units and pressing with the pen upright, until a drop appears.
4. Dial the prescribed dose.
5. Pinch the skin (abdomen, thigh, upper arm), insert the needle (usually at 90°) and press the button fully.
6. **Hold for about 10 seconds** before withdrawing.
7. Remove and safely discard the needle; rotate injection sites.

**Storage of insulin:** unopened pens in a refrigerator at 2–8 °C, never frozen; the pen in use may be kept at room temperature away from heat and light for the period stated on the leaflet (often about 4 weeks).

**5. Other dosage forms (brief)**

| Dosage form | Correct use | Storage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tablets/capsules | Swallow with water; do not crush SR or enteric tablets | Cool, dry place, in original strip |
| Suspensions/emulsions | Shake well; use measuring cup or spoon | As labelled; reconstituted antibiotics in refrigerator, discard after stated days |
| Eye drops | Pull lower lid, instil, close eye, press inner corner; do not touch tip | Discard 4 weeks after opening |
| Ear drops | Warm in hand, tilt head, instil, stay tilted for a few minutes | Cool place |
| Suppositories | Remove wrapper, insert pointed end first; lie down for a few minutes | Cool place (melt in heat) |
| Transdermal patch | Clean, dry, hairless skin; rotate sites; fold used patch and discard safely | Room temperature |

## Result

The correct use and storage of common dosage forms and special devices were studied and demonstrated.

## Precautions

1. Use placebo or demonstration devices only.
2. Never share insulin pens or needles between patients.
3. Check expiry dates and storage instructions.

## Viva questions

1. **Why rinse the mouth after a steroid inhaler?** To prevent oral candidiasis (thrush) and hoarseness.
2. **Advantage of a spacer?** No need to coordinate pressing and breathing; more drug reaches the lungs.
3. **Why prime an insulin pen?** To remove air and make sure the needle is clear, so the full dose is given.
4. **Storage of unopened insulin?** 2–8 °C in a refrigerator, not frozen.
5. **MDI vs DPI inhalation?** MDI: slow, deep breath; DPI: fast, deep breath.

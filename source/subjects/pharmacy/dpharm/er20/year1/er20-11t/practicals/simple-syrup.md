# Simple Syrup IP

**Aim:** To prepare and dispense 100 g of Simple Syrup.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ.

## Principle

Simple Syrup is a concentrated solution of **sucrose in purified water (66.7% w/w)**. At this concentration there is so little free water that microorganisms cannot grow, so it is **self-preserving**. Gentle heat speeds dissolution, but too much heat causes **inversion** of sucrose (into glucose and fructose) and darkening (caramelisation).

## Formula

| Ingredient | Master formula | Quantity for 100 g |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sucrose | 66.7 g | 66.7 g |
| Purified water | to 100 g | to 100 g |

## Requirements

Sucrose, purified water, beaker, glass rod, water bath, balance, muslin cloth, amber or clear glass bottle.

## Procedure

1. Weigh the beaker (tare) and weigh 66.7 g of sucrose into it.
2. Add about 33 ml of purified water.
3. Heat on a water bath with stirring until the sugar dissolves completely; do not boil.
4. Strain through muslin while hot.
5. Cool, then add purified water to make the total weight **100 g**.
6. Mix well and transfer to a clean, dry bottle; close tightly.

## Observations

Clear, colourless, viscous, sweet liquid.

## Result

100 g of Simple Syrup IP was prepared and dispensed.

## Label

Simple Syrup IP, 100 g. Store in a cool place in a well-closed container.

## Precautions

1. Do not overheat (inversion and caramelisation).
2. Make up to weight, not volume, because the formula is w/w.
3. Use clean dry bottles; avoid sugar crystals on the neck (cap locking).

## Viva questions

1. **What is the concentration of Simple Syrup IP?** 66.7% w/w sucrose (USP: 85% w/v).
2. **Why is it self-preserving?** High osmotic pressure; little free water for microbes.
3. **What is inversion?** Hydrolysis of sucrose into glucose and fructose by heat and acid.
4. **Uses of simple syrup?** Sweetening agent and vehicle; base for medicated syrups; binder in tablet granulation.

# Dusting powder (Zinc, Starch and Talc)

**Aim:** To prepare and dispense 50 g of Zinc, Starch and Talc Dusting Powder.

## Principle

Dusting powders are fine, free-flowing powders applied to the skin. **Zinc oxide** is mildly astringent and antiseptic, **starch** is absorbent and soothing, and **talc** is a lubricant that helps the powder spread. Powders are mixed by **geometric (doubling-up) dilution** and passed through a fine sieve so they are smooth and free from grit. Powders meant for wounds or broken skin must be sterile, because talc and starch may contain bacterial spores.

## Formula

| Ingredient | For 100 g | For 50 g |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zinc oxide | 25 g | 12.5 g |
| Starch | 25 g | 12.5 g |
| Purified talc | 50 g | 25 g |

## Requirements

Ingredients, mortar and pestle, fine sieve (e.g. sieve no. 120), spatula, sifter-top container.

## Procedure

1. Weigh all ingredients and sieve each separately.
2. Place zinc oxide in the mortar, add an equal amount of starch and triturate.
3. Add the rest of the starch, then the talc, in portions equal to the mixture already present, triturating after each addition.
4. Pass the whole mixture through the fine sieve and mix again lightly.
5. Fill into a sifter-top container.

## Observations

White, smooth, fine, free-flowing powder without grit.

## Result

50 g of Zinc, Starch and Talc Dusting Powder was prepared and dispensed.

## Label

Dusting Powder, 50 g. **For external use only. Not to be applied to open wounds or broken skin.** Store in a dry place.

## Precautions

1. Sieve to remove grit.
2. Use geometric dilution for uniform mixing.
3. Keep dry; sterilise if meant for wounds.

## Viva questions

1. **What is a dusting powder?** A fine powder applied to the skin for local action.
2. **Why must dusting powders for wounds be sterile?** Talc may carry spores of bacteria such as tetanus.
3. **Role of talc?** Lubricant, helps spreading.
4. **What is an insufflation?** A powder blown into a body cavity with an insufflator.

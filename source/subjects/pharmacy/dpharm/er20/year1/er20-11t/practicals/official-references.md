# Handling and referring to official references

**Aim:** To learn how to use official references (pharmacopoeias, formularies and reference books) to find formulas, standards, methods and procedures for preparing and testing dosage forms.

> Use the editions available in your college library (for example IP 2018 or IP 2022, BP, Martindale, National Formulary of India). Handle the books carefully and return them to the shelf.

## Principle

Every official preparation has a **monograph** that gives its formula or composition, method of preparation (where given), tests and storage. The **general notices** explain the words used, and the **appendices / general chapters** give the official test methods. A pharmacist must be able to find this information quickly and correctly.

## Requirements

Indian Pharmacopoeia (current edition), British Pharmacopoeia, Martindale (Extra Pharmacopoeia), National Formulary of India, BPC (older formulas), practical record book.

## Procedure

1. Note the edition, year, publisher and number of volumes of each book.
2. Read the **general notices** of the IP and write the meaning of: "about", "constant weight", storage terms (cool place, room temperature), solubility terms (very soluble … practically insoluble).
3. Use the **index** to find a monograph, for example *Paracetamol Tablets*. Write down: definition and content limits, identification, tests (uniformity of weight, dissolution, related substances), assay, storage and labelling.
4. Open the **appendix** for one test (for example disintegration test) and write the apparatus, conditions and limits.
5. From a formulary or BPC, copy the official master formula of one preparation (for example Calamine Lotion) and convert it into a **working formula** for 50 ml or 100 ml.
6. In Martindale, look up one drug and note: uses, dose, adverse effects, interactions and two proprietary names.
7. Record each search in a table.

## Observations

| Reference | Item searched | Information found | Page / section |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IP | Paracetamol Tablets | Content limits, tests, storage | |

**Working formula calculation**

```text
Working quantity = (Master quantity × Quantity required) ÷ Master formula quantity
Example: Calamine 15 g per 100 ml → for 50 ml: 15 × 50 ÷ 100 = 7.5 g
```

## Result

Official references were studied and information on the given monographs, test methods and formulas was retrieved and recorded.

## Precautions

1. Always use the current official edition for standards.
2. Copy numbers and units exactly; check w/v, w/w and v/v.
3. Check the scale of the master formula before calculating.

## Viva questions

1. **What is a pharmacopoeia?** A legally recognised book of drug standards published under government authority.
2. **Who publishes the IP?** The Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission, Ghaziabad.
3. **What are general notices?** Rules and meanings of terms that apply to the whole pharmacopoeia.
4. **What is a master formula?** The official formula of a preparation for a stated quantity.
5. **What is a working formula?** The master formula scaled to the quantity to be prepared.
6. **Is Martindale an official book of standards?** No, it is a reference book of drug information.

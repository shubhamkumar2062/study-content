# Unit 7: Pharmaceutical manufacturing plant, quality control and quality assurance

This unit covers syllabus chapter 6: the basic structure, layout, sections and activities of a pharmaceutical manufacturing plant; definitions and concepts of quality control (QC) and quality assurance (QA); current good manufacturing practice (cGMP); and an introduction to calibration and validation.

## 7.1 Pharmaceutical manufacturing plant – basic structure

A pharmaceutical plant is a licensed factory where drugs and formulations are made, tested, packed and stored. In India it needs a **manufacturing licence** under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and its Rules, and must follow **Schedule M** (Good Manufacturing Practices and requirements of premises, plant and equipment).

### Requirements of the site and building

1. **Location:** away from sources of pollution, dust, smoke, open drains, and from residential crowding; good water supply and power; good transport.
2. **Building:** designed so that materials and people move in one direction with no back-flow (to avoid mix-ups and cross-contamination); enough space for each operation.
3. **Surfaces:** walls, floors and ceilings smooth, free from cracks, easy to clean and disinfect; coved (rounded) corners in production areas; no ledges collecting dust.
4. **Lighting, ventilation, temperature and humidity** suited to the products; air handling units (HVAC) with filters.
5. **Drainage** of adequate size with traps; no open drains in production areas.
6. **Protection** against insects, rodents and birds.
7. **Separate areas** for different activities; changing rooms, toilets not opening directly into production.

### Layout principle – flow of materials

```text
 RECEIPT → QUARANTINE → (QC sampling & approval) → APPROVED RAW MATERIAL STORE
     → DISPENSING (weighing) → PRODUCTION (mixing, granulation, compression,
       coating, filling) → IN-PROCESS CONTROL → PACKAGING → QUARANTINE
     → QC RELEASE → FINISHED GOODS STORE → DISPATCH

 Rule: one-way (linear or U-shaped) flow; people, materials and waste
 have separate routes; sterile area entered only through air locks.
```

### Sections of a pharmaceutical plant and their activities

| Section | Activities |
| --- | --- |
| **Stores (warehouse)** | Receipt, checking and quarantine of raw and packing materials; storage of approved materials under proper conditions (cool, cold room, dry); separate rejected-material area; first expiry, first out (FEFO) |
| **Dispensing (weighing) area** | Weighing of raw materials for each batch under dust control; checking by a second person |
| **Production blocks** | Separate blocks for tablets, capsules, oral liquids, external preparations, and sterile products; for β-lactam antibiotics, hormones and cytotoxic drugs, dedicated and segregated facilities |
| **Sterile (parenteral) area** | Clean rooms with HEPA-filtered air, air locks, gowning rooms; aseptic filling under Grade A laminar air flow; ampoule and vial washing, sterilisation, filling, sealing |
| **Packaging section** | Primary packing (strips, blisters, filling bottles), labelling, secondary packing; line clearance between batches |
| **Quality control laboratory** | Testing of raw materials, in-process samples, packing materials and finished products; chemical, instrumental and microbiological testing; stability studies; kept separate from production |
| **Quality assurance department** | Documentation, SOPs, batch record review, release of batches, audits, change control, deviations, complaints and recalls |
| **Engineering / maintenance** | Upkeep of machines and buildings, preventive maintenance, calibration of instruments |
| **Utilities** | Water system (purified water, water for injections), steam, compressed air, HVAC, power |
| **Effluent treatment plant (ETP)** | Treatment of waste water before discharge |
| **Administration, canteen, rest rooms** | Kept away from production areas |

### Clean area grades (sterile products)

| Grade | Typical use |
| --- | --- |
| A | Critical aseptic operations: filling, stoppering, open sterile containers (laminar air flow) |
| B | Background room for Grade A aseptic work |
| C | Less critical steps, e.g. solution preparation for products to be sterile filtered |
| D | Handling of components after washing, less critical steps |

## 7.2 Quality – basic concepts

**Quality** of a medicine means it is fit for its intended use: it has the right **identity, strength, purity and quality**, and is safe and effective, every time, in every batch.

Quality cannot be "tested into" a product only at the end; it must be **built in** at every step.

## 7.3 Quality control (QC)

**Definition:** quality control is the part of GMP concerned with **sampling, specifications and testing**, and with the organisation, documentation and release procedures that make sure the necessary tests are actually carried out and that materials are not released for use, or products for sale, until their quality has been judged satisfactory.

**Functions of QC**

1. Testing raw materials (active ingredients, excipients) against specifications.
2. Testing packing materials (containers, closures, labels, foils).
3. **In-process quality control (IPQC)** during manufacture: for tablets – weight, hardness, thickness, friability, disintegration; for liquids – pH, volume, clarity.
4. Testing finished products before release (assay, dissolution, sterility as required).
5. Stability testing and keeping reference (retention) samples.
6. Environmental monitoring of clean areas (air and surface microbial counts).
7. Calibration of laboratory instruments; keeping records.

QC is mainly **product-oriented** and **detects** defects.

## 7.4 Quality assurance (QA)

**Definition:** quality assurance is a wide concept covering **all matters that individually or collectively influence the quality of a product**. It is the total of the organised arrangements made to make sure that medicines are of the quality required for their intended use. QA includes GMP, which in turn includes QC.

```text
   ┌──────────────── QUALITY ASSURANCE ─────────────────┐
   │   (design, development, systems, documentation)     │
   │      ┌─────────────── GMP ────────────────┐         │
   │      │   (manufacturing practices)        │         │
   │      │      ┌──── QUALITY CONTROL ────┐   │         │
   │      │      │ sampling, testing,      │   │         │
   │      │      │ release decisions       │   │         │
   │      │      └─────────────────────────┘   │         │
   │      └────────────────────────────────────┘         │
   └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Functions of QA:** preparing and controlling SOPs and documents; reviewing batch manufacturing records and releasing batches; training staff; self-inspection and audits; vendor (supplier) approval; handling deviations, change control, complaints and **product recalls**; overseeing validation and calibration programmes.

### QC vs QA

| Point | Quality control | Quality assurance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focus | Product | Process and system |
| Nature | Reactive – finds defects | Proactive – prevents defects |
| Activities | Sampling, testing, inspection | Planning, documentation, audits, validation, training |
| Scope | Part of GMP | Wider; includes GMP and QC |
| Done by | QC laboratory | QA department, whole organisation |

## 7.5 Current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP)

**Definition:** GMP is the part of quality assurance which makes sure that products are **consistently produced and controlled to the quality standards** appropriate to their intended use and as required by the marketing authorisation. "**Current**" means firms must use up-to-date technology and systems, not just the minimum written long ago.

**In India:** GMP requirements are given in **Schedule M** of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945 (revised from time to time; a revised Schedule M aligned with WHO-GMP has been notified). WHO GMP, US FDA cGMP and EU GMP are international standards followed for export.

### Main elements of GMP (the "5 Ps" plus documents)

| Element | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| **Premises** | Suitable location, design, clean, one-way flow, separate areas (see 7.1) |
| **Personnel** | Qualified and trained staff; health checks; personal hygiene; proper clothing (gowns, caps, masks, gloves); no eating or smoking in production areas |
| **Plant and equipment** | Suitable design, easy to clean, made of non-reactive material (stainless steel 316), calibrated, maintained, labelled with status ("clean", "in use", "under maintenance") |
| **Processes** | Validated processes; written **standard operating procedures (SOPs)**; line clearance; prevention of mix-ups and cross-contamination |
| **Products and materials** | Approved suppliers; quarantine, sampling and testing before use; proper labelling and storage |
| **Documentation** | Master formula record, batch manufacturing record (BMR), batch packing record, SOPs, specifications, test records, distribution records – "if it is not written, it was not done" |
| Sanitation and hygiene | Cleaning schedules, pest control |
| Complaints and recalls | Systems to investigate complaints and to recall defective batches quickly |
| Self-inspection | Regular internal audits |

**Why GMP is important:** protects patients from contaminated, mixed-up or substandard medicines; ensures batch-to-batch consistency; required by law for licences and for export; reduces losses from rejected batches.

## 7.6 Calibration

**Definition:** calibration is the set of operations that establish, under specified conditions, the relationship between the values shown by a **measuring instrument** and the corresponding known values of a **reference standard**.

In simple words: checking an instrument against a known standard and adjusting or correcting it, so that its readings are accurate.

**Examples:**

| Instrument | Calibrated with |
| --- | --- |
| Weighing balance | Certified standard weights |
| pH meter | Standard buffer solutions (e.g. pH 4.0, 7.0, 9.2) |
| Thermometer / temperature sensor | Reference (certified) thermometer |
| Burette, pipette, volumetric flask | Weight of water delivered or held |
| UV spectrophotometer | Wavelength and absorbance standards (e.g. potassium dichromate) |
| Tablet hardness tester, disintegration apparatus | Reference loads; speed and temperature checks |

**Points:** calibration is done at a fixed frequency (daily, monthly, yearly) as per SOP; a calibration status label shows the date done and the date due; records are kept; instruments out of calibration are not used.

## 7.7 Validation

**Definition:** validation is the **documented evidence** that gives a **high degree of assurance** that a specific process, method, equipment or system will **consistently** produce a result meeting its predetermined specifications and quality attributes.

**Calibration vs validation:** calibration concerns the *accuracy of an instrument's readings*; validation concerns the *consistency of a whole process or system* and includes calibrated instruments.

### Types of validation (by time)

| Type | When | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Prospective** | Before a new product or process is released for sale | New tablet manufacturing process validated on three consecutive batches |
| **Concurrent** | During routine production, batches released as they are validated | Under special circumstances |
| **Retrospective** | Based on past production data of an established product | Reviewing records of many past batches |
| **Revalidation** | Repeat after a change (equipment, site, formula) or at set intervals | After changing a granulator |

### What is validated

- **Process validation** (e.g. granulation, compression, sterilisation, filling).
- **Cleaning validation** (no residue of the previous product or detergent).
- **Analytical method validation** (accuracy, precision, specificity, linearity, range, limits of detection and quantitation, robustness).
- **Equipment qualification** – stages: **DQ** (design qualification), **IQ** (installation qualification), **OQ** (operational qualification), **PQ** (performance qualification).
- Utilities: water system, HVAC; computer systems.

**Benefits of validation:** consistent quality, fewer rejections and recalls, lower cost, regulatory compliance, better understanding of the process.

## Remember

- GMP in India = **Schedule M** of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules.
- Plant layout: **one-way flow** – receipt → quarantine → approved store → dispensing → production → packing → QC release → dispatch.
- Sterile areas: Grade A (aseptic filling under laminar flow) to Grade D.
- QC = sampling, specifications, **testing** (product, detects defects); QA = **all systems** that assure quality (process, prevents defects). QA ⊃ GMP ⊃ QC.
- Key GMP documents: SOPs, master formula, **batch manufacturing record**.
- Calibration = instrument readings vs reference standard.
- Validation = documented evidence of **consistent** results; prospective, concurrent, retrospective, revalidation; DQ–IQ–OQ–PQ.

# How to use this guide

Pharmaceutics is the biggest first-year D.Pharm subject (75 theory hours). The syllabus has 7 chapters; chapter 5 (dosage forms) alone has about 41 hours, so in this guide it is split into three units (4, 5 and 6). Study one unit every two or three days: read the notes, draw every diagram once from memory, learn the tables, and then answer the question bank without looking.

| Guide unit | Syllabus chapter | Hours in syllabus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. History of pharmacy and pharmacopoeias | 1 | 7 |
| 2. Packaging materials and pharmaceutical aids | 2 and 3 | 5 + 3 |
| 3. Unit operations | 4 | 9 |
| 4. Tablets and capsules | 5 | 8 + 4 |
| 5. Liquid oral, topical, nasal and ear preparations | 5 | 6 + 8 + 2 |
| 6. Powders, granules, sterile and immunological products | 5 | 3 + 6 + 4 |
| 7. Manufacturing plant, QC, QA and cGMP | 6 | 5 |
| 8. Novel drug delivery systems | 7 | 5 |

## Exam pattern (ER-2020 theory paper)

D.Pharm ER-2020 exams are yearly. In Himachal Pradesh the board (HPTSB) conducts them under the PCI ER-2020 regulations. The theory paper is **80 marks, 3 hours**; another 20 marks come from sessional (internal) assessment. The usual ER-2020 board paper looks like this. Confirm the exact split with your college, because boards sometimes change the format.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Objective questions (MCQs / one-word / fill in the blanks) – all compulsory | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| II | Short answers – attempt 10 of 11 | 10 × 3 = 30 |
| III | Long answers – attempt 6 of 7 | 6 × 5 = 30 |

In this guide the **long** questions are 5-mark answers and the **short** questions are 3-mark answers, to match this pattern. A few **very-short** (2-mark) questions are included for quick revision and for sessional tests.

## Probability ratings

- **High** – asked in most years; prepare the full answer with diagrams and tables.
- **Medium** – asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** – rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

Ratings are an estimate from the syllabus weight and the kind of questions usually set in D.Pharm papers. They are not a promise.

## Scoring tips for Pharmaceutics

1. **Start with a definition.** Every answer – tablet, emulsion, validation, insufflation – should begin with a clear one- or two-line definition.
2. **Draw the equipment.** For hammer mill, ball mill, cyclone separator, fluidized bed dryer and triple roller mill, a labelled diagram earns marks. Then write principle → construction → working → uses → merits and demerits.
3. **Use tables for comparisons:** o/w vs w/o emulsion, flocculated vs deflocculated suspension, QC vs QA, liniments vs lotions, sugar vs film coating, types of glass.
4. **Quote official numbers correctly:** disintegration times, weight variation limits, friability (not more than 1%), simple syrup 66.7% w/w, 0.9% NaCl isotonic, autoclave 121 °C for 15 minutes.
5. **Give examples.** Name at least one real preparation for every type (Calamine Lotion, Turpentine Liniment, Lugol's solution, Ocusert, nitroglycerin patch).
6. **For a 5-mark long answer**, aim for about one and a half pages: definition, classification/table, diagram, 4–5 points of explanation, advantages and disadvantages.
7. **For a 3-mark short answer**, write 5–6 crisp points or a small table; do not write an essay.
8. Practical knowledge helps: questions on emulsions, suspensions, effervescent granules and injections often ask for a formula or method you prepared in the laboratory.

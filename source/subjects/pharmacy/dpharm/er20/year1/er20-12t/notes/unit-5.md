# Unit 5: Drugs acting on the central nervous system

For every drug group from here onwards the syllabus asks for: **classification, chemical name, chemical structure (only drugs marked \* in the syllabus), uses, stability and storage, formulations and popular brand names.** In this unit the starred drugs are thiopental sodium, ketamine, diazepam, alprazolam, phenobarbital, chlorpromazine, haloperidol, risperidone, sulpiride, phenytoin, carbamazepine, valproic acid, gabapentin, amitriptyline, imipramine and fluoxetine.

## 5.1 Anaesthetics

A **general anaesthetic** produces reversible loss of consciousness and sensation so that surgery can be done. Inhalational agents (halothane, isoflurane, sevoflurane, nitrous oxide) are given as gases/vapours; **intravenous agents** (below) are mostly used for **induction** (rapid start) of anaesthesia.

**Classification of IV anaesthetics**

| Class | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| Ultra-short-acting barbiturates | Thiopental sodium, methohexital |
| Dissociative anaesthetic | Ketamine |
| Phenol derivative | Propofol |
| Others | Etomidate, benzodiazepines (midazolam) |

### Thiopental sodium\*

- **Chemical name:** sodium salt of 5-ethyl-5-(1-methylbutyl)-2-thiobarbituric acid.
- **Structure:** a barbiturate (pyrimidine-2,4,6-trione) ring in which the oxygen at C-2 is replaced by **sulphur** (thio); C-5 carries an ethyl and a 1-methylbutyl group.

```text
              S
              ‖
        HN    C    N⁻ Na⁺        (enolised at N-1/C-2 in the sodium salt)
          \  / \  /
           C     C
          ‖       ‖
          O   C   O
             / \
     C2H5   CH(CH3)CH2CH2CH3
```

- **Why so fast and short?** The sulphur makes it very **lipid soluble**, so it enters the brain within one arm–brain circulation time (seconds). Its effect ends in minutes because it **redistributes** from the brain to muscle and fat.
- **Uses:** induction of general anaesthesia; short procedures; control of status epilepticus; raised intracranial pressure.
- **Stability and storage:** supplied as a dry powder (with sodium carbonate) in sealed vials; the reconstituted solution is strongly **alkaline** and unstable (absorbs CO2 and precipitates the free acid), so prepare fresh and discard unused solution. Store powder protected from light and moisture. The alkaline solution is irritant; extravasation or intra-arterial injection causes tissue damage.
- **Formulation:** powder for injection (e.g. Pentothal; Thiosol).

### Ketamine hydrochloride\*

- **Chemical name:** 2-(2-chlorophenyl)-2-(methylamino)cyclohexan-1-one hydrochloride.
- **Structure:** a cyclohexanone ring; on C-2 an o-chlorophenyl group and a methylamino group (C-2 is a chiral centre; used as racemate).
- **Action:** blocks **NMDA** (glutamate) receptors → **dissociative anaesthesia** (patient appears awake, eyes open, but feels no pain and has amnesia); strong **analgesia**; raises blood pressure and heart rate; bronchodilator.
- **Uses:** induction and short procedures, especially in shocked patients, asthmatics and children; burns dressing; field anaesthesia.
- **Adverse effects:** emergence reactions (hallucinations, vivid dreams), raised BP and intracranial pressure. It is a drug of abuse.
- **Storage:** vials at room temperature, protected from light; controlled drug.
- **Formulation:** injection (e.g. Ketalar, Ketmin).

### Propofol

- **Chemical name:** 2,6-diisopropylphenol (2,6-bis(propan-2-yl)phenol).
- Oily liquid; formulated as a **1% white oil-in-water emulsion** (soybean oil, egg lecithin, glycerol).
- **Uses:** induction and maintenance of anaesthesia, day-care surgery (smooth, quick recovery with little hangover), sedation in ICU; has antiemetic action.
- **Storage:** do not freeze; shake before use; the emulsion supports bacterial growth, so use aseptically and discard unused drug promptly after opening.
- **Adverse effects:** pain on injection, fall in BP, respiratory depression.

## 5.2 Sedatives and hypnotics

A **sedative** calms and reduces anxiety; a **hypnotic** produces sleep. The same drug often does both depending on dose.

**Classification**

| Class | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| Barbiturates | Phenobarbital (long acting), pentobarbital, thiopental (ultra-short) |
| Benzodiazepines | Diazepam, alprazolam, nitrazepam, lorazepam, clonazepam, midazolam |
| Non-benzodiazepine (Z-drugs) | Zolpidem, zopiclone |
| Others | Chloral hydrate, melatonin agonists |

**Mechanism:** both act on the **GABA-A receptor–chloride channel**. Benzodiazepines **increase the frequency** of channel opening; barbiturates **increase the duration** of opening (and at high doses open it directly), which is why barbiturates are more dangerous in overdose. Benzodiazepine overdose is reversed by **flumazenil**.

**Basic benzodiazepine structure (1,4-benzodiazepine)**

```text
            R1   O
            |    ‖
       ____ N ── C
      /    \      \
 R7 ─|  A   |      CH2        Ring A: benzene (R7 = Cl or NO2 → activity)
      \____/      /           Ring B: seven-membered, N at 1 and 4
            C ═ N             Ring C: 5-phenyl
            |
           Ph (ring C)
```

An electron-withdrawing group at **position 7** (Cl, NO2) is essential for activity.

### Diazepam\*

- **Chemical name:** 7-chloro-1-methyl-5-phenyl-1,3-dihydro-2H-1,4-benzodiazepin-2-one.
- **Structure:** benzodiazepine nucleus with Cl at C-7, methyl on N-1, phenyl at C-5, C=O at C-2.
- **Uses:** anxiety, insomnia (short term), status epilepticus and febrile convulsions (IV or rectal), muscle spasm, alcohol withdrawal, preoperative sedation.
- **Points:** long acting (active metabolite desmethyldiazepam); dependence and withdrawal on long use; avoid with alcohol.
- **Storage:** tablets well-closed, protected from light; injection protected from light (absorbs into PVC plastic).
- **Formulations/brands:** tablets, injection, rectal solution (e.g. Valium, Calmpose).

### Alprazolam\*

- **Chemical name:** 8-chloro-1-methyl-6-phenyl-4H-[1,2,4]triazolo[4,3-a][1,4]benzodiazepine.
- **Structure:** a **triazolo-benzodiazepine**: a triazole ring fused to the benzodiazepine (on the 1,2-bond), Cl on the benzene ring, phenyl group.
- **Uses:** anxiety disorders, **panic disorder**, anxiety with depression.
- **Points:** short-to-intermediate acting; high dependence potential; Schedule H.
- **Storage:** well-closed, room temperature, protected from light and moisture.
- **Brands:** Alprax, Restyl (tablets, sustained-release tablets).

### Nitrazepam

- 7-nitro-5-phenyl-1,3-dihydro-2H-1,4-benzodiazepin-2-one.
- Used as a **hypnotic** for insomnia and in some infant seizures (myoclonic).
- Brand: Nitrosun, Hypnotex. Store protected from light.

### Phenobarbital\* (phenobarbitone)

- **Chemical name:** 5-ethyl-5-phenylpyrimidine-2,4,6(1H,3H,5H)-trione (5-ethyl-5-phenylbarbituric acid).
- **Structure:** barbituric acid ring (pyrimidine-2,4,6-trione) with **ethyl and phenyl** groups at C-5. Both C-5 hydrogens must be replaced for hypnotic activity.

```text
             O
             ‖
        HN ─ C ─ NH
        |         |
   O ═ C          C ═ O
         \       /
           C
          / \
      C2H5   C6H5
```

- **Uses:** long-acting **anticonvulsant** (generalised tonic-clonic and partial seizures, status epilepticus, febrile seizures in children), sedative; neonatal jaundice (enzyme inducer, rarely).
- **Points:** powerful **enzyme inducer** (reduces effect of oral contraceptives, warfarin); sedation; dependence; dangerous in overdose.
- **Stability/storage:** well-closed container; sodium salt solution is alkaline and hydrolyses on standing, so injection is prepared fresh or formulated in non-aqueous solvent (propylene glycol).
- **Brands:** Gardenal, Luminal (tablets, injection).

## 5.3 Antipsychotics (neuroleptics)

Used in **schizophrenia**, mania and other psychoses. Most block **dopamine D2** receptors in the brain; atypical agents also block **5-HT2A** receptors (fewer movement side effects).

**Classification**

| Class | Group | Drugs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical | Phenothiazines | Chlorpromazine, triflupromazine, thioridazine, fluphenazine |
| Typical | Butyrophenones | Haloperidol, droperidol |
| Typical | Substituted benzamides | Sulpiride |
| Atypical | Benzisoxazole | Risperidone |
| Atypical | Thienobenzodiazepine | Olanzapine |
| Atypical | Dibenzothiazepine | Quetiapine |
| Atypical | Benzisothiazole | Lurasidone |

**Common adverse effects:** extrapyramidal symptoms (parkinsonism, acute dystonia, akathisia, tardive dyskinesia) – more with typical drugs; raised prolactin; weight gain and diabetes (olanzapine, quetiapine); sedation; anticholinergic and α-blocking effects.

### Chlorpromazine hydrochloride\*

- **Chemical name:** 3-(2-chloro-10H-phenothiazin-10-yl)-N,N-dimethylpropan-1-amine hydrochloride.
- **Structure:** **phenothiazine** tricyclic nucleus; **Cl at position 2**; a three-carbon chain to a dimethylamino group on **N-10**. A three-carbon bridge between the ring N and side-chain N is needed for antipsychotic activity.

```text
            S
       ____/ \____
      /    \ /    \
      |     X     |─ Cl (2)
      \____/ \____/
            N
            |
            CH2CH2CH2N(CH3)2
```

- **Uses:** schizophrenia, mania, **antiemetic**, intractable **hiccups**, tetanus (adjunct), potentiation of anaesthesia.
- **Adverse effects:** sedation, postural hypotension, extrapyramidal effects, jaundice, **photosensitivity**, skin pigmentation.
- **Stability/storage:** phenothiazines are **oxidised by light and air** to sulphoxides; solutions turn pink/yellow. Store in **light-resistant**, well-closed containers; do not use discoloured injections. Contact dermatitis in staff who handle it.
- **Brands:** Largactil (tablets, injection).

### Haloperidol\*

- **Chemical name:** 4-[4-(4-chlorophenyl)-4-hydroxypiperidin-1-yl]-1-(4-fluorophenyl)butan-1-one.
- **Structure:** a **butyrophenone**: p-fluorophenyl ketone + three-carbon chain + 4-hydroxy-4-(p-chlorophenyl)piperidine.
- **Uses:** schizophrenia, acute mania, agitation and delirium, Tourette's syndrome, antiemetic. Long-acting **decanoate** depot injection.
- **Adverse effects:** marked extrapyramidal symptoms; little sedation or hypotension.
- **Storage:** protect from light.
- **Brands:** Serenace, Senorm (tablets, injection, decanoate depot).

### Risperidone\*

- **Chemical name:** 3-[2-[4-(6-fluoro-1,2-benzisoxazol-3-yl)piperidin-1-yl]ethyl]-2-methyl-6,7,8,9-tetrahydro-4H-pyrido[1,2-a]pyrimidin-4-one.
- **Structure:** **benzisoxazole** (with F) joined through a piperidine and an ethyl chain to a pyrido-pyrimidinone ring.
- **Action:** atypical; blocks 5-HT2A and D2.
- **Uses:** schizophrenia, bipolar mania, irritability in autism.
- **Adverse effects:** weight gain, raised prolactin, EPS at higher doses.
- **Brands:** Risdone, Sizodon (tablets, oral solution, long-acting injection).

### Sulpiride\*

- **Chemical name:** N-[(1-ethylpyrrolidin-2-yl)methyl]-2-methoxy-5-sulfamoylbenzamide.
- **Structure:** a **substituted benzamide** – benzene ring with methoxy (2) and sulphamoyl (5) groups, amide linked to an N-ethylpyrrolidinylmethyl group.
- **Action:** selective D2/D3 blocker.
- **Uses:** schizophrenia (both positive and negative symptoms), depression in low doses (in some countries), vertigo.
- **Adverse effects:** high prolactin (galactorrhoea, menstrual disturbance).
- **Brand:** Sulpitac, Dolmatil (tablets).

### Other atypicals (no structure needed)

| Drug | Ring system | Key point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Olanzapine | Thienobenzodiazepine | Effective; weight gain, diabetes risk |
| Quetiapine | Dibenzothiazepine | Sedating; bipolar depression; low EPS |
| Lurasidone | Benzisothiazole derivative | Less weight gain; take with food |

## 5.4 Anticonvulsants (antiepileptics)

**Epilepsy** is recurrent seizures due to abnormal, excessive firing of brain neurons. Antiepileptics reduce firing by (a) blocking **sodium channels**, (b) enhancing **GABA**, or (c) blocking **T-type calcium channels**.

**Classification**

| Class | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| Barbiturates | Phenobarbital |
| Hydantoins | Phenytoin |
| Iminostilbenes (dibenzazepines) | Carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine |
| Benzodiazepines | Clonazepam, diazepam, lorazepam |
| Aliphatic carboxylic acids | Valproic acid (sodium valproate) |
| GABA analogues | Gabapentin, vigabatrin, pregabalin |
| Phenyltriazine | Lamotrigine |
| Sulphamate | Topiramate |

### Phenytoin\*

- **Chemical name:** 5,5-diphenylimidazolidine-2,4-dione (5,5-diphenylhydantoin).
- **Structure:** **hydantoin** ring (five-membered, two N, C=O at 2 and 4) with **two phenyl groups on C-5**.

```text
          H
          N
        /   \
   O ═ C     C ═ O
       |     |
      HN ─── C
            / \
          Ph   Ph
```

- **Action:** blocks voltage-gated **sodium channels** (stabilises the inactive state).
- **Uses:** generalised tonic-clonic and partial seizures, status epilepticus (IV), trigeminal neuralgia, digitalis-induced arrhythmias.
- **Adverse effects:** **gum hypertrophy**, hirsutism, coarse facial features, ataxia, nystagmus, megaloblastic anaemia, teratogenic (fetal hydantoin syndrome). Shows **saturation (zero-order) kinetics** at higher doses, so small dose increases can cause toxicity; blood level monitoring is useful.
- **Stability:** phenytoin sodium injection is strongly alkaline; it **precipitates in dextrose** solutions – dilute only in normal saline and inject slowly. Tablets/capsules well-closed.
- **Brands:** Eptoin, Dilantin (tablets, capsules, suspension, injection).

### Carbamazepine\*

- **Chemical name:** 5H-dibenzo[b,f]azepine-5-carboxamide.
- **Structure:** **iminostilbene** (two benzene rings fused to a central seven-membered ring with N and a C=C) with a **carboxamide (–CONH2)** on the ring N.
- **Uses:** partial and generalised tonic-clonic seizures (drug of choice for partial seizures), **trigeminal neuralgia** (drug of choice), bipolar disorder.
- **Adverse effects:** dizziness, diplopia, hyponatraemia, rash (serious Stevens–Johnson syndrome, more in people with HLA-B\*1502), blood dyscrasias. Enzyme inducer, including **auto-induction** of its own metabolism.
- **Storage:** protect from **moisture** – tablets exposed to humidity can lose effectiveness; keep in tightly closed containers.
- **Brands:** Tegretol, Mazetol (tablets, CR tablets, syrup).

### Clonazepam

- Nitro-benzodiazepine (5-(2-chlorophenyl)-7-nitro…). Used in absence and myoclonic seizures, panic disorder. Brand: Rivotril.

### Valproic acid\* (sodium valproate)

- **Chemical name:** 2-propylpentanoic acid.
- **Structure:** a simple **branched-chain carboxylic acid**: CH3CH2CH2–CH(COOH)–CH2CH2CH3 (an eight-carbon fatty acid).
- **Action:** blocks Na channels and T-type Ca channels; increases GABA.
- **Uses:** **broad spectrum** – absence, myoclonic, generalised tonic-clonic and partial seizures; bipolar disorder; migraine prophylaxis.
- **Adverse effects:** nausea, weight gain, hair loss, tremor, **hepatotoxicity** (especially young children), pancreatitis, **teratogenic** (neural tube defects – avoid in women who may become pregnant unless no alternative).
- **Stability:** sodium valproate is **hygroscopic**; tablets are enteric coated or sustained-release and must be kept in airtight containers away from moisture.
- **Brands:** Valparin, Encorate (tablets, syrup, injection).

### Gabapentin\*

- **Chemical name:** 2-[1-(aminomethyl)cyclohexyl]acetic acid.
- **Structure:** a **GABA molecule built onto a cyclohexane ring** (aminomethyl and acetic acid groups on the same ring carbon), which makes it lipid soluble enough to enter the brain.
- **Action:** despite the name it does not act on GABA receptors; it binds the **α2δ subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels**, reducing release of excitatory transmitters.
- **Uses:** add-on in partial seizures; **neuropathic pain** (diabetic neuropathy, post-herpetic neuralgia).
- **Points:** not metabolised; excreted unchanged by kidney (adjust dose in renal failure); sedation, dizziness.
- **Brands:** Gabapin, Neurontin (capsules, tablets).

### Other anticonvulsants

| Drug | Nature | Key point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Topiramate | Sulphamate-substituted fructose (monosaccharide) derivative | Partial and generalised seizures, migraine prophylaxis; weight loss, kidney stones |
| Vigabatrin | γ-vinyl GABA | Irreversibly inhibits GABA-transaminase; infantile spasms; visual field loss |
| Lamotrigine | Phenyltriazine | Blocks Na channels; broad spectrum, bipolar depression; skin rash |

## 5.5 Antidepressants

Depression is linked with low brain **noradrenaline (NA)** and **serotonin (5-HT)** activity. Most antidepressants increase these amines at synapses. Effect takes **2–4 weeks**.

**Classification**

| Class | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| Tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs) | Imipramine, amitriptyline, clomipramine, nortriptyline |
| Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) | Fluoxetine, sertraline, citalopram, escitalopram, fluvoxamine, paroxetine |
| Serotonin–noradrenaline reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs) | Venlafaxine, duloxetine |
| MAO inhibitors | Moclobemide (reversible) |
| Atypical | Mirtazapine, bupropion, trazodone |

**TCA adverse effects:** anticholinergic (dry mouth, constipation, urinary retention, blurred vision), sedation, postural hypotension, cardiac arrhythmias, weight gain; **dangerous in overdose**.

### Imipramine hydrochloride\*

- **Chemical name:** 3-(10,11-dihydro-5H-dibenzo[b,f]azepin-5-yl)-N,N-dimethylpropan-1-amine hydrochloride.
- **Structure:** **dibenzazepine** tricyclic nucleus (central seven-membered ring with N, saturated 10,11-bond) with a three-carbon chain to a dimethylamino group on the ring N.
- **Action:** inhibits reuptake of NA and 5-HT.
- **Uses:** depression, **nocturnal enuresis** in children, panic disorder.
- **Storage:** protect from light.
- **Brands:** Depsonil, Antidep (tablets).

### Amitriptyline hydrochloride\*

- **Chemical name:** 3-(10,11-dihydro-5H-dibenzo[a,d]cyclohepten-5-ylidene)-N,N-dimethylpropan-1-amine hydrochloride.
- **Structure:** like imipramine, but the central ring has **carbon** in place of nitrogen, joined to the side chain by a **double bond** (ylidene). Dibenzocycloheptene nucleus.

```text
  Imipramine:   ring N ─ CH2CH2CH2N(CH3)2      (dibenzazepine)
  Amitriptyline: ring C ═ CHCH2CH2N(CH3)2      (dibenzocycloheptene)
```

- **Uses:** depression (especially with insomnia – it is sedating), **neuropathic pain**, **migraine prophylaxis**, tension headache.
- **Brands:** Tryptomer, Amitone (tablets).

### Fluoxetine\*

- **Chemical name:** N-methyl-3-phenyl-3-[4-(trifluoromethyl)phenoxy]propan-1-amine (used as hydrochloride; racemic).
- **Structure:** a phenylpropylamine; the central carbon carries a **phenyl** and a **p-trifluoromethylphenoxy** group; terminal N-methylamine.
- **Action:** **SSRI** – selectively blocks serotonin reuptake.
- **Uses:** depression, obsessive-compulsive disorder, bulimia, panic disorder, premenstrual dysphoric disorder.
- **Points:** very **long half-life** (active metabolite norfluoxetine), so once daily and slow washout; nausea, insomnia, sexual dysfunction; few anticholinergic effects; safer in overdose than TCAs. Never combine with MAO inhibitors (serotonin syndrome).
- **Brands:** Fludac, Prodep (capsules, tablets).

### Other SSRIs and SNRIs

| Drug | Class | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sertraline | SSRI | Widely used; safe in cardiac patients |
| Citalopram / escitalopram | SSRI | Escitalopram is the active S-isomer of citalopram |
| Fluvoxamine | SSRI | OCD; many drug interactions |
| Paroxetine | SSRI | Short half-life; withdrawal symptoms |
| Venlafaxine | SNRI | Depression, anxiety; can raise BP |
| Duloxetine | SNRI | Depression, diabetic neuropathic pain |

## Remember

- Thiopental: 2-thio barbiturate, ultra-short (redistribution), alkaline unstable solution – make fresh.
- Ketamine: NMDA blocker, dissociative, raises BP, emergence reactions. Propofol: 2,6-diisopropylphenol emulsion.
- Benzodiazepines ↑ frequency, barbiturates ↑ duration of GABA-A Cl⁻ opening; 7-Cl/NO2 needed; antidote flumazenil.
- Phenobarbital: 5-ethyl-5-phenyl barbiturate; enzyme inducer.
- Chlorpromazine: 2-Cl phenothiazine, light sensitive; haloperidol: butyrophenone, high EPS; risperidone: benzisoxazole; sulpiride: benzamide.
- Phenytoin: 5,5-diphenylhydantoin, gum hypertrophy, zero-order kinetics, no dextrose. Carbamazepine: iminostilbene carboxamide, trigeminal neuralgia, keep dry.
- Valproic acid = 2-propylpentanoic acid, broad spectrum, hepatotoxic, teratogenic. Gabapentin binds α2δ.
- Imipramine (N-ring, enuresis) vs amitriptyline (C=, neuropathic pain); fluoxetine SSRI, long half-life.

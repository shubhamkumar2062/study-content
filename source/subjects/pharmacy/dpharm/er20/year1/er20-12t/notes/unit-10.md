# Unit 10: Analgesic and anti-inflammatory agents

An **analgesic** relieves pain without loss of consciousness. Analgesics are of two broad types:

| | Opioid (narcotic) analgesics | Non-opioid analgesics (NSAIDs, paracetamol) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Site | Brain and spinal cord (opioid receptors) | Mainly peripheral (inhibit prostaglandin synthesis) |
| Pain type | Severe pain, visceral pain | Mild to moderate pain, inflammatory pain |
| Anti-inflammatory | No | Yes (except paracetamol) |
| Antipyretic | No | Yes |
| Dependence | Yes | No |

The syllabus covers **morphine analogues, narcotic antagonists and NSAIDs: aspirin\*, diclofenac, ibuprofen\*, piroxicam, celecoxib, mefenamic acid, paracetamol\* and aceclofenac**.

## 10.1 Morphine and its analogues (opioid analgesics)

**Morphine** is the main alkaloid of **opium** (dried latex of the unripe capsules of *Papaver somniferum*). It acts on **μ (mu) opioid receptors** in the brain and spinal cord.

### 10.1.1 Structure of morphine

Morphine is a **pentacyclic** (five-ring) alkaloid containing a **phenanthrene** nucleus partly reduced, an oxygen bridge (furan ring), and a piperidine ring with N-methyl. Important groups:

```text
   Position 3 : phenolic –OH        (methylation → codeine, weaker)
   Position 6 : alcoholic –OH        (oxidation to ketone, or acetylation → more potent)
   Position 7–8 : double bond         (reduction → dihydro compounds)
   Position 17 : N–CH3               (replace by allyl / cyclopropylmethyl → antagonist)
   Ring E : piperidine; 4,5-epoxy (oxygen) bridge
```

**Key features needed for opioid activity (morphine rule):** a tertiary basic nitrogen, a quaternary carbon attached to an aromatic ring, and a two-carbon bridge between them (as in the piperidine ring).

### 10.1.2 Classification of opioids

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Natural opium alkaloids | Morphine, codeine |
| Semi-synthetic | Diacetylmorphine (heroin), hydromorphone, oxymorphone, oxycodone, buprenorphine |
| Synthetic | Pethidine (meperidine – phenylpiperidine), methadone (diphenylheptane), fentanyl (anilidopiperidine), tramadol |
| Agonist–antagonists | Pentazocine, nalorphine, butorphanol |
| Pure antagonists | Naloxone, naltrexone |

### 10.1.3 Morphine

- **Uses:** severe pain (cancer, myocardial infarction, post-operative, trauma), acute left ventricular failure (pulmonary oedema – relieves breathlessness), pre-anaesthetic.
- **Effects:** analgesia, euphoria, sedation, **respiratory depression**, miosis (pin-point pupils), nausea, constipation, cough suppression, urinary retention, histamine release.
- **Problems:** **tolerance, dependence (addiction)**; overdose → coma, pin-point pupils, respiratory depression (treated with naloxone).
- **Storage and law:** a narcotic drug under the NDPS Act; kept locked; injection protected from light (morphine darkens on oxidation).

### 10.1.4 Morphine analogues

| Drug | Change from morphine / class | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Codeine | 3-O-methyl ether of morphine | Cough (antitussive), mild–moderate pain; constipation |
| Diacetylmorphine (heroin) | 3,6-diacetyl ester | Very addictive; not used medically in India |
| Hydromorphone | 6-ketone, 7,8-dihydro | Severe pain (potent) |
| Pethidine | Synthetic phenylpiperidine | Labour pain, post-operative pain; metabolite norpethidine causes fits |
| Methadone | Synthetic, long acting | Opioid de-addiction (substitution), chronic pain |
| Fentanyl | Synthetic, very potent (about 80–100 × morphine) | Anaesthesia, transdermal patch for chronic pain |
| Tramadol | Synthetic, weak μ agonist + inhibits NA/5-HT reuptake | Moderate pain; risk of seizures |

## 10.2 Narcotic (opioid) antagonists

These block opioid receptors and reverse the effects of opioids. Replacing the **N-methyl** of morphine/oxymorphone by an **allyl** or **cyclopropylmethyl** group gives antagonist activity.

| Drug | Chemistry | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Naloxone** | N-allyl-noroxymorphone (14-OH) | Drug of choice for **opioid overdose** (IV/IM/nasal); short acting – may need repeated doses; also reverses opioid respiratory depression in newborns |
| **Naltrexone** | N-cyclopropylmethyl-noroxymorphone | Orally active, long acting; maintenance after opioid detoxification; **alcohol dependence** |
| **Nalorphine** | N-allyl-normorphine | Mixed agonist–antagonist; historical use |

## 10.3 Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs)

**Mechanism:** NSAIDs inhibit the enzyme **cyclo-oxygenase (COX)**, which converts arachidonic acid into **prostaglandins and thromboxanes**.

```text
   Membrane phospholipids —(phospholipase A2)→ Arachidonic acid
                                                   │ COX-1, COX-2  ← NSAIDs block here
                                                   ↓
                         Prostaglandins (pain, fever, inflammation),
                         thromboxane A2 (platelet aggregation), prostacyclin
```

- **COX-1** (constitutive): protects stomach lining, platelet function, kidney blood flow.
- **COX-2** (inducible): made at sites of inflammation.
- Non-selective NSAIDs block both → effective but cause **gastric irritation/ulcers**; **COX-2 selective** drugs (celecoxib) cause less stomach injury but more cardiovascular risk.

**Actions:** analgesic, antipyretic, anti-inflammatory, antiplatelet (aspirin).

**Common adverse effects:** gastric pain, peptic ulcer and bleeding, kidney damage and fluid retention, raised BP, bronchospasm in sensitive asthmatics, rashes. Take after food.

### 10.3.1 Classification (chemical)

| Class | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| Salicylates | **Aspirin**, sodium salicylate, diflunisal |
| p-Aminophenol derivative | **Paracetamol** (analgesic–antipyretic; very weak anti-inflammatory) |
| Propionic acid derivatives | **Ibuprofen**, naproxen, ketoprofen, flurbiprofen |
| Aryl/heteroaryl acetic acids | **Diclofenac**, **aceclofenac**, indomethacin, ketorolac |
| Anthranilic acids (fenamates) | **Mefenamic acid** |
| Enolic acids (oxicams) | **Piroxicam**, meloxicam |
| Pyrazolones | Metamizole, phenylbutazone |
| COX-2 selective (coxibs) | **Celecoxib**, etoricoxib |

### Aspirin\* (acetylsalicylic acid)

- **Chemical name:** 2-acetoxybenzoic acid (2-(acetyloxy)benzoic acid).
- **Structure:** benzene ring with a **carboxylic acid (–COOH)** and, next to it (ortho), an **acetate ester (–O–CO–CH3)**.

```text
        COOH
        |
     __/__
    /     \── O ── C(=O) ── CH3
    |     |
    \_____/
```

- **Preparation (outline):** salicylic acid + acetic anhydride (with a little H2SO4/H3PO4) → aspirin + acetic acid.
- **Mechanism:** **irreversibly acetylates** a serine in COX; in platelets (which cannot make new enzyme) the effect lasts the platelet's life (7–10 days).
- **Uses and doses:**
  - Pain and fever: 300–600 mg every 4–6 hours (adults).
  - Anti-inflammatory (rheumatic fever): high doses (now rarely).
  - **Antiplatelet:** **75–150 mg once daily** to prevent heart attack and stroke, and after myocardial infarction.
- **Adverse effects:** gastric irritation and bleeding; **Reye's syndrome** in children with viral fever (avoid aspirin in children below 12–16 years except special indications); **salicylism** at high doses (tinnitus, vertigo); aspirin-induced asthma; bleeding tendency.
- **Stability:** in moist air aspirin **hydrolyses** to **salicylic acid and acetic acid** – old tablets smell of **vinegar**. Store in **airtight containers** in a cool dry place. Pharmacopoeia limits **free salicylic acid**.
- **Formulations/brands:** tablets, dispersible tablets (Disprin), enteric-coated low-dose tablets (Ecosprin), combination with clopidogrel or atorvastatin.

### Paracetamol\* (acetaminophen)

- **Chemical name:** N-(4-hydroxyphenyl)acetamide (4'-hydroxyacetanilide).
- **Structure:** a **p-aminophenol** derivative: benzene ring with –OH and –NH–CO–CH3 in **para** positions.

```text
   HO ──⟨ benzene ⟩── NH ── C(=O) ── CH3      (para-substituted)
```

- **Mechanism:** inhibits COX mainly in the brain (where peroxide levels are low); weak in inflamed tissues → good analgesic and antipyretic, very weak anti-inflammatory; does not irritate the stomach or affect platelets.
- **Uses and dose:** fever and mild–moderate pain (headache, toothache, body ache, dysmenorrhoea). Adults: **500 mg–1 g every 4–6 hours, not more than 4 g in 24 hours**. Safe in children (dose by weight), pregnancy and peptic ulcer patients.
- **Toxicity:** **overdose causes liver necrosis**. A small part is normally oxidised to the toxic metabolite **NAPQI** (N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine), which glutathione detoxifies. In overdose, glutathione is used up and NAPQI damages liver cells. **Antidote: N-acetylcysteine** (best within 8–10 hours). Risk higher in alcoholics and the malnourished.
- **Stability:** stable; store well-closed, protected from light and moisture. Pharmacopoeia limits **4-aminophenol** (hydrolysis product).
- **Formulations/brands:** tablets, syrups, drops, suppositories, IV infusion; Crocin, Calpol, Dolo; with ibuprofen (Combiflam) or other drugs.

### Ibuprofen\*

- **Chemical name:** (RS)-2-(4-isobutylphenyl)propanoic acid (2-[4-(2-methylpropyl)phenyl]propanoic acid).
- **Structure:** a **propionic acid**: benzene ring with an **isobutyl** group at the para position and –CH(CH3)–COOH. The α-carbon is chiral; the **S-(+) isomer is active**, and the R-isomer is partly converted to S in the body; sold as racemate.

```text
   (CH3)2CH–CH2 ──⟨ benzene ⟩── CH(CH3) ── COOH
```

- **Uses and dose:** pain, fever, dysmenorrhoea, rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, soft-tissue injury. Adults commonly 200–400 mg three to four times daily after food.
- **Points:** one of the best tolerated non-selective NSAIDs (less gastric irritation than aspirin at analgesic dose).
- **Storage:** well-closed container.
- **Assay:** acid–base titration with sodium hydroxide in ethanol (it is a carboxylic acid) – done in practicals.
- **Formulations/brands:** tablets, suspension; Brufen, Ibugesic; Combiflam (with paracetamol).

### Diclofenac

- **Chemistry:** 2-[2-(2,6-dichloroanilino)phenyl]acetic acid – a **phenylacetic acid** with a dichlorophenylamino group; used as sodium or potassium salt.
- Potent anti-inflammatory; used in arthritis, back pain, post-operative pain, renal colic; also gel and eye drops.
- Adverse effects: GI, raised liver enzymes, cardiovascular risk. Brand: Voveran.

### Aceclofenac

- **Glycolic acid ester of diclofenac** (–CH2–COO–CH2–COOH); partly converted to diclofenac.
- Claimed better GI tolerance. Used in osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis. Brands: Hifenac, Zerodol.

### Piroxicam

- An **oxicam** (enolic acid, 1,2-benzothiazine with a pyridyl carboxamide).
- **Long half-life (~2 days) → once daily**; more GI side effects.
- Used in rheumatoid and osteoarthritis. Brand: Dolonex.

### Celecoxib

- A **diaryl-substituted pyrazole** carrying a **sulphonamide** group and a trifluoromethyl group.
- **COX-2 selective**: less gastric ulceration but increased risk of heart attack and stroke; avoid in sulpha allergy.
- Used in arthritis, acute pain, dysmenorrhoea. Brand: Celebrex.

### Mefenamic acid

- An **anthranilic acid (fenamate)**: N-(2,3-dimethylphenyl)anthranilic acid.
- Used mainly for **dysmenorrhoea** and menorrhagia, mild pain; diarrhoea is a typical adverse effect. Brand: Meftal.

## 10.4 Summary of starred NSAIDs

| Drug | Class | Chemical name | Key point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aspirin | Salicylate | 2-acetoxybenzoic acid | Irreversible COX block; 75–150 mg antiplatelet; Reye's syndrome; hydrolyses (vinegar smell) |
| Paracetamol | p-Aminophenol | N-(4-hydroxyphenyl)acetamide | No anti-inflammatory action; max 4 g/day; NAPQI liver damage; antidote N-acetylcysteine |
| Ibuprofen | Propionic acid | 2-(4-isobutylphenyl)propanoic acid | S-isomer active; well tolerated |

## Remember

- Morphine: pentacyclic phenanthrene alkaloid; 3-OH phenolic (codeine = 3-methyl ether), 6-OH alcoholic; N-CH3 → N-allyl gives antagonists.
- Naloxone = drug of choice in opioid overdose; naltrexone = oral, long acting (de-addiction, alcoholism).
- NSAIDs block COX → ↓ prostaglandins; COX-1 protects stomach, COX-2 at inflammation; celecoxib COX-2 selective.
- Aspirin: acetylates COX irreversibly; store airtight (hydrolysis → salicylic acid + acetic acid).
- Paracetamol: max 4 g/day; NAPQI; N-acetylcysteine.
- Ibuprofen: propionic acid; diclofenac and aceclofenac: phenylacetic acids; mefenamic acid: fenamate (dysmenorrhoea); piroxicam: oxicam once daily.

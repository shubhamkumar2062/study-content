# Unit 8: Diuretics

A **diuretic** increases the output of urine by increasing the excretion of **sodium and water** (natriuresis) by the kidney. Diuretics are used in oedema (heart failure, liver cirrhosis, kidney disease), hypertension, glaucoma and some electrolyte disorders. The syllabus lists acetazolamide, frusemide\* (furosemide), bumetanide, chlorthalidone, benzthiazide, metolazone, xipamide and spironolactone.

## 8.1 How the kidney handles sodium (where diuretics act)

```text
   Glomerulus → Proximal tubule → Loop of Henle → Distal tubule → Collecting duct → urine
                 (~65% Na reabsorbed) (thick ascending   (~5–8%)       (~2–3%, aldosterone
                                       limb ~25%)                       controlled)

   Site 1  Proximal tubule ........... carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (acetazolamide)
   Site 2  Thick ascending limb ...... loop diuretics (frusemide, bumetanide)
   Site 3  Early distal tubule ....... thiazides and thiazide-like (benzthiazide,
                                        chlorthalidone, metolazone, xipamide)
   Site 4  Late distal / collecting .. potassium-sparing (spironolactone)
```

**Efficacy** depends on how much sodium normally passes the site: loop diuretics act where a large share is reabsorbed, so they are the most powerful (**high ceiling**).

## 8.2 Classification

| Class | Drugs | Efficacy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High ceiling (loop) diuretics** | Frusemide, bumetanide, torsemide | High (can excrete up to about 20–25% of filtered Na) |
| **Thiazides and thiazide-like (medium efficacy)** | Benzthiazide, hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone, metolazone, xipamide, indapamide | Moderate (5–10%) |
| **Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (weak)** | Acetazolamide | Low |
| **Potassium-sparing (weak)** | Aldosterone antagonist: spironolactone, eplerenone; Na-channel blockers: amiloride, triamterene | Low (2–3%) |
| **Osmotic diuretics** | Mannitol, glycerol | Variable |

Most important diuretics (frusemide, thiazides, acetazolamide) contain a **sulphonamide (–SO2NH2) group**, and some patients allergic to sulphonamides react to them.

## 8.3 Loop (high-ceiling) diuretics

**Mechanism:** inhibit the **Na⁺–K⁺–2Cl⁻ cotransporter** in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle. Act within minutes when given IV and work even when kidney function is poor.

### Frusemide\* (furosemide)

- **Chemical name:** 4-chloro-2-[(furan-2-ylmethyl)amino]-5-sulfamoylbenzoic acid.
- **Structure:** an **anthranilic acid** (2-aminobenzoic acid) derivative. On the benzene ring: –COOH (1), –NH–CH2–furan (2), Cl (4) and –SO2NH2 (5). The **furfuryl** group gives the name.

```text
   Benzene ring, positions:
     C-1  –COOH                      (benzoic acid)
     C-2  –NH–CH2–(furan-2-yl)       (furfurylamino; with C-1 = anthranilic acid)
     C-4  –Cl
     C-5  –SO2NH2                    (sulphamoyl)
   C-3 and C-6 carry hydrogen.
```

(COOH and the furfurylamino group are next to each other; Cl and the sulphamoyl group are next to each other.)

- **Uses:** **acute pulmonary oedema** (IV, acts quickly also by dilating veins), oedema of heart failure, cirrhosis and nephrotic syndrome, oedema with renal failure, hypertension (with renal impairment), hypercalcaemia (with saline), forced diuresis in some poisonings.
- **Adverse effects:** **hypokalaemia** (low potassium) and metabolic alkalosis, hyponatraemia, dehydration and low BP, **hyperuricaemia** (gout), hyperglycaemia, **ototoxicity** (deafness with high IV doses, worse with aminoglycosides), hypomagnesaemia, low calcium.
- **Interactions:** with digoxin (hypokalaemia increases digoxin toxicity), aminoglycosides (ear and kidney damage), lithium (raised levels), NSAIDs (reduce diuretic effect).
- **Stability/storage:** frusemide is **light sensitive** (discolours, degrades). Tablets and injections are stored in **light-resistant containers**; do not use discoloured injection. The injection is alkaline (sodium salt) and should not be mixed with acidic solutions (precipitates).
- **Formulations/brands:** tablets 40 mg, injection 10 mg/ml; Lasix; combinations with spironolactone (Lasilactone) and amiloride.

### Bumetanide

- 3-butylamino-4-phenoxy-5-sulphamoylbenzoic acid (also a sulphamoylbenzoic acid).
- About **40 times more potent** than frusemide on weight basis, similar maximum effect; better oral absorption; less ototoxic.
- Uses: same as frusemide. Brand: Bumet.

## 8.4 Thiazides and thiazide-like diuretics

**Mechanism:** inhibit the **Na⁺–Cl⁻ cotransporter** in the early distal tubule. Effect is moderate and long; they **lose effect when kidney function is poor** (except metolazone).

**Extra effects:** reduce calcium excretion (useful in calcium kidney stones and osteoporosis), mild vasodilation (antihypertensive effect continues after diuresis settles), paradoxical antidiuretic action in **nephrogenic diabetes insipidus**.

**Adverse effects (common to the group):** hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, hyperuricaemia, hyperglycaemia, raised lipids, hypercalcaemia, impotence.

**Basic thiazide structure (benzothiadiazine)**

```text
   Thiazide nucleus = 2H-1,2,4-benzothiadiazine 1,1-dioxide

     benzene ring fused to a six-membered ring containing
     S (as SO2) at 1, N at 2, C at 3, N at 4

     position 6 : –Cl (or –CF3)        position 7 : –SO2NH2
     position 3 : varies (benzthiazide: –CH2–S–CH2–C6H5)
     3,4-dihydro forms (e.g. hydrochlorothiazide) are more potent
```

A **6-chloro** (or CF3) and a **7-sulphamoyl** group on the benzothiadiazine-1,1-dioxide ring are essential.

| Drug | Chemistry | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Benzthiazide** | Benzothiadiazine (thiazide) with a benzylthiomethyl group at position 3 | Oedema, hypertension |
| **Chlorthalidone** | **Phthalimidine** (isoindolinone) with chlorobenzenesulphonamide – "thiazide-like", no thiazide ring | **Long acting (48–72 h)**, once daily; widely used in hypertension; brand: Thalitone |
| **Metolazone** | **Quinazolinone** sulphonamide | Effective even when GFR is low; with frusemide for resistant oedema (powerful combination – watch potassium) |
| **Xipamide** | **Salicylanilide** sulphonamide (4-chloro-5-sulphamoyl salicylic acid amide) | Thiazide-like; oedema and hypertension |

## 8.5 Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors

### Acetazolamide

- **Chemistry:** N-(5-sulfamoyl-1,3,4-thiadiazol-2-yl)acetamide – a **thiadiazole sulphonamide**.
- **Mechanism:** inhibits **carbonic anhydrase** in the proximal tubule → less H⁺ formed for exchange with Na⁺ → bicarbonate, sodium and water lost in alkaline urine. Effect is weak and **self-limiting** (metabolic acidosis develops in 2–3 days).
- **Uses:** **glaucoma** (reduces aqueous humour formation – main use), acute **mountain sickness**, to alkalinise urine, some epilepsies, periodic paralysis.
- **Adverse effects:** metabolic acidosis, hypokalaemia, kidney stones, paraesthesia (tingling), drowsiness, sulphonamide allergy.
- Brand: Diamox (tablets).

## 8.6 Potassium-sparing diuretics

### Spironolactone

- **Chemistry:** a **steroid** (similar to aldosterone) with a **lactone** ring at C-17 and a thioacetyl group at C-7.
- **Mechanism:** **competitive antagonist of aldosterone** at mineralocorticoid receptors in the late distal tubule and collecting duct → Na⁺ lost, **K⁺ retained**. Action builds slowly over days.
- **Uses:** with loop or thiazide diuretics to prevent potassium loss; oedema of **liver cirrhosis** (high aldosterone) and nephrotic syndrome; **heart failure** (reduces deaths); **primary hyperaldosteronism** (Conn's syndrome); resistant hypertension; hirsutism and acne in women (anti-androgen effect).
- **Adverse effects:** **hyperkalaemia** (dangerous with ACE inhibitors, potassium supplements, kidney failure), **gynaecomastia** and impotence in men, menstrual irregularity.
- Brands: Aldactone; Lasilactone (with frusemide).

### Other potassium-sparing drugs (for comparison)

- **Amiloride** and **triamterene** block the **epithelial sodium channel (ENaC)** in the collecting duct directly; they do not need aldosterone to be present. Used with thiazides or loop diuretics (e.g. amiloride + frusemide) to prevent hypokalaemia. Risk of hyperkalaemia, as with spironolactone.

## 8.7 Osmotic diuretics (for completeness)

- **Mannitol** (a sugar alcohol) is filtered freely but **not reabsorbed**, so it holds water in the tubule by osmosis.
- Given **IV only** (20% solution). Uses: to lower **raised intracranial pressure** and **intraocular pressure** (acute glaucoma), and to maintain urine flow in some kidney emergencies.
- Mannitol 20% solution **crystallises in the cold**; warm the bottle to dissolve crystals and use an in-line filter. Do not use in heart failure or pulmonary oedema (expands blood volume first).

## 8.8 Uses of diuretics at a glance

| Condition | Preferred diuretic | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acute pulmonary oedema | Frusemide IV | Fast and powerful; also dilates veins |
| Chronic heart failure with oedema | Loop diuretic + spironolactone | Removes fluid; spironolactone improves survival |
| Essential hypertension | Thiazide-like (chlorthalidone) | Long action, cheap, proven to reduce strokes |
| Oedema of liver cirrhosis (ascites) | Spironolactone ± frusemide | Aldosterone is high in cirrhosis |
| Oedema with renal failure | Frusemide (high dose) or metolazone | Thiazides fail when GFR is low |
| Glaucoma, mountain sickness | Acetazolamide | Reduces aqueous humour; causes mild acidosis that stimulates breathing |
| Raised intracranial pressure | Mannitol IV | Draws water out of the brain |
| Calcium kidney stones | Thiazide | Reduces urinary calcium |
| Hypercalcaemia | Frusemide + saline | Increases calcium excretion |

## 8.9 Electrolyte effects to remember

| Effect | Loop | Thiazide | Spironolactone | Acetazolamide |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Potassium | ↓ | ↓ | ↑ | ↓ |
| Calcium in urine | ↑ | ↓ | — | — |
| Uric acid in blood | ↑ | ↑ | — | — |
| Blood glucose | ↑ (mild) | ↑ | — | — |
| Acid–base | Alkalosis | Alkalosis | Acidosis (mild) | Acidosis |

**Counselling points for diuretics:** take in the **morning** (avoid night-time urination); eat potassium-rich foods (banana, orange, coconut water) with loop/thiazide diuretics unless also on a potassium-sparing drug; weigh daily in heart failure; stand up slowly.

## Remember

- Loop diuretics block Na⁺–K⁺–2Cl⁻ in the thick ascending limb – most powerful (high ceiling).
- Frusemide = 4-chloro-2-furfurylamino-5-sulphamoylbenzoic acid; IV for acute pulmonary oedema; hypokalaemia, ototoxicity; light sensitive.
- Bumetanide ≈ 40 × frusemide potency.
- Thiazides block Na⁺–Cl⁻ in early distal tubule; lose effect at low GFR (except metolazone); chlorthalidone longest acting.
- Acetazolamide = CA inhibitor, mainly glaucoma and mountain sickness.
- Spironolactone = aldosterone antagonist, steroid lactone, K-sparing; hyperkalaemia, gynaecomastia.
- Most diuretics are sulphonamides (–SO2NH2).

# Unit 7: Drugs acting on the cardiovascular system

This unit covers three groups: **anti-arrhythmic drugs, anti-hypertensive agents and anti-anginal agents**. Starred drugs: **phenytoin sodium, propranolol and captopril** (propranolol and phenytoin were described in Units 5 and 6; here their cardiac use is added).

## 7.1 Anti-arrhythmic drugs

An **arrhythmia** is an abnormal heart rhythm (rate, regularity or site of origin), e.g. atrial fibrillation, ventricular tachycardia, extrasystoles. Anti-arrhythmics restore normal rhythm by changing ion movement in heart cells.

### 7.1.1 Vaughan Williams classification

| Class | Action | Drugs in syllabus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **IA** | Sodium channel block (moderate); prolong action potential | Quinidine, procainamide |
| **IB** | Sodium channel block (weak); shorten action potential | Lidocaine, phenytoin |
| **IC** | Sodium channel block (strong); little effect on action potential duration | Lorcainide (also flecainide, propafenone) |
| **II** | β-adrenergic blockade | Propranolol (also esmolol, sotalol has II + III) |
| **III** | Potassium channel block; prolong repolarisation | Amiodarone, sotalol |
| **IV** | Calcium channel block | Verapamil (also diltiazem) |

```text
   Cardiac action potential (ventricular muscle)
          ______
   phase 1 /      \  phase 2 (plateau, Ca²⁺ in)
  phase 0 |        \_______  phase 3 (K⁺ out → repolarisation)
 (Na⁺ in) |                \
 ─────────┘                 \________ phase 4 (resting)
   Class I acts on phase 0, class III on phase 3, class IV on phase 2 and nodes
```

### 7.1.2 Individual drugs

**Quinidine sulphate**

- Cinchona alkaloid; the dextro isomer (diastereomer) of quinine; quinoline-containing.
- Class IA. Uses: atrial fibrillation/flutter (now rarely), malaria in some settings.
- Adverse effects: **cinchonism** (tinnitus, headache, vertigo), diarrhoea, QT prolongation (torsades de pointes), raises digoxin levels.
- Store protected from light (quinine alkaloids darken).

**Procainamide hydrochloride**

- **Amide** analogue of the local anaesthetic procaine (amide resists hydrolysis by esterases, so it acts longer and orally).
- Class IA. Uses: ventricular and atrial arrhythmias.
- Adverse effects: **lupus-like syndrome** with long use (especially slow acetylators), blood dyscrasias.

**Lidocaine hydrochloride (lignocaine)**

- Amide local anaesthetic (2-(diethylamino)-N-(2,6-dimethylphenyl)acetamide).
- Class IB; given **IV only** (high first-pass metabolism orally).
- Uses: ventricular arrhythmias, especially after myocardial infarction; also local anaesthesia.
- Adverse effects: CNS (drowsiness, convulsions at high levels).

**Phenytoin sodium\***

- Sodium salt of 5,5-diphenylhydantoin (structure in Unit 5).
- Class IB. Uses: **digitalis-induced arrhythmias** (ventricular); mainly an antiepileptic.
- IV injection must be given slowly in saline (precipitates in dextrose; fast injection causes hypotension and arrhythmias).

**Lorcainide hydrochloride**

- Class IC sodium channel blocker for ventricular arrhythmias; can itself cause arrhythmias (pro-arrhythmic); limited use.

**Amiodarone**

- **Iodine-containing benzofuran** derivative. Mainly class III but has actions of all four classes.
- Very long half-life (weeks), accumulates in tissues.
- Uses: resistant ventricular and atrial arrhythmias (atrial fibrillation, ventricular tachycardia).
- Adverse effects: **thyroid problems** (hypo- or hyperthyroidism, because of iodine), **pulmonary fibrosis**, corneal microdeposits, blue-grey skin discolouration and photosensitivity, liver damage.
- Brands: Cordarone, Tachyra.

**Sotalol**

- Non-selective β blocker with class III action. Uses: ventricular arrhythmias, atrial fibrillation. Risk of QT prolongation.

**Verapamil**

- Phenylalkylamine **calcium channel blocker** (class IV). Slows AV node conduction.
- Uses: **supraventricular tachycardia**, atrial fibrillation rate control, angina, hypertension.
- Adverse effects: constipation, bradycardia; do not combine with β blockers IV (heart block).

**Propranolol\*** (class II) – structure in Unit 6. Uses in arrhythmias due to sympathetic overactivity, thyrotoxicosis, sinus tachycardia.

## 7.2 Anti-hypertensive agents

**Hypertension** is persistently raised blood pressure (≥ 140/90 mm Hg on repeated readings is the usual threshold). Untreated, it damages heart, brain, kidneys and eyes.

### 7.2.1 Classification

| Class | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| ACE inhibitors | Captopril, enalapril, ramipril, lisinopril |
| Angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs) | Losartan, telmisartan |
| Calcium channel blockers | Nifedipine, amlodipine (dihydropyridines); verapamil, diltiazem |
| β blockers | Propranolol, atenolol, metoprolol |
| α1 blockers | Prazosin |
| Centrally acting α2 agonists | Clonidine, methyldopa (methyldopate) |
| Direct vasodilators | Hydralazine, sodium nitroprusside, minoxidil |
| Diuretics | Thiazides, chlorthalidone, furosemide, spironolactone (Unit 8) |

### 7.2.2 Renin–angiotensin system (why ACE inhibitors work)

```text
Angiotensinogen —(renin, from kidney)→ Angiotensin I
Angiotensin I —(ACE, angiotensin converting enzyme)→ Angiotensin II
Angiotensin II → vasoconstriction + aldosterone release (Na, water retention) → BP ↑

ACE also breaks down bradykinin (a vasodilator).
ACE inhibitor: ↓ angiotensin II, ↓ aldosterone, ↑ bradykinin → BP ↓
(bradykinin build-up explains dry cough and angioedema)
```

### Captopril\*

- **Chemical name:** (2S)-1-[(2S)-2-methyl-3-sulfanylpropanoyl]pyrrolidine-2-carboxylic acid.
- **Structure:** an **L-proline** (pyrrolidine-2-carboxylic acid) ring whose nitrogen is acylated by a 2-methyl-3-mercaptopropanoyl group. The **thiol (–SH)** binds the zinc ion of ACE; the proline carboxylate fits the enzyme too.

```text
   HS–CH2–CH(CH3)–C(=O)–N< ............ ring N of proline
                          |
          pyrrolidine ring (5-membered, N + 4 C)
                          |
                 C-2 of the ring carries –COOH

   Read as: 3-mercapto-2-methylpropanoyl group  +  L-proline
            (–SH binds Zn²⁺ of ACE)             (–COO⁻ binds the enzyme)
```

- **Action:** first orally active ACE inhibitor; active as such (not a prodrug); short acting (2–3 times daily).
- **Uses:** hypertension, heart failure, after myocardial infarction, diabetic nephropathy.
- **Adverse effects:** **dry cough**, angioedema, first-dose hypotension, high potassium, taste disturbance and rash (due to the –SH group); **contraindicated in pregnancy** (fetal kidney damage) and bilateral renal artery stenosis.
- **Stability/storage:** the thiol is oxidised to a disulphide; solutions are unstable; tablets in tight, moisture-resistant containers. Food reduces absorption – take 1 hour before meals.
- **Brands:** Capoten, Aceten (tablets).

### Ramipril

- **Prodrug** ester converted in the liver to active ramiprilat; long acting (once daily); **no –SH group** (fewer taste/rash problems).
- Uses: hypertension, heart failure, cardiovascular risk reduction. Brand: Cardace.

### Methyldopate hydrochloride

- **Ethyl ester of methyldopa** – water-soluble form for **IV** use.
- Methyldopa is converted in the brain to **α-methylnoradrenaline**, which stimulates central **α2** receptors and reduces sympathetic outflow.
- Uses: hypertension, especially **in pregnancy** (methyldopa is considered safe); IV for hypertensive emergencies.
- Adverse effects: sedation, positive Coombs test, haemolytic anaemia, liver injury.

### Clonidine hydrochloride

- **Imidazoline** derivative (2,6-dichlorophenylamino-imidazoline); central **α2** agonist.
- Uses: hypertension, opioid and alcohol withdrawal, ADHD.
- Adverse effects: sedation, dry mouth; **rebound hypertension** if stopped suddenly – taper the dose.
- Brand: Catapres, Arkamin.

### Hydralazine hydrochloride

- 1-Hydrazinophthalazine – a **phthalazine** ring with a hydrazine (–NH–NH2) group.
- Direct **arteriolar dilator**.
- Uses: hypertension in pregnancy (pre-eclampsia), heart failure (with nitrates).
- Adverse effects: reflex tachycardia, fluid retention (given with β blocker and diuretic), **lupus-like syndrome** in slow acetylators.

### Nifedipine

- **1,4-Dihydropyridine** calcium channel blocker with an **o-nitrophenyl** group at position 4 and two ester groups.
- Dilates arterioles. Uses: hypertension, angina (Prinzmetal's), Raynaud's phenomenon, preterm labour.
- Adverse effects: headache, flushing, ankle oedema, reflex tachycardia (short-acting forms) – use sustained-release forms.
- **Stability:** very **light sensitive** (the nitrophenyl-dihydropyridine is oxidised to a nitroso-pyridine). Capsules/tablets in light-resistant packs; liquid in capsules is protected by opaque gelatin.
- Brands: Calcigard, Depin, Nicardia.

### Propranolol\* in hypertension

Reduces cardiac output and renin release. Described in Unit 6.

## 7.3 Anti-anginal agents

**Angina pectoris** is chest pain due to too little oxygen reaching the heart muscle (usually narrowed coronary arteries). Treatment either increases oxygen supply or decreases oxygen demand.

| Class | Drugs | Mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organic nitrates | Glyceryl trinitrate, isosorbide dinitrate, isosorbide mononitrate | Release **nitric oxide** → venodilation (↓ preload) and coronary dilation |
| β blockers | Atenolol, propranolol | ↓ heart rate and force (↓ demand) |
| Calcium channel blockers | Nifedipine, amlodipine, verapamil, diltiazem | Vasodilation; verapamil/diltiazem also ↓ heart rate |
| Others | Nicorandil, ranolazine, trimetazidine | K-channel opening, metabolic modulation |

### Isosorbide dinitrate (ISDN)

- **Nitrate ester** of isosorbide (a sugar-derived bicyclic diol) – two –ONO2 groups.
- **Mechanism:** nitrate is converted to **NO** in vascular smooth muscle → ↑ cGMP → relaxation. Mainly dilates **veins** (reduces return of blood and heart work).
- **Uses:** **sublingual** for acute angina attacks and before exertion; oral for prevention; acute and chronic heart failure (with hydralazine).
- **Adverse effects:** throbbing **headache**, flushing, postural hypotension, reflex tachycardia; **tolerance** with continuous use (a nitrate-free interval each day prevents it). Never combine with sildenafil-type drugs (severe hypotension).
- **Storage:** well-closed containers in a cool place.
- **Brands:** Sorbitrate, Isordil (sublingual tablets 5 mg, oral tablets).

## 7.4 Quick comparison of starred drugs

| Drug | Chemical class | Main CVS use | Key adverse effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Phenytoin sodium | Hydantoin | Digitalis-induced arrhythmia | Hypotension if given fast IV |
| Propranolol | Aryloxypropanolamine (naphthyl) | Arrhythmia, hypertension, angina | Bronchospasm, bradycardia |
| Captopril | Proline derivative with –SH | Hypertension, heart failure | Dry cough, angioedema, taste loss |

## Remember

- Vaughan Williams: I Na block (IA quinidine/procainamide, IB lidocaine/phenytoin, IC lorcainide), II β block, III K block (amiodarone, sotalol), IV Ca block (verapamil).
- Quinidine → cinchonism; procainamide and hydralazine → lupus; amiodarone → thyroid, lung fibrosis (contains iodine).
- Lidocaine IV only for ventricular arrhythmias after MI.
- Captopril: proline + thiol (–SH binds zinc of ACE); dry cough (bradykinin); not in pregnancy; take before meals.
- Ramipril is a prodrug; methyldopate = injectable ester of methyldopa (safe in pregnancy); clonidine → rebound hypertension.
- Nifedipine: dihydropyridine, light sensitive. ISDN: nitrate → NO; sublingual for acute angina; headache; tolerance.

# Unit 13: Anti-neoplastic agents

**Cancer (neoplasm)** is uncontrolled growth of abnormal cells that can invade nearby tissue and spread to distant sites (metastasis). **Anti-neoplastic (anticancer, cytotoxic) drugs** kill or stop the division of cancer cells. The syllabus lists **cyclophosphamide\*, busulfan, mercaptopurine, fluorouracil\*, methotrexate, dactinomycin, doxorubicin hydrochloride, vinblastine sulphate, cisplatin\* and dromostanolone propionate**.

## 13.1 Basic ideas

- Most cytotoxic drugs act on **dividing cells**. Cancer cells divide often, but so do normal **bone marrow, gut lining, hair follicles and gonads** – hence the common side effects.
- **Cell-cycle specific** drugs act in one phase (e.g. antimetabolites in S phase, vinca alkaloids in M phase); **cell-cycle non-specific** drugs act at any phase (alkylating agents, platinum compounds, anthracyclines).

```text
              G1 (growth) ──→ S (DNA synthesis) ──→ G2 ──→ M (mitosis)
                    ↑                ↑                          ↑
                    │        antimetabolites:            vinca alkaloids
                    │        5-FU, methotrexate,         (vinblastine)
                    │        mercaptopurine
              G0 (resting) – alkylating agents and platinum act on any phase
```

**Common adverse effects of cytotoxic drugs**

1. **Bone marrow suppression** – low WBC (infections), low platelets (bleeding), anaemia.
2. **Nausea and vomiting** (cisplatin worst) – prevented with ondansetron, dexamethasone.
3. **Mucositis**, mouth ulcers, diarrhoea.
4. **Alopecia** (hair loss).
5. Infertility, teratogenicity, second cancers.
6. **Tumour lysis syndrome** (high uric acid, potassium) – give fluids, allopurinol.

**Combination chemotherapy** (drugs with different mechanisms and toxicities) gives better results and less resistance.

## 13.3 Alkylating agents

**Mechanism:** form highly reactive **carbonium (or aziridinium) ions** that attach **alkyl groups** to DNA bases, mainly the **N-7 of guanine**. Bifunctional agents (two reactive arms) **cross-link** DNA strands → DNA cannot separate or be copied → cell death.

### Cyclophosphamide\*

- **Chemical name:** N,N-bis(2-chloroethyl)-1,3,2-oxazaphosphinan-2-amine 2-oxide (used as monohydrate).
- **Structure:** a **nitrogen mustard** – the **bis(2-chloroethyl)amino group –N(CH2CH2Cl)2** – attached to a phosphorus atom that is part of a six-membered **oxazaphosphorine** ring (containing O, P and N), with P=O.

```text
           O ── CH2
          /        \
   O ═ P            CH2          six-membered ring: P, O, three CH2, NH
       | \         /
       |  NH ── CH2
       |
       N(CH2CH2Cl)2     ← nitrogen mustard part
```

- **Prodrug:** inactive as such; activated in the **liver by CYP450** to 4-hydroxycyclophosphamide → **phosphoramide mustard** (the active alkylating species) and **acrolein** (a toxic by-product).
- **Uses:** very wide – lymphomas (Hodgkin's and non-Hodgkin's), leukaemias, **breast cancer**, ovarian cancer, small-cell lung cancer, multiple myeloma, childhood tumours; also an **immunosuppressant** in lupus nephritis, nephrotic syndrome, rheumatoid arthritis, and before bone marrow transplant.
- **Adverse effects:** bone marrow suppression, alopecia, nausea; **haemorrhagic cystitis** (bladder bleeding due to **acrolein**) – prevented by plenty of fluids and **mesna** (which binds acrolein in the bladder); infertility; secondary cancers.
- **Stability/storage:** tablets and powder below 25 °C; the reconstituted injection should be used promptly (or within the time stated, refrigerated). Handle as a **cytotoxic** (gloves, safe disposal).
- **Brands:** Endoxan, Cyclophos (tablets, injection).

### Busulfan

- **Chemistry:** 1,4-butanediol dimethanesulphonate – an **alkyl sulphonate**: CH3SO2O–(CH2)4–OSO2CH3. The two methanesulphonate groups leave and the butane chain cross-links DNA.
- **Uses:** **chronic myeloid leukaemia** (historically), conditioning before bone marrow transplant.
- **Adverse effects:** marked myelosuppression, **pulmonary fibrosis** ("busulfan lung"), skin pigmentation, seizures at high doses.
- Brand: Myleran.

### Cisplatin\*

- **Chemical name:** cis-diamminedichloroplatinum(II) [(SP-4-2)-diamminedichloridoplatinum].
- **Structure:** a **square planar** platinum(II) complex with **two ammonia (NH3)** and **two chloride** ligands on the **same side (cis)**. The **trans** isomer is inactive.

```text
         H3N       Cl
            \     /
              Pt          cis: both Cl on the same side
            /     \
         H3N       Cl
```

- **Mechanism:** inside the cell (low chloride), the Cl⁻ ligands are replaced by water (aquation) → reactive platinum binds **N-7 of guanine**, forming **intrastrand cross-links** in DNA (works like a bifunctional alkylating agent).
- **Uses:** **testicular cancer** (with bleomycin and etoposide – high cure rate), **ovarian cancer**, bladder, head and neck, lung, cervical cancers.
- **Adverse effects:** **nephrotoxicity** (prevent with good **hydration** with saline and mannitol diuresis), **severe nausea and vomiting** (strong antiemetics), **ototoxicity** (tinnitus, deafness), peripheral neuropathy, low magnesium and potassium, myelosuppression (mild).
- **Stability and handling:**
  - Dilute in **sodium chloride**-containing fluids – chloride keeps the complex stable; avoid plain dextrose/water for long storage.
  - **Do not use aluminium needles or sets** – aluminium reacts with platinum (black precipitate, loss of potency).
  - **Protect from light**; do not refrigerate the solution (it precipitates in the cold).
- **Brands:** Cytoplatin, Platinex (injection).

## 13.4 Antimetabolites

**Antimetabolites** look like normal substances (metabolites) the cell needs to make DNA, so they block enzymes or get into DNA/RNA wrongly. They act in the **S phase**.

### Methotrexate

- **Chemistry:** a **folic acid analogue** – 4-amino-N10-methyl folic acid (pteridine ring + p-aminobenzoyl + glutamic acid, with an amino group in place of the 4-oxo group and a methyl on N-10).
- **Mechanism:** strongly inhibits **dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR)** → no tetrahydrofolate → no thymidylate and purine synthesis → DNA synthesis stops.
- **Uses:** acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL), **choriocarcinoma**, osteosarcoma (high dose), breast cancer; in low weekly doses for **rheumatoid arthritis and psoriasis**; ectopic pregnancy.
- **Adverse effects:** myelosuppression, mouth ulcers, liver fibrosis, lung toxicity, kidney damage at high doses.
- **Rescue:** **folinic acid (leucovorin, calcium folinate)** given after high-dose methotrexate bypasses the block and saves normal cells.
- **Safety note:** for arthritis it is taken **once a week**; daily dosing by mistake has caused deaths – counsel clearly.
- Brands: Folitrax, Biotrexate.

### Mercaptopurine (6-MP)

- **Chemistry:** a **purine** (hypoxanthine analogue) with **–SH at position 6** (purine-6-thiol).
- **Mechanism:** converted to a fraudulent nucleotide (thio-IMP) that blocks purine synthesis.
- **Uses:** maintenance therapy of **ALL**; inflammatory bowel disease (as azathioprine, its prodrug).
- **Interaction:** metabolised by **xanthine oxidase**; **allopurinol** blocks this enzyme → reduce 6-MP dose to about one-quarter when given together.
- Adverse effects: myelosuppression, liver damage.

### Fluorouracil\* (5-FU)

- **Chemical name:** 5-fluoropyrimidine-2,4(1H,3H)-dione (5-fluorouracil).
- **Structure:** **uracil** (a pyrimidine with C=O at 2 and 4) with a **fluorine atom at position 5** in place of hydrogen. Fluorine is about the size of hydrogen, so the enzyme accepts it, but the strong C–F bond cannot be broken.

```text
            O
            ‖
       HN ─ C4 ─ C5 ─ F
       |          ‖
   O ═ C2         C6–H
         \       /
           N1–H
```

- **Mechanism:** converted to **FdUMP** (5-fluoro-2'-deoxyuridine monophosphate), which forms a stable complex with **thymidylate synthase** (with methylene-tetrahydrofolate) → no thymidine → **"thymineless death"**; also incorporated into RNA. Leucovorin is given with 5-FU to **increase** the binding (enhances its effect).
- **Uses:** **colorectal cancer** (main use), stomach, pancreatic, breast, head and neck cancers; **topical cream** for actinic keratoses and superficial basal cell carcinoma.
- **Adverse effects:** mucositis, diarrhoea, myelosuppression, hand–foot syndrome, alopecia; severe toxicity in people with **DPD (dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase) deficiency**.
- **Stability/storage:** store at room temperature, **protected from light**; **do not refrigerate** (crystals form – they can be redissolved by warming and shaking). Given IV (poor oral absorption; capecitabine is the oral prodrug).
- **Brands:** Fluracil, 5-FU injection; Flonida cream.

## 13.5 Antitumour antibiotics

### Dactinomycin (actinomycin D)

- From *Streptomyces*; a **phenoxazinone** chromophore with two cyclic peptides.
- **Intercalates** between DNA base pairs and blocks RNA synthesis.
- Uses: **Wilms' tumour**, rhabdomyosarcoma, choriocarcinoma, Ewing's sarcoma.
- Adverse effects: myelosuppression, mucositis; very irritant if it leaks out of the vein (vesicant).

### Doxorubicin hydrochloride

- **Anthracycline** antibiotic (from *Streptomyces peucetius*): a red tetracyclic **anthraquinone** ring (aglycone) attached to an amino sugar (daunosamine).
- **Mechanism:** intercalates DNA, inhibits **topoisomerase II**, forms free radicals.
- Uses: wide – breast cancer, lymphomas, leukaemias, sarcomas, lung, ovarian cancers.
- **Adverse effects:** **cardiotoxicity** (dose-related cardiomyopathy and heart failure – limit total lifetime dose; dexrazoxane protects), myelosuppression, alopecia, **red-coloured urine** (harmless), vesicant; liposomal form reduces cardiotoxicity.
- Storage: protect from light; reconstituted solution refrigerated.
- Brand: Adriamycin.

## 13.6 Plant products – vinca alkaloids

### Vinblastine sulphate

- **Dimeric indole alkaloid** from the periwinkle plant ***Catharanthus roseus*** (*Vinca rosea*).
- **Mechanism:** binds **tubulin** and prevents microtubule formation → mitotic spindle cannot form → cells stop in **metaphase (M phase)**.
- Uses: **Hodgkin's lymphoma** (ABVD regimen), testicular cancer, Kaposi's sarcoma.
- Adverse effects: myelosuppression (main), alopecia, neurotoxicity (less than vincristine).
- **Critical safety point: for intravenous use only – fatal if given intrathecally.** Labels must carry this warning.
- Storage: refrigerated (2–8 °C), protected from light.

## 13.7 Hormones – Dromostanolone propionate

- **Chemistry:** an **androgen** – the **propionate ester** of **2α-methyl-dihydrotestosterone** (drostanolone), a 17β-hydroxy-5α-androstan-3-one steroid.
- **Use:** was used by IM injection for **advanced metastatic breast cancer in postmenopausal women** (hormone-dependent tumours); now largely replaced by anti-oestrogens (tamoxifen) and aromatase inhibitors (letrozole).
- **Adverse effects:** **virilisation** (deepening of voice, hair growth, acne), fluid retention.
- It is an anabolic steroid and has been misused in sports.

## 13.8 Safe handling of cytotoxic drugs (pharmacist's role)

1. Prepare in a **biological safety cabinet / cytotoxic isolator** with gloves, gown, mask and eye protection.
2. Use closed transfer devices; avoid aerosols; luer-lock syringes.
3. Label clearly as **cytotoxic**; for vinca alkaloids add "for IV use only – fatal if given by other routes".
4. Dispose of waste in marked containers for incineration; manage spills with a spill kit.
5. Pregnant staff should not handle cytotoxics.
6. Check doses carefully (body surface area based); double-check calculations.

## Remember

- Alkylating agents → alkylate N-7 guanine, cross-link DNA, cell-cycle non-specific.
- Cyclophosphamide: prodrug (liver CYP) → phosphoramide mustard + acrolein → haemorrhagic cystitis; mesna and fluids.
- Cisplatin: cis-diamminedichloroplatinum(II), square planar; nephrotoxic (hydrate), emetic, ototoxic; saline diluent, no aluminium, protect from light.
- Methotrexate blocks DHFR (leucovorin rescue); 6-MP + allopurinol → reduce dose; 5-FU → FdUMP blocks thymidylate synthase; do not refrigerate 5-FU.
- Doxorubicin: cardiotoxic, red urine; vinblastine: tubulin, M phase, IV only.
- Handle all cytotoxics with protection and label clearly.

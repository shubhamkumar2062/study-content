# Unit 12: Antibiotics

An **antibiotic** is, strictly, a substance produced by a micro-organism that kills or inhibits other micro-organisms in low concentration (e.g. penicillin from *Penicillium*, streptomycin from *Streptomyces*). Today the word also covers their semi-synthetic and synthetic relatives. The syllabus lists **penicillin G, amoxicillin\*, cloxacillin, streptomycin, tetracyclines (doxycycline, minocycline), macrolides (erythromycin, azithromycin) and miscellaneous (chloramphenicol\*, clindamycin)**.

## 12.1 Classification of antibiotics

**By mechanism of action**

| Target | Antibiotics |
| --- | --- |
| Cell wall synthesis | β-lactams (penicillins, cephalosporins, carbapenems), vancomycin |
| Protein synthesis – 30S ribosome | Aminoglycosides (streptomycin), tetracyclines |
| Protein synthesis – 50S ribosome | Macrolides (erythromycin, azithromycin), chloramphenicol, clindamycin, linezolid |
| Nucleic acid synthesis | Rifampicin, fluoroquinolones |
| Cell membrane | Polymyxins, polyenes |
| Folate synthesis | Sulphonamides, trimethoprim |

**By chemical class**

| Class | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| β-Lactams | Penicillin G, amoxicillin, cloxacillin, cephalexin |
| Aminoglycosides | Streptomycin, gentamicin, amikacin |
| Tetracyclines | Tetracycline, doxycycline, minocycline |
| Macrolides | Erythromycin, azithromycin, clarithromycin |
| Amphenicols | Chloramphenicol |
| Lincosamides | Clindamycin, lincomycin |

**Bactericidal** (kill): β-lactams, aminoglycosides. **Bacteriostatic** (stop growth): tetracyclines, macrolides, chloramphenicol, clindamycin.

## 12.2 Penicillins (β-lactam antibiotics)

### 12.2.1 Structure of penicillins

All penicillins share **6-aminopenicillanic acid (6-APA)**: a **β-lactam ring** (four-membered cyclic amide) fused to a **thiazolidine ring** (five-membered, containing S and N). Different acyl side chains (R–CO–) on the 6-amino group give different penicillins.

```text
            R ─ C(=O) ─ NH ─ C6 ─── C5 ─── S
                             |       |      \
                             C7 ─── N4       C(CH3)2
                             ‖        \      /
                             O         C3 ─
                                       |
                                       COOH
   C7=O + N4 + C5 + C6 = β-lactam ring (4-membered)
   N4, C5, S, C(CH3)2, C3 = thiazolidine ring (5-membered)
```

**Mechanism:** the strained β-lactam ring acylates (binds) the **penicillin-binding proteins (transpeptidases)** that cross-link peptidoglycan in the bacterial cell wall. The wall becomes weak and the bacterium bursts (bactericidal, works on growing bacteria). Human cells have no cell wall, so penicillins are very safe.

**Weak points:**

1. **Acid** – destroys some penicillins in the stomach (penicillin G).
2. **β-Lactamase (penicillinase)** – bacterial enzyme that opens the β-lactam ring → resistance.
3. **Allergy** – the commonest problem (rash to anaphylaxis); always ask about penicillin allergy; cross-allergy among all penicillins.

**Classification of penicillins**

| Group | Drugs | Feature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Natural | Penicillin G (benzylpenicillin), penicillin V | Narrow spectrum; G acid-labile |
| Penicillinase-resistant | **Cloxacillin**, flucloxacillin, methicillin | Bulky side chain protects β-lactam ring |
| Aminopenicillins (extended spectrum) | Ampicillin, **amoxicillin** | Also cover some Gram-negatives |
| Antipseudomonal | Piperacillin, carbenicillin | Pseudomonas |
| With β-lactamase inhibitors | Amoxicillin + **clavulanic acid**; piperacillin + tazobactam | Inhibitor protects the penicillin |

### Penicillin G (benzylpenicillin)

- Side chain: **phenylacetyl** (benzyl). Natural, from *Penicillium chrysogenum*.
- **Acid-labile** (given IM/IV), destroyed by penicillinase, short half-life (prolonged by probenecid; depot forms procaine and **benzathine penicillin**).
- Uses: streptococcal infections (pharyngitis, cellulitis), pneumococcal infections, **syphilis**, meningococcal meningitis, diphtheria, tetanus, gas gangrene, **prevention of rheumatic fever** (benzathine penicillin every 3–4 weeks).
- Stability: dry powder is stable; **solution after reconstitution is unstable** – use soon (store as directed). Unit dose expressed in **units** (lakh units).

### Amoxicillin\*

- **Chemical name:** (2S,5R,6R)-6-[[(2R)-2-amino-2-(4-hydroxyphenyl)acetyl]amino]-3,3-dimethyl-7-oxo-4-thia-1-azabicyclo[3.2.0]heptane-2-carboxylic acid (used as trihydrate).
- **Structure:** 6-APA nucleus with a **D-(−)-α-amino-p-hydroxyphenylacetyl** side chain. It is ampicillin with an extra **–OH at the para position** of the phenyl ring.

```text
   HO ──⟨ benzene ⟩── CH(NH2) ── C(=O) ── NH ── [6-APA nucleus]
         (p-hydroxy)  (α-amino group → Gram-negative activity
                        and acid stability)
```

- **Points:** **acid stable** (oral); **better absorbed than ampicillin** and not affected much by food; broad spectrum (streptococci, pneumococci, *H. influenzae*, *E. coli*, *Salmonella*, *H. pylori*); destroyed by β-lactamase (so combined with **clavulanic acid**).
- **Uses:** respiratory tract infections, otitis media, sinusitis, UTI, typhoid (sensitive strains), **H. pylori** eradication (with clarithromycin + a proton pump inhibitor), dental infections, endocarditis prophylaxis.
- **Adverse effects:** diarrhoea, allergy; **rash in infectious mononucleosis**.
- **Stability/storage:** capsules/tablets in a cool dry place; **dry syrup** for children is reconstituted with water just before use and then kept in a refrigerator and used within about **7 days** (as labelled); shake well.
- **Brands:** Mox, Novamox (capsules, dry syrup); Augmentin, Moxikind-CV (with clavulanic acid).

### Cloxacillin

- **Isoxazolyl penicillin**: side chain carries a 3-(2-chlorophenyl)-5-methylisoxazole group. The bulky group **shields the β-lactam ring from penicillinase**.
- **Acid stable** (oral) and penicillinase-resistant, but narrow spectrum.
- Uses: **penicillinase-producing *Staphylococcus aureus*** infections (boils, abscesses, osteomyelitis); combined with ampicillin/amoxicillin. Not active against MRSA.
- Brand: Klox.

## 12.3 Aminoglycosides – Streptomycin

- From *Streptomyces griseus* (Waksman, 1944) – first drug effective against **TB**.
- **Chemistry:** aminoglycoside – amino sugars (streptose, N-methyl-L-glucosamine) linked by glycosidic bonds to an aminocyclitol (**streptidine**). Highly polar, basic, water soluble.
- **Mechanism:** binds the **30S ribosomal subunit** → misreading of mRNA → faulty proteins; bactericidal.
- **Not absorbed orally** → given **IM** (TB) or IV for other aminoglycosides.
- **Uses:** tuberculosis (now mainly in drug-resistant regimens), plague, tularaemia, with penicillin in enterococcal endocarditis (historically).
- **Adverse effects:** **ototoxicity** – mainly **vestibular** (giddiness, imbalance) with streptomycin; **nephrotoxicity**; neuromuscular blockade; avoid in pregnancy (fetal deafness).
- **Storage:** dry powder vials at room temperature; reconstituted solution under refrigeration for a short time.

## 12.4 Tetracyclines

- **Chemistry:** a **four-ring (tetracyclic) naphthacene** (octahydronaphthacene) nucleus with many –OH, keto and a dimethylamino group, and a carboxamide.
- **Mechanism:** bind the **30S subunit** and block attachment of aminoacyl-tRNA → bacteriostatic.
- **Broad spectrum:** Gram-positive, Gram-negative, rickettsiae, chlamydia, mycoplasma, cholera, some protozoa (malaria prophylaxis/treatment), acne bacteria.

| Drug | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Doxycycline** | Long acting (once/twice daily), well absorbed, can be used in kidney failure; uses: respiratory infections, cholera, rickettsial fevers (**scrub typhus**), leptospirosis, acne, chlamydia, malaria (prophylaxis, with quinine), Lyme disease. Brand: Doxy-1 |
| **Minocycline** | Most lipid soluble; acne, meningococcal carriers; vestibular side effects (dizziness), skin pigmentation |

**Adverse effects of tetracyclines**

1. **Teeth discolouration** (yellow-brown) and bone deposition – **avoid in children below 8 years and in pregnancy/breastfeeding**.
2. GI irritation, **oesophageal ulcers** (swallow with full glass of water, stay upright).
3. **Photosensitivity**.
4. Superinfection (candidiasis, diarrhoea).
5. Liver damage in pregnancy (high IV doses).

**Interaction:** tetracyclines **chelate calcium, magnesium, aluminium, iron, zinc** → do not take with **milk, antacids or iron tablets** (doxycycline less affected than older tetracyclines).

**Stability:** **outdated/degraded tetracycline** (epi-anhydrotetracycline formed by heat, moisture and acid) can damage the kidneys (**Fanconi syndrome**). Store in tight, light-resistant containers, cool and dry; never use expired tetracyclines.

## 12.5 Macrolides

- **Chemistry:** a large **macrocyclic lactone ring** (14-membered in erythromycin and clarithromycin; **15-membered "azalide"** in azithromycin, with a nitrogen in the ring) attached to deoxy sugars (desosamine, cladinose).
- **Mechanism:** bind the **50S subunit** and block translocation → bacteriostatic.
- **Spectrum:** Gram-positive cocci, *Mycoplasma*, *Chlamydia*, *Legionella*, *Bordetella* (whooping cough), *Campylobacter*.

| | Erythromycin | Azithromycin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source | *Saccharopolyspora* (formerly *Streptomyces*) *erythraea* | Semi-synthetic from erythromycin |
| Ring | 14-membered | 15-membered (azalide) |
| Acid stability | **Acid-labile** – given as enteric-coated tablets or stable esters/salts (estolate, stearate, ethylsuccinate) | Acid stable |
| Half-life / dosing | Short; 4 times a day | **Very long tissue half-life**; once daily, **3–5 day course** |
| GI upset | Common (motilin agonist) | Less |
| Interactions | Inhibits CYP3A4 (raises levels of other drugs) | Few |
| Uses | Penicillin-allergy alternative, whooping cough, diphtheria carriers | Respiratory infections, typhoid, chlamydial infections (single dose), trachoma, community-acquired pneumonia |
| Brands | Erythrocin, Althrocin | Azithral, Azee |

Erythromycin **estolate** can cause cholestatic jaundice. Macrolides can prolong the QT interval.

## 12.6 Miscellaneous antibiotics

### Chloramphenicol\*

- **Chemical name:** 2,2-dichloro-N-[(1R,2R)-1,3-dihydroxy-1-(4-nitrophenyl)propan-2-yl]acetamide.
- **Structure:** a **p-nitrophenyl** ring attached to a propane-1,3-diol chain; the C-2 nitrogen carries a **dichloroacetyl** group. Two chiral centres; only the **D-(−)-threo (1R,2R)** isomer is active. Unusual for a natural product to contain a nitro group and chlorine.

```text
   O2N ──⟨ benzene ⟩── CH(OH) ── CH ── CH2OH
                                  |
                                  NH ── C(=O) ── CHCl2
```

- **Source:** first isolated from *Streptomyces venezuelae*; now made **synthetically** (the first antibiotic to be fully synthesised commercially).
- **Mechanism:** binds the **50S** subunit, inhibits peptidyl transferase → bacteriostatic; broad spectrum including anaerobes, rickettsiae, *Salmonella typhi*, *H. influenzae*.
- **Uses:** now limited because of toxicity – **bacterial conjunctivitis and eye infections** (eye drops/ointment), ear drops, serious infections when alternatives fail (meningitis in penicillin allergy, typhoid in resistant cases, brain abscess).
- **Adverse effects:** **bone marrow depression** – dose-related reversible anaemia and rare **fatal aplastic anaemia** (unpredictable, even after eye drops); **grey baby syndrome** in newborns (the immature liver cannot conjugate it → cyanosis, collapse); enzyme inhibition (raises phenytoin, warfarin levels).
- **Stability/formulation:** intensely **bitter** – the tasteless **palmitate ester** is used for oral suspensions; the water-soluble **sodium succinate ester** for injection. Both are hydrolysed in the body to active chloramphenicol. Eye drops are stored in a **refrigerator (2–8 °C)** before opening because the solution slowly loses potency; protect from light.
- **Brands:** Chloromycetin; Chlorocol eye drops.

### Clindamycin

- **Lincosamide** (7-chloro-7-deoxy derivative of lincomycin).
- Binds the **50S** subunit; active against Gram-positive cocci (including some MRSA) and **anaerobes** (*Bacteroides*).
- Uses: anaerobic infections (abdominal, pelvic, lung abscess), bone and joint infections, dental infections, **acne** (topical gel), toxoplasmosis, malaria (with quinine).
- Adverse effect: **antibiotic-associated (pseudomembranous) colitis** due to *Clostridioides difficile* – stop drug if severe diarrhoea.
- Brand: Dalacin C.

## 12.7 Rational antibiotic use (counselling)

1. Take the full course even after feeling better.
2. Do not use antibiotics for viral colds and flu.
3. Keep correct timing (spacing doses); some with food, some on empty stomach.
4. Avoid milk/antacids/iron with tetracyclines and fluoroquinolones.
5. Report rash, breathing difficulty, severe diarrhoea at once.
6. Misuse leads to **antimicrobial resistance** (AMR) – a major public health problem; antibiotics are Schedule H/H1 drugs, sold only on prescription.

## Remember

- Penicillin nucleus = 6-APA: β-lactam + thiazolidine; acts on transpeptidases (cell wall); problems: acid, penicillinase, allergy.
- Penicillin G: acid-labile, IM/IV; benzathine for rheumatic fever prophylaxis.
- Amoxicillin: p-hydroxy ampicillin; acid stable, well absorbed; + clavulanic acid; dry syrup 7 days in fridge after mixing.
- Cloxacillin: isoxazolyl, penicillinase-resistant (staph).
- Streptomycin: aminoglycoside, 30S, IM; vestibular ototoxicity, nephrotoxicity; TB.
- Tetracyclines: 4-ring naphthacene, 30S; teeth staining (avoid < 8 years, pregnancy); chelation; expired → Fanconi syndrome. Doxycycline once daily.
- Macrolides: 50S; erythromycin acid-labile, 14-ring; azithromycin 15-ring azalide, once daily 3–5 days.
- Chloramphenicol: p-nitrophenyl + dichloroacetamide, D-threo; aplastic anaemia, grey baby; palmitate (taste) and succinate (injection) esters; eye drops in fridge.
- Clindamycin: lincosamide, anaerobes, pseudomembranous colitis.

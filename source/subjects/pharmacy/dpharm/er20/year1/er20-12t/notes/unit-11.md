# Unit 11: Anti-infective agents

**Anti-infective agents** kill or stop the growth of microbes (fungi, bacteria, mycobacteria, viruses, malaria parasites). This large unit has six groups. Starred drugs: **ketoconazole, fluconazole, ofloxacin, isoniazid (INH), pretomanid, acyclovir, chloroquine phosphate, mefloquine, sulfacetamide and dapsone**.

## 11.1 Antifungal agents

Fungal cell membranes contain **ergosterol** (human cells contain cholesterol). Most antifungals attack ergosterol or its synthesis.

**Classification**

| Class | Drugs | Mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Polyene antibiotics | Amphotericin B, nystatin | Bind ergosterol → pores in membrane → leakage |
| Heterocyclic benzofuran antibiotic | Griseofulvin | Binds microtubules, stops fungal mitosis |
| Azoles – imidazoles | Miconazole, ketoconazole, clotrimazole | Inhibit **lanosterol 14α-demethylase** (a CYP450 enzyme) → no ergosterol |
| Azoles – triazoles | Fluconazole, itraconazole, voriconazole | Same, more selective for fungal enzyme |
| Allylamines | Naftifine, terbinafine | Inhibit **squalene epoxidase** |

### Amphotericin B

- **Polyene macrolide** antibiotic from *Streptomyces nodosus*.
- Drug of choice for serious **systemic fungal infections** (cryptococcal meningitis, invasive candidiasis, aspergillosis) and **mucormycosis**; also kala-azar.
- Given by slow **IV infusion** (not absorbed orally). **Nephrotoxicity**, fever and chills during infusion, low potassium. **Liposomal** forms are less toxic.
- **Storage:** powder in the **refrigerator (2–8 °C), protected from light**; reconstitute with water/dextrose (precipitates in saline).

### Griseofulvin

- From *Penicillium griseofulvum*; oral, for **dermatophyte** infections of skin, hair and nails (tinea); deposits in new keratin. Take with a **fatty meal** (better absorption). Long course (weeks–months). Not effective on Candida.

### Miconazole

- Imidazole; mainly **topical** (cream, powder, oral gel, vaginal) for candidiasis and tinea.

### Ketoconazole\*

- **Chemical name:** cis-1-acetyl-4-[4-[[2-(2,4-dichlorophenyl)-2-(1H-imidazol-1-ylmethyl)-1,3-dioxolan-4-yl]methoxy]phenyl]piperazine.
- **Structure:** an **imidazole** ring and a **2,4-dichlorophenyl** group on a **1,3-dioxolane** ring, which is linked by –CH2–O– to a phenyl ring carrying an **N-acetylpiperazine**.
- **Uses:** now mainly **topical**: dandruff and seborrhoeic dermatitis (2% shampoo), tinea and pityriasis versicolor (cream). Oral use is restricted because of **liver toxicity**; it also inhibits human steroid synthesis (gynaecomastia) and many CYP enzymes (drug interactions) – oral form occasionally used in Cushing's syndrome.
- **Points:** needs acid for oral absorption.
- **Brands:** Nizral (cream, shampoo), Ketostar.

### Fluconazole\*

- **Chemical name:** 2-(2,4-difluorophenyl)-1,3-bis(1H-1,2,4-triazol-1-yl)propan-2-ol.
- **Structure:** a propan-2-ol whose central carbon carries –OH and a **2,4-difluorophenyl** ring; each end carbon carries a **1,2,4-triazole** ring.

```text
                  OH
                  |
   triazole–CH2 ─ C ─ CH2–triazole
                  |
             2,4-difluorophenyl
```

- **Points:** water soluble, well absorbed orally (also IV), long half-life (once daily), enters **CSF** well, excreted unchanged in urine; fewer interactions and less liver toxicity than ketoconazole.
- **Uses:** vaginal candidiasis (**single 150 mg oral dose**), oral and oesophageal thrush, cryptococcal meningitis, systemic candidiasis, prophylaxis in immunocompromised patients.
- **Brands:** Forcan, Zocon, Flucos (tablets, IV infusion, eye drops).

### Itraconazole

- Triazole; oral (with food) for **dermatophytes, onychomycosis**, aspergillosis, histoplasmosis. Brand: Canditral.

### Naftifine hydrochloride

- **Allylamine**; inhibits squalene epoxidase; topical cream for tinea.

## 11.2 Urinary tract anti-infective agents (fluoroquinolones)

**Quinolones** are synthetic antibacterials. Adding **fluorine at position 6** and a **piperazine at position 7** of the 4-quinolone ring gave the **fluoroquinolones**, which are broad spectrum.

**Mechanism:** inhibit bacterial **DNA gyrase (topoisomerase II)** and **topoisomerase IV** → bacteria cannot coil and separate DNA → bactericidal.

**Basic structure (4-quinolone-3-carboxylic acid)**

```text
   4-Quinolone nucleus: benzene ring (A) fused to a pyridone ring (B)

     N-1  : ethyl or cyclopropyl (ofloxacin: bridged to C-8 by –O–CH2–CH(CH3)–)
     C-3  : –COOH  ┐  together bind DNA gyrase
     C-4  : =O     ┘  (and chelate Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺, Al³⁺, Fe²⁺ → antacid interaction)
     C-6  : –F        (fluorine → "fluoro"quinolone, broad spectrum)
     C-7  : piperazine ring (anti-pseudomonal activity, better tissue spread)
```

| Drug | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Norfloxacin | Low blood levels; used for **urinary tract** and GI infections |
| Ciprofloxacin | N1-cyclopropyl; UTI, typhoid, gonorrhoea, respiratory, bone infections; most active against Gram-negatives |
| **Ofloxacin\*** | Tricyclic; good oral absorption; UTI, respiratory, typhoid, eye/ear drops, leprosy and TB regimens |
| Moxifloxacin | Better against Gram-positive and anaerobes ("respiratory quinolone"); MDR-TB |

### Ofloxacin\*

- **Chemical name:** (±)-9-fluoro-2,3-dihydro-3-methyl-10-(4-methylpiperazin-1-yl)-7-oxo-7H-pyrido[1,2,3-de]-1,4-benzoxazine-6-carboxylic acid.
- **Structure:** a **tricyclic** fluoroquinolone: the quinolone ring is bridged from N-1 to C-8 by an –O–CH2–CH(CH3)– chain (forming a benzoxazine ring); it has the **carboxylic acid, ketone, fluorine** and **N-methylpiperazine** groups. It is a **racemate**; the active **S-(−) isomer is levofloxacin**.
- **Uses:** urinary tract infections, respiratory and soft-tissue infections, typhoid, gonorrhoea (earlier), eye and ear infections, leprosy and tuberculosis regimens.
- **Adverse effects (fluoroquinolones):** nausea, dizziness, **tendon rupture**, QT prolongation, photosensitivity; **avoid in children and pregnancy** (cartilage damage in young animals).
- **Interaction:** absorption is reduced by **antacids, iron, calcium, zinc** (chelation) – give 2 hours apart.
- **Storage:** protect from light.
- **Brands:** Zanocin, Oflox (tablets, infusion, eye/ear drops).

## 11.3 Anti-tubercular agents

**Tuberculosis** (TB) is caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*. It needs **combination therapy for months** to cure and to prevent resistance. **First-line drugs: isoniazid (H), rifampicin (R), pyrazinamide (Z), ethambutol (E)**; streptomycin (S) was also first line. Under India's National TB Elimination Programme, new cases receive **2 months HRZE + 4 months HRE** (daily, fixed-dose combinations).

**MDR-TB** (resistant to at least H and R) and **XDR-TB** need second-line and newer drugs (bedaquiline, delamanid, pretomanid, linezolid, fluoroquinolones).

| Drug | Chemistry | Mechanism | Key adverse effects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Isoniazid (INH)\*** | Pyridine-4-carbohydrazide | Inhibits **mycolic acid** synthesis (cell wall) | Peripheral neuropathy, hepatitis |
| Rifampicin | Semisynthetic rifamycin (macrocyclic) | Inhibits bacterial **DNA-dependent RNA polymerase** | Hepatitis, **orange-red urine/tears**, enzyme induction (reduces contraceptive effect) |
| Pyrazinamide | Pyrazine-2-carboxamide | Active in acid medium inside macrophages | Hepatotoxicity, **raised uric acid (gout)** |
| Ethambutol | Ethylenediamine dibutanol (D-isomer) | Inhibits arabinogalactan synthesis (cell wall) | **Optic neuritis** (red–green colour vision loss) – avoid in young children who cannot report vision |
| Para-aminosalicylic acid (PAS) | 4-amino-2-hydroxybenzoic acid | Folate synthesis inhibitor (PABA analogue) | GI upset; second line |
| Bedaquiline | **Diarylquinoline** | Inhibits mycobacterial **ATP synthase** | QT prolongation; MDR-TB |
| Delamanid | Nitro-dihydro-imidazo-oxazole | Inhibits mycolic acid synthesis | QT prolongation; MDR-TB |
| **Pretomanid\*** | Nitroimidazo-oxazine | Inhibits mycolic acid synthesis; releases nitric oxide in non-growing bacilli | Liver injury, peripheral neuropathy, myelosuppression (with linezolid) |

### Isoniazid\* (INH)

- **Chemical name:** pyridine-4-carbohydrazide (isonicotinic acid hydrazide).
- **Structure:** a **pyridine** ring with a **–CO–NH–NH2** (carbohydrazide) group at position 4.

```text
          N
       //   \
      |      |
       \\   /
          C4 ── C(=O) ── NH ── NH2
```

- **Mechanism:** a **prodrug** activated by the mycobacterial enzyme **KatG**; inhibits synthesis of **mycolic acids** of the cell wall. Bactericidal for multiplying bacilli.
- **Uses:** all forms of TB (part of every first-line regimen); **TB preventive therapy** (contacts, HIV-positive persons).
- **Adverse effects:** **peripheral neuropathy** (INH causes **pyridoxine** deficiency – give **vitamin B6 10–25 mg/day** to prevent it), **hepatitis** (more with age, alcohol and rifampicin), rash, fits in overdose. **Slow acetylators** keep higher levels (more neuropathy).
- **Interactions:** inhibits metabolism of phenytoin (toxicity).
- **Storage:** protect from light.
- **Brands:** Isokin; part of FDC tablets (e.g. Akurit, Rifa-I-6).

### Pretomanid\*

- **Chemical name:** (6S)-2-nitro-6-{[4-(trifluoromethoxy)benzyl]oxy}-6,7-dihydro-5H-imidazo[2,1-b][1,3]oxazine.
- **Structure:** a **nitroimidazole** ring fused to a **dihydro-oxazine** ring (bicyclic nitroimidazo-oxazine), with a **4-(trifluoromethoxy)benzyloxy** side chain; one chiral centre (S).
- **Mechanism:** a prodrug activated by a mycobacterial nitroreductase; inhibits mycolic acid synthesis in growing bacilli and releases **reactive nitrogen species (nitric oxide)** that kill dormant bacilli.
- **Uses:** with **bedaquiline and linezolid** (the "**BPaL**" regimen; BPaLM adds moxifloxacin) for **drug-resistant (MDR/XDR) pulmonary TB**, over about 6 months.
- **Adverse effects:** liver injury, peripheral and optic neuropathy, anaemia and low blood counts (mostly from linezolid), QT prolongation with bedaquiline.

## 11.4 Antiviral agents

Viruses live inside host cells, so few drugs are selective. Most antivirals are **nucleoside analogues** that block viral nucleic acid synthesis.

| Drug | Chemistry | Mechanism | Uses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Amantadine hydrochloride | **Adamantane** (cage-like tricyclic) amine | Blocks influenza A **M2 ion channel** (uncoating); also releases dopamine | Influenza A (rarely now – resistance); **Parkinsonism** |
| Idoxuridine | 5-Iodo-2'-deoxyuridine (thymidine analogue) | Gets into viral DNA | **Topical** for herpes simplex keratitis (too toxic systemically) |
| **Acyclovir\*** | Acyclic guanosine analogue | Viral DNA polymerase inhibitor / chain terminator | Herpes simplex, varicella-zoster |
| Foscarnet | Pyrophosphate analogue (trisodium phosphonoformate) | Directly blocks viral DNA polymerase | CMV retinitis, acyclovir-resistant herpes; nephrotoxic |
| Zidovudine (AZT) | 3'-azido-3'-deoxythymidine | **Nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor** (NRTI) | **HIV** (with other antiretrovirals), prevention of mother-to-child transmission; bone marrow suppression (anaemia) |
| Ribavirin | Synthetic guanosine-like nucleoside (triazole carboxamide) | Interferes with viral RNA synthesis | RSV (aerosol), hepatitis C (with others), Lassa fever; haemolytic anaemia, teratogenic |
| Remdesivir | Adenosine nucleotide analogue **prodrug** | Inhibits viral **RNA-dependent RNA polymerase** | COVID-19 (IV, hospitalised patients) |
| Favipiravir | Pyrazinecarboxamide derivative (prodrug) | Inhibits viral RNA polymerase | Influenza; was used for mild COVID-19 in some countries |

### Acyclovir\* (aciclovir)

- **Chemical name:** 2-amino-9-[(2-hydroxyethoxy)methyl]-1,9-dihydro-6H-purin-6-one.
- **Structure:** **guanine** (a purine base) attached at N-9 to an **acyclic** side chain –CH2–O–CH2–CH2–OH that mimics an incomplete deoxyribose sugar (no 3'-OH).

```text
        guanine ring (purine)
              |
              N9 ── CH2 ── O ── CH2 ── CH2 ── OH
                    (acyclic "sugar": no ring, no 3'-OH)
```

- **Mechanism:** phosphorylated first by **viral thymidine kinase** (so it is activated only in infected cells – selective), then by host enzymes to acyclovir triphosphate, which inhibits viral DNA polymerase and is incorporated into viral DNA, causing **chain termination** (no 3'-OH to add the next nucleotide).
- **Uses:** genital and oral herpes, herpes keratitis (ointment), **herpes zoster (shingles)**, chickenpox in high-risk patients, herpes encephalitis (IV), prophylaxis in transplant patients.
- **Adverse effects:** usually mild (headache, nausea); IV can cause **crystalluria/renal damage** (keep patient hydrated, infuse slowly).
- **Points:** poor oral absorption (prodrug **valacyclovir** gives higher levels).
- **Brands:** Zovirax, Acivir (tablets, cream, eye ointment, IV).

## 11.5 Antimalarials

Malaria is caused by *Plasmodium* (in India mainly *P. falciparum* and *P. vivax*), spread by female *Anopheles* mosquitoes.

**Classification by chemistry**

| Class | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| Cinchona alkaloid (quinoline methanol) | **Quinine** |
| 4-Aminoquinoline | **Chloroquine**, amodiaquine |
| 8-Aminoquinoline | **Primaquine**, tafenoquine |
| Quinoline methanol (synthetic) | **Mefloquine** |
| Biguanide / dihydrotriazine | Proguanil → active metabolite **cycloguanil** |
| Diaminopyrimidine | **Pyrimethamine** (with sulfadoxine) |
| Sesquiterpene lactone endoperoxide | **Artemisinin** and derivatives (artesunate, artemether, arteether) |

**By stage of action:** blood schizonticides (chloroquine, quinine, mefloquine, artemisinins) cure the attack; **tissue schizonticides** (primaquine) kill liver forms (hypnozoites) to prevent **relapse** in vivax; **gametocides** (primaquine) stop spread.

### Chloroquine phosphate\*

- **Chemical name:** N4-(7-chloroquinolin-4-yl)-N1,N1-diethylpentane-1,4-diamine (as diphosphate).
- **Structure:** a **quinoline** ring with **Cl at position 7** and, at position 4, an amino group carrying a branched diethylaminopentyl side chain (4-aminoquinoline).

```text
   quinoline ring
     C-7 : Cl
     C-4 : NH ─ CH(CH3) ─ CH2CH2CH2 ─ N(C2H5)2
```

- **Mechanism:** concentrates in the parasite's food vacuole and stops conversion of toxic haem into harmless **haemozoin** → parasite dies.
- **Uses:** **vivax** malaria (with primaquine for radical cure); falciparum only where sensitive (widespread resistance); **extra-intestinal amoebiasis**; rheumatoid arthritis and lupus (hydroxychloroquine preferred).
- **Adverse effects:** bitter taste, nausea, **itching** (common in Africans/Indians), headache; long-term high doses → **retinopathy**; IV bolus dangerous (hypotension).
- **Storage:** well-closed, protected from light; keep away from children (overdose is very toxic).
- **Brands:** Lariago, Resochin (tablets, syrup, injection).

### Primaquine phosphate

- 8-Aminoquinoline. Kills liver hypnozoites → **radical cure of vivax** (14 days); single low dose to kill falciparum gametocytes.
- **Causes haemolysis in G6PD deficiency** – test where possible; not in pregnancy or infants.

### Quinine sulphate

- Main alkaloid of **cinchona bark**; quinoline-methanol with a quinuclidine ring.
- Severe/complicated falciparum (IV, when artesunate unavailable), resistant malaria (oral with doxycycline/clindamycin), nocturnal leg cramps.
- **Cinchonism** (tinnitus, deafness, vertigo, nausea), hypoglycaemia (stimulates insulin release).

### Mefloquine\*

- **Chemical name:** [2,8-bis(trifluoromethyl)quinolin-4-yl](piperidin-2-yl)methanol (as hydrochloride; racemic).
- **Structure:** a **quinoline methanol** related to quinine: quinoline ring with **two CF3 groups (positions 2 and 8)**; at position 4 a –CH(OH)– linking to a **2-piperidyl** ring.
- **Uses:** treatment of uncomplicated chloroquine-resistant falciparum malaria (with artesunate); **prophylaxis** for travellers (weekly dosing because of long half-life).
- **Adverse effects:** **neuropsychiatric** (vivid dreams, anxiety, depression, psychosis, seizures), dizziness, cardiac conduction changes; avoid in epilepsy and psychiatric illness.
- **Brand:** Mefliam (tablets).

### Cycloguanil, pyrimethamine, artemisinin

- **Cycloguanil** (dihydrotriazine) – active form of proguanil; inhibits plasmodial **dihydrofolate reductase**.
- **Pyrimethamine** – diaminopyrimidine DHFR inhibitor; combined with sulfadoxine (sequential block of folate synthesis) as **SP**, used with artesunate in India; with sulfadiazine for toxoplasmosis.
- **Artemisinin** – from *Artemisia annua* (qinghaosu); **endoperoxide bridge** generates free radicals inside the parasite. Fastest-acting antimalarial. Used as **artemisinin-based combination therapy (ACT)**: artesunate + SP, artemether + lumefantrine. IV artesunate is the drug of choice for severe malaria.

## 11.6 Sulphonamides and sulphones

**Sulphonamides** are synthetic **analogues of para-aminobenzoic acid (PABA)**. Bacteria must make their own folic acid from PABA; sulphonamides **competitively inhibit dihydropteroate synthase** → no folic acid → bacteriostatic. Humans take ready-made folic acid from food, so are not affected.

**Basic structure (sulphanilamide)**

```text
   H2N ──⟨ benzene ⟩── SO2 ── NH ── R
   (N4: free amino           (N1: R varies – heterocycles
    group, must be para       increase potency and
    to the SO2 group)         change solubility)
```

**SAR:** the free **para-amino group (N4)** is essential (or a group that becomes amino in the body); the sulphur must be directly on the benzene ring; substituting N1 with heterocyclic rings (pyrimidine, isoxazole) gives more active drugs.

| Drug | N1 substituent / chemistry | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sulphanilamide | –H (parent compound) | Historical |
| Sulphadiazine | Pyrimidin-2-yl | Toxoplasmosis (with pyrimethamine); silver sulphadiazine cream for burns |
| Sulphamethoxazole | 5-Methylisoxazol-3-yl | With trimethoprim |
| **Sulphacetamide\*** | Acetyl | **Eye infections** (eye drops) |
| Mafenide acetate | Homosulphanilamide (–CH2NH2 instead of –NH2; not a PABA antagonist) | Burns cream (works in pus) |
| Cotrimoxazole | Sulphamethoxazole + trimethoprim (5 : 1) | UTI, respiratory infections, **Pneumocystis pneumonia**, typhoid |
| **Dapsone\*** | Sulphone (not a sulphonamide) | **Leprosy** |

**Adverse effects of sulphonamides:** **crystalluria** (drink plenty of water; alkaline urine), allergic rashes including **Stevens–Johnson syndrome**, haemolysis in G6PD deficiency, kernicterus in newborns (displace bilirubin – avoid near term and in neonates).

**Cotrimoxazole:** sulphamethoxazole blocks dihydropteroate synthase; **trimethoprim** blocks **dihydrofolate reductase** – **sequential blockade**, bactericidal combination. Brands: Septran, Bactrim.

### Sulphacetamide\* (sulfacetamide sodium)

- **Chemical name:** N-[(4-aminophenyl)sulfonyl]acetamide (used as sodium salt).
- **Structure:** sulphanilamide with an **acetyl group on N1** (–SO2–NH–CO–CH3).
- **Points:** the sodium salt is very **water soluble** and gives a nearly neutral solution that does not irritate the eye much; penetrates eye tissues.
- **Uses:** **conjunctivitis, corneal ulcer, trachoma, ophthalmia neonatorum** (10–30% eye drops, eye ointment).
- **Stability/storage:** eye drops darken on long storage/light exposure (discard discoloured solution); store **protected from light**, tightly closed; use within the stated period after opening.
- **Brands:** Albucid, Locula (eye drops).

### Dapsone\*

- **Chemical name:** 4,4'-sulfonyldianiline (bis(4-aminophenyl) sulphone; diaminodiphenyl sulphone, DDS).
- **Structure:** two **p-aminophenyl** rings joined by a **sulphone (–SO2–)** group.

```text
   H2N ──⟨ benzene ⟩── SO2 ──⟨ benzene ⟩── NH2
```

- **Mechanism:** like sulphonamides, inhibits folate synthesis (PABA antagonist).
- **Uses:** **leprosy** (with rifampicin and clofazimine in multidrug therapy), **dermatitis herpetiformis**, prevention of *Pneumocystis* pneumonia in HIV (alternative to cotrimoxazole).
- **Adverse effects:** **haemolytic anaemia** (especially G6PD deficiency), methaemoglobinaemia, "dapsone syndrome" (fever, rash, hepatitis), lepra reactions.
- **Storage:** protect from light.
- **Brands:** Dapsone tablets; part of leprosy MDT blister packs.

## Remember

- Polyenes bind ergosterol (amphotericin B, IV, nephrotoxic, refrigerate); azoles block 14α-demethylase; allylamines block squalene epoxidase.
- Ketoconazole: imidazole + dioxolane + acetylpiperazine; oral restricted (liver); topical shampoo. Fluconazole: bis-triazole difluorophenyl propanol; CSF, 150 mg single dose for vaginal candidiasis.
- Fluoroquinolones: 6-F, 7-piperazine, 3-COOH, 4-oxo; DNA gyrase; ofloxacin tricyclic racemate (levofloxacin = S); no antacids; avoid in children.
- INH = pyridine-4-carbohydrazide; neuropathy → pyridoxine; HRZE. Pretomanid: nitroimidazo-oxazine, BPaL for drug-resistant TB.
- Acyclovir: acyclic guanine; activated by viral thymidine kinase; chain terminator.
- Chloroquine: 7-Cl 4-aminoquinoline (vivax, amoebiasis, retinopathy); primaquine: 8-aminoquinoline (radical cure, G6PD haemolysis); mefloquine: 2,8-bis-CF3 quinoline methanol (neuropsychiatric); artemisinin: endoperoxide, ACT.
- Sulphonamides: PABA analogues, need free para-NH2; crystalluria. Sulphacetamide = N1-acetyl, eye drops. Dapsone = diaminodiphenyl sulphone, leprosy, G6PD haemolysis.

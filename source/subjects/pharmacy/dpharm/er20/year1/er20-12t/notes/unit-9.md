# Unit 9: Hypoglycaemic agents

**Diabetes mellitus** is a long-term disorder with high blood glucose due to lack of insulin, poor action of insulin, or both.

| | Type 1 | Type 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cause | Autoimmune destruction of pancreatic β cells; no insulin | Insulin resistance + gradual β-cell failure |
| Age | Usually young | Usually adults (obesity, family history) |
| Treatment | **Insulin** essential | Diet, exercise, **oral drugs**; insulin later if needed |

**Hypoglycaemic agents** lower blood glucose. The syllabus covers **insulin and its preparations, metformin\*, glibenclamide\*, glimepiride, pioglitazone, repaglinide, gliflozins and gliptins**.

## 9.1 Classification

```text
                    HYPOGLYCAEMIC AGENTS
        ┌──────────────────┴───────────────────────┐
     Insulin                                   Oral / other
  (and analogues)       ┌──────────┬───────────┬──────────┬───────────┬───────────┐
                    Sulphonylureas Biguanides Meglitinides Thiazolidine- DPP-4       SGLT2
                    glibenclamide  metformin  repaglinide  diones       inhibitors  inhibitors
                    glimepiride                            pioglitazone (gliptins)  (gliflozins)
```

| Class | Drugs | Main action | Causes hypoglycaemia alone? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Insulin | Regular, NPH, analogues | Replaces insulin | Yes |
| Sulphonylureas | Glibenclamide, glimepiride, gliclazide, glipizide | ↑ insulin release | **Yes** |
| Biguanides | Metformin | ↓ liver glucose output, ↑ sensitivity | No |
| Meglitinides | Repaglinide, nateglinide | Short burst of insulin release with meals | Yes (less) |
| Thiazolidinediones | Pioglitazone | ↑ insulin sensitivity (PPAR-γ) | No |
| DPP-4 inhibitors (gliptins) | Sitagliptin, vildagliptin, saxagliptin, linagliptin, teneligliptin | ↑ incretin (GLP-1) action | No |
| SGLT2 inhibitors (gliflozins) | Dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, canagliflozin | Glucose lost in urine | No |

## 9.2 Insulin and its preparations

**Insulin** is a polypeptide hormone made by the **β cells of the islets of Langerhans** of the pancreas. It has **51 amino acids** in two chains: **A chain (21)** and **B chain (30)**, joined by **two disulphide bridges** (plus one more disulphide within the A chain).

**Actions:** helps glucose enter muscle and fat cells; promotes glycogen, fat and protein storage; reduces glucose production by the liver.

**Sources:** earlier from beef and pork pancreas; now **human insulin by recombinant DNA technology** (in bacteria or yeast) and **insulin analogues** (amino acid sequence altered to change speed of action).

**Strength:** labelled in **units** (IU). Common strengths: **U-40** (40 units/ml, vials, use U-40 syringes) and **U-100** (100 units/ml, pens and vials). Using the wrong syringe gives a wrong dose.

### 9.2.1 Types of insulin preparations

| Type | Examples | Onset | Duration (approx.) | Appearance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rapid-acting analogues** | Insulin lispro, aspart, glulisine | 10–20 min | 3–5 h | Clear |
| **Short-acting** | Regular (soluble, plain) insulin | 30–60 min | 6–8 h | Clear |
| **Intermediate-acting** | **NPH (isophane)** insulin – insulin + protamine + zinc; lente (zinc suspension) | 1–2 h | 12–18 h | **Cloudy** (suspension) |
| **Long-acting analogues** | Insulin glargine, detemir, degludec | 1–2 h | ~24 h or more | Clear (glargine) |
| **Premixed** | 30/70 (30% regular + 70% NPH), 50/50 | Biphasic | — | Cloudy |

- Only **clear soluble (regular) insulin** (and some analogues) can be given **intravenously** – used in diabetic ketoacidosis and emergencies.
- Cloudy insulins must be **gently rolled** (not shaken) to mix before use.

### 9.2.2 Uses of insulin

Type 1 diabetes; type 2 not controlled by oral drugs; diabetes in pregnancy; diabetic ketoacidosis and hyperosmolar state; diabetic patients during surgery, serious infection; hyperkalaemia (with glucose).

### 9.2.3 Adverse effects

**Hypoglycaemia** (sweating, tremor, palpitations, confusion – treat with oral glucose or IV dextrose/glucagon), weight gain, lipodystrophy at injection sites (rotate sites), allergy (rare with human insulin).

### 9.2.4 Storage of insulin

1. Unopened vials and pens: **refrigerator, 2–8 °C**. **Never freeze** (frozen insulin is spoiled).
2. In-use vial/pen may be kept at room temperature (below about 25–30 °C) for about **4 weeks**, away from heat and sunlight.
3. Do not use if a clear insulin turns cloudy, or if a cloudy insulin has clumps or frosting.
4. During travel use insulated bags; do not keep in a car or checked-in baggage hold.

## 9.3 Biguanides

### Metformin\*

- **Chemical name:** N,N-dimethylbiguanide (3-(diaminomethylidene)-1,1-dimethylguanidine), used as **hydrochloride**.
- **Structure:** a **biguanide** – two guanidine units joined together – with two methyl groups on one terminal nitrogen. Strongly basic, very water-soluble, small molecule.

```text
       H3C              NH        NH
           \            ‖         ‖
            N ──────── C ── NH ── C ── NH2
           /
       H3C
   (two linked guanidine units = biguanide)
```

- **Mechanism:** mainly **reduces glucose production in the liver** (gluconeogenesis), increases glucose uptake by muscles (better insulin sensitivity), reduces glucose absorption from gut. Does **not** increase insulin release, so does **not cause hypoglycaemia** when used alone. Acts partly through activation of AMP-activated protein kinase.
- **Uses:** **first-line drug for type 2 diabetes**, especially overweight patients (no weight gain); polycystic ovarian syndrome; prediabetes.
- **Dose:** usually started at 500 mg once or twice daily with meals, increased gradually (common maximum 2–2.5 g daily in divided doses).
- **Adverse effects:** GI upset (metallic taste, nausea, diarrhoea – reduced by taking with food and starting low); **vitamin B12 deficiency** with long use; rare but serious **lactic acidosis** (risk in kidney failure, liver failure, heart failure, alcoholism, before iodinated contrast dye).
- **Stability/storage:** stable; store in well-closed containers at room temperature.
- **Formulations/brands:** tablets 500 mg, 850 mg, 1 g, sustained-release (SR/ER) tablets; combinations with glimepiride, sitagliptin, vildagliptin, dapagliflozin, pioglitazone. Brands: Glycomet, Glucophage, Obimet.

## 9.4 Sulphonylureas

**Mechanism:** bind the **sulphonylurea receptor** on pancreatic β cells and **close ATP-sensitive K⁺ channels** → depolarisation → Ca²⁺ entry → **insulin release**. Need functioning β cells, so useless in type 1 diabetes.

**General structure:**

```text
   R1 ─ C6H4 ─ SO2 ─ NH ─ C(=O) ─ NH ─ R2
        (benzene)  (sulphonyl) (urea)
```

| Generation | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| First | Tolbutamide, chlorpropamide (rarely used) |
| Second | **Glibenclamide (glyburide)**, glipizide, gliclazide |
| Third (or later second) | **Glimepiride** |

### Glibenclamide\* (glyburide)

- **Chemical name:** 5-chloro-N-[2-[4-(cyclohexylcarbamoylsulfamoyl)phenyl]ethyl]-2-methoxybenzamide.
- **Structure:** a sulphonylurea: benzene ring bearing –SO2–NH–CO–NH–**cyclohexyl** (R2) on one side and, through an ethyl link, a **5-chloro-2-methoxybenzamide** group (R1) on the other. The large R1 group makes it a potent second-generation drug.
- **Uses:** type 2 diabetes not controlled by diet and metformin.
- **Dose:** usually 2.5–5 mg once daily with breakfast, adjusted; maximum about 15–20 mg/day.
- **Adverse effects:** **hypoglycaemia** (can be prolonged, especially in elderly and in kidney disease – glibenclamide is long acting and has active metabolites), weight gain, GI upset, skin rash.
- **Interactions:** effect increased by alcohol, sulphonamides, some antifungals; β blockers mask warning signs of hypoglycaemia.
- **Storage:** well-closed container, room temperature.
- **Brands:** Daonil, Euglucon (tablets; combinations with metformin).

### Glimepiride

- Sulphonylurea with a pyrroline-carboxamide side chain and a methylcyclohexyl group.
- Once daily; lower risk of hypoglycaemia than glibenclamide; less weight gain.
- Brand: Amaryl, Glimy (alone and with metformin).

## 9.5 Meglitinides

### Repaglinide

- A **benzoic acid** derivative (carbamoylmethyl benzoic acid), non-sulphonylurea.
- Closes K-ATP channels like sulphonylureas, but **acts quickly and briefly**: taken just before each main meal ("one meal, one dose; skip a meal, skip a dose").
- Controls post-meal glucose; less prolonged hypoglycaemia.

## 9.6 Thiazolidinediones (glitazones)

### Pioglitazone

- Contains a **thiazolidine-2,4-dione** ring linked through a benzyl group to an ethylpyridine ether.
- **Mechanism:** activates the nuclear receptor **PPAR-γ** → increases insulin sensitivity in fat, muscle and liver.
- Uses: type 2 diabetes (with metformin or sulphonylurea); slow onset (weeks).
- Adverse effects: **weight gain, fluid retention** (avoid in heart failure), bone fractures, possible bladder cancer risk with long use; liver tests advised.
- Brand: Pioz.

## 9.7 Gliptins (DPP-4 inhibitors)

- After a meal the gut releases **incretins** (GLP-1, GIP), which increase insulin and reduce glucagon in a glucose-dependent way. The enzyme **dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4)** destroys them quickly.
- **Gliptins block DPP-4** → incretin levels rise → better glucose control **without much hypoglycaemia** and **weight neutral**.
- Examples: sitagliptin, vildagliptin, saxagliptin, linagliptin (no dose change in kidney disease), teneligliptin.
- Adverse effects: headache, nasopharyngitis, rare pancreatitis.
- Brands: Januvia (sitagliptin), Galvus (vildagliptin), Trajenta (linagliptin).

## 9.8 Gliflozins (SGLT2 inhibitors)

- **Sodium–glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2)** in the proximal tubule reabsorbs most filtered glucose.
- **Gliflozins inhibit SGLT2** → glucose is passed in the **urine** (glycosuria) → blood glucose falls, with calorie loss (weight loss) and mild diuresis (BP falls).
- Also protect the **heart and kidneys** (reduce heart failure hospitalisation and kidney disease progression).
- Examples: dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, canagliflozin.
- Adverse effects: **genital fungal infections**, urinary tract infections, dehydration, rare ketoacidosis (euglycaemic).
- Brands: Forxiga (dapagliflozin), Jardiance (empagliflozin).

## 9.9 Counselling points

1. Take sulphonylureas with or just before breakfast; never skip meals after taking them.
2. Carry glucose or sugar to treat hypoglycaemia.
3. Metformin with or after food; tell the doctor before contrast X-ray studies.
4. Check blood glucose regularly; HbA1c every 3 months.
5. Foot care, diet and exercise are part of treatment.

## Remember

- Insulin: 51 amino acids, A (21) + B (30) chains, disulphide bonds; regular is clear and can go IV; NPH is cloudy (protamine + zinc); store 2–8 °C, never freeze, in-use ~4 weeks at room temperature.
- Metformin: N,N-dimethylbiguanide; ↓ hepatic glucose; no hypoglycaemia alone; lactic acidosis, B12 deficiency; first line in type 2.
- Glibenclamide: second-generation sulphonylurea (cyclohexyl urea + chloromethoxybenzamide); closes K-ATP channels; prolonged hypoglycaemia.
- Repaglinide: meal-time; pioglitazone: PPAR-γ, fluid retention; gliptins: DPP-4 inhibitors; gliflozins: SGLT2, glucose in urine, genital infections.

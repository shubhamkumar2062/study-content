# How to use this guide

The subject has 13 units that follow the 13 chapters of the ER-2020 syllabus. Units 1–3 are analytical and inorganic chemistry; Unit 4 is naming of compounds; Units 5–13 are drug monographs (class, chemical name, structure of starred drugs, uses, stability, storage, dosage forms and brands).

Study plan: one unit a day. Read the notes, write each starred structure once from memory, then answer the question bank without looking. Finish with the MCQs of that unit.

**Typical ER-2020 D.Pharm theory paper (80 marks, 3 hours) + 20 marks sessional.** Boards and universities following the PCI Education Regulations 2020 commonly use the pattern below. Confirm the exact pattern with your college, because a board may change the number of questions.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective questions (MCQs / fill in the blanks / one word) – all compulsory | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Short answers – attempt 10 of 11 | 10 × 3 = 30 |
| C | Long answers – attempt 6 of 7 | 6 × 5 = 30 |

In this app, **long** questions carry 5 marks and **short** questions 3 marks to match this pattern. **Very-short** (2-mark) questions are for quick revision and viva.

**Probability ratings**

- **High** – asked in most years or carries large syllabus weight (limit tests, titration types, antacids, starred CNS/ANS drugs, anti-TB, antimalarials, penicillins).
- **Medium** – asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** – rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates based on syllabus hours and common question patterns. They are not a promise.

**Scoring tips for Pharmaceutical Chemistry**

1. **For a drug monograph answer use the same order every time:** class → chemical name → structure (if starred) → mechanism (one line) → uses → adverse effects → stability and storage → dosage forms/brands. Examiners give a mark for each heading.
2. **Draw structures neatly** with the key group labelled (e.g. "Cl at C-2" for chlorpromazine, "5,5-diphenyl" for phenytoin). If unsure of a full structure, draw the nucleus and label the substituents in words; that still earns part marks.
3. **For limit tests** always write: principle with equation → reagents and their role → procedure in a two-column table (test vs standard) → observation → result ("passes if…").
4. **For titrations** write the principle, the equation, the indicator with its colour change, and one pharmaceutical example.
5. **Storage is a favourite exam point**: light-sensitive (adrenaline, chlorpromazine, frusemide, nifedipine, silver nitrate), moisture-sensitive (aspirin, ferrous sulphate, carbamazepine), refrigerated (insulin, amphotericin B, reconstituted amoxicillin syrup, vinblastine), not refrigerated (5-FU, cisplatin solution).
6. **Learn one brand name per drug** – the syllabus asks for "popular brand names".
7. In MCQs, watch for pairs that are easily confused: iodimetry vs iodometry, Mohr vs Volhard, chlorpromazine (phenothiazine) vs haloperidol (butyrophenone), imipramine (N in ring) vs amitriptyline (C= in ring), primaquine (8-amino) vs chloroquine (4-amino).

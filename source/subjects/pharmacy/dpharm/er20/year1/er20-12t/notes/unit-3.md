# Unit 3: Inorganic pharmaceuticals

For each compound the syllabus asks for **formulations, market preparations, storage conditions and uses**. Learn the formula, one line on properties, the dosage form, how to store it, and its uses. Brand names given here are common examples; brands change, so learn the generic name first.

## 3.1 Haematinics

**Haematinics** are substances that help form haemoglobin and red blood cells. They are used to prevent and treat **iron-deficiency anaemia** (common in pregnancy, menstruating women, children and worm infestation). Iron is absorbed mainly as the **ferrous (Fe²⁺)** form in the duodenum; vitamin C (ascorbic acid) helps absorption; antacids, tea, calcium and tetracyclines reduce it.

Common side effects of oral iron: nausea, epigastric pain, constipation or diarrhoea, black stools. Accidental overdose in children is dangerous, so keep iron tablets out of reach of children.

### 3.1.1 Ferrous sulphate

- **Formula:** FeSO4·7H2O (dried ferrous sulphate is the partly dehydrated form used in tablets).
- **Properties:** pale bluish-green crystals; in moist air it oxidises to brownish-yellow basic ferric sulphate.
- **Formulations:** tablets (often film-coated), capsules with folic acid, syrups and drops. A 200 mg tablet of dried ferrous sulphate gives about 60–65 mg of elemental iron.
- **Market examples:** ferrous sulphate + folic acid tablets (e.g. Fefol).
- **Storage:** well-closed, airtight containers, protected from moisture and air (to stop oxidation).
- **Uses:** prevention and treatment of iron-deficiency anaemia; iron supplement in pregnancy (with folic acid).

### 3.1.2 Ferrous fumarate

- **Formula:** C4H2FeO4 (iron(II) salt of fumaric acid); contains about one-third iron by weight.
- **Properties:** reddish-orange to reddish-brown powder; more stable to oxidation than ferrous sulphate; almost tasteless.
- **Formulations:** tablets, capsules, suspensions, usually combined with folic acid and vitamin B12.
- **Market examples:** Livogen (with folic acid), Autrin (with B12 and other nutrients).
- **Storage:** well-closed container, cool dry place.
- **Uses:** iron-deficiency anaemia.

### 3.1.3 Ferric ammonium citrate

- **Nature:** a complex salt of indefinite composition containing iron, ammonia and citric acid; occurs as brown scales/granules (also a green variety).
- **Properties:** very soluble in water, **deliquescent**, affected by light.
- **Formulations:** mainly oral liquids (syrups, tonics) for children and elderly because it is soluble and less astringent.
- **Market example:** Dexorange syrup (with vitamin B12 and folic acid).
- **Storage:** airtight, light-resistant containers in a cool place.
- **Uses:** iron-deficiency anaemia. It is a ferric salt, so it is less well absorbed than ferrous salts.

### 3.1.4 Ferrous ascorbate

- **Nature:** synthetic complex of ferrous iron with ascorbic acid.
- **Properties:** ascorbate keeps iron in the ferrous form and improves absorption; usually better tolerated in the stomach.
- **Formulations:** tablets and suspensions, generally with folic acid (and zinc).
- **Market example:** Orofer XT (ferrous ascorbate + folic acid).
- **Storage:** cool, dry place, protected from light.
- **Uses:** iron-deficiency anaemia, anaemia of pregnancy.

### 3.1.5 Carbonyl iron

- **Nature:** very fine, almost pure **elemental iron** particles (about 98% iron) made by heating iron pentacarbonyl.
- **Properties:** dissolves slowly in stomach acid, so absorption is gradual; this lowers stomach upset and the risk of severe poisoning in accidental overdose.
- **Formulations:** capsules, tablets, suspensions, often with folic acid, vitamin B12 and zinc.
- **Storage:** cool dry place, well-closed container.
- **Uses:** iron-deficiency anaemia, supplement in pregnancy.

| Iron preparation | Iron form | Key point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ferrous sulphate | Fe²⁺ | Cheapest, standard; oxidises in moist air |
| Ferrous fumarate | Fe²⁺ | High iron content (~33%), stable |
| Ferric ammonium citrate | Fe³⁺ | Soluble, syrups; deliquescent, light sensitive |
| Ferrous ascorbate | Fe²⁺ + vitamin C | Better absorption, fewer GI effects |
| Carbonyl iron | Elemental Fe | Slow release, safer in overdose |

## 3.2 Gastrointestinal agents

### 3.2.1 Antacids

**Antacids** are weak bases that **neutralise gastric hydrochloric acid** and raise the pH of the stomach. Used in hyperacidity, heartburn, dyspepsia and peptic ulcer (as add-on).

**Qualities of an ideal antacid:** neutralises acid quickly and for a long time; not absorbed (non-systemic); does not cause acid rebound, alkalosis, CO2 production, constipation or diarrhoea; pleasant taste; cheap.

**Classification**

| Type | Examples | Feature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Systemic (absorbable) | Sodium bicarbonate | Absorbed; can cause systemic alkalosis |
| Non-systemic (non-absorbable) | Aluminium hydroxide, magnesium hydroxide, magaldrate, calcium carbonate | Little absorption; local action |

**Why combine aluminium and magnesium?** Aluminium salts cause **constipation**; magnesium salts cause **diarrhoea**. Mixing them balances the bowel effect. Aluminium acts slowly and magnesium acts fast, so the combination gives quick and lasting relief.

**Aluminium hydroxide gel** — Al(OH)3

- White viscous suspension (gel), also dried gel in tablets.
- Al(OH)3 + 3HCl → AlCl3 + 3H2O.
- Side effects: constipation; binds phosphate in gut (long use can cause low phosphate). This phosphate-binding is used to lower phosphate in kidney failure.
- Formulations: suspensions and chewable tablets combined with magnesium hydroxide and simethicone (e.g. Digene, Gelusil, Mucaine with oxetacaine).
- Storage: well-closed containers, not frozen (freezing breaks the gel).
- Uses: antacid, phosphate binder.

**Magnesium hydroxide** — Mg(OH)2

- White powder; its aqueous suspension (about 8% w/w) is **Milk of Magnesia**.
- Mg(OH)2 + 2HCl → MgCl2 + 2H2O.
- Small dose: antacid; larger dose: **osmotic laxative**.
- Avoid in kidney failure (magnesium accumulates).
- Storage: tightly closed (absorbs CO2 from air); suspension not frozen.

**Magaldrate**

- A hydrated **magnesium aluminate** (aluminium magnesium hydroxide sulphate) — a single chemical containing both aluminium and magnesium.
- Acts quickly and lasts long, with little effect on bowel habit.
- Formulations: suspensions and tablets, often with simethicone.
- Storage: well-closed, room temperature.
- Uses: hyperacidity, gastritis, peptic ulcer, heartburn.

**Sodium bicarbonate** — NaHCO3

- White crystalline powder, soluble in water.
- NaHCO3 + HCl → NaCl + H2O + CO2↑ (belching, distension).
- Very fast but short action; systemic absorption can cause **metabolic alkalosis** and sodium load (avoid in hypertension, heart failure); **acid rebound**.
- Formulations: effervescent powders (e.g. Eno, with citric acid), injection (7.5% / 8.4%).
- Storage: well-closed container (slowly loses CO2 in moist warm air).
- Uses: antacid (occasional), **systemic alkaliniser** in metabolic acidosis, **urinary alkaliniser**, ear-wax softener (ear drops).

**Calcium carbonate** — CaCO3

- White, odourless, tasteless powder (precipitated chalk).
- CaCO3 + 2HCl → CaCl2 + H2O + CO2.
- Potent and quick; causes constipation, **acid rebound**; large long-term doses with milk can cause **milk-alkali syndrome** (high calcium, alkalosis, kidney damage).
- Formulations: chewable antacid tablets; calcium supplement tablets with vitamin D3 (e.g. Shelcal).
- Storage: well-closed container.
- Uses: antacid, calcium supplement, phosphate binder, dental abrasive.

### 3.2.2 Acidifying agents

Used when the stomach makes too little acid (**achlorhydria / hypochlorhydria**) or to acidify the body or urine.

- **Dilute hydrochloric acid** (about 10% w/v HCl): given well diluted with water, sipped through a straw to protect the teeth. Use: achlorhydria. Store in glass-stoppered bottles.
- **Ammonium chloride** (NH4Cl): systemic acidifier (in metabolic alkalosis), urinary acidifier, and expectorant in cough syrups.
- **Sodium acid phosphate** (NaH2PO4): urinary acidifier.

### 3.2.3 Adsorbents and protectives

**Adsorbents** bind toxins, gases and bacteria on their large surface. **Protectives** form a coating over an inflamed or ulcerated surface.

- **Kaolin** (light kaolin, natural hydrated aluminium silicate): adsorbent antidiarrhoeal (kaolin mixtures); also a dusting powder and poultice base.
- **Activated charcoal:** adsorbs poisons in acute oral poisoning; flatulence.
- **Bismuth subcarbonate / bismuth salts:** protective and mild astringent coating in diarrhoea and ulcers.
- **Aluminium hydroxide** also acts as a protective.
- **Bentonite** (colloidal aluminium silicate clay): suspending agent and adsorbent.

Storage: well-closed containers. Adsorbents can also bind other drugs, so give other medicines at a different time.

### 3.2.4 Cathartics (purgatives / laxatives)

**Cathartics** promote evacuation of the bowel. Inorganic cathartics are mostly **saline (osmotic) purgatives**: poorly absorbed salts that hold water in the intestine, increase bulk and stimulate movement.

| Compound | Formula | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Magnesium sulphate (Epsom salt) | MgSO4·7H2O | Saline purgative, given in the morning with plenty of water; also IV/IM to control convulsions in eclampsia, and as a paste (magnesium sulphate + glycerin) for boils |
| Magnesium hydroxide | Mg(OH)2 | Milk of Magnesia; laxative in larger dose |
| Sodium phosphate (sodium orthophosphate) | Na2HPO4 / NaH2PO4 | Bowel preparation, enemas |
| Sodium potassium tartrate (Rochelle salt) | KNaC4H4O6·4H2O | Saline purgative |
| Sodium sulphate (Glauber's salt) | Na2SO4·10H2O | Saline purgative |

Store hydrated salts in well-closed containers in a cool place (they effloresce in dry air).

## 3.3 Topical agents

Topical agents act on skin and mucous membranes as **antiseptics** (stop growth of microbes on living tissue), **astringents** (precipitate proteins, tighten tissue) and **protectives**.

**Silver nitrate** — AgNO3

- Colourless crystals; on light exposure in contact with organic matter it turns grey-black (metallic silver), staining skin.
- Action: antiseptic, astringent and **caustic** (in high strength).
- Formulations: toughened silver nitrate **sticks (caustic pencils)**, dilute solutions.
- Uses: removing warts and excess granulation tissue, cauterising small wounds; dilute solutions were used for ophthalmia neonatorum prophylaxis.
- Storage: **light-resistant, well-closed containers**.

**Ionic silver**

- Silver ions (Ag⁺) released slowly from gels, creams and dressings (silver salts, nano/colloidal silver).
- Broad antimicrobial action; ions bind bacterial proteins and DNA.
- Uses: burns, chronic wounds, ulcers, infected wounds. Silver sulfadiazine cream (burns) also releases silver.
- Storage: as directed; protect from light.

**Chlorhexidine gluconate**

- A **bisbiguanide** antiseptic, supplied as an aqueous gluconate solution.
- Broad-spectrum against bacteria; binds to skin and mouth tissue and keeps acting (substantivity).
- Formulations: 0.2% mouthwash (e.g. Hexidine), 2–4% surgical hand scrub, alcoholic skin preparation, combined with cetrimide in antiseptic liquids (e.g. Savlon), vaginal and wound preparations.
- Incompatible with soaps and anionic agents (inactivated).
- Side effect: brown staining of teeth/tongue with long use of mouthwash; taste change.
- Storage: protect from light; well-closed.
- Uses: skin disinfection before surgery and injections, hand washing, gingivitis and plaque control, wound cleaning.

**Hydrogen peroxide** — H2O2

- Usually **6% w/v solution, known as "20 volume"** (1 ml releases about 20 ml of oxygen).
- Action: releases nascent oxygen (catalase in tissue) → oxidises microbes; the **effervescence** loosens debris and pus.
- Uses: cleaning wounds and ulcers, mouthwash (diluted), softening ear wax, bleaching hair.
- Stability: decomposes on exposure to light, heat, alkali and metals; a stabiliser (e.g. acetanilide or phosphoric acid) is added.
- Storage: **cool dark place, in containers with a vented/loose closure** (pressure of oxygen), not more than about 30 °C.

**Boric acid** — H3BO3

- White crystals or powder; very weak acid; weak bacteriostatic and fungistatic.
- Formulations: eye lotions, dusting powders, ear drops, buffer in eye drops.
- Caution: absorbed through broken skin and in infants can be **toxic**; do not use on large raw surfaces or on infants.
- Storage: well-closed container.

**Bleaching powder (chlorinated lime)**

- Approximate formula CaOCl2 (mixture of calcium hypochlorite, chloride and hydroxide). Should contain **not less than about 30% available chlorine**.
- In water it releases **hypochlorous acid / chlorine**, which kills microbes.
- Uses: disinfecting drinking water, swimming pools, drains, latrines, floors; preparing Dakin's solution (surgical chlorinated soda).
- Stability: loses chlorine on exposure to air, moisture, light and heat.
- Storage: **airtight containers in a cool, dry, dark place**.

**Potassium permanganate** — KMnO4

- Dark purple crystals; powerful **oxidising agent**; releases oxygen.
- Dilute solutions (about 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 10,000) for cleaning wounds and ulcers, soaks in fungal foot infections and weeping eczema; was used for gastric lavage in some poisonings.
- Stains skin and clothes brown. Crystals must never be applied directly (caustic).
- Storage: well-closed containers, away from organic matter (fire/explosion risk with reducing substances).

## 3.4 Dental products

**Anticaries agents** reduce tooth decay; **dentifrices** clean teeth; other products care for dentures and the mouth.

| Product | Role | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Calcium carbonate** (precipitated chalk) | Mild **abrasive** (polishing agent) in toothpastes and tooth powders | Removes stains and plaque without damaging enamel |
| **Sodium fluoride** (NaF) | **Anticaries** | Fluoride converts hydroxyapatite of enamel into **fluorapatite**, which resists acid; also reduces bacterial acid formation. Used in toothpastes (as NaF or monofluorophosphate), mouth rinses, topical gels/varnish. Excess intake causes **dental fluorosis** (mottled enamel). |
| **Denture cleaners** | Remove stains, plaque, food and microbes from dentures | Effervescent tablets/powders containing alkaline peroxides (sodium perborate, sodium percarbonate) that release oxygen; also hypochlorite or enzyme-based cleaners |
| **Denture adhesives** | Hold dentures firmly to the gums | Creams, powders or strips of water-swellable polymers such as sodium carboxymethylcellulose and salts of polyvinyl methyl ether–maleic acid copolymer |
| **Mouthwashes** | Freshen breath, reduce plaque and gingivitis | Antiseptics (chlorhexidine 0.2%, cetylpyridinium chloride, povidone-iodine, essential oils such as thymol, menthol, eucalyptol), fluoride rinses, astringents |

Storage of fluoride products: keep out of reach of children (fluoride is toxic if swallowed in large amounts).

## 3.5 Medicinal gases

Medicinal gases are supplied compressed in metal **cylinders** with standard colour codes so they are not mixed up.

| Gas | Formula | Cylinder colour (India) | Uses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oxygen | O2 | Black body with white shoulder | Hypoxia, respiratory and cardiac failure, shock, anaesthesia (carrier gas), resuscitation, CO poisoning |
| Nitrous oxide | N2O | Blue | "Laughing gas"; analgesic and weak general anaesthetic; mixed 50 : 50 with oxygen (Entonox) for labour pain and dental procedures |
| Carbon dioxide | CO2 | Grey | Inflating the abdomen in laparoscopy (insufflation); solid CO2 (dry ice) for cryotherapy of warts; small % with oxygen was used as a respiratory stimulant |

**Storage and handling of gas cylinders**

1. Store upright, chained, in a cool, dry, well-ventilated area away from heat and direct sun.
2. **Never use oil or grease** on oxygen cylinders or valves (oxygen supports combustion strongly; risk of fire/explosion).
3. No smoking or open flame near oxygen.
4. Keep full and empty cylinders apart and labelled.
5. Open valves slowly; use the correct regulator.

Nitrous oxide is stored as a liquid under pressure; it is not flammable but supports combustion.

## Remember

- Iron is absorbed as Fe²⁺; vitamin C helps, antacids and tea reduce absorption.
- Ferrous sulphate oxidises in moist air → store airtight; ferric ammonium citrate is deliquescent and light sensitive.
- Al(OH)3 constipates, Mg(OH)2 loosens → combine; NaHCO3 is systemic (alkalosis, CO2); CaCO3 causes acid rebound.
- Dilute HCl for achlorhydria (through a straw); NH4Cl acidifier and expectorant.
- Saline purgatives: MgSO4 (also eclampsia), sodium phosphate.
- H2O2 = 6% (20 volume), store cool, dark, vented; bleaching powder ≥30% available chlorine, airtight.
- Silver nitrate in light-resistant containers; chlorhexidine inactivated by soap.
- NaF → fluorapatite (anticaries); CaCO3 = abrasive.
- Oxygen: black with white shoulder, never oil or grease; N2O blue; CO2 grey.

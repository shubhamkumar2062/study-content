# Unit 6: Drugs acting on the autonomic nervous system

The **autonomic nervous system (ANS)** controls involuntary functions (heart, blood vessels, glands, smooth muscle). It has two divisions:

| | Sympathetic | Parasympathetic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transmitter at organ | Noradrenaline (NA) | Acetylcholine (ACh) |
| Receptors | α1, α2, β1, β2 (adrenergic) | Muscarinic (M1–M3) |
| General role | "Fight or flight" | "Rest and digest" |

Starred drugs in this unit: **norepinephrine, dopamine, naphazoline, propranolol, atenolol, acetylcholine, neostigmine, atropine sulphate, dicyclomine**.

## 6.1 Sympathomimetic (adrenergic) agents

Drugs that produce effects like stimulation of sympathetic nerves.

**Receptor effects to remember**

| Receptor | Main effect | Use of agonist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| α1 | Vasoconstriction, mydriasis | Nasal decongestion, raise BP |
| α2 | Reduces NA release (central) | Lower BP (clonidine) |
| β1 | Heart rate and force ↑ | Cardiac shock |
| β2 | Bronchodilation, uterine relaxation | Asthma, preterm labour |

**Classification (by mechanism, as in the syllabus)**

| Mechanism | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| **Direct acting** (bind receptors) | Norepinephrine, epinephrine, phenylephrine, dopamine, terbutaline, salbutamol, naphazoline, tetrahydrozoline |
| **Indirect acting** (release stored NA) | Hydroxyamphetamine, pseudoephedrine |
| **Mixed** (both) | Ephedrine, metaraminol |

### 6.1.1 General structure: β-phenylethylamine

```text
        5   6
    4 /‾‾‾‾\ 1 —— CH —— CH2 —— NH —— R
      \____/      |β    α
        3   2     OH
   (3,4-di-OH = catechol)
```

**Structure–activity points**

1. **Catechol** (3,4-dihydroxy) ring → maximum direct activity, but destroyed by **COMT**, so not active orally and short acting.
2. Removing one or both ring –OH (phenylephrine, ephedrine) → oral activity, longer action, less potency.
3. **β-OH** on the side chain → increases direct receptor activity (R-configuration is the active form).
4. Bigger group on N → more **β** activity and less α: NA (H) → adrenaline (CH3) → isoprenaline (isopropyl) → salbutamol/terbutaline (tert-butyl, β2 selective).
5. **α-methyl** group → resists **MAO**, longer action (ephedrine, metaraminol).

### Norepinephrine\* (noradrenaline)

- **Chemical name:** 4-[(1R)-2-amino-1-hydroxyethyl]benzene-1,2-diol.
- **Structure:** catechol ring; side chain –CH(OH)–CH2–NH2 (primary amine, no N-substituent).
- **Action:** mainly **α1** and **β1** agonist; little β2 → raises both systolic and diastolic BP.
- **Uses:** IV infusion in **hypotension and septic/vasodilatory shock**.
- **Stability/storage:** catecholamines are easily **oxidised** (air, light, alkali, metals) to coloured products (pink → brown, adrenochrome-like). Injections contain an antioxidant (**sodium metabisulphite**), are packed under nitrogen in amber ampoules and stored **protected from light**. Do not use if discoloured or precipitated. Given diluted in dextrose (acid pH protects it), not in alkaline fluids. Extravasation causes tissue necrosis.
- **Brands:** Noradrenaline bitartrate injection (e.g. Adrenor).

### Epinephrine (adrenaline)

- N-methyl analogue of noradrenaline; acts on α and β receptors.
- **Uses:** drug of choice in **anaphylactic shock** (0.5 mg IM, i.e. 0.5 ml of 1 mg/ml, for adults), cardiac arrest, with local anaesthetics to prolong action and reduce bleeding, local haemostatic.
- Same oxidation and light sensitivity as noradrenaline.

### Phenylephrine

- 3-hydroxy only (no 4-OH): not a catechol, so resists COMT, longer acting, active orally and topically.
- Selective **α1** agonist: nasal decongestant (drops, cold tablets), mydriatic eye drops, raises BP in hypotension during anaesthesia.

### Dopamine\*

- **Chemical name:** 4-(2-aminoethyl)benzene-1,2-diol.
- **Structure:** catechol ring with a –CH2–CH2–NH2 chain (**no β-OH**). It is the precursor of noradrenaline.
- **Dose-dependent action:** low dose → **D1** receptors (renal and mesenteric vasodilation); moderate → **β1** (heart stimulation); high → **α1** (vasoconstriction).
- **Uses:** IV infusion in **cardiogenic shock**, heart failure with low output, shock with poor urine output.
- Does not cross the blood–brain barrier (so not used for Parkinsonism; levodopa is used instead).
- **Storage:** protect from light; do not mix with alkaline solutions (sodium bicarbonate) – it is oxidised and inactivated; discard discoloured solution.
- **Brands:** Dopamine HCl injection (e.g. Dopar, Intropin).

### Terbutaline and salbutamol (albuterol)

- Selective **β2 agonists**: bronchodilators in **asthma and COPD**; terbutaline also used to delay preterm labour.
- Salbutamol: a **saligenin** – the 3-OH of the catechol is replaced by **–CH2OH**, so it is not destroyed by COMT; **tert-butyl** on N gives β2 selectivity.
- Terbutaline: a **resorcinol** (3,5-dihydroxy) ring with tert-butyl on N.
- Adverse effects: tremor, palpitations, low potassium.
- Forms: inhalers, rotacaps, nebuliser solution, tablets, syrup. Brands: Asthalin (salbutamol), Bricanyl (terbutaline).

### Naphazoline\*

- **Chemical name:** 2-(naphthalen-1-ylmethyl)-4,5-dihydro-1H-imidazole (used as hydrochloride or nitrate).
- **Structure:** an **imidazoline** ring joined through –CH2– to a **1-naphthyl** group.
- **Action:** α-adrenergic agonist → local vasoconstriction.
- **Uses:** **nasal decongestant** (drops) and **ocular decongestant** for red eyes (eye drops).
- **Caution:** prolonged nasal use causes **rebound congestion (rhinitis medicamentosa)**; can cause CNS depression and coma in infants if swallowed – avoid in young children.
- **Storage:** light-resistant containers.

### Tetrahydrozoline

- Another imidazoline α agonist; ocular and nasal decongestant (eye drops for red eyes).

### Indirect and mixed-acting agents

| Drug | Type | Uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hydroxyamphetamine | Indirect | Mydriatic eye drops; helps locate the lesion in Horner's syndrome |
| Pseudoephedrine | Indirect (releases NA) | Oral nasal decongestant in cold preparations |
| Ephedrine | Mixed (direct + NA release); orally active, crosses brain | Hypotension during spinal anaesthesia, nasal decongestant, earlier used in asthma |
| Metaraminol | Mixed | Hypotension (IV/IM) |

## 6.2 Adrenergic antagonists (blockers)

### 6.2.1 Alpha-adrenergic blockers

| Drug | Type / chemistry | Uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tolazoline | Imidazoline, competitive, non-selective | Peripheral vascular disease, persistent pulmonary hypertension of newborn (earlier) |
| Phentolamine | Imidazoline, competitive, non-selective, short acting | Diagnosis and control of BP in **phaeochromocytoma**; treating extravasation of noradrenaline |
| Phenoxybenzamine | **β-haloalkylamine**; forms a reactive aziridinium ion that binds the receptor covalently → **irreversible**, long acting | Phaeochromocytoma (before surgery) |
| Prazosin | **Quinazoline**, selective **α1** | Hypertension, benign prostatic hyperplasia; **first-dose hypotension** (give first dose at bedtime) |

### 6.2.2 Beta-adrenergic blockers

**General structure: aryloxypropanolamine**

```text
   Ar ── O ── CH2 ── CH ── CH2 ── NH ── CH(CH3)2
                     |
                     OH
   (aromatic ring, then –O–CH2– inserted between ring and the β-carbon;
    isopropyl or tert-butyl on N is best for β blocking)
```

**Classification**

| Type | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| Non-selective (β1 + β2) | Propranolol, timolol, sotalol |
| Cardioselective (β1) | Atenolol, metoprolol, bisoprolol |
| With α1 blocking action | Carvedilol, labetalol |

**Uses (general):** hypertension, angina, arrhythmias, after myocardial infarction, heart failure (carvedilol, bisoprolol, metoprolol), thyrotoxicosis, migraine prophylaxis, anxiety tremor, glaucoma (timolol).

**Cautions:** non-selective drugs can cause **bronchospasm** (avoid in asthma); mask signs of hypoglycaemia in diabetics; bradycardia; sudden stopping can cause rebound angina.

### Propranolol\*

- **Chemical name:** 1-(isopropylamino)-3-(naphthalen-1-yloxy)propan-2-ol (1-(propan-2-ylamino)-3-naphthalen-1-yloxypropan-2-ol), used as hydrochloride; racemic, S-isomer is active.
- **Structure:** **1-naphthyl** ring–O–CH2–CH(OH)–CH2–NH–isopropyl.
- **Points:** non-selective; very **lipid soluble** (enters brain – vivid dreams, fatigue); high first-pass metabolism; membrane-stabilising (local anaesthetic) action.
- **Uses:** hypertension, angina, arrhythmias, migraine prophylaxis, essential tremor, anxiety, thyrotoxicosis, portal hypertension.
- **Storage:** protect from light.
- **Brands:** Inderal, Ciplar (tablets, SR capsules, injection).

### Atenolol\*

- **Chemical name:** 2-[4-[2-hydroxy-3-(isopropylamino)propoxy]phenyl]acetamide.
- **Structure:** aryloxypropanolamine with a **para-acetamide (–CH2CONH2)** group on the benzene ring. The para substituent gives **β1 (cardio-) selectivity**.
- **Points:** **hydrophilic** → little brain entry, few CNS effects; excreted by kidney (reduce dose in renal failure); once daily.
- **Uses:** hypertension, angina, arrhythmias.
- **Brands:** Aten, Tenormin (tablets; combinations with amlodipine, chlorthalidone).

### Carvedilol

- Carbazole-containing β blocker that also blocks **α1** (vasodilation) and has antioxidant action. Used in **heart failure** and hypertension.

## 6.3 Cholinergic drugs (parasympathomimetics) and related agents

**Classification**

| Type | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| Direct acting (choline esters) | Acetylcholine, carbachol, bethanechol |
| Direct acting (alkaloid) | Pilocarpine |
| Cholinesterase inhibitors – reversible | Neostigmine, physostigmine, edrophonium, pyridostigmine, tacrine |
| Cholinesterase inhibitors – irreversible (organophosphates) | Echothiophate, malathion, parathion |
| Cholinesterase reactivator | Pralidoxime |

### Acetylcholine\*

- **Chemical name:** 2-acetoxy-N,N,N-trimethylethan-1-aminium (as chloride).
- **Structure:** CH3COO–CH2–CH2–N⁺(CH3)3 – an **ester of acetic acid and choline**, with a **quaternary ammonium** group.
- **Points:** destroyed in seconds by **acetylcholinesterase** and plasma cholinesterase, and acts on all cholinergic receptors, so it has **no systemic therapeutic use**. Used only as an intraocular solution to produce rapid **miosis** during eye surgery.
- **Stability:** esters hydrolyse in water; supplied as a powder to be dissolved immediately before use.

**SAR of choline esters**

1. Quaternary nitrogen is essential.
2. Two carbons between O and N ("rule of five").
3. Replacing the acetyl by **carbamoyl** (–OCONH2) → resists hydrolysis → **carbachol**.
4. A **β-methyl** on the choline part → more muscarinic selectivity → methacholine; both changes → **bethanechol** (urinary retention).

### Carbachol and pilocarpine

- **Carbachol:** carbamic ester of choline; resistant to cholinesterase; muscarinic + nicotinic; eye drops for **glaucoma** and miosis in surgery.
- **Pilocarpine:** imidazole alkaloid from *Pilocarpus* leaves; muscarinic agonist; eye drops for **glaucoma** (opens drainage), to reverse mydriasis; dry mouth (oral tablets).

### Neostigmine\*

- **Chemical name:** 3-[(dimethylcarbamoyl)oxy]-N,N,N-trimethylanilinium (as bromide or methylsulphate).
- **Structure:** a benzene ring carrying a **dimethylcarbamate ester** and a **quaternary trimethylammonium** group (meta to each other).
- **Action:** reversible **anticholinesterase**: carbamylates the enzyme (slow recovery) → ACh accumulates; also a weak direct action at the neuromuscular junction.
- **Being quaternary**, it does not enter the brain and is poorly absorbed orally.
- **Uses:** **myasthenia gravis**, reversal of non-depolarising neuromuscular blockers after surgery (given with atropine or glycopyrrolate to block muscarinic effects), postoperative urinary retention and paralytic ileus.
- **Storage:** protect from light.
- **Brands:** Myostigmin, Tilstigmin (tablets, injection).

### Other cholinesterase inhibitors

| Drug | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Edrophonium chloride | Very short acting (minutes); **diagnosis of myasthenia gravis** (Tensilon test) |
| Tacrine hydrochloride | **Acridine** derivative (tetrahydroaminoacridine); first drug for Alzheimer's disease; now largely withdrawn (liver toxicity) |
| Echothiophate iodide | **Organophosphate** (irreversible), long acting; eye drops for glaucoma |
| Pralidoxime chloride (2-PAM) | Not an inhibitor but a **cholinesterase reactivator**: oxime that removes the phosphate group from enzyme in **organophosphate poisoning**; must be given early (before "ageing"); used with atropine |

## 6.4 Cholinergic blocking agents (anticholinergics / antimuscarinics)

They block **muscarinic** receptors, producing dry mouth, reduced secretions, rapid heart rate, mydriasis and cycloplegia (loss of accommodation), relaxed smooth muscle, urinary retention.

**Classification**

| Type | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| Natural alkaloids | Atropine, hyoscine (scopolamine) |
| Semi-synthetic / quaternary | Ipratropium bromide, hyoscine butylbromide |
| Synthetic – mydriatics | Tropicamide, cyclopentolate |
| Synthetic – antispasmodics | Dicyclomine, clidinium bromide |

### Atropine sulphate\*

- **Chemical name:** (8-methyl-8-azabicyclo[3.2.1]oct-3-yl) 3-hydroxy-2-phenylpropanoate sulphate – the ester of **tropine** (a tropane amino alcohol) and **(±)-tropic acid**. Atropine is racemic **hyoscyamine**.
- **Structure:** bicyclic **tropane** ring (pyrrolidine + piperidine bridged, N-methyl) esterified at C-3 with tropic acid (which has a phenyl group and –CH2OH).

```text
      N–CH3 bridge
     /         \
  tropane ring  C3 ─ O ─ C(=O) ─ CH(C6H5) ─ CH2OH
                               (tropic acid part)
```

- **Source:** *Atropa belladonna*, *Datura*, *Hyoscyamus*.
- **Uses:** **pre-anaesthetic** medication (dries secretions, prevents vagal bradycardia), **organophosphate poisoning** (large doses, antidote), sinus bradycardia and heart block, mydriatic-cycloplegic for refraction in children (1% eye ointment/drops; long lasting), antispasmodic.
- **Toxicity:** "dry as a bone, red as a beet, hot as a hare, blind as a bat, mad as a hatter" – dry mouth, flushing, fever, blurred vision, delirium.
- **Stability:** the ester **hydrolyses** in alkaline solution (and racemises); injections are kept slightly acidic. Store protected from light; it is a poison (Schedule).
- **Formulations:** injection 0.6 mg/ml, eye drops/ointment.

### Ipratropium bromide

- **Quaternary** derivative of atropine (N-isopropyl); not absorbed when inhaled → local **bronchodilation** in COPD and asthma without systemic effects. Inhaler/respules (e.g. Ipravent).

### Synthetic anticholinergics

| Drug | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Tropicamide | Short-acting **mydriatic** for fundus examination (eye drops) |
| Cyclopentolate hydrochloride | Mydriatic-cycloplegic for refraction (shorter than atropine) |
| Clidinium bromide | Quaternary GI antispasmodic, combined with chlordiazepoxide for irritable bowel |

### Dicyclomine hydrochloride\* (dicycloverine)

- **Chemical name:** 2-(diethylamino)ethyl [1,1'-bi(cyclohexyl)]-1-carboxylate hydrochloride.
- **Structure:** an **ester**: a bicyclohexyl carboxylic acid (two cyclohexane rings, no aromatic ring) esterified with 2-diethylaminoethanol; tertiary amine.
- **Action:** antimuscarinic plus direct smooth-muscle relaxant.
- **Uses:** **abdominal colic**, irritable bowel syndrome, dysmenorrhoea, infantile colic (with caution).
- **Formulations/brands:** tablets, injection, drops; Cyclopam (with paracetamol), Colimex.

## Remember

- Sympathomimetic SAR: catechol → potent but COMT-sensitive; bigger N-group → β; tert-butyl → β2; α-methyl → MAO resistant.
- NA = α1 + β1, shock; dopamine = D1 → β1 → α1 with dose, cardiogenic shock; both oxidise → metabisulphite, light-resistant, discard pink.
- Naphazoline: imidazoline nasal/eye decongestant; rebound congestion.
- β blockers = aryloxypropanolamine; propranolol non-selective, lipophilic; atenolol β1-selective, hydrophilic, para-acetamide.
- Phenoxybenzamine irreversible α blocker; prazosin quinazoline, first-dose hypotension.
- ACh = choline ester, quaternary, no systemic use; carbachol resists cholinesterase.
- Neostigmine: quaternary carbamate, myasthenia gravis, reversal of NM block. Pralidoxime reactivates cholinesterase in OP poisoning.
- Atropine: tropine + tropic acid ester; pre-anaesthetic, OP poisoning; dicyclomine: bicyclohexyl ester antispasmodic.

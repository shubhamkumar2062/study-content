# Assay of ibuprofen (alkalimetry)

**Aim:** To determine the percentage purity of the given ibuprofen by titration with 0.1 M sodium hydroxide.

> Ibuprofen is insoluble in water, so it is dissolved in ethanol. Follow your IP edition.

## Principle

Ibuprofen is a carboxylic acid (2-(4-isobutylphenyl)propanoic acid). It is titrated directly with standard NaOH using phenolphthalein. The ethanol is neutralised to phenolphthalein first so that only the drug uses alkali.

C12H17–COOH + NaOH → C12H17–COONa + H2O

**Factor:** each ml of 0.1 M NaOH ≡ 0.02063 g of C13H18O2 (MW 206.3).

## Requirements

Ibuprofen, 95% ethanol, phenolphthalein, standardised 0.1 M NaOH, burette, conical flask, balance.

## Procedure

1. Neutralise 100 ml ethanol (95%) by adding phenolphthalein and NaOH dropwise to a faint pink.
2. Weigh accurately about 0.5 g ibuprofen (W) and dissolve in the neutralised ethanol.
3. Titrate with 0.1 M NaOH to a faint pink lasting 30 seconds.
4. Repeat for concordant readings.

## Observations and calculation

| No. | Weight (g) | Volume of 0.1 M NaOH (ml) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.5010 | 24.1 |

% purity = (V × 0.02063 × M ÷ 0.1 × 100) ÷ W

Example: 24.1 × 0.02063 × 100 ÷ 0.5010 = 99.2%.

## Result

The given sample contains ____% of ibuprofen.

## Precautions

1. Neutralise the ethanol before adding the sample.
2. Avoid CO2 absorption; titrate promptly and use CO2-free NaOH.
3. Ensure the sample is fully dissolved.
4. Take the first permanent faint pink as end point.

## Viva questions

1. **Why ethanol and not water?** Ibuprofen is practically insoluble in water.
2. **Why phenolphthalein?** Weak acid–strong base titration; equivalence point above pH 7.
3. **Why neutralise the ethanol?** Ethanol may contain traces of acid that would consume NaOH.
4. **What is the active isomer of ibuprofen?** The S-(+) isomer.
5. **Class of ibuprofen?** Propionic acid NSAID.

# Assay of sodium chloride (modified Volhard's method)

**Aim:** To determine the percentage purity of the given sodium chloride by the modified Volhard's method.

> Quantities below are typical for a teaching lab. Nitrobenzene is toxic; dibutyl phthalate is a safer alternative. Work in a ventilated area.

## Principle

This is a **back (residual) titration** in nitric acid medium. A known excess of standard silver nitrate precipitates all chloride as AgCl. The unreacted silver nitrate is titrated with standard ammonium thiocyanate using **ferric ammonium sulphate** as indicator; the first excess thiocyanate gives a **reddish-brown** ferric thiocyanate colour.

NaCl + AgNO3 (excess) → AgCl↓ + NaNO3
AgNO3 (remaining) + NH4SCN → AgSCN↓ + NH4NO3
Fe³⁺ + SCN⁻ → [Fe(SCN)]²⁺ (red)

AgCl is more soluble than AgSCN, so near the end point it would react with thiocyanate. **Nitrobenzene (or dibutyl phthalate)** is added to coat the AgCl particles and stop this – hence "modified".

**Factor:** each ml of 0.1 M AgNO3 ≡ 0.005844 g of NaCl.

## Requirements

Sodium chloride, 0.1 M silver nitrate, 0.1 M ammonium thiocyanate (both standardised), dilute nitric acid, nitrobenzene or dibutyl phthalate, ferric ammonium sulphate solution, stoppered conical flask, burettes, pipette.

## Procedure

1. Weigh accurately about 0.1 g NaCl (W); dissolve in about 50 ml water in a stoppered flask.
2. Add 5 ml dilute nitric acid and exactly 50.0 ml of 0.1 M AgNO3.
3. Add 2 ml nitrobenzene (or dibutyl phthalate); stopper and shake well to coagulate the precipitate.
4. Add 2 ml ferric ammonium sulphate solution.
5. Titrate the excess AgNO3 with 0.1 M NH4SCN to a permanent reddish-brown colour (V2 ml).
6. Perform a **blank** with 50.0 ml AgNO3 and the same reagents, without sample (V1 ml).

## Calculation

Volume of AgNO3 used by NaCl (as 0.1 M) = V1 − V2 (ml of 0.1 M thiocyanate equivalent)

% purity = ((V1 − V2) × 0.005844 × 100) ÷ W (correct for actual molarity)

Example: V1 = 50.0, V2 = 33.0, W = 0.1000 g → 17.0 × 0.005844 × 100 ÷ 0.1000 = 99.3%.

## Result

The given sample contains ____% of NaCl.

## Precautions

1. Shake vigorously after adding nitrobenzene so AgCl is fully coated.
2. Keep the solution acidic with nitric acid (iron indicator precipitates in neutral solution).
3. Protect from bright light.
4. Measure 50.0 ml AgNO3 with a pipette or burette accurately.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is this called a back titration?** Excess AgNO3 is added and the excess is titrated.
2. **Role of nitrobenzene?** Coats AgCl to prevent its reaction with thiocyanate.
3. **Why can Mohr's method not be done in acid?** Chromate changes to dichromate and silver chromate dissolves.
4. **What is the indicator?** Ferric ammonium sulphate (ferric alum).
5. **Why is a blank done?** To find the exact volume of AgNO3 used by the sample.

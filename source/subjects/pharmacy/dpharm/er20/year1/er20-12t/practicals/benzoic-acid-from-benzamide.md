# Preparation of benzoic acid from benzamide

**Aim:** To prepare benzoic acid from benzamide by alkaline hydrolysis and report the practical yield and melting point.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Wear goggles; hot alkali is corrosive.

## Principle

Amides are hydrolysed by boiling with aqueous alkali. Benzamide gives sodium benzoate and ammonia (detected by smell or moist red litmus). Acidifying with HCl liberates **benzoic acid**, which is sparingly soluble in cold water and precipitates.

C6H5CONH2 + NaOH → C6H5COONa + NH3↑
C6H5COONa + HCl → C6H5COOH↓ + NaCl

## Requirements

Benzamide 2 g, 10% w/v NaOH 30 ml, concentrated HCl, round-bottom flask with reflux condenser, heating mantle or water bath, beaker, Buchner funnel, filter paper, litmus paper, ice.

## Procedure

1. Place 2 g benzamide and 30 ml 10% NaOH in a round-bottom flask with a few porcelain pieces.
2. Reflux for 30–40 minutes until no more ammonia is evolved (moist red litmus held at the condenser top no longer turns blue).
3. Cool the flask; transfer the solution to a beaker kept in ice.
4. Add concentrated HCl dropwise with stirring until the solution is acidic to Congo red/litmus; white benzoic acid separates.
5. Filter on a Buchner funnel, wash with a little cold water.
6. Recrystallise from hot water; dry and weigh. Determine the melting point.

## Calculation of yield

C6H5CONH2 (MW 121.1) → C6H5COOH (MW 122.1)

Theoretical yield from 2 g = 2 × 122.1 ÷ 121.1 = 2.02 g

% yield = (practical yield ÷ theoretical yield) × 100

Example: practical yield 1.60 g → 1.60 ÷ 2.02 × 100 = 79%.

## Precautions

1. Reflux until ammonia evolution stops (complete hydrolysis).
2. Acidify in cold, slowly, with stirring.
3. Use minimum hot water for recrystallisation.
4. Dry the product completely before weighing.

## Viva questions

1. **What type of reaction is this?** Alkaline hydrolysis of an amide.
2. **Which gas is evolved?** Ammonia.
3. **Why acidify?** To convert soluble sodium benzoate into insoluble benzoic acid.
4. **Why recrystallise from water?** Benzoic acid is much more soluble in hot than cold water.
5. **Uses of benzoic acid?** Preservative (as sodium benzoate), antifungal in Whitfield's ointment.

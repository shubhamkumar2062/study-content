# Melting point and boiling point of organic compounds

**Aim:** (a) To determine the melting point of the given organic solid (e.g. benzoic acid, acetanilide) by the capillary method. (b) To determine the boiling point of the given organic liquid by the capillary (Siwoloboff) method.

## Principle

**Melting point** is the temperature at which a solid changes into liquid at atmospheric pressure. A pure compound melts sharply over 0.5–1 °C; impurities **lower** the melting point and **widen** the range. It is used to identify a compound and check its purity. A **mixed melting point** with an authentic sample confirms identity (no depression if the two are the same).

**Boiling point** is the temperature at which the vapour pressure of a liquid equals the atmospheric pressure. Pure liquids boil at a constant temperature.

## Requirements

Thiele tube or melting point apparatus, liquid paraffin (heating bath), thermometer (0–250 °C), capillary tubes (one end sealed), small fusion tube, rubber band or thread, burner, porous tile, mortar and pestle.

## Procedure

**(a) Melting point**

1. Powder the dry sample finely; fill a capillary sealed at one end to a height of 2–3 mm by tapping.
2. Tie the capillary to the thermometer so the sample is beside the mercury bulb.
3. Place in the Thiele tube filled with liquid paraffin; heat the side arm gently.
4. Heat quickly to about 10 °C below the expected melting point, then slowly (1–2 °C per minute).
5. Record the temperature when the solid starts to melt (t1) and when it is completely liquid (t2).

**(b) Boiling point**

1. Put a few drops of liquid in a small fusion tube; place inside it a capillary sealed at the upper end, open end down.
2. Tie to the thermometer and heat in the bath.
3. Bubbles escape slowly, then a rapid continuous stream appears; stop heating.
4. Note the temperature at which bubbling stops and the liquid just enters the capillary – this is the boiling point.

## Precautions

1. Sample must be dry and finely powdered.
2. Heat slowly near the melting point.
3. Thermometer bulb and sample must be at the same level.
4. Stir or use a Thiele tube for uniform heating; do not overheat paraffin.

## Viva questions

1. **Effect of impurity on melting point?** Lowers it and broadens the range.
2. **What is mixed melting point?** Melting point of a mixture with an authentic sample; no depression means same compound.
3. **Why liquid paraffin as bath?** High boiling point, non-volatile, safe to about 250 °C.
4. **Effect of pressure on boiling point?** Higher pressure raises it; lower pressure lowers it.
5. **Why is the Thiele tube shaped so?** Convection currents in the side arm give uniform heating.

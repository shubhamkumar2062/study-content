# Limit test for heavy metals

**Aim:** To perform the limit test for heavy metals on the given sample as per the Indian Pharmacopoeia (Method A).

> Method A is for substances that give a clear, colourless solution in water. Other methods in the IP destroy organic matter first. Follow the monograph and your teacher.

## Principle

Heavy metals (lead, copper, mercury, bismuth, antimony, tin, cadmium, silver) react with **hydrogen sulphide** (or sodium sulphide / thioacetamide reagent in newer editions) in a weakly acidic medium (**pH 3–4**) to form **brown to black colloidal metal sulphides**. The colour is compared with a standard lead solution; the limit is expressed in **ppm of lead**.

Pb²⁺ + H2S → PbS (brown–black) + 2H⁺

At pH 3–4 the heavy metal sulphides give a uniform colour, while iron and similar metals do not precipitate.

## Requirements

Nessler cylinders, dilute acetic acid, dilute ammonia, acetate buffer pH 3.5, freshly prepared hydrogen sulphide solution (or sulphide reagent), standard lead solution (from lead nitrate), distilled water, pH paper.

## Procedure

| Step | Test | Standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Solution of specified quantity of sample in about 25 ml water | Specified volume of standard lead solution diluted to about 25 ml |
| 2 | Adjust to pH 3–4 with dilute acetic acid or dilute ammonia; add acetate buffer | Same |
| 3 | Dilute to 35 ml with water | Same |
| 4 | Add 10 ml freshly prepared H2S solution | Same |
| 5 | Dilute to 50 ml, stand 5 minutes | Same |

6. View down the cylinders against a white background and compare the brown colour.

## Observation

Colour in test is lighter / darker than standard.

## Result

The given sample **passes / fails** the limit test for heavy metals.

## Precautions

1. Hydrogen sulphide is toxic with a rotten-egg smell: work in a fume hood.
2. Check pH with paper; wrong pH gives wrong colours.
3. Use freshly prepared sulphide reagent.
4. Treat test and standard together and identically.

## Viva questions

1. **Name some heavy metals.** Lead, copper, mercury, bismuth, cadmium, antimony, tin.
2. **Why is pH kept at 3–4?** To get uniform colloidal sulphides and avoid interference by iron.
3. **How is the limit expressed?** In parts per million of lead.
4. **Why are heavy metals dangerous?** They are toxic and accumulate in the body.
5. **What replaces H2S in newer methods?** Sodium sulphide or thioacetamide reagent.

# Limit test for chlorides

**Aim:** To perform the limit test for chlorides on the given sample (for example sodium bicarbonate, potassium chloride-free salts or any IP substance) as per the Indian Pharmacopoeia.

> Quantities of sample and standard are given in each IP monograph. Follow your lab manual and teacher where they differ. Wear a lab coat and goggles.

## Principle

Chloride ions react with silver nitrate in the presence of dilute nitric acid to give silver chloride. In the small amounts allowed, AgCl appears as a faint **opalescence**, not a precipitate. The opalescence of the sample is compared with that produced by a known amount of chloride (standard) under identical conditions.

Cl⁻ + AgNO3 → AgCl (white opalescence) + NO3⁻

Dilute nitric acid keeps the medium acidic so that carbonate, phosphate or hydroxide do not precipitate with silver, and it gives a uniform opalescence.

## Requirements

Two matched Nessler cylinders (50 ml), dilute nitric acid, 0.1 M silver nitrate, standard chloride solution (dilute NaCl, e.g. 0.05845% w/v), chloride-free distilled water, glass rods, black tile or paper.

## Procedure

| Step | Test cylinder | Standard cylinder |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Dissolve the specified weight of sample in water | Take the specified volume of standard NaCl solution |
| 2 | Add 10 ml dilute nitric acid | Add 10 ml dilute nitric acid |
| 3 | Dilute to 50 ml with water | Dilute to 50 ml with water |
| 4 | Add 1 ml 0.1 M silver nitrate, stir with a glass rod | Same |
| 5 | Keep 5 minutes protected from direct sunlight | Same |

6. View both cylinders from above (down the length) against a black background and compare the opalescence.

## Observation

| Cylinder | Opalescence |
| --- | --- |
| Test | Less / more than standard |
| Standard | Reference |

## Result

The given sample **passes / fails** the limit test for chlorides (passes if its opalescence is not more than the standard).

## Precautions

1. Use chloride-free water and clean cylinders rinsed with distilled water.
2. Add reagents to both cylinders in the same order and at the same time.
3. Protect from direct light (silver chloride darkens).
4. Stir both cylinders equally; compare against a black background.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is nitric acid added?** To prevent precipitation of other silver salts and give uniform opalescence.
2. **Why not hydrochloric acid?** It contains chloride and would give a false positive.
3. **Why compare against a black background?** White opalescence is seen best on black.
4. **What is a Nessler cylinder?** A flat-bottomed glass cylinder marked at 50 ml, used for comparing colour or turbidity.
5. **Is a limit test quantitative?** It is semi-quantitative: it only shows whether the impurity is below the permitted limit.

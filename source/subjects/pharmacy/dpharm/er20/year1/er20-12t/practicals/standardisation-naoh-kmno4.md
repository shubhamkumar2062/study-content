# Preparation and standardisation of 0.1 N NaOH and 0.1 N KMnO4

**Aim:** (a) To prepare and standardise 0.1 N sodium hydroxide using oxalic acid. (b) To prepare and standardise 0.1 N potassium permanganate using oxalic acid.

> NaOH and KMnO4 are secondary standards: they cannot be weighed exactly, so their exact strength is found against a primary standard. Wear goggles; NaOH is caustic.

## Principle

**(a) NaOH vs oxalic acid (acid–base):**
H2C2O4 + 2NaOH → Na2C2O4 + 2H2O. Indicator: phenolphthalein (colourless → pale pink), because oxalic acid is a weak acid titrated with a strong base.

**(b) KMnO4 vs oxalic acid (redox):**
2MnO4⁻ + 5C2O4²⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO2 + 8H2O. Done hot (60–70 °C) in dilute H2SO4. KMnO4 is its own indicator (first permanent faint pink).

## Requirements

NaOH pellets, KMnO4 crystals, oxalic acid dihydrate (AR), dilute H2SO4, phenolphthalein, CO2-free distilled water, volumetric flasks (1 L, 100 ml), burette, pipette, conical flasks, water bath, thermometer, glass wool.

## Preparation of solutions

1. **0.1 N oxalic acid (primary standard):** equivalent weight 63.03; weigh exactly about 0.63 g, dissolve and make up to 100 ml in a volumetric flask. Calculate exact normality from the weight.
2. **0.1 N NaOH:** dissolve about 4.2 g NaOH in CO2-free water and dilute to 1 L.
3. **0.1 N KMnO4:** equivalent weight 31.6 (MW 158.03 ÷ 5); dissolve about 3.2 g in 1 L water, boil 15 minutes, keep 2 days in the dark, filter through glass wool (not paper) and store in an amber bottle.

## Procedure

**(a) NaOH:** Pipette 10 ml oxalic acid into a conical flask, add 2 drops phenolphthalein, titrate with NaOH from the burette to a faint permanent pink. Repeat for three concordant readings.

**(b) KMnO4:** Pipette 10 ml oxalic acid, add about 10 ml dilute H2SO4, warm to 60–70 °C, titrate with KMnO4 (burette, read upper meniscus) until a faint pink lasts 30 seconds. Repeat.

## Observations and calculation

| Titration | Initial (ml) | Final (ml) | Volume used (ml) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.0 | 10.2 | 10.2 |
| 2 | 10.2 | 20.3 | 10.1 |
| 3 | 20.3 | 30.4 | 10.1 |

N1V1 = N2V2 → N(NaOH or KMnO4) = (N oxalic acid × 10) ÷ concordant volume.

Example: 0.1002 × 10 ÷ 10.1 = 0.0992 N.

## Precautions

1. Use CO2-free water for NaOH; keep the bottle closed (absorbs CO2).
2. Do not filter KMnO4 through paper (reduces it to MnO2).
3. Heat oxalic acid to 60–70 °C, not boiling.
4. Read the upper meniscus for coloured KMnO4.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is NaOH not a primary standard?** It is hygroscopic and absorbs CO2.
2. **Why is the KMnO4 titration done hot?** The reaction is slow at room temperature; heat (and the Mn²⁺ formed, which catalyses it) speeds it up.
3. **Why H2SO4 and not HCl?** Permanganate oxidises chloride.
4. **What is the equivalent weight of KMnO4 in acid?** 31.6.
5. **Why is the KMnO4 solution boiled and kept?** To oxidise organic matter in the water and settle MnO2, which would otherwise decompose the solution.

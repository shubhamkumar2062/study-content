# Identification tests for anions and cations (IP)

**Aim:** To identify the anion and cation present in the given inorganic salt using the identification tests of the Indian Pharmacopoeia.

> Use small amounts (a few mg to 0.1 g) and test tubes. Follow your teacher where tests differ.

## Principle

Each ion gives a characteristic reaction (precipitate, colour, gas or flame colour) with specific reagents. The IP lists these "tests for identification" in its appendix and refers to them in monographs.

## Requirements

Test tubes, burner, platinum or nichrome wire, dilute and concentrated acids, ammonia, silver nitrate, barium chloride, ammonium oxalate, ammonium molybdate, potassium ferrocyanide, potassium ferricyanide, ammonium thiocyanate, ferrous sulphate, disodium hydrogen phosphate, sodium cobaltinitrite, lime water, magnesium sulphate.

## Tests for anions

| Anion | Test | Observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chloride | Solution + dilute HNO3 + AgNO3 | White curdy ppt, soluble in dilute ammonia |
| Bromide | Same | Pale yellow ppt, sparingly soluble in ammonia |
| Iodide | Same | Yellow ppt, insoluble in ammonia |
| Sulphate | Solution + dilute HCl + BaCl2 | White ppt insoluble in HCl |
| Carbonate | Solid + dilute HCl | Effervescence; gas turns lime water milky |
| Bicarbonate | Solution + MgSO4 | No ppt in cold; white ppt on boiling |
| Phosphate | Solution + HNO3 + ammonium molybdate, warm | Yellow crystalline ppt |
| Nitrate | Solution + FeSO4 + conc. H2SO4 poured along the side | Brown ring at the junction |
| Acetate | Solid + H2SO4 + ethanol, warm | Fruity smell of ethyl acetate |

## Tests for cations

| Cation | Test | Observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sodium | Flame test | Persistent golden yellow flame |
| Potassium | Flame test; solution + sodium cobaltinitrite (acetic acid) | Lilac flame (through blue glass); yellow ppt |
| Calcium | Flame test; solution + ammonium oxalate | Brick-red flame; white ppt insoluble in acetic acid, soluble in HCl |
| Magnesium | Solution + NH4Cl + NH3 + disodium hydrogen phosphate | White crystalline ppt |
| Ferrous iron | Potassium ferricyanide | Dark blue ppt |
| Ferric iron | Potassium ferrocyanide; ammonium thiocyanate | Prussian blue ppt; blood-red colour |
| Ammonium | Warm with NaOH | Smell of ammonia; moist red litmus turns blue |
| Zinc | NaOH dropwise | White ppt soluble in excess |
| Aluminium | NaOH dropwise | White gelatinous ppt soluble in excess |

## Result

## Precautions

1. Clean the flame-test wire by dipping in conc. HCl and heating until no colour.
2. Add reagents dropwise; note colours immediately.
3. Use a fume hood for tests that release gases.
4. Do not taste or smell chemicals directly; waft gently.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is the silver chloride ppt dissolved in ammonia?** AgCl forms the soluble diammine silver complex, distinguishing chloride from iodide.
2. **What is the brown ring?** The nitroso-ferrous sulphate complex formed in the nitrate test.
3. **Why view potassium flame through blue glass?** It absorbs the yellow of any sodium present.
4. **Prussian blue is formed by?** Ferric ion with potassium ferrocyanide.
5. **Which cation gives a brick-red flame?** Calcium.

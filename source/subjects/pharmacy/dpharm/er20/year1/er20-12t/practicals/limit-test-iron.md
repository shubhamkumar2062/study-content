# Limit test for iron

**Aim:** To perform the limit test for iron on the given sample as per the Indian Pharmacopoeia.

> Iron-free reagents must be used. Follow the monograph quantities and your teacher's instructions.

## Principle

Iron (ferric or ferrous) is reduced to the ferrous state by **thioglycollic acid**. In a solution made alkaline with ammonia and containing citric acid, ferrous iron forms **ferrous thioglycollate**, which is pale pink to deep reddish-purple. The colour is compared with that of a standard prepared from a known quantity of iron.

Fe³⁺ → (thioglycollic acid) → Fe²⁺ → ferrous thioglycollate (purple, in alkaline medium)

| Reagent | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Citric acid (20% w/v, iron-free) | Complexes iron; prevents precipitation as Fe(OH)3 in alkali |
| Thioglycollic acid | Reducing agent and colour-forming reagent |
| Iron-free ammonia | Makes the medium alkaline; colour appears only in alkali |

## Requirements

Nessler cylinders, 20% w/v iron-free citric acid, thioglycollic acid, iron-free ammonia solution, standard iron solution (from ferric ammonium sulphate, 20 µg Fe per ml), distilled water.

## Procedure

| Step | Test | Standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Dissolve specified quantity of sample in 40 ml water | 2 ml standard iron solution diluted with 40 ml water |
| 2 | Add 2 ml citric acid solution | Same |
| 3 | Add 0.1 ml thioglycollic acid, mix | Same |
| 4 | Make alkaline with iron-free ammonia; dilute to 50 ml | Same |
| 5 | Allow to stand 5 minutes | Same |

6. Compare the colours by viewing down the cylinders against a white background.

## Observation

Purple colour in test is lighter / deeper than standard.

## Result

The given sample **passes / fails** the limit test for iron.

## Precautions

1. Use iron-free reagents and glassware (no rusty stands or spatulas).
2. Thioglycollic acid has an unpleasant smell; use in a fume hood.
3. Make both solutions alkaline to the same extent.
4. Compare against a white background.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is citric acid added?** To prevent precipitation of iron as hydroxide when ammonia is added.
2. **Why thioglycollic acid?** It reduces Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺ and forms the coloured complex.
3. **Why must the solution be alkaline?** Ferrous thioglycollate colour develops only in alkaline medium.
4. **What is the colour of the complex?** Pale pink to reddish-purple.
5. **Source of iron impurity?** Iron vessels and equipment, raw materials.

# Limit test for sulphates

**Aim:** To perform the limit test for sulphates on the given sample as per the Indian Pharmacopoeia.

> Use the sample weight and standard volume given in the monograph. Follow your lab manual and teacher where they differ.

## Principle

Sulphate ions react with barium chloride in the presence of dilute hydrochloric acid to give a **turbidity of barium sulphate**. The turbidity of the sample is compared with that of a standard containing a known amount of sulphate.

SO4²⁻ + BaCl2 → BaSO4 (white turbidity) + 2Cl⁻

- **Dilute HCl** prevents precipitation of barium carbonate, phosphate or oxalate.
- **Barium sulphate reagent** contains barium chloride, sulphate-free alcohol and a small amount of potassium sulphate. Alcohol prevents supersaturation; potassium sulphate provides seed crystals so turbidity forms quickly and evenly.

## Requirements

Nessler cylinders, dilute hydrochloric acid, barium sulphate reagent, standard sulphate solution (potassium sulphate, e.g. 0.1089% w/v), distilled water, glass rods.

## Procedure

| Step | Test | Standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Dissolve specified quantity of sample in water in a Nessler cylinder | Specified volume of standard potassium sulphate solution |
| 2 | Add 2 ml dilute HCl | Add 2 ml dilute HCl |
| 3 | Dilute to 45 ml with water | Dilute to 45 ml with water |
| 4 | Add 5 ml barium sulphate reagent, stir | Same |
| 5 | Keep for 5 minutes | Same |

6. Compare the turbidity by viewing down the cylinders against a black background.

## Observation

Turbidity in test cylinder is less / more than the standard.

## Result

The given sample **passes / fails** the limit test for sulphates.

## Precautions

1. Use sulphate-free water and clean apparatus.
2. Use freshly prepared barium sulphate reagent.
3. Treat test and standard identically and simultaneously.
4. Stir both equally; compare after the same time.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is alcohol present in barium sulphate reagent?** To prevent supersaturation and give uniform turbidity.
2. **Role of potassium sulphate in the reagent?** Seeding for rapid, reproducible turbidity.
3. **Why dilute HCl?** To stop other barium salts precipitating.
4. **What is the difference between opalescence and turbidity?** Opalescence is a faint milky appearance (chloride test); turbidity is a heavier cloudiness (sulphate test).
5. **Name a source of sulphate impurity.** Sulphuric acid used in manufacture.

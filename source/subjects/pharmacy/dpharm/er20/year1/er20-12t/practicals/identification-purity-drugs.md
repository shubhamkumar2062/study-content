# Identification and test for purity: aspirin, caffeine, paracetamol, sulfanilamide

**Aim:** To identify the given pharmaceutical substances and perform simple tests for their purity.

> Use small quantities. The pharmacopoeia also uses melting point, IR and TLC; the chemical tests below are the classic ones done in D.Pharm labs.

## Principle

Each drug has a functional group that gives a characteristic reaction (ester, phenol, purine, primary aromatic amine). Purity tests check for known impurities (e.g. free salicylic acid in aspirin, 4-aminophenol in paracetamol) and physical constants such as melting point.

## Requirements

Samples, ferric chloride solution, NaOH, dilute H2SO4, HCl, potassium dichromate solution, potassium chlorate (or hydrogen peroxide), ammonia, sodium nitrite, β-naphthol in NaOH, ethanol, test tubes, water bath, melting point apparatus.

## Tests

### Aspirin

| Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Boil 0.5 g with 10 ml NaOH solution, cool, add excess dilute H2SO4 | White crystalline ppt (salicylic acid), smell of acetic acid | Acetyl ester present |
| Add ferric chloride to the hydrolysed solution | Violet colour | Phenolic –OH of salicylic acid formed |
| Purity: free salicylic acid – dissolve sample in ethanol, add dilute ferric ammonium sulphate | Violet colour not deeper than standard | Limit of free salicylic acid passes |

Unhydrolysed pure aspirin gives no immediate violet colour with ferric chloride.

### Caffeine

| Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Murexide test: evaporate a little caffeine with HCl and a crystal of potassium chlorate (or H2O2) to dryness; expose residue to ammonia vapour | Reddish residue turns purple | Purine (xanthine) nucleus |
| Saturated solution + tannic acid | White ppt soluble in excess reagent | Caffeine (alkaloid) |
| Purity: melting point; loss on drying; sulphated ash | Within limits | Pure |

### Paracetamol

| Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solution + ferric chloride | Blue-violet colour | Phenolic –OH |
| Boil 0.1 g with 1 ml HCl for 3 minutes, add 10 ml water, cool, add a drop of 0.0167 M potassium dichromate | Violet colour slowly develops, not turning red | p-Aminophenol from hydrolysis (indophenol reaction) |
| Purity: limit test for 4-aminophenol; melting point (about 169 °C) | Within limits | Pure |

### Sulfanilamide

| Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dissolve in dilute HCl, cool in ice, add sodium nitrite solution, then pour into alkaline β-naphthol | Orange-red precipitate (azo dye) | Primary aromatic amine |
| Soluble in dilute NaOH and in dilute HCl | Dissolves in both | Amphoteric (acidic sulphonamide + basic amine) |
| Purity: melting point (about 165 °C) | Sharp | Pure |

## Result

The given samples were identified as ____ and found to comply / not comply with the purity tests performed.

## Precautions

1. Use clean test tubes for each test.
2. Diazotisation must be done at 0–5 °C.
3. Evaporate gently in the murexide test; do not char.
4. Compare purity tests with standards prepared the same way.

## Viva questions

1. **Why does aspirin give violet colour only after hydrolysis?** The free phenolic –OH of salicylic acid is needed; in aspirin it is acetylated.
2. **What is the murexide test used for?** Purines such as caffeine and uric acid.
3. **Which impurity is tested in paracetamol?** 4-Aminophenol.
4. **What reaction identifies sulfanilamide?** Diazotisation and coupling with β-naphthol.
5. **Why is ice used in diazotisation?** Diazonium salts decompose above about 5 °C.

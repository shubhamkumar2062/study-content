# Preparation of picric acid from phenol

**Aim:** To prepare picric acid (2,4,6-trinitrophenol) from phenol and report the yield and melting point.

> **Safety first.** Dry picric acid is explosive and forms shock-sensitive metal picrates. Keep the product moist, never grind it dry, avoid metal spatulas, and prepare only small amounts. Phenol burns skin. Concentrated acids react violently: use a fume hood, goggles and gloves. Follow your teacher's quantities.

## Principle

Direct nitration of phenol with concentrated nitric acid oxidises it to tar. So phenol is first **sulphonated** with concentrated sulphuric acid to phenol-2,4-disulphonic acid. Then concentrated nitric acid replaces the sulphonic acid groups with nitro groups and nitrates the remaining ortho position, giving picric acid.

C6H5OH + 2H2SO4 → C6H3(OH)(SO3H)2 + 2H2O
C6H3(OH)(SO3H)2 + 3HNO3 → C6H2(OH)(NO2)3 + 2H2SO4 + H2O

## Requirements

Phenol 2 g, concentrated H2SO4 (about 4 ml), concentrated HNO3 (about 7 ml), round-bottom or conical flask, water bath, dropping funnel, beaker of ice water, Buchner funnel.

## Procedure

1. Place 2 g phenol in a dry flask, add 4 ml concentrated H2SO4 and heat on a boiling water bath for about 30 minutes (sulphonation).
2. Cool the flask in ice water.
3. In a fume hood, add 7 ml concentrated HNO3 **dropwise** with shaking; a vigorous reaction with brown fumes occurs.
4. When the reaction slows, heat on the water bath for 1–1.5 hours with occasional shaking.
5. Cool, add about 20 ml cold water; yellow crystals separate.
6. Filter, wash with cold water, and recrystallise from hot dilute acid/water if directed. Keep the product moist.
7. Take a small, dry portion only for melting point.

## Calculation

C6H5OH (MW 94.1) → C6H3N3O7 (MW 229.1)

Theoretical yield from 2 g = 2 × 229.1 ÷ 94.1 = 4.87 g

% yield = practical ÷ theoretical × 100

## Precautions

1. Never let large amounts of picric acid dry out; store under water.
2. Add nitric acid slowly with cooling.
3. Do all steps in a fume hood (nitrogen oxides are toxic).
4. Avoid contact with metals.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is phenol sulphonated before nitration?** Direct nitration oxidises phenol to tar; sulphonic groups protect the ring and are later replaced by nitro groups.
2. **Why is picric acid strongly acidic?** Three electron-withdrawing nitro groups stabilise the phenoxide ion.
3. **Why is picric acid dangerous?** Dry picric acid and its metal salts are explosive.
4. **Uses of picric acid?** Earlier as an antiseptic for burns; as a reagent (precipitates alkaloids, Jaffe reaction for creatinine).
5. **Colour of picric acid?** Yellow.

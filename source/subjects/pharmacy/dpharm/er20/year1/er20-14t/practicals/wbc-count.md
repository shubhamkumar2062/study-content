# Determination of total WBC count

**Aim:** To determine the total leucocyte (WBC) count of the given blood sample using a haemocytometer.

## Principle

Blood is diluted 20 times with **Turk's fluid**, which lyses the RBCs (glacial acetic acid) and stains WBC nuclei (gentian violet). The WBCs are counted in a known volume of the **Neubauer counting chamber**, and the count per mm³ (per µL) of blood is calculated.

## Requirements

Haemocytometer (improved Neubauer chamber, WBC pipette with white bead marked 0.5, 1 and 11, cover slip), Turk's fluid (about 2 % glacial acetic acid tinted with gentian violet), microscope, lancet, spirit, cotton.

## The Neubauer chamber

```text
   Ruled area 3 mm × 3 mm = 9 large squares (each 1 mm²)

   ┌─────┬─────┬─────┐
   │ W   │     │  W  │   W = four corner squares used for WBC
   │     │     │     │       (each divided into 16 smaller squares)
   ├─────┼─────┼─────┤
   │     │  R  │     │   R = central square (25 groups) for RBC
   ├─────┼─────┼─────┤
   │ W   │     │  W  │   Depth (cover slip to floor) = 0.1 mm
   └─────┴─────┴─────┘
```

## Procedure

1. Clean the chamber and cover slip; place the cover slip over the ruled area.
2. Prick the finger, discard the first drop; draw blood to the **0.5 mark** of the WBC pipette. Wipe the tip.
3. Draw Turk's fluid up to the **11 mark** (dilution 1 in 20). Mix by rolling the pipette for 2–3 minutes.
4. Discard the first few drops (fluid in the stem has no cells).
5. Touch the tip to the edge of the cover slip and let the chamber fill by capillary action, without overflow or bubbles.
6. Wait 2–3 minutes for cells to settle. Count WBCs under low power in the **four corner large squares**. Count cells touching the top and left lines, not those on the bottom and right lines.

## Observations and calculation

| Square | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | Total (N) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| WBCs | ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ |

- Area counted = 4 × 1 mm² = 4 mm²; depth = 0.1 mm; volume = 0.4 mm³.
- Dilution = 20.

**Total WBC/mm³ = (N × dilution) ÷ volume = (N × 20) ÷ 0.4 = N × 50**

**Worked example:** N = 150 → 150 × 50 = **7,500 cells/mm³** (normal).

**Normal:** 4,000–11,000/mm³. More = leucocytosis (bacterial infection, leukaemia); less = leucopenia (typhoid, some viral infections, drug toxicity).

## Precautions

1. Fill the pipette exactly to the marks; no air bubbles.
2. Mix well and discard the first drops.
3. Do not overfill the chamber.
4. Follow the "count top and left, not bottom and right" rule to avoid counting a cell twice.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the composition of Turk's fluid?** Glacial acetic acid (lyses RBCs) and gentian violet (stains nuclei) in water.
2. **What is the dilution in WBC counting?** 1 in 20.
3. **Why discard the first drops from the pipette?** The stem holds diluting fluid without cells.
4. **What is the multiplication factor?** 50.
5. **Why is the bead in the pipette bulb?** It helps mixing and identifies the pipette.

# Study of the human skeleton (axial and appendicular)

**Aim:** To study the bones of the axial and appendicular skeleton using an articulated skeleton and loose bones.

## Principle

The adult skeleton has 206 bones: the **axial skeleton** (80 bones – skull, ear ossicles, hyoid, vertebral column, thoracic cage) forms the central axis, and the **appendicular skeleton** (126 bones – girdles and limbs) is attached to it.

## Requirements

Articulated human skeleton (model), loose bones (skull, vertebrae, ribs, sternum, humerus, femur, hip bone etc.), charts.

## Procedure

1. Identify each bone on the articulated skeleton and note its position.
2. Pick up the loose bone, hold it in the anatomical position and identify the side (right or left) from its features.
3. Note important features, joints formed and muscles or structures related.
4. Draw neat labelled sketches.

## Observations

**Axial skeleton (80)**

| Part | Bones | Number | Key features |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Skull – cranium | Frontal, parietal (2), temporal (2), occipital, sphenoid, ethmoid | 8 | Sutures; foramen magnum in occipital |
| Skull – face | Nasal, maxilla, zygomatic, lacrimal, palatine, inferior conchae (2 each), vomer, mandible | 14 | Mandible the only movable skull bone |
| Ear ossicles | Malleus, incus, stapes (each side) | 6 | Stapes smallest bone |
| Hyoid | U-shaped | 1 | No joint with other bones |
| Vertebral column | Cervical 7, thoracic 12, lumbar 5, sacrum, coccyx | 26 | Atlas, axis; bodies largest in lumbar region |
| Thoracic cage | Sternum 1, ribs 24 | 25 | True 7, false 3, floating 2 pairs |

**Appendicular skeleton (126)**

| Part | Bones (each side) |
| --- | --- |
| Pectoral girdle | Clavicle, scapula |
| Upper limb | Humerus, radius, ulna, 8 carpals, 5 metacarpals, 14 phalanges |
| Pelvic girdle | Hip bone (ilium + ischium + pubis; acetabulum) |
| Lower limb | Femur, patella, tibia, fibula, 7 tarsals, 5 metatarsals, 14 phalanges |

**Identifying features (examples):** humerus – round head fits glenoid cavity of scapula; femur – head fits acetabulum, longest bone; tibia – sharp anterior border (shin), medial malleolus; typical thoracic vertebra – facets for ribs, long downward spine.

## Result

The bones of the axial and appendicular skeleton were identified and their features studied.

## Precautions

1. Handle bones and models gently.
2. Hold a bone in the anatomical position before deciding its side.

## Viva questions

1. **How many bones in the axial and appendicular skeleton?** 80 and 126.
2. **Which is the only movable bone of the skull?** Mandible.
3. **Which bones form the hip bone?** Ilium, ischium and pubis.
4. **Name the first two cervical vertebrae.** Atlas (C1) and axis (C2).
5. **Which is the largest sesamoid bone?** Patella.

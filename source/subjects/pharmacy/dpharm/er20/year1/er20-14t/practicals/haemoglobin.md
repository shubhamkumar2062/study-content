# Determination of haemoglobin content (Sahli's method)

**Aim:** To estimate the haemoglobin content of the given blood sample by Sahli's acid haematin method.

## Principle

Haemoglobin is converted to brown **acid haematin** by 0.1 N HCl. The brown solution is diluted with distilled water until its colour matches the standard brown glass rods of the comparator. The Hb value (g/dL) is read directly from the graduated tube.

Hb + 0.1 N HCl → acid haematin (brown)

## Requirements

Sahli's haemoglobinometer (comparator with standard brown glass, graduated tube, Hb pipette marked 20 µL (20 cu mm), stirrer, dropper), 0.1 N HCl, distilled water, lancet, spirit, cotton.

## Procedure

1. Fill the graduated tube with 0.1 N HCl up to the lowest mark (mark 2 g% or the "20" mark as given on your tube).
2. Prick the finger; discard the first drop.
3. Suck blood exactly to the 20 µL mark of the Hb pipette; wipe the outside of the tip.
4. Blow the blood into the bottom of the acid; rinse the pipette with the acid by sucking up and blowing out 2–3 times; mix with the stirrer.
5. Allow to stand for **10 minutes** for full conversion to acid haematin.
6. Add distilled water drop by drop, stirring after each addition, and compare with the standard against daylight.
7. Stop when the colour exactly matches. Read the lower level of the meniscus in g/dL (g%).

**Normal haemoglobin (approx.):** men 13–17 g/dL; women 12–15 g/dL; newborn higher (about 14–20 g/dL).

Low Hb = **anaemia** (iron deficiency, blood loss, B12/folate deficiency); high Hb = polycythaemia, dehydration, high altitude.

## Precautions

1. Fill the pipette exactly to the mark without air bubbles; wipe the outside.
2. Wait the full 10 minutes before diluting.
3. Add water slowly – once over-diluted, the test must be repeated.
4. Match colour in daylight, with the tube in the comparator.

## Viva questions

1. **What is formed when blood is mixed with 0.1 N HCl?** Brown acid haematin.
2. **Why wait 10 minutes?** For complete conversion of Hb to acid haematin.
3. **What is the main error of Sahli's method?** Colour matching by eye is subjective, and some Hb forms (e.g. carboxyhaemoglobin) are not converted.
4. **Name a more accurate method.** Cyanmethaemoglobin (Drabkin) method.
5. **Normal Hb in adult males?** About 13–17 g/dL.

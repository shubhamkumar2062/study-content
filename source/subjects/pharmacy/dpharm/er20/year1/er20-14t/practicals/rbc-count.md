# Determination of total RBC count

**Aim:** To determine the total red blood cell count of the given blood sample using a haemocytometer.

## Principle

Blood is diluted 200 times with **Hayem's fluid** (isotonic; preserves RBCs and keeps them separate). RBCs are counted in a known small volume of the Neubauer chamber and the number per mm³ of blood is calculated.

## Requirements

Haemocytometer (improved Neubauer chamber, RBC pipette with red bead marked 0.5, 1 and 101, cover slip), Hayem's fluid (sodium chloride, sodium sulphate and mercuric chloride in water) or another isotonic RBC diluent, microscope, lancet, spirit, cotton.

## Procedure

1. Place the clean cover slip on the chamber.
2. Prick the finger, discard the first drop; draw blood exactly to the **0.5 mark** of the RBC pipette. Wipe the tip.
3. Draw Hayem's fluid up to the **101 mark** (dilution 1 in 200). Mix by rolling for 2–3 minutes.
4. Discard the first few drops; charge the chamber by capillary action.
5. Wait 2–3 minutes. Under high power (40×), count RBCs in **5 medium squares** of the central large square – the four corners and the centre one (each has 16 smallest squares, 80 smallest squares in all).

```text
   Central 1 mm² square = 25 medium squares (each 0.2 mm × 0.2 mm)
   ┌───┬───┬───┬───┬───┐
   │ 1 │   │   │   │ 2 │   Count squares 1–5
   ├───┼───┼───┼───┼───┤   (each = 16 tiny squares)
   │   │   │   │   │   │
   ├───┼───┼───┼───┼───┤
   │   │   │ 5 │   │   │
   ├───┼───┼───┼───┼───┤
   │   │   │   │   │   │
   ├───┼───┼───┼───┼───┤
   │ 3 │   │   │   │ 4 │
   └───┴───┴───┴───┴───┘
```

## Observations and calculation

| Square | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | Total (N) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RBCs | ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ |

- Area of 5 medium squares = 5 × (1/25) mm² = 1/5 mm²; depth 0.1 mm; volume = 1/50 mm³ (0.02 mm³).
- Dilution = 200.

**RBC/mm³ = (N × 200) ÷ 0.02 = N × 10,000**

**Worked example:** N = 480 → 480 × 10,000 = **4.8 million/mm³** (normal).

**Normal:** men about 4.5–5.5 million/mm³; women about 4.0–5.0 million/mm³. Low = anaemia; high = polycythaemia (high altitude, dehydration).

## Precautions

1. Fill exactly to the marks; mix well.
2. Count under high power; distribute cells evenly (no clumps).
3. Use the "top and left lines counted" rule.
4. Clean the pipette with water, alcohol and ether after use.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the dilution in RBC counting?** 1 in 200.
2. **Why is Hayem's fluid used?** It is isotonic, so RBCs neither swell nor shrink, and it prevents clumping.
3. **What is the multiplication factor?** 10,000.
4. **Why is the RBC count higher in people living at high altitude?** Low O2 stimulates erythropoietin and more RBCs are made.
5. **What colour is the bead in the RBC pipette?** Red.

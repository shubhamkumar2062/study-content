# Measurement of height, weight and BMI

**Aim:** To measure the height and weight of a subject and calculate the body mass index (BMI).

## Principle

**Body mass index** relates weight to height and is used to screen for underweight, overweight and obesity.

**BMI = weight (kg) ÷ [height (m)]²** (unit kg/m²)

## Requirements

Stadiometer or wall-mounted height scale with a head-board, weighing machine (calibrated, set to zero), calculator.

## Procedure

**Height**

1. Subject removes shoes and headwear, stands straight on a flat floor with heels together, back of heels, buttocks, shoulders and back of head touching the scale.
2. Head held level (looking straight ahead).
3. Lower the head-board gently to touch the top of the head; read to the nearest 0.5 cm. Convert to metres.

**Weight**

4. Check the machine reads zero.
5. Subject in light clothing, without shoes, stands still in the centre of the platform.
6. Read to the nearest 0.1 kg (or 0.5 kg).

## Observations and calculation

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Height | ___ cm = ___ m |
| Weight | ___ kg |
| BMI | ___ kg/m² |

**Worked example:** weight 68 kg, height 1.65 m → BMI = 68 ÷ (1.65 × 1.65) = 68 ÷ 2.72 ≈ **25.0 kg/m²**.

## Interpretation

| BMI (kg/m²) | WHO classification (adults) |
| --- | --- |
| Below 18.5 | Underweight |
| 18.5–24.9 | Normal |
| 25.0–29.9 | Overweight |
| 30 and above | Obese |

For Asian Indians, lower cut-offs are often used because health risk starts at lower BMI: normal about 18.5–22.9, overweight 23–24.9, obese 25 and above. By these, the worked example above would be classed as obese, though it is at the WHO overweight border.

**Limitations:** BMI does not separate muscle from fat (athletes may read "overweight"), and does not show where fat is stored; waist circumference adds this information. Not used in the same way for children (age-specific charts) or pregnant women.

## Precautions

1. Remove shoes and heavy clothing.
2. Zero the weighing machine before use.
3. Keep the head level when measuring height.
4. Measure at the same time of day when comparing readings.

## Viva questions

1. **Formula of BMI?** Weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²).
2. **Normal BMI range (WHO)?** 18.5–24.9 kg/m².
3. **Why are lower cut-offs used for Indians?** Diabetes and heart-disease risk rises at lower BMI in South Asians.
4. **One limitation of BMI?** It cannot tell fat from muscle.
5. **Why is BMI important for pharmacists?** Obesity counselling and some drug doses are based on body weight.

# Recording of peak expiratory flow with a peak flow meter

**Aim:** To record the force of air expelled from the lungs – the peak expiratory flow rate (PEFR) – using a peak flow meter.

## Principle

**Peak expiratory flow rate** is the maximum speed of air (litres per minute) blown out in a short, sharp forced expiration after a full inspiration. It depends mainly on the diameter of the large airways and the strength of the expiratory muscles. It falls when the airways are narrowed, as in **asthma** and COPD, so it is used to monitor these patients at home.

## Requirements

Mini Wright (or similar) peak flow meter with a disposable mouthpiece, predicted PEFR chart (by age, sex and height), height scale.

## Procedure

1. Note the subject's age, sex and height.
2. Fix a clean mouthpiece; move the pointer to zero.
3. The subject **stands** (or sits upright), holds the meter horizontally without blocking the scale or the vent with the fingers.
4. Take the **deepest possible breath**, close the lips tightly round the mouthpiece, and blow out **as hard and fast as possible** in one short blast (a "huff" – not a slow long breath).
5. Read the pointer position (L/min); return it to zero.
6. Repeat **three times** with rest in between. Record the **highest** of the three.
7. Compare with the predicted value from the chart.

Predicted PEFR (from chart) = ___ L/min

**Percentage of predicted = (best reading ÷ predicted) × 100**

Example: best 400 L/min, predicted 500 L/min → 80 % of predicted.

## Interpretation (zone system used in asthma care)

| Zone | % of predicted / personal best | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Green | 80–100 % | Good control |
| Yellow | 50–80 % | Caution – airways narrowing; follow action plan |
| Red | Below 50 % | Medical alert – urgent treatment |

PEFR is higher in men, taller people and young adults; lower in women, children and the elderly. A rise after a bronchodilator inhaler shows reversible airway narrowing (asthma).

## Precautions

1. Correct posture and a tight seal round the mouthpiece.
2. Blow short and sharp; do not cough or spit into the meter.
3. Use a fresh mouthpiece for each person.
4. Take the best of three, not the average.

## Viva questions

1. **Define PEFR.** Maximum rate of air flow during a forced expiration after full inspiration.
2. **Unit of PEFR?** Litres per minute.
3. **In which disease is it most useful?** Bronchial asthma.
4. **Why is the best of three taken?** Effort varies; the best reading shows true capacity.
5. **How does a bronchodilator change PEFR in asthma?** It increases it by widening the airways.

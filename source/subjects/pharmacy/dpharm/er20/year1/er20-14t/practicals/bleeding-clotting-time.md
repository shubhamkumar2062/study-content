# Determination of bleeding time and clotting time

**Aim:** To determine the bleeding time (Duke's method) and clotting time (capillary tube method) of the given subject.

## Principle

- **Bleeding time (BT):** the time from a standard skin puncture until bleeding stops. It depends mainly on **platelet** number and function and on capillary constriction.
- **Clotting time (CT):** the time from the escape of blood until it forms a clot (fibrin threads appear). It depends on the **clotting factors** (coagulation cascade).

## Requirements

Sterile lancet, spirit swabs, cotton, filter paper (circular), stopwatch, glass capillary tubes (about 10 cm long, 1–2 mm bore).

## Procedure A: Bleeding time (Duke's method)

1. Clean the fingertip (or ear lobe) with spirit and let it dry.
2. Make a quick puncture about 3 mm deep and **start the stopwatch** as the first drop appears.
3. Do not squeeze. Every **30 seconds**, touch the drop gently with the edge of the filter paper without touching the skin.
4. Move to a fresh part of the paper each time. The spots become smaller.
5. **Stop the watch** when no more blood stains the paper.
6. BT = number of blood spots × 30 seconds (or the time on the watch).

## Procedure B: Clotting time (capillary tube method)

1. Prick the finger and **start the stopwatch** when blood appears.
2. Fill a capillary tube with the free-flowing blood (hold it slightly downward).
3. After 2 minutes, break off a small piece of the tube every **30 seconds**, gently separating the pieces.
4. Note the time when a **fibrin thread** is first seen between the broken pieces. This is the clotting time.

**Commonly quoted normal ranges** (vary with method; use your lab manual's range): BT about 1–5 minutes (Duke); CT about 4–10 minutes (capillary tube).

- **BT prolonged:** thrombocytopenia (e.g. dengue), platelet function defects, aspirin use, von Willebrand disease.
- **CT prolonged:** haemophilia, vitamin K deficiency, liver disease, anticoagulant therapy (heparin). In haemophilia **BT is normal but CT is prolonged**.

## Precautions

1. Do not squeeze the finger or touch the wound with the filter paper.
2. Start timing at the first appearance of blood.
3. Use clean, dry capillary tubes; break pieces gently.
4. Room temperature affects CT – do the test at normal room temperature.

## Viva questions

1. **What does bleeding time test?** Platelet function and vessel constriction.
2. **What does clotting time test?** The coagulation factors.
3. **In haemophilia, which is prolonged?** Clotting time; bleeding time stays normal.
4. **Why does aspirin prolong BT?** It blocks thromboxane A2 formation in platelets.
5. **What is the fibrin thread?** Insoluble fibrin formed from fibrinogen by thrombin.

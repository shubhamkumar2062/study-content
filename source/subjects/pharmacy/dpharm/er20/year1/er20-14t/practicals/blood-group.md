# Determination of blood group

**Aim:** To determine the ABO and Rh blood group of the given blood sample (slide method).

## Principle

Blood groups depend on antigens (agglutinogens) on the RBC surface. When RBCs carrying an antigen meet the matching antibody (antisera), they clump together – **agglutination**.

- Anti-A serum clumps RBCs with antigen A; anti-B clumps antigen B; anti-D clumps Rh (D)-positive cells.

## Requirements

Clean glass slide (or tile), antisera anti-A (blue), anti-B (yellow) and anti-D, sterile lancet, spirit swabs, cotton, toothpicks or mixing sticks, glass marker.

## Procedure

1. Mark three areas on a clean slide: A, B and D.
2. Place one drop of anti-A, anti-B and anti-D serum in the marked areas.
3. Clean the fingertip with spirit, let it dry, prick and discard the first drop.
4. Add one small drop of blood near each antiserum drop.
5. Mix each with a separate clean stick, spreading into a circle.
6. Rock the slide gently for about 1–2 minutes (anti-D up to about 3 minutes) and look for clumping with the naked eye (or a hand lens) against a white background.

## Observations

| Anti-A | Anti-B | Anti-D | Blood group |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| + | − | + | A positive |
| − | + | + | B positive |
| + | + | + | AB positive |
| − | − | + | O positive |
| (as above) | (as above) | − | Same ABO group, Rh negative |

## Precautions

1. Use a separate stick for each drop so sera are not mixed.
2. Blood drop should be smaller than the serum drop.
3. Read within the time given; drying at the edges can look like clumping.
4. Check expiry date of antisera.

## Viva questions

1. **Who discovered the ABO system?** Karl Landsteiner.
2. **Which group is the universal donor and universal recipient?** O is the universal donor; AB is the universal recipient.
3. **What is agglutination?** Clumping of RBCs when antigen meets its antibody.
4. **Why is Rh group important in pregnancy?** An Rh-negative mother may form anti-D against an Rh-positive baby, causing haemolytic disease of the newborn in a later pregnancy.
5. **Is a cross-match still needed before transfusion?** Yes, to check compatibility of donor cells with recipient serum.

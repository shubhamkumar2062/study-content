# Determination of differential leucocyte count (DLC)

**Aim:** To prepare and stain a blood smear and determine the percentage of each type of white blood cell.

## Principle

A thin blood film is stained with **Leishman's stain** (a Romanowsky stain: eosin + methylene blue in methyl alcohol). Methyl alcohol fixes the cells; nuclei stain purple-blue, eosinophil granules orange-red, basophil granules dark blue. **100 WBCs** are counted and classified, so each count equals a percentage.

## Requirements

Clean grease-free slides, spreader slide, Leishman's stain, buffered (or distilled) water, dropper, staining rack, microscope with oil immersion, cedarwood/immersion oil, lancet, spirit, cotton.

## Procedure

**A. Preparing the smear**

1. Put a small drop of blood about 1–2 cm from one end of a clean slide.
2. Hold the spreader at about 45°, touch the drop, let blood spread along its edge, and push forward in one smooth movement.
3. A good smear is thin, tongue-shaped, with a feathered tail. Air-dry it.

**B. Staining**

4. Cover the smear with Leishman's stain (count the drops); leave 2 minutes (fixation).
5. Add double the number of drops of buffered water; mix by blowing gently; leave 7–10 minutes (staining).
6. Wash with water, wipe the back, air-dry.

**C. Counting**

7. Examine under oil immersion, in the thin part near the tail, moving in a zig-zag (battlement) path.
8. Identify and tally 100 WBCs.

**Clinical meaning:** neutrophilia – bacterial infection; lymphocytosis – viral infections, TB; eosinophilia – allergy, asthma, worm infestation; monocytosis – TB, malaria.

## Precautions

1. Slides must be grease-free; smear must be thin and even.
2. Do not let the stain dry on the slide.
3. Count in the thin area and avoid counting the same field twice.

## Viva questions

1. **What does Leishman's stain contain?** Eosin and methylene blue dissolved in methyl alcohol.
2. **Why is methyl alcohol present?** It fixes the cells to the slide.
3. **Which WBC is most common?** Neutrophils.
4. **Which cell rises in allergy?** Eosinophil.
5. **Why count 100 cells?** So the number of each type equals its percentage directly.

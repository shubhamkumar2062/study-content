# Study of the compound microscope

**Aim:** To study the parts, working and care of a compound microscope.

## Principle

A compound microscope uses two lens systems. The **objective** lens forms a magnified real image of the specimen; the **eyepiece (ocular)** magnifies this image again to give an enlarged virtual image.

**Total magnification = magnification of eyepiece × magnification of objective.**

## Requirements

Compound microscope, prepared slide (e.g. blood smear), cedarwood or immersion oil, lens paper, xylene.

## Parts of the compound microscope

```text
          eyepiece (10×) ──┐
                          [ ]
             body tube ── | |
             revolving    | |      ── arm (limb)
             nosepiece ──(===)
     objectives 10×,40×,100× \|/
                stage ── ═══════════ ── stage clips
            condenser ──   (  )      ── coarse adjustment knob
    iris diaphragm ──       ‾‾       ── fine adjustment knob
     mirror / lamp ──      (O)
                base ── ▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▄
```

| Part | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Base and arm | Support; hold the microscope by the arm |
| Eyepiece | Magnifies the image formed by the objective (usually 10×) |
| Body tube | Holds eyepiece and nosepiece in line |
| Revolving nosepiece | Holds and changes objectives |
| Objectives | Low power 10×, high power 40×, oil immersion 100× |
| Stage with clips | Holds the slide; mechanical stage moves it |
| Condenser | Focuses light on the specimen |
| Iris diaphragm | Controls the amount of light |
| Mirror / light source | Sends light upward (concave side for daylight) |
| Coarse and fine adjustment | Bring the specimen into focus quickly and precisely |

## Procedure

1. Place the microscope on a firm table, arm towards you; adjust the mirror for even bright light.
2. Put the slide on the stage, specimen centred over the hole; fix with clips.
3. Using the 10× objective, lower the tube with the coarse knob while watching from the side, then raise it slowly while looking through the eyepiece until the image appears.
4. Sharpen with the fine knob. Adjust the diaphragm for contrast.
5. Turn to 40× and focus with the fine knob only.
6. For 100×: place a drop of immersion oil on the slide, swing in the oil objective so it touches the oil, focus with the fine knob.
7. After use, clean the oil lens with lens paper (a trace of xylene if needed), return to low power, lower the stage and cover.

## Observations

| Eyepiece | Objective | Total magnification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10× | 10× | 100× |
| 10× | 40× | 400× |
| 10× | 100× (oil) | 1000× |

## Result

The parts of the compound microscope were identified and their functions and handling studied.

## Precautions

1. Carry with one hand under the base and one on the arm.
2. Never focus downward while looking through the eyepiece (slide may break).
3. Do not touch lenses with fingers; clean only with lens paper.
4. Use oil only with the 100× objective; wipe it off after use.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the magnification with a 10× eyepiece and 45× objective?** 450×.
2. **Why is oil used with the 100× objective?** Oil has a refractive index close to glass, so less light is lost by bending and the image is brighter and sharper.
3. **What is resolving power?** The ability to show two close points as separate.
4. **What does the iris diaphragm do?** Controls the amount of light reaching the specimen.
5. **Which knob is used at high power?** Only the fine adjustment knob.

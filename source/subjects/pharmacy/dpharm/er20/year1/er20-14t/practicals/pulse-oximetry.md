# Recording of pulse oxygen saturation (before and after exertion)

**Aim:** To record the oxygen saturation of arterial blood (SpO2) and pulse rate with a pulse oximeter before and after exertion.

## Principle

A pulse oximeter clips on a fingertip. It shines **red and infrared light** through the finger. Oxyhaemoglobin and deoxyhaemoglobin absorb these two lights differently. By measuring absorption only during the pulsatile (arterial) part of each beat, the device calculates the percentage of haemoglobin carrying oxygen – **SpO2** – and also shows the pulse rate.

## Requirements

Fingertip pulse oximeter, spirit swabs, nail-polish remover if needed, stopwatch.

## Procedure

1. Seat the subject and let them rest for 5 minutes. Hands should be warm.
2. Remove nail polish or artificial nails from the test finger (usually the index or middle finger).
3. Switch on the oximeter and place the finger fully inside, nail upward. Keep the hand still and at heart level.
4. Wait until the reading is steady (about 30–60 seconds) and a good pulse waveform/bar appears. Record SpO2 and pulse rate.
5. Ask the subject to exercise (e.g. 2–3 minutes of stair climbing or spot jogging).
6. Record SpO2 and pulse immediately after exercise and then every minute for 5 minutes.

## Interpretation

- **Normal SpO2 at rest: about 95–100 %** in healthy people at sea level.
- Readings persistently below about 94 % need attention; around 90 % or less indicates significant **hypoxaemia** (e.g. pneumonia, COPD, severe asthma).
- In a healthy subject SpO2 stays nearly the same after moderate exercise while the pulse rises; a clear fall in SpO2 on exertion suggests heart or lung disease.

## Precautions

1. Cold fingers, poor circulation, movement, bright light and nail polish give false readings.
2. Wait for a steady reading with a good signal.
3. The oximeter cannot tell carboxyhaemoglobin from oxyhaemoglobin, so it reads falsely normal in carbon monoxide poisoning.

## Viva questions

1. **What does SpO2 mean?** Percentage saturation of haemoglobin with oxygen measured by a pulse oximeter.
2. **What lights are used?** Red and infrared.
3. **Normal SpO2?** About 95–100 %.
4. **Why does nail polish affect the reading?** It absorbs and blocks the light.
5. **Name one clinical use.** Monitoring patients with pneumonia, COVID-19, asthma, during anaesthesia.

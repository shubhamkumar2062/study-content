# Determination of ESR (Westergren method)

**Aim:** To determine the erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) of the given blood sample by the Westergren method.

## Principle

When anticoagulated blood stands in a vertical tube, RBCs settle down under gravity, leaving clear plasma above. The fall of the upper level of RBCs in **one hour**, measured in mm, is the ESR. Stacking of RBCs (rouleaux), favoured by raised fibrinogen and globulins in inflammation, makes them settle faster.

## Requirements

Westergren tube (200 mm long, graduated 0–200 mm), Westergren stand, 3.8 % trisodium citrate, syringe and needle, test tube, timer.

## Procedure

1. Take 0.4 mL of 3.8 % trisodium citrate in a clean tube.
2. Collect venous blood and add 1.6 mL to the citrate (ratio 4 parts blood : 1 part citrate). Mix gently by inversion.
3. Draw the mixture into the Westergren tube exactly to the 0 mark, avoiding air bubbles.
4. Fix the tube **exactly vertical** in the stand at room temperature, away from vibration and sunlight.
5. Start the timer. After exactly **1 hour**, read the height of the clear plasma column from 0 to the top of the RBC column.

## Normal values (Westergren, approx.)

- Men: 0–15 mm in the first hour
- Women: 0–20 mm in the first hour (slightly higher; also higher in pregnancy and old age)

**ESR rises in:** tuberculosis, rheumatoid arthritis, rheumatic fever, infections, anaemia, multiple myeloma, pregnancy. **ESR falls in:** polycythaemia, sickle-cell anaemia. ESR is a non-specific test – it shows that disease is present, not which one.

## Precautions

1. Keep the blood–citrate ratio exactly 4:1.
2. Tube must be clean, dry and exactly vertical (a tilt speeds up settling).
3. Avoid air bubbles and clotting.
4. Do the test within about 2 hours of collection; read at exactly 60 minutes.

## Viva questions

1. **Define ESR.** The distance in mm that RBCs fall in anticoagulated blood in a vertical tube in one hour.
2. **Which anticoagulant and ratio are used?** 3.8 % trisodium citrate, 1 part to 4 parts blood.
3. **Name another method.** Wintrobe method.
4. **Why does ESR rise in inflammation?** More fibrinogen and globulins promote rouleaux formation.
5. **Is ESR diagnostic?** No – it is non-specific; useful to follow disease activity (e.g. TB, RA).

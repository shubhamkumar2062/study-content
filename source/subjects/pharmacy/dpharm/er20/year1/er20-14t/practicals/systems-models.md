# Study of organ systems with charts, models and specimens

**Aim:** To study the cardiovascular, respiratory, digestive, urinary, endocrine, reproductive and nervous systems, and the eye, ear and skin, with the help of charts, models and specimens.

## Principle

Models and charts show the shape, position and internal structure of organs that cannot be seen in a living person. Identifying the parts and linking each to its function builds the base for theory answers and viva.

## Requirements

Torso model, heart, lung, kidney, brain, eye, ear and skin models; charts of each system; preserved specimens where available.

## Procedure

1. Identify the system and the position of each organ on the torso model or chart.
2. Open the model (where possible) and name the internal parts.
3. Write the function of each part.
4. Draw a neat labelled diagram in the record.

## Observations

| System / organ | Parts to identify | Key functions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cardiovascular (heart model)** | Four chambers, tricuspid, mitral, pulmonary and aortic valves, chordae tendineae, aorta, venae cavae, pulmonary trunk and veins, coronary arteries | Pumping of blood; pulmonary and systemic circulation |
| **Respiratory** | Nasal cavity, pharynx, larynx, trachea with rings, bronchi, lungs (3 right lobes, 2 left), pleura, diaphragm | Air conduction and gas exchange |
| **Digestive** | Mouth, oesophagus, stomach (fundus, body, pylorus), duodenum, jejunum, ileum, caecum, appendix, colon, rectum, liver, gall bladder, pancreas | Digestion and absorption |
| **Urinary (kidney model)** | Cortex, medulla, pyramids, calyces, pelvis, ureter, bladder, urethra; nephron chart | Urine formation, fluid and salt balance |
| **Endocrine** | Pituitary, thyroid, parathyroids, adrenals, pancreas (islets), ovaries/testes | Hormone secretion |
| **Reproductive** | Male: testes, epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicles, prostate, urethra. Female: ovaries, uterine tubes, uterus, cervix, vagina | Gamete and hormone production; pregnancy |
| **Nervous (brain model)** | Cerebral lobes, cerebellum, midbrain, pons, medulla, spinal cord, cranial nerves | Control and coordination |
| **Eye** | Cornea, sclera, choroid, iris, pupil, lens, ciliary body, retina, optic nerve, aqueous and vitreous humour | Vision |
| **Ear** | Pinna, external canal, tympanic membrane, malleus, incus, stapes, Eustachian tube, cochlea, semicircular canals | Hearing and balance |
| **Skin** | Epidermis layers, dermis, hair follicle, sebaceous and sweat glands, receptors, hypodermis | Protection, temperature control, sensation |

## Result

The organ systems were studied with models, charts and specimens and their parts identified and labelled.

## Precautions

1. Handle models carefully; replace parts in the right position.
2. Draw diagrams in pencil and label on one side with straight lines.

## Viva questions

1. **Which chamber of the heart has the thickest wall?** Left ventricle.
2. **How many lobes does each lung have?** Right 3, left 2.
3. **Which gland is called the master gland?** Pituitary.
4. **Name the three ear ossicles.** Malleus, incus, stapes.
5. **Where is the nephron's glomerulus located – cortex or medulla?** Cortex.
6. **Which part of the eye controls pupil size?** Iris.

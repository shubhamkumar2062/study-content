# General techniques for the collection of blood

**Aim:** To learn the general techniques of collecting capillary and venous blood for haematological tests.

> Work only under the teacher's supervision. Use sterile disposable lancets and needles, wear gloves, and discard sharps in a puncture-proof container.

## Principle

Small volumes for Hb, cell counts, smears, blood grouping, BT and CT are taken by **skin puncture** (capillary blood) from the fingertip, ear lobe or (in infants) the heel. Larger volumes are taken by **venepuncture** from a superficial vein, usually the median cubital vein at the elbow. An **anticoagulant** is added when unclotted blood is needed.

| Anticoagulant | How it works | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EDTA (dipotassium salt) | Binds calcium | Blood counts, smears |
| Trisodium citrate 3.8 % | Binds calcium | ESR (1:4), coagulation tests (1:9) |
| Double oxalate | Precipitates calcium | Some counts, PCV |
| Heparin | Inhibits thrombin (with antithrombin) | Some chemistry tests |

## Requirements

Sterile lancet, 70 % alcohol (spirit) swabs, dry cotton, gloves; for venepuncture – tourniquet, sterile syringe and needle (or vacuum tube), anticoagulant tubes, labels, sharps container.

## Procedure A: Capillary blood (finger prick)

1. Choose the side of the tip of the ring or middle finger; warm and massage the hand to improve flow.
2. Clean with spirit and **let it dry** (wet spirit causes haemolysis and stings).
3. Hold the finger firmly and make a quick prick 2–3 mm deep with the lancet.
4. **Wipe away the first drop** (it contains tissue fluid).
5. Let a free-flowing drop form without squeezing hard; collect as required.
6. Press with dry cotton to stop bleeding.

## Procedure B: Venous blood (venepuncture)

1. Seat the subject with the arm extended; tie the tourniquet 7–8 cm above the elbow (not more than about one minute).
2. Ask the subject to make a fist; feel for the median cubital vein.
3. Clean the site with spirit and let it dry.
4. Insert the needle bevel up at about 15–30° along the vein; draw blood slowly.
5. Release the tourniquet, then withdraw the needle and press with cotton.
6. Transfer blood gently into labelled tubes and mix with the anticoagulant by gentle inversion (do not shake).

## Observations

| Site | Volume collected | Container | Test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fingertip | Few drops | Slide / pipette | Hb, counts, grouping |
| Median cubital vein | ___ mL | EDTA / citrate tube | ESR, counts |

## Result

Capillary and venous blood were collected by the correct aseptic technique.

## Precautions

1. Strict asepsis; never reuse lancets or needles.
2. Allow spirit to dry before pricking.
3. Avoid squeezing the finger (dilutes blood with tissue fluid).
4. Label every sample with name, date and time.
5. Do not leave the tourniquet on too long.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is the first drop wiped off?** It is mixed with tissue fluid and gives wrong values.
2. **Which vein is preferred for venepuncture?** Median cubital vein – large, superficial, well fixed.
3. **Which anticoagulant is used for ESR?** 3.8 % trisodium citrate.
4. **Why does EDTA prevent clotting?** It binds calcium, which is needed for clotting.
5. **Name sites for skin puncture.** Fingertip, ear lobe, heel (infants).

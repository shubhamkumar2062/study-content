# Unit 6: Digestive system

Syllabus chapter 9: anatomy and physiology of the gastrointestinal tract (GIT); anatomy and functions of the accessory glands; physiology of digestion and absorption.

The digestive system breaks food down into small molecules that can be absorbed into the blood and lymph, and removes the undigested waste.

**Main processes:** ingestion → propulsion (swallowing, peristalsis) → mechanical digestion (chewing, churning, segmentation) → chemical digestion (enzymes) → absorption → defaecation.

## 6.1 Organisation of the digestive system

```text
          DIGESTIVE SYSTEM (front view)

   Mouth (teeth, tongue) ── salivary glands (parotid, submandibular,
     |                                      sublingual)
   Pharynx
     |
   Oesophagus (~25 cm)
     |            (diaphragm)
   STOMACH  ←──────────── LIVER (right upper abdomen) + GALL BLADDER
     |  (pyloric sphincter)       |  bile duct
   DUODENUM ←──── common bile duct + pancreatic duct (ampulla, sphincter of Oddi)
     |                               ↑
   JEJUNUM                        PANCREAS (behind the stomach)
     |
   ILEUM ── (ileocaecal valve)
     |
   LARGE INTESTINE: caecum (+ appendix) → ascending colon → transverse
   colon → descending colon → sigmoid colon → rectum → anal canal → anus
```

**GIT (alimentary canal):** mouth, pharynx, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, anus – about 9 metres long.
**Accessory organs:** teeth, tongue, salivary glands, liver, gall bladder, pancreas.

## 6.2 General structure of the GIT wall

From oesophagus to anus the wall has four layers:

| Layer | Structure | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mucosa** (innermost) | Epithelium (stratified squamous in mouth/oesophagus/anus; simple columnar in stomach and intestines), lamina propria, muscularis mucosae | Secretion, absorption, protection |
| **Submucosa** | Loose connective tissue, blood vessels, lymphatics, **submucosal (Meissner's) nerve plexus** | Supports mucosa; controls secretion |
| **Muscularis externa** | Inner circular + outer longitudinal smooth muscle (stomach has an extra oblique layer); **myenteric (Auerbach's) plexus** between them | Peristalsis and mixing |
| **Serosa / adventitia** | Visceral peritoneum (serosa) or connective tissue | Protects; reduces friction |

The **peritoneum** is the large serous membrane of the abdomen; folds such as the mesentery hold the intestines.

## 6.3 Mouth, pharynx and oesophagus

**Mouth (oral cavity):** food is chewed (mastication), mixed with saliva and shaped into a **bolus**.

- **Teeth:** temporary (milk/deciduous) set 20; permanent set **32** (per half jaw: 2 incisors, 1 canine, 2 premolars, 3 molars). Each tooth has crown, neck and root; made of enamel (hardest substance in the body), dentine, pulp.
- **Tongue:** voluntary muscle covered with papillae and **taste buds**; helps chewing, swallowing and speech.
- **Salivary glands** (three pairs):

| Gland | Location | Duct opens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Parotid** (largest) | In front of and below the ear | Inside the cheek opposite the upper second molar (Stensen's duct) |
| **Submandibular** | Below the mandible | Floor of the mouth (Wharton's duct) |
| **Sublingual** (smallest) | Under the tongue | Several small ducts in the floor of the mouth |

**Saliva:** about 1–1.5 litres per day; pH about 6.5–7. Contains water, mucin, **salivary amylase (ptyalin)**, lysozyme, IgA, electrolytes.
**Functions:** moistens and lubricates food, starts starch digestion (starch → maltose), dissolves food for taste, cleans the mouth and kills some bacteria, helps speech. Secretion is controlled by the parasympathetic nervous system (sight and smell of food).

**Swallowing (deglutition):** voluntary oral stage → involuntary pharyngeal stage (soft palate closes the nasopharynx, **epiglottis closes the larynx**, breathing stops briefly) → oesophageal stage (peristalsis).

**Oesophagus:** muscular tube about 25 cm long from pharynx to stomach, passing through the diaphragm. Upper part skeletal muscle, lower part smooth muscle. The **lower oesophageal (cardiac) sphincter** prevents reflux of acid (weakness → heartburn, GERD).

## 6.4 Stomach

```text
                 STOMACH (J-shaped)

   oesophagus
       \     FUNDUS
        \    _____
  cardiac\  /     \
  sphincter|       \   lesser curvature (top right)
           |  BODY  \
           |         \___
           |  (rugae)     \___  PYLORIC ANTRUM
            \                 \______ pyloric sphincter → DUODENUM
             \___greater curvature___/
```

- J-shaped bag in the left upper abdomen. Parts: cardia, **fundus**, **body**, **pylorus** (antrum and canal). The **pyloric sphincter** controls emptying into the duodenum.
- Empty stomach shows folds called **rugae**; capacity about 1.5 litres in adults.
- Three muscle layers (oblique, circular, longitudinal) for churning.

**Gastric glands (in the mucosa)**

| Cell | Secretes | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Chief (peptic) cells** | **Pepsinogen** (and gastric lipase) | Pepsinogen → pepsin by HCl; protein digestion |
| **Parietal (oxyntic) cells** | **Hydrochloric acid** and **intrinsic factor** | Acid activates pepsin and kills microbes; intrinsic factor needed for vitamin B12 absorption |
| **Mucous neck cells** | Mucus and bicarbonate | Protect the stomach lining from acid and pepsin |
| **G cells** (pylorus) | **Gastrin** (hormone) | Stimulates acid secretion and motility |

**Gastric juice:** about 2–3 litres per day; pH about 1.5–3.5. Contents: HCl, pepsinogen, mucus, intrinsic factor, water, gastric lipase.

**Functions of the stomach:** storage of food; mixing to form semi-liquid **chyme**; start of protein digestion; killing of microbes; intrinsic factor secretion; some absorption (water, alcohol, aspirin-like drugs).

**Phases of gastric secretion:** cephalic (sight, smell, thought of food – via vagus), gastric (food in stomach → gastrin), intestinal (chyme in duodenum – first slight stimulation, then inhibition by secretin and CCK).

**Lack of intrinsic factor** → vitamin B12 not absorbed → **pernicious anaemia**. Excess acid or *Helicobacter pylori* → **peptic ulcer**.

## 6.5 Small intestine

About 6 metres long; the main site of **digestion and absorption**. Three parts:

| Part | Length | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Duodenum** | About 25 cm, C-shaped | Receives bile and pancreatic juice through the **hepatopancreatic ampulla** (sphincter of Oddi); Brunner's glands make alkaline mucus |
| **Jejunum** | About 2.5 m | Most absorption |
| **Ileum** | About 3.5 m | Absorbs vitamin B12 and bile salts; Peyer's patches (lymphoid tissue); ends at the **ileocaecal valve** |

**Adaptations for absorption (huge surface area):**

1. **Circular folds** (plicae circulares) of the mucosa.
2. **Villi** – finger-like projections, each with a capillary network and a central lymph vessel (**lacteal**).
3. **Microvilli** – the "brush border" on each absorptive cell, carrying digestive enzymes.

```text
         One villus

        _____  microvilli (brush border) on the cells
       /     \
      | ||||| |  columnar absorptive cells + goblet cells
      | |   | |
      | | L | |  L = central lacteal (absorbs fat → lymph)
      | |   | |
      |~~~~~~~|  capillary network (absorbs sugars, amino acids → blood)
      |_______|
   ___|crypt  |___ intestinal glands (crypts of Lieberkühn)
```

**Intestinal juice (succus entericus):** about 1–2 litres per day, alkaline. Brush-border enzymes: **maltase, sucrase, lactase**, peptidases (aminopeptidase, dipeptidase), intestinal lipase, **enterokinase** (activates trypsinogen).

**Movements:** **segmentation** (mixing) and **peristalsis** (moving forward).

## 6.6 Large intestine

About 1.5 metres long; wider than the small intestine.

- **Caecum** with the **vermiform appendix** (lymphoid tissue; inflammation = appendicitis).
- **Colon** – ascending, transverse, descending, sigmoid. Features: **taeniae coli** (three bands of longitudinal muscle), **haustra** (pouches), fatty appendices.
- **Rectum** and **anal canal** – internal (involuntary) and external (voluntary) anal sphincters.

**Functions:** absorbs water and electrolytes (forms solid faeces); **bacteria** (gut flora) make **vitamin K** and some B vitamins and ferment fibre; secretes mucus; stores faeces until **defaecation** (a reflex started by stretch of the rectum, controlled voluntarily by the external sphincter).

## 6.7 Accessory glands

### Liver

- **Largest gland** in the body (about 1.5 kg); in the right upper abdomen under the diaphragm. Two main lobes (right larger, left smaller) plus caudate and quadrate lobes.
- Blood supply: **hepatic artery** (oxygenated, about 25 %) + **hepatic portal vein** (nutrient-rich blood from the gut, about 75 %); drained by hepatic veins into the inferior vena cava.
- Structural unit: the **hepatic lobule** – hexagonal, with plates of liver cells (hepatocytes) radiating from a **central vein**, sinusoids between them lined by **Kupffer cells** (phagocytes), and **portal triads** (branch of hepatic artery, portal vein, bile duct) at the corners.

**Functions of the liver**

1. **Bile production** (about 500–1000 mL/day) – bile salts emulsify fats.
2. **Carbohydrate metabolism** – stores glucose as glycogen (glycogenesis), releases it (glycogenolysis), makes new glucose (gluconeogenesis).
3. **Protein metabolism** – deamination of amino acids; forms **urea**; makes plasma proteins (albumin, fibrinogen, prothrombin).
4. **Fat metabolism** – makes cholesterol and lipoproteins; breaks down fatty acids.
5. **Detoxification** – breaks down drugs, alcohol, toxins and hormones (**drug metabolism**, first-pass effect).
6. **Storage** – glycogen, iron, copper, vitamins A, D, B12.
7. **Excretion of bilirubin** (from old RBCs) in bile.
8. **Heat production** and, in the fetus, blood cell formation.
9. Kupffer cells remove bacteria and worn-out cells.

### Gall bladder and bile

- Pear-shaped sac under the liver; **stores and concentrates bile** (capacity about 30–50 mL).
- Fatty food in the duodenum → hormone **cholecystokinin (CCK)** → gall bladder contracts, sphincter of Oddi relaxes → bile flows into the duodenum.
- **Bile contains:** water, **bile salts** (sodium glycocholate, taurocholate), bile pigments (bilirubin, biliverdin), cholesterol, lecithin, electrolytes. No digestive enzymes.
- **Functions of bile:** **emulsification of fats** (breaks big fat drops into small ones for lipase); helps absorb fats and fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K); excretion of bilirubin and cholesterol; neutralises acid chyme; bile salts are reabsorbed in the ileum (enterohepatic circulation).
- **Gallstones** (cholelithiasis) – usually cholesterol stones.

### Pancreas

- Soft, elongated gland behind the stomach: head (in the curve of the duodenum), body, tail (near the spleen).
- **Mixed gland:** **exocrine** part (acini, about 98 %) makes pancreatic juice; **endocrine** part (**islets of Langerhans**) makes insulin, glucagon, somatostatin (see Unit 10).
- **Pancreatic juice:** about 1.2–1.5 litres per day; **alkaline (pH about 8)** due to bicarbonate, which neutralises acid chyme.

| Enzyme | Acts on → gives |
| --- | --- |
| **Trypsinogen** → trypsin (by enterokinase); chymotrypsinogen → chymotrypsin (by trypsin); carboxypeptidase | Proteins and polypeptides → smaller peptides and amino acids |
| **Pancreatic amylase** | Starch → maltose |
| **Pancreatic lipase** | Fats → fatty acids + monoglycerides (glycerol) |
| Nucleases | Nucleic acids → nucleotides |

**Control:** **secretin** (from duodenum, in response to acid) → bicarbonate-rich juice; **CCK** → enzyme-rich juice; vagus nerve.

## 6.8 Physiology of digestion – summary

| Food | Mouth | Stomach | Small intestine (final products) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Carbohydrates** | Salivary amylase: starch → maltose | (Salivary amylase works briefly until acid stops it) | Pancreatic amylase: starch → maltose; maltase → glucose; sucrase: sucrose → glucose + fructose; lactase: lactose → glucose + galactose |
| **Proteins** | — | Pepsin: proteins → peptones, polypeptides | Trypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidase, peptidases → **amino acids** |
| **Fats** | (Lingual lipase, slight) | Gastric lipase (slight) | Bile emulsifies; pancreatic lipase → **fatty acids + glycerol / monoglycerides** |

## 6.9 Absorption

- **Main site: small intestine** (jejunum and ileum). The stomach absorbs only water, alcohol and some drugs; the large intestine absorbs water and electrolytes.

| Nutrient | How absorbed | Into |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glucose, galactose | Active transport with Na+ (co-transport) | Blood capillaries → portal vein → liver |
| Fructose | Facilitated diffusion | Blood |
| Amino acids | Active transport with Na+ | Blood |
| Fatty acids, monoglycerides | Form **micelles** with bile salts → enter cells → made into triglycerides → packed as **chylomicrons** | **Lacteals** (lymph) → thoracic duct → blood |
| Water | Osmosis (about 9 L enters the gut daily; most absorbed in small intestine) | Blood |
| Vitamins | Fat-soluble with fats; water-soluble by diffusion or carriers; **B12 with intrinsic factor in the ileum** | Blood / lymph |
| Iron, calcium | Active transport, mainly duodenum | Blood |

## Remember

- GIT wall: mucosa, submucosa, muscularis externa, serosa; Meissner's and Auerbach's plexuses.
- Parotid is the largest salivary gland; saliva has ptyalin (amylase).
- Parietal cells → HCl + intrinsic factor; chief cells → pepsinogen; G cells → gastrin.
- Small intestine: duodenum, jejunum, ileum; villi with lacteals; main site of digestion and absorption.
- Liver = largest gland: bile, glycogen, urea, plasma proteins, detoxification.
- Bile has no enzymes; bile salts emulsify fats. CCK contracts the gall bladder.
- Pancreatic juice is alkaline; trypsinogen is activated by enterokinase.
- Fats are absorbed into lacteals as chylomicrons; sugars and amino acids into blood.
- Large intestine absorbs water; gut bacteria make vitamin K.

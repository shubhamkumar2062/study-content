# Unit 5: Respiratory system

Syllabus chapter 8: anatomy of the respiratory organs and their functions; regulation and mechanism of respiration; respiratory volumes and capacities (definitions).

**Respiration** has four parts: (1) **pulmonary ventilation** – breathing air in and out; (2) **external respiration** – gas exchange between alveoli and blood; (3) **transport** of gases in blood; (4) **internal respiration** – gas exchange between blood and tissue cells. (Cellular respiration – using O2 to make ATP – happens inside cells.)

## 5.1 Organs of the respiratory system

```text
              Respiratory tract (front view)

     nasal cavity ──┐
     pharynx ───────┤   UPPER RESPIRATORY TRACT
     larynx ────────┘   (voice box)
        |
     trachea  (C-shaped cartilage rings)
        |
   _____|_____  <- carina (division)
  |           |
 right       left        primary (main) bronchi

  |  \         /  |

                    → respiratory bronchioles → alveolar ducts
                    → ALVEOLI (gas exchange)

  RIGHT LUNG: 3 lobes          LEFT LUNG: 2 lobes + cardiac notch
  (upper, middle, lower)       (upper, lower)

```

| Organ | Key features | Functions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nose and nasal cavity** | Divided by the nasal septum; lined by ciliated mucous membrane; hairs at the entrance; conchae increase surface; olfactory area in the roof | Filters, warms and moistens air; sense of smell; resonance of voice |
| **Pharynx** | Muscular tube behind nose, mouth and larynx: nasopharynx (adenoids), oropharynx (tonsils), laryngopharynx | Common passage for air and food; tonsils help defence |
| **Larynx** | Made of cartilages: thyroid ("Adam's apple"), cricoid, epiglottis, arytenoids; contains the **vocal cords** | Voice production; passage of air; **epiglottis** closes the larynx during swallowing |
| **Trachea** | About 10–12 cm long; 16–20 **C-shaped hyaline cartilage rings** open at the back; lined by pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium with goblet cells | Keeps the airway open; mucus traps dust and cilia sweep it up (mucociliary escalator); cough reflex |
| **Bronchi and bronchioles** | Trachea divides into right (shorter, wider, more vertical – inhaled objects usually go here) and left bronchi; branching "bronchial tree"; bronchioles have no cartilage but have smooth muscle | Conduct air; smooth muscle controls airway size (narrowed in asthma) |
| **Alveoli** | About 300 million tiny air sacs; wall of simple squamous cells (type I) + type II cells making **surfactant**; covered by capillaries; alveolar macrophages | **Gas exchange** |
| **Lungs** | Cone-shaped, spongy organs in the thoracic cavity; apex above the clavicle, base on the diaphragm; hilum where bronchus, vessels and nerves enter | Gas exchange; also help acid–base balance |
| **Pleura** | Double serous membrane: parietal (lines chest wall) and visceral (covers lung); pleural cavity with a thin film of fluid | Reduces friction; surface tension holds lungs to the chest wall |

**Respiratory membrane:** alveolar wall + fused basement membranes + capillary wall – only about 0.5 µm thick, with a huge total area (roughly the size of a tennis court), so gases diffuse quickly.

**Surfactant** (a lipoprotein from type II alveolar cells) lowers surface tension and stops the alveoli from collapsing. Premature babies lacking it develop **respiratory distress syndrome**.

**Muscles of breathing:** **diaphragm** (main muscle) and **external intercostal muscles** for inspiration; internal intercostals and abdominal muscles for forced expiration; neck muscles (sternocleidomastoid, scalenes) help in deep inspiration.

## 5.2 Mechanism of breathing (pulmonary ventilation)

Air moves from high pressure to low pressure. Changing the size of the thorax changes the pressure in the lungs (**Boyle's law**: at constant temperature, pressure of a gas is inversely related to its volume).

```text
  INSPIRATION (active)                    EXPIRATION (quiet: passive)
  Diaphragm contracts → flattens (down)   Diaphragm relaxes → domes up
  External intercostals contract          External intercostals relax
  → ribs and sternum move up and out      → ribs move down and in
  Thoracic volume ↑                       Thoracic volume ↓
  Intrapulmonary pressure ↓ below         Intrapulmonary pressure ↑ above
  atmospheric                             atmospheric
  AIR FLOWS IN                            AIR FLOWS OUT
                                          (lung elastic recoil helps)
```

- **Quiet expiration is passive** – due to elastic recoil of the lungs and chest wall.
- **Forced expiration** (coughing, blowing) is active – internal intercostals and abdominal muscles contract.
- **Intrapleural pressure** is always slightly below atmospheric (negative), which keeps the lungs expanded. Air entering the pleural cavity (**pneumothorax**) lets the lung collapse.

**Compliance** = how easily the lungs stretch (low in fibrosis). **Airway resistance** rises when bronchioles narrow (asthma).

## 5.3 Gas exchange and transport

Gases move by **diffusion** from higher to lower partial pressure.

| Site | PO2 (mmHg) | PCO2 (mmHg) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alveolar air | About 104 | About 40 |
| Blood entering lung (venous) | About 40 | About 45 |
| Blood leaving lung (arterial) | About 95–100 | About 40 |
| Tissue cells | About 40 or less | About 45 or more |

So O2 moves from alveoli → blood → tissues, and CO2 moves the opposite way.

**Transport of O2:** about **97 %** as **oxyhaemoglobin**, about 3 % dissolved in plasma. Each Hb molecule carries 4 O2. Release of O2 to tissues increases with high CO2, low pH, higher temperature (the Bohr effect).

**Transport of CO2:** about **70 % as bicarbonate (HCO3−)** in plasma (formed in RBCs by **carbonic anhydrase**: CO2 + H2O ⇌ H2CO3 ⇌ H+ + HCO3−; HCO3− leaves the RBC in exchange for Cl− – the **chloride shift**), about 23 % as **carbaminohaemoglobin**, about 7 % dissolved.

## 5.4 Regulation of respiration

Breathing is automatic but can be changed voluntarily for a short time.

**A. Nervous (respiratory centres in the brainstem)**

| Centre | Location | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Medullary rhythmicity area** – dorsal respiratory group (inspiratory) and ventral respiratory group (used in forced breathing) | Medulla oblongata | Sets the basic rhythm of breathing |
| **Pneumotaxic centre** | Upper pons | Switches off inspiration; controls rate (shorter, faster breaths) |
| **Apneustic centre** | Lower pons | Prolongs inspiration |

**B. Chemical regulation**

- **Central chemoreceptors** in the medulla respond to **CO2 / H+** in the cerebrospinal fluid. A rise in arterial **PCO2 is the most powerful stimulus** for breathing.
- **Peripheral chemoreceptors** – **carotid bodies** (at the fork of the common carotid artery; signal via the glossopharyngeal nerve) and **aortic bodies** (vagus nerve) – respond mainly to a fall in **PO2** (below about 60 mmHg), and also to high CO2 and H+.

```text
  CO2 ↑ / H+ ↑ / O2 ↓
        ↓
  Chemoreceptors (central + peripheral)
        ↓
  Medullary respiratory centre
        ↓  (phrenic nerve → diaphragm; intercostal nerves)
  Rate and depth of breathing ↑  → CO2 washed out, O2 restored
```

**C. Reflexes and other factors**

- **Hering–Breuer (inflation) reflex** – stretch receptors in the lungs send impulses by the vagus to stop over-inflation.
- **Irritant reflexes** – coughing, sneezing.
- Higher centres – speech, emotion, voluntary breath-holding; body temperature, pain, exercise.

## 5.5 Respiratory volumes and capacities

Measured with a **spirometer**. Values below are typical for a healthy young adult male (lower in females).

```text
  mL
 5800 ┬───────────────────────────────────────
      │  IRV  ~3000              ┐       ┐
      │                          │ IC    │
 2800 ┤ ~~~~ TV ~500 ~~~~        ┘ 3500  │ VC
 2300 ┤  (quiet breathing)               │ ~4600   TLC
      │  ERV  ~1100              ┐       ┘        ~5800
 1200 ┤                          │ FRC
      │  RV   ~1200              │ ~2300
    0 ┴──────────────────────────┘
```

**Volumes**

| Volume | Definition | Normal (approx.) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tidal volume (TV)** | Air breathed in or out in one quiet breath | 500 mL |
| **Inspiratory reserve volume (IRV)** | Extra air that can be breathed in by forceful inspiration after a normal inspiration | 3000 mL |
| **Expiratory reserve volume (ERV)** | Extra air that can be breathed out by forceful expiration after a normal expiration | 1100 mL |
| **Residual volume (RV)** | Air left in the lungs after the most forceful expiration (cannot be breathed out) | 1200 mL |

**Capacities** (sum of two or more volumes)

| Capacity | Formula | Normal (approx.) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Inspiratory capacity (IC)** | TV + IRV | 3500 mL |
| **Functional residual capacity (FRC)** | ERV + RV | 2300 mL |
| **Vital capacity (VC)** | TV + IRV + ERV (largest amount breathed out after the deepest breath in) | 4600 mL |
| **Total lung capacity (TLC)** | VC + RV | 5800 mL |

**Other terms**

- **Respiratory rate:** 12–20 breaths per minute in adults (higher in infants).
- **Minute (pulmonary) ventilation** = TV × respiratory rate ≈ 500 × 12 = 6 L/min.
- **Anatomical dead space** ≈ 150 mL – air in the conducting passages that does not reach alveoli.
- **Alveolar ventilation** = (TV − dead space) × rate ≈ (500 − 150) × 12 ≈ 4.2 L/min.
- **Forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1)** – normally about 80 % of the forced vital capacity; reduced in asthma and COPD.
- **Peak expiratory flow** – the fastest rate of air blown out (see practical guide).
- **Vital capacity is greater** in athletes and men, and is reduced in lung diseases, pregnancy and old age.

## 5.6 Terms and common disorders

- **Eupnoea** – normal quiet breathing; **apnoea** – stopped breathing; **dyspnoea** – difficult breathing; **tachypnoea** – fast breathing; **hypoxia** – low O2 in tissues; **cyanosis** – blue skin from much deoxygenated Hb.
- **Asthma** – reversible narrowing of bronchioles (smooth muscle spasm, mucus, inflammation); wheeze. Treated with bronchodilators (salbutamol) and inhaled steroids.
- **COPD** (chronic bronchitis, emphysema) – mostly smoking; emphysema destroys alveolar walls.
- **Pneumonia** – infection filling alveoli with fluid.
- **Tuberculosis** – *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* infection of the lungs.

## Remember

- Path of air: nose → pharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli.
- Right lung 3 lobes, left lung 2 lobes (cardiac notch).
- Trachea has C-shaped rings; epiglottis closes the larynx during swallowing.
- Surfactant (type II cells) prevents alveolar collapse.
- Inspiration is active (diaphragm + external intercostals); quiet expiration is passive.
- O2 travels mainly as oxyhaemoglobin; CO2 mainly as bicarbonate.
- Medulla sets rhythm; pneumotaxic centre (pons) limits inspiration.
- CO2 is the strongest stimulus to breathe (central chemoreceptors).
- TV 500, IRV 3000, ERV 1100, RV 1200 mL; VC = TV + IRV + ERV; TLC = VC + RV.

# Unit 8: Sense organs

Syllabus chapter 12: anatomy and physiology of the eye, ear, skin, tongue and nose.

Sense organs contain **receptors** that change a stimulus (light, sound, touch, chemicals) into nerve impulses, which travel to the brain where the sensation is felt.

| Sense | Organ | Receptor type | Cranial nerve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vision | Eye | Photoreceptors (rods, cones) | II (optic) |
| Hearing and balance | Ear | Mechanoreceptors (hair cells) | VIII (vestibulocochlear) |
| Touch, pain, temperature | Skin | Mechano-, thermo-, nociceptors | Spinal and trigeminal nerves |
| Taste (gustation) | Tongue | Chemoreceptors (taste buds) | VII, IX (X) |
| Smell (olfaction) | Nose | Chemoreceptors (olfactory cells) | I (olfactory) |

## 8.1 The eye

### Accessory structures

- **Eyebrows and eyelashes** – keep out sweat and dust.
- **Eyelids** – protect; blinking spreads tears.
- **Conjunctiva** – thin mucous membrane lining the lids and covering the white of the eye (inflammation = conjunctivitis).
- **Lacrimal apparatus** – lacrimal gland (upper outer part of the orbit) makes **tears**, which drain through the lacrimal canals and nasolacrimal duct into the nose. Tears moisten and clean the eye and contain **lysozyme** (antibacterial).
- **Six extrinsic eye muscles** – move the eyeball (supplied by cranial nerves III, IV, VI).

   _____    |   humour  ____/  \____   HUMOUR           |  FOVEA (in macula
  (     )   |  (ant.   |   (o)     |   (jelly)          |  = sharpest vision)
  (  pupil  |  chamber)|___ \__/ __|                    |  BLIND SPOT (optic disc)

The wall has **three layers (tunics):**

| Layer | Parts | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Outer fibrous** | **Sclera** (white, tough) behind; **cornea** (transparent, no blood vessels) in front | Protects and shapes the eye; cornea bends (refracts) light most |
| **Middle vascular (uvea)** | **Choroid** (blood vessels, dark pigment), **ciliary body** (ciliary muscle + processes), **iris** (coloured, with the pupil) | Nourishes; absorbs stray light; ciliary muscle changes lens shape; ciliary processes make aqueous humour; iris controls pupil size |
| **Inner nervous** | **Retina** – photoreceptors (rods and cones), bipolar cells, ganglion cells | Converts light into nerve impulses |

**Contents (refracting media):**

- **Aqueous humour** – watery fluid in front of the lens; made by the ciliary processes and drained through the **canal of Schlemm**; nourishes cornea and lens; maintains pressure (normal intraocular pressure about 10–21 mmHg). Poor drainage raises pressure → **glaucoma**.
- **Lens** – transparent, biconvex, elastic; held by suspensory ligaments; focuses light on the retina. Clouding = **cataract**.
- **Vitreous humour** – clear jelly behind the lens; keeps the eyeball's shape and holds the retina in place.

**Rods and cones**

| Rods | Cones |
| --- | --- |
| About 120 million; mostly at the periphery | About 6–7 million; most dense in the **fovea** |
| Work in **dim light** (night vision) | Work in **bright light** |
| Black-and-white vision | **Colour vision** (red, green, blue types) |
| Pigment **rhodopsin** (needs **vitamin A**) | Pigments iodopsins |

- **Macula lutea** with the **fovea centralis** – only cones; sharpest vision.
- **Optic disc (blind spot)** – where the optic nerve leaves; no rods or cones, so no image is seen there.

### Physiology of vision

1. Light passes through **cornea → aqueous humour → pupil → lens → vitreous humour → retina**.
2. The cornea and lens **refract** the light to form a real, **inverted**, smaller image on the retina.
3. Light breaks down rhodopsin in rods (and pigments in cones) → nerve impulses in the photoreceptors → bipolar cells → ganglion cells → **optic nerve**.
4. Optic nerves cross partly at the **optic chiasma** → optic tracts → thalamus → **visual cortex in the occipital lobe**, where the image is seen upright and in 3D.

- **Accommodation** – focusing on near objects: the ciliary muscle contracts, suspensory ligaments slacken, the lens becomes more convex; at the same time the pupil constricts and the eyes converge. For distant objects the ciliary muscle relaxes and the lens flattens.
- **Pupillary light reflex** – bright light → pupil constricts (parasympathetic, circular muscle of iris); dim light → pupil dilates (sympathetic, radial muscle).
- **Night blindness** – vitamin A deficiency (less rhodopsin).

**Defects of vision**

| Defect | Problem | Correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Myopia** (short sight) | Eyeball too long; image forms in front of the retina; distant objects blurred | Concave lens |
| **Hypermetropia** (long sight) | Eyeball too short; image behind the retina; near objects blurred | Convex lens |
| **Presbyopia** | Lens loses elasticity with age; poor near vision | Convex (reading) lens |
| **Astigmatism** | Uneven curvature of cornea | Cylindrical lens |
| **Cataract** | Opaque lens | Surgery |
| **Glaucoma** | Raised pressure damages the optic nerve | Drops that lower pressure (e.g. timolol, pilocarpine) |

## 8.2 The ear

The ear has two jobs: **hearing** and **balance (equilibrium)**.

```text
                         EAR (section)

  OUTER EAR          |  MIDDLE EAR           |  INNER EAR
                     |  (air-filled)         |  (fluid-filled)
   pinna             |   malleus incus       |   semicircular canals (balance)
    ___              |      \    /  stapes   |       ( ) ( ) ( )
   (   \__ external  |     [M]--[I]--[S]-----|--oval window
   (    __ auditory  |                       |   vestibule (utricle, saccule)
   (___/   canal ===|| tympanic membrane     |   COCHLEA (snail-shaped,
           (wax)    ||                       |    organ of Corti = hearing)
                     |   Eustachian tube     |   round window
                     |   → to nasopharynx    |   → vestibulocochlear nerve (VIII)
```

### Parts of the ear

| Part | Structures | Functions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **External ear** | **Pinna (auricle)** of elastic cartilage; **external auditory canal** (about 2.5 cm) with ceruminous glands (wax) and hairs | Collects and directs sound waves; wax and hairs protect |
| **Middle ear** (tympanic cavity in the temporal bone) | **Tympanic membrane (eardrum)**; three **ossicles** – **malleus** (hammer), **incus** (anvil), **stapes** (stirrup – smallest bone of the body, fits the oval window); **Eustachian (auditory) tube** to the nasopharynx | Transmits and amplifies vibrations; Eustachian tube equalises air pressure on both sides of the eardrum |
| **Internal ear** (labyrinth) | Bony labyrinth filled with **perilymph** enclosing a membranous labyrinth filled with **endolymph**: **cochlea**, **vestibule** (utricle and saccule), **three semicircular canals** | Cochlea – hearing; vestibule and semicircular canals – balance |

**Cochlea:** spiral tube of about 2¾ turns with three channels (scala vestibuli, scala media, scala tympani). The **organ of Corti** on the basilar membrane carries **hair cells** – the receptors for hearing.

### Physiology of hearing

```text
 Sound waves → pinna → external canal → tympanic membrane vibrates
   → malleus → incus → stapes (amplify) → oval window
   → waves in perilymph and endolymph of the cochlea
   → basilar membrane vibrates → hair cells of organ of Corti bend
   → nerve impulses in cochlear nerve (VIII)
   → auditory cortex (temporal lobe) → sound is heard
   (round window bulges out to release the pressure)
```

- High-pitched sounds stimulate the base of the cochlea; low-pitched sounds the apex.
- Humans hear roughly 20–20,000 Hz.

### Physiology of balance

- **Utricle and saccule** (each with a **macula**: hair cells under a gel with tiny calcium carbonate crystals, **otoliths**) – sense **static equilibrium** (head position) and straight-line acceleration.
- **Semicircular canals** (three, at right angles to each other) – each has an **ampulla** with a **crista ampullaris**; endolymph movement bends the hair cells – sense **rotation of the head (dynamic equilibrium)**.
- Impulses go by the vestibular nerve to the brainstem and **cerebellum**, which adjust posture and eye movements.
- Disorders: motion sickness, vertigo, deafness (conductive or nerve), otitis media (middle-ear infection, common in children).

## 8.3 The skin

The skin (integument) is the **largest organ** of the body (about 1.5–2 m² area).

```text
                     SKIN (section)

    hair shaft   sweat pore
       |            |
  _____|____________|_______________  EPIDERMIS (stratified squamous
 |  stratum corneum (dead keratin)  |   keratinised; no blood vessels)
 |  ... granulosum, spinosum        |
 |  stratum basale (dividing cells, |
 |  MELANOCYTES)                    |
 |=====================================  dermal papillae
 |  DERMIS (connective tissue):         - blood vessels, nerves
 |   - sebaceous gland → into follicle  - Meissner's corpuscles (touch)
 |   - hair follicle + arrector pili    - free nerve endings (pain, temp)
 |   - sweat gland (coiled)             - Pacinian corpuscles (deep
 |_____________________________________   pressure, vibration)
 |  HYPODERMIS (subcutaneous fat)    |
```

**Layers**

1. **Epidermis** – outer stratified squamous keratinised epithelium; no blood vessels. Layers from deep to surface: stratum **basale** (germinativum – dividing cells and **melanocytes** making the pigment **melanin**), spinosum, granulosum, lucidum (thick skin of palms and soles only), **corneum** (dead flat cells full of **keratin**, constantly shed).
2. **Dermis** – dense connective tissue with collagen and elastic fibres, blood vessels, lymphatics, nerves, sensory receptors, hair follicles, sebaceous and sweat glands.
3. **Hypodermis (subcutaneous layer)** – fat and loose tissue (not strictly part of the skin) – insulation, energy store; site for subcutaneous injections.

**Appendages:** hair (keratin; arrector pili muscle causes "goose bumps"), nails, **sebaceous glands** (oily **sebum** – keeps hair and skin soft; blocked glands → acne), **sweat glands** – eccrine (all over, temperature control) and apocrine (armpit, groin; odour after bacterial action).

**Sensory receptors in the skin:** Meissner's corpuscles (light touch), Pacinian corpuscles (pressure, vibration), Merkel discs (touch), Ruffini endings (stretch), **free nerve endings** (pain and temperature).

**Functions of the skin**

1. **Protection** – barrier against microbes, chemicals, injury, water loss; melanin protects from UV rays.
2. **Temperature regulation** – sweating and changes in skin blood flow.
3. **Sensation** – touch, pressure, pain, heat and cold.
4. **Vitamin D synthesis** – sunlight (UV) converts 7-dehydrocholesterol in the skin.
5. **Excretion** – small amounts of salts, urea in sweat.
6. **Absorption** – some fat-soluble substances and drugs (ointments, **transdermal patches** such as nitroglycerin, nicotine).
7. **Storage** – fat in the hypodermis.
8. Immunity – Langerhans cells in the epidermis.

## 8.4 The tongue (taste)

- Muscular organ in the floor of the mouth covered by mucous membrane with projections called **papillae**:
  - **Filiform** – most numerous, no taste buds (give a rough surface).
  - **Fungiform** – mushroom-shaped, on the tip and sides, some taste buds.
  - **Circumvallate (vallate)** – largest, 8–12 in a V-shaped row at the back, many taste buds.
  - **Foliate** – on the sides at the back.
- **Taste buds** contain **gustatory (taste) receptor cells** with taste hairs projecting through a taste pore, plus supporting cells.
- **Five basic tastes:** sweet, sour, salty, bitter, **umami** (savoury, e.g. glutamate). Each taste can be sensed over most of the tongue (the old "tongue map" is not accurate).
- **Physiology:** substances must **dissolve in saliva** → bind receptors → impulses through the **facial nerve (VII, front two-thirds)** and **glossopharyngeal nerve (IX, back third)** (and vagus from the throat) → medulla → thalamus → **gustatory cortex**.
- **Functions of the tongue:** taste, chewing and mixing food with saliva, swallowing, speech, cleaning the teeth.
- Much of "flavour" comes from smell; a blocked nose dulls taste.

## 8.5 The nose (smell)

- The **olfactory epithelium** lies in the roof of the nasal cavity (a small area of a few cm²). It contains **olfactory receptor cells** (bipolar neurons with cilia in the mucus), supporting cells and basal (stem) cells. Olfactory receptor cells are one of the few neurons that are replaced throughout life.
- **Physiology:** odour molecules **dissolve in nasal mucus** → bind receptors on the olfactory hairs → impulses travel through the cribriform plate of the ethmoid bone in the **olfactory nerve (I)** → **olfactory bulb** → olfactory tract → **olfactory cortex in the temporal lobe** and limbic system (so smell is linked to memory and emotion). Smell does not pass through the thalamus first.
- Receptors **adapt quickly** – a constant smell soon stops being noticed.
- **Other functions of the nose:** passage of air, filtering, warming and moistening air, voice resonance.
- Loss of smell = **anosmia** (colds, head injury, some viral infections).

## Remember

- Eye wall: fibrous (sclera, cornea), vascular (choroid, ciliary body, iris), nervous (retina).
- Rods = dim light (rhodopsin, vitamin A); cones = colour, concentrated in the fovea; optic disc = blind spot.
- Accommodation for near vision: ciliary muscle contracts, lens bulges.
- Myopia → concave lens; hypermetropia → convex lens; glaucoma = raised eye pressure; cataract = opaque lens.
- Ear: outer (pinna, canal), middle (eardrum, malleus–incus–stapes, Eustachian tube), inner (cochlea, vestibule, semicircular canals).
- Organ of Corti = hearing; semicircular canals = rotation; utricle and saccule = head position.
- Skin = largest organ; epidermis (melanin, keratin), dermis (glands, receptors); makes vitamin D.
- Taste buds: VII (front two-thirds), IX (back third); five tastes including umami.
- Smell: olfactory nerve (I) through the cribriform plate to the olfactory bulb.

# Unit 1: Scope of anatomy and physiology, cell and tissues

Syllabus chapters 1–3: scope of anatomy and physiology and basic terms; structure of the cell, its components and their functions; the four basic tissues (epithelial, connective, muscular, nervous), their sub-types and characteristics.

## 1.1 Scope of anatomy and physiology

**Anatomy** is the study of the **structure** of the body and the relation of its parts to each other. **Physiology** is the study of how the body and its parts **work** (function). Structure and function go together: the thin wall of an alveolus suits gas exchange; the thick wall of the left ventricle suits pumping blood round the whole body.

**Branches of anatomy**

| Branch | What it studies |
| --- | --- |
| Gross (macroscopic) anatomy | Structures seen with the naked eye, e.g. heart, bones |
| Microscopic anatomy | Cells (cytology) and tissues (histology) under a microscope |
| Developmental anatomy / embryology | Growth from fertilised egg to adult |
| Systemic anatomy | Body system by system (skeletal, nervous …) |
| Regional anatomy | Body region by region (head, thorax, limbs) |
| Surface anatomy | Landmarks seen or felt on the skin |
| Pathological anatomy | Structural changes caused by disease |

**Branches of physiology:** cell physiology, neurophysiology, cardiovascular physiology, respiratory physiology, endocrinology, renal physiology, exercise physiology, pathophysiology (how disease disturbs normal function).

**Why a pharmacist needs it:** drugs act on organs and cells; knowing where a drug is absorbed (GIT), carried (blood), acted on (receptors, nerves, hormones) and removed (liver, kidney) is the base of pharmacology. Counselling patients on BP, blood sugar, inhalers or eye drops also needs this knowledge.

## 1.2 Levels of organisation and homeostasis

```text
Chemical level (atoms, molecules: water, proteins, DNA)
        ↓
Cellular level (cell = basic unit of life)
        ↓
Tissue level (similar cells doing one job)
        ↓
Organ level (two or more tissues, e.g. stomach)
        ↓
System level (organs working together, e.g. digestive system)
        ↓
Organism (the whole human body)
```

**Homeostasis** is keeping the internal environment (body temperature, blood pH, blood glucose, BP, water and salt balance) nearly constant even when the outside changes.

A control system has three parts:

1. **Receptor** – senses the change (e.g. thermoreceptors in skin).
2. **Control centre** – compares it with the set point (e.g. hypothalamus).
3. **Effector** – makes the correction (e.g. sweat glands, blood vessels).

- **Negative feedback** (most common): the response reverses the change. Example: blood glucose rises → insulin released → glucose falls back. Body temperature rises → sweating and skin vasodilation → temperature falls.
- **Positive feedback** (rare): the response increases the change until an event ends it. Examples: uterine contractions during childbirth (oxytocin), blood clotting, LH surge before ovulation.

**Homeostatic imbalance** means the control fails, leading to disease, e.g. diabetes mellitus (glucose not controlled), hypertension.

## 1.3 Basic anatomical terms

**Anatomical position:** body erect, facing forward, feet together, arms by the sides with palms facing forward. All directions are described from this position.

| Term | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Superior (cranial) | Towards the head | Heart is superior to the stomach |
| Inferior (caudal) | Towards the feet | Kidneys are inferior to the diaphragm |
| Anterior (ventral) | Front | Sternum is anterior to the heart |
| Posterior (dorsal) | Back | Vertebral column is posterior |
| Medial | Towards the midline | Nose is medial to the eyes |
| Lateral | Away from the midline | Ears are lateral to the eyes |
| Proximal | Nearer the trunk / point of attachment | Elbow is proximal to the wrist |
| Distal | Farther from the trunk | Fingers are distal to the wrist |
| Superficial | Nearer the surface | Skin is superficial to muscle |
| Deep | Away from the surface | Bone is deep to muscle |
| Ipsilateral / contralateral | Same side / opposite side | — |

**Body planes**

- **Sagittal plane** – vertical, divides into right and left (mid-sagittal = equal halves).
- **Frontal (coronal) plane** – vertical, divides into anterior and posterior.
- **Transverse (horizontal) plane** – divides into superior and inferior parts.

**Body cavities**

- **Dorsal cavity:** cranial cavity (brain) and vertebral canal (spinal cord).
- **Ventral cavity:** thoracic cavity (lungs, heart) separated by the **diaphragm** from the abdominopelvic cavity (stomach, liver, intestines, kidneys, bladder, reproductive organs).

## 1.4 Structure of the cell

The **cell** is the smallest structural and functional unit of life. The body has trillions of cells of over 200 types (nerve cells, muscle cells, red cells …), but all share a common plan: **cell membrane, cytoplasm with organelles, and nucleus**.

### 1.4.1 Cell membrane (plasma membrane)

- **Fluid mosaic model:** a **phospholipid bilayer** (hydrophilic heads outside, hydrophobic tails inside) with proteins floating in it, plus cholesterol and carbohydrate chains (glycocalyx).
- **Proteins:** channels, carriers, pumps (Na+/K+ ATPase), receptors for hormones and drugs, enzymes, identity markers.
- **Functions:** encloses the cell, **selectively permeable** (controls entry and exit), cell recognition, receives signals.

**Transport across the membrane**

| Type | Energy | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple diffusion (high → low concentration) | Passive | O2, CO2, lipid-soluble drugs |
| Facilitated diffusion (carrier or channel) | Passive | Glucose into most cells |
| Osmosis (water through a membrane) | Passive | Water moving into a cell in hypotonic fluid |
| Active transport (against gradient) | ATP | Na+/K+ pump: 3 Na+ out, 2 K+ in |
| Endocytosis (phagocytosis, pinocytosis) | ATP | Neutrophils swallowing bacteria |
| Exocytosis | ATP | Release of hormones, neurotransmitters |

### 1.4.2 Cytoplasm and organelles

| Organelle | Structure | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nucleus | Largest organelle; double nuclear membrane with pores; contains chromatin (DNA + protein) and a nucleolus | Controls all cell activity; stores genetic information; site of DNA replication and RNA synthesis |
| Nucleolus | Dense body inside nucleus | Makes ribosomal RNA, assembles ribosomes |
| Mitochondria | Double membrane; inner membrane folded into cristae; own DNA | Aerobic respiration, **ATP production** – "power house of the cell" |
| Ribosomes | Small granules of RNA + protein, free or on rough ER | **Protein synthesis** |
| Rough endoplasmic reticulum | Membrane channels studded with ribosomes | Makes proteins for export and for membranes |
| Smooth endoplasmic reticulum | Channels without ribosomes | Lipid and steroid synthesis, detoxification of drugs (liver), stores Ca2+ in muscle (sarcoplasmic reticulum) |
| Golgi apparatus | Stack of flattened sacs | Modifies, packs and sends out proteins in vesicles; forms lysosomes |
| Lysosomes | Membrane sacs with digestive enzymes | Digest worn-out organelles and engulfed bacteria – "suicide bags" |
| Peroxisomes | Small sacs with oxidase and catalase | Break down fatty acids, destroy H2O2 |
| Centrosome (two centrioles) | Short cylinders of microtubules near nucleus | Form the spindle in cell division |
| Cytoskeleton | Microfilaments, microtubules, intermediate filaments | Shape, movement, transport inside cell |
| Cilia and flagella | Hair-like projections | Cilia move mucus in airways; flagellum moves sperm |

### 1.4.3 Cell division (brief)

- **Mitosis** – one cell → two identical daughter cells with 46 chromosomes; for growth and repair. Stages: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase.
- **Meiosis** – in gonads; gives four cells with 23 chromosomes (haploid) to form sperm and ova.

## 1.5 Tissues of the human body

A **tissue** is a group of similar cells (with the material between them) that work together for one function. **Histology** is the study of tissues. There are four basic tissues:

```text
                    Tissues
   ______________________|______________________
  |               |                |            |
Epithelial    Connective       Muscular      Nervous
(covering)    (support)        (movement)    (control)
```

## 1.6 Epithelial tissue

**Characteristics:** cells packed close together with very little matrix; rest on a **basement membrane**; no blood vessels (avascular – nourished by diffusion); good power of regeneration; one free surface.

**Functions:** protection, absorption, secretion, excretion, sensation, filtration.

### Simple epithelium (one layer)

| Type | Shape | Location | Function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Simple squamous | Flat, tile-like | Alveoli, lining of blood vessels (endothelium), Bowman's capsule | Diffusion, filtration |
| Simple cuboidal | Cube-shaped | Kidney tubules, thyroid follicles, gland ducts | Secretion, absorption |
| Simple columnar | Tall | Stomach, intestine (with goblet cells and microvilli) | Absorption, mucus secretion |
| Ciliated columnar | Tall with cilia | Uterine tubes, small bronchi | Moves ova / mucus |
| Pseudostratified ciliated columnar | Looks layered but is one layer; nuclei at different levels | Trachea, bronchi | Traps and moves mucus |

### Compound (stratified) epithelium (many layers)

| Type | Location | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stratified squamous keratinised | Skin (epidermis) | Protection, waterproofing |
| Stratified squamous non-keratinised | Mouth, oesophagus, vagina, cornea | Protection against wear |
| Transitional epithelium | Urinary bladder, ureters | Stretches when bladder fills |

**Glandular epithelium:** forms glands. **Exocrine** glands pour secretion through ducts (sweat, salivary glands). **Endocrine** glands are ductless and release hormones into blood (thyroid, pituitary).

## 1.7 Connective tissue

**Characteristics:** cells widely scattered in a large amount of **matrix** (ground substance + fibres); usually rich blood supply (except cartilage); develops from mesoderm.

**Cells:** fibroblasts (make fibres), macrophages (phagocytosis), mast cells (histamine, heparin), plasma cells (antibodies), fat cells (adipocytes).

**Fibres:** collagen (white, strong), elastic (yellow, stretchy), reticular (fine supporting network).

| Type | Features | Location / function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Areolar (loose) | Loose fibres, many cells | Under skin, around organs; binds and cushions |
| Adipose | Fat cells | Under skin, around kidneys; energy store, insulation |
| Dense regular (white fibrous) | Parallel collagen bundles | **Tendons** (muscle to bone), **ligaments** (bone to bone) |
| Elastic | Mainly elastic fibres | Walls of large arteries, lungs |
| Reticular | Reticular fibres | Framework of spleen, lymph nodes, bone marrow |
| Cartilage – hyaline | Glassy, smooth | Ends of long bones, nose, trachea rings, costal cartilage |
| Cartilage – fibrocartilage | Collagen bundles, tough | Intervertebral discs, pubic symphysis |
| Cartilage – elastic | Elastic fibres | External ear, epiglottis |
| Bone (osseous tissue) | Hard matrix of calcium phosphate and collagen; osteocytes in lacunae | Skeleton; support, protection |
| Blood (fluid connective tissue) | Cells in liquid plasma | Transport, defence |

Cartilage has no blood vessels, so it heals slowly.

## 1.8 Muscular tissue

Made of long cells called **muscle fibres** that can contract.

| Feature | Skeletal | Cardiac | Smooth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Location | Attached to bones | Wall of the heart | Walls of stomach, intestine, blood vessels, bladder, uterus |
| Shape | Long cylindrical | Branched | Spindle-shaped |
| Striations | Present | Present | Absent |
| Nuclei | Many, peripheral | One (or two), central | One, central |
| Special feature | — | **Intercalated discs** | — |
| Control | Voluntary | Involuntary | Involuntary |
| Fatigue | Tires | Does not tire | Slow to tire |

## 1.9 Nervous tissue

- **Neurons** – carry nerve impulses. Parts: cell body (soma), dendrites (carry impulses towards the cell body), axon (carries impulses away), myelin sheath, axon terminals.
- **Neuroglia (glial cells)** – support, nourish and protect neurons; do not carry impulses. Examples: astrocytes, oligodendrocytes (make myelin in CNS), microglia (phagocytes), ependymal cells, Schwann cells (make myelin in PNS).

(The labelled neuron diagram is in Unit 7.)

**Membranes:** sheets of epithelium with connective tissue – mucous membrane (lines tracts open to outside), serous membrane (pleura, pericardium, peritoneum), synovial membrane (lines joint cavities), cutaneous membrane (skin).

## Remember

- Anatomy = structure, physiology = function; homeostasis is mostly by negative feedback.
- Anatomical position: standing, palms forward. Proximal/distal used for limbs.
- Mitochondria = ATP; ribosomes = protein; Golgi = packing; lysosomes = digestion; nucleus = control.
- Four tissues: epithelial (cover), connective (support), muscular (move), nervous (control).
- Tendon joins muscle to bone; ligament joins bone to bone.
- Cardiac muscle has intercalated discs; smooth muscle has no striations.
- Transitional epithelium lines the bladder; pseudostratified ciliated columnar lines the trachea.

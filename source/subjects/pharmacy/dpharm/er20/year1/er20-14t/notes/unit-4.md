# Unit 4: Cardiovascular system

Syllabus chapter 7: anatomy and physiology of the heart; blood vessels and circulation (pulmonary, coronary and systemic); cardiac cycle and heart sounds; basics of ECG; blood pressure and its regulation.

The cardiovascular system = **heart** (pump) + **blood vessels** (tubes) + **blood**. It carries O2, nutrients, hormones and heat to tissues and removes CO2 and wastes.

## 4.1 Position and coverings of the heart

- Hollow, cone-shaped, muscular organ about the size of a closed fist (about 250–300 g).
- Lies in the **mediastinum** of the thoracic cavity, between the lungs, behind the sternum, with about two-thirds to the left of the midline. The **apex** points down and to the left (about the 5th intercostal space); the broad **base** faces up and back.
- **Pericardium** – a double sac:
  - **Fibrous pericardium** (outer, tough; anchors heart, prevents over-filling).
  - **Serous pericardium**: parietal layer (lines fibrous layer) and visceral layer (epicardium, on the heart surface). Between them is the **pericardial cavity** with a little fluid to reduce friction.

**Layers of the heart wall**

1. **Epicardium** – outer (visceral pericardium).
2. **Myocardium** – thick middle layer of **cardiac muscle**; thickest in the left ventricle.
3. **Endocardium** – inner smooth lining, continuous with the lining of vessels; covers the valves.

## 4.2 Chambers, valves and great vessels

The heart has **four chambers**: two upper **atria** (receiving chambers) and two lower **ventricles** (pumping chambers). The right and left sides are separated by the **interatrial** and **interventricular septa**.

```text
                  INTERIOR OF THE HEART (front view, draw for 10 marks)

          superior vena cava        aorta (arch)     pulmonary trunk
                 |                    |  |               | |
                 |                    |  |      pulmonary veins (4)
            _____|_____          _____|__|____    _______|_|__
           |           |        |   pulmonary |  |            |
           |  RIGHT    |        |   semilunar |  |   LEFT     |
           |  ATRIUM   |<-SA    |   valve     |  |   ATRIUM   |
           |  (deoxy)  |  node  |_____________|  |   (oxy)    |
           |___________|                         |____________|
                 |  TRICUSPID valve                    |  MITRAL (bicuspid)
                 v  (3 cusps)                          v  valve (2 cusps)
           ______________________   |   _______________________
          |                      |  |  |                       |
          |   RIGHT VENTRICLE    |  |  |   LEFT VENTRICLE      |
          |   (thin wall,        |  |  |   (thickest wall,     |
          |    pumps to lungs)   |  |  |    pumps to body)     |
          |______________________|  |  |_______________________|
                 ^                  |               aortic semilunar valve
     inferior vena cava       interventricular        between LV and aorta
                               septum
          chordae tendineae + papillary muscles hold the AV valve cusps
```

| Chamber | Receives from | Sends to | Valve at exit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Right atrium | Superior and inferior venae cavae, coronary sinus (deoxygenated blood) | Right ventricle | **Tricuspid** (right AV valve) |
| Right ventricle | Right atrium | Lungs via **pulmonary trunk** | **Pulmonary semilunar** |
| Left atrium | Four **pulmonary veins** (oxygenated blood) | Left ventricle | **Mitral (bicuspid)** (left AV valve) |
| Left ventricle | Left atrium | Whole body via **aorta** | **Aortic semilunar** |

- The **left ventricle wall is about three times thicker** than the right because it pumps against the high pressure of the systemic circulation.
- **AV valves** are held by **chordae tendineae** attached to **papillary muscles**; they stop the cusps turning back into the atria during ventricular contraction.
- **Semilunar valves** (three half-moon cusps each) stop blood flowing back into the ventricles.
- Valves make blood flow **one way only**.

## 4.3 Conducting system of the heart

Cardiac muscle is **autorhythmic**: it makes its own impulses.

```text
  SA node (right atrium wall, near SVC) – PACEMAKER, about 70–80/min
        ↓  (spreads through both atria → atrial contraction)
  AV node (lower interatrial septum) – short delay (lets ventricles fill)
        ↓
  Bundle of His (AV bundle)
        ↓
  Right and left bundle branches (in the interventricular septum)
        ↓
  Purkinje fibres (ventricular walls) → ventricles contract from apex upward
```

**Nerve control:** sympathetic nerves (noradrenaline) increase heart rate and force; parasympathetic **vagus** nerve (acetylcholine) slows the heart. These act through the cardiac centre in the **medulla oblongata**.

**Cardiac output** = stroke volume × heart rate ≈ 70 mL × 72/min ≈ **5 litres per minute** at rest.

## 4.4 Blood vessels

| Feature | Artery | Vein | Capillary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Carries | Blood away from the heart | Blood towards the heart | Links arterioles and venules |
| Wall | Thick, muscular, elastic | Thin, less muscle | One layer of endothelium |
| Lumen | Narrow | Wide | Very narrow (about one RBC wide) |
| Valves | Absent (except at heart exits) | Present, especially in limbs | Absent |
| Pressure | High; pulse felt | Low | Low |
| Blood | Oxygenated (except pulmonary artery) | Deoxygenated (except pulmonary veins) | Exchange site |

**Wall layers of arteries and veins:** tunica **intima** (endothelium), tunica **media** (smooth muscle and elastic fibres – thick in arteries), tunica **adventitia** (externa; connective tissue).

- **Elastic arteries** (aorta) stretch with each beat and recoil, keeping flow smooth.
- **Arterioles** control resistance and BP by constricting or dilating.
- **Capillaries** are the site of exchange of gases, nutrients and wastes with tissue fluid.
- **Venous return** is helped by valves, the **skeletal muscle pump** (calf muscles) and breathing.

## 4.5 Circulation

```text
   Body tissues
       ↑ (systemic arteries)            ↓ (systemic veins)
     AORTA                          SVC + IVC
       ↑                                ↓
   LEFT VENTRICLE                  RIGHT ATRIUM
       ↑ mitral                         ↓ tricuspid
   LEFT ATRIUM                     RIGHT VENTRICLE
       ↑                                ↓
  PULMONARY VEINS (O2-rich)   PULMONARY ARTERY (O2-poor)
       ↑______________ LUNGS ___________↓
                (gas exchange)
```

**1. Pulmonary circulation (lesser circulation)** – right ventricle → pulmonary trunk → right and left pulmonary arteries → lung capillaries (CO2 out, O2 in) → four pulmonary veins → left atrium. Short, low-pressure circuit. Note: the **pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood** and the **pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood**.

**2. Systemic circulation (greater circulation)** – left ventricle → aorta → arteries → arterioles → capillaries of all organs → venules → veins → superior and inferior venae cavae → right atrium. Long, high-pressure circuit.

Main branches of the aorta: ascending aorta (gives the coronary arteries), arch of aorta (brachiocephalic trunk, left common carotid, left subclavian), descending thoracic and abdominal aorta (to gut, kidneys, legs).

Special circulation: the **hepatic portal circulation** carries blood from the stomach, intestines and spleen to the liver (portal vein) before it returns to the heart, so absorbed nutrients and drugs pass the liver first (first-pass metabolism).

**3. Coronary circulation** – supplies the heart muscle itself.

- **Right and left coronary arteries** arise from the base of the aorta just above the aortic valve.
- Left coronary artery → **anterior interventricular (left anterior descending)** and **circumflex** branches; right coronary artery → marginal and posterior interventricular branches.
- Venous blood collects into cardiac veins → **coronary sinus** → right atrium.
- Coronary arteries fill mainly during **diastole** (in systole the contracting muscle squeezes them).
- Narrowing by atherosclerosis causes **angina pectoris**; a complete block causes **myocardial infarction** (heart attack).

## 4.6 Cardiac cycle

The **cardiac cycle** is the series of events in one heartbeat – one contraction (**systole**) and one relaxation (**diastole**) of the chambers. At 75 beats per minute it lasts about **0.8 second**.

| Phase | Duration (approx.) | Events |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Atrial systole | 0.1 s | Atria contract, push the last part of blood into ventricles; AV valves open |
| Ventricular systole | 0.3 s | Ventricles contract; AV valves close (**first sound**); pressure rises (isovolumetric contraction), then semilunar valves open and blood is ejected (ejection) |
| Complete (joint) diastole | 0.4 s | Ventricles relax; semilunar valves close (**second sound**); pressure falls (isovolumetric relaxation); AV valves open and the ventricles fill passively |

- Atria are in diastole for about 0.7 s and ventricles for about 0.5 s of each 0.8 s cycle, giving time to rest and fill.
- About 70–80 % of ventricular filling is passive; atrial systole adds the rest.
- **Stroke volume** (blood ejected per beat per ventricle) ≈ 70 mL. End-diastolic volume ≈ 120–130 mL; end-systolic volume ≈ 50–60 mL.

## 4.7 Heart sounds

Heard with a stethoscope.

| Sound | Character | Cause | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **First (S1)** – "lubb" | Longer, low-pitched | Closure of **AV valves** (tricuspid and mitral) | Start of ventricular systole |
| **Second (S2)** – "dupp" | Shorter, sharper | Closure of **semilunar valves** (aortic and pulmonary) | Start of ventricular diastole |
| Third (S3) | Faint | Rapid ventricular filling | Early diastole (heard in some young people) |
| Fourth (S4) | Faint, usually not heard | Atrial contraction | Late diastole |

**Murmurs** are abnormal sounds caused by turbulent flow through narrowed (stenosed) or leaking (regurgitant) valves.

## 4.8 Basics of ECG

The **electrocardiogram (ECG)** is a recording of the electrical activity of the heart from electrodes on the skin. A standard ECG uses 12 leads.

```text
            R
            /\
           /  \
     P    /    \          T
    /\   /      \        /‾\
___/  \_Q        \_S____/   \______
   |<-PR->|<QRS>|<-ST->|
   |<--------- QT --------->|
```

| Part | What it shows | Normal duration (approx.) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P wave** | Atrial depolarisation | Up to 0.10 s |
| **PR interval** | Time for the impulse to pass from the atria to the ventricles (AV delay) | 0.12–0.20 s |
| **QRS complex** | Ventricular depolarisation (atrial repolarisation is hidden in it) | 0.08–0.10 s |
| **ST segment** | Ventricles fully depolarised | Isoelectric (flat) |
| **T wave** | Ventricular repolarisation | — |

**Uses:** finding heart rate and rhythm, arrhythmias, heart block (long PR), myocardial infarction (ST changes, Q waves), enlarged chambers, effect of drugs and electrolytes (e.g. tall T in high K+).

## 4.9 Blood pressure

**Blood pressure (BP)** is the pressure exerted by blood on the walls of the arteries.

- **Systolic pressure** – highest pressure during ventricular systole, about **120 mmHg**.
- **Diastolic pressure** – lowest pressure during diastole, about **80 mmHg**.
- Written as 120/80 mmHg.
- **Pulse pressure** = systolic − diastolic ≈ 40 mmHg.
- **Mean arterial pressure (MAP)** ≈ diastolic + ⅓ pulse pressure ≈ 93 mmHg.
- Measured with a **sphygmomanometer** and stethoscope in the brachial artery (Korotkoff sounds – see the practical guide).
- **Hypertension**: persistently 140/90 mmHg or higher. **Hypotension**: below about 90/60 mmHg.

**Factors affecting BP**

BP = **cardiac output × peripheral resistance**.

1. Cardiac output (heart rate, stroke volume).
2. Peripheral resistance (mainly arteriolar diameter).
3. Blood volume (bleeding lowers BP; salt and water retention raises it).
4. Elasticity of arteries (falls with age → systolic BP rises).
5. Viscosity of blood.
6. Others: age, sex, exercise, emotion, posture, body weight.

## 4.10 Regulation of blood pressure

**A. Short-term (nervous) – within seconds**

1. **Vasomotor centre and cardiac centre** in the **medulla oblongata** control heart rate and the tone of blood vessels through sympathetic and vagus nerves.
2. **Baroreceptor reflex:** stretch receptors in the **carotid sinus** and **aortic arch** sense BP.

```text
  BP rises → baroreceptors stretched → more impulses (IX, X nerves)
          → medulla: vagus activity ↑, sympathetic activity ↓
          → heart rate ↓, force ↓, vasodilation → BP falls back
  BP falls → the reverse: heart rate ↑, vasoconstriction → BP rises
```

3. **Chemoreceptors** (carotid and aortic bodies) sense low O2 / high CO2 and raise BP.
4. Higher centres (emotion, pain) act through the hypothalamus.

**B. Hormonal – minutes to hours**

| Hormone / system | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system** | Low BP → kidney releases renin → angiotensin II (strong vasoconstrictor) + aldosterone (Na+ and water retention) → BP rises |
| **ADH (vasopressin)** | Water retention by the kidney; vasoconstriction |
| **Adrenaline, noradrenaline** (adrenal medulla) | Raise heart rate and cause vasoconstriction |
| **Atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP)** | Released when atria are stretched; Na+ and water loss → BP falls |

**C. Long-term (renal)** – the kidney controls blood volume by changing the output of salt and water.

## 4.11 Common disorders (for short notes)

- **Hypertension** – raised BP; risk of stroke, heart attack, kidney damage.
- **Atherosclerosis** – fatty plaques in artery walls.
- **Angina pectoris** – chest pain from reduced coronary blood flow.
- **Myocardial infarction** – death of heart muscle from blocked coronary artery.
- **Heart failure** – heart cannot pump enough blood.
- **Arrhythmia** – abnormal heart rhythm.

## Remember

- Four chambers; LV wall thickest. Right side = deoxygenated, left side = oxygenated.
- Valves: tricuspid (right AV), mitral (left AV), pulmonary and aortic semilunar.
- Pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood; pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood.
- SA node = pacemaker → AV node → bundle of His → bundle branches → Purkinje fibres.
- Cardiac cycle 0.8 s: atrial systole 0.1, ventricular systole 0.3, diastole 0.4.
- S1 "lubb" = AV valves close; S2 "dupp" = semilunar valves close.
- ECG: P = atrial depolarisation, QRS = ventricular depolarisation, T = ventricular repolarisation.
- Normal BP 120/80 mmHg; pulse pressure 40; cardiac output ≈ 5 L/min.
- Baroreceptors in carotid sinus and aortic arch; RAAS and ADH raise BP; ANP lowers it.

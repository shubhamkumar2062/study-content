# How to use this guide

The ER-2020 syllabus has 15 chapters. Here they are grouped into 11 units, with related chapters kept together:

| Unit | Syllabus chapters |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 Scope and terms, 2 Cell, 3 Tissues |
| 2 | 4 Osseous system and joints, 10 Skeletal muscles |
| 3 | 5 Haemopoietic system, 6 Lymphatic system |
| 4 | 7 Cardiovascular system |
| 5 | 8 Respiratory system |
| 6 | 9 Digestive system |
| 7 | 11 Nervous system |
| 8 | 12 Sense organs |
| 9 | 13 Urinary system |
| 10 | 14 Endocrine system |
| 11 | 15 Reproductive system |

Study one unit a day: read the notes, draw every labelled diagram once from memory, then answer the question bank without looking. Spend more time on the heavy chapters – blood, heart, digestion, nervous system (8 hours each in the syllabus) – and on endocrine and sense organs (6 hours each).

## Exam pattern

The D.Pharm first-year theory paper under ER-2020 is a yearly board exam of **80 marks in 3 hours** (the other 20 marks are sessional/internal). The usual ER-2020 pattern is below. Confirm the pattern with your college, since boards sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective (multiple-choice) questions – answer all | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Short answers – answer any 10 of 11 | 10 × 3 = 30 |
| C | Long answers – answer any 6 of 7 | 6 × 5 = 30 |

In this pack:

- **MCQs** match Section A.
- **Short (3 marks)** questions match Section B – definitions, "write three functions", "what is …", "name …".
- **Long (5 marks)** questions match Section C. Some long questions cover a big topic (for example, heart structure with the conducting system); in the exam write the diagram plus the 6–8 most important points from the model answer.
- **Very-short (2 marks)** questions are quick one-line revision checks.

## Probability ratings

- **High** – asked in most years or a core diagram/definition; prepare the full answer.
- **Medium** – asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** – rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These are estimates from the syllabus weight and the kind of questions usually set in pharmacy diploma papers. They are not a promise.

## Scoring tips for Anatomy and Physiology

1. **Draw first.** A neat labelled diagram earns marks even before you write. Practise these until you can draw them in two minutes: cell, neuron, heart (interior), nephron, eye, ear, skin, villus, spinal cord section, reflex arc, synovial joint, long bone, female and male reproductive systems.
2. **Label with arrows and straight lines**, in pencil, on the right side of the figure. Write the title under the diagram.
3. **Learn normal values exactly**: Hb, RBC, WBC, platelets, DLC, BP 120/80 mmHg, heart rate 72/min, respiratory rate 12–20/min, GFR 125 mL/min, tidal volume 500 mL, blood glucose 70–100 mg/dL, body temperature 37 °C (98.6 °F). One correct value often gives the full MCQ mark.
4. **Use tables for comparisons**: artery vs vein, sympathetic vs parasympathetic, rods vs cones, skeletal vs cardiac vs smooth muscle, spermatogenesis vs oogenesis. Examiners mark tables quickly and kindly.
5. **Write steps as flow charts** for processes: clotting cascade, urine formation, RAAS, muscle contraction, hearing, menstrual cycle, parturition.
6. **3-mark answers:** definition + two or three points (or one small diagram). Don't write a page.
7. **5-mark answers:** heading, short definition, labelled diagram, 5–6 points, one disorder or clinical link. Six of these carry 30 marks, so practise finishing each in about 15 minutes.
8. **Link to pharmacy** in one line where you can (e.g. ACE inhibitors and RAAS, atropine and the pupil, anti-D in Rh incompatibility). It shows understanding.
9. **Spell key words correctly**: haemoglobin, oesophagus, glomerulus, parathormone, cerebellum.
10. Attempt all 20 MCQs; a blank answer is a sure zero. Read all four options before choosing – many options differ by one word (e.g. "afferent" and "efferent").

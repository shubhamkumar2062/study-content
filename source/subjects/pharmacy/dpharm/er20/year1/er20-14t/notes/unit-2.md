# Unit 2: Osseous system, joints and skeletal muscles

Syllabus chapters 4 and 10: structure and functions of the bones of the axial and appendicular skeleton; classification, types and movements of joints and disorders of joints; histology of skeletal muscle, physiology of muscle contraction and disorders of skeletal muscle.

## 2.1 Functions of the skeleton

1. **Support** – framework that holds the body and its organs.
2. **Protection** – skull protects brain, rib cage protects heart and lungs, vertebrae protect spinal cord.
3. **Movement** – bones act as levers; muscles pull on them across joints.
4. **Blood cell formation (haemopoiesis)** – in **red bone marrow**.
5. **Mineral store** – calcium and phosphate are stored and released as needed.
6. **Fat store** – yellow bone marrow.

## 2.2 Structure of bone

**Bone tissue** is a hard connective tissue: about two-thirds inorganic salts (mainly calcium phosphate as hydroxyapatite) giving hardness, and one-third organic matter (mainly collagen) giving some flexibility.

**Bone cells**

| Cell | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Osteoblasts | Bone-forming cells; lay down new matrix |
| Osteocytes | Mature bone cells trapped in lacunae; maintain bone |
| Osteoclasts | Large cells that dissolve (resorb) bone; help remodel bone and release Ca2+ |

**Types of bone tissue**

- **Compact bone** – dense, outer layer. Built of units called **Haversian systems (osteons)**: a central Haversian canal (blood vessels, nerve) surrounded by rings (lamellae) of matrix with osteocytes in lacunae joined by tiny canals (canaliculi).
- **Spongy (cancellous) bone** – a mesh of thin bars (trabeculae) with red marrow in the spaces; found in the ends of long bones and inside flat bones.

**Parts of a long bone (e.g. femur)**

```text
          Long bone (longitudinal section)

     ____________  <- articular (hyaline) cartilage
    /  spongy    \   PROXIMAL EPIPHYSIS (red marrow)
   |   bone       |
    \____________/   <- epiphyseal plate / line (growth)
      |  |    |  |
      |  |    |  |   DIAPHYSIS (shaft)
      |  |    |  |   - compact bone wall
      |  |    |  |   - medullary cavity (yellow marrow)
      |  |    |  |   - periosteum covers outside
      |  |    |  |   - endosteum lines cavity
     _|__|____|__|_
    /             \  DISTAL EPIPHYSIS
    \_____________/  <- articular cartilage
```

- **Diaphysis** – shaft, compact bone around the medullary cavity.
- **Epiphyses** – ends, spongy bone covered by a thin compact layer.
- **Epiphyseal (growth) plate** – cartilage where the bone grows in length; becomes the epiphyseal line in adults.
- **Periosteum** – tough fibrous membrane outside; has osteoblasts; needed for growth in thickness, repair and attachment of tendons.
- **Articular cartilage** – hyaline cartilage at joint surfaces.

**Classification of bones by shape**

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Long | Femur, humerus, radius, ulna, tibia, fibula, phalanges |
| Short | Carpals, tarsals |
| Flat | Skull bones, sternum, ribs, scapula |
| Irregular | Vertebrae, some facial bones |
| Sesamoid (in tendons) | Patella |

**Ossification** (bone formation): intramembranous (flat skull bones form directly in membrane) and endochondral (most bones; cartilage model replaced by bone).

## 2.3 The skeleton: 206 bones

### 2.3.1 Axial skeleton

**Skull (22 bones)**

- **Cranium (8):** frontal 1, parietal 2, temporal 2, occipital 1, sphenoid 1, ethmoid 1. Encloses the brain. Bones are joined by immovable **sutures**.
- **Face (14):** nasal 2, maxilla 2, zygomatic 2, lacrimal 2, palatine 2, inferior nasal conchae 2, vomer 1, mandible 1. The **mandible** is the only movable skull bone.
- In the newborn, unossified membranous gaps called **fontanelles** allow the skull to mould during birth; the anterior fontanelle closes by about 18 months.

**Ear ossicles (6):** malleus, incus, stapes in each ear. **Hyoid (1):** U-shaped bone in the neck; supports the tongue; does not articulate with any other bone.

**Vertebral column (26 bones in adult)**

| Region | Number | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cervical | 7 | Small; transverse foramina; C1 = atlas (no body, supports skull, "yes" nod), C2 = axis (dens, allows "no" rotation) |
| Thoracic | 12 | Articulate with ribs; long downward spines |
| Lumbar | 5 | Largest bodies; carry most body weight |
| Sacrum | 1 (5 fused) | Joins the hip bones |
| Coccyx | 1 (4 fused) | Tail bone |

A typical vertebra has a **body**, a **vertebral arch** (pedicles + laminae) enclosing the **vertebral foramen** for the spinal cord, one spinous process, two transverse processes and articular processes. **Intervertebral discs** (fibrocartilage) between bodies absorb shock.

**Normal curves:** cervical and lumbar (convex forward), thoracic and sacral (concave forward). Abnormal: kyphosis (hunchback), lordosis (excess lumbar curve), scoliosis (sideways curve).

**Thoracic cage:** sternum (manubrium, body, xiphoid process) + 12 pairs of ribs: ribs 1–7 **true ribs** (join sternum directly by own costal cartilage), 8–10 **false ribs** (join through cartilage of rib above), 11–12 **floating ribs** (no anterior attachment). Protects heart and lungs and helps breathing.

## 2.4 Joints (articulations)

A **joint** is a place where two or more bones meet.

### Structural / functional classification

| Type | Movement | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fibrous joints** (synarthroses) | Immovable | Skull sutures, teeth in sockets (gomphosis), distal tibiofibular joint (syndesmosis, slight) |
| **Cartilaginous joints** (amphiarthroses) | Slightly movable | Intervertebral discs, pubic symphysis, first rib–sternum |
| **Synovial joints** (diarthroses) | Freely movable | Shoulder, hip, knee, elbow |

### Structure of a synovial joint

```text
            Synovial joint (e.g. knee, simplified)

        bone 1
     ____|____
    |         |
    |_________|   <- articular cartilage
   (  synovial  ) <- joint cavity with synovial fluid
   (   cavity   )
    |‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾‾|   <- articular cartilage
    |_________|
   [  fibrous capsule lined inside by synovial membrane ]
         |
       bone 2        + ligaments outside, sometimes bursae and menisci
```

1. **Articular cartilage** – smooth hyaline cartilage reducing friction.
2. **Joint (synovial) cavity** – space between the bones.
3. **Articular capsule** – outer fibrous layer + inner **synovial membrane** that secretes **synovial fluid** (lubricates, nourishes cartilage, absorbs shock).
4. **Ligaments** – strengthen the joint.
5. Accessory structures: menisci (knee), bursae (fluid sacs reducing friction).

### Types of synovial joints

| Type | Movement | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ball and socket | All directions (most mobile) | Shoulder, hip |
| Hinge | One plane (flexion–extension) | Elbow, knee, interphalangeal |
| Pivot | Rotation | Atlanto-axial joint, radio-ulnar |
| Condyloid (ellipsoid) | Two planes | Wrist (radiocarpal), metacarpophalangeal |
| Saddle | Two planes, wide range | Carpometacarpal joint of thumb |
| Gliding (plane) | Sliding | Intercarpal, intertarsal joints |

### Movements at joints

- **Flexion** – decreasing the angle (bending the elbow); **extension** – increasing it (straightening).
- **Abduction** – moving away from the midline; **adduction** – towards the midline.
- **Rotation** – turning around its own axis (shaking head "no").
- **Circumduction** – circular movement of a limb (shoulder).
- **Pronation** – turning palm down; **supination** – palm up.
- **Inversion / eversion** – sole turned in / out.
- **Dorsiflexion / plantar flexion** – foot up / pointing toes down.
- **Elevation / depression** – raising / lowering (shoulders, mandible).

### Disorders of joints

| Disorder | Key points |
| --- | --- |
| **Osteoarthritis** | Degenerative wear of articular cartilage, mostly in old age and weight-bearing joints (knee, hip); pain, stiffness, bony outgrowths (osteophytes) |
| **Rheumatoid arthritis** | Autoimmune inflammation of the synovial membrane; small joints of hands and feet, symmetrical; swelling, deformity, morning stiffness |
| **Gout** | Deposition of **uric acid (monosodium urate) crystals** in joints due to high blood uric acid; sudden painful swelling, often of the big toe |
| **Sprain** | Stretching or tearing of ligaments |
| **Dislocation** | Bones forced out of their normal position |
| **Bursitis** | Inflammation of a bursa |
| **Ankylosing spondylitis** | Inflammation causing fusion of the vertebral joints |

Bone disorders worth knowing: **osteoporosis** (loss of bone mass, fractures; common after menopause), **rickets** (children) and **osteomalacia** (adults) from vitamin D deficiency, fractures.

## 2.5 Skeletal muscle – histology

```text
   Organisation of a skeletal muscle

 Muscle (wrapped in EPIMYSIUM)
   └─ Fascicles (bundles, wrapped in PERIMYSIUM)
        └─ Muscle fibres = muscle cells (each in ENDOMYSIUM)
             └─ Myofibrils
                  └─ Sarcomeres (contractile units)
                       └─ Myofilaments: thick (myosin), thin (actin)
```

- **Muscle fibre** – a long, cylindrical, multinucleated cell; membrane = **sarcolemma**, cytoplasm = **sarcoplasm**, ER = **sarcoplasmic reticulum** (stores Ca2+); **T-tubules** carry the impulse inside.
- **Myofibrils** show alternating dark **A bands** (myosin, anisotropic) and light **I bands** (actin only, isotropic), giving the striped look.
- **Sarcomere** – the part between two **Z lines**; the functional unit of contraction. Contains a central **H zone** (myosin only) and **M line**.
- Thin filament proteins: **actin**, **tropomyosin** (covers binding sites at rest), **troponin** (binds Ca2+).

```text
 Sarcomere
 Z|----I----|=======A band=======|----I----|Z

```

## 2.6 Physiology of muscle contraction (sliding filament theory)

1. A motor nerve impulse reaches the **neuromuscular junction**; **acetylcholine (ACh)** is released.
2. ACh binds to receptors on the motor end plate → the sarcolemma depolarises → **action potential** spreads along the sarcolemma and down the T-tubules.
3. The sarcoplasmic reticulum releases **Ca2+**.
4. Ca2+ binds **troponin**; tropomyosin moves aside and uncovers the binding sites on actin.
5. Myosin heads (already energised by splitting ATP) attach to actin → **cross-bridges**.
6. **Power stroke**: myosin heads pivot and pull the thin filaments towards the centre (M line).
7. A new ATP binds → myosin detaches → ATP is split → head re-cocks; the cycle repeats while Ca2+ is present.
8. **Result:** thin filaments slide over thick filaments; **Z lines come closer, the sarcomere shortens; A band length stays the same, I band and H zone shorten**.
9. **Relaxation:** acetylcholinesterase destroys ACh; Ca2+ is pumped back into the SR (needs ATP); tropomyosin covers the sites again.

**Energy sources:** creatine phosphate (seconds), anaerobic glycolysis (gives lactic acid), aerobic respiration (long activity). Without ATP, cross-bridges cannot detach – this is why muscles stiffen after death (**rigor mortis**).

**Terms:** motor unit (one motor neuron + all fibres it supplies); all-or-none law (a fibre contracts fully or not at all); tetanus (sustained contraction from rapid repeated stimuli); muscle tone (slight continuous contraction); isotonic (muscle shortens, load moves) vs isometric (tension rises, no shortening) contraction.

## 2.7 Disorders of skeletal muscle

| Disorder | Key points |
| --- | --- |
| **Myasthenia gravis** | Autoimmune; antibodies against ACh receptors at the neuromuscular junction; muscle weakness and fatigue, drooping eyelids; treated with anticholinesterases (neostigmine, pyridostigmine) |
| **Muscular dystrophy** | Inherited; progressive degeneration of muscle fibres (Duchenne type in boys) |
| **Muscle cramp** | Painful involuntary sustained contraction |
| **Fatigue** | Fall in force after prolonged activity |
| **Strain** | Over-stretching or tearing of muscle fibres |
| **Atrophy** | Wasting from disuse or loss of nerve supply |
| **Tetany** | Spasms due to low blood calcium |

## Remember

- 206 bones: axial 80, appendicular 126. Femur longest; stapes smallest.
- Osteoblasts build, osteoclasts break, osteocytes maintain.
- Vertebrae: 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, sacrum, coccyx. Atlas = C1, axis = C2.
- Ribs: 7 true, 3 false, 2 floating pairs.
- Synovial joint: cartilage, cavity, capsule with synovial membrane, fluid, ligaments.
- Ball-and-socket = shoulder, hip; hinge = elbow, knee; pivot = atlas–axis.
- Gout = uric acid crystals; RA = autoimmune synovitis; OA = cartilage wear.
- Contraction: ACh → AP → Ca2+ from SR → troponin → cross-bridges → sliding; I band and H zone shorten.
- Myasthenia gravis = antibodies against ACh receptors.

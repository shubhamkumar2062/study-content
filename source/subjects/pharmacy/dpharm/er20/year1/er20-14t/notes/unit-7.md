# Unit 7: Nervous system

Syllabus chapter 11: classification of the nervous system; anatomy and physiology of the cerebrum, cerebellum and midbrain; functions of the hypothalamus, medulla oblongata and basal ganglia; spinal cord – structure and reflexes; names and functions of cranial nerves; anatomy and physiology of the sympathetic and parasympathetic nervous systems (ANS).

The nervous system senses changes inside and outside the body, decides what to do, and controls muscles and glands. It works fast, by electrical impulses and chemical transmitters.

## 7.2 Neuron – the structural and functional unit

```text
                       NEURON (motor, myelinated)

  dendrites                                    myelin sheath
    \  |  /                                    (Schwann cell)    node of
     \ | /          axon hillock               _____   _____    Ranvier
  ----( • )====>==================[_____]=[_____]=[_____]=====< axon
       cell                  |                                 terminals
       body               AXON (carries impulse away          (synaptic
      (nucleus,            from the cell body)                 knobs)
      Nissl bodies)
   Direction of impulse:  dendrites → cell body → axon → terminals
```

- **Cell body (soma)** – nucleus, Nissl granules (rough ER), neurofibrils.
- **Dendrites** – short, branched; carry impulses **towards** the cell body.
- **Axon** – single long process; carries impulses **away** from the cell body. Many axons have a **myelin sheath** (fatty insulation) with gaps called **nodes of Ranvier**; impulses jump from node to node (**saltatory conduction**), so myelinated fibres conduct faster.
- **Types by function:** sensory (afferent), motor (efferent), interneurons (association, in CNS).
- **Types by structure:** multipolar (motor), bipolar (retina), unipolar/pseudounipolar (sensory ganglia).
- **Neuroglia** support neurons (astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, microglia, ependymal cells, Schwann cells).

**Nerve impulse (brief):** resting membrane potential about −70 mV (Na+ outside, K+ inside, kept by the Na+/K+ pump). A stimulus opens Na+ channels → Na+ rushes in → **depolarisation** (action potential) → K+ moves out → **repolarisation**. It follows the all-or-none law.

**Synapse:** junction between two neurons. The impulse releases a **neurotransmitter** (acetylcholine, noradrenaline, dopamine, serotonin, GABA, glutamate) from the presynaptic knob; it crosses the synaptic cleft and acts on receptors of the next cell. Transmission is one-way.

## 7.3 Protection of the CNS

- **Bones:** skull and vertebral column.
- **Meninges** (three membranes): **dura mater** (outer, tough), **arachnoid mater** (middle, web-like), **pia mater** (inner, delicate, on the brain surface). The subarachnoid space (between arachnoid and pia) contains **CSF**. Inflammation = meningitis.
- **Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF):** clear fluid (about 120–150 mL) made by the **choroid plexuses** in the four **ventricles** of the brain; cushions the brain, carries nutrients and removes wastes; absorbed into venous sinuses via arachnoid villi. Sample taken by **lumbar puncture**.
- **Blood–brain barrier:** tight capillary walls that keep many drugs and toxins out of the brain.

The adult brain weighs about 1.3–1.4 kg.

### Cerebrum

- Largest part; two **cerebral hemispheres** joined by the **corpus callosum**.
- Outer **cerebral cortex** of **grey matter** (cell bodies), folded into **gyri** (ridges) and **sulci** (grooves); inner **white matter** (myelinated tracts) with deep grey masses (basal ganglia).
- Each hemisphere controls the **opposite side** of the body (fibres cross over).

| Lobe | Main functions |
| --- | --- |
| **Frontal** | **Primary motor area** (precentral gyrus – voluntary movement); **Broca's (motor speech) area** (usually left side); thinking, judgement, personality, planning |
| **Parietal** | **Primary somatosensory area** (postcentral gyrus – touch, pain, temperature, position); understanding of sensations |
| **Temporal** | **Hearing** (auditory area); **Wernicke's area** (understanding speech); memory; smell |
| **Occipital** | **Vision** (visual area) |

**Functions of the cerebrum:** intelligence, learning, memory, reasoning, speech, voluntary movement, conscious sensation, emotion.

### Diencephalon

- **Thalamus** – relay station for almost all sensory impulses (except smell) to the cortex; crude awareness of pain.
- **Hypothalamus** – small but very important (below the thalamus, connected to the pituitary).

**Functions of the hypothalamus**

1. **Controls the autonomic nervous system** (heart rate, BP, gut activity).
2. **Body temperature regulation** ("thermostat").
3. **Hunger and satiety** (feeding centres) and **thirst**.
4. **Water balance** – makes **ADH**; also **oxytocin** (stored in the posterior pituitary).
5. **Controls the anterior pituitary** through releasing and inhibiting hormones – link between the nervous and endocrine systems.
6. Sleep–wake cycle and circadian rhythm.
7. Emotions and behaviour (with the limbic system) – fear, rage, pleasure; sexual behaviour.

### Midbrain

- Upper part of the brainstem, between the diencephalon and pons. Contains the **cerebral aqueduct**, cerebral peduncles (tracts), and the **corpora quadrigemina**: superior colliculi (**visual reflexes** – turning eyes towards light or movement) and inferior colliculi (**auditory reflexes** – turning head towards sound).
- Contains the **substantia nigra** (dopamine-producing; its loss causes Parkinson's disease) and nuclei of cranial nerves III and IV.
- **Functions:** relay pathway between cerebrum and lower parts; visual and auditory reflexes; control of eye movements and pupil size; posture.

### Pons

- Bridge of fibres between the cerebellum, medulla and higher centres; has the **pneumotaxic and apneustic** respiratory centres; nuclei of cranial nerves V–VIII.

### Medulla oblongata

- Lowest part of the brainstem, continuous with the spinal cord. Motor tracts **cross over** (decussation of pyramids) here.

**Functions of the medulla oblongata (vital centres)**

1. **Cardiac centre** – controls heart rate and force.
2. **Vasomotor centre** – controls the diameter of blood vessels and so BP.
3. **Respiratory centre** – sets the rhythm of breathing.
4. **Reflex centres** – swallowing, vomiting, coughing, sneezing, hiccupping, salivation.
5. Carries all tracts between the brain and spinal cord; nuclei of cranial nerves IX–XII.

Damage to the medulla is often fatal because of these vital centres.

### Cerebellum

- Second largest part; behind the brainstem, below the occipital lobe. Two hemispheres joined by the vermis; outer grey cortex, inner white matter ("arbor vitae").
- **Functions:** coordination of voluntary movements (smooth, accurate movement); **maintains balance and equilibrium** (with the inner ear); **muscle tone and posture**; motor learning of skilled movements.
- Damage → **ataxia** (unsteady gait), intention tremor, poor balance.

### Basal ganglia (basal nuclei)

- Masses of grey matter deep in the cerebrum: caudate nucleus, putamen, globus pallidus (with links to the substantia nigra and subthalamic nucleus).
- **Functions:** control and smoothing of voluntary movement, starting and stopping movements, regulation of muscle tone, control of automatic movements such as arm swinging while walking.
- Disorders: **Parkinson's disease** (dopamine deficiency – tremor, rigidity, slow movement), Huntington's chorea.

## 7.5 Spinal cord – structure and reflexes

**Structure**

- Cylindrical cord about **45 cm** long, from the foramen magnum (continuous with the medulla) to the level of the **first–second lumbar vertebra**, within the vertebral canal. Below this the nerve roots form the **cauda equina**.
- Two enlargements: cervical (nerves to arms) and lumbar (nerves to legs).
- **31 pairs of spinal nerves:** cervical 8, thoracic 12, lumbar 5, sacral 5, coccygeal 1.
- Each spinal nerve is formed by a **dorsal (posterior) root** (sensory; has a **dorsal root ganglion**) and a **ventral (anterior) root** (motor); so spinal nerves are **mixed** nerves.

```text
        Spinal cord – cross section

              posterior median sulcus
          dorsal root   |   dorsal root ganglion (•)
            (sensory) \ | /
         ______________\|/______________
        /  WHITE MATTER (tracts)        \
       |      \  posterior horn  /       |
       |       \    (sensory)   /        |
       |        \____ __ ______/  GREY   |
       |         |   (o)   |   MATTER    |  (o) = central canal (CSF)
       |        /    lateral \  (H or    |
       |       /  anterior horn\ butterfly)
        \_____/___(motor)_______\_______/
               /        \
     ventral root (motor)   anterior median fissure
```

- **Grey matter** (inside, H-shaped): posterior horns (sensory), anterior horns (motor neurons), lateral horns (autonomic, in thoracic region).
- **White matter** (outside): **ascending (sensory) tracts** carry impulses up to the brain (e.g. spinothalamic – pain and temperature); **descending (motor) tracts** carry impulses down (e.g. corticospinal/pyramidal – voluntary movement).

**Functions:** conducts impulses between body and brain; centre for **spinal reflexes**.

**Reflex action** – a fast, automatic, involuntary response to a stimulus, without conscious thought. The pathway is a **reflex arc**:

```text
   REFLEX ARC (e.g. withdrawal from a hot object)

  1. RECEPTOR (skin) ──→ 2. SENSORY NEURON ──→ (dorsal root)
                                                    │
                              3. INTEGRATING CENTRE (spinal cord grey matter,
                                 often via an interneuron)
                                                    │
  5. EFFECTOR (muscle) ←── 4. MOTOR NEURON ←── (ventral root)
        contracts → hand pulled away
```

**Examples:** knee jerk (patellar reflex – a **monosynaptic stretch reflex**, tests spinal segments L2–L4), withdrawal reflex, plantar reflex, blinking, coughing, sneezing (cranial reflexes). Doctors test reflexes to check the nervous system.

## 7.7 Autonomic nervous system (ANS)

The ANS controls involuntary organs – heart, smooth muscle (gut, blood vessels, bronchi, bladder, eye) and glands – mainly through the hypothalamus and brainstem. Each pathway has **two neurons**: a **preganglionic** neuron (from CNS to a ganglion) and a **postganglionic** neuron (from ganglion to the organ).

| Feature | **Sympathetic** | **Parasympathetic** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Origin (outflow) | **Thoracolumbar** (T1–L2 segments of the spinal cord) | **Craniosacral** (cranial nerves III, VII, IX, X and sacral S2–S4) |
| Ganglia | Close to the spinal cord (**sympathetic chain**) | Close to or in the wall of the organ |
| Preganglionic fibre | Short | Long |
| Postganglionic fibre | Long | Short |
| Transmitter at ganglion | Acetylcholine | Acetylcholine |
| Transmitter at organ | **Noradrenaline** (adrenergic) – except sweat glands (ACh) | **Acetylcholine** (cholinergic) |
| Overall role | **"Fight or flight"** – prepares body for stress and exercise | **"Rest and digest"** – conserves energy |

**Effects on organs**

| Organ | Sympathetic | Parasympathetic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eye (pupil) | Dilates (mydriasis) | Constricts (miosis) |
| Heart | Rate and force increase | Rate decreases |
| Bronchi | Dilate | Constrict |
| Blood vessels (skin, gut) | Constrict | — (little effect) |
| Salivary glands | Little, thick saliva | Much watery saliva |
| Gut movement and secretion | Decreased | Increased |
| Sphincters of gut and bladder | Contract | Relax |
| Urinary bladder wall | Relaxes (urine retained) | Contracts (urination) |
| Liver | Glycogen breakdown → blood glucose rises | Glycogen formation |
| Adrenal medulla | Releases adrenaline | — |
| Sweat glands | Sweating | — |

**Drug link:** atropine blocks parasympathetic (muscarinic) effects and dilates the pupil; salbutamol copies sympathetic action on bronchi; beta blockers reduce sympathetic action on the heart.

## Remember

- CNS = brain + spinal cord; PNS = 12 cranial + 31 spinal nerve pairs.
- Dendrites → cell body → axon; myelin speeds conduction (saltatory).
- Cerebrum lobes: frontal (motor, Broca's speech), parietal (sensation), temporal (hearing), occipital (vision).
- Cerebellum = balance and coordination; damage → ataxia.
- Hypothalamus = temperature, hunger, thirst, ADH, controls pituitary and ANS.
- Medulla = cardiac, vasomotor, respiratory and reflex centres.
- Basal ganglia = smooth movement; dopamine loss → Parkinson's disease.
- Spinal cord ends at L1–L2; dorsal root sensory, ventral root motor; knee jerk is monosynaptic.
- Vagus (X) is the main parasympathetic nerve.
- Sympathetic = thoracolumbar, noradrenaline, fight or flight; parasympathetic = craniosacral, ACh, rest and digest.

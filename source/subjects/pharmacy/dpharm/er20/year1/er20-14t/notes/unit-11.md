# Unit 11: Reproductive system

Syllabus chapter 15: anatomy of the male and female reproductive systems; physiology of menstruation; spermatogenesis and oogenesis; pregnancy and parturition.

The reproductive system produces **gametes** (sperm and ova), **sex hormones**, and – in the female – supports the growing baby. The primary sex organs (**gonads**) are the testes and ovaries.

## 11.1 Male reproductive system

```text
            MALE REPRODUCTIVE SYSTEM (side view, simplified)

                     urinary bladder
                          |  ____ seminal vesicle (behind bladder)
                 ureter→  | /
                          |/   ejaculatory duct
         vas deferens →  PROSTATE (around urethra)
         (from testis,       |  bulbourethral (Cowper's) gland
          over bladder)      |
                         URETHRA → through PENIS → external opening
                             |
         EPIDIDYMIS  ←  TESTIS (in SCROTUM)
         (coiled tube on      seminiferous tubules → sperm
          back of testis)     Leydig cells → testosterone
```

| Organ | Structure | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scrotum** | Pouch of skin and muscle outside the body | Holds the testes about 2–3 °C below body temperature, which is needed for sperm production |
| **Testes** (2) | Oval glands about 4–5 cm long; each divided into lobules containing coiled **seminiferous tubules** (with **Sertoli cells**); **Leydig (interstitial) cells** between tubules | Produce **sperm** (spermatogenesis) and **testosterone** |
| **Epididymis** | Highly coiled tube (about 6 m long) on the back of each testis | Stores sperm while they **mature** and gain the ability to swim |
| **Vas deferens (ductus deferens)** | Muscular tube about 45 cm long, in the spermatic cord, through the inguinal canal | Carries sperm to the ejaculatory duct (cut in **vasectomy**) |
| **Seminal vesicles** (2) | Glands behind the bladder | Make about 60 % of semen: alkaline fluid with **fructose** (energy for sperm) and prostaglandins |
| **Ejaculatory ducts** | Formed by vas deferens + seminal vesicle duct | Pass through the prostate into the urethra |
| **Prostate gland** | Walnut-sized gland below the bladder, around the urethra | Milky, slightly acidic fluid (about 25–30 % of semen) with citric acid and enzymes; enlarges in old age (BPH → difficult urination) |
| **Bulbourethral (Cowper's) glands** | Two pea-sized glands below the prostate | Mucus that lubricates and neutralises the urethra before ejaculation |
| **Urethra** | About 18–20 cm | Common passage for urine and semen |
| **Penis** | Root, shaft, glans; erectile tissue (corpora cavernosa, corpus spongiosum) | Copulatory organ; erection by filling with blood (parasympathetic) |

**Semen:** sperm + secretions of seminal vesicles, prostate and bulbourethral glands. Volume about 2–5 mL per ejaculation; slightly alkaline.

**Structure of a sperm (spermatozoon):**

```text
   HEAD [acrosome cap + nucleus (23 chromosomes)] — NECK —
   MIDDLE PIECE (mitochondria → energy) ———— TAIL (flagellum → swimming)
```

The **acrosome** holds enzymes that help the sperm pass through the layers around the ovum.

## 11.2 Spermatogenesis

**Spermatogenesis** is the formation of sperm in the **seminiferous tubules**, from puberty onwards throughout life. It takes about 64–74 days.

```text
  Spermatogonium (2n = 46)  ← stem cells near the basement membrane;
        |   divide by MITOSIS (multiplication phase)
        ↓   growth
  Primary spermatocyte (2n = 46)
        |   MEIOSIS I
        ↓
  2 Secondary spermatocytes (n = 23)
        |   MEIOSIS II
        ↓
  4 Spermatids (n = 23)
        |   SPERMIOGENESIS (change in shape: acrosome, tail form;
        ↓   cytoplasm lost) – nourished by Sertoli cells
  4 Spermatozoa (n = 23) → released into the lumen → epididymis
```

- One primary spermatocyte gives **four** functional sperm.
- **Control:** FSH (with testosterone) stimulates spermatogenesis through Sertoli cells; LH stimulates testosterone from Leydig cells; inhibin from Sertoli cells feeds back to lower FSH.
- Sperm carry either an **X or a Y** chromosome, so the sperm decides the sex of the baby.

```text
                          |  (cavity)  |  ENDOMETRIUM (lining – shed

```

| Organ | Structure | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ovaries** (2) | Almond-shaped, about 3 cm, in the pelvis on each side of the uterus; cortex with **ovarian follicles** at different stages | Produce **ova** (oogenesis) and the hormones **oestrogen** and **progesterone** |
| **Uterine (fallopian) tubes** (2) | About 10 cm long; parts: infundibulum with finger-like **fimbriae**, ampulla, isthmus; ciliated lining | Catch the ovum at ovulation; **fertilisation usually takes place in the ampulla**; cilia and peristalsis move the embryo to the uterus |
| **Uterus** | Hollow, pear-shaped muscular organ (about 7.5 cm long) between bladder and rectum; parts: fundus, body, cervix. Wall: **perimetrium** (outer), **myometrium** (thick smooth muscle), **endometrium** (inner lining) | Receives and nourishes the embryo (implantation); forms the maternal part of the placenta; contracts in labour; endometrium is shed in menstruation |
| **Cervix** | Narrow lower part of the uterus opening into the vagina | Mucus plug; dilates in labour |
| **Vagina** | Muscular tube about 8–10 cm; acidic secretions protect against infection | Receives the penis; birth canal; outlet for menstrual flow |
| **External genitalia (vulva)** | Mons pubis, labia majora and minora, clitoris, vestibule | Protection; sexual sensation |
| **Mammary glands** (accessory) | Lobes of glandular tissue with ducts opening at the nipple | Milk production (prolactin) and ejection (oxytocin) |

## 11.4 Oogenesis

**Oogenesis** is the formation of ova in the ovaries.

```text
  BEFORE BIRTH (fetal ovary):
  Oogonia (2n) → mitosis → PRIMARY OOCYTES (2n) start meiosis I and
  STOP at prophase I; each in a primordial follicle. (About 1–2 million
  at birth; about 4 lakh by puberty – no new ones are made.)

  FROM PUBERTY, EACH MONTH (under FSH):
  Primary oocyte completes MEIOSIS I →
     SECONDARY OOCYTE (n) + FIRST POLAR BODY (small, degenerates)
        |
     OVULATION (secondary oocyte released, arrested at metaphase II)
        |
     Only IF a sperm enters (fertilisation): MEIOSIS II completes →
     OVUM (n) + SECOND POLAR BODY
```

- One primary oocyte gives **only one** functional ovum (plus polar bodies), unlike four sperm from one spermatocyte. The unequal division gives the ovum most of the cytoplasm (food for the early embryo).
- About 400–500 ova are released during a woman's reproductive life; the rest of the follicles degenerate (atresia).
- Oogenesis stops at **menopause** (about 45–50 years).
- Every ovum carries an **X** chromosome.

**Follicle development:** primordial → primary → secondary → mature **Graafian follicle** (fluid-filled antrum) → ruptures at ovulation → the remaining follicle becomes the **corpus luteum** (yellow body, secretes progesterone and oestrogen) → if no pregnancy, it degenerates into the **corpus albicans** (white scar).

## 11.5 Physiology of menstruation (menstrual cycle)

The **menstrual cycle** is the regular monthly series of changes in the ovary and uterus that prepares for pregnancy. Average length **28 days** (range about 21–35). It starts at puberty (**menarche**, about 11–14 years) and stops at **menopause**.

| Phase (28-day cycle) | Days | Ovary | Uterus (endometrium) | Hormones |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Menstrual phase** | 1–5 | New follicles begin to grow | Functional layer of endometrium breaks down and is shed with blood (about 50–80 mL) | Oestrogen and progesterone low (corpus luteum has died) |
| **Proliferative (follicular) phase** | 6–13 | Follicles grow; one becomes the mature **Graafian follicle** | Endometrium **repairs and thickens**; glands and blood vessels grow | **FSH** ↑ → follicle makes **oestrogen** ↑ |
| **Ovulation** | About day 14 | Graafian follicle ruptures and releases the secondary oocyte | — | **LH surge** (triggered by high oestrogen – positive feedback) |
| **Secretory (luteal) phase** | 15–28 | **Corpus luteum** forms | Endometrium becomes thick, soft, rich in glands and blood vessels, ready for implantation | **Progesterone** ↑ (and oestrogen) from corpus luteum; FSH and LH suppressed |

```text
   HORMONE LEVELS DURING THE CYCLE (sketch)

  LH          .                   (sharp peak just before day 14)
             / \
  FSH  __.--'   '--.______________
  Oestrogen    /\ (peak before ovulation)   /‾‾\ (smaller rise)
  ____________/  \_________________________/    \____
  Progesterone                     ______
  ________________________________/      \___________
  Days: 1    5          14                 21         28
        |menses|proliferative|  ovulation |  secretory  |
```

- **If no fertilisation:** the corpus luteum degenerates after about 10–12 days → progesterone and oestrogen fall → the endometrium loses its support → **menstruation** begins; FSH rises again and a new cycle starts.
- **If fertilisation occurs:** the embryo makes **hCG** (human chorionic gonadotropin), which keeps the corpus luteum alive, so progesterone stays high and menstruation stops. hCG in urine is the basis of the **pregnancy test**.

**Terms:** amenorrhoea (absence of menses), dysmenorrhoea (painful menses), menorrhagia (heavy bleeding). Oral contraceptive pills (oestrogen + progestin) work by suppressing FSH and LH, so ovulation does not occur.

## 11.6 Fertilisation and pregnancy

- **Fertilisation:** fusion of a sperm and a secondary oocyte, normally in the **ampulla of the uterine tube** within about 24 hours of ovulation. Only one sperm enters; the oocyte then completes meiosis II and the nuclei fuse → **zygote** (2n = 46).
- **Cleavage:** the zygote divides as it moves down the tube → morula → **blastocyst** (reaches the uterus in about 4–5 days).
- **Implantation:** the blastocyst sinks into the endometrium about **day 6–7** after fertilisation.
- **Placenta:** formed from the embryo's chorion (chorionic villi) and the mother's endometrium; joined to the fetus by the **umbilical cord** (two arteries, one vein). Maternal and fetal blood do **not** mix.

**Functions of the placenta:** exchange of O2 and CO2 (respiration); supply of nutrients; removal of wastes; passage of maternal antibodies (IgG); barrier to many (not all) microbes and drugs; **endocrine** – hCG, oestrogen, progesterone, human placental lactogen. Some drugs cross the placenta and can harm the fetus (e.g. thalidomide, alcohol), so pharmacists must check drugs in pregnancy.

**Pregnancy (gestation):** about **40 weeks (280 days) from the first day of the last menstrual period**, divided into three trimesters. Embryo up to 8 weeks (organs form – most sensitive to drugs); fetus from 9 weeks to birth.

**Changes in the mother:** uterus enlarges; menstruation stops; breasts enlarge; increased blood volume, cardiac output and breathing; weight gain; nausea in early weeks; frequent urination.

**Membranes and fluid:** the **amnion** encloses the fetus in **amniotic fluid**, which cushions it and allows movement.

## 11.7 Parturition (childbirth)

**Parturition** is the birth of the baby at the end of pregnancy. The process of getting there is **labour**.

**Hormonal and nervous control**

1. Near term, oestrogen rises and progesterone's calming effect on the uterus falls; oxytocin receptors increase.
2. The fetus presses on the cervix → stretch → impulses to the hypothalamus → **oxytocin** released from the posterior pituitary.
3. Oxytocin (and prostaglandins) → stronger uterine contractions → more pressure on the cervix → more oxytocin.
4. This **positive feedback** loop continues until the baby is born.

```text
  Baby's head stretches cervix → nerve impulses → hypothalamus
        ↑                                              ↓
  Stronger contractions  ←  OXYTOCIN (posterior pituitary) + prostaglandins
        (cycle repeats until delivery – POSITIVE FEEDBACK)
```

**Stages of labour**

| Stage | What happens | Approx. duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Dilation stage** | Regular contractions; cervix dilates fully to about 10 cm; amniotic sac ruptures ("waters break") | Longest – several hours (longer in a first baby) |
| **2. Expulsion stage** | Baby is pushed through the birth canal and delivered (usually head first) | Minutes to about 1–2 hours |
| **3. Placental stage** | Placenta (afterbirth) separates and is expelled; uterus contracts to stop bleeding | About 15–30 minutes |

**After birth:** **lactation** – prolactin (from the anterior pituitary) makes milk; oxytocin ejects it when the baby suckles. The first milk, **colostrum**, is rich in antibodies. Oxytocin-type drugs are used to start or strengthen labour and control bleeding after delivery.

## Remember

- Male: testes (sperm in seminiferous tubules, testosterone from Leydig cells) → epididymis → vas deferens → ejaculatory duct → urethra.
- Seminal vesicles give fructose; prostate surrounds the urethra.
- Spermatogenesis: 1 primary spermatocyte → 4 sperm; oogenesis: 1 primary oocyte → 1 ovum + polar bodies.
- Female: ovaries, uterine tubes (fertilisation in ampulla), uterus (endometrium, myometrium, perimetrium), cervix, vagina.
- Menstrual cycle 28 days: menstrual (1–5), proliferative (FSH, oestrogen), ovulation (day 14, LH surge), secretory (progesterone from corpus luteum).
- hCG keeps the corpus luteum alive in pregnancy; it is detected by pregnancy tests.
- Pregnancy ≈ 40 weeks; placenta exchanges gases and nutrients and makes hormones.
- Parturition: oxytocin, positive feedback; three stages – dilation, expulsion, placental.

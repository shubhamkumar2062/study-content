# Unit 3: Haemopoietic and lymphatic systems

Syllabus chapters 5 and 6: composition and functions of blood; haemopoiesis; characteristics and functions of RBCs, WBCs and platelets; mechanism of blood clotting; importance of blood groups. Lymph and the lymphatic system (composition, function, formation); structure and functions of the spleen and lymph nodes.

## 3.1 Blood – general features

Blood is a **fluid connective tissue** that circulates in the heart and blood vessels.

| Property | Value (adult) |
| --- | --- |
| Volume | About 5 litres (about 7–8 % of body weight) |
| pH | 7.35–7.45 (slightly alkaline) |
| Colour | Bright red in arteries (oxygenated), dark red in veins |
| Specific gravity | About 1.05–1.06 |
| Viscosity | About 4–5 times that of water |

## 3.2 Composition of blood

```text
            Blood (centrifuged in a tube)
           ________________________________
          |  PLASMA  ~55 %                 |  straw-coloured liquid
          |________________________________|
          |  buffy coat <1 % (WBCs,        |
          |  platelets)                    |
          |________________________________|
          |  RED CELLS  ~45 %              |  = haematocrit (PCV)
          |________________________________|
```

**Plasma (about 55 %)**

- Water about 90–92 %.
- **Plasma proteins** (about 6–8 g/dL):
  - **Albumin** – maintains colloid osmotic (oncotic) pressure; carries drugs, bilirubin, hormones.
  - **Globulins** – α and β carry substances; γ-globulins are **antibodies**.
  - **Fibrinogen** – forms fibrin in clotting.
  - Prothrombin and other clotting factors.
- Nutrients (glucose, amino acids, fats), electrolytes (Na+, K+, Ca2+, Cl−, HCO3−), wastes (urea, creatinine, uric acid), hormones, enzymes, gases.

**Serum** = plasma minus fibrinogen and clotting factors (the fluid left after blood clots).

**Formed elements (about 45 %):** red blood cells (erythrocytes), white blood cells (leucocytes), platelets (thrombocytes).

## 3.3 Functions of blood

1. **Transport** – O2 from lungs to tissues, CO2 back; nutrients from gut; wastes to kidneys; hormones to targets.
2. **Regulation** – body temperature (distributes heat), pH (buffers such as bicarbonate and haemoglobin), water balance.
3. **Protection** – WBCs and antibodies fight infection; clotting prevents blood loss.

## 3.4 Haemopoiesis

**Haemopoiesis (haematopoiesis)** is the formation of blood cells.

- **Sites:** embryo – yolk sac, then liver and spleen; from birth – **red bone marrow**. In adults, red marrow is mainly in flat and irregular bones (sternum, ribs, vertebrae, pelvis, skull) and the upper ends of the femur and humerus.
- All blood cells come from one **pluripotent haemopoietic stem cell** in the marrow.

```text
                Pluripotent stem cell (red bone marrow)
                   ________________|________________
                  |                                 |
           Myeloid stem cell                 Lymphoid stem cell
     _________|____________________                  |
    |          |           |       |            Lymphocytes
Erythroblasts Megakaryocytes Myeloblasts Monoblasts    (B and T)
    |          |              |          |
   RBCs     Platelets   Neutrophils,   Monocytes
                        eosinophils,   (→ macrophages
                        basophils        in tissues)
```

**Erythropoiesis** (RBC formation): stem cell → proerythroblast → erythroblasts (haemoglobin appears, nucleus shrinks and is expelled) → **reticulocyte** → mature RBC. Takes about 7 days.

**Needs:** protein, iron, **vitamin B12** and **folic acid** (DNA synthesis), vitamin C, copper.

**Control:** low O2 in tissue → kidneys release the hormone **erythropoietin** → marrow makes more RBCs (negative feedback). This is why people living at high altitude have more RBCs.

## 3.5 Red blood cells (erythrocytes)

| Feature | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Shape | **Biconcave disc**, no nucleus, no mitochondria |
| Size | About 7.5 µm diameter |
| Count | Men about 4.5–5.5 million/µL; women about 4.0–5.0 million/µL |
| Life span | About **120 days**; old cells destroyed by macrophages in spleen and liver |
| Contents | **Haemoglobin** (about 33 % of the cell) |

The biconcave shape gives a large surface area for gas exchange and lets the cell bend through narrow capillaries.

**Haemoglobin (Hb):** protein **globin** (4 chains) + 4 **haem** groups, each with one Fe2+ that binds one O2 molecule. Normal Hb: men about 13–17 g/dL, women about 12–15 g/dL.

- Oxyhaemoglobin (Hb + O2), carbaminohaemoglobin (Hb + CO2), carboxyhaemoglobin (Hb + CO, binds very tightly – carbon monoxide poisoning).

**Fate of old RBCs:** haem → iron (reused) + **bilirubin** (to the liver, excreted in bile); globin → amino acids.

**Functions:** carry O2 and part of the CO2; Hb acts as a buffer.

**Disorders:** **anaemia** (low Hb – iron deficiency, vitamin B12 / folate deficiency (megaloblastic), haemolytic, aplastic, sickle-cell); **polycythaemia** (too many RBCs); **jaundice** (excess bilirubin).

## 3.6 White blood cells (leucocytes)

**Total count:** about 4,000–11,000 per µL. Increase = **leucocytosis** (infection); decrease = **leucopenia**. WBCs have nuclei and can leave capillaries (**diapedesis**) and move to infection by **chemotaxis**.

| Type | Normal % (approx.) | Features | Function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Neutrophils** | 40–75 | Multi-lobed nucleus (2–5 lobes), fine granules | First to arrive; **phagocytosis** of bacteria; pus = dead neutrophils |
| **Eosinophils** | 1–6 | Bilobed nucleus, red-orange granules | Increase in **allergy** and worm infections |
| **Basophils** | 0–1 | Granules hide the S-shaped nucleus; blue granules | Release **histamine** and **heparin**; allergic reactions |
| **Lymphocytes** | 20–40 | Large round nucleus, little cytoplasm | **Immunity**: B cells make antibodies, T cells kill infected cells |
| **Monocytes** | 2–10 | Largest WBC, kidney-shaped nucleus | Become **macrophages** in tissues; phagocytosis |

Granulocytes = neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils. Agranulocytes = lymphocytes, monocytes.

**Memory aid (most to least):** "**N**ever **L**et **M**onkeys **E**at **B**ananas" = Neutrophils > Lymphocytes > Monocytes > Eosinophils > Basophils.

## 3.7 Platelets (thrombocytes)

- Small, disc-shaped **cell fragments** without a nucleus, broken off from **megakaryocytes** in the marrow.
- Count: about **1.5–4 lakh per µL** (150,000–400,000/µL). Life span about 7–10 days.
- **Functions:** form the **platelet plug**; release substances that cause vasoconstriction (serotonin, thromboxane A2) and promote clotting; help clot retraction.
- Low count = **thrombocytopenia** (bleeding spots, e.g. in dengue).

## 3.8 Haemostasis and blood clotting

**Haemostasis** = stopping of bleeding. Three steps:

1. **Vascular spasm** – the injured vessel constricts.
2. **Platelet plug** – platelets stick to exposed collagen (adhesion), release ADP and thromboxane A2, and more platelets clump (aggregation).
3. **Coagulation** – a chain of reactions forms a **fibrin** mesh that traps cells → clot.

**Clotting factors**

| No. | Name |
| --- | --- |
| I | Fibrinogen |
| II | Prothrombin |
| III | Tissue factor (thromboplastin) |
| IV | Calcium (Ca2+) |
| V | Proaccelerin (labile factor) |
| VII | Proconvertin (stable factor) |
| VIII | Antihaemophilic factor |
| IX | Christmas factor |
| X | Stuart–Prower factor |
| XI | Plasma thromboplastin antecedent |
| XII | Hageman factor |
| XIII | Fibrin-stabilising factor |

(There is no factor VI.)

**Mechanism (cascade)**

```text
 EXTRINSIC pathway                  INTRINSIC pathway
 (tissue injury)                    (blood touches collagen / glass)
 Tissue factor (III) + VII          XII → XI → IX (+ VIII)
          \                               /
           \______  + Ca2+  _____________/
                     ↓
              Factor X activated  (+ V, Ca2+, platelet phospholipid)
                     ↓
          PROTHROMBIN ACTIVATOR
                     ↓
      Prothrombin (II) ──────→ THROMBIN
                                  ↓
      Fibrinogen (I) (soluble) ──→ FIBRIN threads (loose)
                                  ↓  factor XIII + Ca2+
                          Stable fibrin mesh = CLOT
```

Common pathway (three key steps to learn): formation of **prothrombin activator** → **prothrombin to thrombin** → **fibrinogen to fibrin**.

- **Extrinsic** pathway is fast (seconds); **intrinsic** is slower (minutes).
- **Vitamin K** is needed in the liver to make factors II, VII, IX and X.
- **Clot retraction** squeezes out serum; **fibrinolysis** later dissolves the clot by **plasmin**.

**Anticoagulants:** heparin (from basophils/mast cells; drug acts with antithrombin), warfarin (blocks vitamin K action), and in the lab: EDTA, sodium citrate, oxalate (remove Ca2+).

**Disorders:** **haemophilia** (inherited lack of factor VIII – haemophilia A; or IX – haemophilia B / Christmas disease); vitamin K deficiency; thrombosis (unwanted clot inside a vessel); embolism (a moving clot).

## 3.9 Blood groups

**ABO system** – based on antigens (agglutinogens) on RBCs and antibodies (agglutinins) in plasma (Landsteiner).

| Blood group | Antigen on RBC | Antibody in plasma | Can donate to | Can receive from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | A | Anti-B | A, AB | A, O |
| B | B | Anti-A | B, AB | B, O |
| AB | A and B | None | AB | All (**universal recipient**) |
| O | None | Anti-A and anti-B | All (**universal donor**) | O |

**Rh system:** Rh (D) antigen present = **Rh positive** (most people); absent = Rh negative. Anti-D antibodies are not present naturally; they form only after exposure (transfusion or pregnancy).

**Importance of blood groups**

1. **Safe blood transfusion** – mismatched blood causes agglutination and haemolysis (transfusion reaction, kidney failure, death). Cross-matching is always done.
2. **Haemolytic disease of the newborn (erythroblastosis fetalis)** – Rh-negative mother carrying an Rh-positive baby forms anti-D after the first delivery; in the next Rh-positive pregnancy these antibodies cross the placenta and destroy the baby's RBCs. Prevented by giving **anti-D immunoglobulin** to the mother within 72 hours of delivery.
3. Organ transplantation, paternity disputes, forensic work.

## 3.10 Lymphatic system

The lymphatic system is a one-way network of vessels and organs that returns tissue fluid to the blood and defends the body.

**Parts:** lymph, lymph capillaries, lymph vessels, lymph nodes, lymph ducts (thoracic duct, right lymphatic duct), and lymphoid organs (spleen, thymus, tonsils, Peyer's patches, bone marrow).

### Lymph – formation and composition

- At the arterial end of capillaries, fluid is pushed out into tissue spaces (tissue fluid). Most returns at the venous end; the extra fluid and escaped proteins enter blind-ended **lymph capillaries** – now called **lymph**.
- **Composition:** like plasma but with **less protein**; contains many **lymphocytes**; no RBCs or platelets. Lymph from the intestine after a fatty meal is milky (**chyle**) because of absorbed fat.
- **Flow:** lymph capillaries → lymph vessels (with valves) → lymph nodes → larger trunks → **thoracic duct** (drains lower body and left upper body into the **left subclavian vein**) and **right lymphatic duct** (right side of head, neck, chest and right arm into the **right subclavian vein**). Flow is helped by muscle contraction, breathing movements and valves.

### Functions of the lymphatic system

1. Returns excess tissue fluid and proteins to the blood (prevents oedema).
2. Absorbs fats from the small intestine (lacteals in villi).
3. **Defence** – lymph nodes filter microbes; lymphocytes give immunity.

**Oedema** = swelling from excess tissue fluid, e.g. from blocked lymph vessels (filariasis – elephantiasis).

## 3.11 Lymph node

```text
                Lymph node (bean-shaped)

     afferent lymph vessels (several, enter on the convex side)
           \      |      /
        .-----------------------.
       /  capsule               \
      |  CORTEX: lymphoid        |
      |  follicles (B cells)     |
      |   (o)   (o)   (o)        |
      |  MEDULLA: medullary      |-- HILUM --> efferent lymph
      |  cords and sinuses       |             vessel (one or two)
       \  (macrophages)         /    + artery in, vein out
        '-----------------------'
```

- Small bean-shaped bodies in groups along lymph vessels: neck (cervical), armpit (axillary), groin (inguinal).
- Covered by a fibrous **capsule**; inside are the outer **cortex** (follicles of B lymphocytes) and inner **medulla**.
- **Afferent** vessels bring lymph in; **efferent** vessels take it out at the **hilum** (fewer efferent than afferent, so lymph flows slowly and is filtered well).
- **Functions:** filter lymph and trap microbes and cancer cells; macrophages destroy them; produce and activate lymphocytes and antibodies. Nodes swell and become tender during infection.

## 3.12 Spleen

- **Largest lymphoid organ**; lies in the **left upper abdomen (left hypochondrium)** behind the stomach, under the diaphragm, about the size of a fist.
- Covered by a capsule; inside is **red pulp** (blood-filled sinuses and cords, destroys old RBCs) and **white pulp** (lymphoid tissue around arteries, immune function).

**Functions of the spleen**

1. **Destroys old and damaged RBCs and platelets** ("graveyard of RBCs"); keeps iron for reuse.
2. **Immunity** – lymphocytes and antibody production; filters microbes from blood.
3. **Blood reservoir** – stores blood and platelets that can be released in haemorrhage.
4. **Haemopoiesis in the fetus.**

Removal of the spleen (**splenectomy**) after injury raises the risk of some serious infections. Enlarged spleen = **splenomegaly** (malaria, typhoid, leukaemia).

**Thymus:** in the upper chest behind the sternum; large in children, shrinks after puberty; T lymphocytes mature here (hormone thymosin).

## Remember

- Blood: plasma 55 %, cells 45 %; volume about 5 L; pH 7.35–7.45.
- Serum = plasma − fibrinogen.
- RBC: biconcave, no nucleus, 120 days; erythropoietin from the kidney.
- WBC 4,000–11,000/µL; neutrophils most, basophils least.
- Platelets 1.5–4 lakh/µL from megakaryocytes.
- Clotting: prothrombin activator → thrombin → fibrin; needs Ca2+ and vitamin K (II, VII, IX, X).
- Haemophilia A = factor VIII deficiency.
- O = universal donor; AB = universal recipient; Rh incompatibility → haemolytic disease of the newborn.
- Lymph has less protein than plasma; thoracic duct drains into the left subclavian vein.
- Spleen = graveyard of RBCs, in the left upper abdomen.

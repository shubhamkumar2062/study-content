# Unit 10: Endocrine system

Syllabus chapter 14: hormones and their functions – pituitary gland, adrenal gland, thyroid and parathyroid glands, pancreas and gonads.

## 10.1 Introduction

- **Endocrine glands** are **ductless** glands that release chemical messengers called **hormones** directly into the blood, which carries them to **target cells** with matching **receptors**.
- Exocrine glands, by contrast, pour secretions through ducts (salivary, sweat glands). The **pancreas** and **gonads** are both endocrine and exocrine (**mixed glands**).
- The endocrine system works **slowly but its effects last longer** than nervous control; together they keep homeostasis. The **hypothalamus** links the two systems.

**Chemical types of hormones**

| Type | Examples | How they act |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Amino acid derivatives (amines) | Adrenaline, noradrenaline, thyroxine (T3, T4) | Catecholamines act on membrane receptors; thyroid hormones enter the cell |
| Peptides and proteins | Insulin, glucagon, GH, ADH, oxytocin, PTH, TSH | Bind membrane receptors → second messenger (e.g. cyclic AMP) inside the cell |
| Steroids (from cholesterol) | Cortisol, aldosterone, testosterone, oestrogen, progesterone | Lipid-soluble; enter the cell, bind receptors in cytoplasm or nucleus → change gene expression |

**Control of secretion:** mostly **negative feedback** (e.g. high thyroxine suppresses TSH), plus nervous control (adrenal medulla) and blood levels of substances (glucose → insulin; Ca2+ → PTH).

## 10.2 Pituitary gland (hypophysis)

- Pea-sized gland (about 0.5 g) in the **sella turcica** of the sphenoid bone, attached to the hypothalamus by a stalk (**infundibulum**).
- Called the "**master gland**" because it controls several other glands – but it is itself controlled by the hypothalamus.
- Two lobes:

**Anterior pituitary (adenohypophysis)** – glandular; controlled by **releasing and inhibiting hormones** from the hypothalamus carried in the **hypophyseal portal veins**.

| Hormone | Target | Main actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Growth hormone (GH, somatotropin)** | Most tissues, bone, muscle, liver | Growth of bones and muscles (through IGF-1 from the liver); protein synthesis; raises blood glucose; fat breakdown |
| **Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH)** | Thyroid | Release of T3 and T4; growth of thyroid |
| **Adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH)** | Adrenal cortex | Release of cortisol (glucocorticoids) |
| **Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH)** | Ovary / testis | Female: growth of ovarian follicles, oestrogen secretion. Male: spermatogenesis |
| **Luteinising hormone (LH)** (ICSH in males) | Ovary / testis | Female: **ovulation**, forms corpus luteum (progesterone). Male: testosterone from Leydig cells |
| **Prolactin** | Breast | Milk production after childbirth |
| (Melanocyte-stimulating hormone) | Skin | Pigmentation |

**Posterior pituitary (neurohypophysis)** – nervous tissue; **stores and releases** two hormones **made in the hypothalamus**:

| Hormone | Actions |
| --- | --- |
| **Antidiuretic hormone (ADH, vasopressin)** | Water reabsorption in kidney collecting ducts → less, concentrated urine; vasoconstriction in large doses |
| **Oxytocin** | Contraction of the uterus during labour (positive feedback); milk ejection ("let-down") during suckling |

**Disorders**

- **GH excess:** in children – **gigantism**; in adults – **acromegaly** (enlarged hands, feet, jaw).
- **GH deficiency in children:** **pituitary dwarfism** (short but well-proportioned, normal intelligence).
- **ADH deficiency:** **diabetes insipidus** – large volumes of dilute urine, thirst.

## 10.3 Thyroid gland

- Butterfly-shaped gland in the front of the neck, below the larynx: two **lobes** joined by an **isthmus** in front of the trachea; about 20–30 g.
- Made of many spherical **follicles** lined by cuboidal cells and filled with **colloid** (thyroglobulin – stored hormone). **Parafollicular (C) cells** lie between follicles.

**Hormones**

| Hormone | From | Actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Thyroxine (T4)** and **triiodothyronine (T3)** – contain **iodine**; T3 is more active | Follicular cells | Raise the **basal metabolic rate (BMR)**, O2 use and heat production; needed for normal **growth and brain development** in children; increase heart rate; help carbohydrate, fat and protein metabolism |
| **Calcitonin** | Parafollicular (C) cells | **Lowers blood calcium** by reducing bone resorption (opposes PTH) |

- **Iodine** from food (iodised salt) is essential to make T3 and T4.
- **Control:** hypothalamus (TRH) → anterior pituitary (TSH) → thyroid (T3, T4); high T3/T4 inhibit TRH and TSH (negative feedback).

**Disorders**

| Condition | Features |
| --- | --- |
| **Hypothyroidism – cretinism** (in infants/children) | Stunted growth, mental retardation, coarse features, protruding tongue |
| **Hypothyroidism – myxoedema** (adults) | Low BMR, weight gain, cold intolerance, dry skin, puffy face, sluggishness |
| **Hyperthyroidism – Graves' disease** | High BMR, weight loss, heat intolerance, nervousness, fast heart rate, **exophthalmos** (bulging eyes) |
| **Simple (endemic) goitre** | Enlarged thyroid from **iodine deficiency** (common in hilly areas – prevented by iodised salt) |

## 10.4 Parathyroid glands

- **Four** small glands (two pairs) on the **back of the thyroid** lobes.
- Secrete **parathormone (PTH)** – the main hormone that **raises blood calcium**.

**Actions of PTH**

1. **Bone:** stimulates osteoclasts → calcium and phosphate released from bone.
2. **Kidney:** increases calcium reabsorption, increases phosphate excretion; activates **vitamin D** (calcitriol).
3. **Intestine:** (through calcitriol) increases calcium absorption.

Normal blood calcium is about 9–11 mg/dL. Low Ca2+ stimulates PTH; high Ca2+ stimulates calcitonin.

**Disorders:** **hypoparathyroidism** (e.g. accidental removal in thyroid surgery) → low calcium → **tetany** (muscle spasms, tingling, carpopedal spasm). **Hyperparathyroidism** → high calcium, weak bones (decalcification), kidney stones.

## 10.5 Adrenal (suprarenal) glands

- Two triangular glands, one **on top of each kidney**.
- Each has an outer **cortex** (about 80 %, from mesoderm, makes steroids) and an inner **medulla** (from nervous tissue, part of the sympathetic system).

```text
     ADRENAL GLAND (section)

   capsule
   ┌─────────────────────────────┐
   │ Zona glomerulosa → MINERALOCORTICOIDS (aldosterone)     "Salt"
   │ Zona fasciculata → GLUCOCORTICOIDS (cortisol)           "Sugar"
   │ Zona reticularis → SEX HORMONES (androgens)             "Sex"
   │   ┌───────────────────┐
   │   │ MEDULLA → adrenaline (≈80 %), noradrenaline          │
   │   └───────────────────┘
   └─────────────────────────────┘
   Memory: "Salt, Sugar, Sex – the deeper you go, the sweeter it gets"
```

**Adrenal cortex hormones**

| Group | Main hormone | Actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mineralocorticoids** | **Aldosterone** | Na+ and water reabsorption and K+ excretion in the DCT and collecting duct → raises blood volume and BP. Controlled mainly by the **renin–angiotensin system** and blood K+ |
| **Glucocorticoids** | **Cortisol (hydrocortisone)** | Raises blood glucose (gluconeogenesis); protein breakdown; fat redistribution; **anti-inflammatory** and **immunosuppressive**; helps the body resist **stress**. Controlled by ACTH (with a morning peak) |
| **Sex hormones** | Androgens (small amounts) | Pubic and axillary hair; sex drive in females |

**Adrenal medulla hormones – adrenaline (epinephrine) and noradrenaline (norepinephrine)**

- Released in response to sympathetic stimulation during stress, fear, exercise – the "**fight or flight**" response.
- Actions: increase heart rate and force, raise BP, dilate bronchi, raise blood glucose (glycogen breakdown), dilate pupils, divert blood to muscles, reduce gut activity.

**Disorders**

| Disorder | Cause | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Addison's disease** | Low cortisol and aldosterone (cortex destroyed) | Weakness, weight loss, low BP, low blood sugar, **bronze pigmentation** of skin |
| **Cushing's syndrome** | Excess cortisol (tumour or long-term steroid drugs) | **Moon face**, buffalo hump, central obesity, purple stretch marks, high BP, high blood sugar, weak bones |
| **Conn's syndrome** (primary aldosteronism) | Excess aldosterone | High BP, low K+ |
| **Phaeochromocytoma** | Tumour of adrenal medulla | Episodes of very high BP, palpitations, sweating |

## 10.6 Pancreas (endocrine part)

- The **islets of Langerhans** – about a million small clusters of cells scattered among the exocrine acini (about 1–2 % of the pancreas).

| Cell | Proportion (approx.) | Hormone | Actions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Alpha (α)** | 20 % | **Glucagon** | **Raises blood glucose**: glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis in the liver |
| **Beta (β)** | 70 % | **Insulin** | **Lowers blood glucose**: helps glucose enter muscle and fat cells; glycogen formation in liver and muscle; fat and protein synthesis; stops gluconeogenesis |
| **Delta (δ)** | 5–10 % | **Somatostatin** | Inhibits insulin and glucagon release; slows digestion |
| PP (F) cells | Small | Pancreatic polypeptide | Regulates pancreatic secretion |

```text
   Blood glucose control (negative feedback)

  After a meal: glucose ↑ → β-cells → INSULIN → cells take up glucose,
                liver stores glycogen → glucose falls to normal
  Fasting:      glucose ↓ → α-cells → GLUCAGON → liver breaks glycogen,
                makes new glucose → glucose rises to normal

  Normal fasting blood glucose ≈ 70–100 mg/dL
```

**Diabetes mellitus** – high blood glucose from lack of insulin or poor response to it.

- **Type 1** – autoimmune destruction of β-cells; usually children and young adults; needs insulin injections.
- **Type 2** – insulin resistance (most common, adults, linked to obesity); treated with diet, exercise, oral drugs (metformin), sometimes insulin.
- **Features:** the "3 Ps" – **polyuria** (much urine), **polydipsia** (thirst), **polyphagia** (hunger); glycosuria; weight loss; ketoacidosis in severe type 1; long-term damage to eyes, kidneys, nerves and blood vessels.
- **Hypoglycaemia** (too low glucose, e.g. insulin overdose) – sweating, tremor, confusion, coma.

## 10.7 Gonads

### Testes (male)

- **Leydig (interstitial) cells** between the seminiferous tubules secrete **testosterone** (under LH/ICSH).
- **Actions of testosterone:** development of male sex organs; **male secondary sexual characteristics** at puberty (beard, deep voice, muscle growth, body hair); spermatogenesis (with FSH); sex drive; protein building (anabolic).
- **Sertoli cells** secrete **inhibin** (inhibits FSH) and support sperm formation.

### Ovaries (female)

- **Oestrogens** (mainly oestradiol) from developing **follicles**: development of female sex organs and **female secondary sexual characteristics** (breast development, broad pelvis, fat distribution); growth of the uterine lining (proliferative phase); regulation of the menstrual cycle.
- **Progesterone** from the **corpus luteum** (and the placenta in pregnancy): prepares and maintains the uterine lining for implantation (secretory phase); maintains pregnancy; prepares breasts for milk; raises body temperature slightly after ovulation.
- **Relaxin** (pregnancy) – relaxes pelvic ligaments; **inhibin** – inhibits FSH.

**Control:** hypothalamus (GnRH) → anterior pituitary (FSH, LH) → gonads (sex hormones) → feedback to hypothalamus and pituitary. (Details of the cycle are in Unit 11.)

**Other endocrine tissues (for MCQs):** pineal gland (**melatonin** – sleep–wake rhythm), thymus (**thymosin** – T-cell maturation), kidney (erythropoietin, renin), heart (ANP), stomach and duodenum (gastrin, secretin, CCK), placenta (hCG).

## Remember

- Endocrine glands are ductless; hormones travel in blood to target cells.
- Anterior pituitary: GH, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, prolactin. Posterior pituitary stores ADH and oxytocin (made in the hypothalamus).
- GH excess: gigantism (child), acromegaly (adult); ADH lack: diabetes insipidus.
- Thyroid: T3, T4 (need iodine; raise BMR) and calcitonin (lowers Ca2+). Cretinism, myxoedema, Graves' disease, goitre.
- Parathyroids: PTH raises blood Ca2+; deficiency → tetany.
- Adrenal cortex: glomerulosa (aldosterone), fasciculata (cortisol), reticularis (androgens); medulla: adrenaline.
- Addison's = deficiency (bronze skin); Cushing's = cortisol excess (moon face).
- Islets: α → glucagon (raises glucose), β → insulin (lowers glucose), δ → somatostatin.
- Testes → testosterone (Leydig cells); ovaries → oestrogen (follicles), progesterone (corpus luteum).

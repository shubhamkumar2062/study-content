# Unit 9: Urinary system

Syllabus chapter 13: anatomy and physiology of the urinary system; physiology of urine formation; renin–angiotensin system; clearance tests and micturition.

The urinary system removes wastes from the blood and keeps the volume, composition and pH of body fluids constant.

**Organs:** two **kidneys** (make urine), two **ureters** (carry urine), one **urinary bladder** (stores urine), one **urethra** (passes urine out).

## 9.1 Kidneys – gross anatomy

- Bean-shaped, reddish-brown organs, each about 11 cm long, 6 cm wide, 3 cm thick and about 150 g.
- Lie on the posterior abdominal wall **behind the peritoneum (retroperitoneal)**, one on each side of the vertebral column, from about T12 to L3. The **right kidney is slightly lower** because the liver lies above it.
- The **adrenal gland** sits on top of each kidney.
- The medial concave border has the **hilum**, where the renal artery enters and the renal vein and ureter leave.
- Coverings: fibrous **renal capsule**, a layer of fat, and renal fascia.

```text
                 KIDNEY (longitudinal section)

            ___________________________
           /  capsule                   \
          /  CORTEX (outer, granular)    \
         |   ___     ___     ___          |
         |  /   \   /   \   /   \  <- renal pyramids (MEDULLA)
         | | ||| | | ||| | | ||| |        |   striped, apex = papilla
         |  \_v_/   \_v_/   \_v_/         |
         |    \ minor calyx /     renal   |
         |     \___________/      columns |
         |       MAJOR CALYX              |
          \        \ RENAL PELVIS ========= HILUM
           \        \________ ureter ↓    | renal artery in,
            \___________________________/   renal vein out
```

**Internal parts**

1. **Cortex** – outer granular layer; contains glomeruli and convoluted tubules.
2. **Medulla** – inner part made of 8–18 cone-shaped **renal pyramids** (striped because of loops of Henle and collecting ducts); their tips (**papillae**) open into the calyces. **Renal columns** are cortex extending between pyramids.
3. **Minor calyces → major calyces → renal pelvis → ureter.**

**Blood supply:** the kidneys receive about **1.2 litres of blood per minute (about 20–25 % of cardiac output)**.
Renal artery → segmental → interlobar → arcuate → interlobular arteries → **afferent arteriole** → **glomerulus** → **efferent arteriole** → **peritubular capillaries** (and vasa recta around loops of Henle) → veins → renal vein.

## 9.2 Nephron – the functional unit

Each kidney has about **1 million nephrons**.

```text
                 NEPHRON (draw and label – 10 marks)

    afferent arteriole →  ___
    efferent arteriole ← (GLOMERULUS)  ← tuft of capillaries
                         \  BOWMAN'S /    } RENAL CORPUSCLE
                          \_CAPSULE_/     } (in cortex)
                               |
                  PROXIMAL CONVOLUTED TUBULE (PCT)   ~~~~ (cortex)
                               |
                 DESCENDING LIMB  |        ^  ASCENDING LIMB
                    of loop       |        |  (thick part pumps
                                  |        |   Na+, Cl− out)
                                  \___U___/  LOOP OF HENLE (medulla)
                                           |
                  DISTAL CONVOLUTED TUBULE (DCT) ~~~~ (cortex)
                     (macula densa next to afferent arteriole =
                      juxtaglomerular apparatus)
                                           |
                                  COLLECTING DUCT → papilla → calyx
                                  (several nephrons share one)
```

| Part | Structure | Main job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Glomerulus** | Tuft of capillaries between afferent (wider) and efferent (narrower) arterioles | Filtration |
| **Bowman's capsule** | Double-walled cup; inner layer of **podocytes** | Collects filtrate |
| **Proximal convoluted tubule** | Cuboidal cells with brush border (microvilli), many mitochondria | Reabsorbs most water and solutes |
| **Loop of Henle** | Descending (permeable to water) and ascending (permeable to salts, not water) limbs | Concentration of urine (counter-current) |
| **Distal convoluted tubule** | Shorter, fewer microvilli | Fine adjustment under hormones (aldosterone); K+ and H+ secretion |
| **Collecting duct** | Receives several DCTs | Water reabsorption under **ADH** |

- **Cortical nephrons** (about 85 %) have short loops; **juxtamedullary nephrons** have long loops reaching deep into the medulla and help make concentrated urine.
- **Juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA):** **juxtaglomerular cells** (modified smooth muscle in the wall of the afferent arteriole; secrete **renin**) + **macula densa** (special DCT cells that sense Na+/Cl−). Controls GFR and BP.

## 9.3 Physiology of urine formation

Urine is formed in three steps:

```text
  BLOOD in glomerulus
        ↓ 1. GLOMERULAR FILTRATION (glomerulus → Bowman's capsule)
  FILTRATE (~180 L/day)
        ↓ 2. TUBULAR REABSORPTION (tubule → blood): ~99 % returned
        ↓ 3. TUBULAR SECRETION (blood → tubule): H+, K+, drugs added
  URINE (~1–1.5 L/day)
```

### Step 1: Glomerular filtration

- Blood plasma is filtered through the **filtration membrane** (capillary endothelium with pores + basement membrane + filtration slits between podocytes).
- **Filtered:** water, glucose, amino acids, salts, urea, creatinine, uric acid, small drugs.
- **Not filtered:** blood cells and plasma proteins (too large). So the filtrate is plasma without proteins.
- **Driving force – net filtration pressure (approximate values):**

| Pressure | Value | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure | About 55 mmHg | Pushes fluid out (favours filtration) |
| Plasma colloid osmotic pressure | About 30 mmHg | Pulls fluid back (opposes) |
| Capsular hydrostatic pressure | About 15 mmHg | Opposes |
| **Net filtration pressure** | 55 − (30 + 15) ≈ **10 mmHg** | — |

- Glomerular pressure is high because the efferent arteriole is narrower than the afferent.
- **Glomerular filtration rate (GFR)** ≈ **125 mL/min ≈ 180 L/day** in a healthy adult.
- GFR falls if BP falls very low (shock) or in kidney disease; it is kept fairly steady over a wide BP range by **autoregulation**.

### Step 2: Tubular reabsorption

About 99 % of the filtrate goes back into the blood (peritubular capillaries).

| Site | What is reabsorbed |
| --- | --- |
| **PCT** (most, about 65 % of filtrate) | All **glucose** and **amino acids** (active), most Na+ (active), Cl−, K+, HCO3−, water (osmosis), some urea |
| **Descending limb of loop** | Water |
| **Ascending limb** | Na+, Cl−, K+ (not water) → makes the medulla salty (hypertonic) |
| **DCT** | Na+ (under **aldosterone**, exchanged for K+), Ca2+ (under parathormone), water |
| **Collecting duct** | Water (under **ADH**), urea (inner medulla) |

- **Renal threshold for glucose** ≈ 180 mg/dL: above this blood level, carriers are saturated and glucose appears in urine (**glycosuria**, as in diabetes mellitus).
- **ADH** makes collecting ducts permeable to water → small volume of concentrated urine. No ADH → large volume of dilute urine (**diabetes insipidus**).

### Step 3: Tubular secretion

Substances are moved from the peritubular blood into the tubule: **H+** and **NH4+** (keeps blood pH normal), **K+** (DCT, under aldosterone), creatinine, and many **drugs** (penicillin, para-aminohippuric acid). This removes substances quickly and helps acid–base balance.

### Normal urine

| Property | Normal |
| --- | --- |
| Volume | About 1–1.5 L/day (varies with intake) |
| Colour | Pale yellow (urochrome) |
| pH | About 6 (range about 4.5–8) |
| Specific gravity | About 1.010–1.025 |
| Contents | About 95 % water; urea (main solid), creatinine, uric acid, Na+, K+, Cl−, phosphates |
| Abnormal | Glucose, proteins (albumin), blood, ketones, bile pigments, pus cells |

**Functions of the kidney (summary):** excretion of wastes (urea, creatinine, uric acid, drugs); water and electrolyte balance; acid–base balance; BP control (renin); RBC production (**erythropoietin**); activation of **vitamin D** (calcitriol); gluconeogenesis in starvation.

## 9.4 Renin–angiotensin system (RAAS)

```text
  BP ↓ / blood volume ↓ / Na+ ↓ at macula densa / sympathetic stimulation
        ↓
  JG cells of the kidney release RENIN (enzyme)
        ↓
  ANGIOTENSINOGEN (plasma protein from the liver)
        ↓  renin
  ANGIOTENSIN I (inactive)
        ↓  ANGIOTENSIN-CONVERTING ENZYME (ACE) – mainly in the lungs
  ANGIOTENSIN II (active)
        ↓
   ┌──────────────┬──────────────────────┬──────────────────────┐
   Strong         Adrenal cortex →        Thirst ↑ and           Posterior pituitary
   VASOCONSTRICTION  ALDOSTERONE →        drinking               → ADH → water
   (resistance ↑)    Na+ and water                                retention
                     retained, K+ lost
   └──────────────┴──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┘
        ↓
  Blood volume and BP RISE → renin release falls (negative feedback)
```

**Importance:** maintains BP, blood volume and Na+ balance. Over-activity causes **hypertension**. Drugs: **ACE inhibitors** (enalapril, ramipril), **angiotensin receptor blockers** (losartan), aldosterone antagonists (spironolactone).

## 9.5 Clearance tests

**Renal clearance** of a substance is the volume of plasma completely cleared of that substance by the kidneys per minute.

**Formula:** C = (U × V) ÷ P

- C = clearance (mL/min)
- U = concentration of the substance in urine (mg/mL)
- V = urine flow rate (mL/min)
- P = concentration in plasma (mg/mL)

**Worked example:** creatinine in urine 1.2 mg/mL, urine flow 1.0 mL/min, plasma creatinine 0.01 mg/mL → C = (1.2 × 1.0) ÷ 0.01 = **120 mL/min** (normal).

| Test | What it measures | Approximate normal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Inulin clearance** | Exact GFR (inulin is filtered, not reabsorbed or secreted) | About 125 mL/min |
| **Creatinine clearance** | Practical estimate of GFR (endogenous, no injection needed) | About 110–125 mL/min |
| **Urea clearance** | Kidney function (urea partly reabsorbed, so value is lower than GFR) | About 70 mL/min |
| **PAH (para-aminohippuric acid) clearance** | Effective renal plasma flow (filtered and secreted almost completely) | About 600–650 mL/min |

**Uses:** assess kidney function, detect early kidney disease, and **adjust drug doses** in renal impairment (important for pharmacists – e.g. aminoglycosides, digoxin). Blood urea and serum creatinine are simple blood tests that also rise in kidney failure.

## 9.6 Ureters, urinary bladder and urethra

- **Ureters:** two muscular tubes about 25–30 cm long from the renal pelvis to the bladder; urine moves by **peristalsis**; they enter the bladder obliquely, which prevents back-flow.
- **Urinary bladder:** hollow muscular bag in the pelvis behind the pubic symphysis. Wall of smooth **detrusor muscle**; lining of **transitional epithelium** (stretches); the **trigone** is the smooth triangular area between the two ureteric openings and the urethral opening. Normally holds about 400–600 mL comfortably.
- **Urethra:** carries urine out. **Female** – about 4 cm (short, so urinary infections are more common in women). **Male** – about 18–20 cm, also carries semen; passes through the prostate and penis.
- **Sphincters:** **internal urethral sphincter** (smooth muscle, involuntary) and **external urethral sphincter** (skeletal muscle, voluntary, supplied by the pudendal nerve).

## 9.7 Micturition

**Micturition** (urination) is the emptying of the urinary bladder. It is a **spinal reflex** controlled by higher brain centres.

```text
  Bladder fills → stretch receptors in the wall (urge felt at about
  200–400 mL)
        ↓  sensory impulses (pelvic nerves)
  Micturition centre in the SACRAL spinal cord (S2–S4)
        ↓  parasympathetic (pelvic) nerves
  DETRUSOR CONTRACTS + internal sphincter relaxes
        ↓
  Impulses also reach the brain (pons, cortex) → person decides:
   - convenient → external sphincter relaxed voluntarily → URINE PASSED
   - not convenient → external sphincter kept closed, reflex inhibited
```

- **Sympathetic** nerves relax the detrusor and close the internal sphincter (help storage).
- **Infants** have no voluntary control until the nerve pathways mature (about 2–3 years).
- **Disorders:** **incontinence** (loss of control – old age, spinal injury), **retention** (cannot empty – enlarged prostate, after surgery), cystitis, kidney stones (renal calculi).

**Common kidney disorders:** renal failure (acute, chronic – needs dialysis), glomerulonephritis, nephrotic syndrome (heavy protein loss), urinary tract infection.

## Remember

- Kidneys are retroperitoneal, T12–L3; right kidney lower; adrenal on top.
- Nephron = renal corpuscle (glomerulus + Bowman's capsule) + PCT + loop of Henle + DCT → collecting duct; about 1 million per kidney.
- Urine formation: filtration → reabsorption → secretion.
- GFR ≈ 125 mL/min ≈ 180 L/day; urine ≈ 1.5 L/day; net filtration pressure ≈ 10 mmHg.
- PCT reabsorbs all glucose and amino acids; renal threshold for glucose ≈ 180 mg/dL.
- ADH → water reabsorption in collecting duct; aldosterone → Na+ in, K+ out in DCT.
- RAAS: renin → angiotensin I → (ACE) → angiotensin II → vasoconstriction + aldosterone.
- Clearance C = UV/P; inulin = GFR; PAH = renal plasma flow.
- Micturition reflex centre S2–S4; parasympathetic contracts the detrusor; external sphincter voluntary.

# Microscopical observation of microbes (ready-made slides)

**Aim:** To observe and identify different micro-organisms using ready-made stained slides under the compound microscope.

## Principle

Microbes are too small to see with the eye. A compound microscope with the oil-immersion objective (100×) and a 10× eyepiece magnifies 1000 times. Stains make cells visible: in the **Gram stain**, Gram-positive bacteria keep crystal violet and look purple; Gram-negative bacteria lose it and take the pink counter-stain (safranin). In the **Ziehl–Neelsen (acid-fast)** stain, mycobacteria hold carbol fuchsin and look red on a blue background.

## Requirements

Compound microscope, cedar wood or immersion oil, lens paper, xylene (for cleaning), ready-made slides: Staphylococcus, Streptococcus, E. coli, Bacillus, M. tuberculosis (ZN), yeast (Candida), a mould (Aspergillus/Penicillium), malaria parasite in blood smear, helminth egg (Ascaris).

## Procedure

1. Place the microscope on a firm table; switch on the light and centre the slide on the stage.
2. Focus first with the low-power (10×) objective using the coarse knob.
3. Move to high power (40×) and refine with the fine knob.
4. For bacteria, put a drop of immersion oil on the slide and turn the 100× objective into the oil; focus with the fine knob only.
5. Observe shape, arrangement and colour; draw what you see in a circle.
6. After use, wipe oil from the objective with lens paper, lower the stage and switch off.

## Observations

| Slide | What is seen | Identification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Staphylococcus | Purple round cells in grape-like clusters | Gram-positive cocci in clusters |
| Streptococcus | Purple round cells in chains | Gram-positive cocci in chains |
| E. coli | Pink short rods, scattered | Gram-negative bacilli |
| Bacillus | Purple large rods, often in chains | Gram-positive bacilli |
| M. tuberculosis (ZN) | Thin red rods on a blue background | Acid-fast bacilli |
| Candida | Oval budding cells (purple) | Yeast |
| Aspergillus | Branched threads with spore heads | Mould (fungus) |
| Malaria smear | Ring forms inside red blood cells | Plasmodium |
| Ascaris egg | Oval egg with thick, bumpy shell | Roundworm egg |

Total magnification = objective × eyepiece (e.g. 100 × 10 = 1000×).

## Result

Different micro-organisms were observed and identified from ready-made slides as recorded above.

## Precautions

- Use oil only with the 100× objective; clean it after use.
- Never lower the objective while looking through the eyepiece (it can break the slide).
- Handle slides by the edges; return them to the box.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the total magnification with oil immersion?** About 1000× (100× objective × 10× eyepiece).
2. **Why do Gram-positive bacteria stay purple?** Their thick peptidoglycan wall holds the crystal violet–iodine complex.
3. **Which organisms are acid-fast?** Mycobacteria (TB and leprosy bacilli).
4. **Why is oil used?** It has nearly the same refractive index as glass, so less light is lost and the image is clearer.
5. **Name a disease caused by a yeast.** Oral thrush (Candida).

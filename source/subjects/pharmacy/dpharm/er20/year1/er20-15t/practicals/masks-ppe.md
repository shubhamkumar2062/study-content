# Types of masks and PPE: wearing, removing and disposal

**Aim:** To identify different types of masks and personal protective equipment (PPE) and to demonstrate correct donning, doffing and disposal.

## Principle

PPE forms a barrier between the wearer and infectious material. A **cloth mask** reduces spread of droplets from the wearer; a **surgical (3-ply) mask** protects against droplets and splashes; an **N95 respirator** filters at least 95% of very small airborne particles when it fits tightly. Wrong removal can contaminate the wearer, so the order of putting on and taking off matters.

## Requirements

Cloth mask, 3-ply surgical mask, N95 respirator, face shield, goggles, gown or coverall, gloves, cap and shoe covers, hand rub, yellow and red bio-medical waste bags.

## Procedure

**Wearing a surgical mask:** clean hands → check sides (coloured side out, metal strip on top) → place over nose and mouth → fix ear loops or ties → press the metal strip on the nose → do not touch the front while wearing → change when wet.

**N95 fit:** hold the respirator, place it under the chin, pull straps over the head, mould the nose clip with both hands, and do a seal check (breathe out — no air should leak at the edges).

**Donning sequence (full PPE):**

1. Hand hygiene
2. Gown/coverall
3. Mask or respirator
4. Goggles or face shield
5. Gloves (over the gown cuffs)

**Doffing sequence:**

1. Gloves (glove-in-glove technique)
2. Goggles or face shield (hold by the strap)
3. Gown (roll inside-out, away from the body)
4. Mask or respirator (by the straps, without touching the front)
5. Hand hygiene (also between steps if hands get contaminated)

**Disposal:** used masks, gloves and gowns from patient care go into the bio-medical waste stream as per the hospital's colour coding; at home, used masks are wrapped in paper and put in a closed dustbin.

## Observations

| Type | Protects against | Reusable? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cloth mask | Wearer's droplets (source control) | Yes, after washing |
| Surgical 3-ply mask | Droplets and splashes | No |
| N95 respirator | Airborne particles (TB, aerosols) | No (single use in routine care) |
| Face shield / goggles | Splashes to eyes and face | Reusable after disinfection |
| Gown/coverall | Body contamination | Disposable or washable types |
| Gloves | Hand contamination | No |

## Result

Different masks and PPE were identified, and correct donning, doffing and disposal were demonstrated.

## Precautions

- Clean hands before wearing and after removing PPE.
- Never pull a mask under the chin and back again; never reuse disposable masks.
- Beards prevent an N95 from sealing.

## Viva questions

1. **What does "N95" mean?** Filters at least 95% of airborne particles when properly fitted.
2. **Which item is removed first?** Gloves.
3. **Which item is removed last?** The mask or respirator, followed by hand hygiene.
4. **What is a seal check?** Breathing out after wearing an N95 to feel for leaks at the edges.
5. **What is the main use of a cloth mask?** To reduce spread of droplets from the wearer.

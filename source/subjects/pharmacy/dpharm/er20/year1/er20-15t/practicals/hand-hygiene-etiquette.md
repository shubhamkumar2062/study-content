# Personal hygiene and etiquette: hand-washing, cough and sneeze etiquette

**Aim:** To demonstrate correct hand-washing and hand-rub technique, and cough and sneeze etiquette.

## Principle

Hands carry microbes from faeces, surfaces and respiratory secretions to the mouth, eyes and other people. Washing with soap and water removes dirt and microbes; alcohol-based hand rub (60–80% alcohol) kills many microbes when hands are not visibly dirty. Covering coughs and sneezes stops droplets that spread influenza, COVID-19, TB and colds.

## Requirements

Wash basin with running water, plain soap, alcohol-based hand rub, disposable paper towels, a fluorescent lotion and UV lamp (optional) or a coloured paint to show missed areas, tissues, a covered dustbin, chart showing the WHO hand-wash steps.

## Procedure — hand-washing with soap and water (about 40–60 seconds)

1. Wet hands with clean running water and apply enough soap.
2. Rub palm to palm.
3. Right palm over the back of the left hand with fingers interlaced, and vice versa.
4. Palm to palm with fingers interlaced.
5. Backs of fingers against the opposite palm with fingers interlocked.
6. Rotational rubbing of each thumb clasped in the other palm.
7. Rotational rubbing of the fingertips in the opposite palm (cleans nails), and wrists.
8. Rinse well, dry with a single-use towel, and use the towel to turn off the tap.

**Hand rub:** apply a palmful, follow steps 2–7 until hands are dry (about 20–30 seconds).

**Cough and sneeze etiquette**

1. Cover mouth and nose with a tissue; throw the tissue in a closed bin at once.
2. If no tissue, cough or sneeze into the bent elbow, not the hands.
3. Clean hands afterwards.
4. Wear a mask when sick with cough or cold; keep distance from others; stay home if unwell.

Commonly missed areas (seen with paint or UV lotion): thumbs, fingertips, backs of hands, wrists.

**Five key moments for hand-washing at home:** before eating, before preparing food, before feeding a child, after using the toilet, after cleaning a child or touching waste. In health care, WHO lists five moments: before touching a patient, before a clean/aseptic procedure, after body fluid exposure, after touching a patient, after touching patient surroundings.

## Result

Correct hand-washing, hand-rub and cough/sneeze etiquette were demonstrated and practised.

## Precautions

- Remove rings and watches; keep nails short.
- Use soap and water (not rub) when hands are visibly dirty.
- Do not touch the face with unwashed hands.

## Viva questions

1. **How long should hand-washing take?** About 40–60 seconds (hand rub 20–30 seconds).
2. **Which diseases does hand-washing prevent?** Diarrhoea, cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A, worm infections, respiratory infections.
3. **When should soap and water be used instead of hand rub?** When hands are visibly soiled.
4. **Where should you sneeze if you have no tissue?** Into the bent elbow.
5. **Which areas are most often missed?** Thumbs, fingertips and backs of hands.

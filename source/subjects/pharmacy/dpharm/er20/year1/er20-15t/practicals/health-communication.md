# Health communication on communicable diseases (posters, podcasts, slides, short films)

**Aim:** To prepare health-communication material in a regional language on five communicable diseases — their signs, symptoms and prevention — for mass education.

## Principle

**Information, Education and Communication (IEC)** gives people knowledge; **behaviour change communication (BCC)** helps them adopt healthy practices. Good messages are simple, correct, in the local language, culturally acceptable, focused on one action, and repeated through many channels (posters, audio podcasts, videos, slides, social media, wall paintings, street plays).

## Requirements

Computer or smartphone with free editing apps, microphone, chart paper and colours, reference notes on the diseases, a local-language translator if needed.

## Procedure

1. Choose five diseases, for example dengue, tuberculosis, diarrhoea, COVID-19 and typhoid (or malaria, hepatitis, HIV).
2. For each disease write the key messages: what it is, how it spreads, warning signs, what to do, how to prevent it.
3. Choose the audience (school children, mothers, farmers, elderly) and the language (e.g. Hindi or the local dialect).
4. Prepare one product per disease: a poster, a 1–2 minute audio podcast, a 3–5 slide presentation, or a short video.
5. Pre-test the material with a few people and correct confusing parts.
6. Present to the class; collect feedback on clarity and accuracy.

## Observations — sample message plan

| Disease | Key message | Action asked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dengue | Day-biting mosquito breeds in clean stored water | Empty coolers and pots every week; use repellents; see a doctor for high fever |
| Tuberculosis | Cough for 2 weeks or more may be TB; treatment is free | Get sputum tested; complete the full course |
| Diarrhoea | Dehydration kills children | Give ORS and zinc; continue feeding; wash hands |
| COVID-19 / flu | Spreads by droplets and air | Cover coughs, wear a mask when ill, get vaccinated |
| Typhoid | Spreads through unsafe water and food | Drink boiled or chlorinated water; wash hands; eat hot food |

**Checklist for good material:** correct facts (checked against notes); one clear action; large readable text; local language; pictures over words; contact of nearest health centre or helpline.

## Result

Health-communication materials on five communicable diseases were prepared in a regional language and presented.

## Precautions

- Do not spread unverified claims or fear; avoid blaming or stigmatising patients.
- Use only images you have made or are free to use.
- Mention when to seek medical help.

## Viva questions

1. **What is IEC?** Information, Education and Communication — activities that give health knowledge to the public.
2. **What is BCC?** Behaviour change communication — messages aimed at changing a specific behaviour.
3. **Why use the regional language?** People understand and trust messages in their own language.
4. **What is pre-testing?** Trying material on a small group of the target audience before wide use.
5. **Name three channels for health messages.** Posters, radio/podcasts, videos (also street plays, social media).

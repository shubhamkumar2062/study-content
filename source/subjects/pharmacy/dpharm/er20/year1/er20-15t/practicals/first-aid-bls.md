# First aid and Basic Life Support (CPR, AED, choking)

**Aim:** To learn the basics of first aid and to practise Basic Life Support (BLS) — recognising sudden cardiac arrest, giving CPR, using an AED, and relieving foreign-body airway obstruction (choking).

> Practise only on a manikin. Follow the current resuscitation guidelines taught by your instructor; techniques are updated from time to time.

## Principle

**First aid** is the immediate care given to an injured or suddenly ill person before medical help arrives — to preserve life, prevent the condition from getting worse, and promote recovery.

In **sudden cardiac arrest (SCA)** the heart stops pumping; the brain starts to be damaged within minutes. **Cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR)** — chest compressions with or without rescue breaths — keeps blood flowing to the brain and heart. An **automated external defibrillator (AED)** gives an electric shock that can restore a normal rhythm. Early CPR and early defibrillation greatly improve survival (the "chain of survival").

## Requirements

Adult and infant CPR manikins, AED trainer, pocket mask, gloves, first-aid box, audio-visual material.

## Procedure — adult BLS

1. **Scene safety:** make sure the area is safe for you and the victim.
2. **Check response:** tap the shoulders and shout "Are you all right?"
3. **Call for help:** shout for help; ask someone to call the ambulance (108 or 112) and bring an AED.
4. **Check breathing** for no more than 10 seconds (no breathing or only gasping = cardiac arrest).
5. **Start chest compressions:** heel of one hand on the centre of the chest (lower half of the breastbone), other hand on top, arms straight; push hard and fast — depth about 5–6 cm, rate 100–120 per minute; allow the chest to rise fully between compressions; minimise pauses.
6. **Rescue breaths (if trained):** open the airway by head tilt–chin lift, give 2 breaths, each over about 1 second, watching the chest rise. Ratio **30 compressions : 2 breaths**. Untrained rescuers may give compressions only (hands-only CPR).
7. **AED:** switch on and follow voice prompts; stick pads (upper right chest, lower left side); make sure no one touches the victim during analysis and shock; resume CPR immediately after the shock.
8. Continue until the victim breathes normally, help arrives, or you are exhausted.

**Children and infants:** compression depth about one-third of the chest (about 5 cm in children, about 4 cm in infants; two fingers or two thumbs for infants); single rescuer 30:2, two trained rescuers 15:2.

**Choking (FBAO):**

- Conscious adult who cannot speak or cough effectively: give up to 5 back blows between the shoulder blades, then up to 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich manoeuvre); repeat.
- Infant: 5 back blows face-down on your forearm, then 5 chest thrusts; no abdominal thrusts.
- If the person becomes unconscious: start CPR.

## Result

## Precautions

- Ensure your own safety first; wear gloves when possible.
- Do not stop CPR to check the pulse repeatedly.
- No one should touch the victim while the AED analyses or shocks.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the compression-to-breath ratio for an adult?** 30:2.
2. **What is the rate of chest compressions?** 100–120 per minute.
3. **What does an AED do?** Analyses the heart rhythm and gives a shock if it is shockable.
4. **What is the Heimlich manoeuvre?** Abdominal thrusts to expel an object blocking the airway.
5. **What is hands-only CPR?** Continuous chest compressions without rescue breaths, for untrained bystanders.

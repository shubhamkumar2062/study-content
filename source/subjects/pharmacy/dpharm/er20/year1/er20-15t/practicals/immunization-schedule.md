# National immunization schedule (children and adults)

**Aim:** To prepare a chart of the National Immunization Schedule for children and pregnant women, an adult vaccine schedule, and a list of vaccines not included in the national programme.

> This is a chart and counselling activity. India updates its schedule from time to time; check your chart against the latest MoHFW immunisation card before display.

## Principle

Vaccines give active artificial immunity. Doses must be given at the right age and interval for full protection. The Universal Immunization Programme (UIP) gives these vaccines free. A pharmacist uses the schedule to remind parents of due doses and to advise adults on vaccines they need.

## Requirements

Chart paper, coloured pens or a computer, the current MoHFW immunisation card, sample empty vaccine vials or pictures, a model vaccine carrier with ice packs.

## Procedure

1. Draw a table with columns: Age – Vaccine – Dose – Route – Site.
2. Fill the childhood schedule (below), using one colour for each disease group.
3. Add the pregnant-women row (Td).
4. Make a second chart for adults and for vaccines outside the UIP.
5. Add a box on cold chain (+2 to +8 °C, VVM) and common minor side effects.
6. Role play: one student is a mother with a 10-week-old baby; another, as pharmacist, explains the due vaccines and the next visit.

## Observations — chart content

**Children (UIP)**

| Age | Vaccines |
| --- | --- |
| At birth | BCG, OPV-0, Hepatitis B birth dose (within 24 hours) |
| 6 weeks | OPV-1, Pentavalent-1, Rotavirus-1, fIPV-1, PCV-1 |
| 10 weeks | OPV-2, Pentavalent-2, Rotavirus-2 |
| 14 weeks | OPV-3, Pentavalent-3, Rotavirus-3, fIPV-2, PCV-2 |
| 9–12 months | MR-1, JE-1 (endemic districts), PCV booster, Vitamin A (1st dose) |
| 16–24 months | MR-2, JE-2, DPT booster-1, OPV booster, Vitamin A (2nd dose, then every 6 months till 5 years) |
| 5–6 years | DPT booster-2 |
| 10 years and 16 years | Td |
| Pregnant women | Td-1 early in pregnancy, Td-2 four weeks later (or one Td booster if vaccinated in the last 3 years) |

Pentavalent = DPT + Hepatitis B + Hib. Routes: BCG intradermal (left upper arm); OPV and rotavirus oral; pentavalent, PCV, Td intramuscular (thigh in infants); MR subcutaneous; fIPV intradermal.

**Adults (advised, mostly outside UIP):** Td booster every 10 years and after dirty wounds; yearly influenza vaccine for those over 60, chronic patients, health workers and pregnant women; hepatitis B (health workers, high-risk groups); pneumococcal vaccine for elderly and chronic patients; rabies pre-exposure for vets and animal handlers.

**Vaccines not in the national programme (examples):** varicella, hepatitis A, typhoid conjugate, MMR (UIP gives MR), influenza, meningococcal, HPV (being introduced for adolescent girls — check the current status), cholera, herpes zoster, rabies pre-exposure.

## Result

A chart of the national immunization schedule, adult vaccines and non-UIP vaccines was prepared and used for a counselling role play.

## Precautions

- Use only the current official schedule; note the date of the source.
- Never advise skipping doses; a missed dose is given at the next visit without restarting.
- Live vaccines are avoided in pregnancy and severe immunodeficiency.

## Viva questions

1. **What does pentavalent vaccine protect against?** Diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, hepatitis B and Hib.
2. **Which vaccines are given at birth?** BCG, OPV-0 and hepatitis B birth dose.
3. **Why is MR given twice?** The second dose catches children who did not respond to the first and raises protection.
4. **What is Mission Indradhanush?** A catch-up drive to vaccinate children and pregnant women who missed doses.
5. **Which vaccine is given to pregnant women?** Td (tetanus–diphtheria), two doses or one booster.
6. **What is a VVM?** Vaccine vial monitor; shows whether the vial has had too much heat.

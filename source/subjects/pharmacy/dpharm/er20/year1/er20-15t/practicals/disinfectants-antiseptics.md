# Marketed disinfectants, antiseptics, fumigating agents, antilarval agents and repellents

**Aim:** To study marketed preparations of disinfectants, antiseptics, fumigating agents, antilarval agents and mosquito repellents, noting their active ingredients, uses and precautions.

## Principle

- **Disinfectant:** a chemical that kills or removes harmful microbes on **non-living** surfaces and objects (floors, instruments).
- **Antiseptic:** a chemical safe enough to apply to **living** tissue (skin, wounds) to reduce microbes.
- **Sterilisation:** killing all forms of life including spores (autoclave, not chemicals at routine strength).
- **Fumigation:** disinfection of a closed room with a gas or vapour.
- **Antilarval agent (larvicide):** kills mosquito larvae in water.
- **Repellent:** keeps insects away from the skin or a room.

## Requirements

Labels or packs of marketed products (collect from local shops/pharmacy), chart paper.

## Procedure

1. Collect at least two products of each type.
2. Read each label and note: active ingredient and strength, category, directions, dilution, precautions, storage.
3. Prepare a table and a chart.
4. Prepare a 1 in 20 dilution of a disinfectant concentrate as instructed by the teacher (e.g. 5 ml made up to 100 ml) and label it.

## Observations

| Category | Active ingredient (examples) | Uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phenolic disinfectants | Cresol (as soap solution), phenol | Floors, drains, toilets |
| Chloroxylenol antiseptic | Chloroxylenol (PCMX) solution | Skin, cuts, bath water, general household |
| Chlorhexidine + cetrimide | Chlorhexidine gluconate with cetrimide | Wound cleaning, skin, surgical scrub |
| Iodophor | Povidone-iodine 5–10% | Wounds, pre-operative skin, dog bites |
| Alcohols | Ethanol or isopropyl alcohol 60–80% | Hand rub, skin before injection |
| Chlorine compounds | Sodium hypochlorite, bleaching powder | Surfaces, blood spills, water disinfection |
| Hydrogen peroxide | 3% (about 10 volume) solution | Wound cleaning |
| Aldehydes | Glutaraldehyde 2%, formaldehyde | Instrument disinfection; formaldehyde for fumigation |
| Quaternary ammonium | Benzalkonium chloride | Antiseptic, surface cleaner, preservative |
| Fumigating agents | Formaldehyde vapour (formalin with potassium permanganate), hydrogen peroxide vapour | Operation theatres, isolation rooms |
| Antilarval agents | Temephos, Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis (Bti), larvivorous fish (Gambusia, guppy), mineral oil | Water tanks, ponds, drains |
| Repellents | DEET, picaridin, citronella oil creams; allethrin/transfluthrin coils and vaporisers; permethrin-treated nets | Personal protection, rooms |

## Result

The active ingredients, uses and precautions of marketed disinfectants, antiseptics, fumigants, larvicides and repellents were studied and tabulated.

## Precautions

- Never mix bleach with acids or ammonia-containing cleaners (toxic gases).
- Use the dilution on the label; strong solutions damage skin.
- Keep products away from children; repellents are not applied on wounds or near eyes.
- Ventilate rooms well after using coils and vaporisers; fumigated rooms are entered only after the vapour is cleared.

## Viva questions

1. **Difference between antiseptic and disinfectant?** Antiseptic is used on living tissue; disinfectant on non-living objects.
2. **Which concentration of alcohol is best as a hand rub?** About 60–80%.
3. **Name a chemical used for fumigation.** Formaldehyde (formalin).
4. **What is Gambusia?** A larvivorous fish that eats mosquito larvae.
5. **Name a common skin repellent.** DEET (N,N-diethyl-meta-toluamide).

# Role of the pharmacist in disaster management

**Aim:** To study the disaster management cycle and the role of the pharmacist in preparedness and response, through a chart or case study.

## Principle

A **disaster** is a serious disruption of a community's functioning, causing loss of life, property or environment, beyond the community's ability to cope with its own resources. Disasters may be natural (earthquake, flood, landslide, cloudburst, cyclone, epidemic) or man-made (fire, industrial gas leak, road and rail accidents, war). Himachal Pradesh is prone to earthquakes, landslides, flash floods and cloudbursts.

India's **Disaster Management Act, 2005** set up the **National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA)** headed by the Prime Minister, State Disaster Management Authorities, District authorities and the **National Disaster Response Force (NDRF)**.

## Requirements

Chart paper, maps of the district, a sample emergency drug kit list, newspaper reports of a recent disaster, the district disaster management plan (if available).

## Procedure

1. Draw the disaster management cycle (below).
2. List the medicines and supplies needed after a flood or earthquake.
3. Study one recent disaster from newspapers and write what health problems arose.
4. Prepare a table of pharmacist roles in each phase.
5. Hold a mock drill: set up a dispensing point in a relief camp and prioritise patients.

```text
        PREPAREDNESS  --->  RESPONSE
             ^                  |
             |                  v
        MITIGATION  <---   RECOVERY
   (the cycle repeats; prevention and mitigation reduce future harm)
```

## Observations

| Phase | Pharmacist's role |
| --- | --- |
| Mitigation and preparedness | Prepare emergency drug kits and a stock list; maintain buffer stocks and a cold-chain backup; train staff and volunteers in first aid; plan alternative supply routes; keep a list of patients on essential long-term drugs (insulin, ART, TB, dialysis) |
| Response | Dispense and manage donated medicines; supply ORS, antibiotics, analgesics, tetanus vaccine, chlorine tablets; maintain cold chain; prevent outbreaks (water purification, hygiene advice); give first aid and counselling; keep records |
| Recovery | Restore supply chains; continue care of chronic patients; dispose of expired or unusable donated drugs safely; support mental health referrals; review and update the plan |

**Common health problems after floods:** diarrhoea and cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A and E, leptospirosis, skin and eye infections, malaria and dengue, snake bites, injuries.

**Essential relief items:** ORS, zinc, paracetamol, antibiotics as per protocol, antiseptics, dressings, tetanus vaccine, chlorine tablets/bleaching powder, insulin and chronic medicines, sanitary pads, mosquito nets.

## Result

The disaster management cycle and the role of the pharmacist in each phase were studied and presented.

## Precautions

- Accept donated medicines only if needed, labelled, in date and in a known language.
- Keep records of all medicines received and dispensed.
- Follow instructions of the incident command system.

## Viva questions

1. **Define disaster.** A serious disruption of a community causing losses beyond its capacity to cope with its own resources.
2. **Which Act governs disaster management in India?** The Disaster Management Act, 2005.
3. **Name the four phases of the disaster cycle.** Mitigation, preparedness, response, recovery.
4. **Which diseases rise after floods?** Diarrhoeal diseases, typhoid, hepatitis A/E, leptospirosis, malaria, dengue.
5. **What is NDRF?** National Disaster Response Force — a specialised force for disaster response.

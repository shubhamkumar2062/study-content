# First aid in medical emergencies: snake bite, dog bite, insecticide poisoning, fractures, burns, epilepsy

**Aim:** To learn and demonstrate first-aid and emergency measures for common medical emergencies.

## Principle

Correct first aid in the first minutes reduces harm and buys time to reach hospital. Many harmful traditional practices (tourniquets, cutting, applying toothpaste on burns) make things worse; the pharmacist must replace them with correct advice.

## Requirements

First-aid box, bandages, triangular sling, splints (or cardboard/rolled newspaper), clean cloth, cling film, soap and water, povidone-iodine, gloves, charts, role-play cards.

## Procedure and observations

| Emergency | Do | Do not | Definitive care |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Snake bite** | Reassure; keep the person still; immobilise the bitten limb like a fracture; remove rings, watch, tight clothing; carry to hospital quickly; note time and the snake's appearance if safe | Tourniquet, cutting, sucking, ice, herbal remedies, trying to catch the snake | Polyvalent anti-snake venom (covers cobra, krait, Russell's viper, saw-scaled viper) in hospital |
| **Dog (animal) bite** | Wash the wound with soap and running water for about 15 minutes; apply povidone-iodine or alcohol; go to a health centre the same day | Chilli, oil, turmeric on the wound; early stitching | Anti-rabies vaccine as per category; rabies immunoglobulin for deep (category III) bites; tetanus cover |
| **Insecticide (organophosphate) poisoning** | Remove from exposure; remove contaminated clothes; wash skin with soap and water; keep airway clear, recovery position if drowsy; take the container to hospital | Inducing vomiting; giving milk or oil | Atropine and pralidoxime in hospital; observe for breathing failure |
| **Fracture** | Stop bleeding; immobilise the joint above and below with a splint; support an arm in a sling; check circulation below the splint | Trying to straighten the bone; moving a suspected spine injury | X-ray, reduction, plaster or surgery |
| **Burns** | Cool under clean running tap water for about 20 minutes; remove rings and loose clothing (not stuck cloth); cover loosely with cling film or a clean cloth; give fluids if conscious | Ice, butter, toothpaste, oil; bursting blisters | Hospital for large, deep, face, hand, genital or electrical burns; area by the "rule of nines" |
| **Epileptic seizure** | Stay calm; protect the head with something soft; move hard objects away; loosen tight clothing; time the seizure; after it stops, turn into recovery position | Putting anything in the mouth; holding the person down; giving water during the fit | Call emergency if the fit lasts over 5 minutes, repeats, or occurs in water or pregnancy |

**Rule of nines (adult):** head and neck 9%, each arm 9%, front of trunk 18%, back of trunk 18%, each leg 18%, genitals 1%.

**Recovery position:** person on their side, top knee bent, head tilted back to keep the airway open — used for an unconscious person who is breathing.

## Result

First-aid steps for snake bite, dog bite, insecticide poisoning, fractures, burns and epilepsy were learnt and demonstrated through role plays.

## Precautions

- Protect yourself (gloves, avoid contact with poison).
- Do not give anything by mouth to an unconscious person.
- Always arrange transport to a health facility after first aid.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is a tourniquet not used in snake bite?** It can cause gangrene and does not stop venom effectively.
2. **What is the first step after a dog bite?** Wash the wound with soap and running water for about 15 minutes.
3. **What is the antidote treatment for organophosphate poisoning?** Atropine and pralidoxime (in hospital).
4. **How long should a burn be cooled?** About 20 minutes under running water.
5. **What should not be done during a seizure?** Put anything in the mouth or hold the person down.

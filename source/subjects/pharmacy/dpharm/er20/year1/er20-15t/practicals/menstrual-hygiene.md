# Menstrual hygiene and products used

**Aim:** To study menstrual hygiene practices and products, and to prepare counselling and awareness material.

## Principle

Menstruation is a normal monthly process. Poor menstrual hygiene can cause genital and urinary infections, skin rashes and school absence; myths and stigma stop girls from asking for help. Safe, clean absorbent products, regular changing, washing and proper disposal keep girls and women healthy.

## Requirements

Samples or pictures of disposable sanitary pads, reusable cloth pads, tampons, menstrual cup, period underwear; a chart; a model of the female reproductive system; paper bags for disposal.

## Procedure

1. Explain the menstrual cycle in simple words (about 28 days; bleeding about 3–7 days).
2. Display each product and explain how it is used, how often it is changed and how it is cleaned or disposed of.
3. Prepare a chart of good practices and common myths.
4. Role play: a pharmacist counselling a 13-year-old girl about her first period, and a woman asking about a menstrual cup.
5. Mention government schemes: menstrual hygiene under RKSK, low-cost sanitary napkins at Jan Aushadhi Kendras.

## Observations

| Product | Use | Change / care | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Disposable sanitary pad | External, sticks to underwear | Every 4–6 hours | Wrap and put in a covered bin or incinerator |
| Reusable cloth pad | External | Wash with soap and cold water, dry in sunlight | Low cost, eco-friendly |
| Tampon | Inserted in vagina | Every 4–8 hours; use lowest absorbency needed | Rare toxic shock syndrome if left too long |
| Menstrual cup (medical-grade silicone) | Inserted, collects blood | Empty every 8–12 hours; boil between cycles | Reusable for years |
| Period underwear | Absorbent layer | Wash after use | Light flow or backup |

**Good practices:** bathe daily, wash the genital area front-to-back with plain water, change products regularly, wash hands before and after, keep a spare pad, eat iron-rich food.

**Myths to correct:** "Do not bathe or exercise during periods", "girls are impure", "do not enter the kitchen" — none has a health basis.

**When to see a doctor:** very heavy bleeding, periods lasting more than 7 days, severe pain, no periods by age 15, foul-smelling discharge, itching.

## Result

Menstrual hygiene practices and products were studied, and counselling material was prepared.

## Precautions

- Respect privacy and speak sensitively.
- Never flush pads or tampons; they block drains.
- Do not reuse cloth that is damp or not sun-dried.

## Viva questions

1. **How often should a sanitary pad be changed?** About every 4–6 hours.
2. **What is toxic shock syndrome?** A rare, serious bacterial toxin illness linked to leaving tampons in too long.
3. **How is a menstrual cup cleaned?** Rinsed when emptied and boiled between cycles.
4. **Where can low-cost sanitary napkins be bought?** At Jan Aushadhi Kendras.
5. **Why dry cloth pads in sunlight?** Sunlight helps kill microbes and dries them fully.

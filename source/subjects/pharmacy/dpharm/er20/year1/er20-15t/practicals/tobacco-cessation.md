# Tobacco cessation counselling and identification of tobacco products

**Aim:** To identify various tobacco-containing products through charts or pictures and to practise tobacco cessation counselling using the 5 As and 5 Rs.

## Principle

Nicotine is highly addictive, and tobacco in any form causes cancers (mouth, throat, lung), heart attack, stroke, COPD and pregnancy problems. Brief advice by a health professional increases quit attempts. The **5 As** guide a willing user; the **5 Rs** motivate an unwilling user. Nicotine replacement therapy (NRT) and other medicines double the chance of quitting.

## Requirements

Charts or pictures of tobacco products (no real products need to be handled), NRT samples or pictures (gum, patch, lozenge), a Fagerström dependence questionnaire, role-play cards.

## Procedure

1. Prepare a chart of smoked and smokeless tobacco products used in India.
2. Note the legal points from COTPA 2003 and the ban on e-cigarettes (2019).
3. Role play: pharmacist counsels (a) a willing smoker using the 5 As; (b) an unwilling gutkha user using the 5 Rs.
4. Explain NRT use: gum — "chew and park" between cheek and gum; patch — apply each morning on clean hairless skin, change site daily.
5. Record a quit plan with a quit date.

## Observations

**Tobacco products**

| Smoked | Smokeless |
| --- | --- |
| Cigarettes, bidis, cigars, cheroots, hookah, chillum | Gutkha, khaini, zarda, pan masala with tobacco, mawa, mishri, gul, gudakhu, snuff |
| (E-cigarettes and heat-not-burn devices — banned in India) | Betel quid with tobacco |

**5 As (willing to quit)**

| Step | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Ask | Ask about tobacco use at every visit |
| Advise | Clear, strong, personal advice to quit |
| Assess | Is the person willing to quit now? |
| Assist | Set a quit date, remove tobacco, plan for cravings, offer NRT, refer to cessation centre/quitline |
| Arrange | Follow-up within the first week |

**5 Rs (not yet willing)**: **R**elevance (why quitting matters to them), **R**isks (of continuing), **R**ewards (of quitting — money, health, family), **R**oadblocks (barriers and how to overcome them), **R**epetition (repeat at every visit).

**Benefits after quitting:** better breathing and taste within weeks; heart risk falls over the following year; cancer risk falls over years.

## Result

Tobacco products were identified from charts, and cessation counselling was practised using the 5 As and 5 Rs.

## Precautions

- Be supportive, not judgemental; relapse is common and is part of quitting.
- NRT is used with caution in recent heart attack and pregnancy (doctor's advice).
- Do not handle or sample tobacco products during the practical.

## Viva questions

1. **Which law controls tobacco in India?** COTPA, 2003 (Cigarettes and Other Tobacco Products Act).
2. **What are the 5 As?** Ask, Advise, Assess, Assist, Arrange.
3. **Name two forms of NRT.** Nicotine gum and nicotine patch (also lozenges).
4. **What is the addictive substance in tobacco?** Nicotine.
5. **Name two smokeless tobacco products.** Gutkha and khaini.

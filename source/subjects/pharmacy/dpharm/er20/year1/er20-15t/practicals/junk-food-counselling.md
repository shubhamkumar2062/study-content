# Counselling children on junk foods and balanced diet (simulation)

**Aim:** To counsel school children on the harms of junk food and the importance of a balanced diet, using IEC material and simulated (role-play) counselling.

## Principle

Children copy habits that are easy, tasty and advertised. Junk foods (high in fat, salt and sugar — HFSS) lead to obesity, dental caries and early diabetes and replace healthy food. Counselling works better when it is friendly, uses pictures and games, offers tasty healthy swaps, and involves parents and teachers.

## Requirements

Empty packets of chips, noodles, biscuits and soft drinks (to read labels); a teaspoon and sugar or salt to show the amount in a product; pictures of healthy snacks; a "healthy plate" chart; quiz cards.

## Procedure

1. **Prepare IEC material:** a poster "Swap it!" (chips → roasted chana; cola → lemon water or buttermilk; noodles → vegetable poha or upma; biscuits → fruit or peanuts).
2. **Label-reading demo:** read the nutrition panel of a soft drink; calculate the sugar in teaspoons (grams of sugar ÷ 4 ≈ teaspoons) and show it with real sugar.
3. **Role play:** one student acts as a child who skips breakfast and eats chips daily; another acts as the pharmacist. Use open questions ("What did you eat today?"), listen, praise good habits, give 2–3 simple goals.
4. **Quiz/game:** sort pictures into "everyday foods" and "sometimes foods".
5. Give a take-home sheet for parents.

## Observations

**Sample label calculation:** a 300 ml soft drink with 10.6 g sugar per 100 ml contains about 32 g sugar ≈ **8 teaspoons**.

| Junk food | Main problem | Healthier swap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Potato chips | Salt, fat, no fibre | Roasted chana, makhana, peanuts |
| Soft drinks | Sugar (empty calories) | Water, lemon water, buttermilk, coconut water |
| Instant noodles | Refined flour, salt | Vegetable poha, upma, dalia |
| Fried snacks | Fat, reused oil | Sprouts chaat, fruit |
| Pastries, biscuits | Sugar, trans fat | Fruits, homemade laddu in small amounts |

**Counselling checklist:** greeting and rapport ✓, open questions ✓, listening ✓, simple facts ✓, practical swaps ✓, goal setting ✓, follow-up ✓.

## Result

IEC material was prepared and simulated counselling of children on junk food and balanced diet was carried out.

## Precautions

- Do not shame children about weight or food; be positive.
- Keep facts simple and correct; avoid frightening language.
- Respect food culture and family budget when suggesting swaps.

## Viva questions

1. **What are HFSS foods?** Foods high in fat, salt and sugar.
2. **How many grams of sugar make one teaspoon (approx.)?** About 4 g.
3. **Name two ill effects of junk food in children.** Obesity and dental caries (also early diabetes, poor concentration).
4. **What is IEC?** Information, Education and Communication.
5. **Give two healthy snack swaps.** Roasted chana instead of chips; buttermilk instead of soft drinks.

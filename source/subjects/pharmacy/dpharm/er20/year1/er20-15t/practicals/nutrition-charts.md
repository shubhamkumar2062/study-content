# Nutrition charts, local food sources, caloric needs and glycaemic index

**Aim:** To prepare charts on nutrients and their locally available food sources, to calculate the caloric needs of different groups, and to prepare a chart of the glycaemic index of foods.

## Principle

Every nutrient has cheap local sources. Energy needs depend on age, sex, body weight, physical activity and physiological state (pregnancy, lactation). A simple estimate is: **energy need = basal metabolic rate (BMR) × physical activity level (PAL)**. The **glycaemic index (GI)** shows how fast a carbohydrate food raises blood glucose compared with glucose (GI = 100); low-GI foods are better for people with diabetes.

## Requirements

Chart paper, pictures of local foods, food composition tables (ICMR–NIN), weighing scale, calculator.

## Procedure

1. Prepare chart 1: nutrient → function → local sources → deficiency.
2. Prepare chart 2: caloric needs of a child, a pregnant or lactating mother, and a sedentary adult, with the calculation shown.
3. Prepare chart 3: glycaemic index of common foods in three colour bands (low, medium, high).
4. Display and explain the charts.

## Observations

**Chart 1 — local sources (examples for Himachal Pradesh and North India)**

| Nutrient | Local sources |
| --- | --- |
| Energy | Wheat, rice, maize, millets (ragi, bajra), potato, jaggery |
| Protein | Rajma, chana, moong, masoor, soya, milk, curd, paneer, eggs, local fish and meat |
| Iron | Green leaves (spinach, mustard greens, bathua), jaggery, dried fruits, pulses, liver |
| Calcium | Milk, curd, ragi, sesame, green leaves |
| Vitamin A | Carrot, pumpkin, mango, papaya, green leaves, milk, eggs |
| Vitamin C | Amla, guava, citrus fruits, tomato, sprouted pulses |
| Fibre | Whole wheat atta, millets, vegetables, fruits, pulses |

**Chart 2 — caloric needs (approximate method)**

BMR ≈ 1 kcal per kg per hour for men and about 0.9 kcal per kg per hour for women.

| Group | Calculation | Approx. need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sedentary man, 60 kg | 60 × 24 × 1.0 = 1440 kcal BMR; × PAL 1.5 | ≈ 2160 kcal/day |
| Sedentary woman, 55 kg | 55 × 24 × 0.9 = 1188 kcal BMR; × PAL 1.5 | ≈ 1780 kcal/day |
| Pregnant woman (2nd–3rd trimester) | Her need + about 350 kcal | ≈ 2130 kcal/day (for the woman above) |
| Lactating mother (first 6 months) | Her need + about 600 kcal | ≈ 2380 kcal/day |
| Child, 6 years (rule of thumb) | 1000 + 100 × age in years | ≈ 1600 kcal/day |

PAL values used: sedentary about 1.4–1.5, moderate about 1.7–1.8, heavy work about 2.0 or more. These are estimates for teaching; actual recommended allowances come from ICMR–NIN tables.

**Chart 3 — glycaemic index (approximate)**

| Band | GI | Foods |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low | 55 or less | Lentils and dals, rajma, chana, milk, curd, apple, orange, barley |
| Medium | 56–69 | Brown rice, whole wheat roti, banana (ripe), sweet corn |
| High | 70 or more | Glucose, white bread, cornflakes, white rice (some varieties), potato (boiled/mashed), watermelon |

## Result

Charts on nutrients and local sources, caloric needs of different groups, and glycaemic index of foods were prepared and explained.

## Precautions

- Label all values as approximate; quote the source of tables used.
- GI changes with cooking, ripeness and what is eaten together; use it as a guide only.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the glycaemic index?** A measure of how fast a carbohydrate food raises blood glucose compared with glucose (100).
2. **Give two low-GI foods.** Dals/lentils and milk (also apple, rajma).
3. **How much extra energy does a pregnant woman need?** About 350 kcal a day in the second and third trimesters.
4. **Name a cheap local source of vitamin C.** Amla (also guava).
5. **What is PAL?** Physical activity level — the factor by which BMR is multiplied to get total energy need.

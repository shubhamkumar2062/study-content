# Unit 5: Health Systems and National Health Programmes in India

India runs many **national health programmes** to control diseases that affect large numbers of people. Most of them now work under the **National Health Mission (NHM)**. For each programme, learn four things: **objective → main components (functioning) → outcome → role of the pharmacist.**

> Programmes are renamed and updated from time to time. Learn the purpose and components; check the current name with your teacher.

## 5.1 Introduction to health systems

A **health system** includes all organisations, people and actions whose main purpose is to promote, restore or maintain health.

**WHO building blocks of a health system**

```text
 1. Service delivery        4. Access to essential medicines and vaccines
 2. Health workforce        5. Financing
 3. Health information      6. Leadership and governance
```

The pharmacist works mainly in block 4 (medicines) but supports all others.

**Health system in India (summary)**

| Level | Structure |
| --- | --- |
| Central | Ministry of Health & Family Welfare; DGHS; ICMR (research); NACO, Central TB Division and other programme divisions; CDSCO (drug regulation) |
| State | State health department; Directorate of Health Services; state drug controller |
| District | Chief Medical Officer; district hospital; district programme officers (TB, malaria, immunisation) |
| Block and village | CHC, PHC, sub-centre / Ayushman Arogya Mandir (Health and Wellness Centre); ANM, ASHA, Anganwadi worker |

**Ayushman Bharat** (2018) has two arms:

1. **Ayushman Arogya Mandirs** (earlier Health and Wellness Centres) — upgraded sub-centres and PHCs that give comprehensive primary care: maternal and child health, communicable diseases, screening for diabetes, high BP and common cancers, mental health, elderly care, free medicines and tests, yoga and wellness.
2. **Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (PM-JAY)** — health insurance cover for secondary and tertiary hospital care for poor and vulnerable families, usable in empanelled public and private hospitals.

**Pradhan Mantri Bhartiya Janaushadhi Pariyojana (PMBJP)** — **Jan Aushadhi Kendras** sell good-quality **generic medicines** at low prices; run by a pharmacist. Also sells low-cost sanitary napkins.

## 5.2 National Health Mission and RMNCH+A

(See Unit 1 for NHM structure.) Under NHM, **RMNCH+A** (Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, Child and Adolescent health) uses a "continuum of care" approach.

| Life stage | Main schemes |
| --- | --- |
| Reproductive | Family planning — spacing methods, sterilisation, home delivery of contraceptives by ASHA |
| Maternal | Janani Suraksha Yojana (cash for institutional delivery), Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram (free delivery, drugs, diet, transport), Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (free ANC check-up on a fixed day each month) |
| Newborn | Newborn care corners, sick newborn care units, home-based newborn care by ASHA |
| Child | Universal Immunization Programme, Rashtriya Bal Swasthya Karyakram (screening for defects, deficiencies, diseases, developmental delays), diarrhoea and pneumonia control |
| Adolescent | Rashtriya Kishor Swasthya Karyakram — nutrition, sexual and reproductive health, mental health, substance misuse; weekly iron–folic acid; menstrual hygiene |

**Pharmacist's role:** supply and counsel on IFA, ORS–zinc, contraceptives; check vaccination; refer high-risk pregnancies.

## 5.3 Universal Immunization Programme (UIP) and Mission Indradhanush

- **Objective:** protect children and pregnant women from vaccine-preventable diseases.
- **Components:** free vaccines on a national schedule (BCG, OPV, IPV, hepatitis B, pentavalent, rotavirus, PCV, MR, JE in endemic districts, DPT boosters, Td); cold chain; electronic vaccine stock monitoring (eVIN); AEFI surveillance; immunisation sessions at sub-centres and outreach.
- **Mission Indradhanush** (and its intensified rounds) — catch-up drives for children and pregnant women who missed doses.
- **Pulse Polio** campaigns and AFP surveillance maintain polio-free status.
- **Outcome:** polio eliminated; maternal and neonatal tetanus eliminated; rising full-immunisation coverage.
- **Pharmacist:** cold-chain care, reminders, counselling on side effects, dispelling myths, reporting AEFI.

## 5.4 National Tuberculosis Elimination Programme (NTEP)

Earlier called the Revised National TB Control Programme (RNTCP); renamed NTEP in 2020.

- **Objective:** end TB in India, in line with the SDG target — fewer cases, fewer deaths, no catastrophic costs for families.
- **Strategy (Detect – Treat – Prevent – Build):**
  1. **Detect:** active case finding; rapid molecular tests (CBNAAT, Truenat); private-sector notification.
  2. **Treat:** free daily fixed-dose drugs; drug-resistant TB regimens; treatment supporters; adherence technology.
  3. **Prevent:** TB preventive therapy for contacts and people with HIV; BCG; airborne infection control.
  4. **Build:** **Ni-kshay** online case-based system; **Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana** (nutrition support through direct benefit transfer); **Pradhan Mantri TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan** (community "Ni-kshay Mitras" adopt patients).
- **Pharmacist's role:** refer presumptive TB (cough ≥ 2 weeks); act as treatment supporter; ensure no gaps in treatment; counsel on side effects; notify TB cases; follow rules on sale of anti-TB drugs (record keeping).

## 5.5 National Leprosy Eradication Programme (NLEP)

- **Objective:** reduce leprosy burden, prevent disability, and finally interrupt transmission.
- **Components:** early case detection (house-to-house campaigns, the Sparsh awareness campaign), free multidrug therapy (MDT) blister packs, single-dose rifampicin as post-exposure prophylaxis for contacts, disability prevention and medical rehabilitation (self-care, footwear, reconstructive surgery), fighting stigma.
- **Outcome:** leprosy eliminated as a public health problem at national level (prevalence below 1 per 10,000) in 2005; new cases still occur, so efforts continue.
- **Pharmacist:** recognise anaesthetic patches and refer; counsel on MDT (clofazimine causes skin darkening, rifampicin red urine); ensure adherence.

## 5.6 National Vector Borne Disease Control Programme (NVBDCP)

Covers **six** diseases: **malaria, dengue, chikungunya, lymphatic filariasis, Japanese encephalitis and kala-azar**. Now run by the National Center for Vector Borne Diseases Control.

- **Objective:** prevent and control vector-borne diseases; eliminate malaria, filariasis and kala-azar.
- **Components:**
  - Early diagnosis (RDT, microscopy) and complete treatment (ACT, chloroquine + primaquine).
  - Integrated vector management: long-lasting insecticidal nets, indoor residual spraying, larvivorous fish, source reduction, larvicides, fogging in outbreaks.
  - Mass drug administration for filariasis; morbidity management of swollen limbs and hydrocele.
  - JE vaccination in endemic districts; kala-azar case search and treatment.
  - Behaviour change communication; surveillance.
- **Pharmacist:** treat fevers only with proper diagnosis; paracetamol in dengue, avoid NSAIDs; counsel on complete antimalarial course; promote nets and repellents; tell people to empty water containers weekly ("dry day").

## 5.7 National AIDS Control Programme (NACP)

Run by **NACO** with State AIDS Control Societies.

- **Objective:** prevent new HIV infections and provide care, support and treatment to people living with HIV, reducing stigma.
- **Components:**
  - Targeted interventions for high-risk groups (sex workers, injecting drug users, men who have sex with men, truckers, migrants); condom promotion; needle–syringe exchange and opioid substitution therapy.
  - **ICTC** — free voluntary counselling and testing.
  - **PPTCT** — prevention of parent-to-child transmission.
  - **ART centres** — free lifelong antiretroviral therapy; link ART centres.
  - Blood safety (screened blood); STI clinics ("Suraksha clinics") with syndromic management.
  - IEC and the **HIV/AIDS (Prevention and Control) Act, 2017**, which protects people from discrimination.
- **Pharmacist:** supply condoms and needles discreetly, refer for testing, counsel on ART adherence and side effects, keep confidentiality, post-exposure prophylaxis awareness.

## 5.8 National Programme for Prevention and Control of Non-Communicable Diseases (NP-NCD)

Earlier called NPCDCS (cancer, diabetes, cardiovascular diseases and stroke).

- **Objective:** prevent and control common NCDs through health promotion, early diagnosis, management and referral.
- **Components:** population-based screening of adults (30 years and above) for **high BP, diabetes and common cancers (oral, breast, cervical)** at Ayushman Arogya Mandirs; NCD clinics at CHCs and district hospitals; free drugs; lifestyle counselling (diet, exercise, tobacco and alcohol); cardiac and cancer care units.
- **Pharmacist:** BP and sugar checks, counselling on adherence to antihypertensives and antidiabetics, lifestyle advice, insulin storage and injection technique.

## 5.9 National Mental Health Programme (NMHP)

- **Objective:** ensure minimum mental health care for all, especially the most vulnerable; integrate mental health with general health care; promote community participation.
- **Components:** **District Mental Health Programme (DMHP)** — outpatient and community services in districts; training of general doctors and health workers; suicide prevention; school and college mental health; **Tele-MANAS** (national tele-mental health helpline 14416); Mental Healthcare Act, 2017.
- **Pharmacist:** recognise depression and suicide risk, counsel on adherence to psychiatric drugs (they take weeks to act; do not stop suddenly), refer, store sedatives securely.

## 5.10 National Tobacco Control Programme (NTCP)

- **Objective:** create awareness about the harms of tobacco, help people quit, and implement tobacco control laws.
- **Components:** enforcement of **COTPA 2003** (no smoking in public places, no sale to minors or near schools, pictorial warnings, no advertising); tobacco cessation centres; national quitline; school programmes; ban on e-cigarettes (2019).
- **Pharmacist:** brief cessation advice (5 As), nicotine replacement therapy, refusing to stock or promote tobacco.

| Programme | Objective | Key components |

## 5.12 Outcome of programmes — what India has achieved

- Smallpox eradicated (1977), guinea worm eradicated, **polio-free** certification (2014), maternal and neonatal tetanus eliminated (2015), yaws declared eliminated, trachoma eliminated as a public health problem (2024).
- Big falls in infant and maternal mortality since the 1990s.
- Much wider access to free ART, TB drugs and vaccines.

**Continuing challenges:** TB, anaemia and malnutrition, rising NCDs, mental health, antimicrobial resistance, urban slums, shortages of trained staff, out-of-pocket costs.

## 5.13 Role of pharmacists in national health programmes (general answer)

1. **Drug supply and logistics:** forecasting, storage (cold chain), stock records, avoiding stock-outs and expiry.
2. **Dispensing and counselling:** right dose, duration, side effects, adherence — especially TB, leprosy, HIV, malaria.
3. **Health education and IEC:** posters, health days (World TB Day, World AIDS Day, ORS Day), school talks.
4. **Screening and referral:** BP, sugar, BMI; cough, fever, anaemia signs, anaesthetic patches.
5. **Surveillance and reporting:** notify TB cases, report adverse drug reactions (Pharmacovigilance Programme of India) and AEFI.
6. **Rational use of medicines:** prevent antibiotic misuse; promote generics and Jan Aushadhi.
7. **Community link:** work with ASHAs, ANMs, Anganwadi workers and NGOs; join camps and campaigns.
8. **Emergencies and disasters:** emergency medicine supply, mass drug administration.

## Remember

- NHM = NRHM + NUHM; RMNCH+A = continuum of care.
- Ayushman Bharat = Ayushman Arogya Mandirs (primary care) + PM-JAY (hospital insurance).
- NTEP (renamed 2020): Detect–Treat–Prevent–Build; Ni-kshay portal; Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana.
- NVBDCP covers 6 diseases: malaria, dengue, chikungunya, filariasis, JE, kala-azar.
- NACP run by NACO: ICTC, PPTCT, ART centres; HIV/AIDS Act 2017.
- NP-NCD screens adults 30+ for BP, diabetes, oral/breast/cervical cancer.
- NMHP → DMHP, Tele-MANAS 14416; NTCP → COTPA 2003.
- Jan Aushadhi Kendras = generic medicines run by pharmacists.

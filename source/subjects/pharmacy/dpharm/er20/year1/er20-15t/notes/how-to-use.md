# How to use this guide

Social Pharmacy is a theory-heavy, "write what you understand" subject. There are no reactions or calculations to memorise, but there are many **definitions, lists, tables and disease facts**. Study one unit at a time: read the notes, write the "Remember" list from memory, then answer the question bank without looking.

**Where the marks are.** Unit 4 (microbiology, epidemiology and communicable diseases) takes about 28 of the 75 teaching hours, so expect the most questions from it. Next come Unit 2 (preventive healthcare, about 18 hours) and Unit 3 (nutrition, about 10 hours). Units 1 and 5 give steady long and short questions; Unit 6 (pharmacoeconomics) usually gives one short question or a few objective ones.

**Exam pattern.** Under PCI Education Regulations 2020, each D.Pharm theory subject carries **100 marks: 20 sessional (internal) + 80 in the yearly board exam, 3 hours**. In Himachal Pradesh the yearly exam is conducted by HPTSB. The usual ER-2020 board paper is set out below, and the mock paper in this guide follows it. Boards can vary it, so **confirm the pattern with your college**.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Objective questions — answer all | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| II | Short answers — answer 10 of 11 | 10 × 3 = 30 |
| III | Long answers — answer 6 of 7 | 6 × 5 = 30 |
| | **Total** | **80** |

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — a topic asked in most years or carrying big syllabus weight; prepare the full answer.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for objective questions and as a backup choice.

The rating is an estimate from common exam patterns and syllabus weight. It is not a promise.

**Scoring tips for Social Pharmacy**

1. **Start with a definition.** Health, epidemiology, quarantine, balanced diet, pharmacoeconomics — a clear one-line definition earns the first mark.
2. **Use a fixed format for every disease:** causative agent → mode of spread → incubation period → clinical features → treatment → prevention → role of pharmacist. Examiners look for each heading.
3. **Draw tables and simple diagrams:** the epidemiological triad, three-tier health system, classification of contraceptives, types of immunity, colour coding of bio-medical waste.
4. **Always end with "Role of the pharmacist"** in 4–6 points. It is the purpose of the subject, and many papers ask it directly.
5. **Quote Indian examples:** NHM, NTEP, Ni-kshay, Jan Aushadhi, COTPA 2003, NDPS Act 1985, FSSAI, IMS Act, Mission Indradhanush.
6. **Write numbers only when you are sure:** fast-breathing cut-offs, ORS osmolarity 245, residual chlorine 0.5 mg/L, cold chain +2 to +8 °C, breastfeeding within 1 hour and exclusive for 6 months.
7. **Practise the very short questions aloud** — they are quick 2-mark wins (e.g. "Define incubation period", "Name two live vaccines").

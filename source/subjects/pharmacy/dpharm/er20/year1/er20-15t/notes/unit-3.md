# Unit 3: Nutrition and Health

**Nutrition** is the science of food and how the body uses it for growth, energy, repair and protection from disease. Good nutrition is the base of health; poor nutrition (too little or too much) underlies anaemia, stunting, obesity, diabetes and heart disease.

## 3.1 Basics of nutrition — macronutrients and micronutrients

**Nutrients** are substances in food that the body needs.

```text
NUTRIENTS
├── Macronutrients (needed in grams; give energy or build tissue)
│   ├── Carbohydrates  4 kcal/g
│   ├── Proteins       4 kcal/g
│   └── Fats           9 kcal/g
├── Micronutrients (needed in mg or µg; no energy)
│   ├── Vitamins: fat-soluble A, D, E, K; water-soluble B-complex, C
│   └── Minerals: calcium, iron, iodine, zinc, sodium, potassium, fluoride ...
└── Water and dietary fibre
```

(Alcohol also gives about 7 kcal/g, but it is not a nutrient.)

### Carbohydrates

- **Function:** main and cheapest source of energy; spare protein; brain uses glucose.
- **Types:** simple sugars (glucose, fructose, sucrose, lactose) and complex carbohydrates (starch in rice, wheat, potato; fibre).
- **Sources:** cereals, millets, roots and tubers, sugar, jaggery, fruits.
- Excess refined sugar → obesity, dental caries, diabetes risk.

### Proteins

- **Function:** body-building (muscle, enzymes, hormones, antibodies, haemoglobin), repair, growth.
- Made of **amino acids**; 9 are **essential** (must come from food) for adults (e.g. lysine, tryptophan, methionine, leucine).
- **Quality:** animal proteins (milk, egg, meat, fish) are complete; most plant proteins lack one amino acid. Cereals lack lysine, pulses lack methionine — so **cereal + pulse** (dal-roti, khichdi, rajma-chawal) gives good-quality protein ("supplementary action").
- **Sources:** pulses, soya, milk, curd, paneer, egg, meat, fish, groundnuts.

### Fats (lipids)

- **Function:** concentrated energy, carry fat-soluble vitamins, essential fatty acids, insulation, taste.
- **Types:** saturated (ghee, butter, coconut oil), monounsaturated (groundnut, mustard, olive oil), polyunsaturated — omega-6 (sunflower, safflower) and omega-3 (fish, flaxseed, mustard). **Trans fats** (vanaspati, repeatedly heated oil, bakery foods) raise heart-disease risk.
- **Essential fatty acids:** linoleic acid and alpha-linolenic acid.
- Excess fat → obesity, high cholesterol, heart disease.

### Vitamins

| Vitamin | Main sources | Deficiency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A (retinol) | Liver, milk, egg yolk, green leafy and yellow vegetables, papaya, mango (β-carotene) | Night blindness, Bitot's spots, xerophthalmia, keratomalacia (blindness) |
| D | Sunlight on skin, fish oils, egg, fortified milk | Rickets in children, osteomalacia in adults |
| E | Vegetable oils, nuts, seeds | Haemolysis, nerve problems (rare) |
| K | Green leafy vegetables, gut bacteria | Bleeding; haemorrhagic disease of newborn |
| B1 (thiamine) | Whole grains, pulses, unpolished rice | Beriberi (wet: heart failure; dry: neuritis); Wernicke's in alcoholics |
| B2 (riboflavin) | Milk, egg, green leaves | Angular stomatitis, cheilosis, glossitis |
| B3 (niacin) | Groundnut, meat, cereals | Pellagra: 3 Ds — dermatitis, diarrhoea, dementia (4th D = death) |
| B6 (pyridoxine) | Cereals, meat, banana | Peripheral neuritis (e.g. with isoniazid), convulsions in infants |
| Folic acid | Green leafy vegetables, liver, pulses | Megaloblastic anaemia; neural tube defects in foetus |
| B12 | Only animal foods: milk, meat, egg | Megaloblastic anaemia, nerve damage (vegetarians at risk) |
| C (ascorbic acid) | Amla, citrus fruits, guava, tomato | Scurvy: bleeding gums, poor wound healing |

### Minerals

| Mineral | Role | Sources | Deficiency / excess |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Iron | Haemoglobin | Green leaves, jaggery, meat, liver, pulses (vitamin C helps absorption) | Microcytic hypochromic anaemia, tiredness, poor learning |
| Calcium | Bones, teeth, muscle, clotting | Milk, curd, ragi, sesame | Rickets, osteoporosis |
| Iodine | Thyroid hormones | Iodised salt, sea food | Goitre, cretinism, mental retardation (IDD) |
| Zinc | Growth, immunity, wound healing | Meat, pulses, nuts | Growth failure, diarrhoea |
| Fluoride | Teeth | Water, tea | Too little → dental caries; too much → dental and skeletal fluorosis |
| Sodium | Fluid balance | Salt | Excess → high BP |

## 3.2 Importance of water and fibre in the diet

### Water

- About **60% of adult body weight** is water.
- Functions: solvent and transport medium, temperature control (sweat), digestion, kidney excretion of waste, joint lubrication.
- Need: roughly 2–3 litres a day for an adult, more in heat, exercise, fever, diarrhoea, pregnancy and lactation.
- **Dehydration** → thirst, dry mouth, low urine, dizziness, kidney stones, constipation; severe dehydration (e.g. cholera) can kill.

### Dietary fibre

The part of plant food that human enzymes cannot digest.

| Type | Examples | Benefits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soluble | Oats, pulses, isabgol (psyllium husk), apples, guar gum | Lowers blood cholesterol, slows sugar absorption (good in diabetes) |
| Insoluble | Wheat bran, whole grains, vegetables, fruit peels | Adds bulk, prevents constipation, piles and diverticulosis |

Fibre also gives a feeling of fullness (helps weight control) and feeds good gut bacteria. Too little fibre is linked with constipation and colon cancer. Increase fibre slowly and drink enough water.

## 3.3 Balanced diet, malnutrition and deficiency diseases

### Balanced diet

A **balanced diet** contains all nutrients in the right amounts and proportions to meet the body's needs, keep a small reserve, and maintain health.

**Approximate share of energy:** carbohydrates about 50–60%, proteins 10–15%, fats 20–30%.

**Food groups (ICMR–NIN idea)**

| Group | Foods | Main nutrients |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cereals and millets | Rice, wheat, ragi, bajra, maize | Energy, some protein, B vitamins |
| Pulses and legumes | Dals, beans, soya | Protein, iron, fibre |
| Milk and animal foods | Milk, curd, egg, meat, fish | High-quality protein, calcium, B12 |
| Vegetables and fruits | Green leaves, other vegetables, seasonal fruits | Vitamins A and C, minerals, fibre |
| Fats, oils, nuts and sugar | Oil, ghee, nuts, sugar, jaggery | Energy, essential fatty acids |

**Practical tips:** half the plate vegetables and fruits; whole grains and millets; dal or an animal protein every meal; limit salt (WHO advises below 5 g a day), sugar and fried food; drink enough water; eat at regular times.

**Special groups:** pregnant women need extra energy, protein, iron, folic acid and calcium; lactating women need extra energy and protein; growing children need more protein and calcium per kg; elderly people need less energy but enough protein, calcium and vitamin D.

### Malnutrition

**Malnutrition** means an imbalance of nutrients — both **undernutrition** (too little) and **overnutrition** (too much).

**Causes:** poverty, food insecurity, large families, poor feeding practices, infections (diarrhoea, worms), lack of education, low status of women, poor sanitation.

**Protein–energy malnutrition (PEM)** in young children:

| Feature | Marasmus | Kwashiorkor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main deficit | Energy (and protein) | Mainly protein |
| Age | Usually under 1 year | 1–3 years (after weaning) |
| Oedema | Absent | Present (feet, face) |
| Look | "Old man" face, skin and bones, severe wasting | Puffy, "moon" face, flaky-paint skin |
| Hair | Thin | Sparse, pale or reddish (hence "red boy"), easily pulled out |
| Liver | Normal | Fatty, enlarged |
| Appetite | Good | Poor, apathetic, irritable |

**Measurements:** stunting (low height for age — long-term undernutrition), wasting (low weight for height — recent undernutrition), underweight (low weight for age), mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC).

**Overnutrition:** obesity (BMI ≥ 25 kg/m² is overweight on WHO cut-offs; Asian Indians face risk at lower BMI), type 2 diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, some cancers.
**BMI** = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²).

### Nutrition deficiency diseases (summary)

PEM; vitamin A deficiency; iron-deficiency anaemia (the commonest nutritional problem in India); iodine deficiency disorders; rickets and osteomalacia; beriberi; pellagra; scurvy; megaloblastic anaemia; zinc deficiency.

**Control programmes:** ICDS (Anganwadi services), PM POSHAN (school mid-day meal), POSHAN Abhiyaan, Anemia Mukt Bharat, vitamin A supplementation, National Iodine Deficiency Disorders Control Programme.

### Ill effects of junk foods

**Junk food** = food high in calories, salt, sugar or unhealthy fat but low in protein, vitamins, minerals and fibre (chips, instant noodles, fried snacks, sugary soft drinks, burgers, pastries). Also called HFSS (high fat, salt, sugar) food.

**Ill effects:**

- Obesity, especially in children and teenagers.
- Type 2 diabetes, high BP, high cholesterol and early heart disease.
- Dental caries (sugar), acidity, constipation (no fibre).
- Poor concentration, tiredness, mood swings; displaces healthy food → micronutrient deficiency.
- Some additives, colours and repeatedly heated oils may be harmful.

### Calorific and nutritive values of foods

**Calorific value** = energy given by a food, in kilocalories (kcal) per 100 g. **Nutritive value** = its content of protein, fat, vitamins and minerals.

Approximate values per 100 g (edible portion):

| Food | Energy (kcal, approx.) | Notable nutrient |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rice (raw) | 345 | Carbohydrate |
| Wheat flour (atta) | 340 | Carbohydrate, some protein |
| Pulses (dal, raw) | 330–350 | Protein about 20–24 g |
| Groundnuts | 560–570 | Fat, protein |
| Cow's milk | 65–70 | Calcium, protein |
| Egg (one, about 50 g) | 70–80 | High-quality protein |
| Sugar | 400 | Only carbohydrate ("empty calories") |
| Cooking oil | 900 | Only fat |
| Green leafy vegetables | 30–50 | Iron, β-carotene, folic acid |
| Amla | 50–60 | Very rich in vitamin C |

**Calculating energy from a label:** energy (kcal) = 4 × carbohydrate (g) + 4 × protein (g) + 9 × fat (g).
*Example:* a snack with 20 g carbohydrate, 2 g protein and 10 g fat gives 80 + 8 + 90 = 178 kcal.

### Fortification of food

**Fortification** = adding vitamins or minerals to commonly eaten foods to prevent deficiency in the whole population, without changing eating habits.

| Food vehicle | Nutrient added | Prevents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Salt | Iodine (iodised salt); iodine + iron (double-fortified salt) | IDD, anaemia |
| Rice | Iron, folic acid, vitamin B12 (fortified rice kernels) | Anaemia |
| Wheat flour | Iron, folic acid, B12 | Anaemia |
| Edible oil | Vitamins A and D | Vitamin A and D deficiency |
| Milk | Vitamins A and D | Vitamin A and D deficiency |

FSSAI marks fortified foods with the **"+F" logo**. Other terms: **enrichment** (adding back nutrients lost in processing) and **biofortification** (breeding crops richer in nutrients, e.g. iron-rich pearl millet).

## 3.4 Food safety

**Food safety** means food will not harm the consumer when prepared and eaten as intended. In India the **Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006** is enforced by **FSSAI** (Food Safety and Standards Authority of India).

**Five keys to safer food (WHO):** keep clean; separate raw and cooked food; cook thoroughly; keep food at safe temperatures; use safe water and raw materials.

### Adulteration of foods

**Adulteration** = adding cheaper or harmful substances, removing valuable parts, or selling spoiled food as good.

| Food | Common adulterant | Harm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Milk | Water, starch, urea, detergent | Low nutrition, gut and kidney harm |
| Ghee/butter | Vanaspati, animal fat | Heart disease risk |
| Mustard oil | Argemone oil | Epidemic dropsy (oedema, heart failure, glaucoma) |
| Arhar dal | Khesari dal (Lathyrus sativus) | Lathyrism (paralysis of legs) |
| Turmeric | Metanil yellow, lead chromate | Toxic, possibly cancer-causing |
| Chilli powder | Brick powder, artificial colours | Gut irritation, toxicity |
| Black pepper | Dried papaya seeds | Fraud, low quality |
| Tea | Used tea leaves, colour | Liver harm (from colour) |
| Coffee | Chicory, tamarind seed powder | Fraud |
| Sweets | Non-permitted colours, aluminium foil instead of silver | Toxicity |

**Simple home tests:** a drop of iodine turns starch-adulterated milk or paneer blue; papaya seeds float in spirit while true pepper sinks; brick powder in chilli settles quickly in water.

### Effects of artificial ripening

Fruits such as mango, banana and papaya are sometimes ripened quickly with **calcium carbide**. With moisture it gives **acetylene** gas; industrial carbide also carries traces of **arsenic and phosphorus**.

**Effects:** headache, dizziness, vomiting, burning and ulcers in mouth and stomach, skin and eye irritation; long-term toxicity. Fruit looks ripe outside but is raw and tasteless inside. Calcium carbide use is **banned** under FSSAI rules; controlled **ethylene gas** ripening chambers are the permitted method.

**Signs of carbide-ripened fruit:** patchy colour (yellow skin with green patches), uniform colour all over a batch, bland taste, quick spoilage. Advice: wash fruit well, peel, buy seasonal fruit.

### Use of pesticides

Pesticides (insecticides, herbicides, fungicides) protect crops but residues in food can harm health.

**Problems:** acute poisoning (organophosphates), residues in vegetables, fruits and milk; long-term effects on nerves, hormones and possibly cancer; harm to bees and birds; resistance in pests.

**Control:** maximum residue limits (MRL) set by FSSAI; banning of dangerous pesticides; integrated pest management; organic farming. **Home advice:** wash vegetables and fruits well in running water (soaking in salt water helps), peel where possible, cook.

### Genetically modified (GM) foods

GM foods come from organisms whose DNA has been changed by genetic engineering to add a useful trait.

| Possible benefits | Concerns |
| --- | --- |
| Pest resistance (e.g. Bt cotton), less pesticide use | Allergy to new proteins |
| Higher yield, drought or salt tolerance | Gene flow to wild plants, loss of biodiversity |
| Better nutrition (e.g. "golden rice" with β-carotene) | Resistance in pests over time |
| Longer shelf life | Dependence on seed companies; labelling and ethics |

In India, GM crops need approval of the **Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee (GEAC)**; Bt cotton is the main GM crop grown commercially, and GM food crops remain tightly controlled.

## 3.5 Dietary supplements, nutraceuticals and drug–food interactions

**Dietary (food) supplement:** a product taken by mouth to add to the diet (vitamins, minerals, proteins, herbs), usually as tablets, capsules, powders or liquids.

**Nutraceutical:** a food or part of a food that gives medical or health benefits, including prevention and support in disease (word made from *nutrition* + *pharmaceutical*). In India these are regulated by FSSAI, not as drugs.

| Product | Indications / benefits |
| --- | --- |
| Iron + folic acid, calcium + vitamin D | Anaemia in pregnancy; bone health, osteoporosis |
| Multivitamin–multimineral | Poor diet, recovery from illness, elderly |
| Omega-3 fatty acids (fish oil, flaxseed) | Lowering triglycerides, heart health |
| Probiotics (Lactobacillus, Bifidobacterium) | Antibiotic-associated diarrhoea, gut health |
| Prebiotics (inulin, FOS) | Feed good gut bacteria |
| Protein powders | Undernutrition, elderly, athletes, recovery after surgery |
| Glucosamine, chondroitin | Joint pain in osteoarthritis (evidence is mixed) |
| Antioxidants (vitamins C, E, lycopene) | General wellness claims |
| Psyllium (isabgol) | Constipation, cholesterol |

**Pharmacist's caution:** supplements are not a substitute for a balanced diet; overdose is possible (vitamin A and D toxicity, iron poisoning in children); some interact with drugs; check quality and FSSAI licence; avoid unproven claims.

### Drug–food interactions

A **drug–food interaction** is a change in a drug's effect because of food or drink taken with it.

| Food / drink | Drug | Result | Advice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Milk, dairy, antacids, iron | Tetracyclines, ciprofloxacin | Chelation → less absorption | Gap of 2 hours |
| Grapefruit juice | Felodipine, simvastatin, some others | Blocks gut CYP3A4 → higher blood levels | Avoid grapefruit |
| Tyramine-rich foods (aged cheese, fermented foods, some wines) | MAO inhibitors | Hypertensive crisis | Avoid such foods |
| Green leafy vegetables (vitamin K) | Warfarin | Lower anticoagulant effect | Keep vitamin K intake steady |
| Alcohol | Metronidazole | Disulfiram-like reaction (flushing, vomiting) | No alcohol during and 2 days after |
| Alcohol | Sedatives, antihistamines, paracetamol (liver) | More sedation; liver damage | Avoid alcohol |
| High-protein meal | Levodopa | Amino acids compete for absorption into brain | Spread protein intake |
| Fatty meal | Griseofulvin | Better absorption | Take with fatty food |
| Food in general | Rifampicin, levothyroxine, alendronate | Less absorption | Take on empty stomach |
| Caffeine | Theophylline | Additive stimulation | Limit tea and coffee |
| Bananas, salt substitutes (potassium) | ACE inhibitors, spironolactone | High potassium | Avoid excess |

**Role of pharmacist:** tell patients clearly "before food / after food / empty stomach"; warn about alcohol; counsel patients on warfarin and MAOIs; check supplements the patient is already taking.

## Remember

- Energy: carbohydrate 4, protein 4, fat 9 kcal/g; fats carry vitamins A, D, E, K.
- Cereal + pulse together supply all essential amino acids.
- Kwashiorkor = oedema, "red boy" hair, fatty liver; marasmus = wasting, no oedema.
- Pellagra 3 Ds; scurvy = vitamin C; beriberi = B1; night blindness = vitamin A.
- Argemone oil → epidemic dropsy; khesari dal → lathyrism; metanil yellow in turmeric.
- Calcium carbide (acetylene, arsenic traces) is banned for ripening; ethylene is allowed.
- FSS Act 2006, FSSAI; "+F" logo for fortified foods.
- Classic drug–food pairs: tetracycline + milk, MAOI + cheese, warfarin + green leaves, metronidazole + alcohol.

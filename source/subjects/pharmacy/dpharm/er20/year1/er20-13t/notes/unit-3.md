# Chapter 3: Quality control of crude drugs

A crude drug is useful only if it is the **right drug**, **pure**, and **strong enough**. Quality control has two parts in this syllabus:

1. Different methods of **adulteration** of crude drugs.
2. **Evaluation** of crude drugs.

## 3.1 Adulteration

**Adulteration** is the practice of substituting the genuine drug, fully or partly, with a similar-looking but inferior, cheaper, exhausted, harmful or useless material, or of mixing such material with it.

Adulteration may be **deliberate** (for profit) or **accidental** (confusion of names, careless collection, poor storage).

### 3.1.1 Why adulteration happens

- High price or shortage of the genuine drug.
- Similar appearance of two plants, or the same local name for two plants.
- Careless collection (other plant parts or weeds mixed in).
- Poor drying and storage, leading to decay, fungus or insects.
- Lack of knowledge of the collector or trader.

### 3.1.2 Types (methods) of adulteration

| Type | What is done | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Substitution with inferior commercial varieties | A cheaper variety or species with similar appearance is supplied | Senna leaves of *Cassia auriculata* (Palthe senna) for genuine senna; *Amomum* species (greater cardamom) for small cardamom; cassia bark for Ceylon cinnamon |
| 2. Substitution with artificially manufactured material | A man-made imitation is sold | Paraffin wax coloured yellow sold as beeswax; artificial invert sugar sold as honey; compressed chicory pellets made to look like coffee beans |
| 3. Substitution with exhausted drugs | The drug is first extracted (its active matter used), dried and sold again | Exhausted ginger, exhausted cloves (oil removed); colour or taste is often restored with additives |
| 4. Substitution with superficially similar but cheaper natural material | A different natural object that looks the same | Papaya seeds mixed with black pepper; stalks and mother cloves (fruits) mixed with clove buds |
| 5. Adding worthless heavy material | Weight is increased with sand, stones, lead shots, clay or chalk | Stones in asafoetida; sand in opium; lead pieces in opium |
| 6. Adding synthetic principles | A cheap synthetic chemical is added to an oil or drug | Citral added to lemon oil; benzyl benzoate added to balsams |
| 7. Using vegetative parts of the same plant | Stems, petioles or other parts of the same plant are mixed with the official part | Stalks in clove; stems in senna |
| 8. Harmful adulterants | Toxic or dirty material added | Metanil yellow or lead chromate in turmeric powder; sweepings and dust in powders |
| 9. Deteriorated or spoiled drugs | Old, mouldy or insect-damaged drug sold | Mouldy ginger, worm-eaten seeds |

**Sophistication** means a deliberate, skilful adulteration in which the adulterant is prepared to closely imitate the genuine drug, especially in powders (for example, powdered drug mixed with an artificial powder of matching colour).

### 3.1.3 Well-known adulterants (learn for MCQs)

| Genuine drug | Adulterant |
| --- | --- |
| Black pepper | Dried papaya seeds |
| Clove | Clove stalks, mother cloves, exhausted cloves |
| Cinnamon | Cassia bark |
| Senna | *Cassia auriculata*, *Cassia obovata* |
| Digitalis | Leaves of *Verbascum thapsus*, *Symphytum officinale* |
| Belladonna | *Phytolacca* and *Ailanthus* leaves |
| Ipecacuanha | Roots of *Psychotria* and *Richardsonia* species |
| Turmeric powder | Metanil yellow, lead chromate, other *Curcuma* species |
| Beeswax | Paraffin wax, Japan wax, tallow, rosin |
| Acacia | Ghatti gum and gums of other acacias |
| Saffron | Stigmas of other flowers, dyed fibres |

## 3.2 Evaluation of crude drugs

**Evaluation** means confirming the **identity** of a drug and measuring its **quality and purity**. The reasons are:

1. Biochemical variation in plants (season, soil, age).
2. Effect of drying and storage.
3. Adulteration and substitution.

```text
                     Evaluation of crude drugs
                                |
   ------------------------------------------------------------
   |              |              |             |               |
Morphological  Microscopical  Physical     Chemical       Biological
(organoleptic)                (constants)  (assay, tests) (bioassay)
```

### 3.2.1 Morphological (organoleptic) evaluation

Organoleptic means using the **sense organs** — eyes, nose, tongue and touch. The following are noted:

| Feature | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Colour | Senna is pale greyish-green; cinchona bark is reddish-brown |
| Odour | Aromatic in clove, cinnamon, cardamom; disagreeable (garlic-like) in asafoetida |
| Taste | Bitter in cinchona and aloe; pungent in ginger and black pepper; sweet in liquorice |
| Size and shape | Clove 10–17.5 mm long, nail-shaped |
| Surface and fracture | Fibrous fracture of cinchona; short fracture of rauwolfia |
| Special features | Quills of cinnamon; asymmetric base of senna leaflet |

### 3.2.2 Microscopical evaluation

The drug is studied under the microscope to see cells and cell contents. This is essential for **powdered drugs**, which cannot be identified by shape.

**What is seen**

- **Transverse section (TS)** to see the arrangement of tissues.
- **Powder study** to see diagnostic elements: trichomes (hairs), stomata, fibres, sclereids (stone cells), vessels, calcium oxalate crystals, starch grains.
- **Staining**: phloroglucinol + HCl stains lignin pink; iodine stains starch blue; Sudan red III stains oils red; ruthenium red stains mucilage pink.

**Quantitative microscopy (leaf constants)**

| Constant | Definition | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stomatal number | Average number of stomata per sq. mm of epidermis | Distinguishes species |
| Stomatal index | Percentage of stomata out of all epidermal cells plus stomata in a given area | Fairly constant for a species |
| Palisade ratio | Average number of palisade cells beneath one epidermal cell | Distinguishes leaves of related species |
| Vein-islet number | Number of vein islets per sq. mm of leaf surface | Distinguishes species (e.g. senna varieties) |
| Vein termination number | Number of vein endings per sq. mm | Same |

**Stomatal index formula**

SI = S ÷ (S + E) × 100

where S = number of stomata and E = number of epidermal cells in the same area.

**Other microscopic measurements**

- **Micrometry**: size of starch grains, fibres or crystals measured with an eyepiece micrometer calibrated against a stage micrometer.
- **Lycopodium spore method**: the percentage of a genuine drug in a powder is found by counting its characteristic particles against a known weight of lycopodium spores (about 94,000 spores per mg), because the spores are uniform in size and number.

### 3.2.3 Physical evaluation

Physical constants are measured and compared with the limits in the pharmacopoeia.

| Physical parameter | What it detects |
| --- | --- |
| Moisture content (loss on drying) | Excess moisture causes fungal growth and decay |
| Ash values | Inorganic matter, sand, earth |
| Extractive values | Active matter soluble in water or alcohol; low value suggests exhausted drug |
| Volatile oil content | Strength of aromatic drugs (clove, cardamom) |
| Swelling factor | Quality of mucilage drugs (ispaghula) |
| Viscosity | Quality of gums (acacia, tragacanth) |
| Melting point | Purity of waxes and fats (beeswax) |
| Refractive index, optical rotation, specific gravity | Purity of volatile and fixed oils |
| Foaming index | Saponin content |
| Fluorescence under UV light | Identity of powders and extracts |

**Ash values**

When a drug is burned, the organic matter disappears and the inorganic matter (ash) remains.

| Ash value | How obtained | Shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total ash | Drug incinerated at about 450 °C | All inorganic matter (natural plus added) |
| Acid-insoluble ash | Total ash boiled with dilute HCl; residue weighed | Silica, sand, earth — a sign of soil contamination |
| Water-soluble ash | Total ash boiled with water; loss in weight | Water-soluble inorganic salts; detects exhausted drugs |
| Sulphated ash | Drug treated with sulphuric acid, then ignited | Used for drugs where total ash is not reliable |

**Extractive values**

The percentage of matter dissolved from a known weight of drug by a solvent:

- **Alcohol-soluble extractive** — for drugs with resins, alkaloids, glycosides.
- **Water-soluble extractive** — for drugs with tannins, sugars, mucilage.
- **Ether-soluble extractive** — for volatile oils and fixed oils.

### 3.2.4 Chemical evaluation

1. **Chemical (identification) tests** — specific colour or precipitation reactions, e.g. Borntrager's test for anthraquinones in senna and aloe; Keller–Killiani test for digitalis; murexide test for caffeine in tea; thalleioquin test for quinine.
2. **Assay** — quantitative estimation of the active constituent: total alkaloids in belladonna or cinchona, volatile oil in clove, sennosides in senna.
3. **Chemical constants** — acid value, saponification value, iodine value, ester value of oils, fats and waxes; acetyl value of castor oil.
4. **Instrumental methods** — chromatography (TLC, HPTLC, HPLC, GC) and spectroscopy for markers.

### 3.2.5 Biological evaluation (bioassay)

Used when the active compound cannot be estimated chemically, or when chemical potency does not match the effect. The response of a living animal, tissue or microbe is compared with that of a standard.

| Drug | Biological method |
| --- | --- |
| Digitalis | Effect on the heart of pigeon, frog or guinea pig |
| Ergot | Effect on blood pressure or uterus of animals |
| Antibiotics | Zone of inhibition of a test microbe |
| Insulin (animal product) | Fall of blood sugar in rabbits or mice |

**Microbial and other contamination tests** — total microbial count, absence of *E. coli* and *Salmonella*, aflatoxins, pesticide residues and heavy metals are also checked, especially for herbal products.

## 3.3 Factors that affect quality

- **Collection**: right part, right season, right age of plant.
- **Drying**: proper temperature (digitalis below 60 °C), shade drying for aromatic drugs.
- **Garbling**: removal of foreign matter and unwanted parts after drying.
- **Storage**: cool, dry, airtight containers protected from light, insects and rodents.

## Remember

- Adulteration = substitution or mixing with inferior, exhausted, artificial or useless material.
- Classic examples: **papaya seeds in black pepper**, **cassia bark for cinnamon**, **paraffin wax in beeswax**, **exhausted ginger and clove**, **metanil yellow in turmeric**.
- Evaluation = **morphological, microscopical, physical, chemical, biological**.
- Stomatal index = S ÷ (S + E) × 100.
- **Acid-insoluble ash** shows sand and earth; **water-soluble ash** helps detect exhausted drugs.
- Lycopodium spore method counts the share of a genuine powder (about 94,000 spores per mg).
- Bioassay is used for digitalis and ergot.

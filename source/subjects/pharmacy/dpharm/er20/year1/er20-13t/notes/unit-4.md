# Chapter 4: Alkaloids, terpenoids, glycosides, volatile oils, tannins and resins

The syllabus asks for a **brief outline** of each group: occurrence, distribution, isolation, identification tests, therapeutic activity and pharmaceutical applications. These six groups explain the action of most drugs in Chapter 5, so this chapter links the whole subject.

## 4.1 Alkaloids

### 4.1.1 Definition

**Alkaloids** are basic (alkaline), nitrogen-containing organic compounds of plant origin, usually with the nitrogen in a heterocyclic ring, and with marked physiological action on humans and animals.

General properties:

- Most are colourless crystalline solids; a few are liquids (nicotine, coniine).
- Most are bitter.
- **Free bases** dissolve in organic solvents (chloroform, ether) but not in water.
- **Salts** (alkaloid + acid) dissolve in water but not in organic solvents. This difference is the basis of their isolation.

### 4.1.2 Occurrence and distribution

- Mostly in **flowering plants (dicotyledons)**; rare in lower plants (exceptions: ergot alkaloids in a fungus, ephedrine in a gymnosperm).
- Rich families: Solanaceae, Papaveraceae, Apocynaceae, Rubiaceae, Leguminosae, Ranunculaceae, Rutaceae.
- Present in all parts: leaves (belladonna, coca, tea), bark (cinchona), roots (rauwolfia, ipecacuanha), seeds (nux-vomica, colchicum, coffee), latex (opium), fruits (black pepper).
- Usually occur as salts of plant acids (meconic acid in opium, quinic acid in cinchona).

### 4.1.3 Classification (by ring system)

| Class | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Tropane | Atropine, hyoscyamine, hyoscine, cocaine |
| Quinoline | Quinine, quinidine |
| Isoquinoline | Morphine, codeine, papaverine, emetine |
| Indole | Reserpine, vincristine, ergometrine, strychnine |
| Purine (xanthine) | Caffeine, theophylline, theobromine |
| Imidazole | Pilocarpine |
| Quinazoline | Vasicine |
| Protoalkaloids (amines, N not in ring) | Ephedrine, colchicine |

### 4.1.4 General method of isolation

```text
Powdered drug
     │  moisten with alkali (lime / ammonia / Na2CO3) → frees alkaloid bases
     ▼
Extract with organic solvent (chloroform / ether)
     │  (fats and pigments also come out)
     ▼
Shake solvent with dilute acid (H2SO4 / HCl)
     │  alkaloids form salts and move into the water layer
     ▼
Acid layer made alkaline with ammonia
     │  free bases precipitate
     ▼
Extract again with chloroform → evaporate → crude alkaloids
     │
     ▼
Purify by crystallisation or chromatography
```

A second method extracts the drug directly with acidified water or alcohol, then makes it alkaline and shakes with an organic solvent.

### 4.1.5 Identification tests (general precipitating reagents)

| Reagent | Composition | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mayer's reagent | Potassium mercuric iodide | Cream (white) precipitate |
| Dragendorff's reagent | Potassium bismuth iodide | Orange-red (reddish-brown) precipitate |
| Wagner's reagent | Iodine in potassium iodide | Reddish-brown precipitate |
| Hager's reagent | Saturated picric acid | Yellow precipitate |
| Tannic acid | Tannic acid solution | Buff precipitate |

Caffeine does not respond well to these reagents and is detected by the **murexide test**.

### 4.1.6 Therapeutic activity and applications

| Action | Alkaloid |
| --- | --- |
| Analgesic | Morphine |
| Antitussive | Codeine, noscapine |
| Antimalarial | Quinine |
| Antiarrhythmic | Quinidine, ajmaline |
| Antihypertensive | Reserpine |
| Anticholinergic / antispasmodic | Atropine, hyoscine |
| Local anaesthetic | Cocaine |
| CNS stimulant | Caffeine |
| Bronchodilator | Ephedrine |
| Anticancer | Vincristine, vinblastine |
| Uterine stimulant | Ergometrine |
| Amoebicide | Emetine |
| Antigout | Colchicine |

## 4.2 Terpenoids

### 4.2.1 Definition

**Terpenoids** (terpenes) are compounds built from **isoprene units** (C5H8, 2-methyl-1,3-butadiene) joined head to tail. This is the **isoprene rule**.

```text
Isoprene unit:        CH2 = C – CH = CH2
                            |
                           CH3
```

### 4.2.2 Classification

| Class | Isoprene units | Carbons | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hemiterpenes | 1 | C5 | Isoprene |
| Monoterpenes | 2 | C10 | Menthol, camphor, citral, linalool, geraniol |
| Sesquiterpenes | 3 | C15 | Zingiberene, artemisinin, santonin |
| Diterpenes | 4 | C20 | Taxol, gibberellins, phytol |
| Triterpenes | 6 | C30 | Squalene, glycyrrhetinic acid, gymnemic acids, arjunolic acid |
| Tetraterpenes | 8 | C40 | Carotenoids (β-carotene, lycopene) |
| Polyterpenes | many | — | Rubber |

### 4.2.3 Occurrence

Widely spread in plants: volatile oils (mono- and sesquiterpenes), resins (diterpenes), saponins and sterols (triterpenes), plant pigments (carotenoids), and latex (rubber).

### 4.2.4 Isolation

- **Volatile terpenes** by steam distillation, then fractional distillation.
- **Non-volatile terpenes** by extraction with organic solvents and chromatography.

### 4.2.5 Tests

| Test | Procedure | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Salkowski test | Extract in chloroform + conc. H2SO4 along the side | Reddish-brown colour at the junction (sterols and triterpenoids) |
| Liebermann–Burchard test | Extract in chloroform + acetic anhydride + conc. H2SO4 | Colour change through pink/red to blue-green (sterols and triterpenoids) |

### 4.2.6 Uses

Flavouring and perfumes (menthol, citral), counter-irritants (camphor), antimalarial (artemisinin), anticancer (taxol), anti-inflammatory (glycyrrhetinic acid), provitamin A (β-carotene).

## 4.3 Glycosides

### 4.3.1 Definition

**Glycosides** are compounds in which a **sugar** is joined through a **glycosidic bond** to a non-sugar part called the **aglycone** (or genin). On hydrolysis by acids or enzymes they split into sugar + aglycone.

```text
Glycoside  ──(acid or enzyme, + H2O)──▶  Sugar (glycone)  +  Aglycone (genin)
```

The sugar improves water solubility; the **aglycone usually decides the action**.

### 4.3.2 Classification by the atom joining sugar and aglycone

| Type | Linkage | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| O-glycoside | Sugar–O–aglycone | Sennosides, digitoxin |
| C-glycoside | Sugar–C–aglycone (resists acid hydrolysis) | Barbaloin (aloin) |
| S-glycoside | Sugar–S–aglycone | Sinigrin (mustard) |
| N-glycoside | Sugar–N–aglycone | Nucleosides (adenosine) |

### 4.3.3 Classification by aglycone (more useful)

| Class | Drug examples | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anthraquinone glycosides | Senna, Aloe, Rhubarb, Cascara | Purgative |
| Cardiac glycosides (cardenolides and bufadienolides) | Digitalis, Squill, Strophanthus | Heart failure |
| Saponin glycosides | Liquorice, Dioscorea, Arjuna | Expectorant, steroid source |
| Cyanogenetic glycosides | Bitter almond (amygdalin), wild cherry | Flavour (source of HCN) |
| Isothiocyanate glycosides | Black mustard (sinigrin) | Counter-irritant |
| Flavonoid glycosides | Rutin (buckwheat), hesperidin (citrus) | Capillary fragility |
| Coumarin glycosides | Psoralea | Leucoderma |
| Phenol glycosides | Bearberry (arbutin), Willow (salicin) | Urinary antiseptic, analgesic |

### 4.3.4 Occurrence

Widely spread in flowering plants, especially Scrophulariaceae and Apocynaceae (cardiac), Leguminosae and Liliaceae (anthraquinone), Rosaceae (cyanogenetic). Present in leaves, barks, roots and seeds.

### 4.3.5 Isolation (Stas–Otto method, outline)

1. Powder the drug and extract with alcohol (heat or quick treatment first to stop the plant's own enzymes from hydrolysing the glycoside).
2. Treat the extract with lead acetate to precipitate tannins and other impurities; filter.
3. Remove excess lead with hydrogen sulphide.
4. Concentrate and crystallise, or separate by chromatography.

### 4.3.6 Identification tests

| Test | For | Procedure in short | Positive result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Borntrager's test | Anthraquinones (O-glycosides) | Boil drug with dilute H2SO4, filter, shake filtrate with benzene/chloroform, separate organic layer, add dilute ammonia | Pink to red colour in ammonia layer |
| Modified Borntrager's test | C-glycosides (aloe) | Hydrolyse with ferric chloride + HCl, then as above | Pink to red colour |
| Keller–Killiani test | Deoxy-sugars of cardiac glycosides (digitalis) | Extract in glacial acetic acid with a trace of FeCl3, conc. H2SO4 poured down the side | Reddish-brown ring at junction; upper layer bluish-green |
| Legal's test | Cardenolides | Extract in pyridine + sodium nitroprusside + NaOH | Pink to red |
| Baljet's test | Cardenolides | Sodium picrate | Yellow to orange |
| Foam test | Saponins | Shake extract with water | Stable froth for some minutes |
| Haemolysis test | Saponins | Mix with blood | Lysis of red cells |
| Sodium picrate (Guignard) test | Cyanogenetic glycosides | Moist drug in a tube with picrate paper hanging above | Paper turns yellow to brick-red (HCN given off) |

### 4.3.7 Uses

Cardiotonics (digoxin, digitoxin), purgatives (sennosides), expectorants (saponins), starting materials for steroid hormones (diosgenin), flavouring (vanillin from glucovanillin), capillary protectives (rutin).

## 4.4 Volatile oils

### 4.4.1 Definition

**Volatile oils** (essential oils, ethereal oils) are odorous, oily liquids obtained from plants that **evaporate at room temperature** and can be **distilled with steam**. They are mixtures of terpenes and aromatic compounds (phenylpropanoids).

| Fixed oil | Volatile oil |
| --- | --- |
| Glycerides of fatty acids | Mixture of terpenes, alcohols, aldehydes, phenols, esters |
| Leaves a permanent grease spot on paper | Spot evaporates completely |
| Not distilled with steam | Distilled with steam |
| Saponifiable | Usually not saponifiable |
| Little odour | Strong odour |

### 4.4.2 Occurrence

| Secretory structure | Family | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glandular hairs | Labiatae | Tulsi, mint, lavender, rosemary |
| Oil cells | Zingiberaceae, Lauraceae | Ginger, cinnamon |
| Schizogenous oil ducts (vittae) | Umbelliferae | Coriander, fennel, ajwain |
| Schizolysigenous oil glands | Myrtaceae, Rutaceae | Clove, orange peel |

They occur in flowers (lavender, clove), fruits (fennel, coriander), seeds (cardamom, nutmeg), leaves (tulsi, eucalyptus), barks (cinnamon), wood (sandalwood) and rhizomes (ginger).

### 4.4.3 Methods of isolation

| Method | Principle | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hydrodistillation / steam distillation | Oil carried over with steam, separated from water in a receiver | Clove, fennel, eucalyptus |
| Expression (ecuelle method, sponge method) | Squeezing peel by hand or machine | Lemon, orange oil |
| Enfleurage | Fresh flowers spread on fat-coated glass; fat absorbs the oil; oil later taken out with alcohol | Jasmine, rose |
| Solvent extraction | Extracted with light solvent, solvent removed under vacuum | Delicate flower oils |
| Supercritical CO2 extraction | CO2 above its critical point acts as a solvent | High-quality oils |

### 4.4.4 Identification tests

- Thin section treated with **Sudan red III** — oil globules stained red.
- **Tincture of alkanna** — oil stained red.
- A drop of oil on filter paper evaporates without a permanent stain.
- Physical constants: specific gravity, refractive index, optical rotation, solubility in alcohol.

### 4.4.5 Uses

Carminatives (fennel, cardamom), flavouring (peppermint, cinnamon), perfumes (lavender, sandalwood), antiseptics (clove, eucalyptus, thyme), counter-irritants (turpentine, wintergreen), dental analgesic (clove oil), insect repellents (citronella).

## 4.5 Tannins

### 4.5.1 Definition

**Tannins** are complex polyphenolic compounds that **precipitate proteins** and can convert animal hide into leather (tanning). They have an astringent (puckering) taste.

### 4.5.2 Classification

| Class | Nature | On heating | With FeCl3 | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hydrolysable tannins | Esters of gallic or ellagic acid with sugar; split by acid or enzyme | Give pyrogallol | Blue-black | Nutgalls, myrobalan, chebulinic acid |
| Condensed tannins (phlobatannins) | Polymers of flavan-3-ols (catechins); not hydrolysed; acid gives red phlobaphenes | Give catechol | Greenish-black | Black catechu, pale catechu, cinchona, tea |
| Pseudotannins | Small molecules that give some tannin tests but do not tan hide | — | Varies | Gallic acid, catechin, chlorogenic acid |

### 4.5.3 Occurrence

Common in barks, galls, unripe fruits, leaves and heartwoods: oak galls, myrobalan, acacia bark and heartwood, tea leaves, cinchona bark.

### 4.5.4 Isolation (outline)

Extract with water or acetone–water, remove impurities with organic solvents, precipitate with lead acetate or gelatin, then purify by chromatography.

### 4.5.5 Identification tests

| Test | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Ferric chloride | Blue-black (hydrolysable) or greenish-black (condensed) |
| Gelatin solution (1% with NaCl) | White precipitate (protein precipitated) |
| Goldbeater's skin test | Skin soaked in tannin solution, washed, then dipped in FeCl3 turns brown or black |
| Lead acetate | Precipitate |
| Bromine water | Precipitate with condensed tannins |
| Vanillin–HCl | Pink to red with catechins |
| Matchstick (phloroglucinol) test | Matchstick dipped in catechu extract, dried, moistened with HCl and warmed turns pink/red (catechins) |

### 4.5.6 Uses

Astringent in diarrhoea (catechu, myrobalan), local haemostatic, treatment of burns and piles (tannic acid ointment), antidote in alkaloid and heavy-metal poisoning (precipitates them), tanning leather, making ink.

## 4.6 Resins

### 4.6.1 Definition

**Resins** are amorphous, solid or semi-solid plant products, usually formed by oxidation and polymerisation of terpenes. They are **insoluble in water**, **soluble in alcohol, ether and chloroform**, burn with a smoky flame, and soften on heating.

### 4.6.2 Classification by associated substances

| Class | Composition | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resins | Mainly resin | Colophony (rosin), Podophyllum resin |
| Oleoresins | Resin + volatile oil | Copaiba, turpentine, ginger oleoresin |
| Gum resins | Resin + gum | Ammoniacum |
| Oleo-gum-resins | Resin + gum + volatile oil | Asafoetida, Myrrh, Guggul |
| Balsams | Resin containing benzoic and/or cinnamic acid or their esters | Benzoin, Tolu balsam, Peru balsam |

### 4.6.3 Chemical classes of resin constituents

Resin acids (abietic acid), resin alcohols (resinols, resinotannols), resin esters (benzyl benzoate, coniferyl benzoate), and resenes (inert neutral substances).

### 4.6.4 Occurrence and isolation

Formed in resin ducts or cells of trees (Pinaceae, Burseraceae, Styracaceae). Obtained by:

- **Incision** of bark (benzoin, tolu, asafoetida, myrrh, guggul) — the exudate hardens in air.
- **Extraction** with alcohol and precipitation by pouring into water (podophyllum resin).
- **Distillation** to remove volatile oil (colophony is the residue left after distilling turpentine).

### 4.6.5 Tests

- Insoluble in water, soluble in alcohol; an alcoholic solution turns milky when poured into water.
- Balsams on heating give a sublimate of **benzoic acid**; those with cinnamic acid give an odour of **benzaldehyde** when warmed with potassium permanganate.

### 4.6.6 Uses

Expectorants (tolu, benzoin), antiseptic inhalation (compound benzoin tincture), purgative and wart remover (podophyllum resin), carminative (asafoetida), hypolipidaemic (guggul), mouthwash (myrrh), varnishes and adhesives.

## Remember

- **Alkaloids**: basic, nitrogenous, bitter; free base in chloroform, salt in water; Mayer's (cream), Dragendorff's (orange-red), Wagner's (reddish-brown), Hager's (yellow).
- **Terpenoids**: isoprene (C5) units; mono C10, sesqui C15, di C20, tri C30, tetra C40.
- **Glycosides**: sugar + aglycone; Borntrager's (anthraquinones), Keller–Killiani (digitalis), foam test (saponins), sodium picrate (HCN).
- **Volatile oils**: evaporate, steam-distillable; obtained by distillation, expression, enfleurage, extraction.
- **Tannins**: precipitate protein; hydrolysable (blue-black with FeCl3) vs condensed (greenish-black).
- **Resins**: insoluble in water, soluble in alcohol; balsams contain benzoic/cinnamic acid; oleo-gum-resins = asafoetida, myrrh, guggul.

# Chapter 11: Herbal cosmetics

The syllabus asks for the **sources, chemical constituents, commercial preparations, therapeutic and cosmetic uses** of six materials: **aloe vera gel, almond oil, lavender oil, olive oil, rosemary oil and sandalwood oil**.

## 11.1 Introduction

**Cosmetic** (Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, in simple words): any article meant to be rubbed, poured, sprinkled or sprayed on, or applied to, the human body for **cleansing, beautifying, promoting attractiveness or altering the appearance**.

**Herbal cosmetics** are cosmetics in which plant materials or their products (oils, extracts, gels, powders) provide the main beneficial effect.

**Cosmeceuticals** are products in between cosmetics and medicines — for example, anti-ageing creams with antioxidants.

**Why herbal cosmetics are popular**

- Perceived as mild and suitable for regular use.
- Plant oils are natural emollients; many herbs are antioxidant, antimicrobial and soothing.
- Pleasant natural fragrance.
- Long traditional use in India (turmeric, sandalwood, neem, besan ubtan).

**Caution:** "natural" does not mean free of allergy. Essential oils can cause skin irritation and must be diluted.

**Categories of herbal cosmetics**

| Category | Herbal ingredients |
| --- | --- |
| Skin care (creams, lotions, face packs) | Aloe gel, sandalwood, turmeric, almond oil, olive oil |
| Hair care (oils, shampoos, conditioners) | Amla, bhringraj, rosemary oil, henna, almond oil |
| Oral care | Neem, clove, babool, meswak |
| Perfumes and deodorants | Lavender, sandalwood, rose, jasmine |
| Sun care | Aloe gel, carrot seed oil |

## 11.2 Aloe vera gel

- **Source:** The colourless, mucilaginous **gel from the inner parenchyma (leaf pulp)** of the fresh leaves of *Aloe barbadensis* Miller (*Aloe vera* Linn.).
- **Family:** Liliaceae (Asphodelaceae).
- **Geographical source:** Grown in hot, dry regions — Mexico, USA, the Caribbean; India (Gujarat, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh).

**Important difference:** Aloe **gel** (inner pulp) is different from aloe **latex/juice** (the bitter yellow juice under the leaf skin, which contains purgative aloin). Cosmetic gel should be **free of aloin**.

```text
Cross-section of an aloe leaf

   ___________________________________
  /  green outer skin (rind)           \
 |   yellow latex (aloin) just under it |   ← laxative, NOT for cosmetics
 |                                      |
 |     clear inner gel (parenchyma)     |   ← cosmetic gel
 |                                      |
  \____________________________________/
```

**Chemical constituents**

| Constituent | Note |
| --- | --- |
| Water | About 99% of fresh gel |
| Polysaccharides | **Acemannan** (acetylated glucomannan) — main active; moisturising, wound healing |
| Glycoproteins | Anti-inflammatory |
| Vitamins | A, C, E and some B vitamins |
| Enzymes | Bradykinase, others |
| Amino acids, minerals, sterols, salicylic acid | Minor |

**Commercial preparations:** aloe vera gels, moisturising creams and lotions, after-sun lotions, face washes, shampoos and conditioners, shaving gels, lip balms.

**Therapeutic and cosmetic uses**

- **Moisturiser** and skin softener.
- **Soothing and cooling** in sunburn and minor burns; promotes wound healing.
- Anti-inflammatory; relieves itching and minor skin irritation.
- Hair conditioning.
- Gel taken orally as a health drink (traditional).

## 11.3 Almond oil

- **Source:** Fixed oil obtained by **expression** from the seeds (kernels) of *Prunus amygdalus* Batsch var. ***dulcis*** (sweet almond) or var. *amara* (bitter almond).
- **Family:** Rosaceae.
- **Geographical source:** USA (California), Spain, Italy, Iran; India (Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh).

**Note:** The **expressed fixed oil** is bland and safe from both varieties. Bitter almond also gives a **volatile oil** (after soaking the press-cake in water), which contains benzaldehyde and toxic HCN formed from amygdalin; this volatile oil is not used on skin.

**Chemical constituents**

| Constituent | Note |
| --- | --- |
| Glycerides of **oleic acid** (main) | Monounsaturated; about two-thirds or more of the fatty acids |
| Glycerides of **linoleic acid** | Polyunsaturated |
| Palmitic and stearic acid glycerides | Small amounts |
| **Vitamin E** (tocopherols) | Antioxidant |
| Phytosterols | Minor |

**Commercial preparations:** baby massage oils, hair oils, **cold creams** (traditional cold cream uses almond oil with beeswax and borax), moisturising creams and lotions, lip balms, under-eye creams; as a carrier oil for essential oils.

**Therapeutic and cosmetic uses**

- **Emollient** — softens and smoothens dry skin.
- Massage oil for infants and adults.
- Hair nourishment, reduces dryness.
- Carrier oil in aromatherapy.
- Pharmaceutical: softening ear wax (ear drops), vehicle for some oily injections.

## 11.4 Lavender oil

- **Source:** Volatile oil obtained by **steam distillation of the fresh flowering tops** of *Lavandula angustifolia* Miller (*L. officinalis* Chaix).
- **Family:** Labiatae (Lamiaceae).
- **Geographical source:** France, Bulgaria, England, Spain; India (Jammu and Kashmir).

**Chemical constituents**

| Constituent | Note |
| --- | --- |
| **Linalyl acetate** | Main ester; gives the sweet floral odour |
| **Linalool** | Main alcohol |
| 1,8-Cineole, camphor | Smaller amounts (more in the spike lavender variety) |
| Lavandulyl acetate, terpinen-4-ol, β-ocimene | Minor |

Good-quality lavender oil has a high ester (linalyl acetate) content and little camphor.

**Commercial preparations:** perfumes and colognes, soaps, bath oils, shampoos, hair oils, room fresheners, aromatherapy oils and massage blends, lotions.

**Therapeutic and cosmetic uses**

- **Fragrance** in cosmetics and toiletries.
- **Calming and relaxing** in aromatherapy; helps sleep and reduces anxiety.
- Mild antiseptic; used on minor cuts, burns and insect bites (diluted).
- **Insect repellent** (moths, mosquitoes).
- Carminative (old internal use).

## 11.5 Olive oil

- **Source:** Fixed oil obtained by **expression** of the ripe fruits of *Olea europaea* Linn. **Virgin olive oil** is obtained only by mechanical pressing without chemical treatment.
- **Family:** Oleaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Mediterranean countries — Spain, Italy, Greece, Tunisia, Turkey.

**Chemical constituents**

| Constituent | Note |
| --- | --- |
| Glycerides of **oleic acid** | Main fatty acid (about 55–83%) |
| Glycerides of linoleic, palmitic and stearic acids | Smaller |
| **Squalene** | Hydrocarbon; emollient |
| **Polyphenols** — hydroxytyrosol, oleuropein | Antioxidant (mainly in virgin oil) |
| Tocopherols, phytosterols, chlorophyll | Minor |

**Commercial preparations:** creams, lotions, **castile soap**, hair oils and conditioners, lip balms, massage oils, baby oils; pharmaceutical liniments and ointments.

**Therapeutic and cosmetic uses**

- **Emollient and moisturiser** for dry skin; softens skin and nails.
- Hair conditioner; helps dandruff and dry scalp.
- Massage oil.
- Pharmaceutical: base for liniments and ointments; laxative (orally); softens ear wax.
- Diet: cardioprotective oil (Mediterranean diet).

## 11.6 Rosemary oil

- **Source:** Volatile oil obtained by **steam distillation** of the fresh flowering tops and leaves of *Rosmarinus officinalis* Linn. (now *Salvia rosmarinus*).
- **Family:** Labiatae (Lamiaceae).
- **Geographical source:** Spain, Tunisia, Morocco, France, Italy; cultivated in parts of India (Nilgiris, Himachal Pradesh).

**Chemical constituents**

| Constituent | Note |
| --- | --- |
| **1,8-Cineole** | Major monoterpene oxide |
| **Camphor** | Major ketone |
| **α-Pinene** | Hydrocarbon |
| **Borneol**, **bornyl acetate**, camphene | Others |

The **leaf extract** (not the oil) contains the phenolic antioxidants **rosmarinic acid** and **carnosic acid**, used to preserve foods and cosmetics.

**Commercial preparations:** **hair tonics** and hair oils, shampoos, perfumes and colognes (eau de cologne), soaps, liniments and rubefacient balms; rosemary extract as natural antioxidant in creams.

**Therapeutic and cosmetic uses**

- **Hair care** — stimulates the scalp, used against hair fall and dandruff.
- **Rubefacient** — in liniments for muscle and joint pain (improves local blood flow).
- Fragrance in toiletries.
- Antimicrobial and antioxidant.
- Carminative and mild stimulant (traditional internal use).

## 11.7 Sandalwood oil

- **Source:** Volatile oil obtained by **steam distillation of the heartwood** (and roots) of *Santalum album* Linn.
- **Family:** Santalaceae.
- **Geographical source:** India (Karnataka — Mysore, Tamil Nadu, Kerala); Indonesia, Australia. Indian (East Indian) sandalwood oil is highly prized.

**Chemical constituents**

| Constituent | Note |
| --- | --- |
| **α-Santalol and β-santalol** | Sesquiterpene alcohols; together the main part of the oil (about 90%) and responsible for the odour |
| Santalyl acetate, santalene, santalal | Minor |

**Commercial preparations:** perfumes and **attars**, soaps (Mysore sandal soap), face creams, face packs and powders, after-shave lotions, incense sticks (agarbatti), talcum powders.

**Therapeutic and cosmetic uses**

- **Fixative and fragrance** in perfumes (long-lasting woody note).
- **Cooling and soothing** on skin; used in face packs for acne, rashes and prickly heat.
- Mild antiseptic and anti-inflammatory.
- Traditional internal use as urinary antiseptic.
- Religious and cultural use in India.

**Adulterants:** oils from cheaper sandalwood species (*Santalum spicatum* — Australian), East African sandalwood, cedarwood oil, castor oil, synthetic sandalwood-like chemicals.

**Conservation:** *Santalum album* is over-exploited; its harvest and trade are controlled by state governments in India.

## Remember

- Herbal cosmetics use plant oils, gels and extracts for cleansing and beautifying.
- **Aloe gel** = inner leaf pulp (acemannan) — must be aloin-free; latex is the laxative part.
- **Almond oil** = expressed fixed oil, mostly oleic glycerides; emollient; used in cold cream and baby oil.
- **Lavender oil** = flowering tops; **linalyl acetate** and **linalool**; perfume and calming.
- **Olive oil** = expressed fruit oil; oleic acid, squalene, polyphenols; castile soap.
- **Rosemary oil** = cineole, camphor, α-pinene; hair tonic, rubefacient.
- **Sandalwood oil** = heartwood of *Santalum album* (Santalaceae); **santalols**; perfume fixative; Mysore.
- Fixed oils (almond, olive) are **expressed**; volatile oils (lavender, rosemary, sandalwood) are **steam-distilled**.

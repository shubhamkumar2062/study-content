# Chapter 9: Herbs as health food

The syllabus asks for a brief introduction and therapeutic applications of: **nutraceuticals, antioxidants, probiotics, prebiotics, dietary fibres, omega-3 fatty acids, spirulina, carotenoids, soya and garlic**.

## 9.1 Nutraceuticals

### 9.1.1 Definition

The word joins **nutrition** and **pharmaceutical**. It was coined by **Stephen DeFelice** in **1989**.

A **nutraceutical** is a food or part of a food that provides medical or health benefits, including the prevention or treatment of disease.

Related terms:

| Term | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dietary supplement | Product taken by mouth to add nutrients or other substances to the diet | Vitamin capsules, fish oil capsules |
| Functional food | Ordinary-looking food that gives a health benefit beyond basic nutrition | Probiotic yoghurt, oats, omega-3 enriched eggs |
| Health food | Food believed to be especially good for health | Spirulina, sprouts, green tea |

In India, these products are regulated by the **Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI)** under the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006.

### 9.1.2 Classification of nutraceuticals

| Basis | Groups | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source | Plant, animal, microbial, mineral | Curcumin; fish oil; probiotics; calcium |
| Chemical nature | Isoprenoids (carotenoids), phenolics (flavonoids), fatty acids, carbohydrates (fibres), proteins, minerals | β-carotene, quercetin, EPA, inulin, soy protein, zinc |
| Function | Antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, hypolipidaemic, immunity boosters | Vitamin C, curcumin, garlic, spirulina |

### 9.1.3 Uses

Prevention of heart disease, diabetes, obesity, cancer and ageing changes; improving immunity, bone health, gut health and eyesight.

## 9.2 Antioxidants

### 9.2.1 Free radicals and oxidative stress

- A **free radical** is an atom or molecule with an **unpaired electron**, making it highly reactive. Examples: superoxide (O2•−), hydroxyl radical (•OH). Together with hydrogen peroxide they are called **reactive oxygen species (ROS)**.
- They are formed during normal metabolism and increase with smoking, pollution, radiation, infection and stress.
- Excess ROS damage lipids (cell membranes), proteins and DNA. This **oxidative stress** is linked to atherosclerosis, cancer, diabetes complications, cataract, ageing and neurodegenerative diseases.

### 9.2.2 Definition

An **antioxidant** is a substance that, at low concentration, delays or prevents the oxidation of a substrate, usually by neutralising free radicals.

### 9.2.3 Types and sources

| Type | Examples | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enzymatic (made in the body) | Superoxide dismutase, catalase, glutathione peroxidase | — |
| Vitamins | Vitamin C (ascorbic acid), vitamin E (tocopherols) | Amla, citrus; vegetable oils, nuts |
| Carotenoids | β-carotene, lycopene, lutein | Carrot, tomato, marigold |
| Polyphenols / flavonoids | Catechins (EGCG), quercetin, resveratrol, curcumin | Green tea, onion, grapes, turmeric |
| Minerals (cofactors) | Selenium, zinc | Nuts, grains |

**Herbs rich in antioxidants:** amla (*Emblica officinalis*), green tea, turmeric, tulsi, ginkgo, grape seed, ginger.

### 9.2.4 Therapeutic applications

Supportive use in cardiovascular disease, diabetes, cancer prevention, cataract, skin ageing (in creams), and as **preservatives** in foods and medicines (vitamin E, ascorbic acid, rosemary extract).

## 9.3 Probiotics

### 9.3.1 Definition

**Probiotics** are **live microorganisms** which, when administered in adequate amounts, confer a health benefit on the host (FAO/WHO definition).

### 9.3.2 Common probiotic organisms

| Group | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Lactobacilli | *Lactobacillus acidophilus*, *L. rhamnosus*, *L. casei*, *L. plantarum* |
| Bifidobacteria | *Bifidobacterium bifidum*, *B. longum* |
| Others | *Streptococcus thermophilus*, *Bacillus coagulans* (spore-former, survives storage) |
| Yeast | *Saccharomyces boulardii* |

**Sources:** curd (dahi), yoghurt, buttermilk, fermented foods (idli batter, kanji, kimchi), capsules and sachets.

### 9.3.3 Properties of a good probiotic

Survives stomach acid and bile, sticks to the gut lining, is safe (non-pathogenic), and stays alive during storage.

### 9.3.4 How they act

- Compete with harmful bacteria for nutrients and attachment sites.
- Produce lactic acid and antibacterial substances (bacteriocins).
- Strengthen the gut barrier and modulate immunity.

### 9.3.5 Therapeutic applications

- Prevention of **antibiotic-associated diarrhoea**; acute infectious diarrhoea in children.
- Irritable bowel syndrome, lactose intolerance.
- Vaginal infections (with lactobacilli).
- Support of immunity and possibly allergy prevention.

## 9.4 Prebiotics

### 9.4.1 Definition

**Prebiotics** are **non-digestible food ingredients** that pass undigested to the colon and **selectively stimulate the growth or activity of beneficial gut bacteria** (mainly bifidobacteria and lactobacilli).

### 9.4.2 Examples and sources

| Prebiotic | Sources |
| --- | --- |
| **Inulin** | Chicory root, onion, garlic, banana, asparagus |
| **Fructo-oligosaccharides (FOS)** | Onion, banana, wheat |
| **Galacto-oligosaccharides (GOS)** | Made from lactose; human milk oligosaccharides |
| Lactulose | Synthetic (also used as a laxative) |
| Resistant starch | Cooked and cooled rice and potato, green banana |

**Synbiotics** = probiotics + prebiotics in one product.

### 9.4.3 Therapeutic applications

Better bowel movement, increased calcium absorption, lower risk of colon infections, improved gut flora after antibiotics, help in hepatic encephalopathy (lactulose).

## 9.5 Dietary fibres

### 9.5.1 Definition

**Dietary fibre** is the part of plant food that is **not digested by human enzymes** in the small intestine — mainly non-starch polysaccharides and lignin.

### 9.5.2 Types

| Type | Examples | Sources | Main effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Soluble** (form gel in water) | Pectin, gums, mucilage, β-glucan | Isabgol, oats, barley, fruits, guar, fenugreek | Lower cholesterol and blood sugar rise |
| **Insoluble** (do not dissolve) | Cellulose, hemicellulose, lignin | Wheat bran, whole grains, vegetables | Increase stool bulk, prevent constipation |

### 9.5.3 Therapeutic applications

- Relieve **constipation** (bulk laxatives: isabgol, bran).
- Lower serum **cholesterol** (soluble fibre binds bile acids).
- Slow sugar absorption — helpful in **diabetes**.
- Give fullness — help in **weight control**.
- Reduce risk of diverticular disease, haemorrhoids and possibly colon cancer.

**Caution:** take with plenty of water; can reduce absorption of some drugs if taken together.

## 9.6 Omega-3 fatty acids

### 9.6.1 Introduction

Omega-3 fatty acids are **polyunsaturated fatty acids** in which the first double bond is at the **third carbon from the methyl (omega) end**. The body cannot make them sufficiently, so they must come from food (essential fatty acids).

| Omega-3 fatty acid | Carbons : double bonds | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **α-Linolenic acid (ALA)** | 18 : 3 | Flaxseed (alsi), walnuts, chia seeds, soybean oil, mustard oil |
| **Eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA)** | 20 : 5 | Fish oils (cod liver, salmon, sardine) |
| **Docosahexaenoic acid (DHA)** | 22 : 6 | Fish oils, algal oil |

### 9.6.2 Therapeutic applications

- **Cardiovascular protection:** lower blood **triglycerides**, reduce platelet aggregation, mild lowering of blood pressure.
- **Anti-inflammatory:** rheumatoid arthritis.
- **Brain and eye development:** DHA is a major fat of brain and retina; important in pregnancy and infancy.
- Possible benefit in depression and dry eye.

## 9.7 Spirulina

- **Source:** Dried biomass of the blue-green alga (cyanobacterium) ***Spirulina platensis*** (*Arthrospira platensis*) and *S. maxima*. Grown in open ponds of alkaline water; produced in India (Tamil Nadu and elsewhere).
- **Shape:** Spiral (helical) multicellular filaments — hence the name.

**Constituents:**

| Constituent | Note |
| --- | --- |
| Protein | Very high (about 55–70% of dry weight), with all essential amino acids |
| Phycocyanin | Blue pigment-protein; antioxidant |
| β-Carotene and other carotenoids | Provitamin A |
| γ-Linolenic acid (GLA) | Anti-inflammatory fatty acid |
| Vitamins | B-group vitamins, vitamin E |
| Minerals | Iron, calcium, magnesium |
| Chlorophyll | Green pigment |

**Therapeutic applications:** protein and micronutrient supplement in **malnutrition** and anaemia; antioxidant; immunity booster; supportive use in high cholesterol and diabetes; food for astronauts and in disaster relief.

**Caution:** contamination with toxic algae or heavy metals is possible if not grown under control.

## 9.8 Carotenoids

### 9.8.1 Introduction

**Carotenoids** are yellow, orange and red **tetraterpenoid (C40)** pigments made by plants, algae and some microbes. They are of two types:

| Type | Nature | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Carotenes** | Pure hydrocarbons | β-carotene, α-carotene, lycopene |
| **Xanthophylls** | Contain oxygen | Lutein, zeaxanthin, astaxanthin, capsanthin |

**Sources:** carrot and sweet potato (β-carotene), tomato and watermelon (lycopene), marigold flowers and green leafy vegetables (lutein), red pepper (capsanthin), the alga *Dunaliella salina* (β-carotene), *Haematococcus* alga and shrimps (astaxanthin).

### 9.8.2 Therapeutic applications

- **Provitamin A** — β-carotene is converted to vitamin A; prevents night blindness.
- **Antioxidant** — quenches singlet oxygen and free radicals.
- **Eye health** — lutein and zeaxanthin protect the macula (age-related macular degeneration).
- **Lycopene** — studied for protection of the prostate and heart.
- **Skin** — protection against sun damage; natural food colours.

## 9.9 Soya

- **Biological source:** Seeds (beans) of ***Glycine max*** (Linn.) Merrill.
- **Family:** Leguminosae (Fabaceae).
- **Geographical source:** USA, Brazil, Argentina, China; India (Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan).

**Constituents:**

| Constituent | Note |
| --- | --- |
| Protein (about 40%) | Good-quality plant protein |
| Fixed oil (about 20%) | Soybean oil, rich in linoleic acid; also some ALA |
| **Isoflavones** | **Genistein, daidzein** (phytoestrogens) |
| Lecithin (phosphatidylcholine) | Emulsifier; obtained from oil refining |
| Saponins, phytosterols | Lower cholesterol |
| Dietary fibre | — |

**Therapeutic applications:** protein supplement for vegetarians; **lowers cholesterol**; isoflavones relieve **menopausal symptoms** (hot flushes) and may help bone health; lecithin as emulsifier in food and medicines; soy sterols as starting material for steroid drugs.

## 9.10 Garlic

- **Biological source:** Fresh or dried bulbs of ***Allium sativum*** Linn.
- **Family:** Liliaceae (now Amaryllidaceae / Alliaceae).
- **Geographical source:** China, India (Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh), Egypt.

**Constituents and their formation:**

```text
Intact clove: alliin (odourless, sulphur amino acid)  +  enzyme alliinase (kept apart in cells)
                                │
            crushing / chopping │  (cells broken, enzyme meets alliin)
                                ▼
                        ALLICIN (strong garlic smell; antibacterial)
                                │  unstable
                                ▼
       Diallyl disulphide, diallyl trisulphide, ajoene, vinyldithiins
```

**Therapeutic applications:**

- **Hypolipidaemic** — lowers cholesterol and triglycerides.
- **Antiplatelet** (ajoene) and mild antihypertensive — cardioprotective.
- **Antimicrobial** — antibacterial and antifungal (allicin).
- Expectorant; possible anticancer and blood-sugar-lowering effects.

**Caution:** may increase bleeding with anticoagulants (warfarin) and aspirin.

## Remember

- **Nutraceutical** (DeFelice, 1989) = food or food part with medical or health benefit; regulated by FSSAI in India.
- **Antioxidants** neutralise free radicals (ROS); vitamins C and E, carotenoids, polyphenols.
- **Probiotics** = live beneficial microbes (*Lactobacillus*, *Bifidobacterium*, *S. boulardii*); **prebiotics** = non-digestible food for them (inulin, FOS); together = synbiotics.
- **Dietary fibre**: soluble (isabgol, oats, pectin) lowers cholesterol; insoluble (bran, cellulose) relieves constipation.
- **Omega-3**: ALA (flaxseed), EPA and DHA (fish oil); lower triglycerides; DHA for brain and eye.
- **Spirulina** = *Spirulina platensis*, blue-green alga; very high protein; phycocyanin.
- **Carotenoids** = C40 tetraterpenes; carotenes vs xanthophylls; β-carotene is provitamin A.
- **Soya** = *Glycine max*; protein, oil, isoflavones (genistein, daidzein).
- **Garlic**: alliin → (alliinase) → **allicin**; hypolipidaemic, antiplatelet, antimicrobial.

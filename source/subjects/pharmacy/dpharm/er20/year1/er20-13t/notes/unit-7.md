# Chapter 7: Basic principles of traditional systems of medicine

India officially recognises several traditional systems under the **Ministry of AYUSH** (Ayurveda, Yoga and Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, Sowa-Rigpa and Homoeopathy). The syllabus asks for the basic principles of **Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani and Homoeopathy**, and the method of preparation of the Ayurvedic dosage forms **Arishta, Asava, Gutika, Taila, Churna, Lehya and Bhasma**.

## 7.1 Ayurveda

**Meaning:** *Ayu* (life) + *veda* (knowledge) = knowledge or science of life.

**Origin:** Rooted in the Atharvaveda. Main classical texts:

| Text | Author | Main focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Charaka Samhita | Charaka | Internal medicine (Kayachikitsa) |
| Sushruta Samhita | Sushruta | Surgery (Shalya tantra) |
| Ashtanga Hridaya | Vagbhata | Summary of the whole system |

These three are called the **Brihat Trayi** (great trio).

### 7.1.1 Basic principles

1. **Panchamahabhuta (five great elements).** Everything in the universe, including the body and drugs, is made of five elements: **Prithvi** (earth), **Jala/Ap** (water), **Teja/Agni** (fire), **Vayu** (air) and **Akasha** (space or ether).

2. **Tridosha theory.** The five elements combine in the body as three functional forces (doshas):

| Dosha | Elements | Main functions | Excess causes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Vata** | Air + space | Movement, breathing, nerve impulses, excretion | Pain, dryness, nervous disorders |
| **Pitta** | Fire + water | Digestion, metabolism, body heat, vision | Inflammation, acidity, fever |
| **Kapha** | Earth + water | Structure, lubrication, stability, immunity | Congestion, obesity, mucus |

**Health** is the balanced state of the three doshas; **disease** is their imbalance.

3. **Sapta dhatu (seven tissues):** rasa (plasma), rakta (blood), mamsa (muscle), meda (fat), asthi (bone), majja (marrow) and shukra (reproductive tissue).

4. **Mala (wastes):** purisha (faeces), mutra (urine) and sweda (sweat). Proper elimination is needed for health.

5. **Agni (digestive fire):** governs digestion and metabolism. Weak agni produces **ama** (undigested toxic matter), a root of disease.

6. **Prakriti (constitution):** each person is born with a particular dosha balance (vata, pitta or kapha type, or mixed), which guides diet and treatment.

7. **Concept of drug action:** each drug is described by its **rasa** (taste — six tastes: sweet, sour, salty, pungent, bitter, astringent), **guna** (qualities), **virya** (potency — hot or cold), **vipaka** (post-digestive effect) and **prabhava** (special action).

### 7.1.2 Approach to treatment

- **Shodhana** — purification therapy, mainly **Panchakarma** (vamana — emesis; virechana — purgation; basti — medicated enema; nasya — nasal medication; raktamokshana — bloodletting).
- **Shamana** — pacifying therapy with drugs, diet and lifestyle.
- **Rasayana** — rejuvenation therapy (ashwagandha, amla, chyawanprash).
- **Ahara and vihara** — correct diet and daily routine (dinacharya) and seasonal routine (ritucharya).

## 7.2 Siddha system

- **Origin:** Mainly in **Tamil Nadu** and the Tamil-speaking regions. It is credited to **18 Siddhars** (perfected saints), with **Agastya** regarded as its founder.
- **Language:** Classical texts are in Tamil.

### 7.2.1 Basic principles

1. Like Ayurveda, the body is made of the **five elements** and controlled by three humours — **vatham, pitham and kapham** (vali, azhal, iyam).
2. The human body has **96 thathuvas** (basic principles or factors) that make up the physical, mental and spiritual person.
3. **Seven physical constituents (udal thathukkal)** similar to the dhatus.
4. Diagnosis by the **eight-fold examination (envagai thervu)**: pulse (naadi), tongue, colour, voice, eyes, touch, urine and stools. **Pulse reading** is especially important.
5. Siddha makes great use of **metals and minerals** (mercury, sulphur, gold, iron, arsenic compounds after purification) along with plants and animal products. The aim is not only to cure but also to give long life (**kayakalpa** — rejuvenation).

### 7.2.2 Treatment

Herbal and mineral medicines, diet, yoga and **varmam** (pressure-point therapy). Siddha preparations include chendooram (red-coloured calcined powders), parpam (white calcined powders), kudineer (decoctions), legiyam (semi-solid jams) and thailam (oils).

## 7.3 Unani system

- **Origin:** Greece. It is based on the teachings of **Hippocrates** and **Galen**, developed further by Arab and Persian physicians such as **Avicenna (Ibn Sina)** and **Rhazes**. It came to India with the Arabs and Mughals. *Unani* means Greek (from Ionia).

### 7.3.1 Basic principles

1. **Arkan (elements):** fire, air, water and earth.
2. **Mizaj (temperament):** hot, cold, wet and dry qualities; every person and drug has a temperament.
3. **Akhlat (four humours):**

| Humour | Nature |
| --- | --- |
| Dam (blood) | Hot and moist |
| Balgham (phlegm) | Cold and moist |
| Safra (yellow bile) | Hot and dry |
| Sauda (black bile) | Cold and dry |

   **Health** is the balance of the four humours; disease results from disturbance in their quantity or quality.

4. **Tabiyat (medicatrix naturae):** the body's own power to heal itself; the physician helps this power.
5. **Asbab-e-sitta zarooriya** — six essentials for health: air, food and drink, body movement and rest, mental movement and rest, sleep and wakefulness, and elimination and retention.

### 7.3.2 Treatment

| Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Ilaj-bil-tadbeer | Regimental therapy (cupping, massage, exercise, Turkish bath) |
| Ilaj-bil-ghiza | Dietotherapy |
| Ilaj-bil-dawa | Pharmacotherapy (single or compound drugs) |
| Ilaj-bil-yad | Surgery |

Dosage forms include majoon (semi-solid), khamira, arq (distillate), sharbat (syrup), habb (pills), safoof (powder) and roghan (oil).

## 7.4 Homoeopathy

- **Founder:** **Samuel Hahnemann**, a German physician, in **1796**. *Homoios* = similar, *pathos* = suffering.

### 7.4.1 Basic principles

1. **Law of similars** (*similia similibus curentur* — let likes be cured by likes). A substance that produces certain symptoms in a healthy person can cure similar symptoms in a sick person. (Hahnemann noticed that cinchona bark produced fever-like symptoms in him.)
2. **Drug proving.** Drugs are tested on **healthy human volunteers** to record the symptoms they produce. These records form the **materia medica**.
3. **Single remedy.** One medicine at a time.
4. **Minimum dose.** The smallest dose that can cure.
5. **Potentisation (dynamisation).** Drugs are diluted serially with **succussion** (vigorous shaking) for liquids or **trituration** (grinding with lactose) for solids. Potency scales: **decimal (X, 1 in 10)** and **centesimal (C, 1 in 100)**. The starting preparation from plants is the **mother tincture (Ø)**.
6. **Vital force.** A non-material force keeps the body healthy; disease is a disturbance of it.
7. **Individualisation.** Treatment is chosen for the whole person, not just the disease name.
8. **Chronic disease theory (miasms):** psora, sycosis and syphilis.

## 7.5 Comparison of the four systems

| Feature | Ayurveda | Siddha | Unani | Homoeopathy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Origin | India (Vedic) | South India (Tamil) | Greece via Arabia | Germany (1796) |
| Basic theory | Panchamahabhuta, tridosha | Five elements, three humours, 96 thathuvas | Four elements, four humours | Law of similars |
| Main drugs | Plants, minerals | Metals, minerals, plants | Plants, minerals, animal products | Plant, mineral, animal substances in potencies |
| Special feature | Panchakarma, rasayana | Pulse diagnosis, kayakalpa | Regimental therapy | Potentisation, drug proving |

## 7.6 Ayurvedic formulations — methods of preparation

The official reference is the **Ayurvedic Formulary of India (AFI)**; standards are in the **Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India (API)**.

### 7.6.1 Churna (powders)

**Definition:** A fine powder of one or more dried drugs.

**Method:**

1. Clean and dry the drugs (sun or shade drying).
2. Powder each drug separately.
3. Sieve through a fine sieve (the formulary specifies fine powder) to get uniform particles.
4. Mix the powders in the stated proportions until uniform.
5. Pack in airtight containers; protect from moisture.

**Examples:** Triphala churna, Sitopaladi churna, Hingvastaka churna, Avipattikar churna.

**Shelf life:** shorter than most forms because powders take up moisture.

### 7.6.2 Gutika (vati, pills and tablets)

**Definition:** Solid dosage forms made in the shape of pills or tablets from powdered drugs with a binding agent.

**Method:**

1. Prepare fine powder of the drugs (churna).
2. Mix with a binder — honey, jaggery syrup, guggulu, juice (svarasa) or decoction — and grind (bhavana: levigation with liquids) to a smooth, sticky mass.
3. Roll into pills of the stated size by hand or a pill machine, or compress into tablets.
4. Dry in shade and store in airtight containers.

**Examples:** Chitrakadi vati, Sanjivani vati, Arogyavardhini vati, Chandraprabha vati.

### 7.6.3 Lehya (avaleha, linctus or jam)

**Definition:** Semi-solid preparations of drugs with **jaggery or sugar**, usually with ghee or oil and honey.

**Method:**

1. Prepare the decoction (kwatha) or juice of the drugs.
2. Add jaggery or sugar to the liquid, dissolve and filter.
3. Heat on mild fire until it reaches the right consistency (forms a thread between fingers; sinks in water without spreading).
4. Add ghee or oil, then the fine powders (prakshepa) with stirring.
5. Cool and add honey (honey must not be heated).
6. Fill into wide-mouthed containers.

**Examples:** **Chyawanprash**, Agastya haritaki rasayana, Kushmanda rasayana.

### 7.6.4 Taila (medicated oils)

**Definition:** Oils prepared by boiling a fixed oil with a paste (kalka) and a liquid (drava: decoction, juice or milk) of drugs, by **sneha paka** (oil-boiling process).

**Usual proportion:** Kalka : oil : drava = **1 : 4 : 16** (when not stated otherwise).

**Method:**

1. Prepare the kalka (paste of fresh or dried drugs) and the drava (decoction, juice or milk).
2. Mix oil, kalka and drava in a vessel; heat on mild fire with stirring.
3. The water slowly evaporates and the active matter passes into the oil.
4. Test the **end point**: the kalka rolled between fingers forms a non-sticky wick (varti), there is no crackling sound when a bit of kalka is put on fire (shows no moisture), and froth subsides.
5. Three stages of paka: **mridu** (mild — for nasal use), **madhyama** (medium — for internal and general use), **khara** (hard — for external massage).
6. Filter while warm through cloth; perfume (gandha dravya) may be added; store.

**Examples:** Mahanarayan taila, Bhringaraj taila, Ksheerabala taila, Anu taila.

### 7.6.5 Asava and Arishta (fermented preparations)

Both are **self-fermented alcoholic** liquid preparations made by fermenting drug liquids with sugar or jaggery. The alcohol produced extracts the actives and preserves the preparation.

| Feature | Asava | Arishta |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drug liquid | Fresh juice or cold infusion of drugs | **Decoction** (kwatha) of drugs |
| Heating | Not boiled | Drugs boiled to make decoction |
| Examples | Kumaryasava, Kanakasava, Chandanasava | Dashmularishta, Ashokarishta, Arjunarishta, Draksharishta |

**Method (general):**

1. Prepare the decoction (for arishta) or juice / cold infusion (for asava).
2. Dissolve jaggery, sugar or honey in it and filter.
3. Add the fermenting agent — flowers of **Dhataki** (*Woodfordia fruticosa*) — and prakshepa (powdered aromatic drugs).
4. Fill into a clean earthen pot or wooden vat (previously coated with ghee or smoked with spices); seal the mouth.
5. Keep in a quiet place for the stated time (about one month or as the formulary says).
6. **End point of fermentation**: no hissing sound, no more gas bubbles, a burning matchstick held at the mouth keeps burning (no carbon dioxide), and the liquid becomes clear with a characteristic smell.
7. Filter, allow fine particles to settle, decant and store in airtight bottles.

These keep well for a long time because of the alcohol formed during fermentation.

### 7.6.6 Bhasma (calcined preparations)

**Definition:** Fine powders obtained by **calcination (incineration)** of metals, minerals or animal products after purification, in Ayurveda (and similar forms in Siddha).

**Method:**

1. **Shodhana (purification):** the raw metal or mineral is heated and dipped repeatedly in liquids such as cow's urine, buttermilk, sesame oil or plant juices to remove impurities and toxic properties.
2. **Marana (incineration):** the purified material is ground (bhavana) with herbal juices, made into small cakes (chakrika), dried, sealed in earthen saucers (sharava samputa) and heated in a pit fire called **puta** (e.g. gaja puta — a large pit with cow-dung cakes). This is repeated many times.
3. The product is ground to a very fine powder.

**Quality tests (pariksha):**

| Test | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Rekhapurnatva | Powder fills the lines of the fingertip (very fine) |
| Varitara | Powder floats on still water (light, fine) |
| Nishchandratva | No metallic lustre left |
| Apunarbhava | Does not return to metal form when heated with certain substances |
| Nirutha | Does not combine with silver when heated together |

**Examples:** Swarna bhasma (gold), Abhraka bhasma (mica), Lauha bhasma (iron), Shankha bhasma (conch shell), Praval bhasma (coral), Yashada bhasma (zinc).

## Remember

- Ayurveda: **panchamahabhuta** + **tridosha (vata, pitta, kapha)** + 7 dhatus + 3 malas + agni + prakriti; texts of Charaka, Sushruta and Vagbhata.
- Siddha: Tamil, 18 Siddhars (Agastya), 96 thathuvas, eight-fold examination, metals and minerals.
- Unani: Greek (Hippocrates, Galen, Avicenna); **four humours** — dam, balgham, safra, sauda; mizaj; tabiyat.
- Homoeopathy: **Hahnemann (1796)**; similia similibus curentur; drug proving; minimum dose; potentisation (X and C scales).
- Churna = powder; Gutika = pill; Lehya = jam (chyawanprash); Taila = oil (kalka : oil : drava = 1 : 4 : 16); Asava = juice fermented (not boiled); Arishta = decoction fermented; Bhasma = calcined metal/mineral after shodhana and marana.
- Fermenting agent for asava–arishta: **Dhataki flowers**.

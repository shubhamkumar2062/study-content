# Chapter 12: Phytochemical investigation of drugs

**Phytochemical investigation** is the systematic study of a plant to find out which chemical constituents it contains, to separate them, to purify them and to identify their structures. It is the scientific bridge between a traditional herb and a modern drug (for example, from rauwolfia root to reserpine).

## 12.1 Aims

1. To find which **groups of compounds** are present (alkaloids, glycosides, flavonoids, etc.).
2. To **isolate** the active compound responsible for a known use.
3. To **identify and characterise** the compound (structure).
4. To develop **standards** (markers) for quality control of the crude drug and its products.
5. To discover **new drug molecules** or lead compounds.

## 12.2 General scheme

```text
1. Selection of plant (traditional use, literature, chemotaxonomy)
                    ↓
2. Collection & authentication (botanist, herbarium voucher specimen)
                    ↓
3. Drying & size reduction (coarse powder)
                    ↓
4. Extraction (successive solvents or single solvent)
                    ↓
5. Concentration of extracts (rotary evaporator, low temperature)
                    ↓
6. Preliminary phytochemical screening (chemical tests, TLC)
                    ↓
7. Biological screening of extracts (activity tests) — optional but common
                    ↓
8. Fractionation & isolation (chromatography, crystallisation)
                    ↓
9. Purification & tests of purity (melting point, single TLC spot)
                    ↓
10. Characterisation & structure elucidation (UV, IR, NMR, MS)
                    ↓
11. Confirmation of activity of the pure compound
```

## 12.3 Selection, collection and authentication

- **Selection** is based on traditional use (ethnobotany), reported activity, or chemotaxonomic clues (related plants contain related compounds).
- The plant is collected at the right **season and stage** of growth, from the right part.
- **Authentication** is done by a qualified botanist; a **herbarium (voucher) specimen** is kept with a number so that the work can be checked later.

## 12.4 Drying and size reduction

- Fresh material is dried quickly to stop enzyme action and microbial growth — in shade (for aromatic drugs), in sun, or in an oven at controlled temperature (usually not above about 40–60 °C).
- Dried material is ground to a **coarse powder**. Too fine a powder blocks the flow of solvent; too coarse a powder extracts slowly.

## 12.5 Extraction

### 12.5.1 Choice of solvent

Solvents are chosen by **polarity** — "like dissolves like".

| Solvent (low → high polarity) | Extracts mainly |
| --- | --- |
| Petroleum ether / hexane | Fats, fixed oils, waxes, sterols, some terpenoids (defatting step) |
| Chloroform / dichloromethane | Free alkaloid bases, aglycones, terpenoids |
| Ethyl acetate | Flavonoid aglycones, phenolics, less polar glycosides |
| Ethanol / methanol | Glycosides, alkaloid salts, tannins, flavonoids, most constituents |
| Water | Sugars, gums, mucilage, tannins, polar glycosides, proteins |

### 12.5.2 Successive (sequential) extraction

The same powder is extracted one after another with solvents of **increasing polarity** (petroleum ether → chloroform → ethyl acetate → ethanol → water). This divides the constituents into groups from the start. A Soxhlet apparatus is often used.

### 12.5.3 Methods

| Method | Feature |
| --- | --- |
| Maceration | Soaking at room temperature; simple; for heat-sensitive compounds |
| Percolation | Slow downward flow of fresh solvent; more complete |
| Infusion and decoction | With water; decoction involves boiling |
| Soxhlet (continuous hot) extraction | Small volume of solvent recycled; efficient; not for heat-sensitive compounds |
| Steam distillation | For volatile oils |
| Ultrasound-assisted extraction | Sound waves break cells; quick |
| Microwave-assisted extraction | Rapid heating inside the material |
| Supercritical fluid extraction (CO2) | Clean, no solvent residue; for oils and delicate compounds |

### 12.5.4 Concentration

Extracts are concentrated under reduced pressure in a **rotary vacuum evaporator** at low temperature, then dried (vacuum drying, freeze drying or spray drying). The **percentage yield** of extract is recorded:

Yield (%) = (weight of dried extract ÷ weight of powdered drug taken) × 100

## 12.6 Preliminary phytochemical screening

Simple chemical tests show which groups are present in each extract.

| Group | Test | Positive result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Alkaloids** | Mayer's / Dragendorff's / Wagner's / Hager's reagent | Cream / orange-red / reddish-brown / yellow precipitate |
| **Carbohydrates** | Molisch's test (α-naphthol + conc. H2SO4) | Violet ring at junction |
| Reducing sugars | Fehling's or Benedict's test | Brick-red precipitate |
| **Proteins / amino acids** | Biuret test; Ninhydrin test; Millon's test | Violet; purple/blue; red |
| **Anthraquinone glycosides** | Borntrager's test | Pink to red in ammonia layer |
| **Cardiac glycosides** | Keller–Killiani; Legal's; Baljet's test | Reddish-brown ring with bluish-green layer; pink-red; yellow-orange |
| **Saponins** | Foam test; haemolysis | Persistent froth; lysis of red cells |
| **Cyanogenetic glycosides** | Sodium picrate paper | Yellow to brick-red |
| **Flavonoids** | Shinoda test (Mg ribbon + conc. HCl); lead acetate; NaOH | Pink to red; yellow precipitate; yellow colour |
| **Tannins / phenolics** | Ferric chloride; gelatin solution; lead acetate | Blue-black or green-black; white precipitate; precipitate |
| **Steroids / triterpenoids** | Salkowski; Liebermann–Burchard | Reddish-brown at junction; colours ending in blue-green |
| **Fixed oils and fats** | Spot test on filter paper; Sudan III | Permanent translucent spot; red stain |
| **Volatile oils** | Spot test; odour | Spot evaporates; aromatic smell |
| **Gums and mucilage** | Swelling in water; ruthenium red; precipitation with alcohol | Swells; pink stain; precipitate |

**Thin layer chromatography (TLC)** of each extract gives a quick picture of how many compounds are present.

## 12.7 Separation and isolation

### 12.7.1 Classical methods

- **Liquid–liquid partition** (for example, acid–base partition for alkaloids).
- **Fractional crystallisation** — compounds crystallise out at different stages.
- **Fractional distillation** — for volatile oils.
- **Precipitation** — e.g. tannins with gelatin or lead acetate; gums with alcohol.
- **Sublimation** — e.g. caffeine, benzoic acid.

### 12.7.2 Chromatographic methods

**Chromatography** separates compounds by their different distribution between a **stationary phase** and a **mobile phase**.

| Technique | Stationary phase | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper chromatography | Filter paper (water held in cellulose) | Sugars, amino acids |
| **Thin layer chromatography (TLC)** | Silica gel layer on plate | Quick identification, monitoring fractions |
| **Column chromatography** | Silica gel or alumina packed in a glass column | Isolation of compounds in larger amounts |
| **HPTLC** | Fine silica plates, instrumental application and scanning | Fingerprinting and assay of herbal drugs |
| **HPLC** | Fine particles in steel column, liquid under high pressure | Assay of markers; purification |
| **Gas chromatography (GC)** | Liquid on solid support in a column, gas mobile phase | Volatile oils |

**Rf value (TLC and paper):**

Rf = distance travelled by the compound ÷ distance travelled by the solvent front

Rf is always between 0 and 1 and helps identify a compound against a standard run on the same plate.

```text
TLC plate after development

 ┌─────────┐ ← solvent front
 │         │
 │    ●    │ ← spot (compound) — distance a
 │         │
 │         │
 │ ─ ─ ─ ─ │ ← start line (sample applied)
 └─────────┘
 Rf = a ÷ (distance from start to solvent front)
```

**Visualising spots:** UV light (254 nm and 365 nm), iodine vapour, and spray reagents (Dragendorff's for alkaloids, anisaldehyde–sulphuric acid for terpenoids, ferric chloride for phenolics).

## 12.8 Purification and tests of purity

- **Recrystallisation** from a suitable solvent until constant properties are reached.
- **Melting point** — a pure compound has a sharp melting point.
- **Single spot on TLC** in two or more solvent systems.
- **Single peak on HPLC**.

## 12.9 Characterisation and structure elucidation

| Method | What it tells |
| --- | --- |
| Physical constants | Melting point, boiling point, optical rotation, solubility |
| Chemical tests and derivatives | Functional groups |
| **UV–visible spectroscopy** | Conjugated systems, chromophores (e.g. flavonoids) |
| **Infrared (IR) spectroscopy** | Functional groups (OH, C=O, NH, C=C) |
| **Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR)** | Carbon–hydrogen framework |
| **Mass spectrometry (MS)** | Molecular weight and fragmentation pattern |
| X-ray crystallography | Exact three-dimensional structure |

The structure is compared with published data for known compounds; a new compound is fully characterised.

## 12.10 Biological evaluation

Extracts, fractions and pure compounds are tested for the expected activity (antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, antidiabetic, etc.). **Bioassay-guided fractionation** means testing every fraction and following only the active ones, so that the active compound is reached quickly.

## 12.11 Applications of phytochemical investigation

1. Discovery of new drugs (reserpine, vincristine, artemisinin, paclitaxel).
2. Standardisation of crude drugs and herbal products using **marker compounds**.
3. Detection of adulterants by chemical fingerprints (TLC/HPTLC).
4. Chemotaxonomy — classification using chemical constituents.
5. Finding cheaper or renewable sources of known compounds.
6. Semi-synthesis of new derivatives (artesunate from artemisinin, etoposide from podophyllotoxin).

## Remember

- Scheme: **select → authenticate → dry and powder → extract → concentrate → screen → isolate → purify → identify → test activity**.
- Keep a **voucher specimen** for authentication.
- Successive extraction uses solvents of **increasing polarity**: petroleum ether → chloroform → ethyl acetate → alcohol → water.
- Screening tests: Dragendorff's (alkaloids), Molisch's (carbohydrates), Borntrager's (anthraquinones), Keller–Killiani (cardiac glycosides), foam (saponins), Shinoda (flavonoids), FeCl3 (tannins), Salkowski (steroids).
- Chromatography: paper, TLC, column, HPTLC, HPLC, GC; **Rf = distance of spot ÷ distance of solvent front**.
- Structure by **UV, IR, NMR and MS**.
- Bioassay-guided fractionation leads to the active compound.

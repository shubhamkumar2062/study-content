# How to use this guide

Pharmacognosy is a memory subject: botanical names, families, constituents, uses and tests. Short daily revision works far better than one long session. Study one chapter at a time, read the notes, cover them and write the monograph headings from memory, then answer the question bank without looking.

## Exam pattern (HPTSB, ER-2020)

The D.Pharm first-year theory paper is a yearly (annual) examination conducted by the board under the PCI Education Regulations 2020.

| Part | Marks |
| --- | --- |
| Board (university) theory examination | 80 marks, 3 hours |
| Sessional (internal) assessment by the college | 20 marks |
| **Total for ER20-13T** | **100 marks** |

The practical (ER20-13P) is assessed separately in the same way (board practical examination plus sessional marks).

**Paper pattern (80 marks, 3 hours).** This follows the ER-2020 board theory paper scheme. Confirm the pattern with your college, because the board may change the number and type of questions.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective (multiple-choice) questions — answer all 20 | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Short answers — answer any 10 of 11 | 10 × 3 = 30 |
| C | Long answers — answer any 6 of 7 | 6 × 5 = 30 |

In this guide, long questions carry 5 marks, short questions 3 marks, and a few 2-mark very short questions are kept for quick revision. A 3-mark answer needs about half a page: definition, source and three or four key points. A 5-mark answer needs about a page with headings.

## Where the marks come from

Chapter 5 (crude drug monographs) carries 30 of the 75 teaching hours, so expect **at least one long question and several short notes from it**. Chapters 3 (quality control), 4 (phytoconstituents) and 7 (traditional systems and Ayurvedic dosage forms) are the next most important. Chapters 1, 8 and 12 are short and usually appear as short answers or objective questions.

## Probability ratings

- **High** — appears in most years in some form; prepare the full answer.
- **Medium** — appears every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for very short answers and as a backup choice.

The ratings are estimates from the syllabus weight and the kind of questions commonly set in D.Pharm Pharmacognosy papers. They are not a promise of what will be asked.

## Scoring tips for Pharmacognosy

1. **Use the monograph headings** for every drug: synonyms, biological source, family, geographical source, chemical constituents, uses, adulterants, test. Each heading earns marks even if one detail is wrong.
2. **Write the biological source completely**: part used + botanical name + author + family. "Dried leaflets of *Cassia angustifolia* Vahl, family Leguminosae" scores; "senna plant" does not.
3. **Underline botanical names** in the answer sheet (you cannot italicise by hand). Write the genus with a capital letter and the species in small letters.
4. **Name the active principle** in bold or underline: sennosides, digitoxin, quinine, reserpine, piperine, eugenol.
5. **Give one chemical test** wherever you know it (Borntrager's, Keller–Killiani, Vitali–Morin, murexide, thalleioquin). Examiners value it.
6. **Draw simple diagrams** for TS questions and for flow charts (alkaloid isolation, phytochemical investigation, evaluation of crude drugs).
7. For "classification" questions, give the **basis, examples, merits and demerits** of each system.
8. For Ayurvedic dosage forms, write **definition → method step by step → end point/tests → examples**.
9. In very short and objective questions, one exact fact is enough — botanical name, family or chief constituent.
10. Revise the **"Remember"** list at the end of each chapter and the last-night revision sheet before the exam.

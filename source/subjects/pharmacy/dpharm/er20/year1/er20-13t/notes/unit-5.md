# Chapter 5: Biological source, chemical constituents and therapeutic efficacy of crude drugs

This is the largest chapter (30 of 75 hours) and the main source of questions. Each drug is given as a short **monograph** with standard headings. In the exam, write in this order:

```text
1. Synonyms (local names)        5. Chemical constituents
2. Biological source             6. Uses (therapeutic efficacy)
3. Family                        7. Adulterants / substitutes (if any)
4. Geographical source           8. A chemical test (if known)
```

**Tip:** the **biological source** is "the part used + the botanical name + its author". Write the Latin name in italics or underline it in the answer sheet.

## 5.1 Laxatives

### 5.1.1 Aloe

- **Synonyms:** Musabbar, Kumari (plant), Elio.
- **Biological source:** Dried juice (latex) of the leaves of *Aloe barbadensis* Miller (Curaçao aloe), *Aloe ferox* Miller (Cape aloe) and *Aloe perryi* Baker (Socotrine aloe).
- **Family:** Liliaceae (now placed in Asphodelaceae / Xanthorrhoeaceae).
- **Geographical source:** West Indies (Curaçao), South Africa (Cape), Socotra islands; cultivated in India (Gujarat, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu).
- **Chemical constituents:** Anthraquinone C-glycosides — **barbaloin (aloin)** as the main one, isobarbaloin; free aloe-emodin; aloesin (chromone); resins.
- **Uses:** Stimulant purgative (acts on the large intestine); the colourless leaf **gel** (a different product) is used for burns, wounds and in cosmetics.
- **Tests:** Modified Borntrager's test (pink); **borax (Schouteten's) test** — aloe solution heated with borax gives a green fluorescence.

### 5.1.2 Castor oil

- **Synonyms:** Oleum ricini, Erandi tel.
- **Biological source:** Fixed oil obtained by cold expression of the seeds of *Ricinus communis* Linn.
- **Family:** Euphorbiaceae.
- **Geographical source:** India (Gujarat is a major producer), Brazil, China.
- **Chemical constituents:** Triglycerides of **ricinoleic acid** (about 80–90%), with small amounts of oleic, linoleic and stearic acids. The seed (not the oil) contains the very toxic protein **ricin** and the alkaloid **ricinine**; they stay in the press-cake.
- **Uses:** Purgative (lipase in the intestine releases ricinoleic acid, which irritates the bowel); emollient; lubricant; plasticiser; base for soaps and hair oils.
- **Special feature:** Very viscous, soluble in alcohol (unlike most fixed oils), high acetyl value.

### 5.1.3 Ispaghula

- **Synonyms:** Isabgol, Psyllium (blond psyllium), Spogel.
- **Biological source:** Dried ripe seeds of *Plantago ovata* Forskal. The seed husk (epidermis) is used separately as ispaghula husk.
- **Family:** Plantaginaceae.
- **Geographical source:** India (Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh); India is the world's main producer and exporter.
- **Chemical constituents:** **Mucilage** in the outer seed coat (made of xylose, arabinose and galacturonic acid); fixed oil; proteins; the glycoside aucubin.
- **Uses:** **Bulk laxative** (swells with water, softens stool); in diarrhoea and irritable bowel syndrome; lowers blood cholesterol; demulcent.
- **Evaluation:** Swelling factor; seeds are boat-shaped with a whitish membrane on the concave side.

### 5.1.4 Senna

- **Synonyms:** Sanay, Sonamukhi.
- **Biological source:** Dried leaflets of *Cassia angustifolia* Vahl (Indian or Tinnevelly senna) and *Cassia acutifolia* Delile (Alexandrian senna). Pods are also used.
- **Family:** Leguminosae (Caesalpinioideae).
- **Geographical source:** Indian senna — Tamil Nadu (Tinnevelly), cultivated in Gujarat and Rajasthan; Alexandrian senna — Sudan and Egypt.
- **Chemical constituents:** Dianthrone glycosides **sennosides A and B** (main), sennosides C and D; glycosides of rhein and aloe-emodin; flavonoids (kaempferol); mucilage.
- **Uses:** Stimulant purgative for constipation and bowel cleansing before X-ray or surgery.
- **Adulterants:** *Cassia auriculata* (Palthe or Avaram senna — gives red colour with 80% H2SO4), *Cassia obovata* (dog senna).
- **Microscopy:** Isobilateral leaf, paracytic (rubiaceous) stomata, unicellular warty hairs, calcium oxalate crystal sheath.
- **Test:** Borntrager's test — pink in the ammonia layer.

## 5.2 Cardiotonics

### 5.2.1 Digitalis

- **Synonyms:** Foxglove leaves.
- **Biological source:** Dried leaves of *Digitalis purpurea* Linn., dried at a temperature not above 60 °C to protect the glycosides.
- **Family:** Scrophulariaceae (now Plantaginaceae).
- **Geographical source:** England, Germany, France; cultivated in India in Kashmir, the Nilgiris and Darjeeling.
- **Chemical constituents:** Cardiac (cardenolide) glycosides — **purpurea glycosides A and B**, which give **digitoxin** and **gitoxin**; gitaloxin. *Digitalis lanata* gives **lanatosides** and **digoxin**. Also saponins (digitonin).
- **Uses:** Congestive heart failure (increases force of heart contraction), atrial fibrillation (slows the heart rate).
- **Adulterants:** Leaves of *Verbascum thapsus* (mullein), *Symphytum officinale* (comfrey), *Primula vulgaris*.
- **Test:** **Keller–Killiani test** positive (deoxy-sugar digitoxose). Potency is checked by bioassay.

### 5.2.2 Arjuna

- **Synonyms:** Arjun bark.
- **Biological source:** Dried stem bark of *Terminalia arjuna* (Roxb.) Wight and Arn.
- **Family:** Combretaceae.
- **Geographical source:** India — along river banks in central and northern India.
- **Chemical constituents:** Triterpenoid saponins (**arjunolic acid**, arjunic acid, arjunglucosides), flavonoids, tannins, β-sitosterol and calcium salts.
- **Uses:** Cardioprotective and cardiotonic in Ayurveda; used in angina, mild heart failure and hypertension; astringent.

## 5.3 Carminatives and gastro-intestinal regulators

**Carminatives** relieve flatulence (gas) and colic. Most contain volatile oils.

### 5.3.1 Coriander

- **Synonyms:** Dhania.
- **Biological source:** Dried ripe fruits of *Coriandrum sativum* Linn.
- **Family:** Umbelliferae (Apiaceae).
- **Geographical source:** India (Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat), Russia, Morocco.
- **Chemical constituents:** Volatile oil (about 0.3–1%) rich in **linalool (coriandrol)**; fixed oil; proteins.
- **Uses:** Carminative, stomachic, flavouring agent; spice.
- **Note:** The fruit is a globular cremocarp; the two mericarps do not separate easily.

### 5.3.2 Fennel

- **Synonyms:** Saunf, Badi saunf.
- **Biological source:** Dried ripe fruits of *Foeniculum vulgare* Miller.
- **Family:** Umbelliferae.
- **Geographical source:** India (Gujarat, Rajasthan), Europe, Egypt.
- **Chemical constituents:** Volatile oil (about 3–6%) with **anethole** (main, sweet) and **fenchone** (bitter); fixed oil; proteins.
- **Uses:** Carminative, flavouring; in gripe water for infant colic; mouth freshener.
- **Adulterant:** Exhausted fennel (oil removed).

### 5.3.3 Cardamom

- **Synonyms:** Elaichi, Small cardamom.
- **Biological source:** Dried ripe seeds of *Elettaria cardamomum* Maton var. *minuscula* Burkill, kept in their fruits (capsules) until use.
- **Family:** Zingiberaceae.
- **Geographical source:** India (Kerala, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu — the Western Ghats), Sri Lanka, Guatemala.
- **Chemical constituents:** Volatile oil with **1,8-cineole** and **α-terpinyl acetate**; limonene; starch; fixed oil.
- **Uses:** Carminative, stomachic, flavouring of food and medicines.
- **Adulterants/substitutes:** Seeds of *Amomum subulatum* (greater or Nepal cardamom) and other *Amomum* species.

### 5.3.4 Ginger

- **Synonyms:** Sonth (dry ginger), Adrak (fresh ginger), Zingiber.
- **Biological source:** Dried rhizomes of *Zingiber officinale* Roscoe, scraped (peeled) or unscraped.
- **Family:** Zingiberaceae.
- **Geographical source:** India (Kerala — Cochin ginger, Meghalaya, Karnataka), Jamaica, China, Africa.
- **Chemical constituents:** Volatile oil (about 1–3%) with **zingiberene**, bisabolene; oleoresin containing the pungent **gingerols** and **shogaols**; starch.
- **Uses:** Carminative, stomachic, **antiemetic** (motion and morning sickness), flavouring; in Trikatu (with black and long pepper).
- **Adulterants:** Exhausted ginger; Japanese ginger (*Zingiber mioga*).

### 5.3.5 Clove

- **Synonyms:** Laung, Caryophyllum.
- **Biological source:** Dried flower buds of *Syzygium aromaticum* (Linn.) Merrill and Perry (*Eugenia caryophyllus*).
- **Family:** Myrtaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Zanzibar and Pemba (Tanzania), Madagascar, Indonesia; India (Kerala, Tamil Nadu).
- **Chemical constituents:** Volatile oil (about 15–20%) with **eugenol** (about 70–90% of the oil), acetyl eugenol and β-caryophyllene; tannins; oleanolic acid.
- **Uses:** Carminative, antiseptic, **dental analgesic** (clove oil for toothache), flavouring; source of eugenol.
- **Adulterants:** Clove stalks, mother cloves (ripe fruits), blown cloves (opened flowers), exhausted cloves.
- **Evaluation:** Good cloves sink in water; exhausted cloves float. Oil cells in the hypanthium.

### 5.3.6 Black pepper

- **Synonyms:** Kali mirch, Piper.
- **Biological source:** Dried unripe fruits of *Piper nigrum* Linn.
- **Family:** Piperaceae.
- **Geographical source:** India (Kerala — Malabar coast, Karnataka), Indonesia, Brazil, Vietnam.
- **Chemical constituents:** Alkaloid **piperine** (main pungent principle), chavicine, piperidine; volatile oil (about 1–2.5%); resin; starch.
- **Uses:** Carminative, stomachic, condiment; **bioavailability enhancer** (piperine increases the absorption of other drugs); part of Trikatu.
- **Adulterant:** **Dried papaya seeds**; also exhausted pepper and stones.
- **Note:** White pepper is the ripe fruit with the outer pericarp removed.

### 5.3.7 Asafoetida

- **Synonyms:** Hing, Devil's dung.
- **Biological source:** Oleo-gum-resin obtained by incising the living rhizomes and roots of *Ferula assa-foetida* Linn., *Ferula foetida* Regel and other *Ferula* species.
- **Family:** Umbelliferae.
- **Geographical source:** Iran and Afghanistan; India imports it and processes it.
- **Chemical constituents:** Resin (40–65%) containing ferulic acid esters; gum (about 25%); volatile oil with **sulphur compounds** (disulphides, giving the garlic-like smell); free ferulic acid. Umbelliferone is formed on hydrolysis.
- **Uses:** Carminative, flatulence, expectorant, antispasmodic; condiment in Indian cooking.
- **Adulterants:** Gum arabic, rosin, gypsum, red clay, chalk, wheat or rice flour, stones.
- **Tests:** (1) Triturated with water it forms an orange-yellow emulsion. (2) Fresh fracture + conc. sulphuric acid → red to reddish-brown. (3) Boiled with HCl, filtered, filtrate made alkaline with ammonia → **blue fluorescence** (umbelliferone).

### 5.3.8 Nutmeg

- **Synonyms:** Jaiphal; the aril is mace (Javitri).
- **Biological source:** Dried kernels of the seeds of *Myristica fragrans* Houttuyn. **Mace** is the dried arillus of the same seed.
- **Family:** Myristicaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Indonesia (Banda islands), Grenada (West Indies), Sri Lanka; India (Kerala).
- **Chemical constituents:** Volatile oil (about 5–15%) with **myristicin**, elemicin, safrole and terpenes; fixed oil (about 25–40%, called nutmeg butter, mainly **trimyristin**); starch.
- **Uses:** Carminative, stomachic, flavouring. Large doses are toxic (myristicin causes hallucinations).
- **Adulterant of mace:** Bombay mace (*Myristica malabarica*).

### 5.3.9 Cinnamon

- **Synonyms:** Dalchini, Ceylon cinnamon.
- **Biological source:** Dried inner bark of the shoots of coppiced trees of *Cinnamomum zeylanicum* Nees (*C. verum*).
- **Family:** Lauraceae.
- **Geographical source:** Sri Lanka (main), India (Kerala), Seychelles.
- **Chemical constituents:** Volatile oil (about 0.5–1%) with **cinnamaldehyde** (about 60–75%) and **eugenol** (about 4–10%); tannins; mucilage; calcium oxalate.
- **Uses:** Carminative, stomachic, antiseptic, flavouring.
- **Adulterant:** **Cassia bark** (*Cinnamomum cassia*, Chinese cinnamon) — thicker, coarser, and its oil has little eugenol.
- **Identification:** Thin, compound quills, one inside another; light brown; fragrant, sweet and warm taste.

## 5.4 Astringents

Astringents precipitate proteins on mucous membranes; used in diarrhoea, sore throat and bleeding gums. Most contain **tannins**.

### 5.4.1 Myrobalan

- **Synonyms:** Harad, Haritaki, Chebulic myrobalan.
- **Biological source:** Dried ripe fruits of *Terminalia chebula* Retzius.
- **Family:** Combretaceae.
- **Geographical source:** India (Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, Bengal), Myanmar.
- **Chemical constituents:** Hydrolysable tannins (about 30–40%) — **chebulagic acid**, **chebulinic acid**; gallic and ellagic acids; anthraquinone glycosides; resin.
- **Uses:** Astringent, mild laxative, component of **Triphala** (with amla and bahera); tanning and dyeing.

### 5.4.2 Black catechu

- **Synonyms:** Kattha, Cutch.
- **Biological source:** Dried aqueous extract prepared from the **heartwood** of *Acacia catechu* Willd.
- **Family:** Leguminosae (Mimosoideae).
- **Geographical source:** India (Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Karnataka), Myanmar.
- **Preparation:** Heartwood chips boiled with water, the decoction concentrated and poured into moulds to set.
- **Chemical constituents:** **Catechutannic acid** (condensed tannin), **catechin (acacatechin)**, quercetin, catechu-red, gum.
- **Uses:** Astringent in diarrhoea, gargle for sore throat and bleeding gums; ingredient of pan; dyeing and tanning.
- **Tests:** Matchstick test (pink), gelatin test (precipitate), FeCl3 (greenish-black).

### 5.4.3 Pale catechu

- **Synonyms:** Gambir, Gambier.
- **Biological source:** Dried aqueous extract prepared from the **leaves and young shoots** of *Uncaria gambier* Roxb.
- **Family:** Rubiaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Malaysia, Indonesia (Sumatra, Borneo), Singapore.
- **Chemical constituents:** **Catechins** (about 30–35%), catechutannic acid, quercetin, gambir-fluorescein, catechu-red.
- **Uses:** Astringent in diarrhoea, local astringent in gargles; tanning and dyeing.
- **Special test (gambir-fluorescein):** Alcoholic extract + sodium hydroxide, shaken with petroleum ether → **green fluorescence** in the petroleum ether layer. Black catechu does not give this.

| Feature | Black catechu | Pale catechu |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plant part | Heartwood | Leaves and young shoots |
| Plant | *Acacia catechu* | *Uncaria gambier* |
| Family | Leguminosae | Rubiaceae |
| Colour | Dark brown to black | Pale brown cubes |
| Gambir-fluorescein test | Negative | Positive (green fluorescence) |

## 5.5 Drugs acting on the nervous system

### 5.5.1 Hyoscyamus

- **Synonyms:** Henbane, Khurasani ajwain (seed).
- **Biological source:** Dried leaves and flowering tops of *Hyoscyamus niger* Linn. Egyptian henbane is *Hyoscyamus muticus*.
- **Family:** Solanaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Europe; India (Himalayas, Kashmir, Punjab hills).
- **Chemical constituents:** Tropane alkaloids — **hyoscyamine** (main), **hyoscine (scopolamine)**, atropine (formed from hyoscyamine during extraction).
- **Uses:** Antispasmodic in intestinal and urinary colic, sedative, anticholinergic; hyoscine for motion sickness.
- **Test:** **Vitali–Morin test** — residue treated with fuming nitric acid, dried, then methanolic KOH added → violet colour (tropane alkaloids).

### 5.5.2 Belladonna

- **Synonyms:** Deadly nightshade.
- **Biological source:** Dried leaves and flowering tops (herb) of *Atropa belladonna* Linn. (European belladonna) or *Atropa acuminata* Royle (Indian belladonna).
- **Family:** Solanaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Europe (England, Germany); India (Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh).
- **Chemical constituents:** Tropane alkaloids — **hyoscyamine** (main), **atropine** (the racemic form), hyoscine (small amount); scopoletin (a coumarin, gives fluorescence).
- **Uses:** Anticholinergic; antispasmodic (gut colic), reduces secretions before anaesthesia, mydriatic in eye examination (atropine), antidote in organophosphate poisoning (atropine).
- **Adulterants:** Leaves of *Phytolacca americana*, *Ailanthus*, *Scopolia*.
- **Test:** Vitali–Morin test (violet).

### 5.5.3 Ephedra

- **Synonyms:** Ma Huang, Soma (in some old texts).
- **Biological source:** Dried young stems of *Ephedra gerardiana* Wall., *Ephedra sinica* Stapf, *Ephedra equisetina* Bunge and other species.
- **Family:** Ephedraceae (Gnetaceae).
- **Geographical source:** China; India (Himalayas — Sikkim, Kashmir); Pakistan.
- **Chemical constituents:** Protoalkaloids (amine alkaloids) — **ephedrine** (main), **pseudoephedrine**, norephedrine.
- **Uses:** Bronchodilator in asthma, nasal decongestant (pseudoephedrine), CNS stimulant, raises blood pressure (used in hypotension during spinal anaesthesia).
- **Note:** Ephedrine has no nitrogen in a ring, so it gives poor results with general alkaloid reagents.

### 5.5.4 Opium

- **Synonyms:** Afeem, Raw opium.
- **Biological source:** Dried latex obtained by making shallow incisions on the **unripe capsules** of *Papaver somniferum* Linn.
- **Family:** Papaveraceae.
- **Geographical source:** India (licensed cultivation in Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh), Turkey, Afghanistan.
- **Chemical constituents:** About 25 alkaloids, present as salts of **meconic acid**: **morphine** (about 10%, the main one), **codeine**, **thebaine**, **papaverine**, **noscapine (narcotine)**, narceine. Also resin, sugars and mucilage.
- **Uses:** Morphine — strong analgesic; codeine — antitussive and mild analgesic; papaverine — smooth-muscle relaxant; noscapine — antitussive; opium preparations — antidiarrhoeal. Opium is a narcotic drug under the NDPS Act.
- **Adulterants:** Starch, sand, stones, other plant latices, capsule pieces.
- **Test:** **Meconic acid test** — aqueous extract + ferric chloride → deep red colour that does not disappear with dilute HCl.

### 5.5.5 Tea leaves

- **Synonyms:** Chai, Thea.
- **Biological source:** Prepared leaves and leaf buds of *Thea sinensis* Linn. (*Camellia sinensis* (L.) O. Kuntze).
- **Family:** Theaceae.
- **Geographical source:** India (Assam, Darjeeling, Nilgiris), China, Sri Lanka, Kenya.
- **Chemical constituents:** Purine alkaloid **caffeine** (about 1–4%), small amounts of theophylline and theobromine; **tannins / catechins** (epigallocatechin gallate); volatile oil; enzymes.
- **Uses:** CNS stimulant, diuretic, beverage; commercial source of caffeine; green tea catechins are antioxidants.
- **Test:** **Murexide test** — caffeine + potassium chlorate + HCl, evaporated to dryness; residue + ammonia vapour → purple colour.

### 5.5.6 Coffee seeds

- **Synonyms:** Coffee beans.
- **Biological source:** Dried ripe seeds of *Coffea arabica* Linn. (Arabian coffee) and *Coffea canephora* (robusta coffee), freed from the seed coat; roasted for use.
- **Family:** Rubiaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Brazil, Colombia, Ethiopia; India (Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu).
- **Chemical constituents:** **Caffeine** (about 1–2%), **trigonelline**, **chlorogenic acid**, fixed oil, sugars, proteins.
- **Uses:** CNS stimulant, diuretic, beverage; source of caffeine.
- **Adulterants (of powder):** Roasted chicory root, roasted cereals.

### 5.5.7 Coca

- **Synonyms:** Coca leaves.
- **Biological source:** Dried leaves of *Erythroxylum coca* Lamarck (Bolivian or Huanuco coca) and *Erythroxylum truxillense* Rusby (Peruvian or Truxillo coca).
- **Family:** Erythroxylaceae.
- **Geographical source:** South America (Peru, Bolivia, Colombia); formerly Java and Sri Lanka.
- **Chemical constituents:** Tropane alkaloids (ecgonine derivatives) — **cocaine** (methyl benzoyl ecgonine, main), cinnamoyl cocaine, truxillines; hygrine and cuscohygrine.
- **Uses:** Cocaine — **surface local anaesthetic** in eye, ear, nose and throat surgery; CNS stimulant. Highly addictive; a controlled narcotic.
- **Identification:** Leaf with two curved lines (one on each side of the midrib) on the lower surface.

## 5.6 Antihypertensive

### 5.6.1 Rauwolfia

- **Synonyms:** Sarpagandha, Indian snakeroot.
- **Biological source:** Dried roots of *Rauwolfia serpentina* Benth. ex Kurz.
- **Family:** Apocynaceae.
- **Geographical source:** India (sub-Himalayan tracts, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Assam, Western Ghats), Bangladesh, Myanmar, Thailand.
- **Chemical constituents:** Indole alkaloids (about 0.7–2.4%) — **reserpine**, rescinnamine, deserpidine (reserpine group, hypotensive and sedative); **ajmaline**, ajmalicine, serpentine, yohimbine.
- **Uses:** **Hypertension** (reserpine depletes noradrenaline stores), tranquilliser in mental illness, ajmaline as antiarrhythmic.
- **Adulterants:** Roots of other *Rauwolfia* species (*R. tetraphylla*, *R. vomitoria*) and stems.
- **Identification:** Cylindrical, slightly tortuous pieces with a short fracture and a bitter taste.

## 5.7 Antitussives

### 5.7.1 Vasaka

- **Synonyms:** Adusa, Malabar nut.
- **Biological source:** Fresh or dried leaves of *Adhatoda vasica* Nees (*Justicia adhatoda* Linn.).
- **Family:** Acanthaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Throughout India, especially the sub-Himalayan region.
- **Chemical constituents:** Quinazoline alkaloids — **vasicine** (main) and **vasicinone**; volatile oil; vasicinol.
- **Uses:** **Expectorant** and bronchodilator in cough, bronchitis and asthma (vasaka syrup). The synthetic expectorants **bromhexine** and **ambroxol** were developed from vasicine.

### 5.7.2 Tolu balsam

- **Synonyms:** Balsam of Tolu.
- **Biological source:** Balsam obtained by making V-shaped incisions in the trunk of *Myroxylon balsamum* (Linn.) Harms (*Toluifera balsamum*).
- **Family:** Leguminosae (Papilionaceae).
- **Geographical source:** Colombia, Venezuela, Peru.
- **Chemical constituents:** Resin esters (**benzyl benzoate**, **benzyl cinnamate**); free **cinnamic acid** and **benzoic acid**; vanillin; volatile oil.
- **Uses:** Expectorant and flavouring in cough syrups (syrup of tolu); ingredient of compound benzoin tincture; antiseptic.
- **Test:** Warmed with potassium permanganate solution → odour of **benzaldehyde** (from cinnamic acid).

## 5.8 Antirheumatic

### 5.8.1 Colchicum seed

- **Synonyms:** Meadow saffron seed, Autumn crocus seed.
- **Biological source:** Dried ripe seeds of *Colchicum autumnale* Linn. (the corm is also used).
- **Family:** Liliaceae (now Colchicaceae).
- **Geographical source:** Europe (England, Central Europe); related *Colchicum luteum* in the Himalayas.
- **Chemical constituents:** Alkaloid **colchicine** (a tropolone alkaloid, almost neutral), demecolcine; fixed oil; sugars.
- **Uses:** **Acute gout** (reduces inflammation by stopping white cell movement); familial Mediterranean fever; in plant breeding to double chromosomes (polyploidy), because it stops cell division at metaphase.
- **Test:** Colchicine gives a yellow colour with mineral acids (e.g. conc. H2SO4).

## 5.9 Antitumour

### 5.9.1 Vinca

- **Synonyms:** Sadabahar, Madagascar periwinkle, Catharanthus.
- **Biological source:** Dried whole plant (mainly leaves) of *Catharanthus roseus* (Linn.) G. Don (*Vinca rosea* Linn.).
- **Family:** Apocynaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Native to Madagascar; grows throughout India and the tropics.
- **Chemical constituents:** More than 100 indole alkaloids — dimeric **vinblastine (vincaleukoblastine)** and **vincristine (leurocristine)**; monomers ajmalicine, serpentine, vindoline, catharanthine.
- **Uses:** **Vincristine** — acute leukaemia in children; **vinblastine** — Hodgkin's disease and other lymphomas; both stop cell division (block microtubules). Roots (ajmalicine) — hypotensive.

### 5.9.2 Podophyllum

- **Synonyms:** Indian podophyllum, Papra (Indian); May apple (American).
- **Biological source:** Dried rhizomes and roots of *Podophyllum hexandrum* Royle (*P. emodi*, Indian podophyllum) and *Podophyllum peltatum* Linn. (American podophyllum).
- **Family:** Berberidaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Indian — Himalayas (Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Sikkim); American — eastern USA and Canada.
- **Chemical constituents:** A resin, **podophyllin**, containing lignans — **podophyllotoxin**, 4′-demethylpodophyllotoxin; α- and β-peltatins (in the American drug). The Indian drug gives more resin and more podophyllotoxin.
- **Uses:** Podophyllin paint for **warts** (genital warts); drastic purgative (old use); **etoposide** and **teniposide**, semi-synthetic anticancer drugs made from podophyllotoxin.
- **Note:** Over-collection has made the Himalayan plant endangered.

## 5.10 Antidiabetics

### 5.10.1 Pterocarpus

- **Synonyms:** Vijaysar, Indian kino tree, Bijasal.
- **Biological source:** Heartwood of *Pterocarpus marsupium* Roxb. (the dried gum exuding from the bark is Indian kino).
- **Family:** Leguminosae (Papilionaceae).
- **Geographical source:** India (central and southern forests, Western Ghats), Sri Lanka.
- **Chemical constituents:** **(−)-Epicatechin**, **pterostilbene**, **marsupin**, **pterosupin**, isoflavonoids; kino tannins (in the gum).
- **Uses:** **Antidiabetic** — water kept overnight in tumblers made of the heartwood is drunk in the morning; kino is an astringent in diarrhoea.

### 5.10.2 Gymnema

- **Synonyms:** Gurmar ("sugar destroyer"), Meshashringi.
- **Biological source:** Dried leaves of *Gymnema sylvestre* R. Br.
- **Family:** Asclepiadaceae (now Apocynaceae).
- **Geographical source:** India (central and southern India, Western Ghats), Sri Lanka, Africa.
- **Chemical constituents:** Triterpenoid saponins — **gymnemic acids**; the peptide **gurmarin**; flavonoids; stigmasterol.
- **Uses:** **Antidiabetic** (reduces sugar absorption from the gut and is reported to help insulin secretion); chewing the leaf temporarily **abolishes the sweet taste**; used in weight control.

## 5.11 Diuretics

### 5.11.1 Gokhru

- **Synonyms:** Small caltrops, Gokshura, Puncture vine.
- **Biological source:** Dried ripe fruits of *Tribulus terrestris* Linn.
- **Family:** Zygophyllaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Throughout India in dry, sandy areas; also Africa and Europe.
- **Chemical constituents:** Steroidal saponins (**protodioscin**, glycosides of diosgenin and tigogenin); flavonoids (kaempferol, quercetin); alkaloids (harman in small amounts); potassium nitrate; fixed oil.
- **Uses:** **Diuretic**, in urinary stones and painful urination; tonic and aphrodisiac in Ayurveda.
- **Identification:** Fruit made of five wedge-shaped cocci, each with two pairs of hard spines.
- **Note:** "Large gokhru" is *Pedalium murex* (Pedaliaceae), used as a substitute.

### 5.11.2 Punarnava

- **Synonyms:** Spreading hogweed, Gadahpurna.
- **Biological source:** Dried whole plant (especially roots) of *Boerhaavia diffusa* Linn.
- **Family:** Nyctaginaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Throughout India as a weed, especially in the rainy season.
- **Chemical constituents:** Alkaloid **punarnavine**; rotenoids (**boeravinones**); β-sitosterol, ursolic acid; potassium nitrate.
- **Uses:** **Diuretic** in oedema, ascites and kidney disorders; hepatoprotective; anti-inflammatory; rasayana (rejuvenator) in Ayurveda.
- **Adulterant:** *Trianthema portulacastrum* (Aizoaceae).

## 5.12 Antidysenteric

### 5.12.1 Ipecacuanha

- **Synonyms:** Ipecac.
- **Biological source:** Dried roots or rhizomes and roots of *Cephaelis ipecacuanha* (Brot.) A. Rich. (Brazilian or Rio ipecac) and *Cephaelis acuminata* Karsten (Cartagena or Colombian ipecac).
- **Family:** Rubiaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Brazil, Colombia, Nicaragua; cultivated in India (West Bengal — near Darjeeling).
- **Chemical constituents:** Isoquinoline alkaloids (about 2–3%) — **emetine**, **cephaeline**, psychotrine; the glycoside ipecacuanhin; starch; calcium oxalate raphides.
- **Uses:** Emetine — **amoebic dysentery**; small doses — **expectorant**; larger doses — **emetic** (ipecac syrup in some poisonings).
- **Adulterants:** Roots of *Psychotria emetica* (striated ipecac), *Richardsonia scabra* (undulated ipecac).
- **Identification:** Annulated (ring-like) roots that look like a string of beads.

## 5.13 Antiseptics and disinfectants

### 5.13.1 Benzoin

- **Synonyms:** Gum benjamin, Loban.
- **Biological source:** Balsamic resin obtained from incised stems of *Styrax benzoin* Dryander and *Styrax paralleloneurus* Perkins (**Sumatra benzoin**), or *Styrax tonkinensis* (Pierre) Craib (**Siam benzoin**).
- **Family:** Styracaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Sumatra (Indonesia); Thailand, Laos, Vietnam (Siam).
- **Chemical constituents:** Sumatra — free **benzoic acid** and **cinnamic acid**, coniferyl cinnamate, vanillin, sumaresinolic acid; Siam — mainly **coniferyl benzoate** and free benzoic acid, vanillin; little or no cinnamic acid.
- **Uses:** **Compound benzoin tincture (Friar's balsam)** as inhalation in cough and cold and as a protective on skin; antiseptic; preservative (benzoinated lard); incense.
- **Tests:** Heated in a dry test tube → white sublimate of **benzoic acid**; warmed with KMnO4 solution → odour of **benzaldehyde** (Sumatra; Siam negative).

### 5.13.2 Myrrh

- **Synonyms:** Bola, Gum myrrh.
- **Biological source:** Oleo-gum-resin obtained from the stems of *Commiphora molmol* Engler and other *Commiphora* species.
- **Family:** Burseraceae.
- **Geographical source:** Somalia, Ethiopia, Arabia (Yemen).
- **Chemical constituents:** Volatile oil (about 7–17%) with sesquiterpenes (heerabolene, cadinene) and eugenol; resin (about 25–40%, commiphoric acids); gum (about 57–61%).
- **Uses:** **Mouthwash and gargle** for mouth ulcers, gingivitis and sore throat; astringent; antiseptic; carminative; incense.

### 5.13.3 Neem

- **Synonyms:** Margosa, Nimba.
- **Biological source:** Fresh or dried leaves, stem bark and seed oil of *Azadirachta indica* A. Juss.
- **Family:** Meliaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Throughout India; Myanmar, Pakistan, Africa.
- **Chemical constituents:** Limonoids (tetranortriterpenoids) — **azadirachtin** (seeds), **nimbin**, **nimbidin**, nimbolide, salannin; flavonoids; seed oil (margosa oil) with bitter principles.
- **Uses:** Antiseptic, antibacterial and antifungal for skin diseases; in soaps and toothpastes; twigs as tooth-brush (datun); blood purifier; **natural insecticide** (azadirachtin).

### 5.13.4 Turmeric

- **Synonyms:** Haldi, Curcuma, Indian saffron.
- **Biological source:** Dried rhizomes of *Curcuma longa* Linn. (*C. domestica* Valeton), boiled or steamed and dried.
- **Family:** Zingiberaceae.
- **Geographical source:** India (Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu — Erode, Maharashtra, Odisha); India is the largest producer.
- **Chemical constituents:** Yellow **curcuminoids** (about 3–5%) — **curcumin**, demethoxycurcumin, bisdemethoxycurcumin; volatile oil with **turmerone** and ar-turmerone, zingiberene; starch.
- **Uses:** Antiseptic on wounds, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, hepatoprotective; in skin creams; colouring and spice.
- **Tests:** Powder + alkali → reddish-brown; + boric acid / borax and acid → orange-red.
- **Adulterants:** Other *Curcuma* species (*C. aromatica*, *C. zedoaria*); metanil yellow and lead chromate in powder.

## 5.14 Antimalarials

### 5.14.1 Cinchona

- **Synonyms:** Peruvian bark, Jesuit's bark, Red bark (*C. succirubra*).
- **Biological source:** Dried bark of the stem and root of cultivated trees of *Cinchona calisaya* Weddell, *Cinchona ledgeriana* Moens, *Cinchona officinalis* Linn., *Cinchona succirubra* Pavon, or their hybrids.
- **Family:** Rubiaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Native to the Andes (Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador); cultivated in Indonesia (Java), India (Nilgiris; Mungpoo near Darjeeling), Sri Lanka, Congo.
- **Chemical constituents:** Quinoline alkaloids — **quinine**, **quinidine**, **cinchonine**, **cinchonidine**; cinchotannic acid; quinic acid; cinchona red; bitter glycoside quinovin.
- **Uses:** Quinine — **antimalarial**, bitter stomachic, used in nocturnal leg cramps; quinidine — **antiarrhythmic**; quinine as bitter in tonic water.
- **Tests:** (1) **Thalleioquin test** — quinine solution + bromine water + ammonia → **emerald green**. (2) Quinine in dilute H2SO4 shows **blue fluorescence**. (3) **Grahe's test** — bark heated in a dry test tube gives red (crimson) tarry vapours.
- **Adulterants:** Barks of *Remijia* species (cuprea bark).

### 5.14.2 Artemisia

- **Synonyms:** Sweet wormwood, Qinghao, Annual wormwood.
- **Biological source:** Dried leaves and flowering tops of *Artemisia annua* Linn.
- **Family:** Compositae (Asteraceae).
- **Geographical source:** China, Vietnam; cultivated in India (Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand), Africa.
- **Chemical constituents:** **Artemisinin (qinghaosu)** — a sesquiterpene lactone with an **endoperoxide bridge** essential for activity; artemisinic acid; flavonoids; volatile oil.
- **Uses:** Artemisinin and its semi-synthetic derivatives **artesunate, artemether and arteether** are fast-acting antimalarials effective against chloroquine-resistant *Plasmodium falciparum*; given as artemisinin-based combination therapy (ACT).
- **Related drug:** *Artemisia maritima* gives santonin (an old anthelmintic).

## 5.15 Oxytocic

### 5.15.1 Ergot

- **Synonyms:** Rye ergot, Ergot of rye.
- **Biological source:** Dried **sclerotium** of the fungus *Claviceps purpurea* Tulasne, developed in the ovary of the rye plant *Secale cereale* Linn. (Gramineae).
- **Family (fungus):** Clavicipitaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Spain, Portugal, Central Europe; now also grown by artificial infection of rye and by fermentation.
- **Chemical constituents:** Indole alkaloids (ergoline derivatives) — **ergometrine (ergonovine)**, water-soluble; **ergotamine**; ergotoxine group (ergocristine, ergocornine, ergocryptine). Also histamine, tyramine and acetylcholine.
- **Uses:** **Ergometrine** — contracts the uterus; used to control bleeding after childbirth (postpartum haemorrhage); **ergotamine** — **migraine**; bromocriptine (semi-synthetic) — hyperprolactinaemia and Parkinson's disease.
- **Test:** **Van Urk's reagent** (p-dimethylaminobenzaldehyde) → blue colour.
- **Note:** Eating ergot-contaminated bread causes **ergotism**.

## 5.16 Vitamins

### 5.16.1 Cod liver oil

- **Synonyms:** Oleum morrhuae.
- **Biological source:** Fixed oil obtained from the fresh livers of the cod *Gadus morhua* Linn. and other *Gadus* species.
- **Family:** Gadidae.
- **Geographical source:** Norway, Iceland, Newfoundland, Scotland.
- **Chemical constituents:** **Vitamin A** and **vitamin D**; glycerides of unsaturated fatty acids including **EPA and DHA** (omega-3); cholesterol.
- **Uses:** Source of vitamins A and D in deficiency (night blindness, rickets); nutritive tonic; omega-3 supplement.
- **Test:** Oil in chloroform + conc. H2SO4 → transient violet/purple colour (vitamin A).
- **Adulterants:** Other fish and marine oils.

### 5.16.2 Shark liver oil

- **Biological source:** Fixed oil obtained from the livers of sharks such as *Hypoprion brevirostris* and *Galeocerdo tigrinus* (tiger shark) and other species.
- **Family:** Carcharhinidae.
- **Geographical source:** Indian coasts (Kerala, Gujarat, Maharashtra), worldwide seas.
- **Chemical constituents:** **Vitamin A** (much richer than cod liver oil) and vitamin D; **squalene** (a triterpene hydrocarbon); alkoxyglycerols; unsaturated fatty acids.
- **Uses:** Source of vitamin A; nutritional supplement; squalene in cosmetics.

## 5.17 Enzymes

**Enzymes** are biological catalysts made of protein. They are destroyed by heat and are sensitive to pH.

### 5.17.1 Papaya (papain)

- **Synonyms:** Papaw, Papita.
- **Biological source:** Dried and purified latex obtained by incising the green unripe fruits of *Carica papaya* Linn.
- **Family:** Caricaceae.
- **Geographical source:** India, Sri Lanka, Tanzania, Uganda, Zaire.
- **Chemical constituents:** Proteolytic enzymes **papain** and **chymopapain**; lysozyme; lipase.
- **Uses:** **Digestive aid** for proteins; meat tenderiser; cleaning wounds (debriding); removing protein deposits from contact lenses; clarifying beer; chymopapain injected into slipped discs (chemonucleolysis).

### 5.17.2 Diastase

- **Synonyms:** Amylase, Malt diastase.
- **Biological source:** Enzyme preparation obtained from **malt**, the dried germinated grain of barley, *Hordeum vulgare* Linn. (Gramineae). Fungal diastase (Taka-diastase) is obtained from *Aspergillus oryzae*.
- **Chemical constituents:** Amylolytic enzymes (α- and β-amylase).
- **Action:** Converts starch into dextrins and maltose.
- **Uses:** Digestive aid in starch dyspepsia; in brewing and baking; textile desizing.

### 5.17.3 Pancreatin

- **Biological source:** Enzyme preparation from the fresh pancreas of the hog *Sus scrofa* Linn. (Suidae) or the ox *Bos taurus* Linn. (Bovidae).
- **Chemical constituents:** **Amylase** (starch-digesting), **trypsin** and chymotrypsin (protein-digesting), **lipase** (fat-digesting).
- **Uses:** Replacement therapy in **pancreatic insufficiency** (chronic pancreatitis, cystic fibrosis); digestive aid. Given as enteric-coated tablets because stomach acid destroys it.

### 5.17.4 Yeast

- **Synonyms:** Brewer's yeast, Baker's yeast.
- **Biological source:** Fresh or dried cells of the unicellular fungus *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* Meyen.
- **Family:** Saccharomycetaceae.
- **Chemical constituents:** **Vitamin B complex** (thiamine, riboflavin, niacin, pyridoxine); proteins; **ergosterol** (provitamin D2); enzymes **zymase** and **invertase**; glutathione.
- **Uses:** Source of B vitamins and protein; nutritional supplement; fermentation in bread, alcohol and vinegar production; source of ergosterol.

## 5.18 Pharmaceutical aids

These have little or no therapeutic action but are used to **make** dosage forms (excipients).

### 5.18.1 Kaolin

- **Synonyms:** China clay, Light kaolin.
- **Source:** Native **hydrated aluminium silicate** (approximately Al2O3·2SiO2·2H2O), purified by washing (levigation) to remove sand and grit. A mineral drug.
- **Geographical source:** India (Kerala, Rajasthan, Gujarat), China, England (Cornwall).
- **Uses:** Adsorbent in diarrhoea (kaolin mixture); poultice; dusting powder; tablet and capsule diluent; clarifying agent; cosmetics (face packs).

### 5.18.2 Lanolin

- **Synonyms:** Wool fat (anhydrous lanolin); hydrous wool fat (lanolin).
- **Biological source:** Purified fat-like secretion obtained from the wool of the sheep *Ovis aries* Linn. (Bovidae). Hydrous lanolin contains about 25–30% water.
- **Geographical source:** Australia, New Zealand, England.
- **Chemical constituents:** A **wax** — esters of high-molecular-weight alcohols (**cholesterol**, lanosterol, isocholesterol) with fatty acids; free alcohols. It contains no glycerides.
- **Uses:** **Water-absorbing ointment base** (it can take up water to form a water-in-oil emulsion); emollient in creams and lotions; hair products.

### 5.18.3 Beeswax

- **Synonyms:** Yellow beeswax (Cera flava); white beeswax (Cera alba, bleached).
- **Biological source:** Purified wax obtained from the honeycomb of the honey bee *Apis mellifera* Linn. and other *Apis* species.
- **Family:** Apidae.
- **Geographical source:** India, Africa, the West Indies, the USA.
- **Chemical constituents:** **Myricin** (mainly **myricyl palmitate**, about 70–80%); free **cerotic acid**; hydrocarbons; colouring matter.
- **Uses:** Stiffening agent in ointments, creams and cerates; cold cream; lipsticks; polishes; candles.
- **Adulterants:** **Paraffin wax**, ceresin, Japan wax, carnauba wax, stearic acid, tallow, rosin.

### 5.18.4 Acacia

- **Synonyms:** Gum arabic, Babool gum (Indian substitute).
- **Biological source:** Dried gummy exudation from the stems and branches of *Acacia senegal* (Linn.) Willd. and other African *Acacia* species.
- **Family:** Leguminosae (Mimosoideae).
- **Geographical source:** Sudan (Kordofan), Senegal, Nigeria; India (Rajasthan, Gujarat — mainly *A. arabica*).
- **Chemical constituents:** **Arabin** — calcium, magnesium and potassium salts of **arabic acid** (a polysaccharide that gives arabinose, galactose, rhamnose and glucuronic acid on hydrolysis); **oxidase and peroxidase** enzymes.
- **Uses:** **Emulsifying agent**, suspending agent, tablet **binder**, demulcent, base for pastilles.
- **Tests:** Aqueous solution + lead subacetate → white precipitate; + tincture of guaiacum and hydrogen peroxide → blue (oxidase); no blue with iodine (no starch).
- **Adulterants:** Ghatti gum (*Anogeissus latifolia*), other acacia gums.

### 5.18.5 Tragacanth

- **Synonyms:** Gum tragacanth.
- **Biological source:** Dried gummy exudation obtained by incising the stems of *Astragalus gummifer* Labillardiere and other Asiatic *Astragalus* species.
- **Family:** Leguminosae (Papilionaceae).
- **Geographical source:** Iran (Persian tragacanth), Turkey, Syria.
- **Chemical constituents:** **Tragacanthin** (water-soluble, about 30–40%) and **bassorin** (water-insoluble, swells, about 60–70%); traces of starch.
- **Uses:** **Suspending agent** and thickener; emulsifier; binder in tablets and lozenges; in lotions and creams.
- **Tests:** Swells in water to a thick gel; + iodine → a few blue particles (traces of starch); ruthenium red stains it pink.
- **Adulterant:** Karaya gum (*Sterculia urens*, "Indian tragacanth").

### 5.18.6 Sodium alginate

- **Source:** Sodium salt of **alginic acid**, extracted with alkali from **brown seaweeds** (Phaeophyceae) such as *Macrocystis pyrifera*, *Laminaria* species and *Ascophyllum nodosum*.
- **Geographical source:** Coasts of the USA, UK, Norway, Japan; India (Tamil Nadu and Gujarat coasts).
- **Chemical nature:** Polysaccharide made of **D-mannuronic acid** and **L-guluronic acid**.
- **Uses:** Thickening and suspending agent; emulsion stabiliser; tablet binder and disintegrant; **calcium alginate** dressings (haemostatic); dental impression material; antacid preparations that form a raft over stomach contents.
- **Tests:** Solution + calcium chloride → gelatinous precipitate (calcium alginate); + dilute H2SO4 → precipitate of alginic acid.

### 5.18.7 Agar

- **Synonyms:** Agar-agar, Japanese isinglass.
- **Biological source:** Dried gelatinous substance extracted with hot water from **red algae** (Rhodophyceae) such as *Gelidium amansii* Lamouroux and *Gracilaria* species.
- **Family:** Gelidiaceae, Gracilariaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Japan, Spain, Portugal, USA; India (Tamil Nadu coast, from *Gracilaria*).
- **Chemical constituents:** Two polysaccharides — **agarose** (gelling part) and **agaropectin** (sulphated part); calcium and magnesium salts.
- **Uses:** **Bacterial culture media** (solid medium); bulk laxative; gelling agent in emulsions and jellies; dental impressions; suspending agent.
- **Tests:** 1–1.5% solution boiled and cooled forms a firm gel; + iodine → crimson to brownish-purple; ruthenium red → pink; no precipitate with tannic acid (gelatin gives one).

### 5.18.8 Guar gum

- **Synonyms:** Guaran.
- **Biological source:** Gum obtained from the ground **endosperm** of the seeds of *Cyamopsis tetragonolobus* (Linn.) Taubert.
- **Family:** Leguminosae.
- **Geographical source:** India (Rajasthan, Gujarat, Haryana) — the world's largest producer; Pakistan, USA.
- **Chemical constituents:** **Galactomannan** (guaran) — a chain of mannose units with galactose side units (mannose : galactose about 2 : 1).
- **Uses:** Thickener, binder and disintegrant in tablets; bulk laxative; lowers cholesterol and slows sugar absorption; food, paper, textile and oil-drilling industries.
- **Tests:** Disperses in cold water forming a very viscous solution; ruthenium red → pink; no blue with iodine.

### 5.18.9 Gelatin

- **Biological source:** Protein obtained by partial hydrolysis of **collagen** from the skin, white connective tissue and bones of animals such as the ox (*Bos taurus*, Bovidae) and the pig (*Sus scrofa*, Suidae).
- **Types:** **Type A** — acid-treated (mainly pig skin); **Type B** — alkali-treated (mainly bones and hides).
- **Chemical constituents:** Protein rich in **glycine, proline and hydroxyproline**; lacks tryptophan (so it is an incomplete protein).
- **Uses:** **Hard and soft capsule shells**; glycerogelatin suppositories and pastes; haemostatic sponge; tablet binder; plasma substitute (modified gelatin); culture media; food.
- **Tests:** Solution sets to a gel on cooling; + **tannic acid** → buff precipitate; + picric acid → yellow precipitate; heated with soda lime → ammonia given off.

## 5.19 Miscellaneous

### 5.19.1 Squill

- **Synonyms:** Jangli pyaz (Indian squill), Sea onion.
- **Biological source:** Dried, sliced bulbs of the white variety of *Urginea maritima* (Linn.) Baker (European or Mediterranean squill) or *Urginea indica* Kunth (Indian squill), with the outer dry scales removed.
- **Family:** Liliaceae.
- **Geographical source:** Mediterranean countries; Indian squill throughout India.
- **Chemical constituents:** Bufadienolide cardiac glycosides — **scillaren A** (main), scillaren B, glucoscillaren A; mucilage; calcium oxalate raphides. Red squill contains **scilliroside**.
- **Uses:** **Expectorant** (syrup of squill); cardiotonic (rarely); red squill as **rat poison** (rodenticide).

### 5.19.2 Galls

- **Synonyms:** Nutgalls, Majuphal, Aleppo galls.
- **Biological source:** Pathological outgrowths on the young twigs of the oak *Quercus infectoria* Olivier, formed after the gall wasp *Adleria gallae-tinctoriae* lays its eggs in the plant tissue.
- **Family:** Fagaceae (plant); Cynipidae (insect).
- **Geographical source:** Turkey, Syria, Iran (Aleppo).
- **Chemical constituents:** **Tannic acid (gallotannic acid)** about 50–70%; gallic acid; ellagic acid; starch.
- **Uses:** Commercial source of **tannic acid**; astringent (gall ointment for piles); in ink manufacture; tanning and dyeing.

### 5.19.3 Ashwagandha

- **Synonyms:** Asgandh, Winter cherry, Indian ginseng.
- **Biological source:** Dried roots of *Withania somnifera* (Linn.) Dunal.
- **Family:** Solanaceae.
- **Geographical source:** India (Madhya Pradesh — Mandsaur and Neemuch, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Punjab).
- **Chemical constituents:** Steroidal lactones — **withanolides** (withaferin A and others); alkaloids (withanine, somniferine, tropine, pseudotropine); starch.
- **Uses:** **Adaptogen** and rasayana (rejuvenating tonic); anti-stress; general debility; sedative; aphrodisiac; anti-inflammatory in arthritis.

### 5.19.4 Tulsi

- **Synonyms:** Holy basil.
- **Biological source:** Fresh or dried leaves (and the whole plant) of *Ocimum sanctum* Linn. (*Ocimum tenuiflorum*).
- **Family:** Labiatae (Lamiaceae).
- **Geographical source:** Throughout India, grown in homes and temples.
- **Chemical constituents:** Volatile oil with **eugenol** (main), methyl eugenol, carvacrol, β-caryophyllene; **ursolic acid**; flavonoids (orientin, vicenin).
- **Uses:** Cough, cold and fever; expectorant; antimicrobial; **adaptogen** and immunomodulator; antioxidant; mosquito repellent.

### 5.19.5 Guggul

- **Synonyms:** Indian bdellium, Guggulu.
- **Biological source:** Oleo-gum-resin obtained by incising the bark of *Commiphora wightii* (Arn.) Bhandari (*Commiphora mukul*).
- **Family:** Burseraceae.
- **Geographical source:** India (Rajasthan, Gujarat, Karnataka), Pakistan, Bangladesh.
- **Chemical constituents:** Steroids **guggulsterones Z and E** (main actives); volatile oil; gum; resin.
- **Uses:** **Hypolipidaemic** (lowers cholesterol and triglycerides); anti-inflammatory in arthritis; obesity; Ayurvedic preparations such as Yogaraj guggulu. Purified guggul (shodhit guggul) is used.

## Remember

- Monograph order: **synonyms → biological source → family → geographical source → constituents → uses → adulterants → test**.
- Families most asked: Solanaceae (belladonna, hyoscyamus, ashwagandha), Rubiaceae (cinchona, ipecac, coffee, pale catechu), Apocynaceae (rauwolfia, vinca), Umbelliferae (coriander, fennel, asafoetida), Zingiberaceae (ginger, cardamom, turmeric), Leguminosae (senna, catechu, acacia, tragacanth, guar, tolu, pterocarpus).
- Classic tests: **Borntrager's** (senna, aloe), **Keller–Killiani** (digitalis), **Vitali–Morin** (belladonna), **murexide** (caffeine), **thalleioquin** (quinine), **van Urk's** (ergot), **meconic acid** with FeCl3 (opium), **gambir-fluorescein** (pale catechu).
- Classic adulterants: papaya seeds (black pepper), cassia (cinnamon), paraffin (beeswax), *Cassia auriculata* (senna), metanil yellow (turmeric).

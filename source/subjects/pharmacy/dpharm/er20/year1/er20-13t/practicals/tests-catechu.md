# Physical and chemical tests: Pale catechu and Black catechu

**Aim:** To identify pale catechu (gambir) and black catechu (kattha) by physical and chemical tests and to distinguish between them.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ.

## Principle

Both catechus are dried aqueous extracts rich in **condensed tannins and catechins**. They therefore give tannin tests (greenish-black with ferric chloride, precipitate with gelatin) and the **matchstick test**: catechins heated with HCl give phloroglucinol-type products that colour the lignin of the matchstick wood pink to red.

Pale catechu also contains **gambir-fluorescein**, which gives a **green fluorescence** in petroleum ether after treatment with alkali. Black catechu does not.

## Requirements

Samples of pale catechu and black catechu; test tubes; beakers; filter paper; matchsticks (wooden, unburnt end); spirit lamp; separating funnel or test tube; reagents — 5% ferric chloride, 1% gelatin solution with 10% sodium chloride, conc. HCl, alcohol, sodium hydroxide solution, petroleum ether, vanillin in HCl (vanillin–HCl reagent), lead acetate solution.

## Procedure

Prepare an extract of each sample: boil 1 g with 20 ml water, cool and filter (aqueous extract). Also prepare an alcoholic extract by warming 0.5 g with 10 ml alcohol and filtering.

## Observations

| No. | Test | Pale catechu | Black catechu | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Appearance | Pale brown to dull reddish-brown **cubes**, porous, friable | **Dark brown to black** irregular pieces, glossy fracture | Two types of catechu |
| 2 | Odour and taste | Odourless; bitter, astringent, then sweetish | Odourless; astringent, then sweetish | Tannin drugs |
| 3 | Aqueous extract + 5% FeCl3 | Greenish-black | Greenish-black | **Condensed tannins** |
| 4 | Aqueous extract + gelatin–NaCl solution | White/buff precipitate | White/buff precipitate | Tannins present (protein precipitated) |
| 5 | Aqueous extract + lead acetate | Precipitate | Precipitate | Tannins / phenolics |
| 6 | **Matchstick test**: dip the wooden end of a matchstick in the extract, dry it, moisten with conc. HCl, warm near (not in) the flame | Pink to red | Pink to red | **Catechins** present |
| 7 | Vanillin–HCl reagent on the extract | Pink to red | Pink to red | Catechins (flavan-3-ols) |
| 8 | **Gambir-fluorescein test**: alcoholic extract + a few drops NaOH, add petroleum ether, shake, allow layers to separate | **Green fluorescence** in the petroleum ether layer | No green fluorescence | Distinguishes **pale catechu** |

**Comparison**

| Feature | Pale catechu | Black catechu |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source | Extract of leaves and young shoots of *Uncaria gambier* | Extract of heartwood of *Acacia catechu* |
| Family | Rubiaceae | Leguminosae |
| Form | Pale brown cubes | Dark brown to black masses |
| Gambir-fluorescein | Positive | Negative |

## Result

Both samples gave positive tests for condensed tannins and catechins. The sample giving a green fluorescence in the gambir-fluorescein test was identified as **pale catechu**; the dark sample that did not was identified as **black catechu**.

## Precautions

1. Use the unburnt wooden end of the matchstick and warm it gently; do not let it catch fire.
2. Use freshly prepared ferric chloride and gelatin solutions.
3. Let the petroleum ether layer separate fully before observing fluorescence; keep petroleum ether away from flames.
4. Filter extracts so that turbidity is not mistaken for a precipitate.

## Viva questions

1. **Give the biological source of pale catechu.** Dried aqueous extract of the leaves and young shoots of *Uncaria gambier*; Rubiaceae.
2. **Give the biological source of black catechu.** Dried aqueous extract of the heartwood of *Acacia catechu*; Leguminosae.
3. **What does the matchstick test show?** The presence of catechins; the matchstick wood turns pink-red after HCl and warming.
4. **Why does catechu give a greenish-black colour with FeCl3?** It contains condensed (catechol-type) tannins.
5. **Which test distinguishes pale from black catechu?** Gambir-fluorescein test — green fluorescence for pale catechu only.
6. **Give two uses of catechu.** Astringent in diarrhoea; gargles for sore throat and bleeding gums (also tanning and dyeing).
7. **What is the gelatin test?** Tannins precipitate gelatin (protein) from its solution.
8. **To which class of tannins do catechus belong?** Condensed tannins (phlobatannins).

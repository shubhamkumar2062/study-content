# Morphology of leaves, flower buds and herbs: Senna, Clove, Ephedra, Punarnava

**Aim:** To identify senna, clove, ephedra and punarnava by their morphological characters.

> Use museum specimens and compare with authentic samples. Follow your lab manual where it differs.

## Principle

Leaves, flower buds, stems and whole herbs have characteristic shape, size, apex, base, margin, venation, surface, colour, odour and taste. Recording these identifies the drug and helps detect substitutes (for example *Cassia auriculata* in senna, or stalks and mother cloves in clove).

## Requirements

Specimens of senna leaflets, clove, ephedra stems and punarnava (whole plant), hand lens, scale, forceps, white paper, beaker of water, blade.

## Procedure

1. Spread each specimen on white paper and note the general form and colour.
2. For senna, examine the shape, apex, base, margin and venation of five leaflets and measure them.
3. For clove, measure the length, identify the hypanthium, sepals and the head of petals; press the hypanthium with a fingernail to see oil come out; drop a few cloves into water.
4. For ephedra, observe stem thickness, ridges, nodes and scale leaves.
5. For punarnava, observe root, stem, nodes, leaves and any flowers or fruits.
6. Note odour and taste (tiny amount; spit out).
7. Record observations and draw labelled sketches.

## Observations

| Feature | Senna (leaflet) | Clove (flower bud) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shape | Lanceolate | Nail-shaped: cylindrical hypanthium with a rounded head |
| Size | About 2.5–5 cm long, 0.7–1.5 cm wide (Indian senna) | About 10–17.5 mm long |
| Colour | Pale yellowish-green | Dark brown to crimson-brown |
| Apex / head | Acute, with a small sharp point (mucronate) | Four thick sepals around a dome of four unopened petals |
| Base | **Asymmetric** (unequal) | Hypanthium tapering below |
| Margin / surface | Entire margin; pinnate venation; fine hairs | Hypanthium wrinkled; oil exudes when pressed with a nail |
| Odour | Faint, characteristic | Strongly aromatic |
| Taste | Mucilaginous, then slightly bitter | Pungent, aromatic, numbing |
| Special | — | Good cloves sink or float upright in water; exhausted cloves float flat |

| Feature | Ephedra (stem) | Punarnava (whole plant) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form | Slender, cylindrical, jointed green stems | Diffuse, spreading herb with a thick root |
| Size | About 1–2 mm thick; internodes a few centimetres long | Root several cm long, up to about 1–2 cm thick |
| Colour | Green to yellowish-green | Root yellowish-brown; stem often purplish |
| Surface | Fine longitudinal ridges and furrows | Stem with swollen nodes; leaves opposite, unequal, ovate, green above and whitish below |
| Nodes | Two small scale leaves at each node, joined at the base into a sheath | Small pink flowers in clusters; small ribbed sticky fruits |
| Odour | Slight | Not distinct |
| Taste | Astringent, slightly bitter | Slightly bitter |

## Result

The given drugs were identified as senna, clove, ephedra and punarnava from their morphological characters.

## Precautions

1. Handle dry leaflets gently; they break easily.
2. Examine several specimens and record ranges.
3. Taste only a tiny amount of safe drugs and spit out; do not taste ephedra in quantity.
4. Return specimens to their labelled containers.

## Viva questions

1. **Give the biological source of senna.** Dried leaflets of *Cassia angustifolia* (Indian) and *Cassia acutifolia* (Alexandrian); Leguminosae.
2. **Which feature of senna leaflet is most diagnostic?** The asymmetric base.
3. **Name an adulterant of senna and a test for it.** *Cassia auriculata* (Palthe senna); it gives a red colour with 80% sulphuric acid.
4. **What part of clove is used?** The dried flower bud.
5. **What are mother cloves?** The ripe fruits of the clove plant, used as an adulterant.
6. **Why do exhausted cloves float flat in water?** Their oil has been removed, so they are lighter.
7. **Name the chief alkaloid of ephedra and its use.** Ephedrine; bronchodilator in asthma and nasal decongestant.
8. **Give the botanical name and family of punarnava.** *Boerhaavia diffusa*, Nyctaginaceae; used as a diuretic.

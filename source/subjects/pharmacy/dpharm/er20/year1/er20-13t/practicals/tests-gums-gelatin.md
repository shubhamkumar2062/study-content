# Physical and chemical tests: Acacia, Tragacanth, Agar, Guar gum and Gelatine

**Aim:** To identify acacia, tragacanth, agar, guar gum and gelatine by physical and chemical tests.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles and a lab coat.

## Principle

Acacia, tragacanth, agar and guar gum are **polysaccharides** (gums and mucilages); gelatine is a **protein**. They differ in how they behave in water (dissolve, swell or gel) and in their reactions:

- Carbohydrates give a violet ring with **Molisch's reagent**.
- Mucilages stain pink with **ruthenium red**.
- **Iodine** turns starch blue and gives a crimson colour with agar.
- Acacia contains **oxidase**, which turns guaiacum blue in the presence of hydrogen peroxide.
- Proteins (gelatine) are precipitated by **tannic acid** and **picric acid** and give a violet **biuret** reaction.

## Requirements

Powdered samples; test tubes, beakers, glass rods, water bath, spirit lamp; reagents — Molisch's reagent (α-naphthol in alcohol), conc. H2SO4, ruthenium red solution, N/50 iodine solution, lead subacetate solution, tincture of guaiacum, dilute hydrogen peroxide, 5% tannic acid, saturated picric acid, 10% NaOH, dilute copper sulphate, soda lime.

## Procedure and observations

**A. Physical behaviour**

| Test | Acacia | Tragacanth | Agar | Guar gum | Gelatine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Appearance | White to yellowish tears or powder | White to pale yellow ribbons/flakes or powder | Yellowish-white strips or powder | Off-white powder | Colourless to pale yellow sheets or granules |
| 1 g + 10 ml cold water, shake | Dissolves slowly to a clear, sticky solution | Swells to a thick, gel-like mass | Swells, does not dissolve | Disperses to a very **viscous** solution | Swells, absorbs water |
| 1 g in 100 ml boiling water, then cool | — | — | Dissolves on boiling; **sets to a firm gel** on cooling | Viscous | Dissolves on warming; **sets to a gel** on cooling |

**B. Chemical tests**

| No. | Test | Acacia | Tragacanth | Agar | Guar gum | Gelatine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Molisch's test: solution + 2 drops Molisch's reagent, conc. H2SO4 poured down the side | Violet ring | Violet ring | Violet ring | Violet ring | No violet ring |
| 2 | Powder + ruthenium red on a slide | — | Pink (mucilage) | Pink | Pink | — |
| 3 | Powder or solution + N/50 iodine | No blue (no starch) | A few blue particles may appear (traces of starch) | **Crimson to brownish-purple** | No blue | — |
| 4 | Solution + lead subacetate | **White precipitate** | — | — | — | — |
| 5 | Solution + tincture of guaiacum + few drops H2O2 | **Blue colour** (oxidase) | No blue | No blue | No blue | — |
| 6 | Solution + 5% tannic acid | — | — | **No precipitate** | — | **Buff precipitate** |
| 7 | Solution + picric acid | — | — | — | — | **Yellow precipitate** |
| 8 | Biuret: solution + NaOH + dilute CuSO4 | — | — | — | — | **Violet colour** (protein) |
| 9 | Solid heated with soda lime | — | — | — | — | Smell of **ammonia** (nitrogen present) |

(— means the test is not used for identification of that sample.)

## Result

The samples were identified as:

- **Acacia** — dissolves in water; white precipitate with lead subacetate; blue with guaiacum + H2O2 (oxidase); no starch.
- **Tragacanth** — swells to a gel in cold water; pink with ruthenium red; traces of starch.
- **Agar** — gels on cooling after boiling; crimson with iodine; no precipitate with tannic acid.
- **Guar gum** — very viscous dispersion in cold water; Molisch-positive; pink with ruthenium red.
- **Gelatine** — gels on cooling; precipitates with tannic and picric acids; biuret-positive; ammonia with soda lime.

## Precautions

1. Add conc. H2SO4 slowly along the side of the tube; do not shake.
2. Use freshly prepared hydrogen peroxide and guaiacum tincture.
3. Dissolve agar and gelatine completely before cooling.
4. Avoid lumps: sprinkle gums slowly into water with stirring.
5. Handle soda lime and acids carefully.

## Viva questions

1. **Give the biological source of acacia.** Dried gummy exudate from stems and branches of *Acacia senegal*; Leguminosae.
2. **Which enzyme in acacia gives the guaiacum test?** Oxidase (with peroxidase).
3. **Why must acacia be heated before use in some emulsions with easily oxidised drugs?** To destroy its oxidase enzyme.
4. **Name the two parts of tragacanth.** Tragacanthin (soluble) and bassorin (swells).
5. **How is agar told from gelatine?** Agar gives no precipitate with tannic acid and is Molisch-positive; gelatine gives a buff precipitate and is biuret-positive.
6. **Give the chemical nature of guar gum.** Galactomannan.
7. **Name the two types of gelatine.** Type A (acid-treated) and Type B (alkali-treated).
8. **Which reagent stains mucilage pink?** Ruthenium red.

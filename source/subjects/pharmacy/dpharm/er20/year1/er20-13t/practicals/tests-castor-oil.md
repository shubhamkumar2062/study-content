# Physical and chemical tests: Castor oil

**Aim:** To identify castor oil by physical and chemical tests and to determine its acid value.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles; keep solvents away from flames.

## Principle

Castor oil is the fixed oil expressed from the seeds of *Ricinus communis* (Euphorbiaceae). It consists mainly of triglycerides of **ricinoleic acid**, a hydroxy fatty acid. The hydroxyl group makes castor oil unusual among fixed oils:

- It is **very viscous**.
- It is **miscible with alcohol** (most fixed oils are not) and only **slightly soluble in light petroleum** (most fixed oils dissolve freely).

Like all fixed oils, it leaves a permanent grease spot, is saponified by alkali, and stains red with Sudan III.

**Acid value** is the number of mg of KOH needed to neutralise the free fatty acids in 1 g of oil. A high value shows rancidity or poor quality.

## Requirements

Castor oil, another fixed oil for comparison (e.g. groundnut oil), filter paper, test tubes, measuring cylinders, Sudan III solution, alcohol (95%), light petroleum (petroleum ether), alcoholic KOH, water bath; for acid value — ethanol–ether (1 : 1) neutralised to phenolphthalein, 0.1 N KOH, phenolphthalein, conical flask, burette, balance.

## Procedure and observations

**A. Identification tests**

| No. | Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Appearance and odour | Pale yellow or nearly colourless, thick, viscous liquid; slight odour; taste bland then slightly acrid | Castor oil characters |
| 2 | Viscosity: tilt a test tube of castor oil and of groundnut oil side by side | Castor oil flows much more slowly | High viscosity |
| 3 | Spot test: a drop on filter paper, leave for some time | Permanent translucent spot | Fixed oil |
| 4 | Oil + Sudan III, shake | Oil layer stained red | Fixed oil |
| 5 | Mix 1 ml oil with 1 ml alcohol (95%) | Clear, uniform solution | Castor oil (miscible with alcohol) |
| 6 | Mix 1 ml oil with 5 ml light petroleum | Turbid; oil not fully dissolved | Castor oil (slightly soluble in light petroleum) |
| 7 | Comparison: repeat tests 5 and 6 with groundnut oil | Turbid in alcohol, clear in light petroleum | Ordinary fixed oil behaves the opposite way |
| 8 | Saponification: warm 1 ml oil with 5 ml alcoholic KOH on a water bath; add water | Clear soapy solution; froths on shaking | Ester (glyceride) saponified |

**B. Acid value**

1. Weigh about 5 g of oil (W) accurately into a conical flask.
2. Add 50 ml of the neutralised ethanol–ether mixture; shake to dissolve.
3. Add a few drops of phenolphthalein; titrate with 0.1 N KOH until a faint pink colour persists for 30 seconds.
4. Note the volume used (V ml); repeat to get concordant readings.

**Formula**

Acid value = (56.1 × N × V) ÷ W

where N = normality of KOH, V = ml of KOH, W = weight of oil in g, 56.1 = molecular weight of KOH.

**Worked example (specimen readings)**

| Trial | Weight of oil (g) | Burette initial (ml) | Burette final (ml) | Volume used (ml) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 5.02 | 0.0 | 1.2 | 1.2 |
| 2 | 5.00 | 1.2 | 2.4 | 1.2 |

Acid value = (56.1 × 0.1 × 1.2) ÷ 5.0 = 6.73 ÷ 5.0 ≈ **1.35 mg KOH/g**

Compare your result with the pharmacopoeial limit given in your lab manual (a low value indicates good-quality, fresh oil).

## Result

The sample was identified as **castor oil** (viscous, miscible with alcohol, slightly soluble in light petroleum, permanent spot, saponifiable). Its acid value was found to be ___ mg KOH/g.

## Precautions

1. Neutralise the ethanol–ether before use so only free acids of the oil consume alkali.
2. Keep ether and light petroleum away from flames; warm only on a water bath.
3. Use clean, dry glassware for the solubility tests (water causes turbidity).
4. Titrate slowly near the end point and shake well.

## Viva questions

1. **Give the biological source of castor oil.** Fixed oil obtained by cold expression of the seeds of *Ricinus communis*; Euphorbiaceae.
2. **What is the chief fatty acid of castor oil?** Ricinoleic acid (a hydroxy fatty acid), about 80–90%.
3. **Why is castor oil soluble in alcohol?** The hydroxyl group of ricinoleic acid makes the oil more polar.
4. **How does castor oil act as a purgative?** Intestinal lipase releases ricinoleic acid, which irritates the intestine and increases movement.
5. **Why is castor oil cold-pressed?** To keep the toxic protein ricin (and ricinine) in the press-cake and out of the oil; heat also lowers quality.
6. **Define acid value.** Mg of KOH required to neutralise the free fatty acids in 1 g of fat or oil.
7. **Why does acid value rise on storage?** Moisture or lipase hydrolyses glycerides to free fatty acids (rancidity).
8. **Give two non-medicinal uses of castor oil.** Lubricant and plasticiser (also in soaps, paints and hair oils).

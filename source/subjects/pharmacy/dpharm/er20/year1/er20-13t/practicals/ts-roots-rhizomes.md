# Transverse section of roots, stolons and rhizomes: Ashwagandha, Liquorice and Curcuma

**Aim:** To prepare and study the transverse sections of ashwagandha root, liquorice (root/stolon) and curcuma (turmeric) rhizome, and to note their diagnostic characters.

> Follow your lab manual for the staining schedule. Handle razor blades with care.

## Principle

Dicot roots and stolons with secondary growth (ashwagandha, liquorice) show cork, cortex or phelloderm, secondary phloem, cambium, secondary xylem and medullary rays. A monocot rhizome (curcuma) shows no cambium; its vascular bundles are scattered in a cortex and in a stele separated by an endodermis. Inclusions such as starch, crystal sheaths, oil cells and colouring matter help identify each drug.

## Requirements

Soaked pieces of ashwagandha root, liquorice and turmeric rhizome, potato block, razor blade, chloral hydrate, phloroglucinol (1% alcoholic), conc. HCl, iodine solution, dilute glycerine, slides, cover slips, microscope.

## Procedure

1. Soften the drugs by soaking (or brief boiling).
2. Cut thin transverse sections; keep them in water.
3. Mount one section of each in iodine to observe starch.
4. Clear another in chloral hydrate, stain with phloroglucinol + conc. HCl, mount in dilute glycerine.
5. Observe and draw labelled diagrams.

## Observations

| Tissue | Ashwagandha root (*Withania somnifera*) | Liquorice (*Glycyrrhiza glabra*) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cork | Thin layer of brownish cork cells | Several layers of **reddish-brown** cork (absent in peeled liquorice) |
| Cortex / phelloderm | Parenchyma **packed with starch grains**; some cells with microsphenoidal (sandy) crystals of calcium oxalate | Few layers of parenchyma with starch and some prismatic crystals |
| Secondary phloem | Narrow, with phloem parenchyma and starch | Wedge-shaped strands of phloem with **groups of lignified phloem fibres surrounded by a sheath of prismatic calcium oxalate crystals** (crystal sheath); wavy medullary rays |
| Cambium | Thin ring | Distinct ring |
| Secondary xylem | Vessels scattered in xylem parenchyma rich in starch; medullary rays | Large vessels, **xylem fibres with crystal sheath**, xylem parenchyma with starch; broad medullary rays |
| Pith | Absent (root) | Present in stolon; absent in root |

| Tissue | Curcuma (turmeric) rhizome (*Curcuma longa*) |
| --- | --- |
| Outer layer | Epidermis (often worn off) and a few layers of cork formed in the outer cortex |
| Cortex | Thin-walled parenchyma with **scattered closed collateral vascular bundles** and many **oil cells** with yellow oil |
| Endodermis | Distinct single layer separating cortex from stele |
| Stele | Parenchyma with numerous **scattered vascular bundles**, crowded just inside the endodermis |
| Cell contents | Parenchyma filled with **gelatinised starch** and yellow colouring matter (curcumin), so the section looks yellow |
| Cambium | Absent (monocot) |

## Result

The sections were identified as **ashwagandha root** (thin cork, starch-filled parenchyma, sandy crystals, no pith), **liquorice** (reddish-brown cork, phloem and xylem fibres with crystal sheaths, wavy rays) and **curcuma rhizome** (scattered closed bundles, endodermis, oil cells, yellow gelatinised starch).

## Precautions

1. Sections should be thin and include all tissues from outside to centre.
2. Turmeric stains fingers and slides yellow; wash promptly.
3. Do not over-clear sections meant for starch observation.
4. Wipe off excess acid after staining.

## Viva questions

1. **What is a crystal sheath?** A layer of cells, each with a prism of calcium oxalate, surrounding a group of fibres, as in liquorice.
2. **What is the sweet principle of liquorice?** Glycyrrhizin (a triterpenoid saponin glycoside).
3. **Why is the starch of turmeric gelatinised?** The rhizomes are boiled or steamed before drying.
4. **Is curcuma a monocot or dicot? How do you know?** Monocot; it has scattered closed vascular bundles and no cambium.
5. **Name the active constituents of ashwagandha.** Withanolides (such as withaferin A) and alkaloids.
6. **Name the yellow pigment of turmeric.** Curcumin.
7. **How is a stolon of liquorice told from a root?** The stolon has a pith; the root does not.
8. **Which reagent shows starch, and what colour?** Iodine solution; blue to blue-black.

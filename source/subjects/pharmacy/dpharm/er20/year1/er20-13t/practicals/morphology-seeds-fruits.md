# Morphology of seeds and fruits: Ispaghula, Coriander, Fennel, Cardamom, Nutmeg, Black Pepper, Gokhru

**Aim:** To identify the given crude drugs (seeds and fruits) by their morphological (organoleptic) characters.

> Use the specimens from the college museum and compare with authentic samples. Follow your lab manual where it differs.

## Principle

Each crude drug has fixed external characters — shape, size, colour, surface, odour and taste — that do not change much from sample to sample. Studying them with the naked eye and a hand lens identifies the drug and shows gross adulteration. This is the first step of evaluation (organoleptic evaluation).

## Requirements

Specimens of the seven drugs, hand lens (magnifying glass), forceps, scale (ruler), white tile or paper, beaker of water (for ispaghula swelling and floating tests), blade.

## Procedure

1. Place each drug on white paper and note its general shape and colour.
2. Measure the length and breadth of five to ten pieces with the scale and record the range.
3. Examine the surface with a hand lens for ridges, wrinkles, furrows, spines and scars.
4. Break or cut one piece and observe the inside.
5. Crush a little between the fingers and smell it.
6. Taste a very small piece (only drugs known to be safe; never taste unknown drugs) and spit it out.
7. For ispaghula, put a few seeds in water and observe after 15–30 minutes.
8. Record observations in the table and draw a labelled sketch of each.

## Result

The given drugs were identified as: ispaghula, coriander, fennel, cardamom, nutmeg, black pepper and gokhru, from their morphological characters recorded above.

## Precautions

1. Use a clean, well-lit surface and a good hand lens.
2. Measure several pieces and give a range, not a single value.
3. Taste only safe, edible drugs and only a tiny amount; spit out and rinse the mouth.
4. Do not mix specimens; return each to its labelled jar.

## Viva questions

1. **What is a cremocarp?** A dry fruit of the Umbelliferae that splits into two one-seeded halves called mericarps (coriander, fennel).
2. **Which part of ispaghula forms mucilage?** The outer epidermis of the seed coat (the husk).
3. **What is mace?** The dried arillus that surrounds the nutmeg seed.
4. **Name the adulterant of black pepper.** Dried papaya seeds; they are lighter, less wrinkled and not pungent.
5. **How do you tell coriander from fennel?** Coriander is globular with wavy and straight ridges and mericarps that stay joined; fennel is elongated with five straight ridges per mericarp.
6. **Why is nutmeg sometimes coated with lime?** To protect it from insects during storage.
7. **What is "large gokhru"?** Fruits of *Pedalium murex*, used as a substitute for *Tribulus terrestris*.
8. **Name the chief constituent of fennel oil.** Anethole (with fenchone).

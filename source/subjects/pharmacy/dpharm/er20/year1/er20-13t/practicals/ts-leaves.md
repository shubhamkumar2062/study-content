# Transverse section of leaves: Datura and Vasaka

**Aim:** To prepare, stain and study the transverse section (TS) of the leaves of datura and vasaka, and to identify their diagnostic characters.

> Follow your lab manual for the staining schedule. Handle razor blades with care.

## Principle

The internal structure (anatomy) of a leaf is constant for a species. A thin transverse section through the midrib, cleared and stained, shows the epidermis, trichomes, stomata, mesophyll, vascular bundle and cell inclusions. **Phloroglucinol with conc. HCl** stains lignified walls (xylem) pink-red; **chloral hydrate** clears the tissue by dissolving starch and chlorophyll.

## Requirements

Fresh or soaked leaves (or leaf pieces fixed in a potato or carrot block), sharp razor blade, watch glasses, brush, glass slides and cover slips, chloral hydrate solution, phloroglucinol solution (1% in alcohol), conc. HCl, dilute glycerine, compound microscope, spirit lamp.

## Procedure

1. Soak a dry leaf in water (or boil briefly) to soften it.
2. Hold a piece of the leaf with the midrib between two pieces of potato or pith.
3. Cut many very thin sections at right angles to the midrib with a wet razor, using one smooth stroke.
4. Transfer sections to a watch glass of water with a brush; select the thinnest complete ones.
5. Clear: warm the section in chloral hydrate on a slide for a few minutes.
6. Stain: add a drop of phloroglucinol, wait a minute, add a drop of conc. HCl.
7. Mount in dilute glycerine, place a cover slip without air bubbles.
8. Observe under low power, then high power. Draw a labelled diagram (outline of the whole TS plus a detailed sector).

## Observations

| Tissue | Datura leaf | Vasaka leaf |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Type of leaf | Dorsiventral | Dorsiventral |
| Epidermis | Single layer with cuticle on both surfaces | Single layer with cuticle; some cells contain **cystoliths** (calcium carbonate bodies) |
| Trichomes | **Covering**: uniseriate, multicellular, warty (2–5 cells). **Glandular**: short stalk with multicellular head | Covering: uniseriate, few-celled; glandular trichomes present |
| Stomata | **Anisocytic** (cruciferous), on both surfaces | **Diacytic** (caryophyllaceous), mainly on lower surface |
| Palisade | Single layer under the upper epidermis, not continuous over the midrib | One or two layers, not continuous over the midrib |
| Spongy mesophyll | Loosely arranged; many cells with **cluster crystals** of calcium oxalate; some microcrystals | Loose spongy cells |
| Midrib | Collenchyma under both epidermises; **bicollateral** vascular bundle (phloem above and below the xylem arc) | Collenchyma under both epidermises; crescent-shaped collateral vascular bundle |
| Other | — | **Acicular (needle-like) crystals** of calcium oxalate in parenchyma |

## Result

The section showing anisocytic stomata, warty covering trichomes, glandular trichomes, cluster crystals and a bicollateral bundle was identified as **datura leaf**. The section showing diacytic stomata, cystoliths and acicular crystals was identified as **vasaka leaf**.

## Precautions

1. Sections must be thin, complete and cut at right angles to the midrib.
2. Do not let sections dry; keep them in water.
3. Do not overheat chloral hydrate (bubbles spoil the mount).
4. Add HCl after phloroglucinol, not before; wipe off excess acid to protect the microscope.
5. Remove air bubbles before observing.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is chloral hydrate used?** It clears the section by dissolving starch, chlorophyll and proteins, so cell walls and crystals are seen clearly.
2. **What does phloroglucinol + HCl stain?** Lignified walls (xylem, fibres, stone cells) pink-red.
3. **What is a bicollateral vascular bundle?** A bundle with phloem on both the outer and inner side of the xylem, as in Solanaceae.
4. **What type of stomata does datura have?** Anisocytic — three subsidiary cells, one smaller than the other two.
5. **What are cystoliths?** Deposits of calcium carbonate on a stalk inside enlarged epidermal cells, typical of Acanthaceae (vasaka).
6. **Name the alkaloid of vasaka.** Vasicine.
7. **Name the family of datura and its alkaloids.** Solanaceae; hyoscyamine and hyoscine.
8. **What are diacytic stomata?** Stomata with two subsidiary cells at right angles to the guard cells.

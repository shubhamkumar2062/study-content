# Transverse section of clove (flower bud)

**Aim:** To prepare and study the transverse section of the hypanthium of clove and identify its diagnostic characters.

> Follow your lab manual for the staining schedule. Handle razor blades with care.

## Principle

Clove is the dried flower bud of *Syzygium aromaticum* (Myrtaceae). Its stalk-like lower part, the **hypanthium**, contains many large **schizolysigenous oil glands** near the surface, a spongy aerenchyma in the middle and a central columella with vascular bundles. These features, with the strong aromatic oil, identify clove and its powder.

## Requirements

Cloves soaked in water for a few hours, potato block, razor blade, watch glass, brush, chloral hydrate, phloroglucinol (1% alcoholic), conc. HCl, Sudan red III (optional, for oil), dilute glycerine, slides, cover slips, microscope.

## Procedure

1. Soak cloves in water so the hypanthium becomes soft.
2. Fix the hypanthium in a potato block and cut thin transverse sections through its middle.
3. Clear in warm chloral hydrate (oil glands may look empty after clearing; for oil, stain an uncleared section with Sudan red III).
4. Stain with phloroglucinol and conc. HCl.
5. Mount in dilute glycerine; observe under low and high power.
6. Draw an outline diagram and a detailed sector.

## Observations

| Zone (outside → inside) | Character |
| --- | --- |
| Epidermis | Single layer of small cells with a very thick cuticle; a few stomata |
| Outer cortex (oil gland zone) | Parenchyma with **2–3 rows of large oval schizolysigenous oil glands** arranged in a ring just below the epidermis |
| Middle cortex | Parenchyma with a ring of small **bicollateral vascular bundles**, each with a few pericyclic fibres; cluster crystals of calcium oxalate |
| Aerenchyma | Loose tissue with large air spaces (spongy zone) |
| Central columella | Dense parenchyma with a ring of small vascular bundles and many **cluster crystals** |
| Stains | Lignified fibres and xylem pink-red; oil glands red with Sudan red III |

## Result

The section showing a thick cuticle, rows of large oil glands below the epidermis, a ring of bicollateral bundles, aerenchyma and a central columella with cluster crystals was identified as **clove (hypanthium)**.

## Precautions

1. Cut through the hypanthium, not through the head.
2. Sections must be thin and complete.
3. Do not overheat during clearing; oil may be lost.
4. Remove air bubbles before observation.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the hypanthium?** The cup-like, stalk-shaped part of the flower below the sepals, formed by the fused base of floral parts.
2. **What type of oil glands occur in clove?** Schizolysigenous oil glands.
3. **Name the chief constituent of clove oil.** Eugenol.
4. **What is aerenchyma?** Spongy parenchyma with large air spaces.
5. **What are the adulterants of clove?** Clove stalks, mother cloves (fruits), blown cloves and exhausted cloves.
6. **How do clove stalks differ under the microscope?** They have stone cells (sclereids), which are absent in good clove buds.
7. **What crystals are seen in clove?** Cluster crystals (rosettes) of calcium oxalate.
8. **Why is clove oil used in dentistry?** Eugenol is antiseptic and has a local anaesthetic (numbing) effect.

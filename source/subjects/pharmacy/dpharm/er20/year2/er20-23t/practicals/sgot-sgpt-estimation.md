# Estimation of serum SGOT (AST) and SGPT (ALT)

**Aim:** To estimate the activity of serum glutamate oxaloacetate transaminase (SGOT, aspartate aminotransferase, AST) and serum glutamate pyruvate transaminase (SGPT, alanine aminotransferase, ALT) in the given (simulated) serum sample.

> Two methods are in common use: the colorimetric **Reitman–Frankel (DNPH)** method and the **kinetic (UV, IFCC-type)** method on a semi-auto analyser. Follow the kit insert you are given.

## Principle

### Transamination reactions

```text
 SGOT (AST):  L-Aspartate + α-Ketoglutarate  ⇌  Oxaloacetate + L-Glutamate
 SGPT (ALT):  L-Alanine   + α-Ketoglutarate  ⇌  Pyruvate     + L-Glutamate
```

### A. Reitman–Frankel (colorimetric, end-point) method

Serum is incubated with the buffered substrate at 37 °C for a fixed time. The **keto acid formed (oxaloacetate or pyruvate)** reacts with **2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine (DNPH)** to form a **hydrazone**, which gives a **brown-red colour in alkaline medium (NaOH)**. The colour, read at about **505 nm**, is converted to enzyme activity (units) using a **calibration curve** made with pyruvate standards.

### B. Kinetic (UV) method

The keto acid formed is reduced at once by an indicator enzyme, using NADH:

```text
 AST: Oxaloacetate + NADH + H+  ──malate dehydrogenase (MDH)──→ Malate + NAD+
 ALT: Pyruvate     + NADH + H+  ──lactate dehydrogenase (LDH)──→ Lactate + NAD+
```

NADH absorbs at **340 nm**; NAD+ does not. The **rate of decrease in absorbance per minute (ΔA/min)** is proportional to enzyme activity: Activity (U/L) = ΔA/min × factor (the factor is given in the kit insert, worked out from the molar absorptivity of NADH and the volumes used).

## Requirements

Colorimeter / semi-auto analyser with 340 nm (for kinetic) or about 505 nm filter (for DNPH), water bath at 37 °C, micropipettes, test tubes, timer. Kit reagents: AST and ALT substrates (buffered amino acid + α-ketoglutarate), DNPH colour reagent, 0.4 N NaOH, pyruvate standard (for DNPH method); or kinetic AST/ALT reagents with NADH and MDH/LDH. Fresh, non-haemolysed serum.

## Procedure

### A. Reitman–Frankel method (outline, typical)

1. For each enzyme, take two tubes: **Test** and **Control** (sample blank).
2. Add 0.5 ml of the appropriate substrate to each and warm to 37 °C for a few minutes.
3. Add 0.1 ml serum to the **Test** tube only; incubate at 37 °C – **60 minutes for SGOT, 30 minutes for SGPT** (as commonly specified).
4. Add 0.5 ml DNPH reagent to both tubes; then add 0.1 ml serum to the **Control** tube.
5. Let stand at room temperature for 20 minutes.
6. Add 5 ml of 0.4 N NaOH to both tubes, mix, and after about 10 minutes read the absorbance at about 505 nm against distilled water (or as directed).
7. Subtract the control reading from the test reading and find the activity (U/L) from the calibration curve or chart supplied with the kit.

### B. Kinetic method (typical)

1. Pre-warm the working reagent to 37 °C.
2. Add 100 µl serum to 1000 µl working reagent; mix and transfer to the cuvette at 37 °C.
3. After a lag of about 1 minute, read absorbance at 340 nm every minute for 3 minutes.
4. Calculate the mean ΔA/min.

## Observations and calculations

### Kinetic method (example)

| Time (min) | Absorbance at 340 nm |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 1.250 |
| 2 | 1.236 |
| 3 | 1.222 |
| 4 | 1.208 |

Mean ΔA/min = (1.250 − 1.208) / 3 = **0.014**

```text
 Activity (U/L) = ΔA/min × kit factor
 Example: if the kit factor is 1746, activity = 0.014 × 1746 ≈ 24 U/L
```

(Use the factor printed on your kit; it depends on the volumes and light path.)

### Interpretation (commonly quoted; ranges vary by laboratory and method)

| Enzyme | About normal (adult) | Markedly raised in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SGPT (ALT) | About 5–40 U/L | Acute viral hepatitis, toxic/drug liver injury (paracetamol overdose), fatty liver – more liver-specific |
| SGOT (AST) | About 5–40 U/L | Liver disease, myocardial infarction, muscle injury; AST:ALT above about 2 suggests alcoholic liver disease |

## Precautions

1. Use fresh serum; avoid **haemolysis** – red cells contain much AST and give falsely high values.
2. Keep exact incubation times and temperature (37 °C); enzyme activity depends on both.
3. In the kinetic method, the starting absorbance must be high enough (enough NADH left); very high activity uses up NADH quickly – dilute the sample and repeat.
4. DNPH is toxic and NaOH is corrosive.
5. Results by different methods are not interchangeable; compare with the reference range of the same method.

## Viva questions

1. **What coenzyme do transaminases need?** Pyridoxal phosphate (vitamin B6).
2. **Which is more specific for the liver, ALT or AST?** ALT (SGPT).
3. **Why must haemolysed samples be avoided?** Red cells are rich in AST.
4. **What is measured at 340 nm in the kinetic method?** The fall in NADH absorbance.
5. **What is the role of DNPH in the Reitman–Frankel method?** It reacts with the keto acid formed to give a coloured hydrazone in alkali.
6. **Name the indicator enzymes used in the kinetic method.** Malate dehydrogenase (AST) and lactate dehydrogenase (ALT).

# Qualitative analysis of proteins and amino acids

**Aim:** To perform the colour and precipitation reactions of proteins and amino acids and to identify the given protein (for example egg albumin, casein or gelatin) or amino acid.

> The syllabus asks for 4 experiments, commonly: colour reactions of proteins, precipitation reactions, tests for specific amino acids, and identification of an unknown protein. Follow your lab manual.

## Principle

| Test | Principle |
| --- | --- |
| Biuret | In alkaline medium, Cu2+ forms a violet complex with compounds having two or more peptide bonds |
| Ninhydrin | Ninhydrin oxidatively deaminates α-amino acids; the reduced ninhydrin, ammonia and another ninhydrin form a purple compound (Ruhemann's purple) |
| Xanthoproteic | Concentrated nitric acid nitrates the benzene ring of aromatic amino acids (yellow nitro compounds), which turn orange in alkali |
| Millon's | Mercury salts in nitric/sulphuric acid react with the phenolic –OH of tyrosine to give a red colour |
| Hopkins–Cole (glyoxylic acid) | The indole ring of tryptophan condenses with glyoxylic acid in strong H2SO4 to form a violet ring |
| Sulphur (lead acetate) | Boiling with strong alkali releases sulphide from cysteine/cystine, forming black lead sulphide with lead acetate |
| Sakaguchi | The guanidino group of arginine gives a red colour with α-naphthol and hypobromite |
| Heat coagulation | Heat denatures albumins and globulins; at their isoelectric point (slight acidification) they form a coagulum |
| Heavy metal precipitation | Positively charged metal ions (Hg2+, Pb2+, Cu2+) combine with negatively charged protein (alkaline side of pI) to form insoluble salts |
| Alkaloidal reagents | Picric, tannic and trichloroacetic acid combine with positively charged protein (acid side of pI) |
| Salting out | High salt concentration removes the water shell; globulins precipitate at half saturation and albumins at full saturation with ammonium sulphate |

## Requirements

Test tubes, water bath, burner, droppers. Protein solutions: egg albumin (egg white diluted), casein in dilute alkali, gelatin; amino acid solutions (tyrosine, tryptophan, cysteine, arginine, glycine).

Reagents: 40% NaOH, 1% CuSO4, 0.1% ninhydrin, concentrated HNO3, ammonia, Millon's reagent, glyoxylic acid (Hopkins–Cole reagent), concentrated H2SO4, lead acetate, α-naphthol, sodium hypobromite, 1% acetic acid, mercuric chloride, lead acetate, picric acid, 10% trichloroacetic acid, solid ammonium sulphate, alcohol.

## Procedure

1. **Biuret test:** 2 ml test solution + 2 ml 40% NaOH + 2 drops of 1% CuSO4; mix.
2. **Ninhydrin test:** 2 ml test solution + 0.5 ml ninhydrin solution; boil for 1–2 minutes and cool.
3. **Xanthoproteic test:** 2 ml test solution + 1 ml concentrated HNO3; heat gently; cool and add 40% NaOH or ammonia in excess.
4. **Millon's test:** 2 ml test solution + 2–3 drops Millon's reagent; warm gently.
5. **Hopkins–Cole test:** 2 ml test solution + 2 ml glyoxylic acid reagent; mix, then pour concentrated H2SO4 slowly along the side.
6. **Sulphur test:** 2 ml test solution + 2 ml 40% NaOH; boil for a minute; add a few drops of lead acetate.
7. **Sakaguchi test:** 2 ml test solution + 1 ml 10% NaOH + 2 drops α-naphthol; mix; add a few drops of sodium hypobromite.
8. **Heat coagulation:** heat the upper part of 5 ml protein solution; add 1–2 drops of 1% acetic acid.
9. **Precipitation by heavy metals:** 2 ml protein solution + a few drops of mercuric chloride or lead acetate.
10. **Precipitation by alkaloidal reagents:** 2 ml protein solution + a few drops of picric acid or trichloroacetic acid.
11. **Salting out:** add solid ammonium sulphate to 5 ml protein solution to half saturation, observe, then to full saturation.
12. **Alcohol precipitation:** 2 ml protein solution + 5 ml alcohol.

## Precautions

1. Add only 1–2 drops of CuSO4 in the biuret test; excess gives a blue colour that hides the violet.
2. Concentrated acids and 40% NaOH are corrosive; use droppers and add slowly.
3. Millon's reagent contains mercury – toxic; do not pour into the sink.
4. In Hopkins–Cole test do not shake after adding H2SO4.
5. Use freshly prepared hypobromite for Sakaguchi's test.

## Viva questions

1. **Which test is given by all proteins but not by free amino acids?** Biuret test (needs at least two peptide bonds).
2. **Which amino acid gives a yellow colour with ninhydrin?** Proline (an imino acid).
3. **Why does gelatin give a negative Hopkins–Cole test?** It lacks tryptophan.
4. **What is salting out?** Precipitation of proteins by high concentrations of neutral salts like ammonium sulphate.
5. **Why are egg white and milk given in heavy metal poisoning?** Their proteins combine with the metal ions and precipitate them.
6. **Which test detects sulphur-containing amino acids?** Lead acetate (sulphur) test – cysteine and cystine.

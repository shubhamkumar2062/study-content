# Urine analysis: normal constituents

**Aim:** To study the physical characteristics of the given urine sample and to detect its normal organic and inorganic constituents.

> Simulated or real urine may be used, as your college provides. Wear gloves and treat every urine sample as potentially infectious.

## Principle

Urine normally contains water (about 95%) and dissolved solids: organic substances (urea, creatinine, uric acid, small amounts of ammonia and hippuric acid) and inorganic salts (chlorides, sulphates, phosphates, calcium, sodium, potassium). Each is identified by a simple chemical reaction:

| Constituent | Reaction |
| --- | --- |
| Urea | Urease splits urea into ammonia and CO2; ammonia turns the solution alkaline (pink with phenolphthalein). Sodium hypobromite decomposes urea with brisk effervescence of nitrogen |
| Creatinine | **Jaffe's reaction** – creatinine forms an orange-red complex with alkaline picrate |
| Uric acid | Reduces phosphotungstic acid in alkaline medium (sodium carbonate) to a blue compound (tungsten blue) |
| Chloride | With silver nitrate in nitric acid gives a white curdy precipitate of AgCl, soluble in ammonia |
| Sulphate | With barium chloride in HCl gives a white precipitate of BaSO4 |
| Phosphate | With ammonium molybdate in nitric acid on warming gives a canary-yellow precipitate of ammonium phosphomolybdate |
| Calcium | With ammonium oxalate (in acetic acid medium) gives a white precipitate of calcium oxalate |
| Ammonia | Warming with NaOH releases ammonia (smell; moist red litmus turns blue) |

## Requirements

Urine sample, urinometer and cylinder, pH paper or pH meter, test tubes, water bath, burner.

Reagents: urease (or soybean powder), phenolphthalein, sodium hypobromite, saturated picric acid, 10% NaOH, phosphotungstic acid reagent, 20% sodium carbonate, 5% silver nitrate, concentrated nitric acid, ammonia, 10% barium chloride, dilute HCl, ammonium molybdate, ammonium oxalate, acetic acid, red litmus paper.

## Procedure

### A. Physical examination

1. **Volume:** note the volume of the sample (for a 24-hour collection, measure the whole collection).
2. **Colour and appearance:** observe against a white background.
3. **Odour:** note gently.
4. **pH:** dip pH paper or use a pH meter.
5. **Specific gravity:** fill the urinometer cylinder about three-quarters with urine, float the urinometer without touching the sides, read at the bottom of the meniscus and note the urine temperature. Correct the reading by adding 0.001 for every 3 °C above, or subtracting 0.001 for every 3 °C below, the temperature at which the urinometer is calibrated (marked on it).

### B. Chemical tests

6. **Urea (urease test):** 3 ml urine + 2 drops phenolphthalein (neutralise if pink) + a pinch of urease/soybean powder; keep at about 37 °C for 10–15 minutes. **Hypobromite test:** add a few drops of urine to 2 ml sodium hypobromite.
7. **Creatinine (Jaffe's test):** 5 ml urine + 1 ml saturated picric acid + a few drops of 10% NaOH.
8. **Uric acid:** 2 ml urine + 1 ml phosphotungstic acid reagent + 1 ml 20% sodium carbonate.
9. **Chloride:** 2 ml urine + a few drops of concentrated HNO3 + a few drops of 5% AgNO3; then add ammonia to the precipitate.
10. **Sulphate:** 2 ml urine + a few drops of dilute HCl + 1 ml 10% BaCl2.
11. **Phosphate:** 2 ml urine + 1 ml concentrated HNO3 + 1 ml ammonium molybdate; warm.
12. **Calcium:** 2 ml urine + a few drops of acetic acid + 1 ml ammonium oxalate solution.
13. **Ammonia:** warm 2 ml urine with 2 ml NaOH; hold moist red litmus paper at the mouth of the tube.

## Observations

### Physical characteristics

| Property | Observed | Commonly quoted normal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Volume | ____ | About 1–2 L per day |
| Colour | ____ | Pale yellow to amber (urochrome) |
| Appearance | ____ | Clear |
| Odour | ____ | Faintly aromatic; ammoniacal on standing |
| pH | ____ | About 4.5–8, usually about 6 |
| Specific gravity | ____ | About 1.003–1.030 |

**Specific gravity correction example:** urinometer calibrated at 15 °C reads 1.018 at 24 °C. The urine is 9 °C warmer → 3 steps × 0.001 = 0.003 to be added → corrected specific gravity = **1.021**.

### Chemical tests

| Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Urease + phenolphthalein | Pink colour develops | Urea present |
| Hypobromite | Brisk effervescence | Urea present |
| Jaffe's | Orange-red colour | Creatinine present |
| Phosphotungstic acid | Blue colour | Uric acid present |
| AgNO3 + HNO3 | White curdy precipitate, soluble in ammonia | Chloride present |
| BaCl2 + HCl | White precipitate | Sulphate present |
| Ammonium molybdate | Canary-yellow precipitate | Phosphate present |
| Ammonium oxalate | White precipitate | Calcium present |
| NaOH + warmth | Ammonia smell; red litmus turns blue | Ammonia present |

## Result

The given urine sample has the physical characteristics recorded above and contains the normal constituents urea, creatinine, uric acid, chloride, sulphate, phosphate, calcium and ammonia.

## Precautions

1. Use a fresh sample (or refrigerate); old urine turns alkaline as bacteria split urea.
2. Wear gloves; disinfect spills and dispose of samples properly.
3. The urinometer must float freely, not touching the cylinder.
4. Acidify with HNO3 before adding AgNO3, so phosphates and carbonates do not precipitate.

## Viva questions

1. **What gives urine its yellow colour?** Urochrome (with a little urobilin).
2. **What is the largest organic constituent of urine?** Urea (about 20–30 g per day on a usual diet).
3. **Why does urine smell of ammonia on standing?** Bacteria convert urea to ammonia.
4. **Name the reaction used to detect creatinine.** Jaffe's reaction (alkaline picrate).
5. **What does a fixed specific gravity of about 1.010 suggest?** Loss of concentrating and diluting ability – chronic kidney failure.
6. **Why is nitric acid added before silver nitrate?** To prevent precipitation of phosphates and carbonates, so only AgCl forms.

# Estimation of blood urea (urease–Berthelot method)

**Aim:** To estimate the urea concentration in the given (simulated) serum sample by the enzymatic urease–Berthelot method.

> Many colleges use a urease–Berthelot kit; some use the diacetyl monoxime (DAM) method. Follow your kit insert or manual.

## Principle

```text
                 urease
 Urea + H2O ─────────────→ 2 NH3 + CO2

 NH3 + phenol (or salicylate) + hypochlorite ──(sodium nitroprusside, catalyst; alkaline)──→ indophenol (blue-green)
```

Urease breaks urea into ammonia; ammonia reacts with phenol (or salicylate) and alkaline hypochlorite to form a **blue-green indophenol** dye (**Berthelot reaction**). The colour, read at about **570–600 nm** (as the kit states), is proportional to the urea concentration and is compared with a urea standard (concentration printed on the label).

**DAM method (alternative):** urea condenses directly with diacetyl monoxime in hot acid medium (with thiosemicarbazide and ferric ions) to give a pink-red colour read at about 520–540 nm.

## Requirements

Colorimeter, micropipettes, test tubes, water bath at 37 °C, timer. Urea kit: enzyme (urease) reagent, phenol/salicylate–nitroprusside reagent, alkaline hypochlorite reagent, urea standard. Serum sample (not plasma with ammonium heparin or fluoride, which inhibits urease).

## Procedure (typical two-step kit)

1. Pipette:

| | Blank | Standard | Test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Urease (enzyme) reagent | 1000 µl | 1000 µl | 1000 µl |
| Distilled water | 10 µl | – | – |
| Urea standard | – | 10 µl | – |
| Serum | – | – | 10 µl |

2. Mix and incubate at **37 °C for about 5 minutes** (urease step).
3. Add the colour reagents (phenol/salicylate and hypochlorite) as stated – often 1000 µl of each.
4. Mix and incubate again at **37 °C for about 5–10 minutes** for colour development.
5. Zero the colorimeter with the blank; read standard and test at the stated wavelength.

## Observations (example)

| Tube | Absorbance |
| --- | --- |
| Blank | 0.00 (set) |
| Standard (say 40 mg/dl) | 0.48 |
| Test | 0.36 |

## Calculations

```text
                     A(test)
 Urea (mg/dl)  =  ──────────  ×  Concentration of standard (mg/dl)
                     A(std)

 Blood urea nitrogen (BUN, mg/dl) = Urea (mg/dl) ÷ 2.14
```

**Worked example:** (0.36 / 0.48) × 40 = **30 mg/dl** urea; BUN = 30 / 2.14 ≈ **14 mg/dl**.

### Interpretation (commonly quoted; ranges vary by laboratory)

- **Normal blood urea about 15–40 mg/dl** (BUN about 7–20 mg/dl).
- **Raised:** pre-renal (dehydration, shock, heart failure, high-protein diet, GI bleeding, fever), renal (acute or chronic kidney disease), post-renal (obstruction – stones, enlarged prostate).
- **Low:** severe liver disease, low-protein diet, pregnancy, overhydration.

## Precautions

1. Avoid fluoride and ammonium salts in the sample (fluoride inhibits urease; ammonium salts give falsely high values).
2. Keep reagents free of ammonia contamination (laboratory air, ammonia bottles nearby).
3. Hypochlorite and phenol are corrosive; handle carefully.
4. Keep incubation time and temperature identical for standard and test.
5. Very high values: dilute the sample with saline and multiply the result.

## Viva questions

1. **Where is urea formed?** In the liver by the urea cycle.
2. **What is the principle of the Berthelot reaction?** Ammonia from urea reacts with phenol and hypochlorite to give blue indophenol.
3. **How is BUN related to urea?** Urea (mg/dl) = BUN × 2.14.
4. **Why is serum creatinine a better kidney marker than urea?** Urea is affected by diet, hydration, protein breakdown and liver function.
5. **Name a non-enzymatic method for urea.** Diacetyl monoxime (DAM) method.

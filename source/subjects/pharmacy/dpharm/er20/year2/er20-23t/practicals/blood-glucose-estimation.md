# Estimation of blood glucose (GOD-POD method)

**Aim:** To estimate the glucose concentration in the given (simulated) serum/plasma sample by the glucose oxidase–peroxidase (GOD-POD) method.

> Most laboratories use a commercial kit. Volumes, incubation time and wavelength below are typical; always follow the kit insert.

## Principle

The GOD-POD (Trinder) method is an enzymatic, end-point method:

```text
                  glucose oxidase (GOD)
 Glucose + O2 + H2O ───────────────────→ Gluconic acid + H2O2

                         peroxidase (POD)
 2 H2O2 + phenol + 4-aminoantipyrine ─────────→ red quinoneimine dye + 4 H2O
```

The intensity of the **red (pink) colour** is directly proportional to the glucose concentration and is read at about **505 nm** (green filter, range 490–550 nm) against a reagent blank, and compared with a standard of known concentration (commonly 100 mg/dl). Glucose oxidase is specific for β-D-glucose, so other sugars do not interfere.

## Requirements

Colorimeter or semi-auto analyser, test tubes, micropipettes (10 µl and 1000 µl) with tips, water bath or incubator at 37 °C, timer. GOD-POD reagent kit (working reagent and glucose standard), serum or fluoride plasma sample.

## Procedure

1. Bring the reagents to room temperature.
2. Label three tubes and pipette:

| | Blank (B) | Standard (S) | Test (T) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Working reagent | 1000 µl | 1000 µl | 1000 µl |
| Distilled water | 10 µl | – | – |
| Glucose standard | – | 10 µl | – |
| Serum / plasma | – | – | 10 µl |

3. Mix and incubate at **37 °C for about 10 minutes** (or room temperature for about 15–20 minutes, as the kit states).
4. Set the colorimeter to zero with the blank at 505 nm.
5. Read the absorbance of the standard and the test (colour is usually stable for about an hour; read within the time stated).

## Observations

| Tube | Absorbance (example) |
| --- | --- |
| Blank | 0.00 (set) |
| Standard (100 mg/dl) | 0.32 |
| Test | 0.29 |

## Calculations

```text
                        A(test)
 Glucose (mg/dl)  =  ──────────  ×  Concentration of standard (mg/dl)
                        A(std)
```

**Worked example:** (0.29 / 0.32) × 100 = **90.6 mg/dl**.

To convert to mmol/L, divide mg/dl by 18 (90.6 / 18 ≈ 5.0 mmol/L).

### Interpretation (commonly quoted; ranges vary by laboratory)

| Sample | About normal | Suggests diabetes (guideline cut-off) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fasting plasma glucose | About 70–100 mg/dl (100–125 = impaired fasting glucose) | 126 mg/dl or more |
| 2 hours after 75 g glucose (OGTT) | Below about 140 mg/dl | 200 mg/dl or more |
| Random | Usually below about 140 mg/dl | 200 mg/dl or more with symptoms |

A single value is not a diagnosis; the doctor confirms with repeat tests.

## Result

The glucose concentration of the given sample is ________ mg/dl, which is ________ (normal / raised / low).

## Precautions

1. Collect blood in **fluoride–oxalate** tubes (fluoride stops glycolysis by blood cells) or separate serum quickly; glucose falls on standing.
2. Pipette the small (10 µl) volumes accurately and wipe tips.
3. Keep incubation time and temperature the same for standard and test.
4. Very high results beyond the kit's linearity: dilute the sample with saline, repeat, and multiply by the dilution.
5. Haemolysed, lipaemic or icteric samples can interfere.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the principle of the GOD-POD method?** Glucose oxidase forms H2O2 from glucose; peroxidase uses H2O2 to form a red quinoneimine dye from phenol and 4-aminoantipyrine.
2. **Why is the method specific?** Glucose oxidase acts only on β-D-glucose.
3. **Why is sodium fluoride used in the collection tube?** It inhibits enolase and stops glycolysis.
4. **At what wavelength is the colour read?** About 505 nm.
5. **What is HbA1c?** Glycated haemoglobin, which reflects average blood glucose over the previous 2–3 months.
6. **Name older methods of blood glucose estimation.** Folin–Wu and o-toluidine methods.

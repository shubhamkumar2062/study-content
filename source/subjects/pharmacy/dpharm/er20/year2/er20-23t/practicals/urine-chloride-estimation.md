# Estimation of chlorides in urine (Volhard's method)

**Aim:** To estimate the amount of chloride in the given urine sample by Volhard's method (back titration).

## Principle

Chloride in urine is precipitated as silver chloride by adding a **known excess of standard silver nitrate** in nitric acid medium:

```text
 Cl−  +  Ag+ (excess)  →  AgCl ↓ (white)
```

The **unused silver nitrate** is then titrated with standard **ammonium thiocyanate**, using **ferric alum** (ferric ammonium sulphate) as indicator:

```text
 Ag+ (left over)  +  SCN−  →  AgSCN ↓ (white)
 First excess SCN−  +  Fe3+  →  [Fe(SCN)]2+  (reddish-brown / salmon-pink)  ← end point
```

Volume of AgNO3 that reacted with chloride = volume of AgNO3 added − volume equivalent to the thiocyanate used. Nitric acid prevents precipitation of phosphates and carbonates.

## Requirements

Burette, pipettes (5 ml, 10 ml, 20 ml), conical flask, measuring cylinder, filter paper (optional). Reagents: 0.1 N AgNO3 (standard), 0.1 N NH4SCN (standardised against the AgNO3), concentrated (chloride-free) HNO3, ferric alum indicator (saturated), distilled water. Urine sample.

## Procedure

1. Pipette **5 ml of urine** into a conical flask; add about 20 ml distilled water.
2. Add about **2 ml of concentrated HNO3** (chloride-free).
3. Add exactly **20 ml of 0.1 N AgNO3** from a pipette or burette (excess). Mix well and allow the AgCl to settle (it may be filtered, or a little nitrobenzene/ether added to coat the precipitate, as your manual directs, to prevent AgCl reacting with thiocyanate).
4. Add **1 ml of ferric alum indicator**.
5. Titrate with **0.1 N NH4SCN** from the burette, with constant swirling, until a **permanent faint reddish-brown (salmon-pink) colour** appears.
6. Repeat for concordant readings.
7. **Blank (standardisation):** titrate 20 ml of 0.1 N AgNO3 with the NH4SCN in the same way to confirm their equivalence (if the solutions are exactly equal in normality, 20 ml AgNO3 needs 20 ml NH4SCN).

## Observations

| Trial | Initial (ml) | Final (ml) | NH4SCN used (ml) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.0 | 6.6 | 6.6 |
| 2 | 6.6 | 13.2 | 6.6 |

Concordant NH4SCN volume (y) = 6.6 ml (example; record your own).

## Calculations

```text
 AgNO3 added                              = 20.0 ml of 0.1 N
 AgNO3 left over (equals NH4SCN used)     = y ml  (if both are exactly 0.1 N)
 AgNO3 used by chloride                   = (20.0 − y) ml

 1 ml of 0.1 N AgNO3  ≡  3.545 mg chloride  ≡  5.845 mg NaCl

 Chloride in 5 ml urine (mg)  = (20.0 − y) × 3.545
 Chloride (mg/dl)             = (20.0 − y) × 3.545 × 100 / 5
 As mEq/L                     = (20.0 − y) × 0.1 × 1000 / 5      (1 ml of 0.1 N = 0.1 mEq)
```

**Worked example:** y = 6.6 ml → AgNO3 used = 13.4 ml.
Chloride = 13.4 × 3.545 = 47.5 mg in 5 ml = **950 mg/dl** (as chloride).
As NaCl = 13.4 × 5.845 = 78.3 mg in 5 ml ≈ 1.57 g/dl.
In mEq/L = 13.4 × 0.1 × 1000 / 5 = **268 mEq/L**.
If the 24-hour volume is 1.0 L, daily chloride ≈ 268 mEq.

Commonly quoted daily urinary chloride is about 110–250 mEq, varying greatly with salt intake; ranges vary by laboratory.

## Precautions

1. Use chloride-free nitric acid and distilled water.
2. The AgNO3 must be in excess; if no excess remains (immediate red colour with indicator), repeat with more AgNO3 or less urine.
3. Remove or coat the AgCl precipitate, or the end point fades as AgCl slowly reacts with thiocyanate.
4. Swirl constantly; the first permanent faint colour is the end point.
5. Silver nitrate stains skin and is light-sensitive; store in amber bottles.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is the method called a back titration?** Excess AgNO3 is added and the unreacted part is titrated.
2. **What is the indicator?** Ferric alum (ferric ammonium sulphate); it gives a reddish-brown ferric thiocyanate complex at the end point.
3. **Why is nitric acid added?** To prevent precipitation of phosphates and carbonates and to keep the indicator from hydrolysing.
4. **What is the main source of urinary chloride?** Dietary sodium chloride.
5. **When is urinary chloride low?** Vomiting, diarrhoea, heavy sweating, low salt intake and conditions that retain salt.

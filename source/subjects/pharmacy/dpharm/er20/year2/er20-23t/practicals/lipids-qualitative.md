# Qualitative analysis of lipids

**Aim:** To perform qualitative tests for lipids (oils, fats, glycerol and cholesterol).

> The syllabus asks for 2 experiments, usually (1) general tests for fats and oils and (2) tests for cholesterol. Follow your lab manual.

## Principle

- **Solubility:** lipids are non-polar, so they do not dissolve in water but dissolve in organic solvents (chloroform, ether).
- **Translucent spot test:** oils and fats penetrate paper and change its light scattering, leaving a permanent translucent (grease) spot that does not dry off.
- **Emulsification:** oil and water separate on standing; an emulsifier (bile salt, soap, protein) lowers interfacial tension and gives a stable emulsion.
- **Saponification:** fats are hydrolysed by alcoholic KOH to glycerol and potassium soaps, which froth when shaken with water.
- **Acrolein test:** potassium bisulphate dehydrates glycerol (free or in a fat) on strong heating to acrolein, an unsaturated aldehyde with a sharp, irritating smell.
- **Unsaturation test:** iodine (or bromine) adds to double bonds of unsaturated fatty acids, so its colour disappears.
- **Sudan III test:** the fat-soluble red dye dissolves in the oil layer.
- **Salkowski test:** cholesterol in chloroform is dehydrated by concentrated H2SO4 to coloured products – red in the chloroform layer, yellow with green fluorescence in the acid layer.
- **Liebermann–Burchard test:** cholesterol with acetic anhydride and concentrated H2SO4 gives a red → blue → green colour.

## Requirements

Test tubes, filter paper, porcelain dish, water bath, burner. Samples: a vegetable oil (groundnut, coconut or castor), glycerol, cholesterol in chloroform. Reagents: chloroform, ether, ethanol, 10% bile salt or soap solution, alcoholic KOH, solid potassium bisulphate, Hübl's/dilute iodine solution, Sudan III, concentrated H2SO4, acetic anhydride.

## Procedure

### Experiment 1: Tests for oils and fats

1. **Solubility:** take a few drops of oil in three test tubes; add water, alcohol and chloroform separately; shake and observe.
2. **Translucent spot:** place a drop of oil on filter paper; let it stand for a few minutes; hold against light.
3. **Emulsification:** shake 2 drops of oil with 5 ml water in one tube and with 5 ml water + 1 ml bile salt (or soap) solution in another; let stand.
4. **Saponification:** boil 5 drops of oil with 2–3 ml of alcoholic KOH in a water bath for about 5–10 minutes; add water and shake.
5. **Acrolein test:** take a few drops of oil (or glycerol) in a dry test tube with a pinch of solid KHSO4; heat strongly; cautiously waft the vapours towards the nose.
6. **Unsaturation:** dissolve a few drops of oil in 2 ml chloroform; add iodine solution drop by drop with shaking.
7. **Sudan III:** add 2 drops of Sudan III to oil mixed with water; shake and let stand.

### Experiment 2: Tests for cholesterol

8. **Salkowski test:** to 2 ml of cholesterol in chloroform add 2 ml of concentrated H2SO4 along the side; do not shake; observe both layers.
9. **Liebermann–Burchard test:** to 2 ml of cholesterol in chloroform add 10 drops of acetic anhydride and 2–3 drops of concentrated H2SO4; mix and keep in the dark for a few minutes.

## Observations

| Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solubility | Insoluble in water; soluble in chloroform; slightly in alcohol | Lipid present |
| Translucent spot | Permanent translucent spot | Oil/fat present |
| Emulsification | Stable emulsion only with bile salt/soap | Lipid emulsified |
| Saponification | Froth on shaking | Ester (fat/oil) saponified to soap |
| Acrolein | Sharp, pungent smell | Glycerol present |
| Unsaturation | Iodine colour disappears | Unsaturated fatty acids present |
| Sudan III | Red oil layer | Fat present |
| Salkowski | Red upper chloroform layer; yellow-green fluorescence below | Cholesterol present |
| Liebermann–Burchard | Red → blue → green | Cholesterol present |

## Result

The given sample answers the tests for ________ (oil/fat, glycerol, cholesterol).

## Precautions

1. Use dry test tubes for the acrolein test; acrolein is irritating – do not inhale directly.
2. Add concentrated H2SO4 slowly; acetic anhydride is corrosive.
3. Keep chloroform and ether away from flames.
4. Use a water bath (not a direct flame) when heating alcoholic KOH.

## Viva questions

1. **Why are lipids insoluble in water?** They are non-polar and cannot form hydrogen bonds with water.
2. **What is saponification?** Alkaline hydrolysis of fat to glycerol and soap.
3. **Which test is specific for glycerol?** Acrolein test.
4. **Name two tests for cholesterol.** Salkowski and Liebermann–Burchard tests.
5. **What is the role of bile salts in fat digestion?** They emulsify fats, increasing the surface for lipase action.
6. **What does iodine value indicate?** Degree of unsaturation of a fat.

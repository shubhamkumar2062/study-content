# Estimation of serum cholesterol (CHOD-PAP method)

**Aim:** To estimate the total cholesterol in the given (simulated) serum sample by the enzymatic cholesterol oxidase–peroxidase (CHOD-PAP) method.

> Follow the kit insert for exact volumes, incubation and wavelength.

## Principle

```text
                        cholesterol esterase (CHE)
 Cholesterol esters + H2O ──────────────────────────→ Cholesterol + fatty acids

                          cholesterol oxidase (CHOD)
 Cholesterol + O2 ──────────────────────────────────→ Cholest-4-en-3-one + H2O2

                                   peroxidase (POD)
 2 H2O2 + phenol + 4-aminoantipyrine ─────────────────→ red quinoneimine dye + 4 H2O
```

The esterase frees esterified cholesterol, so the method measures **total cholesterol**. The red colour, read at about **505 nm** (500–550 nm), is proportional to the cholesterol concentration and compared with a standard (commonly 200 mg/dl; check the label).

An older chemical method is the **Liebermann–Burchard** (or Zak's ferric chloride) method; enzymatic methods are now standard.

## Requirements

Colorimeter or semi-auto analyser, micropipettes (10 µl, 1000 µl), test tubes, incubator/water bath at 37 °C, timer. Cholesterol reagent kit (enzyme reagent and standard). Serum sample (preferably fasting).

## Procedure

1. Bring reagents to room temperature.
2. Pipette:

| | Blank | Standard | Test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cholesterol reagent | 1000 µl | 1000 µl | 1000 µl |
| Distilled water | 10 µl | – | – |
| Cholesterol standard | – | 10 µl | – |
| Serum | – | – | 10 µl |

3. Mix and incubate at **37 °C for about 5–10 minutes** (or room temperature for about 15–20 minutes as the kit states).
4. Zero the colorimeter with the blank at 505 nm.
5. Read the absorbance of standard and test within the time the colour is stable.

## Observations (example)

| Tube | Absorbance |
| --- | --- |
| Blank | 0.00 (set) |
| Standard (200 mg/dl) | 0.36 |
| Test | 0.40 |

## Calculations

```text
                            A(test)
 Total cholesterol (mg/dl) = ────────  ×  Concentration of standard (mg/dl)
                            A(std)
```

**Worked example:** (0.40 / 0.36) × 200 = **222 mg/dl**.

### Interpretation (commonly quoted guideline values; ranges vary by laboratory)

| Total cholesterol (mg/dl) | Category |
| --- | --- |
| Below about 200 | Desirable |
| About 200–239 | Borderline high |
| About 240 or more | High |

The example value (222 mg/dl) is borderline high. If HDL and triglycerides are also measured, LDL can be calculated by the Friedewald formula: LDL = TC − HDL − TG/5 (valid when TG < 400 mg/dl).

## Result

## Precautions

1. A fasting sample (about 9–12 hours) is preferred when a full lipid profile is done.
2. Avoid haemolysed samples; high bilirubin and ascorbic acid can lower peroxidase-based results.
3. Pipette the 10 µl samples exactly; serum is viscous, so wipe the tip outside.
4. Do not exceed the stated incubation; protect reagents from light.
5. Values above the linearity limit: dilute with saline, repeat and multiply.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is cholesterol esterase included?** About two-thirds of plasma cholesterol is esterified; esterase frees it so total cholesterol is measured.
2. **What colour is formed and at what wavelength is it read?** Red quinoneimine, about 505 nm.
3. **What is the desirable total cholesterol?** Commonly quoted as below about 200 mg/dl.
4. **Name the rate-limiting enzyme of cholesterol synthesis.** HMG-CoA reductase (inhibited by statins).
5. **Name an older chemical method.** Liebermann–Burchard reaction.

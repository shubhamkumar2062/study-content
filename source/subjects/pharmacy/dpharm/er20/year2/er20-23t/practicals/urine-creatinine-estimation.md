# Estimation of creatinine in urine (Jaffe's method)

**Aim:** To estimate the amount of creatinine in the given urine sample by Jaffe's alkaline picrate method using a colorimeter.

## Principle

Creatinine reacts with **picric acid in alkaline medium** to form an **orange-red creatinine picrate (Janovski) complex** (**Jaffe's reaction**). The intensity of the colour is proportional to the creatinine concentration and is measured in a colorimeter at about **490–520 nm** (a green filter; use the wavelength stated in your kit or manual) against a standard creatinine solution.

Because urine contains much more creatinine than serum, urine is **diluted** first (commonly 1 in 50 or 1 in 100; follow your manual).

## Requirements

Colorimeter with cuvettes, test tubes, pipettes, volumetric flask, timer. Reagents: saturated (about 1%) picric acid, 0.75 N (or 10%) NaOH, standard creatinine solution (concentration printed on the label or in the manual), distilled water. Urine sample.

## Procedure

1. **Dilute the urine:** for example, 1 ml urine made up to 100 ml with distilled water (1 in 100).
2. Label three tubes **Blank (B), Standard (S), Test (T)** and pipette:

| Reagent | Blank | Standard | Test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Distilled water | 1 ml | – | – |
| Standard creatinine | – | 1 ml | – |
| Diluted urine | – | – | 1 ml |
| Picric acid | 1 ml | 1 ml | 1 ml |
| NaOH | 1 ml | 1 ml | 1 ml |

   (Volumes are typical; use those in your manual or kit.)
3. Mix and keep at room temperature for about **15 minutes** for full colour development.
4. Set the colorimeter to zero absorbance with the blank.
5. Read the absorbance (optical density) of the standard and test at the same wavelength.

## Observations

| Tube | Absorbance |
| --- | --- |
| Blank | 0.00 (set) |
| Standard (S) | 0.40 (example) |
| Test (T) | 0.30 (example) |

## Calculations

```text
                         A(test)
 Creatinine (mg/dl)  =  ─────────  ×  Concentration of standard (mg/dl)  ×  Dilution factor
                         A(std)
```

**Worked example:** standard = 1 mg/dl, A(test) = 0.30, A(std) = 0.40, urine diluted 1 in 100:
creatinine = (0.30 / 0.40) × 1 × 100 = **75 mg/dl** of urine.

**24-hour excretion:** if the 24-hour urine volume is 1500 ml (15 dl), creatinine per day = 75 × 15 = 1125 mg ≈ **1.1 g/day**.

Commonly quoted adult urinary creatinine is about 1–2 g per day (depends on muscle mass; ranges vary by laboratory).

## Result

The creatinine content of the given urine sample is ________ mg/dl (________ g/24 h if the 24-hour volume is known).

## Precautions

1. Dilute urine accurately; the dilution factor multiplies any error.
2. Read all tubes after the same time, because the colour keeps changing slowly.
3. Picric acid is explosive when dry – keep it moist and never let it dry on the bench.
4. Glucose, ketones and some drugs (cephalosporins) give a positive interference in Jaffe's reaction.
5. Keep the temperature steady; the reaction is temperature-sensitive.

## Viva questions

1. **What is Jaffe's reaction?** Formation of an orange-red complex between creatinine and alkaline picrate.
2. **From where does creatinine come?** From creatine phosphate of muscle, at a nearly constant rate.
3. **Why is urine diluted before estimation?** Urine creatinine is much higher than the working range of the method.
4. **What is the use of urinary creatinine?** Creatinine clearance (kidney function) and checking that a 24-hour urine collection is complete.
5. **Name substances that interfere.** Acetoacetate, glucose, ascorbic acid, some cephalosporins (non-creatinine chromogens).

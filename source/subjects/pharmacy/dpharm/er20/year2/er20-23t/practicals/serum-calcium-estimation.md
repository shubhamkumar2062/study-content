# Estimation of serum calcium (OCPC method)

**Aim:** To estimate the total calcium in the given (simulated) serum sample by the o-cresolphthalein complexone (OCPC) method.

> Some laboratories use the Arsenazo III method or the older Clark–Collip (permanganate titration) method. Follow your kit insert.

## Principle

In an **alkaline medium**, calcium ions combine with **o-cresolphthalein complexone** to form a **purple (violet-red) complex**. The intensity of the colour is proportional to the calcium concentration and is read at about **570–575 nm** (yellow-green filter) against a reagent blank and a calcium standard (commonly 10 mg/dl; check the label).

**8-Hydroxyquinoline** in the reagent binds magnesium, so magnesium does not interfere.

```text
 Ca2+ + o-cresolphthalein complexone  ──(alkaline pH, 8-hydroxyquinoline)──→  purple complex
```

## Requirements

Colorimeter, micropipettes (20–50 µl and 1000 µl), plastic or acid-washed test tubes, timer. Calcium kit (colour reagent, buffer, standard). Serum sample (not plasma collected in EDTA, oxalate or citrate tubes).

## Procedure

1. Prepare the working reagent by mixing the buffer and colour reagent as the kit states.
2. Pipette (typical volumes):

| | Blank | Standard | Test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Working reagent | 1000 µl | 1000 µl | 1000 µl |
| Distilled water | 20 µl | – | – |
| Calcium standard | – | 20 µl | – |
| Serum | – | – | 20 µl |

3. Mix and let stand at room temperature for about **5 minutes**.
4. Zero the colorimeter with the blank at about 570 nm; read standard and test.

## Observations (example)

| Tube | Absorbance |
| --- | --- |
| Blank | 0.00 (set) |
| Standard (10 mg/dl) | 0.50 |
| Test | 0.47 |

## Calculations

```text
                          A(test)
 Serum calcium (mg/dl) = ────────  ×  Concentration of standard (mg/dl)
                          A(std)
```

**Worked example:** (0.47 / 0.50) × 10 = **9.4 mg/dl**.

To convert to mmol/L, divide mg/dl by 4 (atomic weight 40, divalent): 9.4 / 4 ≈ 2.35 mmol/L.

### Interpretation (commonly quoted; ranges vary by laboratory)

- **About 9–11 mg/dl** (8.5–10.5 in many laboratories) is normal total calcium.
- **Hypocalcaemia:** hypoparathyroidism, vitamin D deficiency (rickets, osteomalacia), chronic kidney disease, low albumin, acute pancreatitis → tetany, cramps.
- **Hypercalcaemia:** hyperparathyroidism, vitamin D excess, cancers with bone spread, multiple myeloma, prolonged immobilisation → kidney stones, confusion, constipation.
- About half of serum calcium is bound to albumin, so a low albumin lowers total calcium without lowering ionised calcium.

## Result

The serum calcium of the given sample is ________ mg/dl.

## Precautions

1. Use **serum**; EDTA, oxalate and citrate bind calcium and give falsely low values.
2. Use plastic or acid-washed glassware; detergents and tap water contain calcium.
3. Avoid prolonged tourniquet application during sample collection.
4. Read within the time the colour is stable.
5. Avoid haemolysed and very lipaemic samples.

## Viva questions

1. **What colour does calcium give with OCPC?** Purple (violet-red) in alkaline medium.
2. **Why is 8-hydroxyquinoline added?** To bind magnesium and prevent its interference.
3. **Why should EDTA plasma not be used?** EDTA chelates calcium.
4. **Name the hormones that control plasma calcium.** Parathyroid hormone, calcitriol, calcitonin.
5. **What is tetany?** Increased nerve and muscle excitability with spasms due to low ionised calcium.

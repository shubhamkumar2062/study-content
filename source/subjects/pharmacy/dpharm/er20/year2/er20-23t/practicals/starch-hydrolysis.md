# Hydrolysis of starch by acid and by salivary amylase

**Aim:** To study the hydrolysis of starch (a) by dilute acid and (b) by salivary amylase, and to follow it with the iodine and Benedict's tests; to find the achromic point of salivary digestion.

## Principle

Starch is a polysaccharide of glucose. It is broken down step by step into smaller molecules:

```text
 Starch → amylodextrin → erythrodextrin → achrodextrin → maltose (→ glucose with acid)
 Iodine:  BLUE          violet/purple    RED-BROWN       no colour   no colour
 Benedict:  –               –               weak +          +           +
```

- **Iodine test:** the blue colour depends on long amylose helices. As chains are shortened, the colour changes from blue → violet → red-brown → no colour.
- **Achromic point:** the stage at which the digest no longer gives any colour with iodine; the time taken to reach it measures the rate of digestion (amylase activity).
- **Benedict's test:** starch is non-reducing; the products (maltose, glucose) are reducing sugars, so Benedict's test becomes positive.

**(a) Acid hydrolysis:** boiling with dilute HCl breaks all α(1→4) and α(1→6) glycosidic bonds; the final product is **glucose**.

**(b) Enzymatic hydrolysis:** **salivary amylase (ptyalin)**, an α-amylase, breaks internal **α(1→4)** bonds at its optimum conditions – **pH about 6.8**, **37 °C**, with **chloride ions as activator**. The main products are **maltose**, some maltotriose and **limit dextrins** (it cannot break α(1→6) branch bonds). It does not produce much free glucose.

## Requirements

Test tubes, beakers, water bath at 37 °C, boiling water bath, porcelain tile (spotting tile), droppers, glass rod, stop-watch.

Reagents: 1% starch solution (freshly prepared), dilute iodine solution, Benedict's qualitative reagent, dilute HCl (about 2 N or as directed), sodium carbonate (or NaOH) to neutralise, phosphate buffer pH 6.8, 1% NaCl solution, saliva (collected by rinsing the mouth with water, then collecting about 5 ml of saliva and diluting it, commonly 1 in 10, with water; filter if needed).

## Procedure

### (a) Acid hydrolysis

1. Take 10 ml of 1% starch solution in a test tube and add 2 ml of dilute HCl.
2. Place in a boiling water bath.
3. Put a drop of iodine in each depression of the spotting tile.
4. Every 2–3 minutes, remove a drop of the mixture with a glass rod and add it to a fresh iodine drop; note the colour.
5. Continue until no colour is given with iodine.
6. Cool a portion, neutralise with sodium carbonate, and do Benedict's test.

### (b) Hydrolysis by salivary amylase

1. Take 5 ml of 1% starch solution, 1 ml of phosphate buffer (pH 6.8) and 1 ml of 1% NaCl in a test tube; keep at 37 °C for a few minutes.
2. Add 1 ml of diluted saliva, mix, and **start the stop-watch**.
3. Keep the tube at 37 °C. Every minute, transfer a drop to a fresh iodine drop on the tile and note the colour.
4. Note the time at which a drop first gives **no colour** with iodine – the **achromic point**.
5. Do Benedict's test on a portion of the digest at the end.
6. **Controls (optional):** (i) boiled saliva (enzyme destroyed) – starch stays blue; (ii) without NaCl – slower digestion; (iii) at 0 °C (ice) – little digestion; (iv) acid pH (add HCl) – digestion stops.

## Observations

### (a) Acid hydrolysis (example)

| Time (min) | Colour with iodine | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Blue | Starch |
| 3 | Blue-violet | Amylodextrin |
| 6 | Red-brown | Erythrodextrin |
| 9 | Pale / no colour | Achrodextrin, sugars |
| End | Benedict's: brick-red precipitate | Glucose formed |

### (b) Salivary amylase (example)

| Time (min) | Colour with iodine |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Blue |
| 1 | Blue |
| 2 | Violet |
| 3 | Red-brown |
| 4 | Light brown |
| 5 | No colour (achromic point) |

Achromic point = **5 minutes** (example; record your own). Benedict's test at the end: positive (maltose formed).

| Control | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Boiled saliva | Blue throughout – enzyme denatured by heat |
| Without chloride | Achromic point delayed – Cl− activates amylase |
| Ice-cold | Little or no digestion – low temperature slows enzyme |
| Acid pH | No digestion – enzyme inactive away from optimum pH |

## Result

1. Starch was hydrolysed by acid to glucose, shown by the loss of iodine colour and a positive Benedict's test.
2. Salivary amylase hydrolysed starch to maltose and dextrins; the achromic point was reached in ________ minutes at 37 °C and pH 6.8.

## Precautions

1. Use freshly prepared starch solution.
2. Use a fresh drop of iodine for each sampling; clean the glass rod each time.
3. Keep the enzyme tube at a steady 37 °C.
4. Neutralise acid before Benedict's test.
5. Saliva should be collected hygienically; each student uses his or her own saliva.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the achromic point?** The stage at which the digest no longer gives a colour with iodine.
2. **What are the optimum conditions for salivary amylase?** pH about 6.8, 37 °C, chloride ions as activator.
3. **What is the end product of acid hydrolysis of starch?** Glucose.
4. **What is the main product of salivary amylase action?** Maltose (with some dextrins).
5. **Why does boiled saliva not digest starch?** Heat denatures the enzyme protein.
6. **Which bonds does α-amylase break?** Internal α(1→4) glycosidic bonds; not α(1→6) branch bonds.

# Unit 12: Introduction to pathology of blood and urine

This unit covers syllabus chapter 13 (6 hours): lymphocytes and platelets, abnormal red cells, and normal and abnormal constituents of urine.

> **About normal values:** blood counts and urine values below are those **commonly quoted** in textbooks and Indian laboratory reports. They are approximate and **ranges vary by laboratory**, age, sex and altitude.

**Clinical pathology** is the branch of pathology that studies body fluids – blood, urine, cerebrospinal fluid, stool – in the laboratory to help diagnosis and treatment.

## 12.1 Blood: a quick background

Blood forms about **7–8% of body weight** (about **5 litres** in an adult). It has two parts:

```text
 BLOOD
  ├─ Plasma (about 55%) – water, proteins (albumin, globulins, fibrinogen),
  │                        glucose, electrolytes, hormones, waste products
  └─ Formed elements (about 45% – haematocrit)
        ├─ Red blood cells (erythrocytes)
        ├─ White blood cells (leucocytes)
        │     ├─ Granulocytes: neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils
        │     └─ Agranulocytes: LYMPHOCYTES, monocytes
        └─ PLATELETS (thrombocytes)
```

| Test | Commonly quoted normal (adults) |
| --- | --- |
| Haemoglobin | Men about 13–17 g/dl; women about 12–15 g/dl |
| RBC count | Men about 4.5–5.5 million/µl; women about 4.0–5.0 million/µl |
| Total WBC count (TLC) | About 4,000–11,000/µl |
| Platelet count | About 1.5–4.5 lakh/µl (150,000–450,000/µl) |
| Haematocrit (PCV) | Men about 40–50%; women about 36–46% |
| ESR (Westergren) | Men about 0–15 mm/hour; women about 0–20 mm/hour |
| Differential count (DLC) | Neutrophils about 40–75%, lymphocytes about 20–40%, monocytes about 2–10%, eosinophils about 1–6%, basophils about 0–1% |
| MCV | About 80–100 fl |
| MCH | About 27–32 pg |
| MCHC | About 32–36 g/dl |
| Reticulocytes | About 0.5–2.5% of red cells |

A **complete blood count (CBC)** report gives most of these values; a **peripheral blood smear** stained with Leishman's or Giemsa stain shows cell shapes.

## 12.2 Lymphocytes and their role in health and disease

### Description

- Small, round white cells with a **large, round, dark-staining nucleus** that fills most of the cell and a thin rim of pale blue cytoplasm.
- Small lymphocytes are about 7–10 µm; large ones up to about 15 µm.
- Formed in the bone marrow and lymphoid organs (thymus, lymph nodes, spleen, tonsils).
- Make up about **20–40%** of white cells in adults (higher in young children).

### Types and role in health

| Type | Matures in | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **B lymphocytes** | Bone marrow | **Humoral immunity:** on contact with an antigen they become **plasma cells**, which make **antibodies (immunoglobulins)**; some become memory cells (basis of vaccination) |
| **T lymphocytes** | Thymus | **Cell-mediated immunity:** helper T cells (CD4) direct the immune response; cytotoxic T cells (CD8) kill virus-infected and cancer cells; regulatory T cells control the response; memory T cells |
| **Natural killer (NK) cells** | Bone marrow | Kill virus-infected and tumour cells without prior exposure |

Functions: defence against viruses, intracellular bacteria (TB), fungi and cancer cells; immunological memory; rejection of transplanted organs; also take part in allergic (delayed hypersensitivity) and autoimmune reactions.

### Role in disease

| Change | Definition (adult, approximate) | Causes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lymphocytosis** | Absolute count above about 4,000/µl | **Viral infections** (infectious mononucleosis, hepatitis, mumps, measles), **whooping cough** (pertussis – very high), **tuberculosis**, typhoid, brucellosis, **chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL)**, acute lymphoblastic leukaemia, lymphomas, thyrotoxicosis |
| **Atypical (reactive) lymphocytes** | Large lymphocytes with abundant cytoplasm | Infectious mononucleosis (Epstein–Barr virus), dengue and other viral fevers |
| **Lymphocytopenia (lymphopenia)** | Absolute count below about 1,000/µl | **HIV/AIDS** (CD4 T cells destroyed), **corticosteroid therapy**, Cushing's syndrome, cancer chemotherapy and radiotherapy, immunosuppressive drugs, severe illness and stress, malnutrition, some inherited immunodeficiencies |

**Clinical uses:** CD4 count monitors HIV infection and guides therapy; lymphocyte count helps separate viral from bacterial infection (bacterial infections usually raise neutrophils).

## 12.3 Platelets and their role in health and disease

### Description

- **Small, disc-shaped, non-nucleated cell fragments** (about 2–4 µm) formed by fragmentation of large bone marrow cells called **megakaryocytes**, under the control of **thrombopoietin** (made by the liver).
- Life span about **7–10 days**; old platelets are removed by the spleen (about one-third of platelets are held in the spleen).
- Contain granules with ADP, serotonin, calcium, fibrinogen, platelet-derived growth factor and other clotting substances.
- **Normal count:** commonly quoted as about **1.5–4.5 lakh/µl**.

### Role in health: haemostasis

```text
 Injury to vessel
   → 1. Vascular spasm (vasoconstriction; serotonin, thromboxane A2)
   → 2. Platelet ADHESION to exposed collagen (von Willebrand factor)
   → 3. Platelet ACTIVATION and release of ADP, thromboxane A2
   → 4. Platelet AGGREGATION → loose PLATELET PLUG (primary haemostasis)
   → 5. Blood COAGULATION on the platelet surface → fibrin mesh → firm clot
   → 6. Clot retraction, then fibrinolysis and repair (growth factors)
```

Other roles: platelet surface provides phospholipid for clotting factors; maintain the lining of blood vessels; wound healing; part in inflammation.

**Drugs acting on platelets:** **aspirin** (irreversibly blocks cyclooxygenase → less thromboxane A2, so its effect lasts for the life of the platelet, about a week), **clopidogrel** (blocks ADP receptors) – used to prevent heart attack and stroke.

### Role in disease

| Change | Definition (approximate) | Causes and effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Thrombocytopenia** | Count below about 1.5 lakh/µl; risk of spontaneous bleeding usually when very low (often quoted below about 20,000/µl) | **Less production:** aplastic anaemia, leukaemia, bone marrow cancer spread, chemotherapy, radiation, B12/folate deficiency, alcohol. **More destruction:** **immune thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP)**, **dengue fever**, malaria, drugs (heparin, quinine, sulphonamides), **DIC**, enlarged spleen (hypersplenism). Effects: **petechiae**, **purpura**, bruises, bleeding gums, nosebleeds, heavy periods, prolonged bleeding time |
| **Thrombocytosis** | Count above about 4.5 lakh/µl | Reactive: infections, inflammation, iron deficiency, after splenectomy, bleeding, cancers. Primary: essential thrombocythaemia and other bone marrow disorders. Risk of clots (and sometimes bleeding) |
| **Platelet function defects** | Normal count, poor function | Aspirin and NSAIDs, uraemia, von Willebrand disease, inherited disorders (Glanzmann's thrombasthenia, Bernard–Soulier syndrome) |

**Tests:** platelet count, **bleeding time** (commonly quoted about 2–7 minutes; prolonged in platelet disorders), clotting time (about 4–10 minutes, method-dependent), clot retraction, peripheral smear.

## 12.4 Erythrocytes: abnormal cells and their significance

### Normal red blood cell

- **Biconcave, non-nucleated disc**, about **7–8 µm** in diameter, with a pale central area (about one-third of the cell) on a stained smear.
- Contains **haemoglobin**; carries O2 and CO2.
- Life span about **120 days**; destroyed in the spleen; formed in red bone marrow under **erythropoietin** (kidney).
- **Normocytic** (normal size) and **normochromic** (normal colour).

### A. Abnormalities of size (anisocytosis)

| Cell | Description | Significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Microcytes** | Smaller than normal (MCV below about 80 fl) | **Iron deficiency anaemia**, **thalassaemia**, anaemia of chronic disease, sideroblastic anaemia |
| **Macrocytes** | Larger than normal (MCV above about 100 fl) | **Megaloblastic anaemia** (vitamin B12 or folate deficiency – oval macrocytes), liver disease, alcoholism, hypothyroidism, reticulocytosis |
| Anisocytosis | Marked variation in size | Most anaemias (high red cell distribution width, RDW) |

### B. Abnormalities of colour (haemoglobin content)

| Cell | Description | Significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hypochromic** | Larger pale centre, less haemoglobin (low MCH, MCHC) | **Iron deficiency anaemia**, thalassaemia |
| **Polychromasia** | Bluish-grey young cells (reticulocytes) | Active marrow response – after bleeding, haemolysis, start of treatment |
| Hyperchromic (apparent) | Dense, no pale centre | Spherocytes |

### C. Abnormalities of shape (poikilocytosis)

| Cell | Description | Significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sickle cells (drepanocytes)** | Crescent / sickle shaped | **Sickle cell anaemia** (HbS); common in some tribal populations of central and western India |
| **Spherocytes** | Small, round, dense, no central pallor | **Hereditary spherocytosis**, autoimmune haemolytic anaemia, burns |
| **Elliptocytes / ovalocytes** | Oval or elliptical | Hereditary elliptocytosis; iron deficiency; megaloblastic anaemia (oval macrocytes) |
| **Target cells (codocytes)** | Central dark spot surrounded by a pale ring – like a target | **Thalassaemia**, liver disease, HbC disease, after splenectomy |
| **Schistocytes (fragmented cells, helmet cells)** | Broken fragments of red cells | **Microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia** – DIC, haemolytic uraemic syndrome, thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura, artificial heart valves, severe burns |
| **Tear-drop cells (dacrocytes)** | Shaped like a tear drop | **Myelofibrosis**, bone marrow infiltration, thalassaemia |
| **Acanthocytes (spur cells)** | Few irregular spiky projections | Severe liver disease, abetalipoproteinaemia |
| **Echinocytes (burr cells, crenated cells)** | Many small regular projections | Uraemia (kidney failure), artefact of old blood |
| **Stomatocytes** | Slit-like (mouth-shaped) central pallor | Alcoholism, liver disease, hereditary stomatocytosis |

### D. Red cell inclusions and other abnormal cells

| Finding | Description | Significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nucleated red cells (normoblasts)** | Immature red cells with nuclei in blood | Severe haemolysis, thalassaemia major, bone marrow infiltration, newborns |
| **Reticulocytes** (increased) | Young red cells with a network of RNA (seen with supravital stain) | **Haemolysis**, after acute blood loss, response to iron/B12 treatment; low in aplastic anaemia and untreated deficiency |
| **Howell–Jolly bodies** | Small, round nuclear remnants | **After splenectomy**, poor spleen function (sickle cell disease), megaloblastic anaemia |
| **Basophilic stippling** | Fine blue dots (RNA) | **Lead poisoning**, thalassaemia |
| **Heinz bodies** | Denatured haemoglobin (seen with supravital stain) | **G6PD deficiency** (after oxidant drugs such as primaquine, dapsone, sulphonamides), unstable haemoglobins |
| **Pappenheimer bodies** | Iron-containing granules | Sideroblastic anaemia, after splenectomy |
| **Malaria parasites** | Ring forms, gametocytes inside red cells | **Malaria** – diagnosis by thick and thin smear |
| **Rouleaux formation** | Red cells stacked like coins | High plasma proteins – **multiple myeloma**, chronic inflammation (also raises ESR) |

### Significance in anaemia (summary)

| Type of anaemia | Red cell picture | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Microcytic hypochromic** | Small, pale cells | Iron deficiency, thalassaemia |
| **Macrocytic (megaloblastic)** | Large, oval cells; hypersegmented neutrophils | B12 or folate deficiency |
| **Normocytic normochromic** | Normal-looking cells | Acute blood loss, aplastic anaemia, chronic disease, kidney failure |
| **Haemolytic** | Spherocytes, sickle cells, schistocytes, many reticulocytes | Hereditary spherocytosis, sickle cell disease, G6PD deficiency, malaria |

**Polycythaemia** (too many red cells) occurs at high altitude, in chronic lung and heart disease and in polycythaemia vera.

## 12.5 Urine: formation and normal characteristics

Urine is formed by **glomerular filtration**, **tubular reabsorption** and **tubular secretion** in the nephrons. Its examination is simple, cheap and gives information about the kidneys, the urinary tract and the whole body (diabetes, liver disease).

**Sample:** a clean **mid-stream** early-morning specimen is best for routine examination (more concentrated); a **24-hour collection** for quantitative tests; examine within about 1–2 hours or refrigerate (bacteria change pH, break down glucose, and cells and casts disintegrate).

### Physical characteristics of normal urine

| Property | Commonly quoted normal | Abnormal and significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Volume** | About 1–2 litres per day | Polyuria (diabetes mellitus and insipidus, diuretics); oliguria/anuria (dehydration, shock, kidney failure) |
| **Colour** | Pale yellow to amber (pigment **urochrome**; also urobilin) | Colourless (dilute, diabetes insipidus); dark yellow (concentrated, fever); **red/brown** (blood, haemoglobin, myoglobin; beetroot; rifampicin gives orange-red); dark brown–yellow with yellow froth (**bilirubin**); **black on standing** (alkaptonuria, melanin); milky (pus, chyle, phosphates); blue-green (methylene blue) |
| **Appearance** | Clear when fresh | Turbid (pus cells, bacteria, phosphates, urates, mucus) |
| **Odour** | Faintly aromatic; ammoniacal on standing (bacteria break urea) | **Fruity** (ketones – diabetic ketoacidosis), **mousy** (phenylketonuria), maple syrup (MSUD), foul (infection) |
| **Reaction (pH)** | About 4.5–8.0, usually slightly **acidic (about 6)** | Acidic: high-protein diet, acidosis, diabetes, starvation, fever; alkaline: vegetarian diet, after meals (alkaline tide), urinary infection with urea-splitting bacteria (Proteus), alkalosis, old sample |
| **Specific gravity** | About 1.003–1.030 (usually 1.015–1.025) | High: dehydration, diabetes mellitus (glucose), proteinuria, contrast media; low: diabetes insipidus, excess water; fixed at about 1.010: chronic kidney failure |
| **Deposits** | Few cells, occasional crystals | See microscopy below |

## 12.6 Normal constituents of urine

**Daily urinary solids** are about 50–60 g, of which about half are organic and half inorganic. Values below are approximate per 24 hours on a usual diet and **vary with diet, fluid intake and laboratory method**.

### Organic constituents

| Constituent | Approximate 24-hour amount | Source and significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Urea** | About 20–30 g (the largest organic constituent; about 80–90% of urinary nitrogen) | End product of protein breakdown (urea cycle). **Increased** with a high-protein diet, fever, diabetes, starvation (tissue breakdown), steroid therapy; **decreased** in liver failure, kidney failure, low-protein diet, growth and pregnancy (positive nitrogen balance) |
| **Creatinine** | About 1–2 g (fairly constant for a person; depends on muscle mass) | From creatine phosphate of muscle; used to check completeness of a 24-hour collection and for creatinine clearance. Decreased in kidney failure and muscle wasting |
| **Uric acid** | About 0.4–0.8 g (about 250–750 mg; varies with diet) | End product of purine breakdown; increased in gout, leukaemia, chemotherapy, high-purine diet (meat, organ meats) |
| **Creatine** | Very small amount in adult men; more in children, pregnancy and women | **Increased (creatinuria)** in muscle diseases (muscular dystrophy), hyperthyroidism, starvation, diabetes |
| **Ammonia** (as ammonium salts) | About 0.5–1 g | Made by kidney tubules to excrete acid; increased in acidosis (diabetic ketoacidosis, starvation) |
| **Hippuric acid** | Small amounts | Glycine conjugate of benzoic acid (from fruits, preservatives) |
| Amino acids | Small amounts | Increased in liver failure and aminoacidurias |
| **Urobilinogen** | Traces (commonly up to about 1–4 mg per day) | From bilirubin by gut bacteria; increased in haemolytic jaundice, absent in complete biliary obstruction |
| Pigments | Urochrome, urobilin | Give the yellow colour |
| Others | Oxalic acid, citric acid, small amounts of hormones, vitamins and enzymes | – |

### Inorganic constituents

| Constituent | Approximate 24-hour amount | Significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Chloride** (mostly as NaCl) | About 110–250 mEq (varies greatly with salt intake) | Decreased in vomiting, diarrhoea, heavy sweating, low salt intake and conditions that retain salt (heart failure, pneumonia); increased with high salt intake, diuretics and Addison's disease; always interpret with the diet |
| **Sodium** | About 100–250 mEq | Depends on intake; regulated by aldosterone |
| **Potassium** | About 25–100 mEq | Increased with diuretics, alkalosis, Cushing's syndrome |
| **Phosphates** | About 0.8–1.3 g (as phosphorus) | Increased in hyperparathyroidism and bone disease; decreased in hypoparathyroidism, rickets; alkaline urine may deposit phosphate crystals |
| **Sulphates** | About 1–3 g | From sulphur amino acids (protein breakdown) |
| **Calcium** | About 100–300 mg | Increased in hyperparathyroidism, vitamin D excess, bone cancer, immobilisation (risk of stones); decreased in hypoparathyroidism, rickets |
| Magnesium | Small amounts | – |
| Bicarbonate | Small (more in alkalosis) | – |

## 12.7 Abnormal constituents of urine and their significance

| Constituent | Test (commonly used) | Normal | Significance when present |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Glucose (glycosuria)** | **Benedict's test** (reducing sugar – green, yellow, orange, brick-red precipitate); **glucose oxidase dipstick** (specific) | Absent by routine tests | **Diabetes mellitus** (blood glucose above the renal threshold, commonly quoted about 180 mg/dl); **renal glycosuria** (low threshold, benign; also in pregnancy); alimentary glycosuria after a heavy sugar meal; stress, Cushing's syndrome, thyrotoxicosis, SGLT2 inhibitor drugs (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin) |
| **Other reducing sugars** | Benedict's positive but glucose oxidase negative | Absent | Galactose (galactosaemia), fructose, lactose (pregnancy, lactation), pentoses; also reducing drugs and homogentisic acid |
| **Proteins (proteinuria)** | **Heat and acetic acid test** (white cloud), **sulphosalicylic acid test**, dipstick (albumin) | Less than about 150 mg/day (not detected by routine tests) | **Glomerular disease** – nephrotic syndrome (heavy, more than about 3.5 g/day), glomerulonephritis, diabetic nephropathy, hypertension, pre-eclampsia in pregnancy; **tubular disease**; fever, heavy exercise, standing (orthostatic, benign); urinary infection |
| **Bence-Jones proteins** | Precipitate on warming to about 40–60 °C and **redissolve near boiling**, reappearing on cooling; electrophoresis | Absent | **Multiple myeloma** (light chains of immunoglobulins) |
| **Ketone bodies (ketonuria)** | **Rothera's test** (sodium nitroprusside + ammonia – **purple/permanganate-coloured ring**); Gerhardt's ferric chloride test; dipstick | Absent | **Uncontrolled diabetes mellitus** (diabetic ketoacidosis), **starvation**, prolonged vomiting, very low carbohydrate diet, severe fever in children, glycogen storage disease |
| **Bilirubin (bilirubinuria)** | **Fouchet's test** (barium chloride to adsorb, then Fouchet's reagent – green/blue colour); Gmelin's test (play of colours with nitric acid); yellow froth on shaking; dipstick | Absent | **Hepatic and obstructive jaundice** (conjugated bilirubin); absent in haemolytic jaundice |
| **Bile salts** | **Hay's sulphur test** (sulphur powder sinks, because bile salts lower surface tension) | Absent | **Obstructive jaundice**, also hepatic jaundice |
| **Urobilinogen** (excess) | **Ehrlich's aldehyde test** (red colour) | Traces | **Increased:** haemolytic jaundice, early hepatitis; **absent:** complete obstructive jaundice, broad-spectrum antibiotics (gut bacteria killed) |
| **Blood (haematuria)** / haemoglobin | **Benzidine test** (older; benzidine is carcinogenic and now rarely used) or **ortho-toluidine/peroxidase dipstick**; microscopy for intact red cells | Absent (very few red cells) | **Haematuria** (intact red cells): stones, infection, glomerulonephritis, tumours of kidney or bladder, trauma, bleeding disorders, anticoagulant excess, menstrual contamination. **Haemoglobinuria** (free haemoglobin): intravascular haemolysis – incompatible transfusion, severe malaria (blackwater fever), G6PD deficiency. **Myoglobinuria:** muscle injury (crush injury, rhabdomyolysis) |
| **Pus cells (pyuria)** | Microscopy (more than about 5 per high-power field); leucocyte esterase dipstick | 0–5 per high-power field | **Urinary tract infection**, kidney infection (pyelonephritis), stones |
| **Nitrite** | Dipstick | Negative | Gram-negative bacterial infection (E. coli convert nitrate to nitrite) |
| **Casts** | Microscopy | Occasional hyaline casts | Cylindrical moulds of the tubules: **red cell casts** (glomerulonephritis), **white cell casts** (pyelonephritis), **granular and waxy casts** (chronic kidney disease), fatty casts (nephrotic syndrome); hyaline casts after exercise |
| **Crystals** | Microscopy | Some crystals normal (calcium oxalate, urates, phosphates) | Excess with stones; **cystine** (cystinuria), tyrosine and leucine (liver failure), sulphonamide crystals (drugs) |
| **Chyle (chyluria)** | Milky urine that clears with ether | Absent | Lymphatic obstruction – **filariasis** |
| **Porphyrins** | Urine turns port-wine coloured on standing | Absent | Porphyrias, lead poisoning |
| **Phenylpyruvic acid** | Ferric chloride test (transient green) | Absent | **Phenylketonuria** |
| **Homogentisic acid** | Darkens on standing or with alkali | Absent | **Alkaptonuria** |

### Urine reagent strips (dipsticks)

Plastic strips with small pads for glucose, protein, ketones, bilirubin, urobilinogen, blood, pH, specific gravity, nitrite and leucocytes. The strip is dipped briefly, excess removed, and colours are read at the stated time against the chart on the bottle. They are quick and widely used in clinics and pharmacies; positive results are confirmed in the laboratory. Pharmacists may counsel diabetic patients on using glucose and ketone strips.

### Common interference by drugs

- Large doses of **vitamin C** can give false-negative glucose oxidase strips and false-positive Benedict's tests.
- **Rifampicin** colours urine orange-red; **metronidazole** may darken it; **methylene blue** turns it blue-green; **levodopa** may darken it on standing.
- **Salicylates, cephalosporins** and some other drugs may give false-positive Benedict's test.

## Remember

- Lymphocytes 20–40% (about): B cells → plasma cells → antibodies; T cells → cell-mediated immunity; lymphocytosis in viral infections, whooping cough, TB, CLL; lymphopenia in HIV, steroids.
- Platelets about 1.5–4.5 lakh/µl, life about 7–10 days, from megakaryocytes; adhesion → aggregation → plug; thrombocytopenia in dengue, ITP, aplastic anaemia, leukaemia → petechiae, purpura, bleeding.
- Normal RBC: biconcave, 7–8 µm, 120 days. Microcytes/hypochromia → iron deficiency, thalassaemia; macrocytes → B12/folate deficiency; sickle cells → HbS; spherocytes → hereditary spherocytosis; target cells → thalassaemia, liver disease; schistocytes → DIC; Howell–Jolly bodies → after splenectomy; basophilic stippling → lead; Heinz bodies → G6PD deficiency.
- Normal urine: about 1–2 L/day, pale yellow (urochrome), slightly acidic (pH about 6), specific gravity about 1.003–1.030; urea about 20–30 g/day, creatinine about 1–2 g/day.
- Abnormal: glucose (Benedict's) – diabetes; protein (heat–acetic acid) – kidney disease; ketones (Rothera's) – diabetic ketoacidosis, starvation; bilirubin (Fouchet's) and bile salts (Hay's) – hepatic/obstructive jaundice; urobilinogen (Ehrlich's) – haemolytic jaundice; Bence-Jones protein – multiple myeloma.

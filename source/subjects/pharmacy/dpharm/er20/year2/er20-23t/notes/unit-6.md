# Unit 6: Vitamins

This unit covers syllabus chapter 7 (6 hours).

## 6.1 Definition

**Vitamins** are organic compounds needed in **very small amounts** in the diet for normal growth, health and metabolism. The body cannot make them, or cannot make enough of them, so they must come from food. They do not give energy themselves, but many of them act as **coenzymes** in energy-releasing reactions. Lack of a vitamin causes a specific **deficiency disease**.

The term "vitamine" (vital amine) was given by Casimir Funk (1912); the "e" was dropped when it was found that not all vitamins are amines.

**Recommended dietary allowance (RDA):** the daily intake that meets the needs of nearly all healthy people of a given age and sex. In India these values are set by **ICMR–NIN** (latest revision 2020). The figures in this unit are **approximate adult values** commonly quoted in textbooks; exact values differ by age, sex, pregnancy, lactation and activity, and by the edition of the recommendations.

## 6.3 Fat-soluble vitamins

### Vitamin A (retinol, anti-xerophthalmic vitamin)

- **Chemical nature:** an alcohol (retinol) with a β-ionone ring and a polyene side chain. Active forms: **retinol**, **retinal** (aldehyde) and **retinoic acid**. **β-carotene** from plants is the provitamin; one molecule can give two of retinal.
- **Sources:** animal – fish liver oils (cod and shark liver oil), liver, egg yolk, milk, butter, ghee. Plant (as carotenes) – carrots, green leafy vegetables, papaya, mango, pumpkin.
- **Functions:**
  1. **Vision:** 11-cis-retinal combines with the protein opsin to form **rhodopsin** (visual purple) in the rods. Light changes 11-cis-retinal to all-trans-retinal, which sends a nerve signal (Wald's visual cycle). Needed for vision in dim light.
  2. Keeps **epithelial tissues** (skin, cornea, linings of the respiratory, gut and urinary tracts) healthy; retinoic acid controls cell growth and differentiation.
  3. Growth of bones and teeth; reproduction; immunity (the "anti-infective vitamin").
  4. β-carotene is an antioxidant.
- **RDA:** about 600–1000 µg retinol (activity) equivalents per day for adults.
- **Deficiency:**
  - **Night blindness (nyctalopia)** – the earliest sign.
  - **Xerophthalmia:** conjunctival xerosis (dry conjunctiva) → **Bitot's spots** (foamy triangular patches on the conjunctiva) → corneal xerosis → **keratomalacia** (softening and ulceration of the cornea) → blindness. A major cause of preventable childhood blindness in India; the national programme gives vitamin A doses to children.
  - Dry, rough skin (follicular hyperkeratosis); frequent infections; poor growth.
- **Excess (hypervitaminosis A):** headache, vomiting, bone pain, liver damage; birth defects in pregnancy (retinoid drugs like isotretinoin are contraindicated in pregnancy).

### Vitamin D (calciferol, anti-rachitic vitamin, "sunshine vitamin")

- **Chemical nature:** a steroid derivative. **D2 (ergocalciferol)** from plants and yeast; **D3 (cholecalciferol)** made in the skin from **7-dehydrocholesterol** by sunlight (UV-B).
- **Activation:** cholecalciferol → (liver, 25-hydroxylase) → **25-hydroxycholecalciferol** (calcidiol, the storage and measured form) → (kidney, 1-α-hydroxylase) → **1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol (calcitriol)**, the **active hormone**. Parathyroid hormone stimulates the kidney step.
- **Sources:** sunlight (main source), fish liver oils, fatty fish, egg yolk, fortified milk and oil.
- **Functions:** raises plasma calcium and phosphate by increasing **calcium absorption from the intestine**, reabsorption in the kidney and mobilisation from bone; needed for **mineralisation of bones and teeth**.
- **RDA:** about 10–15 µg (400–600 IU) per day for adults.
- **Deficiency:**
  - **Rickets** in children – soft, deformed bones: bow legs, knock knees, bossing of skull, rickety rosary (swollen rib ends), pigeon chest, delayed teething.
  - **Osteomalacia** in adults – soft bones, bone pain, fractures.
  - Low calcium may cause tetany.
- **Excess:** hypercalcaemia, calcium deposits in kidneys and blood vessels, kidney stones.

### Vitamin E (tocopherol, "anti-sterility vitamin")

- **Chemical nature:** tocopherols (α, β, γ, δ); **α-tocopherol** is the most active.
- **Sources:** vegetable oils (wheat germ, sunflower, cottonseed), nuts, seeds, green leafy vegetables.
- **Functions:** the most important **fat-soluble antioxidant**; protects polyunsaturated fatty acids of cell membranes and LDL from oxidation by free radicals; protects red cells from haemolysis; works with selenium; also used as an antioxidant in oils and pharmaceutical preparations.
- **RDA:** about 8–10 mg α-tocopherol per day for adults (requirement rises with PUFA intake).
- **Deficiency (rare):** haemolytic anaemia in premature babies, nerve damage (ataxia, peripheral neuropathy), muscle weakness. In rats, deficiency causes sterility, which gave it its old name.

### Vitamin K (phylloquinone, anti-haemorrhagic vitamin)

- **Chemical nature:** naphthoquinone derivatives. **K1 (phylloquinone)** from green plants; **K2 (menaquinones)** made by intestinal bacteria; **K3 (menadione)** synthetic, water-soluble.
- **Sources:** green leafy vegetables (spinach, cabbage), soybean oil, liver; gut bacteria.
- **Functions:** acts as a coenzyme for **γ-carboxylation of glutamic acid** residues in clotting factors **II (prothrombin), VII, IX and X** (and proteins C and S). Carboxylated factors bind calcium and take part in clotting. Also needed for bone proteins (osteocalcin).
- **RDA:** about 55–120 µg per day for adults (sources differ).
- **Deficiency:** **bleeding tendency** with prolonged prothrombin time – bruising, bleeding from gums and gut. **Haemorrhagic disease of the newborn** (newborns have little vitamin K and a sterile gut), so a single dose of vitamin K is given at birth. Also seen with fat malabsorption, obstructive jaundice and long antibiotic courses.
- **Drug link:** **warfarin** and dicoumarol are vitamin K antagonists (block vitamin K epoxide reductase); vitamin K is the antidote for warfarin overdose.

| **B1 Thiamine** (anti-beriberi, anti-neuritic) | Pyrimidine + thiazole ring | **Thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP)** | Oxidative decarboxylation (pyruvate dehydrogenase, α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase); transketolase of HMP shunt; carbohydrate metabolism and nerve function | 1–1.5 mg (about 0.5 mg per 1000 kcal) | **Beriberi**; **Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome** in alcoholics |

| **B3 Niacin** (nicotinic acid / nicotinamide; pellagra-preventing factor) | Pyridine derivative; can be made from tryptophan (about 60 mg tryptophan → 1 mg niacin) | **NAD+**, **NADP+** | Coenzymes of many dehydrogenases (glycolysis, TCA, β-oxidation); NADPH for synthesis | 12–20 mg niacin equivalents | **Pellagra** – the "3 Ds": **dermatitis** (on sun-exposed skin, Casal's necklace), **diarrhoea**, **dementia** (and death if untreated); seen in maize/jowar eaters, Hartnup disease, carcinoid syndrome, isoniazid therapy |

### More on two important B-vitamin diseases

**Beriberi (thiamine deficiency)** – common where polished rice is the staple food.

| Type | Main features |
| --- | --- |
| **Dry beriberi** | Peripheral neuritis: numbness, tingling, muscle wasting, difficulty in walking |
| **Wet beriberi** | Heart involvement: oedema, palpitations, breathlessness, heart failure |
| Infantile beriberi | In breast-fed infants of deficient mothers; may cause sudden heart failure |
| **Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome** | In chronic alcoholics: confusion, ataxia, eye-movement paralysis, memory loss |

**Megaloblastic anaemia (folate or B12 deficiency):** both are needed for DNA synthesis. Without them, red cell precursors grow but cannot divide properly, so large, immature cells (megaloblasts) appear in the bone marrow and large red cells (macrocytes) in blood. B12 deficiency also damages nerves; giving folic acid alone to a B12-deficient patient can correct the anaemia but let the nerve damage progress, so the cause must be found first.

## 6.5 Vitamin C (ascorbic acid, anti-scorbutic vitamin)

- **Chemical nature:** a six-carbon compound (lactone) related to glucose; a strong **reducing agent** and antioxidant; easily destroyed by heat, air, alkali and storage. Humans cannot make it because they lack the enzyme gulonolactone oxidase.
- **Coenzyme form:** acts itself (no special coenzyme form) as a reducing cofactor.
- **Sources:** **amla (Indian gooseberry, one of the richest sources)**, guava, citrus fruits (orange, lemon), tomatoes, green leafy vegetables, sprouted pulses. Very little in milk, cereals and meat.
- **Functions:**
  1. **Collagen synthesis:** needed for **hydroxylation of proline and lysine** in collagen, which gives collagen its strength (wound healing, healthy gums, blood vessels, bones).
  2. Increases **iron absorption** (keeps iron in the ferrous form).
  3. Antioxidant; protects vitamins A, E and folate.
  4. Synthesis of carnitine, noradrenaline, bile acids and steroid hormones; conversion of folic acid to its active form.
  5. Supports immunity.
- **RDA:** about 40–80 mg per day for adults; more in smokers, pregnancy and lactation.
- **Deficiency – scurvy:**
  - Spongy, swollen, bleeding gums; loosening of teeth.
  - Bleeding under the skin (petechiae, bruises), perifollicular haemorrhages, corkscrew hair.
  - Poor wound healing; anaemia; bone and joint pain.
  - In infants (Barlow's disease): painful swollen limbs, bleeding under the bone covering.

## 6.7 Pharmaceutical points

- Vitamins are lost by heat (C, B1, folate), light (riboflavin, A) and air (A, C, E); they need light-resistant, airtight containers and cool storage. Formulations often carry an overage to cover losses.
- Hypervitaminosis is mainly a risk with fat-soluble vitamins A and D.
- Some drug–vitamin interactions: isoniazid–B6; methotrexate and phenytoin–folate; warfarin–vitamin K; metformin–B12; long antibiotic use–vitamin K; mineral oil laxatives–fat-soluble vitamins.

## Remember

- Fat-soluble A, D, E, K (stored, can be toxic); water-soluble B-complex and C (not stored except B12, act as coenzymes).
- A → rhodopsin, night blindness, Bitot's spots; D → calcitriol, rickets/osteomalacia; E → antioxidant; K → factors II, VII, IX, X, bleeding.
- B1 (TPP) beriberi; B2 (FMN, FAD) angular stomatitis; B3 (NAD, NADP) pellagra (3 Ds); B5 (CoA) burning feet; B6 (PLP) convulsions, isoniazid; B7 (biotin) carboxylation, raw egg avidin; B9 (THF) megaloblastic anaemia, neural tube defects; B12 (cobalt, intrinsic factor) pernicious anaemia.
- C → hydroxylation of proline/lysine in collagen, iron absorption; deficiency scurvy; amla is a rich source.

# Unit 2: Proteins and amino acids

This unit covers syllabus chapter 3 (5 hours).

## 2.1 Definition of proteins

**Proteins** are large organic molecules (macromolecules) made of **α-amino acids** joined together by **peptide bonds**. They contain carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and **nitrogen** (about 16% by weight); most also contain sulphur, and some contain phosphorus, iron, copper or zinc.

The name comes from the Greek word "proteios", meaning "of first importance". Proteins form enzymes, hormones, antibodies, transport molecules and the structure of the body.

**Peptide bond:** the bond formed between the carboxyl group (–COOH) of one amino acid and the amino group (–NH2) of the next, with the loss of one molecule of water.

```text
   H2N–CH(R1)–COOH  +  H2N–CH(R2)–COOH
                    ↓  (– H2O)
   H2N–CH(R1)–CO–NH–CH(R2)–COOH      a dipeptide
               └──┬──┘
               peptide bond (–CO–NH–)
```

- 2 amino acids = dipeptide; 3 = tripeptide; up to about 10 = oligopeptide; many = polypeptide.
- A chain of more than about 50 amino acids is usually called a protein.
- Every chain has an **N-terminal** end (free –NH2) and a **C-terminal** end (free –COOH).

## 2.2 Amino acids: definition and structure

**Amino acids** are organic compounds that contain an amino group (–NH2) and a carboxyl group (–COOH). In the amino acids of proteins both groups are attached to the same carbon, the **α-carbon**; hence they are called **α-amino acids**.

```text
            R          (side chain – different in each amino acid)
            |
     H2N – Cα – COOH
            |
            H
```

- About **20 standard amino acids** are coded by DNA and found in proteins.
- All except **glycine** (R = H) have a chiral α-carbon, so they are optically active; amino acids of human proteins are of the **L-form**.
- **Zwitterion:** in solution an amino acid carries both a positive (–NH3+) and a negative (–COO−) charge. The pH at which the net charge is zero is the **isoelectric pH (pI)**; at this pH solubility is lowest. This is used to precipitate proteins (for example casein precipitates near pH 4.6).

## 2.3 Classification of amino acids

### A. Based on the chemical nature of the side chain

| Group | Amino acids |
| --- | --- |
| Aliphatic (simple) | Glycine, alanine |
| Branched chain | Valine, leucine, isoleucine |
| Hydroxyl-containing | Serine, threonine (tyrosine also has –OH) |
| Sulphur-containing | Cysteine, methionine |
| Acidic (extra –COOH) and their amides | Aspartic acid, glutamic acid; asparagine, glutamine |
| Basic (extra amino or basic group) | Lysine, arginine, histidine |
| Aromatic (benzene ring) | Phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan |
| Imino acid | Proline (the nitrogen is part of a ring) |

They can also be grouped by **polarity**: non-polar (alanine, valine, leucine, isoleucine, phenylalanine, methionine, tryptophan, proline), polar uncharged (serine, threonine, cysteine, tyrosine, asparagine, glutamine, glycine is often placed here), polar negatively charged (aspartate, glutamate) and polar positively charged (lysine, arginine, histidine).

### B. Based on nutritional requirement

| Type | Meaning | Amino acids |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Essential** (indispensable) | Cannot be made in the body; must come from the diet | Valine, leucine, isoleucine, threonine, methionine, phenylalanine, tryptophan, lysine |
| **Semi-essential** | Made in the body but not enough during growth (children, pregnancy) | Arginine, histidine |
| **Non-essential** | Can be made in the body | Glycine, alanine, serine, cysteine, aspartic acid, glutamic acid, asparagine, glutamine, tyrosine, proline |

Memory aid for the essential and semi-essential ones: **"PVT TIM HALL"** – Phenylalanine, Valine, Tryptophan, Threonine, Isoleucine, Methionine, Histidine, Arginine, Leucine, Lysine. (Many books now count histidine as essential for adults also.)

### C. Based on metabolic fate (useful later in Unit 9)

- **Purely ketogenic:** leucine, lysine (give acetyl CoA or acetoacetate).
- **Both glucogenic and ketogenic:** isoleucine, phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan, threonine.
- **Glucogenic:** all the rest (can form glucose).

## 2.4 Classification of proteins

### A. Based on composition

```text
                         Proteins
        ┌───────────────────┼────────────────────┐
   Simple proteins    Conjugated proteins    Derived proteins
   (only amino acids) (protein + non-protein  (formed by partial
                       prosthetic group)       breakdown of proteins)
```

**1. Simple proteins** – give only amino acids on hydrolysis (see the solubility table below).

**2. Conjugated (compound) proteins** – a simple protein joined to a non-protein part called the **prosthetic group**.

| Class | Prosthetic group | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glycoproteins | Carbohydrate | Mucin, immunoglobulins, some hormones (FSH, LH) |
| Lipoproteins | Lipid | LDL, HDL, chylomicrons |
| Nucleoproteins | Nucleic acid | Chromatin (DNA + histones), ribosomes |
| Phosphoproteins | Phosphoric acid | Casein (milk), vitellin (egg yolk) |
| Chromoproteins | Coloured pigment | Haemoglobin (haem), cytochromes, rhodopsin |
| Metalloproteins | Metal ion | Ceruloplasmin (copper), ferritin (iron), carbonic anhydrase (zinc) |

**3. Derived proteins**

- **Primary derived proteins** (slight change, little hydrolysis): coagulated proteins (cooked egg white), proteans, metaproteins.
- **Secondary derived proteins** (more hydrolysis, smaller pieces): proteoses → peptones → peptides.

### B. Based on solubility (classes of simple proteins)

| Class | Soluble in | Coagulated by heat? | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Albumins** | Water and dilute salt solutions | Yes | Serum albumin, egg albumin (ovalbumin), lactalbumin |
| **Globulins** | Dilute salt solutions; insoluble in pure water | Yes | Serum globulins, immunoglobulins, myosin, ovoglobulin |
| **Glutelins** | Dilute acids and alkalis | Yes | Glutenin (wheat), oryzenin (rice) |
| **Prolamines** | 70–80% alcohol | No | Gliadin (wheat), zein (maize) |
| **Histones** | Water and dilute acids; basic proteins | No | Proteins bound to DNA in chromatin |
| **Protamines** | Water; strongly basic, small | No | Salmine (salmon sperm); protamine sulphate is used to reverse heparin |
| **Scleroproteins (albuminoids)** | Insoluble in most solvents; fibrous | No | Keratin (hair, nails), collagen (skin, bone, tendon), elastin |

### C. Based on shape

- **Fibrous proteins:** long, thread-like, insoluble, structural – keratin, collagen, elastin, fibrin.
- **Globular proteins:** compact, round, soluble, functional – enzymes, haemoglobin, albumin, insulin, antibodies.

## 2.5 Structure of proteins: four levels of organisation

```text
 Primary      ─ Ala─Gly─Ser─Leu─Lys─...      (sequence)
 Secondary    ─ α-helix  ⌇⌇⌇⌇   or β-sheet  ⇉⇉⇉   (local H-bonded shape)
 Tertiary     ─ whole chain folded into a 3-D shape  (◯)
 Quaternary   ─ several folded chains packed together (◯◯/◯◯ e.g. haemoglobin)
```

### Primary structure

- The **sequence** (order) of amino acids in the polypeptide chain, from the N-terminal to the C-terminal end.
- Held by **peptide bonds** (covalent); disulphide bonds also belong here.
- It is decided by the gene and it decides all higher levels. A single change can cause disease: in **sickle cell anaemia**, glutamic acid at position 6 of the β-chain of haemoglobin is replaced by valine.
- Insulin was the first protein whose sequence was found (Frederick Sanger): two chains, A (21 amino acids) and B (30), linked by disulphide bonds.

### Secondary structure

The regular, local folding of the chain, held by **hydrogen bonds** between the C=O and N–H of peptide bonds.

- **α-helix:** a right-handed coil; hydrogen bonds run along the axis between each C=O and the N–H four residues ahead; about 3.6 amino acids per turn. Found in keratin and in parts of most globular proteins (myoglobin is mostly α-helix). Proline breaks the helix.
- **β-pleated sheet:** extended chains lying side by side, joined by hydrogen bonds between neighbouring chains; can be parallel or antiparallel. Found in silk fibroin.
- **Collagen** has its own special **triple helix** (three chains wound together; glycine at every third position).

### Tertiary structure

The overall **three-dimensional folding** of one complete polypeptide chain. Held by bonds between side chains:

| Bond | Formed between |
| --- | --- |
| Disulphide bonds (covalent, strongest) | Two cysteine –SH groups |
| Hydrogen bonds | Polar side chains |
| Ionic bonds (salt bridges) | Positively and negatively charged side chains (lysine and aspartate) |
| Hydrophobic interactions | Non-polar side chains, which hide inside the protein, away from water |
| Van der Waals forces | Close atoms |

The active site of an enzyme and the binding site of a receptor are formed at this level. Myoglobin is a single chain with only tertiary structure.

### Quaternary structure

The arrangement of **two or more polypeptide chains (subunits)** into one functional protein, held by the same non-covalent bonds (sometimes disulphide bonds). Example: **haemoglobin** = 2 α + 2 β chains (a tetramer); lactate dehydrogenase (4 subunits); immunoglobulin G (2 heavy + 2 light chains).

### Denaturation

**Denaturation** is the loss of the natural (native) secondary, tertiary and quaternary structure, without breaking peptide bonds. The protein loses its biological activity.

- Causes: heat, strong acids and alkalis, heavy metal salts (Hg, Pb, Ag), alcohol, urea, detergents, UV light, vigorous shaking.
- Effects: decreased solubility, loss of enzyme or hormone activity, easier digestion (cooked egg).
- Usually irreversible; sometimes reversible (renaturation).
- Practical uses: sterilisation by heat and alcohol; heavy metal poisoning is treated with egg white or milk, which bind the metal.

## 2.6 Qualitative tests for proteins and amino acids

| Test | Reagent | Positive result | Detects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Biuret** | NaOH + dilute CuSO4 | Violet / purple colour | Peptide bonds (proteins and peptides with 2 or more peptide bonds); not given by free amino acids |
| **Ninhydrin** | Ninhydrin; boil | Purple (Ruhemann's purple); proline and hydroxyproline give yellow | Free α-amino groups – all amino acids and proteins |
| **Xanthoproteic** | Conc. HNO3, heat; then add NaOH/ammonia | Yellow, turning orange with alkali | Aromatic amino acids (tyrosine, tryptophan; phenylalanine weakly) |
| **Millon's** | Mercuric sulphate in H2SO4 + sodium nitrite; warm | Red precipitate or colour | Tyrosine (phenolic –OH) |
| **Hopkins–Cole (glyoxylic acid)** | Glyoxylic acid + conc. H2SO4 along the side | Violet ring | Tryptophan (indole ring) |
| **Sulphur (lead acetate)** | Strong NaOH, boil, then lead acetate | Black precipitate (lead sulphide) | Cysteine / cystine (not methionine) |
| **Sakaguchi** | α-naphthol + sodium hypobromite in alkali | Red colour | Arginine (guanidino group) |
| **Heat coagulation** | Heat, then a drop of acetic acid | White coagulum | Albumins and globulins |
| **Precipitation tests** | Heavy metals, alkaloidal reagents (picric acid, tannic acid), alcohol, salting out with ammonium sulphate | Precipitate | Proteins in general |

**Salting out:** globulins precipitate at half saturation with ammonium sulphate; albumins only at full saturation. This separates the two.

## 2.7 Biological role of proteins and amino acids

**Proteins**

1. **Catalytic:** all enzymes are proteins (amylase, pepsin, DNA polymerase).
2. **Structural:** collagen, keratin, elastin.
3. **Transport:** haemoglobin (oxygen), albumin (drugs, fatty acids, bilirubin), transferrin (iron), lipoproteins (lipids).
4. **Hormones:** insulin, growth hormone, glucagon, oxytocin.
5. **Defence:** antibodies (immunoglobulins), complement proteins, fibrinogen and other clotting factors.
6. **Contraction and movement:** actin, myosin.
7. **Storage:** ferritin (iron), casein (milk), ovalbumin.
8. **Receptors and signalling:** cell surface receptors, ion channels.
9. **Regulation:** maintain colloid osmotic pressure of plasma (albumin) and act as buffers (haemoglobin, plasma proteins).
10. **Energy:** 1 g protein gives about 4 kcal, used mainly in starvation.

**Amino acids** (besides building proteins)

| Amino acid | Important product |
| --- | --- |
| Tyrosine | Thyroxine, adrenaline, noradrenaline, dopamine, melanin |
| Tryptophan | Serotonin, melatonin, niacin |
| Histidine | Histamine |
| Glutamate | GABA (inhibitory neurotransmitter) |
| Glycine | Haem, creatine, glutathione, bile salt conjugation |
| Arginine | Nitric oxide, creatine, urea |
| Methionine | S-adenosylmethionine (methyl donor) |
| Cysteine | Glutathione (antioxidant), taurine |

## 2.8 Diseases related to malnutrition of proteins

**Protein–energy malnutrition (PEM)**, now often called protein–energy undernutrition, is common in young children in developing countries, especially after weaning. Its two extreme forms are kwashiorkor and marasmus.

| Feature | Kwashiorkor | Marasmus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main cause | Lack of protein with fairly adequate calories (weaned onto a starchy diet) | Lack of both protein and calories (near starvation) |
| Usual age | 1–4 years | Below 1 year |
| Oedema | **Present** (low plasma albumin lowers oncotic pressure) | Absent |
| Body weight | 60–80% of expected (oedema hides wasting) | Below 60% of expected; severe wasting |
| Subcutaneous fat | Partly preserved | Lost – "skin and bones", old-man face |
| Face | Moon face | Shrunken, old-looking |
| Liver | Enlarged **fatty liver** (no protein to make lipoproteins that export fat) | Not fatty |
| Skin | "Flaky paint" dermatitis, depigmentation | Dry, loose, wrinkled |
| Hair | Thin, sparse, brittle, reddish (flag sign) | Mostly normal |
| Appetite and mood | Poor appetite, apathy, irritable | Good appetite, alert |
| Plasma albumin | Very low | Normal or slightly low |

**Marasmic kwashiorkor** shows features of both. Other effects of protein deficiency: poor growth, anaemia, low immunity (frequent infections), poor wound healing and delayed mental development.

**Treatment and prevention:** correct dehydration and infection first, then feed slowly with a balanced, energy- and protein-rich diet; promote breast-feeding, proper weaning foods, nutrition programmes (ICDS, mid-day meals) and health education.

**Recommended intake:** commonly quoted as about **0.8–1 g of good-quality protein per kg body weight per day** for adults, more in growing children, pregnancy and lactation.

## Remember

- Proteins are polymers of L-α-amino acids joined by peptide bonds (–CO–NH–); nitrogen about 16%.
- Essential amino acids: PVT TIM HALL (histidine and arginine semi-essential); leucine and lysine are purely ketogenic.
- Simple, conjugated (prosthetic group) and derived proteins; by solubility: albumins (water), globulins (dilute salt), glutelins (dilute acid/alkali), prolamines (70–80% alcohol), histones, protamines, scleroproteins (insoluble).
- Primary = sequence (peptide bonds); secondary = α-helix and β-sheet (H-bonds); tertiary = 3-D fold (side-chain bonds); quaternary = subunits (haemoglobin).
- Biuret = peptide bonds; ninhydrin = α-amino groups; xanthoproteic = aromatic; Millon's = tyrosine; Hopkins–Cole = tryptophan; lead acetate = cysteine; Sakaguchi = arginine.
- Kwashiorkor: protein deficiency, oedema, fatty liver; marasmus: protein + energy deficiency, severe wasting, no oedema.

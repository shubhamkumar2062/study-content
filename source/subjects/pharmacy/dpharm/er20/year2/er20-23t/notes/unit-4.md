# Unit 4: Nucleic acids and introduction to biotechnology

This unit covers syllabus chapter 5 (nucleic acids, 4 hours) and chapter 11 (introduction to biotechnology, 1 hour). They are joined here because biotechnology is built directly on DNA and RNA.

## 4.1 Definition of nucleic acids

**Nucleic acids** are long-chain polymers of **nucleotides** joined by **3′,5′-phosphodiester bonds**. They store and transfer genetic information and direct the synthesis of proteins.

There are two types:

- **DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid):** the genetic material; found mainly in the nucleus (also in mitochondria).
- **RNA (ribonucleic acid):** helps to express the genetic information as proteins; found in the nucleus and cytoplasm.

They were first isolated from pus cells by Friedrich Miescher (1869), who called them "nuclein".

## 4.2 Components of nucleic acids

Each nucleotide has three parts:

```text
   Nitrogenous base  +  Pentose sugar  +  Phosphate
   (purine or           (ribose in RNA,
    pyrimidine)          deoxyribose in DNA)

   Base + sugar                 = NUCLEOSIDE
   Base + sugar + phosphate     = NUCLEOTIDE
   Many nucleotides joined      = NUCLEIC ACID (polynucleotide)
```

### Purine and pyrimidine bases

| Group | Ring structure | Bases | Found in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Purines** | Two fused rings (a six-membered pyrimidine ring fused to a five-membered imidazole ring) | **Adenine (A)**, **Guanine (G)** | Both DNA and RNA |
| **Pyrimidines** | Single six-membered ring with two nitrogens | **Cytosine (C)** | Both DNA and RNA |
| | | **Thymine (T)** (5-methyluracil) | DNA only |
| | | **Uracil (U)** | RNA only |

```text
   Purine (two rings)              Pyrimidine (one ring)
        N                                N
      /   \   N                        /   \
     |     |--- \                     |     |
     |     |    /  (imidazole)        |     |
      \   /   N                        \   /
        N                                N
     6-membered + 5-membered          6-membered
```

**Minor and related bases:** hypoxanthine and xanthine (formed during purine breakdown), 5-methylcytosine, dihydrouracil and pseudouridine (in tRNA). **Uric acid** is the end product of purine breakdown in humans; excess causes **gout**.

**Drugs that are base analogues:** 5-fluorouracil, 6-mercaptopurine and azathioprine (anticancer/immunosuppressant); allopurinol (a hypoxanthine analogue that blocks xanthine oxidase in gout); acyclovir and zidovudine (antiviral nucleoside analogues).

### Pentose sugar

- **D-ribose** in RNA.
- **2-deoxy-D-ribose** in DNA (no –OH at carbon 2′).
- Carbons of the sugar are numbered with a prime (1′ to 5′) to separate them from the atoms of the base.

## 4.3 Nucleosides and nucleotides

### Nucleosides

A **nucleoside** = base + pentose sugar, joined by a **β-N-glycosidic bond** between C1′ of the sugar and N9 of a purine or N1 of a pyrimidine.

| Base | Ribonucleoside | Deoxyribonucleoside |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adenine | Adenosine | Deoxyadenosine |
| Guanine | Guanosine | Deoxyguanosine |
| Cytosine | Cytidine | Deoxycytidine |
| Uracil | Uridine | – |
| Thymine | – | Thymidine (deoxythymidine) |

### Nucleotides

A **nucleotide** = nucleoside + one or more **phosphate** groups, usually esterified at C5′ of the sugar. They are the building blocks of nucleic acids and also have important free roles.

| Nucleotide | Function |
| --- | --- |
| **ATP** (adenosine triphosphate) | Energy currency of the cell; two high-energy phosphate bonds |
| ADP, AMP | Formed when ATP is used; regulate metabolism |
| **cAMP** (cyclic AMP) | Second messenger for hormones such as adrenaline and glucagon |
| cGMP | Second messenger (nitric oxide action, vision) |
| GTP | Energy for protein synthesis; signalling through G-proteins |
| UTP / UDP-glucose | Glycogen synthesis |
| CTP / CDP-choline | Phospholipid synthesis |
| **NAD+, NADP+** | Coenzymes of dehydrogenases (contain AMP and niacin) |
| **FAD** | Coenzyme of flavoproteins (contains AMP and riboflavin) |
| **Coenzyme A** | Carries acyl groups (contains ADP and pantothenic acid) |
| S-adenosylmethionine | Methyl group donor |

```text
   Adenine
     │ (N9 – C1′ glycosidic bond)
   Ribose ──C5′–O–P–O–P–O–P          ATP
                α   β   γ
              (β and γ bonds are high-energy)
```

## 4.4 Structure of DNA: the Watson and Crick model

James Watson and Francis Crick proposed the **double helix** model of DNA in **1953**, using the X-ray diffraction pictures of Rosalind Franklin and Maurice Wilkins and the base-ratio rules of Erwin Chargaff.

### Features of the Watson–Crick (B-form) model

1. DNA is made of **two polynucleotide strands** wound around a common axis to form a **right-handed double helix**.
2. The two strands are **antiparallel**: one runs 5′→3′ and the other 3′→5′.
3. The **sugar–phosphate backbone** lies on the outside; the **bases** lie inside, stacked flat, at right angles to the axis.
4. Bases of the two strands pair by **hydrogen bonds** according to the **complementary base-pairing rule**:
   - **A pairs with T by two hydrogen bonds** (A=T)
   - **G pairs with C by three hydrogen bonds** (G≡C)
   - A purine always pairs with a pyrimidine, so the width of the helix is constant.
5. Therefore, in any double-stranded DNA, A = T and G = C, and total purines = total pyrimidines (**Chargaff's rule**). GC-rich DNA is more stable (melts at a higher temperature).
6. Dimensions: diameter about **2 nm (20 Å)**; one complete turn every **3.4 nm (34 Å)**; about **10 base pairs per turn**; distance between neighbouring base pairs **0.34 nm (3.4 Å)**.
7. The helix has a **major groove** and a **minor groove**, where proteins (like transcription factors) can bind.
8. The two strands are held together by hydrogen bonds and base stacking; they separate on heating (**denaturation / melting**) and join again on cooling (**renaturation / annealing**).

```text
     5′  3′
     │    │      Sugar-phosphate backbones on the outside
     A ═══ T     2 H-bonds
     G ≡≡≡ C     3 H-bonds
     T ═══ A
     C ≡≡≡ G
     │    │
     3′  5′      antiparallel strands, right-handed twist,
                 10 base pairs per turn, 3.4 nm per turn
```

Other forms: **A-DNA** (right-handed, shorter and wider, in dehydrated DNA) and **Z-DNA** (left-handed, zig-zag backbone).

### Functions of DNA

1. Stores the **genetic information** (the genes) in the sequence of its bases.
2. **Replication:** makes an exact copy of itself before cell division (semi-conservative), so the information passes to daughter cells and offspring.
3. **Transcription:** acts as a template for RNA synthesis, controlling protein synthesis and all cell activities.
4. **Mutation** (change in base sequence) is the basis of variation and evolution, and also of genetic disease and cancer.

**Central dogma of molecular biology:**

```text
         replication
        ┌────────┐
        ▼        │
       DNA ──────┘ ── transcription ──▶ RNA ── translation ──▶ Protein
                        (reverse transcription in retroviruses: RNA → DNA)
```

## 4.5 Structure and types of RNA

RNA differs from DNA in:

| Feature | DNA | RNA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sugar | Deoxyribose | Ribose |
| Bases | A, G, C, **T** | A, G, C, **U** |
| Strands | Double-stranded helix | Usually single-stranded (folds back on itself to form hairpin loops) |
| Chargaff's rule | Followed | Not followed |
| Location | Mainly nucleus | Nucleus and cytoplasm |
| Stability | Stable (long-lived) | Less stable; easily hydrolysed by alkali |
| Function | Stores genetic information | Expresses it as protein |

### Types of RNA

| Type | Share of cell RNA | Structure | Function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **mRNA** (messenger) | About 5% | Linear; 5′ **cap** (7-methylguanosine) and 3′ **poly-A tail** in eukaryotes | Carries the genetic **message** (codons) from DNA to ribosomes; each **codon** of 3 bases codes one amino acid |
| **tRNA** (transfer) | About 15% | Small (about 75–90 nucleotides); **cloverleaf** shape (L-shaped in 3-D); has an **anticodon** loop and a **CCA** end that carries the amino acid; has unusual bases | **Carries amino acids** to the ribosome and reads the codon through its anticodon (an "adaptor") |
| **rRNA** (ribosomal) | About 80% | Folded, combined with proteins to form ribosomes | Forms the structure of **ribosomes**; one rRNA acts as an enzyme (peptidyl transferase) that forms peptide bonds |
| Small RNAs (snRNA, miRNA, siRNA) | Small | Short chains | Splicing of mRNA, gene silencing |

```text
 tRNA (cloverleaf)            CCA–3′ (amino acid attaches here)
                               │
              D-loop  ○──┤acceptor stem├──○  TψC loop
                          │
                       anticodon loop
                         (e.g. reads codon on mRNA)
```

**Genetic code:** 64 codons; 61 code amino acids and 3 are stop codons (UAA, UAG, UGA); AUG is the start codon (methionine). The code is universal (with small exceptions), non-overlapping and degenerate (more than one codon for most amino acids).

## 4.6 Introduction to biotechnology

**Biotechnology** is the use of living organisms, cells or their parts (such as enzymes and genes) to make products or processes useful to humans.

- **Traditional biotechnology:** fermentation to make curd, bread, alcohol, vinegar, antibiotics (penicillin), vitamins and enzymes.
- **Modern biotechnology:** recombinant DNA technology (genetic engineering), hybridoma technology (monoclonal antibodies), cell and tissue culture, gene therapy, PCR, genomics.

### Recombinant DNA technology (genetic engineering)

Joining a gene from one organism with the DNA of another so that the host makes the product of that gene.

```text
 1. Isolate the wanted gene (e.g. human insulin gene)
 2. Cut the gene and a vector (plasmid) with the same RESTRICTION ENZYME
 3. Join them with DNA LIGASE → recombinant DNA
 4. Put recombinant DNA into a host cell (E. coli or yeast) → TRANSFORMATION
 5. Select the transformed cells (antibiotic-resistance marker)
 6. Grow them in a fermenter → host makes the protein
 7. Extract and purify the product (downstream processing)
```

| Tool | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **Restriction endonucleases** ("molecular scissors") | Cut DNA at specific base sequences (e.g. EcoRI) |
| **DNA ligase** ("molecular glue") | Joins DNA pieces |
| **Vectors** | Carry the gene into the host: plasmids, bacteriophages, cosmids, viral vectors |
| **Host cells** | E. coli, yeast, mammalian cells (such as CHO cells) |
| **PCR (polymerase chain reaction)** | Makes millions of copies of a DNA segment using a heat-stable (Taq) DNA polymerase; used in diagnosis (COVID-19 RT-PCR, HIV, TB), forensics |

### Products and applications in pharmacy

| Area | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Hormones | Human insulin (the first recombinant drug, approved 1982), insulin analogues, growth hormone |
| Blood products | Erythropoietin (anaemia of kidney failure), clotting factor VIII (haemophilia), tissue plasminogen activator (alteplase) |
| Immune modulators | Interferons (hepatitis, some cancers), interleukins, filgrastim (G-CSF) |
| Vaccines | Recombinant hepatitis B vaccine (surface antigen made in yeast), HPV vaccine |
| Monoclonal antibodies | Made by **hybridoma technology** (Köhler and Milstein, 1975): trastuzumab, rituximab, adalimumab; also diagnostic kits (pregnancy tests) |
| Diagnostics | ELISA, PCR, DNA probes, biosensors |
| Gene therapy | Introducing a normal gene to correct a genetic defect |
| Antibiotics and enzymes | Industrial fermentation; streptokinase, asparaginase |
| Agriculture | Bt cotton and other transgenic crops |

**Benefits:** pure human proteins in large amounts, lower risk of allergy and infection than animal-derived products, new drugs for earlier untreatable diseases. **Concerns:** high cost, safety and ethical issues, need for cold storage (biologics are proteins).

## Remember

- Nucleotide = base + pentose + phosphate; nucleoside = base + pentose.
- Purines: adenine, guanine (two rings). Pyrimidines: cytosine, thymine (DNA), uracil (RNA).
- DNA: deoxyribose, double helix (Watson and Crick, 1953), antiparallel, right-handed, A=T (2 H-bonds), G≡C (3 H-bonds), 10 base pairs and 3.4 nm per turn, 2 nm wide.
- RNA: ribose, uracil, single-stranded; mRNA carries codons, tRNA carries amino acids (cloverleaf, anticodon, CCA end), rRNA forms ribosomes.
- ATP = energy currency; cAMP = second messenger; NAD, FAD, CoA contain nucleotides.
- Biotechnology: restriction enzyme cuts, ligase joins, plasmid carries, E. coli/yeast makes – human insulin, hepatitis B vaccine, erythropoietin, interferons, monoclonal antibodies.

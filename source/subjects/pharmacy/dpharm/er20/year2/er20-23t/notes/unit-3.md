# Unit 3: Lipids

This unit covers syllabus chapter 4 (5 hours).

## 3.1 Definition

**Lipids** are a group of naturally occurring organic compounds that are **insoluble (or poorly soluble) in water** and **soluble in non-polar (fat) solvents** such as chloroform, ether, benzene and acetone. They are esters of fatty acids with alcohols, or substances related to them, and they can be used by living organisms.

Lipids include fats, oils, waxes, phospholipids, steroids (cholesterol) and the fat-soluble vitamins. Because they do not dissolve in water, they are carried in blood as **lipoproteins**.

## 3.2 Classification of lipids (Bloor's classification)

```text
                              Lipids
        ┌───────────────────────┼────────────────────────┐
  Simple lipids            Compound lipids          Derived lipids
  (fatty acid + alcohol)   (simple lipid +          (obtained by hydrolysis
                            another group)           of the first two)
  ├ Fats and oils          ├ Phospholipids           ├ Fatty acids
  │ (triglycerides)        ├ Glycolipids             ├ Glycerol and other alcohols
  └ Waxes                  ├ Lipoproteins            ├ Steroids (cholesterol)
                           └ Others (sulpholipids,   ├ Fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, K
                             aminolipids)            ├ Ketone bodies
                                                     └ Eicosanoids (prostaglandins)
```

### 1. Simple lipids

Esters of fatty acids with alcohols.

- **Fats and oils (triacylglycerols, triglycerides):** esters of fatty acids with **glycerol**. Fats are solid at room temperature (butter, ghee, lard); oils are liquid (groundnut, sunflower, mustard oil).
- **Waxes:** esters of fatty acids with **long-chain monohydric alcohols** (not glycerol). Examples: beeswax, spermaceti, lanolin (wool fat), carnauba wax. Uses: ointment bases, tablet coating, polishes.

### 2. Compound (conjugated) lipids

Esters of fatty acids and alcohol that also contain another group.

| Class | Extra group | Examples | Where / role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Phospholipids** (glycerophospholipids) | Phosphoric acid + a base | **Lecithin** (phosphatidylcholine), **cephalin** (phosphatidylethanolamine), phosphatidylserine, phosphatidylinositol | Cell membranes; lecithin in lung surfactant (as dipalmitoyl lecithin) and as an emulsifier |
| Phospholipids (sphingophospholipids) | Sphingosine instead of glycerol | **Sphingomyelin** | Myelin sheath of nerves |
| **Glycolipids** | Carbohydrate + sphingosine | **Cerebrosides**, **gangliosides** | Brain and nerve tissue; cell recognition |
| **Lipoproteins** | Protein | Chylomicrons, VLDL, LDL, HDL | Transport of lipids in blood |

### 3. Derived lipids

Formed by hydrolysis of simple and compound lipids, but still having lipid properties: fatty acids, glycerol, **steroids** (cholesterol, bile acids, sex hormones), fat-soluble vitamins, ketone bodies, and **eicosanoids** (prostaglandins, thromboxanes and leukotrienes, made from arachidonic acid).

**Amphipathic lipids:** molecules with both a water-loving (polar head) and a water-hating (non-polar tail) part, such as phospholipids, cholesterol and bile salts. They form membranes, micelles and liposomes and act as emulsifiers.

## 3.3 Triglycerides (oils and fats): structure

A triglyceride is formed when the three –OH groups of glycerol are esterified with three fatty acids.

```text
   CH2–OH        HOOC–R1          CH2–O–CO–R1
   |                              |
   CH–OH    +    HOOC–R2   ──→    CH–O–CO–R2     +  3 H2O
   |                              |
   CH2–OH        HOOC–R3          CH2–O–CO–R3
  Glycerol    3 fatty acids      Triacylglycerol (triglyceride)
```

- **Simple triglyceride:** all three fatty acids are the same (tripalmitin, triolein).
- **Mixed triglyceride:** different fatty acids – most natural fats and oils.
- Triglycerides are **neutral fats** (no charge) and are the main **stored energy** of the body (adipose tissue). 1 g of fat gives about **9 kcal**, more than double that of carbohydrate or protein.
- Fats are rich in saturated fatty acids (solid); oils are rich in unsaturated fatty acids (liquid, lower melting point).

## 3.4 Properties of triglycerides

### Physical properties

- Colourless, odourless and tasteless when pure; natural colour comes from pigments such as carotenes.
- Insoluble in water, soluble in fat solvents; lighter than water (float).
- Leave a **translucent grease spot** on paper.
- Form **emulsions** with water in the presence of emulsifiers (bile salts, soaps, proteins).

### Chemical properties

1. **Hydrolysis:** by lipases (in the gut, pancreatic lipase), acids or superheated steam → glycerol + fatty acids.
2. **Saponification:** hydrolysis by an alkali (NaOH or KOH) gives glycerol and **soaps** (sodium or potassium salts of fatty acids).

   ```text
   Triglyceride + 3 NaOH  →  Glycerol + 3 R–COONa (soap)
   ```

3. **Hydrogenation:** hydrogen is added across double bonds of unsaturated fatty acids using a nickel catalyst, turning liquid oils into solid fats (vanaspati). Partial hydrogenation forms **trans fatty acids**, which raise LDL-cholesterol and the risk of heart disease.
4. **Halogenation:** iodine adds to double bonds – the basis of the iodine value.
5. **Rancidity:** the unpleasant smell and taste of fats on storage.
   - **Hydrolytic rancidity:** lipases from microbes release short-chain fatty acids (butyric acid in butter) – helped by moisture and warmth.
   - **Oxidative rancidity:** oxygen attacks double bonds of unsaturated fatty acids forming peroxides, aldehydes and ketones – helped by light, heat and metals.
   - Prevention: store in cool, dark, airtight containers; add **antioxidants** such as vitamin E (tocopherol), BHA, BHT and propyl gallate.
6. **Acrolein formation:** heating glycerol (or a fat) with potassium bisulphate removes water and gives acrolein, which has a sharp, irritating smell.

### Constants used to test fats and oils

| Constant | Definition | What it tells |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Saponification value** | mg of KOH needed to saponify 1 g of fat | Average chain length – higher value means shorter chains (lower molecular weight) |
| **Iodine value** | g of iodine absorbed by 100 g of fat | Degree of unsaturation – higher in oils |
| **Acid value** | mg of KOH needed to neutralise the free fatty acids in 1 g of fat | Free fatty acids – rises with hydrolytic rancidity |
| **Peroxide value** | Amount of peroxides present (milliequivalents of peroxide per kg) | Oxidative rancidity |
| **Reichert–Meissl number** | ml of 0.1 N alkali needed to neutralise the volatile, water-soluble fatty acids from 5 g of fat | Short-chain fatty acids; used to detect adulteration of butter and ghee |

## 3.5 Fatty acids

**Fatty acids** are long-chain carboxylic acids, R–COOH. Natural fatty acids usually have an **even number of carbon atoms** (made from 2-carbon units) and are unbranched.

### A. Classification based on chemical nature

**1. By saturation**

| Type | Double bonds | Examples (carbon atoms : double bonds) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Saturated** | None | Butyric (4:0, in butter), palmitic (16:0), stearic (18:0) |
| **Monounsaturated (MUFA)** | One | Oleic acid (18:1) – olive, groundnut oil; palmitoleic (16:1) |
| **Polyunsaturated (PUFA)** | Two or more | Linoleic (18:2, ω-6), α-linolenic (18:3, ω-3), arachidonic (20:4, ω-6), EPA (20:5, ω-3) and DHA (22:6, ω-3) from fish oils |

**2. By chain length:** short chain (2–6 carbons, e.g. butyric), medium chain (8–12, e.g. coconut oil acids), long chain (14 or more, e.g. palmitic, stearic, oleic).

**3. By position of the first double bond from the methyl end:** omega-3 (α-linolenic, EPA, DHA), omega-6 (linoleic, arachidonic), omega-9 (oleic).

**4. Cis and trans:** natural unsaturated fatty acids are mostly **cis** (bent chain); **trans** fats form during partial hydrogenation and are harmful.

### B. Classification based on nutritional requirement

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Essential fatty acids (EFA)** | Cannot be made in the body because humans cannot put a double bond beyond carbon 9; must come from the diet | **Linoleic acid** (ω-6) and **α-linolenic acid** (ω-3) |
| Conditionally (semi-) essential | Made from linoleic acid, so needed in the diet only if linoleic acid is short | **Arachidonic acid** |
| Non-essential | Made in the body | Palmitic, stearic, oleic acid |

**Functions of essential fatty acids:** parts of membrane phospholipids; precursors of prostaglandins, thromboxanes and leukotrienes; lower blood cholesterol; needed for healthy skin and for brain and retina development (DHA).

**EFA deficiency:** rough, scaly skin (phrynoderma, "toad skin"), hair loss, poor wound healing, poor growth; seen in long-term fat-free parenteral nutrition. Sources: vegetable oils (sunflower, safflower, soybean), fish, flaxseed, nuts.

## 3.6 Cholesterol: structure and functions

**Cholesterol** is the most important **sterol** of animals. It is a derived lipid; it is **not found in plants** (plants contain phytosterols).

### Structure

- Formula C27H46O (**27 carbon atoms**).
- Built on the **cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene** (steroid) nucleus: four fused rings A, B, C (six-membered) and D (five-membered).
- An **–OH group at carbon 3** (so it is an alcohol), a **double bond between carbons 5 and 6**, two methyl groups (C18, C19) and an **8-carbon side chain at carbon 17**.

```text
 Sketch of cholesterol (rings drawn as boxes)

                          CH3 (C18)   8-carbon side chain
                            |        /
                     ┌──────┴──┐  ┌─┴──┐
          CH3 (C19)  │ ring C  ├──┤ D  │  ← C17 (ring D has 5 carbons)
              |      │ (6 C)   │  └────┘
       ┌──────┴──┬───┴─────────┘
       │ ring A  │ ring B  │
 HO ── │ (C3)    │ C5=C6   │    rings A, B, C have 6 carbons each
       └─────────┴─────────┘
```

- In blood about two-thirds of cholesterol is in the **esterified** form (a fatty acid attached at C3); it is carried mainly in **LDL**.
- Sources: diet (egg yolk, meat, liver, butter, ghee) and synthesis in the body, mainly in the **liver**, from **acetyl CoA**. The key (rate-limiting) enzyme is **HMG-CoA reductase**, which is blocked by **statins**.

### Functions of cholesterol

1. **Cell membranes:** an essential part of the plasma membrane; it controls membrane fluidity.
2. **Bile acids and bile salts:** made from cholesterol in the liver; needed for fat digestion and absorption. This is also the main route by which the body gets rid of cholesterol.
3. **Steroid hormones:** cortisol, aldosterone, testosterone, oestrogen and progesterone are all made from cholesterol.
4. **Vitamin D3:** 7-dehydrocholesterol in the skin is converted to cholecalciferol by sunlight (UV rays).
5. **Nerve tissue:** present in large amounts in brain and myelin, helping nerve insulation.
6. **Skin:** helps make skin waterproof.

**Clinical point:** high blood cholesterol (especially LDL) leads to **atherosclerosis** – fatty plaques in arteries – causing coronary heart disease and stroke (see Units 8 and 11). Desirable total serum cholesterol is commonly quoted as below about 200 mg/dl; ranges vary by laboratory.

## 3.7 Lipoproteins

Lipids are insoluble in water, so they travel in blood packed inside **lipoproteins**: spherical particles with a **core** of triglycerides and cholesterol esters and a **surface** of phospholipids, free cholesterol and proteins called **apolipoproteins (apoproteins)**.

```text
        Surface: phospholipids, free cholesterol, apoproteins
            ○ ○ ○ ○ ○ ○ ○
          ○               ○
         ○   CORE: TG +     ○
         ○   cholesterol    ○
          ○    esters      ○
            ○ ○ ○ ○ ○ ○ ○
```

### Types, composition and functions

Lipoproteins are separated by **ultracentrifugation** (by density) or electrophoresis. The more triglyceride a particle carries, the larger and lighter it is.

| Lipoprotein | Made in | Main lipid carried | Main apoproteins | Function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Chylomicrons** | Intestine | Dietary triglycerides (about 85–90%) | B-48, C-II, E | Carry **dietary (exogenous)** fat from the intestine to tissues; largest and lightest |
| **VLDL** (very low density) | Liver | Triglycerides made in the liver | B-100, C-II, E | Carry **endogenous** triglycerides from the liver to tissues |
| **IDL** (intermediate density) | Formed from VLDL in blood | Triglycerides and cholesterol | B-100, E | Short-lived; changes to LDL |
| **LDL** (low density) | Formed from IDL | **Cholesterol** (richest) | B-100 | Carry cholesterol **to the tissues**; excess deposits in artery walls – "**bad cholesterol**" |
| **HDL** (high density) | Liver and intestine | Protein-rich; phospholipids and cholesterol | A-I, A-II, C, E | Carry cholesterol **from tissues back to the liver** (reverse cholesterol transport) – "**good cholesterol**"; smallest and densest |

**Lipoprotein lipase** (on the walls of blood capillaries) breaks down the triglycerides of chylomicrons and VLDL; apo C-II activates it. LDL is taken up by cells through **LDL receptors** (recognising apo B-100); a genetic defect of these receptors causes **familial hypercholesterolaemia**.

```text
 Intestine → Chylomicrons → (LPL) → remnants → liver
 Liver → VLDL → (LPL) → IDL → LDL → tissues (LDL receptor)
 Tissues → cholesterol → HDL → liver → bile acids → excreted
```

## 3.8 Qualitative tests for lipids

| Test | Method | Positive result | Detects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Solubility** | Shake with water, alcohol and chloroform | Insoluble in water, soluble in chloroform/ether | Lipids in general |
| **Translucent spot (grease spot)** | Put a drop on filter paper and let dry | Permanent translucent spot | Fats and oils |
| **Emulsification** | Shake oil with water, then with bile salt or soap solution | Stable emulsion only with the emulsifier | Emulsifying action |
| **Saponification** | Boil with alcoholic KOH, add water and shake | Froth (soap formed) | Fats and oils (esters) |
| **Acrolein** | Heat with solid potassium bisulphate (KHSO4) | Sharp, irritating smell of acrolein | Glycerol (free or in fats) |
| **Sudan III / IV** | Add a few drops of Sudan dye, shake with water | Red-coloured oil layer | Fats |
| **Unsaturation (Hübl's iodine)** | Add iodine solution drop by drop | Colour of iodine disappears | Unsaturated fatty acids |
| **Salkowski** | Cholesterol in chloroform + conc. H2SO4 along the side | Red colour in the upper chloroform layer, yellow-green fluorescence in the acid layer | Cholesterol |
| **Liebermann–Burchard** | Cholesterol in chloroform + acetic anhydride + conc. H2SO4 | Red → blue → **green** colour | Cholesterol (also basis of an old quantitative method) |

## 3.9 Functions of lipids

1. **Energy store:** triglycerides in adipose tissue are the largest energy reserve; 1 g gives about 9 kcal.
2. **Structural:** phospholipids and cholesterol form cell membranes.
3. **Insulation and protection:** subcutaneous fat keeps body heat; fat around kidneys and other organs protects them from injury.
4. **Nerve function:** myelin (sphingomyelin, glycolipids) insulates nerve fibres for fast conduction.
5. **Carriers of fat-soluble vitamins** A, D, E and K; dietary fat is needed for their absorption.
6. **Precursors:** cholesterol → steroid hormones, bile acids, vitamin D; arachidonic acid → prostaglandins, thromboxanes, leukotrienes.
7. **Emulsifiers and surfactants:** bile salts in fat digestion; lung surfactant (dipalmitoyl lecithin) prevents collapse of alveoli – its lack causes respiratory distress syndrome in premature babies.
8. **Signalling:** phosphatidylinositol gives second messengers (IP3, DAG).
9. **Pharmaceutical uses:** oils and fats as vehicles and ointment bases (arachis oil, wool fat, beeswax); lecithin as an emulsifier; phospholipids in liposomes.

## Remember

- Lipids: insoluble in water, soluble in fat solvents; esters of fatty acids with alcohols.
- Bloor: simple (fats, oils, waxes), compound (phospholipids, glycolipids, lipoproteins), derived (fatty acids, steroids, fat-soluble vitamins).
- Triglyceride = glycerol + 3 fatty acids; saponification value → chain length; iodine value → unsaturation; acid value → free fatty acids.
- Essential fatty acids: linoleic (ω-6) and α-linolenic (ω-3); arachidonic is semi-essential.
- Cholesterol: 27 C, steroid nucleus, –OH at C3, double bond C5–C6; makes bile acids, steroid hormones, vitamin D3; HMG-CoA reductase is its key enzyme.
- Chylomicrons (dietary TG), VLDL (liver TG), LDL ("bad", cholesterol to tissues), HDL ("good", cholesterol back to liver).
- Acrolein test = glycerol; Salkowski and Liebermann–Burchard = cholesterol.

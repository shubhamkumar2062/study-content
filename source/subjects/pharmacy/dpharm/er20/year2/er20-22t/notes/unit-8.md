# Unit 8: Setting up a community pharmacy, purchasing and inventory control

Syllabus chapter 10 (Community Pharmacy Management, 25 hours) is the biggest chapter, so it is split into two units in this guide. This unit covers the legal requirements to set up a pharmacy, site selection, pharmacy design and interiors, vendor selection and ordering, procurement, and inventory control and management. Unit 9 covers finance, accounts, software, CRM, audits, SOPs and digital health.

## 8.1 Legal requirements to set up a community pharmacy

A community pharmacy in India is a retail drug sale business. It is controlled mainly by the **Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940** and the **Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945**, and its professional work by the **Pharmacy Act, 1948**. The licence is granted by the **State Licensing Authority** (the State Drugs Controller's office); the premises are inspected by a **Drugs Inspector** before the licence is granted.

### Drug sale licences (Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945)

| Licence | For |
| --- | --- |
| Form 20 | Retail sale of drugs other than those in Schedules C, C1 and X |
| Form 21 | Retail sale of drugs in Schedules C and C1 (other than Schedule X) – for example vaccines, sera, insulin |
| Form 20B / Form 21B | Wholesale of the corresponding drugs (needed if the pharmacy also sells wholesale) |
| Separate licence | Retail sale of Schedule X drugs, granted only with extra conditions (separate storage under lock and key, records) |

A pharmacy usually holds both Form 20 and Form 21. The application is made to the State Licensing Authority in the prescribed form with the fee, documents and plan of the premises. Licences now remain valid as long as the retention fee is paid at the intervals laid down; earlier they had to be renewed every five years. Check the current fee and form with the State Drugs Control Department, because these are revised.

### Main conditions for a retail drug licence

1. **Competent person:** sale and dispensing must be in charge of, or under the personal supervision of, a **registered pharmacist** (registered with the State Pharmacy Council under the Pharmacy Act, 1948).
2. **Area:** the premises must have an adequate area – not less than **10 square metres** for retail sale and not less than **15 square metres** for combined retail and wholesale (as laid down in the Rules).
3. **Storage:** proper storage facilities to protect drugs from light, heat, moisture and dust; a **refrigerator** for drugs that must be kept cold (Schedule C and C1 products, insulin, vaccines).
4. **Records:** purchase invoices, sale bills, and special records for scheduled drugs:
   - Schedule H – sale only on prescription, with a bill showing patient and prescriber.
   - Schedule H1 – separate register (patient, prescriber, drug, quantity) kept for 3 years.
   - Schedule X – prescription copy kept for 2 years; separate register; lock and key storage.
5. **Display:** the licence (and the pharmacist's registration certificate) is displayed in a conspicuous place.
6. **Sale only from licensed sources:** purchase from licensed manufacturers or wholesalers on proper invoices with batch numbers.
7. **No sale of expired, spurious, misbranded or not-of-standard-quality drugs.**
8. **Inspection:** the Drugs Inspector may inspect premises and records at any reasonable time.

### Other registrations commonly needed

| Requirement | Authority |
| --- | --- |
| Shop and Establishment registration (or trade licence) | State Labour Department / local municipal body (as per state law) |
| GST registration (GSTIN) | When required under GST law (depends on turnover and business type) |
| PAN of the business, bank current account | Income Tax Department, bank |
| Pharmacist's registration certificate | State Pharmacy Council |
| Narcotic and psychotropic drugs (if stocked) | Licence / permission under the NDPS Act, 1985 and state rules |
| Biomedical waste arrangement (if screening with lancets etc.) | As per Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules |
| Fire and electrical safety | Local rules |

### Documents usually submitted for a drug licence

- Application and fee receipt.
- Proof of ownership or rent agreement of the premises.
- Site plan / layout of the premises showing area.
- Pharmacist's registration certificate and appointment / consent letter.
- Identity and address proof of the proprietor or partners; partnership deed or company documents.
- Declaration under the Rules.
- Proof of refrigerator purchase (for Form 21).

## 8.2 Site selection requirements

The right location decides how many customers the pharmacy will serve.

| Factor | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Population and catchment area | Enough people within walking distance; growing residential area |
| Nearness to prescribers | Clinics, hospitals, nursing homes, diagnostic centres nearby bring prescriptions |
| Competition | Number of pharmacies nearby; their services and hours |
| Visibility and accessibility | Main road, ground floor, easy entry for elderly and wheelchairs, signboard visible |
| Parking and transport | Parking space, bus stop, auto stand |
| Type of customers | Income level, age profile, chronic patients, language |
| Rent and cost | Rent within budget; long lease to build goodwill |
| Size and shape of premises | At least the legal area; regular shape for good layout |
| Utilities | Reliable electricity (refrigerator, power backup), water, telephone and internet |
| Legal suitability | Commercial use permitted; no legal disputes on the premises |
| Future development | Planned hospitals, housing colonies, markets |

## 8.3 Pharmacy design and interiors

Good design makes work safe and efficient and gives a professional image.

```text
           ┌────────────────────── Front (glass, signboard) ─────────────────────┐
           │  Entrance   OTC / health product shelves     Waiting chairs          │
           │                                                                      │
           │  Billing counter ─────── Dispensing counter ─────── Counselling area  │
           │                     (pharmacist; computer)          (private corner)  │
           │  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── │
           │  Prescription medicine racks (alphabetical / by category)              │
           │  Refrigerator (2–8 °C) │ Schedule X cupboard (lock) │ Expired/returns    │
           │  Store room for bulk stock          Staff area, wash basin              │
           └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Exterior

- Clear signboard with the pharmacy name and a recognised pharmacy symbol (green cross); timings and emergency contact.
- Clean glass front; window display of health messages.
- Ramp or step-free entry where possible.

### Interior

| Area | Features |
| --- | --- |
| Customer area | Clean, well lit, ventilated, uncluttered; seating for elderly; health posters; OTC products on open shelves |
| Dispensing counter | Under pharmacist's control; enough working space; counting trays, measures, label printer, references and computer |
| Counselling area | Private corner or room with chairs; for counselling and screening |
| Prescription drug storage | Behind the counter, out of the customer's reach; shelves labelled; LASA drugs separated |
| Cold storage | Refrigerator with thermometer and temperature log; vaccines and insulin stored correctly |
| Schedule X / narcotics | Separate locked cupboard |
| Store room | Shelving off the floor, away from walls; pest control; protected from moisture |
| Expired / damaged / returned goods | Separate labelled area so they cannot be sold by mistake |
| Utilities | Air-conditioning or temperature control (most drugs: store below 25 °C or as labelled), power backup, fire extinguisher, wash basin |

**Storage terms commonly used on labels:** "store in a refrigerator" = 2–8 °C; "do not freeze"; "protect from light"; "store in a cool place" and "store at room temperature" as defined in the IP general notices.

**Shelf arrangement methods:** alphabetical by generic or brand name; by pharmacological category; by dosage form (tablets, syrups, injections, external); fast-moving items near the counter.

## 8.4 Vendor selection and ordering

### Vendors (suppliers)

Manufacturers (rarely directly), **stockists/distributors** of companies, **wholesalers**, and online B2B platforms. Every vendor must hold a valid wholesale drug licence.

### Criteria for selecting a vendor

| Criterion | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Legal status | Valid wholesale licence (20B/21B), GST registration |
| Quality | Genuine products from authorised companies; proper storage and cold chain |
| Range | Stocks most items needed; can supply urgent items |
| Price and margins | Fair price, trade discounts, schemes |
| Credit terms | Credit period (for example 15–30 days) |
| Delivery | Frequency, speed, reliability, minimum order |
| Returns policy | Accepts expiry and breakage returns |
| Service | Information, easy ordering (phone, app), accurate billing |

A pharmacy usually keeps two or three regular vendors so that supply continues if one fails.

### Ordering

1. Prepare a **want list / shortage book** from shelf checks and the software's reorder report.
2. Check stock levels against reorder levels; consider seasonal demand (more ORS in summer, cough products in winter).
3. Prepare a **purchase order** with item name, strength, pack, quantity, vendor and date.
4. Send the order by phone, email or the vendor's app; keep a copy.
5. On delivery, **receive and check** goods against the invoice and order: name, strength, quantity, batch number, expiry date, price, damage, cold-chain items received cold.
6. Enter the invoice in the purchase register / software; store goods immediately in the right place.
7. Return or report short supply, damaged or near-expiry items.
8. Pay within the credit period.

## 8.5 Procurement

**Procurement** is the whole process of getting medicines of good quality, in the right quantity, at the right time and at the lowest total cost, from reliable sources.

```text
Select items → Estimate quantity → Choose vendor → Order → Receive and inspect
   → Store → Pay → Review supplier and stock movement → (back to estimate)
```

Points of good procurement:

- Buy from licensed vendors only; avoid unknown cheap sources (risk of spurious drugs).
- Prefer quality generics where suitable; keep Jan Aushadhi or other generics to offer lower prices.
- Check expiry: avoid stock that will expire before it can be sold.
- Avoid overstocking (money locked up, expiry) and understocking (lost sales, patient dissatisfaction).

## 8.6 Inventory control methods and inventory management

**Inventory** is the stock of medicines and other goods held for sale. **Inventory control** is the method of keeping the right amount of stock at the lowest cost.

### Important terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Lead time | Time between placing an order and receiving the goods |
| Safety (buffer) stock | Extra stock kept to cover unexpected demand or delays |
| Reorder level | Stock level at which a new order is placed |
| Maximum level | Highest stock to be held |
| Minimum level | Lowest stock allowed before risk of running out |
| Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) | Order size that gives the lowest total of ordering and holding costs |
| Stock turnover | Number of times stock is sold and replaced in a year |
| Dead stock | Items that have not moved for a long time |

### Formulas

```text
Reorder level = (Average daily use × Lead time in days) + Safety stock

EOQ = √( 2 × A × O ÷ H )
   A = annual demand (units), O = cost of placing one order, H = holding cost per unit per year

Example: daily sale 10 strips, lead time 3 days, safety stock 20 strips
         Reorder level = 10 × 3 + 20 = 50 strips
```

### Inventory control methods

| Method | Basis | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ABC analysis** | Annual money value. A – about 10–20% of items, about 70–80% of value; B – middle; C – many items, small value | Tight control and frequent review of A items |
| **VED analysis** | Importance: Vital, Essential, Desirable | Never run out of vital items (insulin, antiepileptics, emergency drugs) |
| **FSN analysis** | Movement: Fast, Slow, Non-moving | Find slow and dead stock; return or clear before expiry |
| **SDE analysis** | Availability: Scarce, Difficult, Easy to obtain | Plan buying of hard-to-get items |
| **HML analysis** | Unit price: High, Medium, Low | Control of costly items |
| **EOQ and reorder level** | Calculated quantities | When and how much to order |
| **Two-bin system** | Two containers; when the first is empty, reorder and use the second | Simple manual control |
| **Perpetual inventory** | Stock updated after every purchase and sale (software) | Real-time stock level |
| **Periodic inventory** | Physical stock count at fixed intervals | Checks software records; finds losses |

ABC and VED are often combined (ABC-VED matrix) – for example a costly vital item gets the closest attention.

### Inventory management practices

1. **FEFO** (first expiry, first out) – sell the stock that expires first; with FIFO (first in, first out) as a general rule.
2. Monthly **expiry check**; list items expiring in the next 3 months and return them to the vendor as per the agreement.
3. **Physical stock verification** and matching with records.
4. Monitor slow-moving items and avoid buying more.
5. Maintain cold-chain records (refrigerator temperature twice daily).
6. Secure high-value and scheduled items; check for theft and loss.
7. Use pharmacy software for reorder alerts, batch and expiry tracking.
8. Dispose of expired drugs properly through the vendor or authorised waste handlers and record it.

## Remember

- Laws: Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940 and Rules 1945 (licences), Pharmacy Act 1948 (registered pharmacist).
- Retail licences: Form 20 (drugs other than C, C1, X) and Form 21 (C and C1); wholesale 20B/21B; Schedule X needs a separate licence.
- Conditions: registered pharmacist, at least 10 m² retail (15 m² retail + wholesale), refrigerator, records (H1 register 3 years, Schedule X 2 years), licence displayed.
- Site: population, nearby doctors, competition, visibility, access, rent, utilities.
- Design: dispensing counter, private counselling area, prescription drugs behind counter, refrigerator, locked Schedule X cupboard, separate expired-goods area.
- Ordering: want list → purchase order → receive and check (batch, expiry, quantity, cold chain) → store → pay.
- Inventory: reorder level = daily use × lead time + safety stock; EOQ = √(2AO/H); ABC (value), VED (importance), FSN (movement); FEFO.

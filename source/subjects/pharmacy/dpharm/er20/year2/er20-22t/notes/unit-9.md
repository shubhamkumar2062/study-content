# Unit 9: Finance, accounts, software, CRM, audits, SOPs and digital health

This unit is the second half of syllabus chapter 10 (Community Pharmacy Management). It covers financial planning and management, accountancy (day book and cash book), pharmacy operation software, Customer Relationship Management (CRM), audits, SOPs of pharmacy management, and digital health, mHealth and online pharmacies.

## 9.1 Financial planning and management

**Financial planning** is estimating how much money is needed to start and run the pharmacy, where it will come from and how it will be used. **Financial management** is controlling the money day to day so that the business stays profitable and can pay its bills.

### Capital needed

| Type | Used for | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fixed capital** | Long-term assets | Furniture and racks, refrigerator, computer and software, air-conditioner, signboard, deposit for premises |
| **Working capital** | Day-to-day running | Opening stock, rent, salaries, electricity, licence and other fees, credit given to customers |

Sources of money: owner's savings, partners, bank loans (including government schemes for small businesses), credit from suppliers.

### Key financial terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Cost price | Price paid to the supplier |
| Selling price / MRP | Price charged to the customer; cannot exceed the MRP printed on the pack |
| Gross margin | Selling price – cost price (often expressed as a percentage) |
| Net profit | Gross profit – all expenses (rent, salary, electricity, interest) |
| Fixed costs | Do not change with sales (rent, salaries) |
| Variable costs | Change with sales (cost of goods sold) |
| Break-even point | Sales level at which total income equals total cost – no profit, no loss |
| Budget | Written plan of expected income and expenses for a period |
| Cash flow | Movement of money in and out; a profitable shop can still fail if cash runs short |
| Depreciation | Fall in value of fixed assets over time |

```text
Break-even sales (in rupees) = Fixed costs ÷ Gross margin ratio
Example: fixed costs ₹60,000 a month, average gross margin 20% (0.20)
         Break-even sales = 60,000 ÷ 0.20 = ₹3,00,000 a month
```

### Price control

Prices of scheduled formulations are controlled under the **Drugs (Prices Control) Order, 2013**, with ceiling prices notified by the **National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA)**. The retailer must not sell above the MRP and must follow notified price revisions.

### Good financial management practices

1. Prepare a yearly budget and compare actual figures monthly.
2. Control stock – stock is the largest investment (see inventory control, Unit 8).
3. Limit credit to customers; collect dues on time.
4. Pay suppliers within the credit period to keep discounts and goodwill.
5. Keep separate business and personal money.
6. Keep complete accounts and file GST and income-tax returns on time.
7. Review margins; avoid heavy discounting that causes losses.
8. Insure the stock and premises.

## 9.2 Accountancy in community pharmacy

**Accountancy** is recording, classifying and summarising business transactions in money terms, so that the owner knows the profit or loss and the financial position.

### Basic ideas

- **Double-entry system:** every transaction affects two accounts – one is debited and the other credited by the same amount.
- **Golden rules:**
  - Personal account – debit the receiver, credit the giver.
  - Real account – debit what comes in, credit what goes out.
  - Nominal account – debit all expenses and losses, credit all incomes and gains.

```text
Transactions → Day book (journal) → Ledger accounts → Trial balance
            → Trading and Profit & Loss account → Balance sheet
```

### Day book (journal)

The **day book** is the book of original entry in which every transaction is recorded on the day it happens, in date order, before being posted to the ledger. Many shops keep separate subsidiary day books:

| Day book | Records |
| --- | --- |
| Purchase day book | Credit purchases from suppliers (invoice number, supplier, amount) |
| Sales day book | Credit sales to customers |
| Purchase returns book | Goods returned to suppliers (expiry, damage) |
| Sales returns book | Goods returned by customers |
| Journal proper | Other entries – depreciation, corrections, opening entries |

Example of a journal entry:

```text
Date        Particulars                         L.F.   Debit (₹)   Credit (₹)
01/07       Purchases A/c               Dr             25,000
               To ABC Distributors A/c                              25,000
            (Medicines bought on credit, Inv. 1172)
```

### Cash book

The **cash book** records all receipts and payments of cash (and, in the two- and three-column forms, bank transactions). Receipts are written on the left (debit) side and payments on the right (credit) side. It serves as both a book of original entry and the cash account in the ledger.

| Type | Columns |
| --- | --- |
| Single-column cash book | Cash only |
| Double-column cash book | Cash and bank |
| Triple-column cash book | Cash, bank and discount |
| Petty cash book | Small daily expenses (tea, courier, stationery), often on the imprest system |

Example (single column):

```text
Dr                                                                    Cr
Date   Receipts                ₹        Date   Payments                 ₹
01/07  To Balance b/d       5,000      01/07  By Electricity bill     2,000
01/07  To Cash sales       18,500      01/07  By Salary (helper)      8,000
                                       01/07  By Balance c/d         13,500
                          23,500                                    23,500
```

The cash balance is counted and matched with the cash book every day. The balance can never be negative (a cash book cannot show a credit balance).

### Other records a pharmacy keeps

Purchase and sale registers (now mostly in software), GST records, stock register, Schedule H1 and X registers, prescription records, expiry and returns register, refrigerator temperature log, staff attendance and salary register.

## 9.3 Pharmacy operation software

Pharmacy management software is a computer program designed for the day-to-day work of a retail pharmacy.

### Usefulness

| Function | Benefit |
| --- | --- |
| Billing | Fast, accurate bills with GST, batch, expiry and MRP; discounts |
| Inventory | Real-time stock, reorder alerts, expiry alerts, FEFO batch selection, dead-stock reports |
| Purchase | Purchase orders, invoice entry, supplier records, pending payments |
| Accounts | Day book, cash book, ledgers, GST returns, profit and loss |
| Patient records | Medication history, allergies, refill reminders |
| Clinical checks | Drug–drug interaction alerts, dose checks (in some packages) |
| Scheduled drugs | Automatic H1 register entries and reports |
| Labels | Printed dispensing labels and barcodes |
| Reports | Sales by item, by doctor, by period; fast and slow movers |
| CRM | Loyalty points, SMS reminders, home-delivery orders |

### Availability

Many Indian software companies sell chemist and pharmacy software, as desktop or cloud (online) versions and mobile apps; chain pharmacies often use their own systems. When choosing, consider: cost and licence type, ease of use, GST compliance, support and training, data backup and security, integration with barcode scanners and printers, and ability to link with distributors and digital health systems.

**Limitations:** cost, need for training, power and internet dependence, data loss if no backup, and privacy risks.

## 9.4 Customer Relationship Management (CRM)

**CRM** is the set of methods a pharmacy uses to build and keep long-term good relations with its customers, so that they are satisfied, trust the pharmacist and keep returning.

### CRM activities in a pharmacy

- Friendly, respectful service; calling regular customers by name.
- Patient records and personalised counselling.
- Refill reminders by SMS or phone for chronic patients.
- Home delivery for the elderly and disabled.
- Loyalty cards, membership and fair discounts.
- Free health camps (BP, glucose) and awareness programmes.
- Quick handling of complaints and feedback forms.
- Ensuring availability of regularly used medicines.
- Follow-up calls after new treatment.

### Benefits

Customer loyalty, more prescriptions, word-of-mouth publicity, better adherence and health outcomes, and stable income.

A CRM programme must respect **privacy** – patient information is used only for the patient's care and not shared or misused.

## 9.5 Audits in pharmacies

An **audit** is a systematic, independent check of records, activities or practices against set standards, to find gaps and improve.

| Type | What is checked | By whom |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Financial audit | Accounts, cash, bills, tax records | Chartered accountant / auditor |
| Stock (inventory) audit | Physical stock vs records; expiry; losses | Owner, auditor, company |
| Statutory / regulatory inspection | Licence conditions, pharmacist presence, records of scheduled drugs, storage, prescriptions | Drugs Inspector |
| Prescription / dispensing audit | Sample of prescriptions for errors, labelling, counselling | Pharmacist in charge |
| Clinical / service audit | Screening services, counselling quality, patient outcomes | Pharmacist, professional body |
| Self-inspection (internal audit) | Compliance with SOPs, cleanliness, cold chain, temperature logs | Pharmacy staff, using a checklist |
| Customer satisfaction audit | Feedback, complaints | Management |

### Audit cycle

```text
Set standard → Measure current practice → Compare with standard → Find gaps
     ↑                                                             ↓
 Re-audit  ←────────────── Make changes (action plan) ←───────────┘
```

Benefits: legal compliance, fewer errors and losses, better quality of service, readiness for inspection, and continual improvement.

## 9.6 SOP of pharmacy management

SOPs (see Unit 1 for definition and format) are needed for the management side of the pharmacy too. Typical management SOPs:

| SOP | Main steps |
| --- | --- |
| Opening and closing the pharmacy | Check security, switch on refrigerator check, record temperature, count opening cash; at close, cash tally, back up data, lock Schedule X cupboard |
| Ordering and receiving stock | Prepare want list, purchase order, check invoice against goods (batch, expiry, quantity, temperature), enter in software, store |
| Storage and cold chain | Storage conditions, temperature recording twice daily, action if out of range |
| Expiry management | Monthly expiry check, separate near-expiry stock, return/dispose and record |
| Billing and cash handling | Bill every sale, cash counting, daily cash book balance, bank deposit |
| Handling of scheduled drugs | Check prescription, enter in register, stamp prescription, store securely |
| Handling complaints and returns | Listen, record, investigate, respond, prevent repeat |
| Data backup | Daily backup, off-site copy, password policy |
| Cleaning and pest control | Daily, weekly and monthly cleaning schedule; record |
| Staff training | Induction, SOP training records, refresher sessions |

## 9.7 Digital health, mHealth and online pharmacies

### Digital health

**Digital health** is the use of information and communication technology to support health and health care – electronic health records, e-prescriptions, telemedicine, health apps, wearable devices and data analysis.

In India:

- **Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM)** is building a national digital health system, including a unique health ID for each person (ABHA – Ayushman Bharat Health Account), registries of health professionals and facilities, and linking of health records with the person's consent.
- **Telemedicine Practice Guidelines (2020)** allow registered doctors to consult by video, phone or chat and issue prescriptions.
- Government apps and portals exist for programmes such as TB (Ni-kshay), vaccination (CoWIN) and teleconsultation (eSanjeevani).

### mHealth (mobile health)

**mHealth** is the use of mobile phones and other wireless devices for health. Examples useful in pharmacy:

- Medication reminder apps and SMS refill reminders.
- Apps that record BP, glucose and peak flow and share them with the doctor.
- Health education by messages and videos.
- Ordering medicines and booking screening services from a phone.
- Connected devices (Bluetooth glucometers, BP monitors).

Benefits: reach and convenience, better adherence, self-monitoring. Problems: poor internet in some areas, low digital literacy, data privacy and unreliable apps.

### Online pharmacies (e-pharmacies)

An **online pharmacy** sells medicines through a website or mobile app and delivers them to the patient's home. The patient uploads a prescription; a pharmacist checks it before dispatch.

| Advantages | Disadvantages / risks |
| --- | --- |
| Convenient; home delivery; useful for elderly, disabled and remote patients | Risk of supply of prescription drugs without proper prescription checking |
| Price comparison and discounts | Fake or spurious medicines from illegal websites |
| Wide range; easy refills | No face-to-face counselling |
| Records and reminders | Delivery delays; cold-chain problems |
| Privacy for sensitive products | Data privacy concerns; misuse of habit-forming drugs |

**Legal position in India:** online sale is not yet covered by a complete separate law. Draft rules on the sale of drugs by e-pharmacies were published by the government in 2018 but had not been finalised for a long time, and the matter has been before the courts. Licensed operators work under the existing retail licence conditions, dispensing against valid prescriptions under a registered pharmacist. Students should check the current rules.

**Role of the community pharmacist:** adopt digital tools (software, e-prescriptions, reminders, home delivery) while keeping the advantage of personal counselling and trust; guide patients to buy only from licensed sources.

## Remember

- Capital: fixed (furniture, refrigerator, computer) and working (stock, rent, salaries); break-even sales = fixed costs ÷ gross margin ratio; prices under DPCO 2013 and NPPA.
- Accounts: double entry; day book (journal) = book of original entry in date order; cash book = cash receipts (left/Dr) and payments (right/Cr); single, double, triple column, petty cash book.
- Software: billing, inventory, expiry alerts, accounts, H1 register, interaction checks, labels, reports.
- CRM: refill reminders, home delivery, loyalty, health camps, complaints handling – with patient privacy.
- Audits: financial, stock, regulatory inspection, prescription, clinical, self-inspection; audit cycle.
- Management SOPs: opening/closing, ordering, cold chain, expiry, cash, scheduled drugs, complaints, backup.
- Digital health: ABDM and ABHA, telemedicine guidelines 2020, mHealth apps; e-pharmacies convenient but need control – draft rules 2018 not finalised.

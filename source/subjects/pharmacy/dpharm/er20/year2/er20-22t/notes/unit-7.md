# Unit 7: Minor ailments and advice for self-care

This unit is the second half of syllabus chapter 9. It covers responding to symptoms and self-care advice for pain, cough, cold, diarrhoea, constipation, vomiting, fever, sore throat, skin disorders and oral health (mouth ulcers, dental pain, gum swelling).

For every condition, learn four things: **what it is and common causes**, **self-care (non-drug) advice**, **suitable OTC products**, and **when to refer**. Doses given here are usual adult doses; always check the product label and adjust for children.

## 7.1 Pain management

### Common types

Headache (tension type, migraine), backache, muscle and joint pain (sprains, strains), period pain (dysmenorrhoea), toothache.

### Self-care

- Rest, good posture, regular sleep, enough water, regular meals.
- For sprains – **RICE**: Rest, Ice (wrapped, 15–20 minutes), Compression, Elevation.
- Warm compresses for muscle stiffness and period pain.
- Relaxation and stress control for tension headache.

### OTC products

| Product | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Paracetamol | First choice for most mild pain and fever; safe in pregnancy at normal doses; watch daily maximum and duplicate products |
| Ibuprofen | Anti-inflammatory; take with food; avoid in peptic ulcer, asthma made worse by NSAIDs, kidney disease, late pregnancy, and with anticoagulants |
| Topical NSAID gels (diclofenac) | Local muscle and joint pain; fewer stomach effects |
| Counter-irritant balms (menthol, methyl salicylate) | Mild muscle pain; not on broken skin |

### Refer when

Sudden severe ("worst ever") headache, headache with fever and stiff neck, after head injury, with vision loss, weakness or confusion; chest pain; back pain with leg weakness, loss of bladder or bowel control; pain lasting more than about a week despite treatment; pain needing painkillers most days.

## 7.2 Cough

### Types

- **Dry (unproductive)** cough – throat irritation, after a cold, allergy, smoke; also a side effect of ACE inhibitors.
- **Productive (wet)** cough – brings up sputum (chest infection, smoking).

### Self-care

Warm fluids, honey and lemon in warm water (not for babies under one year), steam inhalation, avoid smoke and dust, stop smoking.

### OTC products

| Cough | Product type | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dry, irritating | Demulcent / soothing syrups and lozenges | Simple linctus, glycerin-based syrups |
| Dry, disturbing sleep | Cough suppressant (adults) | Dextromethorphan |
| Productive | Expectorant / mucolytic | Guaifenesin, bromhexine, ambroxol |

Avoid giving a suppressant for a productive cough. Codeine-containing syrups are prescription items and are open to misuse.

### Refer when

Cough more than 2–3 weeks; blood in sputum; breathlessness, wheezing or chest pain; high fever; weight loss and night sweats (possible TB – refer for sputum testing); cough in a baby; suspected drug-induced cough (ACE inhibitor) – refer to the prescriber.

## 7.3 Common cold

### What it is

A viral infection of the nose and throat: runny or blocked nose, sneezing, mild sore throat, mild fever, tiredness. It usually gets better in 7–10 days. **Antibiotics do not help.**

### Self-care

Rest, plenty of warm fluids, steam inhalation, saline nasal drops or spray, hand washing and covering coughs and sneezes.

### OTC products

| Symptom | Product | Caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blocked nose | Saline drops; xylometazoline or oxymetazoline nasal drops | Decongestant drops not for more than about 5–7 days (rebound congestion); not for young children unless paediatric strength |
| Runny nose, sneezing | Antihistamines – cetirizine 10 mg once daily; chlorphenamine | Sedating ones cause drowsiness – warn about driving |
| Fever, aches | Paracetamol | Avoid double paracetamol from combination cold products |
| Oral decongestants (phenylephrine combinations) | | Avoid in hypertension, heart disease, thyroid disease |

### Refer when

Symptoms longer than about 10 days; high fever; ear pain; facial pain and thick discharge (sinusitis); breathlessness or wheeze; infants; patients with asthma, COPD or low immunity getting worse.

## 7.4 Diarrhoea

### What it is

Passing loose or watery stools three or more times a day. Most acute diarrhoea is due to infection (viral, bacterial, food poisoning) and settles in a few days. The main danger is **dehydration**, especially in children and the elderly.

### Self-care

- **Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS)** – the most important treatment. Use WHO low-osmolarity ORS; dissolve one sachet in the stated volume of clean water; give small frequent sips; discard after 24 hours.
- Continue feeding; continue breast-feeding in infants.
- Hand washing, safe drinking water and food hygiene.

### OTC products

| Product | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| ORS | For all ages |
| Zinc (children) | Given with ORS for 10–14 days in children with diarrhoea (20 mg a day above 6 months, 10 mg a day below 6 months, as per WHO/national guidance) – shortens the episode |
| Loperamide | Adults only, for symptom control; not with high fever or blood in stool; not for young children |
| Probiotics | May help some patients |

Antibiotics and antimicrobial combinations should not be sold for simple diarrhoea without a prescription.

### Refer when

Blood or mucus in stool; high fever; signs of dehydration (very little urine, dry mouth, sunken eyes, drowsiness, no tears in children); lasting more than 2–3 days in adults (or 24 hours in infants); severe abdominal pain; recent travel or hospital stay; elderly or very young; after an antibiotic course.

## 7.5 Constipation

### What it is

Passing hard stools less often than normal for the person, with straining. Causes: low fibre and fluid intake, inactivity, ignoring the urge, pregnancy, and drugs (opioids, iron, calcium and aluminium antacids, some antidepressants, verapamil).

### Self-care

More fibre (fruits, vegetables, whole grains), 2–3 litres of fluid a day if not restricted, regular exercise, not delaying the toilet, regular routine.

### OTC laxatives

| Type | Examples | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bulk-forming | Ispaghula (psyllium) husk | First choice; take with plenty of water; works in 1–3 days |
| Osmotic | Lactulose, macrogol (PEG) | Safe in many groups; lactulose may cause wind |
| Stimulant | Bisacodyl, senna | Short-term use; may cause cramps; works overnight |
| Stool softener | Docusate, liquid paraffin (old) | Paraffin not for long use |
| Rectal | Glycerin suppository | Quick action |

Warn against long-term laxative misuse (sometimes for weight loss).

### Refer when

Recent change in bowel habit in people over about 40–50 years, blood in stool, weight loss, severe abdominal pain or vomiting, constipation alternating with diarrhoea, lasting more than two weeks despite self-care, suspected drug cause, children with long-standing constipation.

## 7.6 Vomiting (nausea and vomiting)

### Causes

Stomach infection, food poisoning, motion sickness, pregnancy, migraine, alcohol, drugs (antibiotics, metformin, opioids), and serious conditions (appendicitis, raised pressure in the head, diabetic ketoacidosis).

### Self-care

Small sips of ORS or clear fluids; small, bland meals once vomiting settles; avoid fatty and spicy foods; ginger may help mild nausea.

### OTC products

| Use | Product |
| --- | --- |
| Motion sickness | Antihistamines (promethazine, cinnarizine) taken before travel – cause drowsiness |
| Dehydration | ORS |
| Nausea of indigestion | Antacids |

Many antiemetics (for example ondansetron, domperidone) are prescription medicines and need a doctor's assessment.

### Refer when

Blood or "coffee-ground" material in vomit; vomiting for more than 24–48 hours (shorter in babies and elderly); signs of dehydration; severe abdominal pain; headache with stiff neck; head injury; pregnancy with severe vomiting; diabetic patients; suspected poisoning or drug overdose.

## 7.7 Fever

### What it is

Body temperature above normal (about 37 °C); usually taken as 38 °C or more. It is a sign, not a disease – usually a response to infection.

### Self-care

Plenty of fluids, rest, light clothing, tepid sponging (not cold water), light food. Check temperature with a thermometer and keep a record.

### OTC products

- **Paracetamol** is the preferred antipyretic (adults usually 500 mg–1 g every 4–6 hours, within the daily maximum; children by weight using the paediatric product and the measuring device supplied).
- **Ibuprofen** is an alternative where suitable.
- **Aspirin should not be given to children and teenagers** (risk of Reye's syndrome).

### Refer when

Fever more than 3 days; very high fever; fever in infants under about 3 months; fits; rash that does not fade on pressing; stiff neck, severe headache, confusion; breathing difficulty; repeated vomiting; fever after travel or in malaria/dengue areas; fever with burning urine or severe pain.

## 7.8 Sore throat

### What it is

Pain or scratchiness in the throat, mostly viral (with a cold) and self-limiting in about a week. Bacterial (streptococcal) infection is less common.

### Self-care

Warm salt-water gargles, warm drinks, honey (not under one year), rest, avoid smoking.

### OTC products

Lozenges (antiseptic, menthol, local anaesthetic such as benzocaine), throat sprays, povidone-iodine or chlorhexidine gargles, paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain.

### Refer when

Severe pain or difficulty swallowing or breathing; drooling; high fever with white patches on tonsils and swollen glands and no cough; lasting more than about a week; hoarseness for more than 3 weeks; recurrent attacks; patient on drugs that lower white cells (for example carbimazole, clozapine – possible agranulocytosis).

## 7.9 Skin disorders

| Condition | Features | Self-care and OTC | Refer when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Acne | Spots, blackheads on face, chest, back in teenagers | Wash twice daily with mild cleanser; do not squeeze; benzoyl peroxide gel (start low strength; bleaches fabric) | Severe, scarring or cyst acne; acne in adults starting suddenly |
| Fungal infections (ringworm, athlete's foot, jock itch) | Itchy red ring-shaped patches, cracks between toes | Keep area dry, cotton clothes, separate towel; clotrimazole or miconazole cream twice daily, continue 1–2 weeks after clearing | Scalp or nail infection, widespread, diabetic patients, no improvement in 2 weeks |
| Scabies | Severe itch worse at night, burrows between fingers; family members affected | Permethrin 5% cream over whole body from neck down, wash off after 8–12 hours, repeat after a week as directed; treat all contacts at the same time; wash clothes and bedding | Infants, pregnancy, infected sores |
| Dandruff | White flakes, itchy scalp | Ketoconazole or selenium sulphide shampoo | Red, weeping or spreading rash |
| Dry skin / eczema (mild) | Dry, itchy patches | Regular moisturisers (emollients), avoid soaps; mild hydrocortisone cream only for short periods as per label | Infected, widespread or on face of children |
| Insect bites, prickly heat, sunburn | Itch, redness | Calamine lotion, cool compresses, oral antihistamine; sunscreen to prevent | Signs of infection or allergy (swelling of face, breathing difficulty) |
| Minor cuts, burns | | Clean with water; antiseptic; cool burns under running water for about 20 minutes; do not apply butter or toothpaste | Deep, large, facial or electrical burns; signs of infection |
| Head lice | Itchy scalp, nits on hair | Permethrin lotion as directed; wet combing with a fine comb; treat contacts | |

## 7.10 Oral health

### Mouth ulcers (aphthous ulcers)

- Small painful round ulcers inside the mouth; heal in 1–2 weeks. Triggers: injury, stress, spicy food, vitamin deficiency.
- Self-care: avoid spicy and hot foods; good oral hygiene; salt-water rinse.
- OTC: choline salicylate or benzocaine gels, chlorhexidine mouthwash, protective pastes.
- **Refer:** ulcer lasting more than 3 weeks (possible oral cancer, especially in tobacco and areca-nut users), very large or many ulcers, with fever or weight loss, possible drug cause.

### Dental pain (toothache)

- Usually caries (decay), cracked tooth or infection.
- Short-term relief: paracetamol or ibuprofen; clove oil may soothe locally; rinse with warm salt water.
- **Always refer to a dentist** – pain relief only until the dental visit; urgent if facial swelling or fever (abscess).

### Gum swelling and bleeding (gingivitis)

- Caused by plaque from poor brushing; gums red, swollen, bleed on brushing.
- Self-care: brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste using a soft brush; floss; regular dental check-ups; chlorhexidine mouthwash short-term (may stain teeth).
- **Refer:** to a dentist if it persists, pus, loose teeth, or if on drugs that cause gum overgrowth (phenytoin, nifedipine, ciclosporin).

## Remember

- Method for every ailment: assess (WWHAM) → self-care advice → suitable OTC → red flags → refer.
- Pain: paracetamol first; ibuprofen with food, avoid in ulcer, asthma sensitivity, kidney disease; RICE for sprains.
- Cough: suppressant for dry, expectorant for wet; cough longer than 2–3 weeks or blood → refer (TB).
- Cold: viral, no antibiotics; nasal decongestant drops for no more than about a week.
- Diarrhoea: ORS is the key; zinc for children; loperamide adults only; refer if blood, fever, dehydration.
- Constipation: fibre, fluids, bulk laxative first; refer change of bowel habit with weight loss or blood.
- Fever: paracetamol, fluids, tepid sponging; no aspirin in children; refer after 3 days or with danger signs.
- Skin: clotrimazole for fungal, permethrin for scabies (treat all contacts), benzoyl peroxide for acne.
- Oral: mouth ulcer more than 3 weeks → refer; dental pain → dentist; gingivitis → brushing, flossing, chlorhexidine.

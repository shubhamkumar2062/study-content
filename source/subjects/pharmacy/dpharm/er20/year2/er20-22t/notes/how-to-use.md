# How to use this guide

Community Pharmacy and Management (ER20-22T) is a 75-hour second-year D.Pharm subject. It is a practice subject: most answers are about **what the pharmacist does, in what order, and why**. Learn the steps, tables and mnemonics, and practise saying the counselling points aloud as if a patient were in front of you.

The official syllabus numbers its chapters 1 to 7, then 9 and 10 (there is no chapter 8 in the PCI document). In this guide two pairs of small chapters are joined and the two largest chapters are split:

| Guide unit | Syllabus chapter | Hours in syllabus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Community pharmacy practice, responsibilities, GPP and SOPs | 1 and 2 | 2 + 3 |
| 2. Prescription and prescription handling | 3 | 7 |
| 3. Communication skills | 4 | 6 |
| 4. Patient counselling | 5 | 10 |
| 5. Medication adherence and health screening | 6 and 7 | 2 + 5 |
| 6. OTC medications, self-medication, responding to symptoms | 9 (first half) | 15 (shared) |
| 7. Minor ailments and self-care advice | 9 (second half) | |
| 8. Setting up a pharmacy, purchasing and inventory control | 10 (first half) | 25 (shared) |
| 9. Finance, accounts, software, CRM, audits, SOPs, digital health | 10 (second half) | |

Chapters 9 and 10 together carry 40 of the 75 hours, so Units 6–9 deserve more than half of your study time.

## Exam pattern (ER-2020 theory paper)

D.Pharm ER-2020 exams are yearly and are conducted by the state examining board (for example HPTSB in Himachal Pradesh) under the PCI ER-2020 regulations. The theory paper is **80 marks, 3 hours**; another 20 marks come from sessional (internal) assessment. The usual ER-2020 paper looks like this. Confirm the pattern with your college, because boards sometimes change the format.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Objective questions (MCQs / one-word / fill in the blanks) – all compulsory | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| II | Short answers – attempt 10 of 11 | 10 × 3 = 30 |
| III | Long answers – attempt 6 of 7 | 6 × 5 = 30 |

In this guide **long** questions are 5-mark answers and **short** questions are 3-mark answers, to match this pattern. A few **very-short** (2-mark) questions are added for quick revision and sessional tests.

## Probability ratings

- **High** – asked in most years or a core topic of the chapter; prepare the full answer.
- **Medium** – asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** – rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

The ratings are an estimate from the syllabus hours and the kind of questions usually set in D.Pharm papers. They are not a promise.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. **Begin with a definition.** Prescription, patient counselling, adherence, OTC, self-medication, GPP, SOP, CRM, audit, inventory control – one or two clean lines first.
2. **Write steps as a numbered flow.** Prescription handling, dispensing process, stages of counselling, responding to symptoms and ordering stock are all step answers. A small ```text flow chart (A → B → C) earns marks.
3. **Use tables** for parts of a prescription, Latin abbreviations, ancillary labels, barriers and their remedies, WHO adherence factors, OTC categories, ABC/VED/FSN, licences, and types of audit.
4. **Learn the mnemonics:** WWHAM, ASMETHOD, SOLER, RICE, FEFO, and the three prime questions of counselling.
5. **Quote Indian law correctly:** Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940 and Rules 1945; Pharmacy Act 1948; retail licences in Form 20 and Form 21; at least 10 m² retail area; Schedule H1 register kept 3 years; Schedule X prescription copy kept 2 years; Pharmacy Practice Regulations 2015; DPCO 2013. Do not guess fees or form numbers you are not sure of.
6. **For counselling questions** (hypertension, diabetes, asthma, TB, COPD, AIDS), write under headings: disease, medicines and how to take them, side effects, monitoring, lifestyle, danger signs.
7. **For minor ailment questions**, always write four parts: self-care advice, OTC products, counselling, and **when to refer**. Examiners look for referral points.
8. **For a 5-mark long answer**, aim for about one and a half pages: definition, flow chart or table, 5–6 explained points, a short conclusion. **For a 3-mark short answer**, 5–6 crisp points or a small table.
9. **Use your practical work.** Blood pressure measurement, glucometer use, inhaler technique, label writing and prescription checking are asked in theory too; the practical guides in this pack describe them step by step.

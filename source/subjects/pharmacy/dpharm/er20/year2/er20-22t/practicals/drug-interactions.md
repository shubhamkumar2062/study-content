# Identifying drug–drug interactions in prescriptions and follow-up action

**Aim:** To identify drug–drug interactions in given prescriptions and decide the follow-up action (minimum two prescriptions).

## Principle

A **drug–drug interaction** happens when one drug changes the effect of another. It may be:

- **Pharmacokinetic** – one drug changes the absorption, distribution, metabolism or excretion of another (for example rifampicin speeds up the breakdown of oral contraceptives; antacids reduce absorption of tetracyclines and fluoroquinolones).
- **Pharmacodynamic** – the two drugs add to or oppose each other's action (for example warfarin with aspirin increases bleeding; two sedatives increase drowsiness).

Interactions are graded as **major** (avoid or needs urgent action), **moderate** (monitor or adjust) and **minor**. The pharmacist identifies them, judges their importance and acts – by counselling, timing the doses apart, or contacting the prescriber.

## Requirements

Simulated prescriptions with interacting drugs, drug interaction reference (National Formulary of India, standard reference books or interaction-checking software), practical record book.

## Procedure

1. Read the prescription and list all drugs, including any OTC or herbal products the patient says they take.
2. Check each pair of drugs in the reference book or software.
3. Write the interacting pair, the mechanism and the likely result.
4. Grade the severity (major / moderate / minor).
5. Decide the action:
   - Minor – counsel only.
   - Separable by time – advise a gap (for example antacid and ciprofloxacin at least 2 hours apart).
   - Moderate – counsel and monitor; inform the prescriber if needed.
   - Major – contact the prescriber before dispensing; suggest an alternative.
6. Role-play the phone call to the prescriber using SBAR.
7. Record the interaction, action and outcome.

## Observations

| Rx | Interacting drugs | Mechanism | Possible effect | Severity | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Warfarin + aspirin | Pharmacodynamic; aspirin also affects platelets and stomach lining | Increased bleeding | Major | Contacted prescriber; paracetamol suggested for pain |
| 2 | Ciprofloxacin + aluminium–magnesium antacid | Chelation reduces absorption | Lower antibiotic effect | Moderate | Take ciprofloxacin at least 2 hours before the antacid |
| 3 | Rifampicin + oral contraceptive | Enzyme induction | Contraceptive failure | Major | Counsel to use another method; inform prescriber |

## Result

Interactions in the given prescriptions were identified, graded and the appropriate follow-up actions recorded.

## Precautions

1. Use an up-to-date reference; do not rely on memory.
2. Ask about OTC, herbal and home remedies too.
3. Do not alarm the patient; discuss serious problems with the prescriber first.
4. Record every intervention.

## Viva questions

1. **Define drug interaction.** A change in the effect of one drug caused by another drug, food or substance.
2. **Give an example of enzyme induction.** Rifampicin reducing the effect of oral contraceptives.
3. **Why should tetracycline not be taken with antacids?** Metal ions chelate it and reduce absorption.
4. **What do you do for a major interaction?** Contact the prescriber before dispensing.
5. **What is SBAR?** Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation – a structure for talking to prescribers.

# Counselling a simulated patient with rheumatoid arthritis

**Aim:** To counsel a simulated patient with rheumatoid arthritis on the disease, disease-modifying and pain medicines, monitoring and joint care.

## Principle

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a long-term autoimmune disease in which the body's immune system attacks the lining of joints, causing pain, swelling and morning stiffness, usually in small joints of the hands and feet on both sides. Early and regular use of **disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs)** such as methotrexate, hydroxychloroquine and sulfasalazine slows joint damage. NSAIDs and short courses of corticosteroids relieve symptoms.

## Requirements

Counselling area, simulated prescription (for example methotrexate tablets once weekly, folic acid, and an NSAID as needed), weekly calendar, leaflet, assessment form.

## Procedure

1. **Introduction** and prime questions.
2. **Disease:** long-term; medicines control it and protect joints; benefit of DMARDs takes weeks to months.
3. **Methotrexate (most important safety point):**
   - Taken **once a week**, on the same day each week – never daily. Mark the day on a calendar.
   - Folic acid is given to reduce side effects, on the day(s) the doctor states.
   - Regular blood tests (blood counts, liver and kidney function).
   - Avoid alcohol.
   - Report mouth ulcers, sore throat, fever, unusual bruising, breathlessness or cough.
   - Avoid pregnancy during treatment and for the time the doctor advises afterwards (both men and women should ask).
   - Check with the pharmacist before taking other medicines (some antibiotics such as trimethoprim interact).
4. **Hydroxychloroquine (if prescribed):** take with food; regular eye check-ups.
5. **NSAIDs:** take with food; report stomach pain or black stools.
6. **Corticosteroids (if prescribed):** take in the morning with food; do not stop suddenly after long use.
7. **Joint care:** balance rest and gentle exercise; physiotherapy; warm compresses for stiffness; joint protection (use both hands, avoid tight gripping); healthy weight; stop smoking.
8. **Vaccination:** discuss vaccines with the doctor because some DMARDs lower immunity.
9. **Closing:** summary, teach-back ("Which day will you take methotrexate?"), leaflet, follow-up, documentation.

## Result

The simulated patient was counselled on rheumatoid arthritis and correctly stated the once-weekly methotrexate schedule.

## Precautions

1. Repeat and confirm "once a week" for methotrexate – daily dosing has caused serious harm.
2. Use a calendar or weekly reminder.
3. Refer fever, mouth ulcers or bruising promptly.

## Viva questions

1. **How often is methotrexate taken for RA?** Once a week.
2. **Why is folic acid given?** To reduce methotrexate side effects.
3. **Which tests are needed with methotrexate?** Blood counts and liver and kidney function.
4. **What is a DMARD?** A drug that slows the disease process and joint damage.
5. **Eye check-ups are needed with which drug?** Hydroxychloroquine.

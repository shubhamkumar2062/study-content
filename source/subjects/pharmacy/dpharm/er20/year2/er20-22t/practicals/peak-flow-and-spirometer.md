# Lung function assessment with a peak flow meter and incentive spirometer

**Aim:** To measure peak expiratory flow rate (PEFR) with a peak flow meter, and to demonstrate the use of an incentive spirometer.

## Principle

**Peak expiratory flow rate** is the fastest speed (litres per minute) at which a person can blow air out after a full breath in. It falls when the airways are narrowed, so it is used to monitor asthma and to follow an asthma action plan. Results are compared with the person's **personal best** or with a **predicted value** for age, sex and height.

An **incentive spirometer** encourages slow, deep breathing in. The patient breathes in through the mouthpiece and tries to raise a piston or ball to a target level. It is used after surgery and in lung disease to expand the lungs and prevent collapse of air sacs.

## Requirements

Peak flow meter with disposable mouthpieces, predicted-value chart, incentive spirometer, record sheet, tissues.

## Procedure

### Peak flow meter

1. Explain the test; fit a new mouthpiece; set the pointer to zero.
2. The subject stands (or sits upright) and holds the meter horizontally without blocking the pointer.
3. Take the deepest possible breath in.
4. Seal the lips tightly around the mouthpiece.
5. Blow out as hard and fast as possible in one short blast.
6. Note the reading; reset the pointer.
7. Repeat three times; record the **highest** of the three.
8. Compare with personal best or predicted value and find the zone.

### Incentive spirometer

1. Sit upright; hold the device upright at eye level.
2. Breathe out normally, then seal lips around the mouthpiece.
3. Breathe in slowly and deeply so that the indicator rises to the target; keep the flow indicator in the "best" range.
4. Hold the breath for 3–5 seconds, then breathe out slowly.
5. Rest a few seconds; repeat about 10 times as advised; cough to clear secretions.

## Precautions

1. Use a fresh mouthpiece for each person.
2. Fingers must not block the pointer; tongue must not block the mouthpiece.
3. Blow as fast as possible – it is a short blast, not a long blow.
4. Take the best of three readings.
5. With the incentive spirometer, breathe in slowly; stop if dizzy.

## Viva questions

1. **What is PEFR?** The fastest rate of air flow during a forced breath out.
2. **Unit of PEFR?** Litres per minute.
3. **Which reading is recorded?** The highest of three.
4. **What does the red zone mean?** Below 50% of best – severe narrowing, urgent medical care.
5. **Purpose of an incentive spirometer?** Encourages slow deep breaths to expand the lungs, e.g. after surgery.

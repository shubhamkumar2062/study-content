# Handling dummy dosage forms with correct administration techniques

**Aim:** To demonstrate and teach the correct handling and administration of common dosage forms using dummy products: oral liquids with measuring cup, cap or dropper, eye drops, inhalers, nasal drops, insulin pen, nebulizers, different types of tablets, transdermal patches, enemas and suppositories.

## Principle

A correct medicine can fail if it is used wrongly. The pharmacist demonstrates each technique with a dummy product and then asks the patient to repeat it (**teach-back**), correcting any mistakes.

## Requirements

Dummy products: oral liquid with measuring cup, dosing cap, oral syringe and dropper; eye drops; nasal drops and spray; placebo inhaler and spacer; insulin pen; nebulizer; plain, enteric-coated, sustained-release, sublingual, chewable, dispersible and effervescent tablets; transdermal patch; enema pack; suppository. Water, tissues, gloves, assessment form.

## Procedure

| Dosage form | Steps to teach |
| --- | --- |
| Oral liquids | Shake if suspension; use the cup, cap, oral syringe or dropper supplied (not a kitchen spoon); read the measure at eye level on a flat surface; for babies, give slowly into the side of the cheek with an oral syringe; wash the measure after use |
| Plain tablets / capsules | Swallow with a full glass of water, sitting or standing |
| Enteric-coated and sustained-release tablets | Swallow whole; do not crush or chew |
| Sublingual tablet (e.g. glyceryl trinitrate) | Place under the tongue, let it dissolve; do not swallow; sit down |
| Chewable tablet | Chew fully before swallowing |
| Dispersible / effervescent tablet | Dissolve in the stated amount of water and drink at once |
| Eye drops | Wash hands; tilt head back; pull lower lid; one drop into the pocket without touching the tip; close eye, press the inner corner for about a minute; 5 minutes between different drops |
| Nasal drops | Blow nose; tilt head back (or lie with head over the bed edge); instil drops; stay in position a minute; clean dropper |
| Nasal spray | Blow nose; head slightly forward; insert tip, aim away from the middle of the nose; spray while breathing in gently |
| Inhaler and spacer | Shake, breathe out, seal lips, press and breathe in slowly, hold 10 seconds; rinse mouth after steroid (see asthma counselling guide) |
| Insulin pen | New needle, prime 2 units, dial dose, inject at 90°, hold 10 seconds, rotate sites (see diabetes counselling guide) |
| Nebulizer | Measure solution into the cup, attach mask, sit upright, breathe normally until the mist stops, clean parts |
| Transdermal patch | Clean, dry, hairless skin; peel liner without touching the adhesive; press for 30 seconds; change at the stated interval; rotate sites; fold used patch sticky sides together before disposal |
| Rectal suppository | Wash hands; remove wrapper; moisten tip with water; lie on the left side with upper knee bent; insert pointed end first about 2–3 cm beyond the anal sphincter; stay lying a few minutes |
| Enema | Lie on the left side with knees bent; lubricate nozzle; insert gently; squeeze the pack slowly; withdraw; hold the liquid for the time on the label |

1. Explain the purpose of the dosage form.
2. Demonstrate each step with the dummy product.
3. Ask the patient (classmate) to repeat the steps.
4. Correct errors and repeat until right.
5. Give storage advice (eye drops discarded after the in-use period, suppositories in a cool place, insulin in a refrigerator).
6. Record on the assessment form.

## Result

Correct administration techniques for the listed dosage forms were demonstrated and checked by teach-back.

## Precautions

1. Use dummy or placebo products only; never use real medicines on classmates.
2. Maintain hygiene – wash hands; do not touch dropper or nozzle tips.
3. Use the measuring device provided with the medicine.
4. Respect privacy when explaining rectal products.

## Viva questions

1. **Why not use a kitchen spoon?** Sizes vary, giving a wrong dose.
2. **Why press the inner corner of the eye after eye drops?** Reduces drainage into the nose and body, keeping more drug in the eye.
3. **Why must sustained-release tablets not be crushed?** The whole dose would be released at once.
4. **How is a sublingual tablet used?** Placed under the tongue to dissolve.
5. **Which side should a patient lie on for an enema or suppository?** The left side, with the upper knee bent.

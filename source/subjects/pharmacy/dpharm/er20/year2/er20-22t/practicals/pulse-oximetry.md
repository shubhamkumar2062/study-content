# Recording capillary oxygen saturation with a pulse oximeter

**Aim:** To record the oxygen saturation (SpO2) and pulse rate of a subject with a fingertip pulse oximeter and interpret the result.

## Principle

Oxygenated and deoxygenated haemoglobin absorb red and infrared light differently. A **pulse oximeter** shines these two lights through the fingertip and measures how much passes through at each heartbeat. From the ratio, it calculates the percentage of haemoglobin carrying oxygen – the **peripheral oxygen saturation (SpO2)** – and also shows the pulse rate.

## Requirements

Fingertip pulse oximeter with working batteries, alcohol swab, record sheet.

## Procedure

1. Explain the test and get consent.
2. Seat the subject and let them rest for a few minutes.
3. Remove nail polish or artificial nails from the finger used; warm cold hands.
4. Clean the oximeter's finger pad.
5. Place the oximeter on the index or middle finger, nail upwards; keep the hand still, resting at heart level.
6. Wait until the reading is steady (usually 30–60 seconds) and the pulse signal is strong.
7. Record SpO2 and pulse rate.
8. Interpret and advise.

## Precautions

1. Remove nail polish; cold fingers and movement give false readings.
2. Wait for a stable reading.
3. Bright light on the sensor can affect the reading.
4. Readings may be less accurate with very poor circulation or darker skin pigmentation; consider the patient's symptoms too.
5. Breathlessness or chest pain needs referral whatever the reading.

## Viva questions

1. **What does SpO2 measure?** Percentage of haemoglobin saturated with oxygen in peripheral blood.
2. **Normal SpO2?** About 95–100%.
3. **Which lights are used?** Red and infrared.
4. **Why remove nail polish?** It blocks light and gives a false reading.
5. **When would you refer urgently?** SpO2 90% or below, or with breathlessness or chest pain.

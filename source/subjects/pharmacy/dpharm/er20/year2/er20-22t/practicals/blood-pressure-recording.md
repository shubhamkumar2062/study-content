# Blood pressure recording

**Aim:** To record the blood pressure of a subject with a sphygmomanometer and interpret the reading for monitoring or referral.

## Principle

**Blood pressure** is the pressure of blood on the walls of the arteries. **Systolic** pressure is the highest pressure when the heart contracts; **diastolic** pressure is the lowest pressure when the heart relaxes. It is written as systolic/diastolic in mmHg.

In the **auscultatory method**, a cuff on the upper arm is inflated to stop blood flow in the brachial artery and then slowly deflated. The first tapping sound heard with a stethoscope (Korotkoff sounds) marks the systolic pressure, and the point where the sounds disappear marks the diastolic pressure. **Digital (oscillometric) monitors** detect the vibrations of the artery wall and calculate the values automatically.

## Requirements

Validated digital BP monitor or aneroid sphygmomanometer with stethoscope, cuffs of correct sizes, chair with back support, table, record sheet.

## Procedure

1. Explain the test and get consent. Ask the subject to avoid smoking, caffeine and exercise for 30 minutes before.
2. Seat the subject comfortably with back supported, feet flat, legs uncrossed; rest for 5 minutes; no talking during measurement.
3. Bare the upper arm; support it on the table at heart level.
4. Choose the correct cuff size (bladder should cover most of the arm circumference). Wrap the cuff snugly with its lower edge about 2–3 cm above the elbow crease and the artery marker over the brachial artery.
5. **Digital:** press start and wait for the reading.
6. **Manual:** feel the radial pulse, inflate until it disappears and note the pressure; deflate. Place the stethoscope over the brachial artery, inflate to about 30 mmHg above that point, then deflate at 2–3 mmHg per second. Note the first sound (systolic) and the disappearance of sounds (diastolic).
7. Take a second reading after 1–2 minutes; record the average. Note pulse rate.
8. Record date, time, arm, position and readings; interpret and advise.

**Interpretation (adults, office readings):**

| Category | Systolic | Diastolic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | Below 120 | Below 80 |
| Raised / high-normal | 120–139 | 80–89 |
| Hypertension | 140 or more | 90 or more |
| Very high – urgent referral | 180 or more | 110 or more |

## Precautions

1. Rest the subject for 5 minutes; arm at heart level; correct cuff size (a small cuff gives a falsely high reading).
2. Do not measure over clothing.
3. Deflate slowly in the manual method.
4. Use validated, calibrated equipment.
5. One raised reading is not a diagnosis – advise repeat readings or referral.

## Viva questions

1. **What is systolic pressure?** Highest arterial pressure during heart contraction.
2. **What are Korotkoff sounds?** Sounds heard over the artery as the cuff is deflated, used to find systolic and diastolic pressure.
3. **What BP is taken as hypertension in a pharmacy screen?** 140/90 mmHg or more on repeated readings.
4. **Effect of a small cuff?** Falsely high reading.
5. **Why rest before measuring?** Activity and anxiety raise BP.

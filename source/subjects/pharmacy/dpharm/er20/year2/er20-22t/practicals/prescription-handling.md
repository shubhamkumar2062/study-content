# Handling and reviewing prescriptions for legal compliance and completeness

**Aim:** To receive, read and review prescriptions with professional standards, checking each for legal compliance and completeness (minimum five prescriptions).

> Carried out in the model community pharmacy using simulated prescriptions. A teacher or classmate acts as the patient; your work is assessed on a structured checklist.

## Principle

A pharmacist must never dispense from a prescription that is unclear, incomplete or not legally valid. A prescription is valid when it is written by a registered medical practitioner (RMP), dentist or veterinarian, carries the prescriber's name, address and registration number, is signed and dated, names the patient, and states the drug, strength, dose and quantity clearly. Extra rules apply to Schedule H, H1 and X drugs under the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945.

## Requirements

Five or more simulated prescriptions (including some with deliberate faults), prescription checklist, drug reference book (for example National Formulary of India), Schedule H1 register format, rubber stamp of the model pharmacy, pen, practical record book.

## Procedure

1. Greet the "patient", take the prescription with both hands and confirm the patient's name and whether the medicine is for them.
2. Read the whole prescription once without stopping; then read it line by line.
3. **Legal check:** prescriber's name, qualification, registration number, address; signature; date; patient name, age, sex, address.
4. **Completeness check** for each drug: generic or brand name, dosage form, strength, dose, frequency, route, duration, total quantity.
5. **Schedule check:** identify any Schedule H, H1 or X drug and the extra requirements (H1 register entry, Schedule X duplicate copy).
6. **Clinical check:** dose suitable for age and weight; obvious interactions; duplicate drugs; allergy or pregnancy questions to ask.
7. Interpret all abbreviations (od, bd, tds, hs, ac, pc, sos, stat).
8. List every problem found and decide the action: dispense, contact prescriber (role play the phone call), or refuse with a polite explanation.
9. For scheduled drugs, show how the prescription is stamped with the pharmacy name and date of supply and how the H1 register is filled.
10. Record each prescription in the observation table.

## Result

## Precautions

1. Never guess an unclear word or dose; always confirm with the prescriber.
2. Do not alter a prescription yourself; record any change agreed by phone (who, what, when).
3. Be polite when refusing; explain the reason and advise the patient.
4. Keep the patient's information confidential.

## Viva questions

1. **What makes a prescription legally valid?** RMP's name, address, registration number, signature, date, patient details and clear drug details.
2. **What is the superscription?** The symbol Rx.
3. **What is the inscription?** The drug name, dosage form and strength.
4. **For how long is the H1 register kept?** Three years.
5. **How long is a Schedule X prescription copy kept?** Two years.
6. **Why is a scheduled-drug prescription stamped?** To show it has been dispensed so that it cannot be used again.
7. **What would you do with an unsigned prescription?** Not dispense; contact the prescriber.

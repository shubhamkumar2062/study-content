# Counselling a simulated patient with asthma (inhaler, spacer and nebulizer use)

**Aim:** To counsel a simulated patient with asthma on the disease, reliever and preventer medicines, and the correct use of a metered-dose inhaler, spacer, dry powder inhaler and nebulizer.

## Principle

Asthma is long-term inflammation of the airways with episodes of wheezing, breathlessness, chest tightness and cough. **Relievers** (short-acting β2 agonists such as salbutamol) open the airways quickly. **Preventers** (inhaled corticosteroids such as budesonide) reduce inflammation when used every day. Most treatment failures are due to poor inhaler technique and irregular preventer use, so the pharmacist checks technique at every visit.

## Requirements

Placebo metered-dose inhaler (MDI), spacer with mouthpiece and face mask, dummy dry powder inhaler (DPI), nebulizer with mask, peak flow meter, asthma action plan format, leaflet, assessment form.

## Procedure

1. **Introduction** and prime questions.
2. **Disease and triggers:** dust, smoke, pollen, pets, cold air, infections, exercise; avoid triggers and smoking.
3. **Reliever vs preventer:** reliever for symptoms; preventer daily even when well; needing the reliever more than usual means asthma is not controlled.
4. **MDI technique (demonstrate, then teach-back):**
   1. Remove cap, shake well.
   2. Sit or stand upright; breathe out fully away from the inhaler.
   3. Seal lips around the mouthpiece.
   4. Start a slow deep breath in and press the canister once.
   5. Continue breathing in slowly and deeply.
   6. Hold breath for about 10 seconds or as long as comfortable.
   7. Wait about 30–60 seconds before a second puff.
   8. After a steroid inhaler, rinse mouth with water and spit.
5. **Spacer:** fit inhaler, shake, one puff into the spacer, breathe in slowly and deeply (or 4–5 normal breaths through the mask for small children); clean the spacer as instructed and let it air-dry.
6. **DPI:** load the dose; do not shake; breathe out away from the device; breathe in **quickly and deeply**; hold the breath; never breathe into the device; keep it dry.
7. **Nebulizer:** put the measured solution into the cup, attach mask or mouthpiece, sit upright, breathe normally until the mist stops (about 10 minutes), wash and dry the parts after use.
8. **Monitoring:** peak flow diary; explain green, yellow and red zones of the action plan.
9. **Emergency:** reliever not helping, cannot speak in full sentences, lips turning blue → emergency care.
10. **Closing:** summary, teach-back, leaflet, follow-up, documentation.

## Result

The simulated patient was counselled on asthma and demonstrated correct inhaler and spacer technique by teach-back.

## Precautions

1. Always demonstrate with a placebo device, then watch the patient.
2. Do not shake DPIs or breathe into them.
3. Stress daily use of preventers.
4. Refer patients with poor control or frequent reliever use.

## Viva questions

1. **Why rinse the mouth after an inhaled steroid?** To prevent oral thrush and hoarseness.
2. **Advantage of a spacer?** Less coordination needed, more drug reaches the lungs, less in the mouth.
3. **How is breathing different for a DPI?** A quick, deep breath in.
4. **Example of a reliever?** Salbutamol.
5. **Sign of poor asthma control?** Needing the reliever often.

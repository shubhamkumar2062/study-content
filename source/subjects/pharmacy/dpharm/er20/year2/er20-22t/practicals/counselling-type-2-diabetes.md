# Counselling a simulated patient with type 2 diabetes mellitus (with insulin pen use)

**Aim:** To counsel a simulated patient with type 2 diabetes mellitus on the disease, medicines, self-monitoring and lifestyle, and to train the patient in the use of an insulin pen.

## Principle

Type 2 diabetes is a long-term disease in which the body does not use insulin properly, causing high blood glucose. Good control prevents damage to the eyes, kidneys, nerves, heart and feet. Counselling follows the four stages – introduction, content, process and closing – and ends with teach-back so that the patient can show correct use of the pen.

## Requirements

Model community pharmacy counselling area, simulated prescription (for example metformin 500 mg twice daily and a basal insulin by pen), dummy insulin pen with pen needles, glucometer for demonstration, patient information leaflet on diabetes, structured assessment form.

## Procedure

1. **Introduction:** greet, introduce yourself, confirm identity, explain the purpose and time, ensure privacy, ask what the patient already knows.
2. **Disease:** explain in simple words what diabetes is and why lifelong control matters.
3. **Metformin:** take with or just after meals; stomach upset early usually settles; tell the doctor before X-ray dye tests or surgery.
4. **Insulin pen technique (demonstrate, then ask the patient to repeat):**
   1. Wash hands; check insulin name and expiry; roll cloudy insulin gently if required.
   2. Attach a new needle; remove both caps.
   3. Prime: dial 2 units, hold needle up, tap, press until a drop appears.
   4. Dial the prescribed dose.
   5. Clean skin if dirty; inject into the abdomen (away from the navel) or thigh at 90°.
   6. Press the button fully and hold for about 10 seconds before withdrawing.
   7. Remove and discard the needle in a hard container; replace the cap.
   8. Rotate injection sites within an area to avoid lumps.
5. **Storage:** unopened pens in a refrigerator at 2–8 °C, never frozen; the in-use pen at room temperature for the period stated on the leaflet; never share pens.
6. **Hypoglycaemia:** sweating, shaking, hunger, confusion → 15–20 g quick sugar (glucose, sugar, juice), recheck after 15 minutes; carry sugar and an identity card.
7. **Monitoring:** home glucose testing with a meter; HbA1c every 3–6 months; yearly eye, kidney and foot checks.
8. **Lifestyle:** regular meals, less sugar and refined starch, more fibre, regular exercise, weight control, no smoking, limit alcohol.
9. **Foot care:** inspect daily, wear proper footwear, treat cuts early.
10. **Closing:** summarise three key points, teach-back of pen technique, give leaflet and contact number, plan follow-up, document.

## Result

The simulated patient was counselled on type 2 diabetes and correctly demonstrated insulin pen use by teach-back.

## Precautions

1. Use simple language and the patient's own language.
2. Do not overload – focus on key points, give a leaflet for the rest.
3. Use a new needle for every injection; never share pens.
4. Check understanding by teach-back, not by asking "Do you understand?"

## Viva questions

1. **Why hold the pen for 10 seconds after injecting?** So that the full dose is delivered.
2. **Why rotate injection sites?** To prevent fatty lumps that affect absorption.
3. **Storage of unopened insulin?** 2–8 °C in a refrigerator, not frozen.
4. **First aid for hypoglycaemia?** 15–20 g quick-acting sugar, recheck in 15 minutes.
5. **What is HbA1c?** A blood test showing average glucose over about 3 months.

# BMI measurement

**Aim:** To measure the height and weight of a subject, calculate the Body Mass Index (BMI), measure waist circumference, and interpret the results.

## Principle

**Body Mass Index** relates weight to height and is used to screen for underweight, overweight and obesity:

```text
BMI (kg/m²) = Weight (kg) ÷ [Height (m)]²
```

Asian Indians develop diabetes and heart disease at lower BMI, so lower cut-offs are used in India. **Waist circumference** shows fat around the abdomen (central obesity), which is linked with diabetes and heart disease.

## Requirements

Calibrated weighing scale, stadiometer or wall-mounted height scale, non-stretch measuring tape, calculator or BMI chart, record sheet.

## Procedure

1. Explain the test and get consent.
2. **Weight:** check the scale reads zero; subject removes shoes and heavy items, stands still in the centre; record to the nearest 0.1 kg.
3. **Height:** subject removes shoes, stands with heels, buttocks, shoulders and back of head against the scale, looking straight ahead; lower the headpiece to touch the top of the head; record to the nearest 0.1 cm.
4. **Waist:** subject stands relaxed; place the tape horizontally midway between the lowest rib and the top of the hip bone; read at the end of a normal breath out.
5. Calculate BMI and classify; interpret waist circumference.
6. Give advice on diet and activity; refer where needed.

**Worked example:** weight 68 kg, height 1.62 m → 1.62 × 1.62 = 2.62 → BMI = 68 ÷ 2.62 = 26.0 kg/m² → obese (Asian cut-off).

| BMI (Asian Indian adults) | Class |
| --- | --- |
| Below 18.5 | Underweight |
| 18.5–22.9 | Normal |
| 23–24.9 | Overweight |
| 25 or more | Obese |

Waist circumference: central obesity at 90 cm or more in men and 80 cm or more in women (Indian adults).

## Precautions

1. Calibrate and zero the scale before use.
2. Remove shoes and heavy clothing.
3. Convert height from cm to m before calculating.
4. BMI is not suitable alone for children (age-specific charts are used), pregnant women and very muscular people.
5. Be sensitive and non-judgemental when discussing weight.

## Viva questions

1. **Formula of BMI?** Weight in kg divided by the square of height in metres.
2. **Normal BMI for Asian Indians?** 18.5–22.9 kg/m².
3. **BMI cut-off for obesity in India?** 25 kg/m² or more.
4. **Why measure waist?** To detect central obesity, linked to diabetes and heart disease.
5. **Waist cut-off for Indian men and women?** 90 cm and 80 cm.

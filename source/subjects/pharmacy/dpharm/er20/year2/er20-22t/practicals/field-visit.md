# Field visit to a community pharmacy and a medicine distributor

**Aim:** To visit a community pharmacy and a medicine distributor, observe the professional activities of community pharmacists and the supply chain, and write an individual report.

## Principle

The ER-2020 practical syllabus includes a group field visit so that students see real practice: how a licensed pharmacy is organised, how prescriptions are handled, how stock reaches the pharmacy from manufacturers through distributors, and how records and licences are maintained.

## Requirements

Permission letters from the college, visit checklist, notebook, college identity card. Photographs only with the owner's permission and never of patients or prescriptions.

## Procedure

1. Before the visit, revise licences (Form 20, 21, 20B, 21B), schedules (H, H1, X), storage conditions and inventory terms.
2. At the **community pharmacy**, observe and note:
   - display of licences and pharmacist registration certificate;
   - layout – dispensing counter, counselling area, refrigerator, Schedule X cupboard, expired-goods area;
   - prescription receiving, checking, billing and labelling;
   - patient counselling and OTC requests (without listening to private details);
   - registers (H1, Schedule X), temperature log, purchase and sales records;
   - software used and stock arrangement;
   - services offered (BP, glucose, home delivery).
3. At the **distributor / wholesaler**, observe:
   - wholesale licence and storage areas;
   - receipt of goods from manufacturers, checking of batch and expiry;
   - cold-chain storage (cold room or refrigerators, ice packs and boxes for dispatch);
   - order processing, picking, invoicing and delivery to retailers;
   - handling of returns and expired goods.
4. Ask the pharmacist and distributor about common challenges (shortages, expiry losses, regulations).
5. After the visit, write an individual report.

## Observations (report format)

```text
1. Date, names and addresses of places visited
2. Licences and registrations seen
3. Layout of the pharmacy (simple sketch)
4. Dispensing process observed
5. Records and registers maintained
6. Storage and cold-chain arrangements
7. Supply chain: manufacturer → C&F agent → distributor/wholesaler → retailer → patient
8. Services offered to patients
9. Problems discussed
10. What I learnt and suggestions
```

## Result

A field visit to a community pharmacy and a medicine distributor was completed, and an individual report on pharmacy practice and the supply chain was submitted.

## Precautions

1. Behave professionally; follow the instructions of the host.
2. Do not disturb patients or record confidential information.
3. Do not touch stock or records without permission.

## Viva questions

1. **Which licences would you expect at a retail pharmacy?** Form 20 and Form 21 (and Schedule X licence if stocked).
2. **Name the stages of the medicine supply chain.** Manufacturer, C&F agent, distributor or wholesaler, retailer, patient.
3. **What is a cold chain?** Keeping temperature-sensitive products at the required temperature (often 2–8 °C) from manufacture to use.
4. **Which register is required for Schedule H1 drugs?** A separate H1 register kept for 3 years.
5. **Who issues the wholesale drug licence?** The State Licensing Authority.

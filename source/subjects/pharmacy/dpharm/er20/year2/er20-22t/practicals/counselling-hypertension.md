# Counselling a simulated patient with primary hypertension

**Aim:** To counsel a simulated patient with primary (essential) hypertension on the disease, medicines, home BP monitoring and lifestyle changes.

## Principle

Primary hypertension is persistently raised blood pressure without a single identifiable cause. It usually causes no symptoms but damages the heart, brain, kidneys and eyes. Most patients need lifelong medicines plus lifestyle changes, so adherence is the key counselling goal.

## Requirements

Counselling area, simulated prescription (for example amlodipine 5 mg once daily and telmisartan 40 mg once daily), digital BP monitor for demonstration, BP diary, leaflet on hypertension, assessment form.

## Procedure

1. **Introduction:** greet, introduce, confirm identity, purpose, privacy; ask what the doctor told the patient (prime questions).
2. **Disease:** "high BP is silent but harms the heart, brain and kidneys; treatment controls it but usually does not cure it."
3. **Medicines:**
   - Take every day at the same time, even when feeling well.
   - Do not stop suddenly without the doctor's advice.
   - Amlodipine – ankle swelling, flushing, headache.
   - Telmisartan / other ARBs and ACE inhibitors – dizziness; ACE inhibitors may cause dry cough; kidney and potassium tests as advised; not in pregnancy.
   - Diuretics (if prescribed) – take in the morning.
   - Rise slowly from lying or sitting to avoid dizziness.
4. **Home BP monitoring:** demonstrate the digital monitor; rest 5 minutes, arm at heart level, two readings, record in a diary and show it to the doctor.
5. **Lifestyle:** reduce salt (avoid pickles, papad, packaged snacks); more fruits and vegetables; exercise about 30 minutes most days; weight control; stop smoking; limit alcohol; manage stress; good sleep.
6. **Avoid self-medication** with NSAIDs and decongestant cold medicines that can raise BP – ask the pharmacist first.
7. **Warning signs:** severe headache, chest pain, breathlessness, weakness of one side, blurred vision → emergency care.
8. **Closing:** summary, teach-back of how and when to take each tablet, leaflet, follow-up for BP check, documentation.

## Result

The simulated patient was counselled on hypertension and correctly explained the medicine schedule and home BP measurement.

## Precautions

1. Stress that feeling well is not a reason to stop.
2. Advice on salt must be practical for Indian diets.
3. Be non-judgemental about smoking and alcohol.
4. Refer very high readings or warning symptoms.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is hypertension called a silent killer?** It usually has no symptoms but causes stroke, heart and kidney damage.
2. **Common side effect of amlodipine?** Ankle swelling.
3. **Which OTC products may raise BP?** NSAIDs and decongestants.
4. **Main dietary advice?** Reduce salt; more fruit and vegetables.
5. **Why not stop beta-blockers suddenly?** Risk of rebound rise in BP and heart problems.

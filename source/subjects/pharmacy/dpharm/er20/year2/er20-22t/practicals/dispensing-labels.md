# Preparing dispensing labels and auxiliary labels

**Aim:** To prepare main dispensing labels and suitable auxiliary (ancillary) labels for prescribed medications (minimum five).

## Principle

The label is the patient's reminder at home of how to use the medicine. A **main label** carries the identity of the pharmacy, patient and medicine and clear directions. **Auxiliary labels** add important warnings such as "Shake well" or "May cause drowsiness". **Pictograms** help people who cannot read the language.

## Requirements

Simulated prescriptions, blank label sheets or label-printing software, set of auxiliary labels or pictograms, containers, pen, reference book.

## Procedure

1. Read the prescription and interpret the directions; convert abbreviations into plain words (for example "1 tab bd pc" → "Take ONE tablet TWICE a day AFTER food").
2. Write or print the main label with:
   - name and address of the pharmacy;
   - prescription number and date;
   - patient's name;
   - drug name, strength and dosage form;
   - directions in simple words (dose, frequency, timing, route, duration);
   - quantity;
   - prescriber's name;
   - "Keep out of reach of children".
3. Select the auxiliary labels needed for each drug from a reference.
4. Add pictograms where the patient has low literacy.
5. Fix labels straight on the container without covering the manufacturer's batch and expiry details.
6. Check the label against the prescription.
7. Explain the label to the "patient".

## Observations

| --- | --- | --- |
| Amoxicillin oral suspension 250 mg/5 ml, 5 ml tds × 5 days | Take 5 ml THREE times a day for 5 days | Shake well; complete the course; store as directed after mixing |
| Cetirizine 10 mg, 1 od hs | Take ONE tablet at BEDTIME | May cause drowsiness; avoid alcohol |
| Calamine lotion, apply bd | Apply on the affected skin TWICE a day | For external use only; shake well |
| Diclofenac 50 mg, 1 bd pc | Take ONE tablet TWICE a day AFTER food | Take with food; swallow whole |
| Insulin pen | Inject as directed by doctor | Store in refrigerator 2–8 °C; do not freeze |

## Result

Main and auxiliary labels were prepared for five prescribed medicines and checked against the prescriptions.

## Precautions

1. Use simple words; avoid Latin abbreviations on the label.
2. Write numbers clearly ("ONE", "5 ml").
3. Do not cover the batch number or expiry date.
4. Choose only relevant auxiliary labels; too many confuse patients.

## Viva questions

1. **List the contents of a main label.** Pharmacy, patient, drug and strength, directions, quantity, date, prescriber, "keep out of reach of children".
2. **Which auxiliary label is used for metronidazole?** Avoid alcohol.
3. **Why "Swallow whole" on enteric-coated tablets?** Crushing destroys the coating and its protection.
4. **What is a pictogram?** A standard picture showing how to take a medicine.
5. **Why "complete the course" for antibiotics?** To cure the infection and prevent resistance.

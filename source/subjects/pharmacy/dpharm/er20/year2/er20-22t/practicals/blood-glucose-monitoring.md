# Capillary blood glucose monitoring

**Aim:** To measure capillary blood glucose with a glucometer and interpret the result for monitoring or referral.

## Principle

A **glucometer** measures glucose in a small drop of capillary blood placed on a test strip. The strip contains an enzyme (glucose oxidase or glucose dehydrogenase) that reacts with glucose and produces an electric current proportional to the glucose concentration; the meter shows the value in mg/dL.

Values are interpreted according to the time since the last meal (fasting, after meals or random).

## Requirements

Glucometer, matching unexpired test strips, lancing device and sterile single-use lancets, 70% alcohol swabs, dry cotton, disposable gloves, sharps container, biomedical waste bin, record sheet. Control solution for checking the meter.

## Procedure

1. Explain the test, ask about the last meal, and get consent.
2. Wash your hands and wear gloves. Ask the subject to wash and dry hands (warm water improves blood flow).
3. Check the strip expiry and that the strip code (if any) matches the meter. Insert a strip; the meter switches on.
4. Clean the side of a fingertip (middle or ring finger) with alcohol and let it dry completely.
5. Prick the side of the fingertip with the lancing device.
6. Wipe away the first drop if advised by the meter maker; gently press to form a drop – do not squeeze hard.
7. Touch the drop to the strip's edge; let it fill.
8. Read and record the result; press dry cotton on the site.
9. Discard the lancet into the sharps container and the strip and cotton into the correct waste bin.
10. Interpret the result and advise.

| Test | Normal | Prediabetes | Suggests diabetes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fasting | Below 100 | 100–125 | 126 or more |
| 2 hours after glucose / meal | Below 140 | 140–199 | 200 or more |
| Random with symptoms | – | – | 200 or more |

Below 70 mg/dL is low (hypoglycaemia) – give quick sugar and refer if needed.

## Precautions

1. Use a new sterile lancet for every person; never share lancing devices.
2. Let alcohol dry before pricking (it can dilute the drop).
3. Use the side of the fingertip – less painful.
4. Do not squeeze hard (tissue fluid dilutes blood).
5. Store strips in their closed container; check expiry.
6. A meter reading is a screen – refer for laboratory confirmation.

## Viva questions

1. **Which enzyme is used on glucose strips?** Glucose oxidase or glucose dehydrogenase.
2. **Fasting glucose suggesting diabetes?** 126 mg/dL or more.
3. **Why use the side of the fingertip?** Fewer nerve endings, less pain.
4. **How are lancets disposed of?** In a puncture-proof sharps container as biomedical waste.
5. **What is hypoglycaemia?** Blood glucose below about 70 mg/dL with symptoms such as sweating and shaking.

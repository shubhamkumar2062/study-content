# Counselling simulated patients for minor ailments

**Aim:** To respond to symptoms and counsel simulated patients for minor ailments (any three of: headache, GI disturbances – nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, diarrhoea, constipation – worm infestations, pyrexia, upper respiratory tract infections, skin infections, oral and dental disorders).

## Principle

When a person asks for help with a symptom, the pharmacist gathers information in a structured way (**WWHAM** or **ASMETHOD**), looks for danger signs, and decides whether to give self-care advice, supply a suitable OTC product, or refer to a doctor. Every consultation ends with a **safety net** – telling the patient when to come back or see a doctor.

## Requirements

Model pharmacy counter and counselling area, scenario cards, OTC products and dummy packs, thermometer, reference book, leaflets, structured assessment form.

## Procedure (common to every scenario)

1. Greet the patient and move to a private area if needed.
2. Ask WWHAM: Who is it for? What are the symptoms? How long? Action already taken? Medicines already taken?
3. Ask about danger symptoms for that ailment (table below).
4. Decide: self-care only / OTC + advice / refer.
5. If supplying, choose a product suitable for age, pregnancy and other diseases and medicines.
6. Counsel: dose, how and when to take, duration, side effects, what to avoid.
7. Give self-care advice.
8. Safety net: "If not better in … days, or if … happens, see a doctor."
9. Check understanding (teach-back) and record.

## Scenario guide

| Ailment | Key questions | Self-care and OTC | Refer if |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Headache | Type, site, duration, triggers, vision, fever, injury | Rest, fluids, regular meals and sleep; paracetamol (or ibuprofen with food) | Sudden severe, with fever and stiff neck, after head injury, with weakness or vision loss, frequent use of painkillers |
| Nausea and vomiting | Duration, blood, pain, pregnancy, drugs, travel | ORS sips, small bland meals; antihistamine before travel for motion sickness | Blood, more than 24–48 h, dehydration, severe pain, pregnancy, diabetes |
| Dyspepsia (indigestion) | Relation to food, age, weight loss, black stools, NSAID use | Small meals, avoid spicy fatty food and late meals, stop smoking; antacid | Age over about 50 with new symptoms, weight loss, difficulty swallowing, black stools, vomiting blood |
| Diarrhoea | Frequency, blood, fever, dehydration, age | ORS, continue feeding; zinc for children; loperamide only for adults without fever or blood | Blood, fever, dehydration, more than 2–3 days (24 h in infants) |
| Constipation | Usual habit, diet, drugs, blood, weight loss | Fibre, fluids, exercise; ispaghula, lactulose | Blood, weight loss, change of habit after middle age, more than 2 weeks |
| Worm infestation | Itching around anus at night (threadworm), worms seen, family | Albendazole as per label for age; hygiene – hand washing, short nails, washing bedding; treat family members as advised | Children under the label's minimum age, pregnancy, weight loss, persistent symptoms |
| Pyrexia (fever) | Temperature, duration, rash, travel, other symptoms | Fluids, rest, tepid sponging; paracetamol by dose and weight; no aspirin in children | More than 3 days, infants under 3 months, fits, non-fading rash, stiff neck, breathing difficulty |
| URTI (cold, sore throat, cough) | Symptoms, duration, fever, breathlessness, asthma | Fluids, steam, saline drops, salt gargles, lozenges, antihistamine, paracetamol; no antibiotics | Cough more than 2–3 weeks, blood in sputum, breathlessness, high fever, ear pain |
| Skin infection | Appearance, site, itch, spread, diabetes | Fungal – clotrimazole cream; scabies – permethrin 5% for whole family; minor cuts – clean and antiseptic | Spreading redness, pus, fever, scalp or nail involvement, diabetic patient |
| Oral and dental | Ulcer duration, tooth pain, swelling, tobacco use | Mouth ulcer – choline salicylate gel, salt rinse; toothache – painkiller and dentist; gum bleeding – brushing, flossing, chlorhexidine | Ulcer more than 3 weeks, facial swelling, fever; all toothache to a dentist |

## Precautions

1. Never supply prescription-only drugs (antibiotics, steroids, strong antiemetics) without a prescription.
2. Check age limits and pregnancy on product labels.
3. Always give red-flag and safety-net advice.
4. Respect privacy for embarrassing symptoms (worms, piles, skin).

## Viva questions

1. **Expand WWHAM.** Who, What symptoms, How long, Action taken, Medicines.
2. **First-line treatment of diarrhoea?** ORS.
3. **Why treat the whole family for threadworm or scabies?** To prevent reinfection.
4. **When should a headache be referred urgently?** Sudden severe headache, or with fever and stiff neck, weakness or after injury.
5. **Should antibiotics be given for a common cold?** No – it is viral.

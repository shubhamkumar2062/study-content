# Unit 9: Drugs acting on the kidney

This unit covers syllabus chapter 9: diuretics and antidiuretics. A short look at how the nephron handles salt and water makes this unit easy to understand.

## 9.1 The nephron and urine formation

Each kidney has about a million **nephrons**. Blood is filtered at the glomerulus (about 180 litres a day), and the tubules reabsorb more than 99% of the filtered water and sodium.

```text
 Glomerulus ─► Proximal tubule ─► Loop of Henle ─► Distal tubule ─► Collecting duct ─► urine
               (~65% Na+;          (thick ascending   (~5–10% Na+;     (~2–5% Na+;
```

The more sodium a diuretic stops from being reabsorbed (and the higher up the nephron), the stronger its effect, but late segments matter because sodium reaching them can still be saved.

## 9.2 Diuretics

**Definition:** drugs that **increase the output of urine** together with sodium and other ions (natriuresis). They are used to remove excess fluid (oedema) and to lower blood pressure.

### Classification

```text
 A. High-efficacy (high-ceiling, loop) diuretics
    Furosemide (frusemide), torsemide, bumetanide, ethacrynic acid
 B. Medium-efficacy diuretics
    Thiazides: hydrochlorothiazide, chlorothiazide, benzthiazide
    Thiazide-like: chlorthalidone, indapamide, metolazone, xipamide
 C. Weak or adjunctive diuretics
    1. Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors: acetazolamide
    2. Potassium-sparing diuretics
       Aldosterone antagonists: spironolactone, eplerenone
       Epithelial Na+ channel (ENaC) blockers: amiloride, triamterene
    3. Osmotic diuretics: mannitol, glycerol, isosorbide
 (SGLT2 inhibitors such as dapagliflozin also increase urine output and sugar loss)
```

### Loop diuretics (furosemide)

**Mechanism:** inhibit the **Na+-K+-2Cl− co-transporter** in the **thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle** → large amounts of Na+, Cl− and water are lost. Also increase loss of K+, Ca2+ and Mg2+; cause some venous dilatation (quick relief in acute left ventricular failure).

**Features:** strong (high ceiling – effect keeps rising with dose), quick (IV: within minutes; oral: within about an hour), short action (4–6 hours), works even when kidney function is reduced.

**Uses:**
1. **Acute pulmonary oedema** (acute left ventricular failure) – IV furosemide.
2. **Oedema** of heart failure, nephrotic syndrome, liver cirrhosis (often with spironolactone).
3. Hypertension with kidney failure or heart failure.
4. **Hypercalcaemia** (with saline).
5. Forced diuresis in some poisonings (rarely now), cerebral oedema (with mannitol).

**Adverse effects:** **hypokalaemia**, hyponatraemia, hypomagnesaemia, **dehydration and hypotension**, metabolic alkalosis, **hyperuricaemia (gout)**, hyperglycaemia, **ototoxicity** (hearing loss with high IV doses, more with aminoglycosides), allergy (sulphonamide group).

### Thiazides (hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone)

**Mechanism:** inhibit the **Na+–Cl− symporter** in the **early distal convoluted tubule**. Moderate effect (medium ceiling – little extra effect above a certain dose); long action (chlorthalidone up to about 48 hours). Less effective when kidney function is poor (except metolazone). **Reduce urinary calcium** (opposite of loop diuretics).

**Uses:**
1. **Hypertension** – first-line, low doses (hydrochlorothiazide 12.5–25 mg daily).
2. Mild oedema of heart failure.
3. **Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus** (paradoxically reduce urine volume).
4. Recurrent calcium kidney stones (↓ urinary calcium).

**Adverse effects:** **hypokalaemia**, hyponatraemia, **hyperuricaemia** (may precipitate gout), **hyperglycaemia** (worsen diabetes), raised lipids, hypercalcaemia (mild), erectile dysfunction, photosensitivity, allergy (sulpha).

### Carbonic anhydrase inhibitor (acetazolamide)

**Mechanism:** inhibits **carbonic anhydrase** in the proximal tubule → ↓ HCO3− and Na+ reabsorption → alkaline urine; weak diuresis that wears off (metabolic acidosis develops).
**Uses:** **glaucoma** (↓ aqueous formation), **acute mountain sickness** (prevention), some types of epilepsy, to alkalinise urine.
**Adverse effects:** metabolic acidosis, hypokalaemia, kidney stones, tingling in fingers, drowsiness, sulphonamide allergy.

### Potassium-sparing diuretics

| Drug | Mechanism | Uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Spironolactone**, eplerenone | **Aldosterone receptor antagonists** in late distal tubule and collecting duct → ↓ Na+ reabsorption and ↓ K+ loss | **Liver cirrhosis with ascites**, **heart failure** (reduces deaths), primary hyperaldosteronism (Conn's syndrome), resistant hypertension, with loop/thiazide to prevent hypokalaemia; hirsutism and acne in women (antiandrogen) |
| **Amiloride**, triamterene | Block epithelial sodium channels (ENaC) directly | With thiazides or loop diuretics to save potassium |

**Adverse effects:** **hyperkalaemia** (dangerous with ACE inhibitors, ARBs, potassium supplements, kidney failure); spironolactone – **gynaecomastia**, impotence, menstrual problems (eplerenone causes less).
**Contraindications:** hyperkalaemia, kidney failure; potassium supplements.

### Osmotic diuretics (mannitol)

**Mechanism:** mannitol is filtered at the glomerulus but not reabsorbed; it holds water in the tubule by osmosis. Given **IV** (not absorbed orally). It also draws water out of tissues (brain, eye) into blood.
**Uses:** **raised intracranial pressure** (cerebral oedema), **acute glaucoma** (↓ intraocular pressure), to maintain urine flow in some cases of impending acute kidney injury.
**Adverse effects:** expansion of blood volume → **pulmonary oedema** in heart failure, headache, nausea, electrolyte imbalance, dehydration.
**Contraindications:** **congestive heart failure**, pulmonary oedema, established anuria, active bleeding inside the skull.

### Comparison of loop and thiazide diuretics

| Feature | Loop (furosemide) | Thiazide (hydrochlorothiazide) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Site | Thick ascending limb | Early distal tubule |
| Transporter blocked | Na+-K+-2Cl− | Na+-Cl− |
| Efficacy | High ceiling | Medium ceiling |
| Onset / duration | Quick / short | Slower / long |
| Effect in kidney failure | Works | Little effect |
| Urinary calcium | ↑ (used in hypercalcaemia) | ↓ (used in calcium stones) |
| Main use | Oedema, acute pulmonary oedema | Hypertension |
| Common to both | Hypokalaemia, hyperuricaemia, hyperglycaemia, hyponatraemia | |

### Choosing a diuretic (summary)

| Clinical situation | Preferred diuretic |
| --- | --- |
| Essential hypertension | Thiazide or thiazide-like (chlorthalidone, indapamide) |
| Acute pulmonary oedema | Furosemide IV |
| Oedema of heart failure | Loop diuretic + spironolactone |
| Ascites in liver cirrhosis | Spironolactone (± furosemide) |
| Nephrotic syndrome, kidney failure with oedema | Loop diuretic |
| Raised intracranial pressure, acute glaucoma | Mannitol |
| Acute mountain sickness | Acetazolamide |
| Hypercalcaemia | Furosemide with saline |
| Calcium kidney stones, nephrogenic DI | Thiazide |

### Drug interactions with diuretics

- Loop and thiazide diuretics + **digoxin** → hypokalaemia increases digoxin toxicity.
- Loop diuretics + **aminoglycosides** → more ear and kidney damage.
- Thiazides + **lithium** → lithium toxicity (less lithium excreted).
- K+-sparing diuretics + ACE inhibitors/ARBs → hyperkalaemia.
- **NSAIDs** reduce the effect of diuretics.

### Patient counselling

Take diuretics **in the morning** (avoid night-time urination); weigh daily in heart failure; eat potassium-rich foods (banana, orange, coconut water) with loop and thiazide diuretics unless told otherwise; rise slowly from bed (dizziness); report muscle cramps, weakness or palpitations.

## 9.3 Antidiuretics

**Definition:** drugs that **reduce urine volume**, mainly by increasing water reabsorption in the collecting duct. The chief one is **antidiuretic hormone (ADH, vasopressin)** and its analogues.

### Physiology of ADH

ADH is made in the hypothalamus and released from the **posterior pituitary** when plasma osmolality rises or blood volume falls. It acts on **V2 receptors** in the collecting duct → **aquaporin-2 water channels** move into the cell membrane → water is reabsorbed → concentrated urine. At higher levels it acts on **V1 receptors** → vasoconstriction.

**Diabetes insipidus (DI):** passage of large volumes of dilute urine with thirst.
- **Central (neurogenic) DI:** not enough ADH is released (head injury, tumour, surgery).
- **Nephrogenic DI:** kidney does not respond to ADH (lithium, inherited).

### Classification

```text
 1. ADH and analogues
    Vasopressin (arginine vasopressin) – V1 + V2
    Desmopressin (DDAVP) – selective V2, long acting, little pressor effect
    Terlipressin, lypressin – more V1 (used for bleeding)
 2. Thiazide diuretics – nephrogenic DI
 3. Others: carbamazepine, chlorpropamide (increase ADH effect; rarely used for this),
    indomethacin (with thiazide in nephrogenic DI), amiloride (lithium-induced DI)
```

### Desmopressin

**Routes:** nasal spray, oral tablets, injection.
**Uses:**
1. **Central diabetes insipidus** – drug of choice.
2. **Nocturnal enuresis** (bed-wetting) in children – at bedtime.
3. Nocturia in adults.
4. **Haemophilia A (mild) and von Willebrand's disease** – releases factor VIII and von Willebrand factor (before minor surgery).

**Adverse effects:** **water retention and hyponatraemia** (headache, confusion, fits) – restrict fluid intake around the dose; nasal irritation; abdominal cramps.
**Contraindications:** hyponatraemia, conditions needing diuretics, heart failure; caution in elderly.

### Vasopressin and terlipressin

**Uses:** **bleeding oesophageal varices** (terlipressin – V1 vasoconstriction of splanchnic vessels), vasodilatory (septic) shock (vasopressin infusion), central DI (vasopressin, short-acting).
**Adverse effects:** pallor, abdominal cramps, **coronary vasoconstriction (angina)**, hypertension, water intoxication.
**Contraindications:** ischaemic heart disease, peripheral vascular disease, hypertension.

### Thiazides in nephrogenic DI

Thiazides cause mild volume depletion → more sodium and water reabsorbed in the proximal tubule → less water reaches the collecting duct → **urine volume falls** (paradoxical effect). A low-salt diet helps.

### Vasopressin antagonists (for completeness)

**Tolvaptan, conivaptan** block V2 receptors → water loss without sodium loss ("aquaretics"); used in hyponatraemia due to excess ADH (SIADH); risk of too-rapid correction of sodium and liver injury (tolvaptan).

## Remember

- Loop diuretics (furosemide) block Na+-K+-2Cl− in the thick ascending limb – strongest; for acute pulmonary oedema and oedema; ototoxic.
- Thiazides block Na+-Cl− in the early distal tubule – for hypertension; ↓ urinary calcium; useful in nephrogenic DI.
- Loop and thiazide: hypokalaemia, hyperuricaemia, hyperglycaemia.
- Spironolactone (aldosterone antagonist): ascites in cirrhosis, heart failure; hyperkalaemia and gynaecomastia.
- Acetazolamide: glaucoma, mountain sickness; causes metabolic acidosis.
- Mannitol (IV): raised intracranial pressure, acute glaucoma; contraindicated in heart failure.
- ADH acts on V2 receptors and aquaporin-2; desmopressin (V2) for central DI, bed-wetting, mild haemophilia; risk of hyponatraemia.
- Terlipressin (V1) for bleeding oesophageal varices.

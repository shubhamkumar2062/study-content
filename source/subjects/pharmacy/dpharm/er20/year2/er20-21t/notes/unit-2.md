# Unit 2: Drugs acting on the peripheral nervous system

This unit covers syllabus chapter 2: neurohumoral transmission, cholinergic and anticholinergic drugs, adrenergic and anti-adrenergic drugs, neuromuscular blockers, drugs for myasthenia gravis, local anaesthetics and NSAIDs. It is one of the most important units for the exam.

## 2.1 The autonomic nervous system in brief

The **autonomic nervous system (ANS)** controls involuntary organs (heart, smooth muscle, glands). It has two divisions:

| Feature | Sympathetic | Parasympathetic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outflow | Thoraco-lumbar (T1–L2/L3) | Cranio-sacral (cranial nerves III, VII, IX, X; S2–S4) |
| Ganglia | Near the spinal cord (long post-ganglionic fibre) | Near or in the organ (short post-ganglionic fibre) |
| Post-ganglionic transmitter | **Noradrenaline** (acetylcholine at sweat glands) | **Acetylcholine** |
| General role | "Fight or flight" | "Rest and digest" |

```text
 Somatic:          spinal cord ──── ACh (nicotinic NM) ────► skeletal muscle
 Parasympathetic:  CNS ── ACh (NN) ── ganglion ── ACh (muscarinic) ──► organ
 Sympathetic:      CNS ── ACh (NN) ── ganglion ── NA (α / β) ─────────► organ
 Adrenal medulla:  CNS ── ACh (NN) ──► adrenal medulla ── adrenaline into blood
```

### Effects on main organs

| Organ | Sympathetic | Parasympathetic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eye pupil | Dilatation (mydriasis) – radial muscle, α1 | Constriction (miosis) – sphincter, M3 |
| Heart | ↑ rate and force (β1) | ↓ rate (M2) |
| Bronchi | Dilatation (β2) | Constriction, ↑ secretion (M3) |
| Blood vessels | Constriction of skin/gut vessels (α1); dilatation in muscle (β2) | Little direct innervation |
| Gut | ↓ motility, sphincters contract | ↑ motility and secretion, sphincters relax |
| Bladder | Relaxes detrusor (β2/β3), contracts sphincter (α1) | Contracts detrusor (micturition) |
| Glands | Sweating (cholinergic sympathetic) | ↑ salivation, lacrimation |

## 2.2 Steps in neurohumoral transmission

**Neurohumoral transmission** is the passage of a signal across a synapse or neuroeffector junction by a chemical (neurotransmitter).

1. **Synthesis** of the transmitter in the nerve ending.
2. **Storage** in vesicles.
3. **Release** by exocytosis when the action potential arrives and calcium enters.
4. **Combination with receptors** on the post-junctional membrane → excitatory or inhibitory response.
5. **Termination** of action: enzymatic breakdown or reuptake.

### Cholinergic transmission

```text
 Choline + Acetyl-CoA ──(choline acetyltransferase)──► ACh → stored in vesicles
 Nerve impulse → Ca2+ entry → ACh released → binds muscarinic / nicotinic receptors
 ACh ──(acetylcholinesterase)──► choline + acetate   (choline taken back up)
```

Hemicholinium blocks choline uptake; botulinum toxin blocks ACh release.

### Adrenergic transmission

```text
 Tyrosine ─► DOPA ─► Dopamine ─► Noradrenaline ─(adrenal medulla)─► Adrenaline
   (tyrosine hydroxylase is the rate-limiting step)
 Released NA → α / β receptors
 Termination: mainly reuptake into the nerve (uptake-1; blocked by cocaine, TCAs);
              then breakdown by MAO (inside nerve) and COMT (outside)
```

Reserpine depletes stores; guanethidine blocks release.

### Receptors

| Receptor | Main sites and effect |
| --- | --- |
| Muscarinic M1 | Stomach (acid), CNS |
| M2 | Heart – slows rate |
| M3 | Glands, smooth muscle (gut, bronchi, bladder, eye), vascular endothelium (NO release → dilatation) |
| Nicotinic NN | Autonomic ganglia, adrenal medulla |
| Nicotinic NM | Skeletal muscle (neuromuscular junction) |
| α1 | Vascular smooth muscle (constriction), radial muscle of iris, bladder sphincter |
| α2 | Presynaptic – reduces NA release; CNS (clonidine) |
| β1 | Heart (↑ rate, force, conduction); kidney (renin release) |
| β2 | Bronchi, blood vessels of muscle, uterus (relaxation); liver (glycogenolysis) |
| β3 | Fat (lipolysis), bladder (relaxation) |

## 2.3 Cholinergic drugs (parasympathomimetics)

**Definition:** drugs that produce effects like acetylcholine, either by stimulating cholinergic receptors directly or by preventing breakdown of ACh.

### Classification

```text
 A. Direct-acting (receptor agonists)
    1. Choline esters: acetylcholine, methacholine, carbachol, bethanechol
    2. Alkaloids: pilocarpine, muscarine, arecoline
 B. Indirect-acting (anticholinesterases)
    1. Reversible: physostigmine, neostigmine, pyridostigmine, edrophonium,
       donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine
    2. Irreversible: organophosphates (malathion, parathion, echothiophate),
       carbamates (carbaryl, propoxur)
```

### Pharmacological actions (muscarinic)

Heart: bradycardia; blood vessels: dilatation and fall in BP; smooth muscle: increased gut motility, bronchoconstriction, bladder contraction; glands: increased sweat, saliva, tears, bronchial and gastric secretion; eye: **miosis**, spasm of accommodation and fall in intraocular pressure. Nicotinic effects at higher doses: muscle twitching, then weakness; ganglionic stimulation.

### Individual drugs and uses

| Drug | Key points and uses |
| --- | --- |
| **Bethanechol** | Muscarinic, resists cholinesterase; urinary retention after surgery, paralytic ileus |
| **Pilocarpine** | Alkaloid; **glaucoma** (miotic eye drops), to counter mydriasis; dry mouth (Sjögren's) |
| **Physostigmine** | Tertiary amine, enters brain; **atropine/anticholinergic poisoning**, glaucoma |
| **Neostigmine** | Quaternary, does not enter brain; direct NM action too; **myasthenia gravis**, reversal of competitive neuromuscular block (with atropine), postoperative urinary retention and ileus |
| **Pyridostigmine** | Longer action; myasthenia gravis |
| **Edrophonium** | Very short action; diagnosis of myasthenia gravis (Tensilon test) |
| Donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine | Enter brain; **Alzheimer's disease** |

**Adverse effects:** sweating, salivation, abdominal cramps, diarrhoea, bradycardia, hypotension, bronchospasm, blurred vision.
**Contraindications:** **bronchial asthma**, peptic ulcer, heart block or bradycardia, intestinal or urinary obstruction, hyperthyroidism.

### Organophosphate (OP) poisoning

OPs (insecticides) phosphorylate cholinesterase irreversibly.
**Features:** SLUDGE (salivation, lacrimation, urination, defaecation, GI upset, emesis), miosis, sweating, bronchospasm and bronchorrhoea, bradycardia, muscle fasciculations then paralysis, confusion and convulsions; death from respiratory failure.
**Treatment:** remove contaminated clothes and wash skin; gastric lavage if swallowed; airway and oxygen; **atropine** IV in repeated doses until signs of atropinisation (dry secretions, pulse rising); **pralidoxime (PAM)**, a cholinesterase reactivator, given early before "ageing" of the enzyme; diazepam for convulsions.

## 2.4 Anticholinergic drugs (parasympatholytics)

**Definition:** drugs that block muscarinic receptors and so oppose the actions of acetylcholine.

### Classification

```text
 1. Natural alkaloids: atropine, hyoscine (scopolamine)  (from Belladonna, Datura)
 2. Semi-synthetic: homatropine, ipratropium, tiotropium, hyoscine butylbromide
 3. Synthetic:
    – Mydriatics: tropicamide, cyclopentolate
    – Antisecretory/antispasmodic: glycopyrrolate, dicyclomine, propantheline
    – Bladder: oxybutynin, tolterodine
    – Antiparkinsonian: trihexyphenidyl, benztropine
    – Bronchodilator: ipratropium, tiotropium (inhaled)
```

### Pharmacological actions of atropine

- **Heart:** tachycardia (blocks vagal M2); low doses can briefly slow the heart.
- **Glands:** dry mouth, dry skin (no sweating → "atropine fever" in children), reduced bronchial and gastric secretion.
- **Smooth muscle:** relaxes gut (antispasmodic), bladder (urinary retention), bronchi.
- **Eye:** **mydriasis**, **cycloplegia** (paralysis of accommodation), loss of light reflex, rise in intraocular pressure.
- **CNS:** mild stimulation; high doses cause restlessness, hallucinations, delirium. Hyoscine is sedative instead.

### Uses

1. **Pre-anaesthetic medication** (atropine, glycopyrrolate) – reduce secretions and prevent vagal bradycardia.
2. **Organophosphate poisoning** (atropine) and mushroom (muscarine) poisoning.
3. **Sinus bradycardia and heart block** (atropine IV, usual adult dose 0.6 mg).
4. **Eye:** mydriatic-cycloplegics for fundus examination, refraction in children, iritis (alternating with miotics to break adhesions).
5. **Antispasmodic:** intestinal and biliary colic, dysmenorrhoea (dicyclomine, hyoscine butylbromide).
6. **Motion sickness** (hyoscine).
7. **Bronchial asthma and COPD** (ipratropium, tiotropium inhaled).
8. **Parkinsonism** and drug-induced extrapyramidal effects (trihexyphenidyl).
9. **Overactive bladder** (oxybutynin, tolterodine).

### Adverse effects and atropine poisoning

Dry mouth, blurred vision, photophobia, constipation, urinary retention, tachycardia, flushed hot dry skin, fever, delirium ("dry as a bone, red as a beet, blind as a bat, mad as a hatter, hot as a hare"). Treat poisoning with physostigmine and supportive care.

**Contraindications:** **angle-closure glaucoma**, enlarged prostate (urinary retention), paralytic ileus, tachyarrhythmias; care in the elderly and young children.

## 2.5 Adrenergic drugs (sympathomimetics)

**Definition:** drugs that mimic the effects of sympathetic stimulation (adrenaline, noradrenaline).

### Classification

```text
 A. By mode of action
    Direct: adrenaline, noradrenaline, isoprenaline, dopamine, dobutamine,
            phenylephrine, salbutamol
    Indirect (release NA): tyramine, amphetamine
    Mixed: ephedrine
 B. By chemistry
    Catecholamines: adrenaline, noradrenaline, dopamine, isoprenaline, dobutamine
    Non-catecholamines: ephedrine, amphetamine, phenylephrine, salbutamol
 C. By main receptor / clinical use
    α1 agonists (vasopressors, decongestants): phenylephrine, xylometazoline, oxymetazoline
    α2 agonists (centrally acting): clonidine, methyldopa
    β1 (cardiac stimulant): dobutamine
    β2 (bronchodilators, uterine relaxants): salbutamol, terbutaline, salmeterol, formoterol, ritodrine
    Mixed: adrenaline (α + β), noradrenaline (α + β1), isoprenaline (β1 + β2)
```

### Actions of adrenaline

Heart (β1): ↑ rate, force, cardiac output; may cause arrhythmia. Blood vessels: constriction in skin and mucosa (α1), dilatation in muscle (β2); systolic BP rises, diastolic may fall. Bronchi (β2): dilatation. Metabolism: ↑ blood sugar (glycogenolysis), ↑ free fatty acids. Eye: mydriasis (α1). Not active orally (destroyed in gut and liver).

**Dale's vasomotor reversal:** after an α blocker, adrenaline lowers BP instead of raising it, because only its β2 dilator action remains.

### Uses

| Use | Drug |
| --- | --- |
| **Anaphylactic shock** | **Adrenaline 0.5 mg (0.5 ml of 1:1000) IM** in adults – drug of choice |
| Cardiac arrest | Adrenaline IV |
| With local anaesthetics | Adrenaline 1:200,000 – prolongs action, reduces bleeding (not in fingers, toes, penis) |
| Shock, hypotension | Noradrenaline, dopamine, dobutamine (cardiogenic shock), phenylephrine |
| Bronchial asthma | Salbutamol, terbutaline (short-acting), salmeterol, formoterol (long-acting) |
| Nasal decongestion | Xylometazoline, oxymetazoline drops; pseudoephedrine orally |
| Mydriasis | Phenylephrine eye drops (no cycloplegia) |
| Premature labour | Ritodrine, terbutaline (uterine relaxation) |
| Attention deficit / narcolepsy | Amphetamines, methylphenidate |

**Adverse effects:** palpitation, tachycardia, arrhythmias, rise in BP, headache, tremor (β2), anxiety, cold extremities; tissue necrosis if noradrenaline leaks outside the vein.
**Contraindications:** hypertension, ischaemic heart disease, hyperthyroidism, patients on non-selective β-blockers (unopposed α action); adrenaline with halothane anaesthesia (arrhythmias).

## 2.6 Anti-adrenergic drugs (sympatholytics)

### A. α-adrenergic blockers

```text
 Non-selective: phenoxybenzamine (irreversible), phentolamine (reversible)
 α1-selective: prazosin, terazosin, doxazosin, alfuzosin, tamsulosin (α1A)
```

**Uses:** **phaeochromocytoma** (phenoxybenzamine), hypertension (prazosin), **benign prostatic hyperplasia** (tamsulosin, alfuzosin, terazosin – relax prostate and bladder neck), Raynaud's disease.
**Adverse effects:** **first-dose postural hypotension** (give first dose at bedtime), dizziness, nasal stuffiness, reflex tachycardia, failure of ejaculation (tamsulosin).

### B. β-adrenergic blockers

```text
 Non-selective (β1 + β2): propranolol, timolol, sotalol, nadolol
 β1-selective (cardioselective): atenolol, metoprolol, bisoprolol, esmolol, nebivolol
 With α-blocking action too: labetalol, carvedilol
 With partial agonist (intrinsic sympathomimetic) activity: pindolol
```

**Actions:** ↓ heart rate, force and cardiac output; ↓ renin release; ↓ BP over time; reduce O2 demand of the heart; slow AV conduction; ↓ aqueous humour (timolol eye drops); may cause bronchoconstriction (β2 block).

**Uses:** hypertension, **angina pectoris**, after myocardial infarction (reduce death), arrhythmias, chronic heart failure (carvedilol, metoprolol, bisoprolol in low, slowly increased doses), **glaucoma** (timolol), thyrotoxicosis (symptom control), **migraine prophylaxis**, essential tremor, anxiety with palpitations (propranolol).

**Adverse effects:** bradycardia, heart block, worsening of acute heart failure, **bronchospasm**, cold hands, fatigue, masking of hypoglycaemia warning signs in diabetics, sleep disturbances and vivid dreams; sudden withdrawal can precipitate angina (taper slowly).

**Contraindications:** **bronchial asthma and COPD** (especially non-selective), bradycardia and AV block, acute decompensated heart failure, peripheral vascular disease, Prinzmetal (variant) angina; caution in diabetes.

## 2.7 Neuromuscular blocking agents

**Definition:** drugs that block transmission at the neuromuscular junction and cause relaxation of skeletal muscle. Used mainly during surgery.

### Classification

```text
 A. Peripherally acting
    1. Non-depolarising (competitive) blockers:
       d-tubocurarine, pancuronium, vecuronium, atracurium, rocuronium
    2. Depolarising blockers: succinylcholine (suxamethonium)
 B. Directly acting on muscle: dantrolene
 C. Centrally acting (see Unit 4): baclofen, diazepam, tizanidine
```

| Feature | Non-depolarising | Depolarising (succinylcholine) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanism | Competes with ACh at NM receptor; no depolarisation | Persistent depolarisation of end plate → muscle cannot respond |
| Initial fasciculations | No | Yes |
| Onset / duration | Slower, longer (rocuronium fast onset) | Very fast (about 1 min), short (about 5 min) |
| Reversal by neostigmine | **Yes** (with atropine) | No (block is made worse) |
| Metabolism | Varies; atracurium breaks down spontaneously in plasma (Hofmann elimination) | Plasma pseudocholinesterase |

**Uses:** muscle relaxation in surgery (adjunct to general anaesthesia); **endotracheal intubation** (succinylcholine, rocuronium); electroconvulsive therapy; tetanus and status epilepticus needing ventilation.

**Adverse effects:** respiratory paralysis (must have ventilation available); histamine release with d-tubocurarine (hypotension, bronchospasm). **Succinylcholine:** postoperative muscle pain, **hyperkalaemia**, raised intraocular pressure, prolonged apnoea in pseudocholinesterase deficiency, **malignant hyperthermia** (with halothane; treated with **dantrolene**).

**Contraindications:** succinylcholine in burns, crush injuries, paraplegia (hyperkalaemia) and eye injuries; all NM blockers only where facilities for artificial respiration exist; aminoglycosides increase the block.

## 2.8 Drugs used in myasthenia gravis

**Myasthenia gravis** is an autoimmune disease in which antibodies destroy nicotinic receptors at the neuromuscular junction, causing weakness and fatigue of skeletal muscles (drooping eyelids, double vision, difficulty in swallowing).

| Drug group | Drugs and role |
| --- | --- |
| **Anticholinesterases** (mainstay) | **Pyridostigmine**, **neostigmine** – increase ACh at the junction; atropine may be added to block muscarinic side effects |
| Diagnosis | Edrophonium (rapid, short improvement in strength) – now largely replaced by antibody tests |
| Immunosuppressants | Prednisolone, azathioprine, mycophenolate |
| Crisis / severe disease | Plasmapheresis, IV immunoglobulin; **thymectomy** in suitable patients |

**Myasthenic crisis** (too little drug) vs **cholinergic crisis** (too much anticholinesterase): both cause weakness; edrophonium improves the first and worsens the second.

**Drugs that worsen myasthenia (avoid):** aminoglycosides, neuromuscular blockers, quinine, β-blockers, magnesium salts.

## 2.9 Local anaesthetics (LA)

**Definition:** drugs that, when applied locally, reversibly block nerve conduction and cause loss of sensation (and motor power) in a limited area without loss of consciousness.

### Classification

```text
 A. Esters (metabolised by plasma pseudocholinesterase; more allergy)
    Cocaine, procaine, chloroprocaine, tetracaine (amethocaine), benzocaine
 B. Amides (metabolised in liver; longer acting)
    Lidocaine (lignocaine), bupivacaine, ropivacaine, prilocaine, mepivacaine,
    dibucaine (cinchocaine), articaine
 By use: surface (benzocaine, lidocaine), infiltration and nerve block (lidocaine),
         spinal/epidural (bupivacaine, lidocaine)
```

### Mechanism of action

LAs block **voltage-gated sodium channels** from inside the nerve membrane → no sodium entry → no action potential. Small, unmyelinated fibres (pain, temperature) are blocked first; motor fibres last. Sensation returns in the reverse order. Inflamed (acidic) tissue reduces their effect because less unionised drug enters the nerve.

**Adrenaline** (1:200,000) is often added: it constricts vessels, slows absorption, prolongs action, reduces bleeding and systemic toxicity. Not used in fingers, toes, ear, nose and penis (risk of gangrene).

### Techniques

Surface (topical) anaesthesia, infiltration, nerve block, field block, **spinal (subarachnoid)**, **epidural**, and intravenous regional (Bier's block).

### Uses

Minor surgery and suturing, dental procedures, eye surgery (topical), spinal and epidural anaesthesia for surgery and labour, painful skin and mucosal conditions; **lidocaine IV** for ventricular arrhythmias.

### Adverse effects

CNS: tingling around the mouth, light-headedness, tremor, **convulsions**, then CNS depression. CVS: bradycardia, hypotension, arrhythmias; **bupivacaine is the most cardiotoxic**. Allergy (esters). Spinal anaesthesia: hypotension, headache, urinary retention. Cocaine: abuse and dependence.

**Contraindications:** known allergy, infection at the injection site, bleeding disorders (for spinal/epidural); adrenaline-containing LA in end-organs and in uncontrolled hypertension or arrhythmias.

## 2.10 Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs)

**Definition:** drugs with **analgesic, antipyretic and anti-inflammatory** actions that are not steroids. Paracetamol is analgesic and antipyretic but has almost no anti-inflammatory action.

### Mechanism of action

NSAIDs inhibit the enzyme **cyclo-oxygenase (COX)**, so fewer **prostaglandins** are formed from arachidonic acid.

```text
 Membrane phospholipids ─(phospholipase A2; inhibited by steroids)─► Arachidonic acid
        ├─ COX-1 (constitutive): stomach protection, platelets (TXA2), kidney
        │        └► PGs, thromboxane A2
        └─ COX-2 (induced in inflammation) ──► PGs causing pain, fever, swelling
 NSAIDs block COX → ↓ pain, fever, inflammation; COX-1 block → gastric ulcer, bleeding
```

### Classification

```text
 A. Non-selective COX inhibitors
    1. Salicylates: aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid)
    2. Propionic acid derivatives: ibuprofen, naproxen, ketoprofen, flurbiprofen
    3. Acetic acid derivatives: diclofenac, aceclofenac, indomethacin, ketorolac
    4. Anthranilic acid (fenamate): mefenamic acid
    5. Oxicams: piroxicam, tenoxicam
    6. Pyrazolones: metamizol (dipyrone), phenylbutazone (rarely used)
 B. Preferential COX-2 inhibitors: nimesulide, meloxicam, nabumetone, etodolac
 C. Selective COX-2 inhibitors: celecoxib, etoricoxib, parecoxib
 D. Analgesic-antipyretics with poor anti-inflammatory action:
    paracetamol (acetaminophen), metamizol
```

### Aspirin

**Actions:** analgesic (dull aching pain), antipyretic (resets hypothalamic set point), anti-inflammatory (higher doses), **antiplatelet** (low dose, irreversibly acetylates platelet COX → less thromboxane A2 for the platelet's 7–10-day life).
**Uses:** pain, fever, rheumatoid arthritis and rheumatic fever (high dose, now less used); **low dose (75–150 mg/day) to prevent heart attack and stroke**.
**Adverse effects:** gastric irritation, peptic ulcer and GI bleeding; prolonged bleeding time; allergy and **aspirin-induced asthma**; **Reye's syndrome** in children with viral fever (liver damage and encephalopathy) – avoid aspirin in children under 12–16 years with viral illness; salicylism (tinnitus, vertigo, nausea) at high doses; acid–base disturbance in poisoning.

### Paracetamol

Good analgesic and antipyretic; little anti-inflammatory action; does not irritate the stomach or affect platelets. Usual adult dose **500–1000 mg every 4–6 hours, not more than 4 g a day**. Drug of choice for fever in children and for pain in people with ulcers or asthma. **Overdose** → liver failure (toxic metabolite NAPQI when glutathione runs out); antidote **N-acetylcysteine**.

### Common uses of NSAIDs

Headache, toothache, musculoskeletal pain, sprains, dysmenorrhoea (mefenamic acid), postoperative pain (ketorolac), rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, acute gout (not aspirin), fever, and closure of patent ductus arteriosus in newborns (indomethacin).

### Adverse effects (class)

Gastric irritation, peptic ulcer, bleeding; kidney damage and salt/water retention (oedema, raised BP); hypersensitivity and bronchospasm; selective COX-2 inhibitors cause less gastric injury but carry more risk of heart attack and stroke.

**Contraindications:** active peptic ulcer, bleeding disorders, aspirin-sensitive asthma, severe kidney or liver disease, last trimester of pregnancy (premature closure of ductus arteriosus), children with viral fever (aspirin), with anticoagulants (bleeding).

## Remember

- ACh is broken down by acetylcholinesterase; NA action ends mainly by reuptake.
- Neostigmine: myasthenia gravis and reversal of curare-like blockers; physostigmine: atropine poisoning.
- OP poisoning: atropine + pralidoxime (early).
- Atropine: tachycardia, dry mouth, mydriasis, cycloplegia; contraindicated in angle-closure glaucoma and enlarged prostate.
- Adrenaline 0.5 mg IM (1:1000) is the drug of choice in anaphylaxis.
- β-blockers are contraindicated in asthma; prazosin causes first-dose hypotension.
- Succinylcholine is depolarising; its block is not reversed by neostigmine; dantrolene treats malignant hyperthermia.
- LAs block sodium channels; esters vs amides; bupivacaine is most cardiotoxic.
- NSAIDs inhibit COX; aspirin low dose is antiplatelet; avoid aspirin in children (Reye's syndrome); paracetamol overdose → N-acetylcysteine.

# Unit 13: Chemotherapy II – antifungal, antiviral, antiprotozoal, anthelmintic and anticancer drugs

This unit covers the second half of syllabus chapter 12: antifungal, antiviral, antiamoebic, anthelmintic, antimalarial and antineoplastic agents, including the basic principles of chemotherapy of infestations and neoplastic diseases.

## 13.1 Antifungal drugs

**Fungal infections (mycoses)** may be **superficial** (skin, hair, nails – ringworm/dermatophytosis; mouth and vagina – candidiasis) or **systemic/deep** (invasive candidiasis, aspergillosis, cryptococcal meningitis, mucormycosis), mainly in people with weak immunity (diabetes, HIV, cancer, steroids).

Fungal cell membranes contain **ergosterol** (human cells contain cholesterol) – the main target for selective toxicity.

### Classification

```text
 1. Polyene antibiotics: amphotericin B, nystatin, natamycin
 2. Azoles
    Imidazoles: ketoconazole, clotrimazole, miconazole, econazole, luliconazole (mostly topical)
    Triazoles: fluconazole, itraconazole, voriconazole, posaconazole, isavuconazole
 3. Echinocandins: caspofungin, micafungin, anidulafungin
 4. Allylamines: terbinafine
 5. Antimetabolite: flucytosine
 6. Others: griseofulvin; topical – ciclopirox, tolnaftate, Whitfield's ointment (benzoic + salicylic acid)
```

| Drug | Mechanism | Uses | Important adverse effects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Amphotericin B** | Binds **ergosterol** → pores in membrane → leakage → fungicidal | Life-threatening systemic mycoses (cryptococcal meningitis, aspergillosis, **mucormycosis**), **kala-azar** (liposomal) | Infusion reactions (fever, chills), **nephrotoxicity**, hypokalaemia, anaemia; lipid forms less toxic |
| Nystatin | Like amphotericin; too toxic for systemic use | **Oral and vaginal candidiasis** (topical, oral suspension) | Mild GI upset |
| **Azoles** (fluconazole) | Inhibit fungal **cytochrome P450 (14α-demethylase)** → ↓ ergosterol synthesis | Fluconazole: **candidiasis** (oral, vaginal – single 150 mg dose, systemic), **cryptococcal meningitis**; itraconazole: dermatophytes, nail infections; voriconazole: **aspergillosis**; topical clotrimazole, miconazole: skin and vaginal infections | Nausea, liver injury, rash; **drug interactions** (enzyme inhibitors – raise levels of warfarin, statins, cyclosporine); ketoconazole (oral) – liver toxicity and antiandrogen effects (oral use largely stopped); voriconazole – visual disturbance |
| Echinocandins | Inhibit **β-glucan** synthesis (cell wall) | Invasive candidiasis, aspergillosis (IV) | Few; infusion reactions |
| **Terbinafine** | Inhibits squalene epoxidase → ↓ ergosterol, toxic squalene build-up | **Dermatophytosis**, **nail infections (onychomycosis)** – oral; tinea – cream | GI upset, taste change, liver injury (rare) |
| **Griseofulvin** | Binds microtubules (spindle) → stops fungal mitosis; deposited in keratin | Dermatophyte infections of skin, hair (**tinea capitis**) and nails – oral, long courses | Headache, photosensitivity, enzyme induction; **teratogenic** – avoid in pregnancy; better absorbed with fatty food |
| Flucytosine | Converted to 5-fluorouracil in fungi | With amphotericin in cryptococcal meningitis | Bone-marrow suppression |

**Contraindications:** azoles and griseofulvin in **pregnancy**; ketoconazole/azoles with drugs that interact (cisapride, some statins); amphotericin – care in kidney disease.

## 13.2 Antiviral drugs

Viruses live and multiply **inside host cells** using the host's machinery, so few drugs act selectively; most antivirals act on viral enzymes and only on **multiplying** virus (not latent virus). Vaccines remain the main protection.

### Classification by use

```text
 1. Anti-herpes virus: acyclovir, valacyclovir, famciclovir, ganciclovir (CMV), foscarnet
 2. Anti-influenza: oseltamivir, zanamivir (neuraminidase inhibitors); amantadine (older)
 3. Anti-hepatitis
    Hepatitis B: tenofovir, entecavir, lamivudine; interferon
    Hepatitis C: sofosbuvir, daclatasvir, velpatasvir, ledipasvir (direct-acting antivirals)
 4. Antiretroviral (anti-HIV) drugs
    NRTIs: zidovudine, lamivudine, tenofovir, abacavir, emtricitabine
    NNRTIs: efavirenz, nevirapine
    Protease inhibitors: lopinavir/ritonavir, atazanavir, darunavir
    Integrase inhibitors: dolutegravir, raltegravir
    Entry/fusion inhibitors: maraviroc, enfuvirtide
 5. Others: ribavirin (RSV, hepatitis C), remdesivir
```

### Acyclovir

**Mechanism:** a guanosine analogue **activated by viral thymidine kinase** (so mainly in infected cells) → inhibits viral DNA polymerase and terminates DNA chain.
**Uses:** **herpes simplex** (genital herpes, cold sores, herpes encephalitis – IV), **varicella (chickenpox)** and **herpes zoster (shingles)** – start early; prophylaxis in immunocompromised patients. **Valacyclovir** is a prodrug with better oral absorption.
**Adverse effects:** few; headache, nausea; IV – **kidney damage (crystalluria)** – keep patient well hydrated; local irritation.

### Oseltamivir

Inhibits influenza **neuraminidase** → new virus cannot be released from cells. Used for treatment and prophylaxis of **influenza A and B** (including H1N1), best within 48 hours of symptoms. Adverse effects: nausea, vomiting.

### Antiretroviral therapy (ART) in HIV

HIV is a retrovirus that destroys CD4 lymphocytes → AIDS. Drugs suppress the virus but do not cure. Always used as **combinations of three drugs** to prevent resistance. In India, the National AIDS Control Programme's first-line regimen for adults is **tenofovir + lamivudine + dolutegravir (TLD)**, taken once daily for life.

| Class | Mechanism | Example adverse effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NRTIs | Inhibit **reverse transcriptase** (chain terminators) | Zidovudine – **anaemia**, bone-marrow suppression; tenofovir – kidney damage, bone loss; abacavir – hypersensitivity |
| NNRTIs | Bind reverse transcriptase at another site | Efavirenz – vivid dreams, dizziness; nevirapine – rash, liver toxicity |
| Protease inhibitors | Block HIV protease → immature virus | Metabolic effects (lipids, sugar), GI upset; ritonavir – strong enzyme inhibitor (used as "booster") |
| Integrase inhibitors | Block integration of viral DNA into host DNA | Dolutegravir – insomnia, headache, weight gain |

Uses also include **prevention of mother-to-child transmission**, **post-exposure prophylaxis** (needle-stick injury – start within 72 hours, ideally within 2 hours) and pre-exposure prophylaxis.

## 13.3 Antiamoebic agents

**Amoebiasis** is infection with ***Entamoeba histolytica***, spread by contaminated food and water. Forms: **intestinal** (amoebic dysentery – blood and mucus in stools), **extra-intestinal** (amoebic liver abscess), and **asymptomatic cyst passers** (carriers).

### Classification

```text
 1. Tissue (systemic) amoebicides – act in intestinal wall and liver:
    Nitroimidazoles: metronidazole, tinidazole, secnidazole, ornidazole
    Others: dehydroemetine, emetine (older, toxic); chloroquine (liver only)
 2. Luminal amoebicides – act in gut lumen (kill cysts):
    Diloxanide furoate, paromomycin, iodoquinol (quiniodochlor)
```

### Metronidazole

**Mechanism:** a prodrug; in **anaerobic** organisms its nitro group is reduced to toxic radicals that damage DNA → kills.
**Uses:**
1. **Amoebiasis** (intestinal and liver abscess) – followed by a luminal agent (diloxanide furoate) to clear cysts.
2. **Giardiasis** and **trichomoniasis** (treat partner also).
3. **Anaerobic bacterial infections** – abdominal and pelvic infections, dental infections, brain abscess.
4. ***C. difficile* colitis**; **H. pylori** eradication; bacterial vaginosis.
**Adverse effects:** **metallic taste**, nausea, anorexia, headache, dark urine, peripheral neuropathy (long use), seizures (high doses); **disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol** (flushing, vomiting) – avoid alcohol during and for 48 hours after treatment.
**Contraindications:** first trimester of pregnancy (caution), alcohol use, neurological disease, blood disorders.

**Diloxanide furoate:** luminal amoebicide for cyst passers and after tissue amoebicides; flatulence.

## 13.4 Anthelmintics

**Helminths** are parasitic worms: **roundworms (nematodes)** – Ascaris, hookworm (causes iron-deficiency anaemia), pinworm/threadworm (Enterobius), whipworm, Strongyloides, filaria; **tapeworms (cestodes)** – Taenia; **flukes (trematodes)** – Schistosoma. **Anthelmintics** expel or kill these worms.

### Classification and uses

| Drug | Mechanism | Main uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Albendazole** | Binds β-tubulin → blocks glucose uptake and microtubules of worms | **Broad spectrum**: roundworm, hookworm, pinworm, whipworm (single 400 mg dose in adults and children over 2 years), **neurocysticercosis**, hydatid disease; **National Deworming Day** (school children) |
| Mebendazole | Same as albendazole | Roundworm, hookworm, pinworm, whipworm |
| **Pyrantel pamoate** | Depolarising neuromuscular blocker → **spastic paralysis** of worms, expelled | Roundworm, hookworm, pinworm; safe in children |
| Piperazine | GABA agonist → **flaccid paralysis** of worms | Roundworm, pinworm (older) |
| **Ivermectin** | Opens glutamate-gated Cl− channels → paralysis | **Strongyloidiasis**, **filariasis**, onchocerciasis, **scabies**, head lice |
| **Diethylcarbamazine (DEC)** | Makes microfilariae easier for the immune system to kill | **Lymphatic filariasis** (mass drug administration with albendazole ± ivermectin), tropical eosinophilia |
| **Praziquantel** | ↑ Ca2+ entry → paralysis and tegument damage | **Tapeworms**, **schistosomiasis** and other flukes, neurocysticercosis |
| Niclosamide | Blocks energy production of tapeworm | Tapeworms (gut) |

**Adverse effects:** usually mild – abdominal pain, nausea, diarrhoea, dizziness; albendazole – liver enzyme rise and bone-marrow suppression with long courses; DEC and ivermectin – fever, itching, aches from dying parasites (Mazzotti-type reaction); praziquantel in neurocysticercosis – seizures (give with steroids).

**Contraindications/cautions:** **albendazole and mebendazole in the first trimester of pregnancy** and children under 1 year (age limits vary by product); praziquantel in ocular cysticercosis; general hygiene measures to prevent reinfection (hand washing, footwear, clean water).

## 13.5 Antimalarial agents

**Malaria** is caused by ***Plasmodium*** (*P. falciparum*, *P. vivax*, *P. malariae*, *P. ovale*, *P. knowlesi*) and spread by the bite of the female **Anopheles** mosquito. *P. falciparum* causes severe and cerebral malaria; *P. vivax* and *P. ovale* have dormant liver forms (**hypnozoites**) that cause **relapses**.

```text
 Mosquito bite → sporozoites → LIVER (pre-erythrocytic stage; hypnozoites in vivax/ovale)
   → merozoites → RED CELLS (erythrocytic stage → fever, anaemia)
   → some form gametocytes → taken up by mosquito (transmission)
```

### Classification by stage of action

| Group | Drugs | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Blood schizonticides** (erythrocytic stage) | **Chloroquine**, **artemisinin derivatives** (artesunate, artemether, arteether), quinine, mefloquine, lumefantrine, sulfadoxine–pyrimethamine, doxycycline | Treat the acute attack (clinical cure) |
| **Tissue schizonticides** (liver stage) | **Primaquine**, tafenoquine | Radical cure – kill hypnozoites (prevent relapse of vivax/ovale) |
| **Gametocides** | **Primaquine** (falciparum), artemisinins | Stop transmission |
| Causal prophylactics | Primaquine, atovaquone–proguanil | Prevent liver infection |

### Important drugs

| Drug | Points |
| --- | --- |
| **Chloroquine** | Prevents parasite from detoxifying haem (inhibits haem polymerase) → toxic haem kills parasite; **drug of choice for vivax malaria** in India (3 days); also amoebic liver abscess, rheumatoid arthritis, lupus (hydroxychloroquine). Adverse effects: bitter taste, nausea, itching (especially in dark-skinned people), headache; long use – **retinopathy**; toxic in overdose (heart), especially children; resistance common in falciparum |
| **Artemisinin derivatives** (artesunate, artemether) | Fast acting; generate free radicals in parasite; **always used as combinations (ACT)** to prevent resistance: in India **artesunate + sulfadoxine–pyrimethamine** (most areas) or **artemether–lumefantrine** (north-eastern states) for uncomplicated falciparum; **injectable artesunate is the drug of choice for severe malaria** |
| **Primaquine** | Kills liver hypnozoites and gametocytes; **14 days for radical cure of vivax**, single dose as gametocide in falciparum; **haemolysis in G6PD deficiency** (test where possible); **contraindicated in pregnancy and infants** |
| Quinine | Older blood schizonticide; severe malaria (if artesunate not available) and in first-trimester pregnancy; **cinchonism** (tinnitus, headache, nausea), **hypoglycaemia**, arrhythmia |
| Mefloquine | Prophylaxis and treatment; neuropsychiatric effects |
| Doxycycline | With quinine/artesunate; prophylaxis for travellers (not in children or pregnancy) |

**General treatment points (India, national programme):** confirm with blood smear or rapid diagnostic test; vivax – chloroquine 3 days + primaquine 14 days; falciparum – ACT + single-dose primaquine (not in pregnancy or infants); severe malaria – IV/IM artesunate then full ACT course; prevention – bed nets, repellents, removing breeding sites.

## 13.6 Antineoplastic (anticancer) agents

### Basic principles of cancer chemotherapy

**Cancer** is uncontrolled growth of abnormal cells that can invade nearby tissue and spread (metastasise). Treatment uses surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, hormone therapy, targeted therapy and immunotherapy.

```text
 Cell cycle:  G0 (resting) → G1 → S (DNA synthesis) → G2 → M (mitosis) → G1 ...
 Cell-cycle specific drugs act in one phase:
   S phase – antimetabolites (methotrexate, 5-FU); M phase – vinca alkaloids, taxanes
 Cell-cycle non-specific drugs act in all phases: alkylating agents, platinum, anthracyclines
```

Principles:
1. Drugs kill a **fixed fraction** of cells with each cycle (log-kill), so **repeated cycles** are given.
2. **Combination chemotherapy** (drugs with different mechanisms and toxicities) gives better results and less resistance.
3. Rapidly dividing **normal cells are also damaged** – bone marrow, gut lining, hair follicles, gonads – causing the common toxicities.
4. Gaps between cycles allow normal tissue (bone marrow) to recover.
5. Supportive care: antiemetics (ondansetron), growth factors, hydration, allopurinol (prevents uric acid build-up from cell breakdown – tumour lysis syndrome).

### Classification

```text
 1. Alkylating agents: cyclophosphamide, ifosfamide, chlorambucil, melphalan, busulfan,
    nitrosoureas (carmustine), temozolomide; platinum compounds – cisplatin, carboplatin, oxaliplatin
 2. Antimetabolites
    Folate antagonist: methotrexate
    Purine antagonists: 6-mercaptopurine, 6-thioguanine
    Pyrimidine antagonists: 5-fluorouracil, cytarabine, capecitabine, gemcitabine
 3. Natural products
    Vinca alkaloids: vincristine, vinblastine
    Taxanes: paclitaxel, docetaxel
    Topoisomerase inhibitors: etoposide (II); irinotecan, topotecan (I)
    Antitumour antibiotics: doxorubicin, daunorubicin (anthracyclines), bleomycin, actinomycin D
    Enzyme: L-asparaginase
 4. Hormones and antagonists
    Corticosteroids: prednisolone (leukaemias, lymphomas)
    Anti-oestrogens: tamoxifen; aromatase inhibitors: letrozole, anastrozole (breast cancer)
    Anti-androgens: bicalutamide, flutamide; GnRH analogues: leuprolide (prostate cancer)
 5. Targeted therapy
    Tyrosine kinase inhibitors: imatinib (chronic myeloid leukaemia), gefitinib, erlotinib
    Monoclonal antibodies: rituximab, trastuzumab, bevacizumab (see Unit 14)
 6. Miscellaneous: hydroxyurea, thalidomide/lenalidomide (myeloma)
```

### Important drugs

| Drug | Mechanism | Main uses | Characteristic toxicity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cyclophosphamide** | Alkylates DNA (cross-links) | Lymphomas, leukaemias, breast cancer; also immunosuppressant (lupus, nephrotic syndrome) | **Haemorrhagic cystitis** (prevented by mesna and fluids), alopecia, marrow suppression |
| **Cisplatin** | Cross-links DNA | Testicular, ovarian, lung, head and neck cancers | **Nephrotoxicity**, **severe vomiting**, ototoxicity, neuropathy |
| **Methotrexate** | Inhibits **dihydrofolate reductase** | Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia, choriocarcinoma; low weekly dose in **rheumatoid arthritis** and psoriasis | Marrow suppression, mouth ulcers, liver fibrosis; **folinic acid (leucovorin) rescue** |
| **5-Fluorouracil** | Inhibits thymidylate synthase | Colon, breast, stomach cancers; topical for skin lesions | Mucositis, diarrhoea, marrow suppression |
| 6-Mercaptopurine | Purine antagonist | Acute leukaemia | Marrow suppression; **dose reduced with allopurinol** |
| **Vincristine** | Blocks microtubule (spindle) formation – M phase | Leukaemias, lymphomas | **Peripheral neuropathy** (little marrow toxicity) |
| Paclitaxel | Stabilises microtubules | Breast, ovarian, lung cancer | Neuropathy, hypersensitivity |
| **Doxorubicin** | Intercalates DNA, inhibits topoisomerase II, free radicals | Many solid tumours, lymphomas | **Cardiotoxicity** (cumulative dose), red urine, alopecia |
| Bleomycin | DNA strand breaks | Testicular cancer, lymphomas | **Pulmonary fibrosis**, skin changes (little marrow toxicity) |
| **Imatinib** | Blocks BCR-ABL tyrosine kinase | **Chronic myeloid leukaemia**, GIST | Fluid retention, muscle cramps |
| Tamoxifen | Blocks oestrogen receptors in breast | Hormone-receptor-positive breast cancer | Hot flushes, endometrial cancer risk, clots |

### Common adverse effects of anticancer drugs

**Bone-marrow suppression** (infection, bleeding, anaemia), **nausea and vomiting**, **alopecia**, mucositis and diarrhoea, infertility and teratogenicity, tumour lysis syndrome (hyperuricaemia), delayed wound healing, second cancers. **Extravasation** of some drugs (vincristine, doxorubicin) causes severe tissue damage.

**Contraindications/cautions:** pregnancy, severe marrow suppression, active infections, poor kidney/liver function (dose adjustment). **Handling:** cytotoxic drugs must be prepared in a biological safety cabinet with gloves and gown, and waste disposed of safely – an important duty for pharmacists.

## Remember

- Amphotericin B binds ergosterol (nephrotoxic); azoles block ergosterol synthesis (drug interactions); terbinafine for nails; griseofulvin for tinea capitis – teratogenic.
- Acyclovir needs viral thymidine kinase – herpes and varicella; oseltamivir – influenza; HIV first-line in India: tenofovir + lamivudine + dolutegravir.
- Metronidazole: amoebiasis, giardiasis, trichomoniasis, anaerobes; metallic taste; no alcohol; follow with diloxanide for cysts.
- Albendazole 400 mg single dose for common worms; ivermectin – strongyloides, scabies; DEC – filaria; praziquantel – tapeworms, flukes.
- Chloroquine for vivax + primaquine 14 days (radical cure; G6PD test); ACT for falciparum; injectable artesunate for severe malaria.
- Anticancer drugs hit dividing cells: marrow suppression, vomiting, hair loss.
- Signature toxicities: cyclophosphamide – haemorrhagic cystitis (mesna); cisplatin – kidney and vomiting; doxorubicin – heart; bleomycin – lungs; vincristine – neuropathy; methotrexate – folinic acid rescue.

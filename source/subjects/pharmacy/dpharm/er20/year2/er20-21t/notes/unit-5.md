# Unit 5: Drugs acting on the cardiovascular system

This unit covers syllabus chapter 5: antihypertensive, antianginal and antiarrhythmic drugs, drugs used in atherosclerosis (hypolipidaemic drugs), drugs for congestive heart failure and drug therapy of shock.

## 5.1 Antihypertensive drugs

**Hypertension** is a persistently raised arterial blood pressure, usually taken as **140/90 mmHg or more** on repeated readings. Most cases (about 90–95%) are **essential (primary)** with no single cause; the rest are **secondary** (kidney disease, phaeochromocytoma, drugs such as oral contraceptives and NSAIDs). Untreated, it damages the heart, brain, kidneys and eyes.

```text
 BP = Cardiac output × Peripheral resistance
   Cardiac output ↑ by heart rate, force, blood volume (salt and water)
   Resistance ↑ by sympathetic tone, angiotensin II, narrow arterioles

 Renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS):
 Angiotensinogen ─(renin, from kidney)─► Angiotensin I ─(ACE)─► Angiotensin II
   Angiotensin II → vasoconstriction + aldosterone (Na+ and water retention) → ↑ BP
   ACE also breaks down bradykinin (a vasodilator)
```

### Classification

```text
 1. Diuretics
    Thiazides: hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone, indapamide
    Loop: furosemide (in kidney failure or oedema)
    Potassium-sparing: spironolactone, amiloride
 2. ACE inhibitors: captopril, enalapril, lisinopril, ramipril, perindopril
 3. Angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs): losartan, telmisartan, olmesartan,
    valsartan, irbesartan
 4. Direct renin inhibitor: aliskiren
 5. Calcium channel blockers (CCBs)
    Dihydropyridines: amlodipine, nifedipine, felodipine, cilnidipine
    Non-dihydropyridines: verapamil, diltiazem
 6. β-blockers: atenolol, metoprolol, bisoprolol, propranolol, nebivolol
 7. α + β blockers: labetalol, carvedilol
 8. α1 blockers: prazosin, terazosin, doxazosin
 9. Centrally acting α2 agonists: clonidine, methyldopa
 10. Vasodilators
    Arteriolar: hydralazine, minoxidil
    Arteriolar + venous: sodium nitroprusside
```

### Main drug groups

| Class | How they lower BP | Important adverse effects | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Thiazides** | Early: ↓ blood volume (diuresis); later: ↓ peripheral resistance | Low K+, high uric acid (gout), high sugar and lipids, low Na+ | Cheap; good in elderly; low doses |
| **ACE inhibitors** | Block formation of angiotensin II; ↑ bradykinin | **Dry cough** (bradykinin), **angioedema**, **hyperkalaemia**, first-dose hypotension, taste change (captopril), worsening of kidney function in renal artery stenosis | **Contraindicated in pregnancy**; protect kidney in diabetes; also used in heart failure and after MI |
| **ARBs** (losartan) | Block AT1 receptors | Like ACE inhibitors but **no cough**; hyperkalaemia, dizziness | Contraindicated in pregnancy |
| **CCBs** | Block L-type calcium channels → arteriolar dilatation | Dihydropyridines: **ankle oedema**, flushing, headache, reflex tachycardia (short-acting nifedipine), gum swelling; verapamil: constipation, bradycardia, heart block | Amlodipine once daily, long acting; good in elderly |
| **β-blockers** | ↓ cardiac output, ↓ renin, central action | Bradycardia, fatigue, bronchospasm, cold hands, masking of hypoglycaemia | Preferred when angina, past MI, arrhythmia coexist; avoid in asthma |
| **α1 blockers** (prazosin) | Dilate arterioles and veins | **First-dose postural hypotension** | Useful with prostatic enlargement |
| **Clonidine** | Stimulates central α2 → ↓ sympathetic outflow | Sedation, dry mouth; **rebound hypertension** on sudden stopping | Rarely used now |
| **Methyldopa** | Converted to α-methylnoradrenaline (central α2 agonist) | Sedation, positive Coombs' test, haemolytic anaemia, liver injury | **Safe in pregnancy** – drug of choice for hypertension of pregnancy (with labetalol, nifedipine) |
| **Hydralazine** | Direct arteriolar dilator | Reflex tachycardia, fluid retention, **lupus-like syndrome** | Pregnancy; with nitrate in heart failure |
| Minoxidil | Opens K+ channels | Hirsutism (topical form for baldness), fluid retention | Severe resistant hypertension |
| **Sodium nitroprusside** | Releases NO; dilates arteries and veins | Cyanide toxicity on prolonged use; must be protected from light | IV infusion in **hypertensive emergency** |

### Choice of drug

- First-line for most patients: **ACE inhibitor or ARB, CCB, or thiazide**; often two are combined in low doses.
- Diabetes or kidney disease with protein in urine: ACE inhibitor or ARB.
- Elderly: CCB or thiazide.
- Heart failure: ACE inhibitor/ARB, β-blocker (carvedilol, metoprolol, bisoprolol), diuretic, spironolactone.
- Pregnancy: **methyldopa, labetalol, nifedipine**; **never ACE inhibitors or ARBs**.
- Hypertensive emergency: IV nitroprusside, labetalol or nitroglycerin under monitoring.

**Lifestyle measures** are part of treatment: less salt, weight loss, exercise, no smoking, less alcohol.

## 5.2 Antianginal drugs

**Angina pectoris** is chest pain (often radiating to the left arm or jaw) caused by a temporary imbalance between the oxygen supply and oxygen demand of the heart muscle, usually because of narrowed coronary arteries (atherosclerosis).

| Type | Cause |
| --- | --- |
| Stable (classical, exertional) angina | Fixed narrowing; pain on effort, relieved by rest |
| Variant (Prinzmetal) angina | Coronary spasm; pain at rest |
| Unstable angina | Ruptured plaque and clot; increasing pain at rest – emergency (acute coronary syndrome) |

### Classification

```text
 1. Nitrates
    Short-acting: glyceryl trinitrate (nitroglycerin) – sublingual, spray, IV
    Long-acting: isosorbide dinitrate, isosorbide mononitrate, transdermal GTN
 2. β-blockers: atenolol, metoprolol, propranolol, bisoprolol
 3. Calcium channel blockers: amlodipine, nifedipine (long-acting), diltiazem, verapamil
 4. Potassium channel opener: nicorandil
 5. Others: ranolazine, ivabradine, trimetazidine
 Supporting drugs: aspirin, clopidogrel (antiplatelets), statins
```

### Nitrates

**Mechanism:** release **nitric oxide (NO)** → ↑ cGMP → smooth muscle relaxation. Mainly **dilate veins** → less blood returns to the heart (↓ preload) → heart does less work and needs less oxygen; also dilate coronary arteries and relieve spasm.

**Glyceryl trinitrate (GTN):** 0.5 mg sublingual acts in 1–2 minutes and lasts about 30 minutes; used to **stop an attack** or before an activity likely to cause angina. Swallowing destroys it (high first-pass metabolism).

**Patient advice:** sit down before taking (dizziness); if pain is not relieved after the dose, may repeat at 5-minute intervals (up to three) and seek emergency help if pain continues; keep tablets in the original airtight glass bottle away from heat and light and replace as directed after opening.

**Adverse effects:** **throbbing headache**, flushing, dizziness, postural hypotension, reflex tachycardia, **tolerance** with continuous use (give a nitrate-free interval of 8–12 hours each day with long-acting forms).

**Contraindications:** with **sildenafil, tadalafil** (severe fall in BP), hypotension, raised intracranial pressure, severe anaemia.

### Other antianginal drugs

| Drug | Mechanism | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **β-blockers** | ↓ heart rate and force → ↓ O2 demand | First choice for prevention of stable angina; **not in variant angina**; do not stop suddenly |
| **CCBs** | Coronary and peripheral dilatation; verapamil/diltiazem also slow the heart | **Drug of choice in variant angina**; avoid short-acting nifedipine alone |
| Nicorandil | K+ channel opening + nitrate-like action | Headache, mouth ulcers |
| Ranolazine | Inhibits late Na+ current | Add-on drug |
| Ivabradine | Slows sinus node (If channel) | Reduces heart rate without lowering BP |

## 5.3 Antiarrhythmic drugs

An **arrhythmia** is any abnormality in the rate, rhythm or conduction of the heartbeat (tachycardia, bradycardia, atrial fibrillation, ventricular tachycardia, heart block).

### Vaughan Williams classification

| Class | Action | Drugs | Uses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **I** – Na+ channel blockers | ↓ conduction | IA: quinidine, procainamide, disopyramide; **IB: lidocaine**, mexiletine; IC: flecainide, propafenone | IB lidocaine: ventricular arrhythmias after MI |
| **II** – β-blockers | ↓ sympathetic drive, slow AV node | Propranolol, metoprolol, esmolol | Sinus tachycardia, atrial fibrillation rate control, arrhythmias of exercise and thyrotoxicosis |
| **III** – K+ channel blockers | Prolong action potential and refractory period | **Amiodarone**, sotalol, dronedarone | Wide range of atrial and ventricular arrhythmias |
| **IV** – Ca2+ channel blockers | Slow AV conduction | **Verapamil, diltiazem** | Paroxysmal supraventricular tachycardia (PSVT), AF rate control |
| Others | — | **Adenosine** (IV, very short-acting), **digoxin**, atropine, magnesium sulphate | Adenosine: drug of choice to stop PSVT; atropine: sinus bradycardia; magnesium: torsades de pointes |

### Important adverse effects

- **Amiodarone:** thyroid disorders (hypo and hyper), **pulmonary fibrosis**, corneal deposits, blue-grey skin, photosensitivity, liver damage; very long half-life.
- **Lidocaine:** CNS effects (drowsiness, convulsions).
- **Quinidine:** cinchonism (tinnitus, headache), diarrhoea, QT prolongation.
- **Verapamil:** never IV with a β-blocker (heart block, asystole); constipation.
- **Adenosine:** brief flushing, chest discomfort, breathlessness.
- All antiarrhythmics can themselves cause arrhythmias (**proarrhythmia**).

## 5.4 Drugs used in atherosclerosis (hypolipidaemic drugs)

**Atherosclerosis** is the build-up of fatty plaques (cholesterol, foam cells, fibrous tissue) in the walls of arteries, narrowing them and leading to angina, heart attack and stroke. High **LDL ("bad") cholesterol** and low **HDL ("good") cholesterol** raise the risk.

### Classification

```text
 1. HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (statins): atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin, pravastatin
 2. Cholesterol absorption inhibitor: ezetimibe
 3. Fibrates (PPAR-α activators): fenofibrate, gemfibrozil
 4. Bile acid sequestrants (resins): cholestyramine, colestipol, colesevelam
 5. Nicotinic acid (niacin)
 6. Newer: PCSK9 inhibitors (evolocumab, alirocumab – injections), omega-3 fatty acids
```

| Drug | Mechanism | Main effect | Adverse effects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Statins** | Inhibit **HMG-CoA reductase**, the rate-limiting enzyme of cholesterol synthesis → liver makes more LDL receptors | **↓ LDL strongly**; small ↑ HDL; ↓ TG; reduce heart attacks and stroke | **Muscle pain, myopathy, rhabdomyolysis** (more with fibrates), raised liver enzymes; **contraindicated in pregnancy**; short-acting statins given at night |
| Ezetimibe | Blocks cholesterol absorption in intestine | ↓ LDL; added to statin | Mild GI upset |
| **Fibrates** | Activate PPAR-α → ↑ lipoprotein lipase | **↓ triglycerides**, ↑ HDL | Gallstones, myopathy (with statins), GI upset |
| Bile acid resins | Bind bile acids in gut → liver uses cholesterol to make more | ↓ LDL | Constipation, bloating, bind other drugs (give others 1 h before or 4 h after) |
| Niacin | ↓ VLDL production | ↓ TG, ↑ HDL | **Flushing** (reduced by aspirin), itching, high sugar and uric acid, liver toxicity |

Lifestyle measures (diet low in saturated fat, exercise, weight loss, no smoking) are always advised.

## 5.5 Congestive heart failure (CHF)

**Heart failure** is a condition in which the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs, or does so only at high filling pressures. Features: breathlessness, fatigue, swollen ankles, raised jugular venous pressure, enlarged liver, lung crepitations.

```text
 Weak heart → ↓ cardiac output → body activates sympathetic system and RAAS
   → vasoconstriction (↑ afterload) + salt/water retention (↑ preload, oedema)
   → more strain on heart → further failure (vicious cycle)
 Drugs break this cycle at several points.
```

### Classification

```text
 1. Drugs that reduce mortality / disease progression
    ACE inhibitors (enalapril, ramipril) or ARBs (losartan); ARNI (sacubitril–valsartan)
    β-blockers (carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, bisoprolol – start low, go slow)
    Aldosterone antagonists (spironolactone, eplerenone)
    SGLT2 inhibitors (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin)
 2. Diuretics (relieve congestion): furosemide, torsemide; thiazides
 3. Positive inotropic drugs
    Cardiac glycosides: digoxin
    β1 agonists: dobutamine, dopamine (acute)
    Phosphodiesterase-3 inhibitors: milrinone (acute)
 4. Vasodilators: nitrates, hydralazine (with isosorbide dinitrate)
```

### Digoxin

**Source:** leaves of *Digitalis lanata* (foxglove family).
**Mechanism:** inhibits **Na+/K+ ATPase** in heart muscle → ↑ intracellular Na+ → less Ca2+ pumped out by Na+/Ca2+ exchanger → **more Ca2+ for contraction (positive inotropic effect)**. Also increases vagal tone → slows heart rate and AV conduction.
**Uses:** heart failure with **atrial fibrillation** (controls ventricular rate); severe heart failure for symptom relief.
**Narrow therapeutic index.** Toxicity is more likely with **hypokalaemia** (diuretics), hypomagnesaemia, hypercalcaemia, kidney failure, elderly, and interacting drugs (amiodarone, verapamil, quinidine).
**Toxicity:** anorexia, nausea, vomiting (early signs), diarrhoea; **arrhythmias** (ventricular extra beats, bigeminy, heart block); visual disturbances (yellow vision), confusion. **Treatment:** stop digoxin, correct potassium, treat arrhythmia (lidocaine, atropine for bradycardia), **digoxin-specific antibody fragments (Fab)** in severe toxicity.
**Advice:** check pulse; report nausea or visual change.

## 5.6 Drug therapy of shock

**Shock** is a state of inadequate tissue perfusion and oxygen delivery, usually with low BP, cold clammy skin (except early septic shock), fast weak pulse, low urine output and confusion.

| Type | Cause | Main treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hypovolaemic** | Blood or fluid loss (bleeding, burns, diarrhoea) | **Replace volume**: crystalloids (normal saline, Ringer lactate), blood, plasma expanders (albumin, dextran) |
| **Cardiogenic** | Pump failure (large MI) | **Dobutamine**, dopamine, noradrenaline; treat cause (reopen artery) |
| **Septic** | Severe infection | Fluids, **broad-spectrum antibiotics early**, **noradrenaline** (vasopressor of choice), hydrocortisone if fluids and vasopressors fail |
| **Anaphylactic** | Severe allergy | **Adrenaline IM** (0.5 mg of 1:1000 in adults, repeat after 5 minutes if needed), oxygen, IV fluids, then antihistamine and hydrocortisone, salbutamol for wheeze |
| Neurogenic | Spinal injury, loss of sympathetic tone | Fluids, vasopressors |

### Drugs used

- **Fluids and plasma expanders:** normal saline, Ringer lactate, albumin, dextran 40/70, hydroxyethyl starch, blood.
- **Vasopressors/inotropes:** noradrenaline (raises BP by vasoconstriction), adrenaline, **dopamine** (dose-dependent: low dose – kidney vessel dilatation (D1), moderate – β1 inotropic, high – α vasoconstriction), dobutamine (β1, inotropic), vasopressin, phenylephrine.
- **Corticosteroids:** hydrocortisone in septic shock not responding to treatment and in adrenal crisis.
- Correction of acidosis and electrolyte imbalance; oxygen.

## Remember

- ACE inhibitors: dry cough, angioedema, hyperkalaemia; banned in pregnancy; ARBs do not cause cough.
- Methyldopa, labetalol and nifedipine are used in hypertension of pregnancy.
- Amlodipine → ankle oedema; clonidine → rebound hypertension; nitroprusside → hypertensive emergency.
- GTN sublingual relieves an angina attack in 1–2 minutes; headache and tolerance; never with sildenafil.
- CCBs are the drugs of choice in variant angina; β-blockers are not.
- Adenosine stops PSVT; amiodarone causes thyroid and lung problems.
- Statins inhibit HMG-CoA reductase; main risk myopathy; fibrates lower triglycerides.
- Digoxin inhibits Na+/K+ ATPase; toxicity worse with low potassium; antidote digoxin Fab.
- Anaphylactic shock → adrenaline IM; septic shock → fluids, antibiotics, noradrenaline; hypovolaemic → fluids.

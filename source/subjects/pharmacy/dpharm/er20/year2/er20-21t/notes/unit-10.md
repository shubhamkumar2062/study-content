# Unit 10: Hormones and hormone antagonists

This unit covers syllabus chapter 10: the physiological and pathological role and clinical uses of thyroid hormones, antithyroid drugs, parathormone, calcitonin, vitamin D, insulin, oral hypoglycaemic agents, oestrogen, progesterone, oxytocin and corticosteroids.

## 10.1 Hormones in general

A **hormone** is a chemical messenger secreted by an endocrine (ductless) gland directly into the blood and acting on distant target organs. Hormone secretion is mostly controlled by **negative feedback**: a rise in the hormone level reduces the release of the hormone that stimulates it.

```text
 Hypothalamus ─(TRH, CRH, GnRH)─► Anterior pituitary ─(TSH, ACTH, FSH/LH)─► Gland
       ▲                                                                 │
       └──────────────── negative feedback ◄── hormone (T4, cortisol, oestrogen)
```

**Hormone antagonists** block the synthesis, release or action of a hormone (antithyroid drugs, tamoxifen, mifepristone, spironolactone).

## 10.2 Thyroid hormones

The thyroid gland makes **thyroxine (T4)** and **tri-iodothyronine (T3)**, both containing iodine. T4 is the main secreted form; it is converted to the more active **T3** in tissues. Calcitonin is made by separate C-cells (see 10.5).

### Synthesis (steps)

```text
 1. Iodide trapping (Na+/I− symporter) — blocked by perchlorate, thiocyanate
 2. Oxidation of iodide to iodine (thyroid peroxidase)  ┐ blocked by
 3. Iodination of tyrosine in thyroglobulin → MIT, DIT  ├ carbimazole,
 4. Coupling: MIT + DIT → T3; DIT + DIT → T4             ┘ propylthiouracil
 5. Storage in colloid; release by proteolysis — blocked by iodides (Lugol's)
 6. Peripheral T4 → T3 — blocked by propylthiouracil, propranolol, steroids
```

### Physiological role

Increase basal metabolic rate, heat production and oxygen use; essential for **growth and brain development** in children; increase heart rate and force (they increase β-receptor responsiveness); increase breakdown of fats and carbohydrates; needed for normal reproduction and skin and hair.

### Pathological role

| Condition | Features |
| --- | --- |
| **Hypothyroidism** – adults (myxoedema) | Tiredness, weight gain, cold intolerance, dry skin, hair loss, constipation, slow pulse, puffy face, depression, heavy periods |
| Hypothyroidism – infants (**cretinism / congenital hypothyroidism**) | Mental retardation and stunted growth unless treated early (newborn screening) |
| **Hyperthyroidism (thyrotoxicosis)** – Graves' disease, toxic nodular goitre | Weight loss despite appetite, heat intolerance, sweating, tremor, palpitation, tachycardia, anxiety, diarrhoea, protruding eyes (Graves') |
| Goitre | Enlarged thyroid (iodine deficiency – prevented by iodised salt) |

### Clinical uses of thyroid hormones

**Levothyroxine (L-thyroxine sodium, T4)** is the drug of choice; **liothyronine (T3)** acts faster (myxoedema coma).
1. **Hypothyroidism** – lifelong replacement; dose adjusted by **TSH** level.
2. Congenital hypothyroidism – start immediately after diagnosis.
3. Myxoedema coma (emergency) – IV levothyroxine/liothyronine with hydrocortisone.
4. After thyroidectomy; TSH suppression in thyroid cancer; some non-toxic goitres.

**Counselling:** take on an empty stomach in the morning, 30–60 minutes before breakfast; keep apart from iron, calcium and antacids. Start with a low dose in elderly people and those with heart disease.
**Adverse effects (overdose):** symptoms of hyperthyroidism – palpitation, arrhythmia, angina, weight loss, osteoporosis.
**Misuse:** must not be used for weight reduction.

## 10.3 Antithyroid drugs

**Definition:** drugs that reduce the production or effects of thyroid hormones; used in hyperthyroidism.

### Classification

```text
 1. Inhibitors of hormone synthesis (thioamides): carbimazole, methimazole, propylthiouracil (PTU)
 2. Inhibitors of iodide trapping (ionic inhibitors): potassium perchlorate, thiocyanate
 3. Inhibitors of hormone release: iodine and iodides (Lugol's iodine, potassium iodide)
 4. Destroyers of thyroid tissue: radioactive iodine (131I)
 5. Adjuncts (control symptoms): β-blockers (propranolol)
```

| Drug | Points |
| --- | --- |
| **Carbimazole** (prodrug of methimazole) | Blocks thyroid peroxidase; once-daily dosing; effect after 3–4 weeks (stored hormone used first); drug of choice in most patients; **teratogenic in the first trimester** |
| **Propylthiouracil** | Also blocks peripheral T4 → T3; preferred in the **first trimester of pregnancy** and thyroid storm; risk of severe **liver damage** |
| **Lugol's iodine** / KI | Rapid fall in hormone release; makes gland smaller and firmer – **before thyroid surgery** (7–10 days), thyroid storm; effect wears off ("escape"); also protects the thyroid in radiation emergencies |
| **Radioactive iodine (131I)** | Taken up by thyroid and destroys tissue; Graves' disease and toxic nodules in adults; **contraindicated in pregnancy and breastfeeding**; later hypothyroidism common |
| **Propranolol** | Quick relief of tremor, palpitation and anxiety; blocks T4 → T3 a little |

**Adverse effects of thioamides:** rashes, itching, joint pains, **agranulocytosis** (rare, serious – patient must report **sore throat or fever** at once and get a blood count), liver injury (PTU).

**Thyroid storm** (emergency): PTU, iodide (given after PTU), propranolol, hydrocortisone, cooling, fluids.

## 10.4 Parathormone (PTH)

PTH is secreted by the **parathyroid glands** when plasma calcium falls.

**Physiological role:** **raises plasma calcium** and lowers phosphate by: (a) releasing calcium from bone (osteoclast activity); (b) increasing calcium reabsorption and phosphate excretion in the kidney; (c) increasing formation of active vitamin D (calcitriol) in the kidney, which increases intestinal calcium absorption.

**Pathological role:**
- **Hypoparathyroidism** (often after thyroid surgery): low calcium → tingling, muscle cramps, **tetany** (carpopedal spasm), convulsions. Treated with **IV calcium gluconate** in acute tetany, then oral calcium and **vitamin D (calcitriol)**.
- **Hyperparathyroidism:** high calcium → kidney stones, bone pain, abdominal pain, depression ("stones, bones, groans and moans"); treated by surgery; cinacalcet in some cases.

**Clinical uses:** **teriparatide** (recombinant PTH 1–34) given daily SC – intermittent use **builds bone** – for severe **osteoporosis**. Recombinant PTH for hypoparathyroidism in some patients.

## 10.5 Calcitonin

Secreted by the **parafollicular (C) cells of the thyroid** when plasma calcium rises.

**Physiological role:** **lowers plasma calcium** and phosphate by inhibiting osteoclast bone resorption and increasing their loss in urine (opposes PTH).

**Pathological role:** high calcitonin is a marker of **medullary carcinoma of the thyroid**.

**Clinical uses (salmon calcitonin – more potent, injection or nasal spray):** hypercalcaemia (rapid but short effect), **Paget's disease of bone**, pain of osteoporotic fractures; osteoporosis (now less used). **Adverse effects:** nausea, flushing, local reactions, nasal irritation.

## 10.6 Vitamin D

**Forms:** **cholecalciferol (D3)** – formed in skin by sunlight, also from fish oils; **ergocalciferol (D2)** – from plants. Both are converted in the **liver** to **calcidiol** (25-hydroxy D) and in the **kidney** (stimulated by PTH) to the active hormone **calcitriol** (1,25-dihydroxy D).

**Physiological role:** increases **intestinal absorption of calcium and phosphate**; helps bone mineralisation; with PTH, mobilises bone calcium; acts on immune and other cells.

**Pathological role:** deficiency → **rickets** in children (bowed legs, delayed walking) and **osteomalacia** in adults (bone pain, weakness); contributes to osteoporosis. Excess (hypervitaminosis D) → hypercalcaemia, kidney stones, calcification of soft tissues.

**Clinical uses:** prevention and treatment of rickets and osteomalacia; with calcium in **osteoporosis**; **calcitriol or alfacalcidol** in **chronic kidney disease** (kidney cannot activate vitamin D) and hypoparathyroidism; vitamin D-resistant rickets; **calcipotriol** (topical analogue) in psoriasis.

## 10.7 Insulin

**Diabetes mellitus** is a chronic metabolic disorder with high blood glucose due to lack of insulin or resistance to its action.

| Feature | Type 1 | Type 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cause | Autoimmune destruction of β-cells → **absolute insulin deficiency** | **Insulin resistance** + gradual β-cell failure |
| Age | Usually young | Usually adults, obesity |
| Ketoacidosis | Common | Uncommon |
| Treatment | **Insulin essential** | Diet, exercise, oral drugs; insulin later if needed |

Symptoms: polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, weight loss, tiredness. Complications: eye (retinopathy), kidney (nephropathy), nerves (neuropathy), heart attack, stroke, foot ulcers.

### Physiological role of insulin

Made by **β-cells of the islets of Langerhans** (pancreas) as proinsulin (insulin + C-peptide). It **lowers blood glucose**: increases uptake of glucose into muscle and fat (GLUT4), increases glycogen formation, decreases glucose production by the liver (gluconeogenesis, glycogenolysis); increases fat storage and protein synthesis; moves **potassium into cells**.

### Types of insulin preparations

| Type | Examples | Onset | Duration (approx.) | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rapid-acting** analogues | Insulin lispro, aspart, glulisine | 10–20 min | 3–5 h | Just before meals |
| **Short-acting** | Regular (soluble) insulin | 30–60 min | 6–8 h | Before meals; the **standard insulin given IV** (ketoacidosis); NPH and long-acting insulins are never given IV |
| **Intermediate** | NPH (isophane) insulin | 1–2 h | 12–18 h | Twice daily basal |
| **Long-acting** analogues | Insulin glargine, detemir, degludec | 1–2 h | About 24 h or more, peakless | Once-daily basal |
| Premixed | 30/70 regular/NPH | | | Twice daily |

### Clinical uses of insulin

1. **Type 1 diabetes** (always).
2. **Diabetic ketoacidosis** and hyperosmolar state – regular insulin IV infusion with fluids and potassium.
3. Type 2 diabetes not controlled by oral drugs; during surgery, severe infection, MI.
4. **Gestational diabetes** and diabetes in pregnancy (preferred; most oral drugs avoided).
5. **Hyperkalaemia** – insulin with glucose shifts potassium into cells.

### Adverse effects

- **Hypoglycaemia** (most common and dangerous): sweating, tremor, palpitation, hunger, confusion, fits, coma. **Treatment:** sugar or glucose by mouth if conscious; **IV 25–50% dextrose** or **glucagon IM** if unconscious.
- Weight gain; lipodystrophy at injection sites (rotate sites); local allergy; hypokalaemia.

**Storage:** unopened insulin in a refrigerator (2–8 °C), never frozen; the vial or pen in use may be kept at room temperature for a limited period stated on the label (usually about 4 weeks).

## 10.8 Oral hypoglycaemic (antidiabetic) agents

```text
 1. Biguanide: metformin
 2. Sulphonylureas
    First generation: tolbutamide, chlorpropamide (rarely used)
    Second generation: glibenclamide, glipizide, gliclazide, glimepiride
 3. Meglitinides (short-acting secretagogues): repaglinide, nateglinide
 4. Thiazolidinediones (glitazones): pioglitazone
 5. α-glucosidase inhibitors: acarbose, voglibose, miglitol
 6. DPP-4 inhibitors (gliptins): sitagliptin, vildagliptin, teneligliptin, linagliptin
 7. SGLT2 inhibitors (gliflozins): dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, canagliflozin
 (Injectable non-insulin: GLP-1 agonists – liraglutide, dulaglutide, semaglutide;
  semaglutide is also available orally)
```

| Class | Mechanism | Points and adverse effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Metformin** | ↓ glucose production by liver; ↑ insulin sensitivity; does not release insulin | **First-line in type 2 diabetes**; no hypoglycaemia alone; no weight gain; GI upset (take with food), metallic taste, **vitamin B12 deficiency**, rare **lactic acidosis** – avoid in severe kidney/liver failure, heart failure, hypoxia; stop before iodinated contrast and major surgery |
| **Sulphonylureas** | Close K-ATP channels on β-cells → **↑ insulin release** | **Hypoglycaemia** (glibenclamide most; elderly), weight gain; need functioning β-cells |
| Meglitinides | Like sulphonylureas, quick and short | Taken with meals; hypoglycaemia |
| **Pioglitazone** | PPAR-γ agonist → ↑ insulin sensitivity | Weight gain, **fluid retention (avoid in heart failure)**, fractures, possible bladder cancer risk |
| **Acarbose, voglibose** | Inhibit intestinal α-glucosidase → slow carbohydrate digestion | ↓ after-meal glucose; **flatulence**, diarrhoea; take with first bite of meal |
| **DPP-4 inhibitors** | Prevent breakdown of incretins (GLP-1) → ↑ insulin, ↓ glucagon after meals | Weight neutral, low hypoglycaemia risk; nasopharyngitis, rare pancreatitis |
| **SGLT2 inhibitors** | Block glucose reabsorption in proximal tubule → glucose lost in urine | Weight loss, ↓ BP, protect heart and kidney; **genital fungal infections**, urinary infection, dehydration, rare ketoacidosis |

**Contraindications of oral agents generally:** type 1 diabetes, diabetic ketoacidosis, pregnancy (insulin preferred; metformin is used in some cases under specialist advice), major surgery or severe illness, severe kidney or liver disease (depends on drug).

## 10.9 Oestrogens

**Natural:** estradiol (most potent), estrone, estriol – made mainly by the ovarian follicles (also placenta, adrenal, fat). **Synthetic:** **ethinylestradiol**, mestranol; conjugated equine oestrogens; non-steroidal – diethylstilbestrol (historical).

**Physiological role:** development of female sex organs and secondary sex characters (breasts, female fat distribution); growth of the endometrium in the first half of the menstrual cycle (proliferative phase); feedback control of FSH/LH; maintain bone mass (reduce bone resorption); raise HDL; increase clotting factors.

**Pathological role:** deficiency after menopause → hot flushes, vaginal dryness, osteoporosis; excess or unopposed oestrogen → endometrial hyperplasia and cancer risk; oestrogen-dependent breast cancer.

**Clinical uses:**
1. **Oral contraception** (ethinylestradiol + a progestin).
2. **Menopausal hormone therapy** – hot flushes, vaginal atrophy (with a progestin if the uterus is present to protect the endometrium).
3. Hypogonadism and delayed puberty in girls (Turner's syndrome).
4. Atrophic vaginitis (local cream).
5. Prostate cancer (rarely now).

**Adverse effects:** nausea, breast tenderness, fluid retention, headache, **venous thromboembolism**, raised BP, gallstones, **endometrial cancer** (unopposed), breast cancer risk (long-term HRT).
**Contraindications:** pregnancy, **history of thromboembolism**, oestrogen-dependent cancers (breast, endometrium), undiagnosed vaginal bleeding, active liver disease; combined pills in women over 35 who smoke.

**Antagonists/modulators:** **clomiphene citrate** (induces ovulation in infertility), **tamoxifen** (blocks oestrogen in breast – breast cancer), raloxifene (osteoporosis), **letrozole, anastrozole** (aromatase inhibitors – breast cancer).

## 10.10 Progesterone and progestins

**Natural:** progesterone – made by the **corpus luteum** (second half of the cycle) and the **placenta** in pregnancy. **Synthetic progestins:** medroxyprogesterone acetate, norethisterone, levonorgestrel, desogestrel, dydrogesterone.

**Physiological role:** converts the oestrogen-primed endometrium into a **secretory** endometrium ready for implantation; **maintains pregnancy** (relaxes uterus); thickens cervical mucus; develops breast glandular tissue; raises body temperature slightly after ovulation; feedback inhibition of LH (blocks ovulation).

**Pathological role:** low progesterone in the luteal phase → irregular bleeding and some miscarriages; withdrawal causes menstruation.

**Clinical uses:**
1. **Contraception:** combined pill, progestin-only pill, **injectable depot medroxyprogesterone acetate** (every 3 months), implants, **levonorgestrel IUD**, **emergency contraception** (levonorgestrel 1.5 mg single dose, best within 72 hours).
2. **Dysfunctional uterine bleeding**, **endometriosis**, dysmenorrhoea.
3. Menopausal hormone therapy (to protect endometrium).
4. Threatened/recurrent abortion in selected cases and luteal support in IVF (progesterone).
5. Endometrial cancer (palliative).

**Adverse effects:** irregular bleeding, weight gain, bloating, breast tenderness, mood changes, acne (androgenic progestins), lowered HDL.

**Antagonist:** **mifepristone** – blocks progesterone receptors; used with **misoprostol** for **medical termination of early pregnancy** (under the MTP Act, on prescription).

## 10.11 Oxytocin

Posterior pituitary hormone (made in the hypothalamus).

**Physiological role:** **contracts the pregnant uterus** at term (sensitivity rises greatly near delivery) and helps labour progress; **milk ejection reflex** – contracts myoepithelial cells around breast alveoli when the baby suckles.

**Clinical uses:**
1. **Induction of labour** at term (IV infusion, carefully controlled, slowly increased).
2. Augmentation of slow labour.
3. **Prevention and treatment of postpartum haemorrhage (PPH)** – given IM or IV right after delivery of the baby (active management of the third stage).
4. Incomplete abortion (with care).

**Adverse effects:** **uterine hyperstimulation** → foetal distress, **uterine rupture**; **water intoxication** (ADH-like action with large doses in dextrose); hypotension with rapid IV bolus.
**Contraindications:** **cephalopelvic disproportion**, abnormal foetal position, foetal distress, placenta praevia, previous caesarean or uterine surgery (caution), hypersensitivity.

**Other uterine stimulants:** **ergometrine/methylergometrine** (PPH – avoid in hypertension), misoprostol, carboprost (PGF2α analogue).

## 10.12 Corticosteroids

The **adrenal cortex** produces:
- **Glucocorticoids** (cortisol/hydrocortisone) – zona fasciculata, controlled by ACTH.
- **Mineralocorticoids** (aldosterone) – zona glomerulosa, controlled mainly by angiotensin II and potassium.
- Weak androgens – zona reticularis.

### Physiological role

| Glucocorticoid actions | Mineralocorticoid actions |
| --- | --- |
| ↑ blood glucose (gluconeogenesis), ↑ protein breakdown, fat redistribution; **anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive** (↓ phospholipase A2 via lipocortin, ↓ cytokines, ↓ lymphocytes); maintain BP (permissive for catecholamines); help the body cope with stress | **Sodium and water retention, potassium and hydrogen ion loss** in the distal tubule → maintain blood volume and BP |

### Pathological role

- **Addison's disease** (adrenal insufficiency): weakness, weight loss, low BP, low sodium, high potassium, skin pigmentation; **adrenal crisis** is life-threatening (IV hydrocortisone, saline, glucose).
- **Cushing's syndrome** (excess cortisol – disease or long-term steroid therapy): moon face, buffalo hump, central obesity, thin skin, purple striae, high BP, diabetes, osteoporosis, muscle weakness.
- Hyperaldosteronism (Conn's syndrome): high BP, low potassium.

### Preparations

| Drug | Glucocorticoid potency | Mineralocorticoid effect | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hydrocortisone | 1 | Significant | Short |
| Prednisolone | 4 | Moderate | Intermediate |
| Methylprednisolone | 5 | Slight | Intermediate |
| Triamcinolone | 5 | Nil | Intermediate |
| **Dexamethasone**, betamethasone | 25–30 | Nil | Long |
| **Fludrocortisone** | Some | **Very high** | Used as mineralocorticoid |

Topical: betamethasone, clobetasol, mometasone, hydrocortisone creams. Inhaled: beclomethasone, budesonide, fluticasone.

### Clinical uses

1. **Replacement therapy:** Addison's disease (hydrocortisone + fludrocortisone), adrenal crisis, congenital adrenal hyperplasia.
2. **Allergic conditions:** anaphylaxis (after adrenaline), angioedema, severe allergic reactions, allergic rhinitis (nasal).
3. **Bronchial asthma and COPD** (inhaled for control; oral/IV for acute attacks).
4. **Inflammatory and autoimmune diseases:** rheumatoid arthritis, SLE, vasculitis, inflammatory bowel disease, nephrotic syndrome, autoimmune haemolytic anaemia, ITP.
5. **Skin diseases:** eczema, psoriasis (topical), pemphigus.
6. **Eye:** allergic conjunctivitis, uveitis (not in viral or fungal infection of the eye).
7. **Organ transplantation** (prevent rejection) and **cancers** (leukaemias, lymphomas – with other drugs).
8. **Cerebral oedema** due to tumours (dexamethasone); **foetal lung maturity** in threatened preterm labour (betamethasone/dexamethasone to the mother); septic shock not responding to treatment; some severe infections under specialist advice (for example severe COVID-19 needing oxygen, tuberculous meningitis).

### Adverse effects (mainly long-term, high doses)

**Cushingoid features**, **hyperglycaemia**, **hypertension**, salt and water retention, hypokalaemia, **osteoporosis** and fractures, avascular necrosis of hip, **muscle weakness**, **susceptibility to infections** (TB flare, fungal infections) and masking of infection signs, **peptic ulcer** (especially with NSAIDs), delayed wound healing, cataract and glaucoma, mood changes and psychosis, **growth retardation in children**, **adrenal suppression** – sudden stopping after long use can cause **acute adrenal insufficiency** (always taper the dose).

**Contraindications/cautions:** active infections (TB, fungal, herpes of the eye), peptic ulcer, diabetes, hypertension, osteoporosis, psychosis, glaucoma, pregnancy (use only when needed); do not give live vaccines during high-dose therapy.

**Good practice:** lowest effective dose for the shortest time; single morning dose (matches natural rhythm, less suppression); alternate-day therapy where possible; taper slowly; carry a steroid card; calcium and vitamin D with long courses.

## Remember

- Levothyroxine for hypothyroidism – empty stomach in the morning; monitor TSH.
- Carbimazole blocks thyroid peroxidase (agranulocytosis – report sore throat); PTU in first trimester and storm; Lugol's iodine before surgery; 131I not in pregnancy.
- PTH ↑ calcium; calcitonin ↓ calcium (Paget's disease, hypercalcaemia); teriparatide builds bone.
- Vitamin D → calcitriol (kidney); deficiency → rickets/osteomalacia; calcitriol in kidney disease.
- Regular insulin is the standard insulin given IV (ketoacidosis); NPH and glargine are never given IV; glargine is long-acting basal; hypoglycaemia → glucose or glucagon.
- Metformin is first-line in type 2 diabetes (lactic acidosis rare); sulphonylureas cause hypoglycaemia; SGLT2 inhibitors cause genital infections.
- Oestrogen + progestin = combined pill; oestrogen risk: thromboembolism; levonorgestrel 1.5 mg for emergency contraception.
- Oxytocin induces labour and prevents PPH; risk of uterine rupture and water intoxication.
- Corticosteroids: anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive; never stop suddenly after long use; dexamethasone has no mineralocorticoid effect; fludrocortisone is the mineralocorticoid.

# How to use this guide

Pharmacology is a 75-hour second-year D.Pharm subject with 13 syllabus chapters. It is a "classification and facts" subject: most marks come from writing a clear **definition, classification, mechanism, uses, adverse effects and contraindications** for a drug group. Learn one unit every two or three days: read the notes, write each classification from memory, then answer the question bank without looking.

The guide follows the syllabus chapters one to one, except that **chapter 12 (chemotherapeutic agents, 12 hours) is split into two units** (12 and 13), so syllabus chapter 13 (biologicals) becomes guide unit 14.

Units 1, 2, 4, 5, 10 and 12–13 carry the most hours, so expect most long questions from them.

## Exam pattern (ER-2020 theory paper)

D.Pharm ER-2020 exams are yearly and are conducted by the state examining authority (in Himachal Pradesh, the HPTSB) under the PCI ER-2020 scheme. The theory paper is **80 marks, 3 hours**; another 20 marks come from sessional (internal) assessment. The usual paper looks like this. Confirm the exact split with your college, because boards sometimes change the format.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Objective questions (MCQs / one-word / fill in the blanks) – all compulsory | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| II | Short answers – attempt 10 of 11 | 10 × 3 = 30 |
| III | Long answers – attempt 6 of 7 | 6 × 5 = 30 |

In this guide the **long** questions are 5-mark answers and the **short** questions are 3-mark answers, to match this pattern. A few **very-short** (2-mark) questions are included for quick revision and for sessional tests. The full mock paper in the app follows the same pattern, and a 20-question quick MCQ test is there for daily practice.

## Probability ratings

- **High** – asked in most years; prepare the full answer with classification and a table.
- **Medium** – asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** – rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

Ratings are an estimate from the syllabus weight and the kind of questions usually set in D.Pharm papers. They are not a promise.

## Scoring tips for Pharmacology

1. **Use the standard answer frame** for any drug group: definition → classification (with 2–3 examples each) → mechanism of action → pharmacological actions → uses → adverse effects → contraindications → (dose only if you are sure). Examiners look for this order.
2. **Classification carries the most marks.** Write it as a neat tree or numbered list with headings (for example "A. Direct-acting – 1. Choline esters: …"). Learn at least two examples in every group.
3. **One line of mechanism** for every drug you name: "omeprazole irreversibly blocks H+/K+ ATPase", "warfarin blocks vitamin K epoxide reductase".
4. **Draw simple flow diagrams** where they help: synthesis of ACh and NA, arachidonic acid pathway, renin–angiotensin system, clotting cascade, nephron sites of diuretic action, malaria life cycle.
5. **Make comparison tables:** heparin vs warfarin, loop vs thiazide diuretics, depolarising vs non-depolarising blockers, first- vs second-generation antihistamines, TCAs vs SSRIs, typical vs atypical antipsychotics.
6. **Remember the "signature" facts** that MCQs love: antidotes (naloxone, flumazenil, protamine, vitamin K, atropine + pralidoxime, N-acetylcysteine, desferrioxamine), characteristic adverse effects (phenytoin – gum hypertrophy, ethambutol – optic neuritis, ACE inhibitor – dry cough, cyclophosphamide – haemorrhagic cystitis), and drugs of choice.
7. **Doses:** write a dose only when you are certain (for example paracetamol 500–1000 mg, maximum 4 g a day; adrenaline 0.5 mg IM of 1:1000 in anaphylaxis; albendazole 400 mg single dose). A wrong dose can cost marks.
8. **For a 5-mark answer** aim for about one and a half pages; **for a 3-mark answer** write 5–6 crisp points or a small table.
9. **Link to practice:** add one counselling point where it fits (take PPIs before breakfast, rinse mouth after steroid inhaler, complete the antibiotic course). It shows a pharmacist's understanding.
10. Revise the last-night revision sheet on the day before the exam, and attempt the full mock paper at least twice in 3 hours.

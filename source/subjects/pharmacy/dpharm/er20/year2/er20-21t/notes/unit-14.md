# Unit 14: Biologicals

This unit covers syllabus chapter 13: definition, types and indications of biological agents, with examples.

## 14.1 Definition

**Biologicals (biological products, biopharmaceuticals or "biologics")** are medicines that are **made by or derived from living organisms** – human, animal, plant or microbial cells – usually by **biotechnology** (recombinant DNA technology, cell culture) or obtained from blood and tissues. They are usually **large, complex molecules** (proteins, glycoproteins, nucleic acids, whole cells or micro-organisms) as opposed to small, chemically synthesised drugs.

| Feature | Small-molecule drugs | Biologicals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source | Chemical synthesis | Living cells or organisms |
| Size | Small (e.g. aspirin, about 180 Da) | Large (insulin about 5,800 Da; antibodies about 150,000 Da) |
| Structure | Simple, fully defined | Complex; depends on the manufacturing process |
| Route | Often oral | Usually **injection** (destroyed in the gut) |
| Stability | Usually stable at room temperature | Sensitive to heat, light and shaking – **cold chain (2–8 °C)** |
| Immunogenicity | Rare | Can provoke antibodies and allergic reactions |
| Copies | Generics (identical) | **Biosimilars** (highly similar, not identical) |

**Biosimilar:** a biological product that is highly similar to an already approved reference biological in quality, safety and efficacy, with no clinically meaningful differences. In India biosimilars are approved under the "Guidelines on Similar Biologics" (CDSCO with the Department of Biotechnology).

**Regulation in India:** biologicals come under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act and Rules (vaccines, sera, blood products are listed in **Schedule C and C1**); recombinant products also need clearance from the Review Committee on Genetic Manipulation. Vaccines are tested by the Central Drugs Laboratory, Kasauli.

## 14.2 Types of biologicals

```text
 Biologicals
  ├── 1. Vaccines and toxoids
  ├── 2. Immune sera, antitoxins and immunoglobulins (passive immunity)
  ├── 3. Blood and blood products
  ├── 4. Recombinant proteins
  │      ├── Hormones (insulin, growth hormone)
  │      ├── Growth factors / cytokines (erythropoietin, G-CSF, interferons, interleukins)
  │      ├── Enzymes (alteplase, dornase alfa, enzyme replacement)
  │      └── Clotting factors (factor VIII, IX)
  ├── 5. Monoclonal antibodies and fusion proteins
  ├── 6. Cell and gene therapies (CAR-T cells, stem cells, gene therapy)
  └── 7. Others: allergenic extracts, probiotics, diagnostic agents (tuberculin)
```

## 14.3 Vaccines and toxoids

A **vaccine** is a preparation of killed or weakened micro-organisms, or their parts or products, which when given stimulates the body to make its own antibodies and memory cells → **active immunity**.

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| **Live attenuated** (weakened) | BCG, oral polio vaccine (OPV), measles–rubella (MR), MMR, rotavirus, yellow fever, varicella |
| **Killed (inactivated)** | Injectable polio vaccine (IPV), rabies vaccine (cell culture), hepatitis A, Japanese encephalitis (killed type), whole-cell pertussis |
| **Toxoids** (inactivated toxins) | Tetanus toxoid, diphtheria toxoid (in DPT, Td) |
| **Subunit / recombinant / conjugate** | Hepatitis B (recombinant surface antigen), HPV, pneumococcal conjugate (PCV), Hib conjugate, typhoid conjugate vaccine |
| **Newer platforms** | mRNA vaccines, viral-vector vaccines (some COVID-19 vaccines) |

**Indications:** prevention of infectious diseases – **Universal Immunisation Programme (UIP)** covers BCG, OPV/IPV, hepatitis B, pentavalent (DPT + hepatitis B + Hib), rotavirus, PCV, MR, JE (in endemic districts), DPT boosters and Td; travel and occupational vaccines; post-exposure rabies vaccination.

**Adverse effects:** pain, swelling, fever; rarely allergic reactions or anaphylaxis; live vaccines can cause disease in people with weak immunity.
**Contraindications:** severe allergic reaction to an earlier dose; **live vaccines in pregnancy and in immunocompromised patients** (high-dose steroids, cancer treatment, advanced HIV).
**Storage:** most vaccines at **2–8 °C** (never freeze toxoid and hepatitis B vaccines); some live vaccines tolerate freezing; **Vaccine Vial Monitor (VVM)** on the label shows heat exposure; **open vial policy** for some vaccines.

## 14.4 Immune sera, antitoxins and immunoglobulins

These give **passive immunity** – ready-made antibodies that act at once but last only a few weeks.

| Product | Source | Indications |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Anti-snake venom (ASV)** – polyvalent | Horse serum | Bites of the "big four" Indian snakes (cobra, common krait, Russell's viper, saw-scaled viper) |
| **Tetanus immunoglobulin (human)** / tetanus antitoxin (equine) | Human / horse | Tetanus-prone wounds in non-immunised persons; treatment of tetanus |
| **Diphtheria antitoxin** | Horse | Treatment of diphtheria |
| **Rabies immunoglobulin** (human or equine) | Human / horse | Category III rabies exposures – infiltrated into the wound with vaccine |
| Anti-D immunoglobulin | Human | Rh-negative mother after delivery of Rh-positive baby – prevents haemolytic disease in the next pregnancy |
| Hepatitis B immunoglobulin | Human | Newborns of HBsAg-positive mothers, needle-stick injury |
| Normal human immunoglobulin (IVIG) | Pooled human plasma | Immune deficiency, Kawasaki disease, Guillain–Barré syndrome, ITP, myasthenic crisis |

**Adverse effects:** animal (equine) sera can cause **anaphylaxis** and **serum sickness** (fever, rash, joint pain 7–10 days later) – keep adrenaline ready; human products are safer.

## 14.5 Blood and blood products

Whole blood, packed red cells, platelets, fresh frozen plasma, cryoprecipitate, **albumin** (hypovolaemia, burns, low albumin), **clotting factor concentrates** (factor VIII for haemophilia A, factor IX for haemophilia B). Risks: transfusion reactions, infection transmission (screened for HIV, hepatitis B and C, syphilis, malaria), fluid overload.

## 14.6 Recombinant protein drugs

Made by inserting the human gene into bacteria (*E. coli*), yeast or mammalian cells, which then produce the protein.

| Biological | Examples | Indications |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Insulin** and analogues | Human insulin, glargine, lispro | Diabetes mellitus (Unit 10) |
| Growth hormone | Somatropin | Growth hormone deficiency (short stature) |
| **Erythropoietin** | Epoetin alfa, darbepoetin | Anaemia of chronic kidney disease and chemotherapy (Unit 6) |
| **G-CSF** (filgrastim, pegfilgrastim) | Stimulate neutrophil production | Neutropenia after chemotherapy, bone-marrow transplantation |
| **Interferons** | Interferon alfa (peginterferon), interferon beta | Hepatitis B (some cases), certain cancers; **multiple sclerosis** (beta) |
| **Thrombolytic enzymes** | Alteplase, tenecteplase | Acute MI, ischaemic stroke (Unit 6) |
| Other enzymes | Dornase alfa (cystic fibrosis), enzyme replacement (imiglucerase – Gaucher's disease) | Rare enzyme deficiencies |
| **Clotting factors** | Recombinant factor VIII, IX | Haemophilia A, B |
| Hormone analogues | Teriparatide (osteoporosis), GLP-1 agonists (liraglutide, semaglutide) | Osteoporosis, type 2 diabetes, obesity |

## 14.7 Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs)

**Monoclonal antibodies** are identical antibodies produced by a single clone of cells, all binding one specific target (antigen). They are made by **hybridoma technology** (Köhler and Milstein) and now by recombinant techniques.

### Naming (the stem "-mab")

| Ending | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| -**ximab** | Chimeric (part mouse, part human) | **Rituximab**, infliximab |
| -**zumab** | Humanised (mostly human) | **Trastuzumab**, **bevacizumab**, omalizumab |
| -**umab** | Fully human | **Adalimumab**, denosumab, nivolumab |
| -**cept** | Fusion protein (receptor + antibody part) | **Etanercept** |

### Important mAbs and their indications

| Drug | Target | Indications |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rituximab** | CD20 on B cells | Non-Hodgkin lymphoma, chronic lymphocytic leukaemia, rheumatoid arthritis |
| **Trastuzumab** | HER2 receptor | **HER2-positive breast cancer** (cardiotoxicity – monitor heart) |
| **Bevacizumab** | VEGF (blood vessel growth) | Colon, lung and other cancers; eye diseases (wet macular degeneration – related drugs) |
| Cetuximab | EGFR | Colorectal, head and neck cancer |
| **Infliximab, adalimumab**; etanercept (fusion protein) | **TNF-α** | Rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis, ankylosing spondylitis, Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis – **screen for latent TB before starting** |
| **Omalizumab** | IgE | Severe allergic asthma, chronic urticaria |
| Denosumab | RANKL | Osteoporosis, bone metastases |
| Nivolumab, pembrolizumab | PD-1 (immune checkpoint) | Melanoma, lung and other cancers (immunotherapy) |
| Tocilizumab | IL-6 receptor | Rheumatoid arthritis, cytokine release syndrome |
| Palivizumab | RSV | Prevention of RSV infection in high-risk infants |

### Adverse effects of biologicals (general)

- **Infusion reactions** (fever, chills, rash, low BP) – premedication with paracetamol, antihistamine, steroid.
- **Hypersensitivity** and anaphylaxis; formation of antibodies against the drug (loss of effect).
- **Infections** with immunosuppressive biologicals (TB reactivation with anti-TNF drugs, hepatitis B reactivation with rituximab).
- Target-specific effects: heart damage (trastuzumab), bleeding and poor wound healing (bevacizumab), autoimmune reactions (checkpoint inhibitors).
- **Very high cost.**

**Contraindications/cautions:** active serious infection (including untreated TB) for immunosuppressive biologicals; live vaccines during therapy; known hypersensitivity; heart failure (anti-TNF); pregnancy (many – specialist decision).

## 14.8 Cell and gene therapies

- **Stem cell / bone-marrow transplantation:** leukaemias, aplastic anaemia, thalassaemia.
- **CAR-T cell therapy:** patient's T cells are engineered to attack cancer cells (some leukaemias and lymphomas); India has approved its own CAR-T product.
- **Gene therapy:** a working gene is delivered (often by a viral vector) to correct an inherited disorder (spinal muscular atrophy, some haemophilias).

## 14.9 Pharmacist's role with biologicals

1. **Cold chain:** receive, store and transport at 2–8 °C; check the thermometer and log; do not freeze unless allowed; protect from light; keep away from the freezer compartment and door.
2. Do not shake protein solutions (foaming denatures proteins); inspect for particles and colour change.
3. Check batch number and expiry; record batch for traceability (pharmacovigilance).
4. Counsel on **self-injection** (insulin pens, adalimumab pens, LMWH), rotation of sites and safe disposal of sharps.
5. Explain that biosimilars are not automatically interchangeable without the prescriber's decision.
6. Report adverse events (Pharmacovigilance Programme of India; AEFI system for vaccines).

## Remember

- Biologicals are large, complex medicines made from living cells; usually injected; need cold chain (2–8 °C).
- Biosimilars are highly similar, not identical, copies of biologicals.
- Types: vaccines/toxoids (active immunity), sera/immunoglobulins (passive immunity), blood products, recombinant proteins, monoclonal antibodies, cell and gene therapies.
- Live vaccines (BCG, OPV, MR, rotavirus) are contraindicated in pregnancy and immunosuppression.
- Equine sera (ASV, antitoxins) can cause anaphylaxis and serum sickness.
- -ximab chimeric, -zumab humanised, -umab human, -cept fusion protein.
- Rituximab → CD20 (lymphoma); trastuzumab → HER2 (breast cancer); infliximab/adalimumab → TNF-α (rheumatoid arthritis; screen for TB); omalizumab → IgE (asthma).

# Unit 11: Autocoids

This unit covers syllabus chapter 11: the physiological role of histamine, 5-hydroxytryptamine (serotonin) and prostaglandins, and the classification, clinical uses and adverse effects of antihistamines and 5-HT antagonists.

## 11.1 What are autocoids?

**Autocoids** (from Greek *autos* = self, *akos* = remedy) are substances made in many tissues that act **locally** near their site of formation, usually for a short time. They are sometimes called **local hormones**. Unlike true hormones, they are not made by one special gland and do not travel far in the blood.

| Group | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Amine autocoids | **Histamine**, **5-hydroxytryptamine (serotonin)** |
| Lipid-derived (eicosanoids) | **Prostaglandins**, thromboxanes, leukotrienes; platelet-activating factor |
| Peptide autocoids | Bradykinin, angiotensin, substance P |

## 11.2 Histamine

### Formation, storage and release

Histamine is made by **decarboxylation of the amino acid histidine** (enzyme: histidine decarboxylase). It is stored mainly in **mast cells** (skin, lungs, gut mucosa) and **basophils**, and also in gastric enterochromaffin-like cells and brain neurons.

**Release** occurs in:
- **Allergic (type I) reactions:** antigen binds IgE on mast cells → degranulation.
- Tissue injury, burns, insect stings.
- Drugs that release histamine directly: morphine, d-tubocurarine, vancomycin (rapid infusion – "red man syndrome"), radiocontrast media.

It is broken down by **diamine oxidase** and **histamine N-methyltransferase**.

### Histamine receptors and physiological role

| Receptor | Location | Effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **H1** | Smooth muscle of bronchi, gut; blood vessels; nerve endings; brain | **Bronchoconstriction**, gut contraction, **vasodilatation** (via NO) and ↑ capillary permeability → **flushing, swelling (wheal), fall in BP**; **itching and pain** (nerve endings); wakefulness in brain |
| **H2** | **Gastric parietal cells**, heart, blood vessels | **↑ gastric acid secretion**, ↑ heart rate and force, vasodilatation |
| H3 | Presynaptic in brain | Inhibits release of histamine and other transmitters |
| H4 | Blood cells (eosinophils, mast cells) | Chemotaxis in inflammation |

**Triple response of Lewis** (on injecting histamine into skin): **red spot** (local dilatation), **flare** (spreading redness by axon reflex), **wheal** (local swelling from leaky capillaries).

**Pathological role:** allergic diseases – urticaria (hives), allergic rhinitis, conjunctivitis, angioedema, **anaphylaxis** (severe fall in BP, bronchospasm, laryngeal oedema), part of bronchial asthma; gastric acid in peptic ulcer.

Histamine itself has no therapeutic use (once used as a diagnostic agent for gastric secretion). **Betahistine** (a histamine analogue) is used in Ménière's disease (vertigo).

## 11.3 Antihistamines (H1 receptor antagonists)

**Definition:** drugs that **competitively block H1 receptors** and so prevent the effects of histamine. H2 blockers (famotidine) are dealt with as anti-ulcer drugs in Unit 8.

### Classification

```text
 A. First-generation (sedating) H1 antihistamines
    Highly sedating: diphenhydramine, promethazine, hydroxyzine, dimenhydrinate, doxylamine
    Moderately sedating: pheniramine, cyproheptadine, meclizine
    Mildly sedating: chlorpheniramine (chlorphenamine), dexchlorpheniramine, triprolidine, cyclizine
 B. Second-generation (non-sedating) H1 antihistamines
    Cetirizine, levocetirizine, loratadine, desloratadine, fexofenadine,
    ebastine, rupatadine, bilastine, azelastine (nasal/eye), olopatadine (eye)
```

### Comparison

| Feature | First generation | Second generation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry into brain | Yes (lipid-soluble) | Very little |
| **Sedation** | **Marked** | Little or none (cetirizine may cause mild drowsiness) |
| **Anticholinergic effects** | Present (dry mouth, urinary retention, blurred vision) | Absent |
| Antiemetic / anti-motion sickness | Yes (promethazine, dimenhydrinate) | No |
| Duration | Short (several doses a day) | Long (once daily) |
| Use | Motion sickness, vomiting, insomnia, cough mixtures, night-time itching | Allergic rhinitis, urticaria – daytime use |

### Pharmacological actions

Block histamine-induced bronchoconstriction (weakly), vasodilatation, capillary leak, itching and sneezing. First-generation drugs also cause CNS depression, anticholinergic and local anaesthetic effects; some (cyproheptadine) also block 5-HT.

### Clinical uses

1. **Allergic rhinitis** (hay fever) – sneezing, running nose, itchy eyes – second-generation drugs; azelastine nasal spray.
2. **Urticaria**, allergic dermatoses, itching (pruritus), insect bites, atopic dermatitis.
3. **Allergic conjunctivitis** – olopatadine, azelastine eye drops.
4. **Anaphylaxis and angioedema** – IV chlorpheniramine as an **add-on after adrenaline** (never instead of adrenaline).
5. **Motion sickness** – promethazine, dimenhydrinate, meclizine (taken before travel).
6. **Vomiting** – morning sickness (doxylamine with pyridoxine), postoperative vomiting.
7. **Insomnia** (diphenhydramine, doxylamine) and **sedation** for minor procedures (promethazine, hydroxyzine).
8. **Common cold and cough** mixtures (drying of secretions).
9. Drug-induced parkinsonism (diphenhydramine – anticholinergic).
10. Vertigo (cinnarizine, meclizine).
11. Appetite stimulation (cyproheptadine) – not recommended routinely.

### Adverse effects

- **Sedation, drowsiness**, impaired concentration, dizziness – dangerous while **driving or operating machinery**; increased by alcohol and other depressants.
- **Anticholinergic:** dry mouth, blurred vision, constipation, **urinary retention**.
- Paradoxical excitement in children; convulsions in overdose.
- Gastric upset; allergic skin reactions with topical use.
- Older drugs (terfenadine, astemizole) caused dangerous **QT prolongation** with ketoconazole/erythromycin and were withdrawn.

**Contraindications/cautions:** driving and machine work (first generation), **angle-closure glaucoma** and **prostate enlargement** (first generation), epilepsy, infants and newborns, late pregnancy and breastfeeding (some), with alcohol and CNS depressants.

### Other approaches to allergy

**Mast cell stabilisers** (sodium cromoglycate), **corticosteroids** (nasal and systemic), **adrenaline** (physiological antagonist of histamine – drug of choice in anaphylaxis), leukotriene antagonists (montelukast).

## 11.4 5-Hydroxytryptamine (5-HT, serotonin)

### Formation and distribution

Made from the amino acid **tryptophan** (tryptophan → 5-hydroxytryptophan → 5-HT). About **90% is in the enterochromaffin cells of the gut**, most of the rest is carried in **platelets**, and a small amount is in **brain neurons** (raphe nuclei). It is broken down by **MAO** to 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid (5-HIAA), which is excreted in urine. In the pineal gland, 5-HT is converted to **melatonin**.

### Receptors and physiological role

| Receptor | Main actions |
| --- | --- |
| 5-HT1 (1A, 1B, 1D) | Brain: mood, anxiety (1A); **constriction of cranial blood vessels** (1B/1D) – target of triptans |
| 5-HT2 (2A, 2B, 2C) | Contraction of smooth muscle, **platelet aggregation**, vasoconstriction; brain – hallucinations (2A), appetite (2C) |
| **5-HT3** | The only ion-channel 5-HT receptor; on vagal nerve endings in gut and the **CTZ** – **nausea and vomiting** |
| 5-HT4 | Gut – **↑ peristalsis** and secretion |
| 5-HT7 | Brain, blood vessels |

**Physiological roles:**
- **Gut:** increases motility and secretion (peristaltic reflex).
- **Blood vessels:** constriction of large vessels, dilatation of small ones; platelet aggregation at sites of injury (helps haemostasis).
- **CNS:** regulates **mood, sleep, appetite, temperature, pain** perception and vomiting.

**Pathological role:**
- **Depression and anxiety** (low brain 5-HT activity).
- **Migraine** (changes in 5-HT and cranial vessels).
- **Nausea and vomiting** – especially due to **cancer chemotherapy** and radiotherapy (5-HT3).
- **Carcinoid syndrome** – a tumour of enterochromaffin cells releases large amounts of 5-HT → flushing, diarrhoea, bronchospasm, heart valve damage.
- **Serotonin syndrome** with combinations of serotonergic drugs.

**Drugs that act as 5-HT agonists:** **triptans** (sumatriptan, rizatriptan – 5-HT1B/1D; acute migraine), **buspirone** (5-HT1A partial agonist; anxiety), prucalopride (5-HT4; constipation). SSRIs raise 5-HT activity indirectly (Unit 4).

## 11.5 5-HT antagonists

**Definition:** drugs that block one or more types of 5-HT receptors.

### Classification and uses

| Drug | Receptor blocked | Clinical uses | Important adverse effects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ondansetron**, granisetron, palonosetron | **5-HT3** | **Nausea and vomiting due to cancer chemotherapy**, radiotherapy, and **after surgery**; vomiting in gastroenteritis (children, single dose) | Headache, constipation, dizziness, **QT prolongation** |
| **Cyproheptadine** | 5-HT2 (+ H1, anticholinergic) | Allergic itching, **carcinoid diarrhoea**, serotonin syndrome, (appetite stimulant) | Sedation, dry mouth, weight gain |
| **Ketanserin** | 5-HT2A (+ α1) | Hypertension (rarely used) | Hypotension, dizziness |
| **Methysergide** | 5-HT2 | Former migraine prophylaxis, carcinoid | **Retroperitoneal fibrosis** – largely abandoned |
| **Pizotifen** | 5-HT2 | Migraine prophylaxis | Weight gain, drowsiness |
| Atypical antipsychotics (clozapine, risperidone, olanzapine) | 5-HT2A (+ D2) | Schizophrenia (Unit 4) | Weight gain, metabolic effects |

### Drugs for migraine (related)

**Migraine:** recurrent, often one-sided throbbing headache with nausea, light and sound sensitivity, sometimes preceded by an aura.

| Purpose | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| Mild attack | Paracetamol, aspirin, ibuprofen, naproxen + antiemetic (metoclopramide, domperidone) |
| Moderate/severe attack | **Triptans** (sumatriptan) – contraindicated in ischaemic heart disease, uncontrolled hypertension; **ergotamine** (older; vasoconstriction, avoid in pregnancy and vascular disease) |
| Prophylaxis (frequent attacks) | **Propranolol**, **flunarizine**, **amitriptyline**, topiramate, sodium valproate, pizotifen |

## 11.6 Prostaglandins

### Formation

**Prostaglandins (PGs)** are **eicosanoids** – 20-carbon fatty acid derivatives made from **arachidonic acid** in almost all cells. They are not stored but made when needed.

```text
 Membrane phospholipids
        │ phospholipase A2 (inhibited by corticosteroids)
        ▼
 Arachidonic acid
   ├── Cyclo-oxygenase (COX-1, COX-2; inhibited by NSAIDs)
   │      └► PGG2 → PGH2 → PGE2, PGF2α, PGD2
   │                       → PGI2 (prostacyclin; vessel wall)
   │                       → TXA2 (thromboxane; platelets)
   └── Lipoxygenase (5-LOX; inhibited by zileuton)
          └► Leukotrienes (LTB4 – chemotaxis; LTC4, LTD4 – bronchoconstriction)
```

### Physiological role

| Site | Prostaglandin and effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Stomach** | PGE2, PGI2 – **↓ acid, ↑ mucus and bicarbonate** – protect the mucosa (NSAIDs remove this → ulcers) |
| **Platelets and vessels** | **TXA2** (platelets) – **aggregation** and vasoconstriction; **PGI2** (endothelium) – **prevents aggregation**, vasodilatation; balance keeps blood fluid |
| **Kidney** | PGE2, PGI2 – maintain renal blood flow and salt excretion (important in heart failure, dehydration) |
| **Uterus** | PGE2, PGF2α – **contraction**, cervical ripening at term; menstrual cramps |
| **Inflammation** | PGE2, PGI2 – vasodilatation, swelling, **pain** (sensitise nerve endings), **fever** (act on hypothalamus) |
| Bronchi | PGF2α constricts, PGE2 relaxes |
| Ductus arteriosus | PGE1/E2 keep it open in the foetus |
| Eye | PGF2α increases aqueous outflow |

**Pathological role:** pain, fever and swelling of inflammation; dysmenorrhoea (excess uterine PGs); thrombosis (TXA2); patent ductus arteriosus in premature babies.

### Clinical uses of prostaglandin analogues

| Drug | Type | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Misoprostol** | PGE1 analogue | Prevention of **NSAID-induced peptic ulcer**; with mifepristone for **medical abortion**; cervical ripening and labour induction; postpartum haemorrhage (where oxytocin is not available) |
| **Dinoprostone** | PGE2 | **Cervical ripening and induction of labour** (vaginal gel/pessary); termination of pregnancy |
| **Carboprost** | 15-methyl PGF2α | **Postpartum haemorrhage** not controlled by oxytocin (IM); abortion – avoid in asthma |
| **Alprostadil** | PGE1 | Keeps the **ductus arteriosus open** in newborns with some heart defects until surgery; **erectile dysfunction** (injection) |
| **Latanoprost**, bimatoprost, travoprost | PGF2α analogues | **Glaucoma** (↑ uveoscleral outflow) – Unit 3 |
| Epoprostenol, iloprost | PGI2 | **Pulmonary arterial hypertension**; antiplatelet during dialysis |

**Adverse effects of PG analogues:** **diarrhoea**, abdominal cramps, nausea and vomiting, fever, flushing; uterine contractions (**misoprostol contraindicated in pregnancy** when used for ulcers); bronchospasm (PGF2α); iris pigmentation (latanoprost); apnoea in newborns (alprostadil).

**Drugs that reduce PG synthesis:** NSAIDs (block COX) and corticosteroids (block phospholipase A2 indirectly) – see Units 2 and 10.

## Remember

- Autocoids = local hormones: histamine, 5-HT, prostaglandins, leukotrienes, bradykinin.
- Histamine from histidine; stored in mast cells; H1 → bronchoconstriction, vasodilatation, itching; H2 → gastric acid; triple response = red spot, flare, wheal.
- First-generation antihistamines sedate and are anticholinergic (avoid driving, glaucoma, prostate); second-generation (cetirizine, fexofenadine) are non-sedating.
- In anaphylaxis, adrenaline first; antihistamines are only add-on.
- 5-HT from tryptophan; 90% in gut; 5-HT3 → vomiting; ondansetron (5-HT3 antagonist) for chemotherapy vomiting; triptans (5-HT1B/1D agonists) for acute migraine.
- PGs from arachidonic acid by COX; PGE2 protects stomach; TXA2 aggregates platelets, PGI2 prevents it.
- Misoprostol: NSAID ulcer prevention and abortion; dinoprostone: labour induction; carboprost: PPH; alprostadil: patent ductus; latanoprost: glaucoma.

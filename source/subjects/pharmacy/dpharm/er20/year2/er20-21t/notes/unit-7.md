# Unit 7: Drugs acting on the respiratory system

This unit covers syllabus chapter 7: bronchodilators, expectorants, antitussive agents and mucolytic agents. It also explains bronchial asthma and COPD, because most bronchodilator questions are asked in that setting.

## 7.1 Bronchial asthma and COPD

**Bronchial asthma** is a chronic inflammatory disease of the airways with **reversible** narrowing (bronchospasm), mucosal swelling and thick mucus. Attacks are triggered by allergens (dust mites, pollen), cold air, exercise, smoke, infections, aspirin and β-blockers. Symptoms: wheeze, breathlessness, chest tightness and cough, often worse at night.

**COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease)** – chronic bronchitis and emphysema – is a mostly **irreversible**, slowly progressive airflow limitation, usually from **smoking**.

```text
 Allergen → IgE on mast cells → release of histamine, leukotrienes, PGs
   → early phase: bronchospasm (minutes)
   → late phase: inflammation, eosinophils, swelling, mucus (hours)
 Drugs: bronchodilators relieve spasm; corticosteroids control inflammation
```

### Approaches to treatment

| Approach | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| Relieve bronchospasm | β2 agonists, anticholinergics, methylxanthines |
| Reduce inflammation (controllers) | **Inhaled corticosteroids**, leukotriene antagonists |
| Prevent mediator release | Mast cell stabilisers |
| Block IgE | Omalizumab (anti-IgE antibody) |

## 7.2 Bronchodilators

**Definition:** drugs that relax bronchial smooth muscle and widen the airways.

### Classification

```text
 1. β2-adrenergic agonists
    Short-acting (SABA, relievers): salbutamol (albuterol), terbutaline, levosalbutamol
    Long-acting (LABA): salmeterol, formoterol, (once daily: indacaterol)
 2. Anticholinergics (muscarinic antagonists)
    Short-acting: ipratropium bromide
    Long-acting: tiotropium bromide, glycopyrronium
 3. Methylxanthines: theophylline, aminophylline, doxofylline, etofylline
 4. Leukotriene antagonists: montelukast, zafirlukast (controllers, mild bronchodilation)
 Also used: adrenaline (emergency), inhaled corticosteroids (anti-inflammatory controllers)
```

### β2 agonists

**Mechanism:** stimulate **β2 receptors** → ↑ cAMP → bronchial smooth muscle relaxes; they also reduce mediator release from mast cells.

| Feature | Salbutamol / terbutaline (SABA) | Salmeterol / formoterol (LABA) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Onset | Within minutes | Formoterol fast; salmeterol slow |
| Duration | 4–6 hours | About 12 hours |
| Role | **Relief of acute attack** ("reliever") | Regular use for control, **always with an inhaled steroid** in asthma (never alone) |

**Routes:** inhalation (metered-dose inhaler, dry powder inhaler, nebuliser) is preferred – fast, small dose, few side effects; oral and injection forms exist.

**Adverse effects:** **fine tremor** of hands, palpitation and tachycardia, muscle cramps, **hypokalaemia** (with high doses), headache; tolerance with regular use.
**Cautions:** heart disease, hyperthyroidism, diabetes.

### Anticholinergics

**Ipratropium and tiotropium** block M3 receptors in the airways → bronchodilation and less mucus. Given by inhalation, so few systemic effects (dry mouth, bitter taste). **More useful in COPD** than in asthma; ipratropium is added to salbutamol in nebulisation for acute severe asthma. Care in glaucoma (avoid spraying into eyes) and prostate enlargement.

### Methylxanthines (theophylline, aminophylline)

**Mechanism:** inhibit **phosphodiesterase (PDE)** → ↑ cAMP; also block **adenosine receptors**.
**Actions:** bronchodilation, CNS stimulation, cardiac stimulation, mild diuresis, stimulation of breathing (useful in apnoea of prematurity – caffeine).
**Uses:** asthma and COPD not controlled by inhaled drugs (sustained-release theophylline), aminophylline IV in severe cases (now less used), apnoea of prematurity (caffeine citrate).
**Narrow therapeutic index** – monitor blood levels. **Toxicity:** nausea, vomiting, insomnia, restlessness, tachycardia, **arrhythmias, convulsions**. Levels increased by erythromycin, ciprofloxacin, cimetidine, liver disease; decreased by smoking, rifampicin, phenytoin.

### Other drugs in asthma

| Group | Drugs | Points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS)** | Beclomethasone, budesonide, fluticasone, ciclesonide | **Most effective controllers**; reduce inflammation; not for relief of an attack; **oral thrush** and hoarse voice – rinse mouth after use, use spacer |
| Systemic corticosteroids | Prednisolone (oral), hydrocortisone, methylprednisolone (IV) | Acute severe asthma and exacerbations (short course) |
| **Leukotriene antagonists** | **Montelukast**, zafirlukast | Block CysLT1 receptors; aspirin-induced and exercise-induced asthma, with allergic rhinitis; neuropsychiatric effects (mood changes, sleep problems) |
| **Mast cell stabilisers** | Sodium cromoglycate, ketotifen | **Prophylaxis only** – useless in an attack |
| Anti-IgE antibody | Omalizumab | Severe allergic asthma |

### Treatment of acute severe asthma (status asthmaticus)

1. Oxygen.
2. **Nebulised salbutamol** (repeated) **+ ipratropium**.
3. **Systemic corticosteroid** – IV hydrocortisone or oral prednisolone.
4. If not improving: IV magnesium sulphate; aminophylline only with care; adrenaline in anaphylaxis-related cases.
5. Fluids, treat infection; ventilator support if needed.
6. **Avoid sedatives** and β-blockers.

## 7.3 Cough and its management

**Cough** is a protective reflex that clears the airway of mucus and foreign matter. It is controlled by the cough centre in the medulla.

| Type | Description | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dry (unproductive) cough** | Irritating, no sputum | **Antitussives** (suppress cough) |
| **Productive (wet) cough** | With sputum | **Expectorants / mucolytics** (help removal); do not suppress |

Always look for the cause (infection, asthma, reflux, ACE inhibitor, smoking, TB) – cough lasting more than 2 weeks may need a TB test.

## 7.4 Antitussive agents

**Definition:** drugs that **suppress the cough reflex**, used for dry, irritating cough.

### Classification

```text
 1. Opioids: codeine, pholcodine, (ethylmorphine)
 2. Non-opioids: dextromethorphan, noscapine, (chlophedianol)
 3. Antihistamines (sedating): chlorpheniramine, diphenhydramine, promethazine
 4. Peripherally acting (demulcents / soothing agents): syrups, lozenges,
    honey, liquorice, glycerine
 5. Local anaesthetic: benzonatate (not common in India)
```

| Drug | Mechanism and points |
| --- | --- |
| **Codeine** | Opioid; suppresses the cough centre; also analgesic; **constipation**, drowsiness, respiratory depression in high doses, dependence; not for children under 12 years and not in asthma |
| Pholcodine | Opioid; little analgesia and dependence |
| **Dextromethorphan** | Non-opioid (no analgesia, little dependence at normal doses); acts centrally (NMDA-related); dizziness, nausea; abuse in high doses; serotonin syndrome with MAOIs and SSRIs |
| Noscapine | Opium alkaloid without opioid effects; safe; may cause headache, nausea |
| Antihistamines | Sedation and drying of secretions; useful in cough from postnasal drip or allergy |
| Demulcents | Form a soothing coat over the irritated throat (pharyngeal cough) |

**Contraindications of antitussives:** **productive cough** (sputum retention), asthma and COPD (opioids depress breathing), young children (codeine); avoid alcohol and driving with sedating products.

## 7.5 Expectorants

**Definition:** drugs that **increase the volume of bronchial secretion and make it thinner**, so sputum is coughed out more easily.

```text
 1. Sodium and potassium salts: potassium iodide, sodium citrate, potassium citrate,
    ammonium chloride  (act reflexly by gastric irritation, or directly on glands)
 2. Guaiphenesin (guaifenesin), vasaka (Adhatoda vasica), ipecacuanha (low dose)
 3. Steam inhalation with volatile oils (eucalyptus, menthol) – simple and effective
```

**Uses:** productive cough, chronic bronchitis; often present in cough syrups. Evidence for many is weak; good **hydration and steam** help.
**Adverse effects:** nausea and vomiting (gastric irritation); **potassium iodide** – iodism (metallic taste, running nose, rash), thyroid effects; avoid in pregnancy and thyroid disease.

## 7.6 Mucolytic agents

**Definition:** drugs that **break down mucus** and reduce its stickiness (viscosity), so it can be cleared more easily.

| Drug | Mechanism | Uses and points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bromhexine** | Derived from vasicine (Adhatoda); breaks mucopolysaccharide fibres, ↑ thin secretion | Chronic bronchitis, productive cough; GI upset |
| **Ambroxol** | Active metabolite of bromhexine; also ↑ surfactant | Productive cough, bronchitis |
| **Acetylcysteine (N-acetylcysteine)** | Free –SH group breaks **disulphide bonds** in mucus | Nebulised in thick secretions (cystic fibrosis, COPD); **antidote in paracetamol poisoning** (IV/oral); may cause bronchospasm when inhaled |
| Carbocisteine | Changes mucus structure | COPD |
| Dornase alfa | DNA-breaking enzyme (DNase) – thins pus-laden sputum | **Cystic fibrosis** |
| Hypertonic saline | Draws water into mucus | Cystic fibrosis |

**Caution:** mucolytics and expectorants may worsen bronchospasm in asthma; use with care in peptic ulcer (bromhexine, ambroxol).

## 7.7 Summary table: drugs used in respiratory disorders

| Problem | First choice |
| --- | --- |
| Acute asthma attack | Inhaled salbutamol (SABA) |
| Long-term asthma control | Inhaled corticosteroid (± LABA) |
| COPD maintenance | Long-acting inhaled bronchodilator (tiotropium, LABA) |
| Exercise / aspirin-induced asthma | Montelukast; SABA before exercise |
| Dry irritating cough | Dextromethorphan, codeine (adults), demulcents |
| Productive cough | Fluids, steam, mucolytic (ambroxol, bromhexine) or expectorant |
| Thick sputum in cystic fibrosis | Dornase alfa, hypertonic saline, acetylcysteine |

### Inhaler counselling (pharmacist's role)

1. Shake the metered-dose inhaler; breathe out fully.
2. Put the mouthpiece in the mouth, start breathing in slowly and press the canister once.
3. Continue the slow deep breath, then **hold the breath for about 10 seconds**.
4. Wait about a minute before a second puff.
5. Use a **spacer** for children and the elderly (better lung delivery, less throat deposit).
6. **Rinse the mouth** after a steroid inhaler.
7. Carry the reliever inhaler at all times; report if it is needed more often than usual (poor control).

## Remember

- Salbutamol is the reliever (SABA); salmeterol/formoterol are LABAs – never alone in asthma, always with an inhaled steroid.
- β2 agonists cause tremor, tachycardia and low potassium.
- Ipratropium/tiotropium (anticholinergics) are especially useful in COPD.
- Theophylline inhibits PDE and blocks adenosine receptors; narrow therapeutic index; toxicity → arrhythmias and fits.
- Inhaled corticosteroids are the best controllers; rinse mouth to prevent thrush.
- Montelukast blocks leukotriene receptors; cromoglycate is for prevention only.
- Antitussives (codeine, dextromethorphan, noscapine) are for dry cough only.
- Expectorants increase and thin secretions; mucolytics (bromhexine, ambroxol, acetylcysteine) break mucus; acetylcysteine is also the paracetamol antidote.

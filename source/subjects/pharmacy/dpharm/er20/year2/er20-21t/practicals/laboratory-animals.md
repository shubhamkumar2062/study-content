# Study of laboratory animals: mice, rats, guinea pigs and rabbits

**Aim:** To study the common laboratory animals, their features, handling, housing and uses in experimental pharmacology.

> This is a theory-and-record exercise. Live animals are not used under ER-2020; use pictures, charts and the simulation software.

## Principle

The choice of animal depends on the response being studied, size, cost, ease of handling, breeding time and how closely the animal's response resembles humans. Animals must be healthy, of known strain, age, sex and weight, and kept under standard conditions (about 22 ± 3 °C, 12-hour light/dark cycle, clean cages, food and water freely available).

## Requirements

Charts or software images of animals, practical record book.

## Procedure

1. Study the scientific name, strain, features and uses of each animal (table below).
2. Learn the correct method of holding each animal (from charts or video).
3. Note the common routes used in each and the uses in screening.
4. Record the information in the practical book.

## Observations

| Animal | Common strains | Key features | Main uses in pharmacology |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Mouse** (*Mus musculus*) | Swiss albino, BALB/c | Small (about 20–30 g), cheap, breeds fast, needs little drug | Screening of CNS drugs (actophotometer, rota-rod), analgesics (writhing, hot plate), anticonvulsants, acute toxicity (LD50), chemotherapy |
| **Rat** (*Rattus norvegicus*) | Wistar, Sprague–Dawley | Medium size (about 150–250 g), no vomiting reflex, no gall bladder | Anti-inflammatory (paw oedema), anticonvulsant (MES), antiulcer, antidiabetic, toxicity studies, isolated organs (uterus, ileum) |
| **Guinea pig** (*Cavia porcellus*) | Dunkin–Hartley | Very sensitive to histamine; needs dietary vitamin C | Antihistamine and anti-asthma screening, isolated ileum (histamine, ACh), anaphylaxis, local anaesthetics (wheal test) |
| **Rabbit** (*Oryctolagus cuniculus*) | New Zealand white | Large ear veins, large eyes, stable body temperature | **Pyrogen test**, eye studies (mydriatics, miotics, local anaesthetics), skin and eye irritation tests, antibody production |

### Handling (summary)

- **Mouse:** lift by the base of the tail; hold the loose skin behind the neck between thumb and finger for injection.
- **Rat:** hold round the shoulders/chest with the thumb under the jaw; or by the scruff.
- **Guinea pig:** support the hind quarters with one hand and hold the chest with the other.
- **Rabbit:** never lift by the ears; hold the scruff and support the hind quarters; use a rabbit holder (restrainer) for ear-vein work.

## Result

The features, handling and pharmacological uses of mice, rats, guinea pigs and rabbits were studied.

## Precautions

1. Handle animals gently to avoid stress (stress changes results).
2. Use animals of the same sex, age and weight in a group.
3. Wear gloves and lab coat; wash hands after handling.

## Viva questions

1. **Which animal is used for the pyrogen test?** Rabbit.
2. **Why is the guinea pig used for antihistamine studies?** It is very sensitive to histamine.
3. **Which common laboratory animal cannot vomit?** The rat.
4. **Which strain of rat is common in Indian labs?** Wistar albino rats.
5. **Why should a rabbit not be lifted by the ears?** It causes pain and injury; support the scruff and hind quarters.

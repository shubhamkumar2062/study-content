# Miotic effect on the rabbit eye

**Aim:** To study the effect of miotic drugs on pupil size and light reflex of the rabbit eye using simulation software.

## Principle

**Miotics** constrict the pupil by contracting the sphincter pupillae (M3 receptors). Direct cholinergic agonists (pilocarpine, carbachol) act on the receptor; anticholinesterases (physostigmine) prevent breakdown of acetylcholine. A miotic can also reverse the mydriasis produced by a sympathomimetic, while the mydriasis of atropine (a receptor blocker) is resisted by pilocarpine because the receptors are blocked.

## Requirements

Simulation software; in the simulated set-up: rabbit in a holder, pilocarpine nitrate 1% (or physostigmine) eye drops, normal saline, measuring scale (mm), torch, stopwatch.

## Procedure

1. Measure the pupil diameter of both eyes and test the light reflex.
2. Instil saline (control) into one eye and the miotic into the other.
3. Record pupil diameter and light reflex at fixed intervals (e.g. 5, 10, 20, 30 minutes).
4. Optional: instil a miotic into an eye previously dilated with phenylephrine and observe reversal.

1. Keep lighting constant during measurements.
2. Use the other eye as control.
3. Do not let the dropper touch the eye.

## Viva questions

1. **What is a miotic?** A drug that constricts the pupil.
2. **Mechanism of pilocarpine?** Direct muscarinic (M3) agonist on the sphincter pupillae.
3. **Clinical use of miotics?** Glaucoma (especially acute angle-closure), reversal of mydriasis.
4. **How does physostigmine produce miosis?** By inhibiting cholinesterase, so ACh accumulates.
5. **Which systemic drug causes pin-point pupils?** Morphine (opioids).

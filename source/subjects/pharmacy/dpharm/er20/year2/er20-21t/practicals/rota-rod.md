# Screening of muscle relaxants using the rota-rod apparatus

**Aim:** To study the muscle relaxant (motor coordination-impairing) effect of a drug such as diazepam in mice using the rota-rod apparatus, by simulation.

## Principle

The rota-rod is a horizontal rod (with a rough surface) that rotates at a constant speed (commonly 20–25 rpm). A normal mouse can keep its balance on it for a long time. A **centrally acting muscle relaxant** or sedative (diazepam, baclofen) reduces muscle tone and coordination, so the mouse **falls off sooner**. The **fall-off time** is a measure of muscle relaxant activity.

## Requirements

Simulation software; in the simulated set-up: mice, rota-rod apparatus, diazepam (standard), test drug, saline, stopwatch.

## Procedure

1. Train the mice on the rotating rod; select those that stay on for a set time (e.g. 3–5 minutes) in a trial.
2. Record the basal fall-off time of each mouse.
3. Give saline, diazepam or test drug by the route shown.
4. Place each mouse on the rod at fixed intervals (e.g. 30, 60, 90 minutes) and record the fall-off time.
5. Calculate the percentage decrease in fall-off time.

## Precautions

1. Use only mice that pass the training trial.
2. Keep the rod speed constant.
3. Place mice gently in the same way each time.
4. Avoid noise and distraction in the room.

## Viva questions

1. **What does the rota-rod test measure?** Motor coordination and muscle relaxant activity.
2. **What is the standard drug?** Diazepam.
3. **How do benzodiazepines relax muscles?** By enhancing GABA-A-mediated inhibition in the spinal cord and brain.
4. **Name two centrally acting muscle relaxants.** Baclofen and tizanidine.
5. **Why are mice trained before the test?** To select animals that can normally stay on the rod, so results are reliable.

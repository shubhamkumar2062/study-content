# Screening of CNS stimulants and depressants using an actophotometer

**Aim:** To study the effect of a CNS stimulant (caffeine or amphetamine) and a CNS depressant (diazepam or chlorpromazine) on the locomotor activity of mice using an actophotometer, by simulation.

## Principle

**Locomotor activity** is an index of alertness. In an actophotometer, beams of light fall on **photocells**; each time the animal moves across a beam, the beam is cut and a count is recorded on a digital counter. **CNS stimulants** increase the count; **CNS depressants** (sedatives, anxiolytics, antipsychotics) decrease it.

## Requirements

Simulation software; in the simulated set-up: mice, actophotometer, caffeine (stimulant), diazepam or chlorpromazine (depressant), saline, syringes, stopwatch.

## Procedure

1. Switch on the actophotometer and check that the counter works.
2. Place each mouse in the arena for a fixed time (e.g. 10 minutes) and record the **basal activity score**.
3. Give saline, the stimulant or the depressant by the route shown.
4. After the absorption time (e.g. 30 minutes), place the mouse again for the same period and record the activity score.
5. Calculate the percentage change in activity.

## Observations

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

```text
 % change in activity = (Count after drug − Basal count) ÷ Basal count × 100
   (positive = stimulation; negative = depression)
```

## Result

Caffeine increased and diazepam decreased the locomotor activity, showing CNS stimulant and depressant effects respectively.

## Precautions

1. Test in a quiet room with constant light.
2. Observe each mouse for exactly the same time.
3. Clean the arena between animals (odours affect behaviour).

## Viva questions

1. **What does an actophotometer measure?** Locomotor activity using photocells.
2. **Give two CNS stimulants.** Caffeine and amphetamine.
3. **Give two CNS depressants.** Diazepam and chlorpromazine.
4. **Mechanism of caffeine?** Adenosine receptor blockade (and phosphodiesterase inhibition).
5. **Why should the arena be cleaned between animals?** The smell left by one animal changes the behaviour of the next.

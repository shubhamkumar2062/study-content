# Types of pre-clinical experiments: in vivo, in vitro, ex vivo

**Aim:** To study the different types of pre-clinical experiments used in drug research and their merits and limitations.

## Principle

Before a new drug is tested in humans (clinical trials), its activity and safety are studied in **pre-clinical** experiments. These are classified by where the experiment is done.

## Requirements

Charts, simulation software, practical record book.

## Procedure

1. Study the definition and examples of each type (table).
2. Note the place of pre-clinical studies in new drug development.
3. Record in the practical book.

## Observations

| Type | Meaning | Examples | Merits | Limitations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **In vivo** ("in the living") | Experiment on a whole living animal | Analgesic test on mice, anticonvulsant test in rats, pyrogen test in rabbits, toxicity studies | Shows the effect in a whole body with absorption, metabolism and excretion | Uses animals, costly, variable, ethical issues |
| **In vitro** ("in glass") | Experiment outside a living organism on cells, enzymes, receptors or microbes | Enzyme inhibition assays, cell culture, receptor binding, MIC testing of antibiotics | Fast, cheap, no animal use, good for mechanism | Does not show whole-body effects |
| **Ex vivo** ("out of the living") | Tissue taken from an organism is studied outside the body, often after the animal was given the drug | Isolated organ studies (ileum, heart, aorta), tissue taken from treated animals | Closer to real tissue than cells; controlled conditions | Tissue survives only for hours; nerve and hormonal links lost |
| **In silico** | Computer modelling and simulation | Molecular docking, Ex-Pharm simulations | No animals, very cheap | Only as good as the model |
| In situ | Organ studied in its normal place in an anaesthetised animal | Blood pressure recording in an anaesthetised animal | Natural blood supply | Needs anaesthesia and skill |

### Place in new drug development

```text
 Drug discovery → Pre-clinical studies (in silico, in vitro, ex vivo, in vivo:
   pharmacology, pharmacokinetics, acute/sub-acute/chronic toxicity,
   mutagenicity, carcinogenicity, teratogenicity)
 → Clinical trial phases I, II, III → Approval (in India, CDSCO / DCGI
   under the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019) → Phase IV (post-marketing)
```

## Result

In vivo, in vitro, ex vivo, in situ and in silico experiments and their roles in pre-clinical drug development were studied.

## Precautions

1. Always include control groups.
2. Results from one model should be confirmed in another before conclusions are drawn.

## Viva questions

1. **What is an in vitro study?** An experiment done outside a living organism, e.g. on cells or enzymes.
2. **Give an example of an ex vivo study.** Isolated frog heart or guinea pig ileum in an organ bath.
3. **What is in silico?** Computer-based modelling or simulation.
4. **Why are pre-clinical studies needed?** To show activity and safety before giving a drug to humans.
5. **Who approves clinical trials in India?** The Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (DCGI).

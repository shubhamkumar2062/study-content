# Commonly used instruments in experimental pharmacology

**Aim:** To study the instruments used in experimental pharmacology, their principle and uses.

> Study the instruments from the laboratory, charts or the simulation software. No animals are used.

## Principle

Each instrument measures a particular drug response in a standard, repeatable way: movement, muscle coordination, pain reaction, convulsions, or the contraction of an isolated tissue.

## Requirements

Instruments or their images, simulation software, practical record book.

## Procedure

1. Identify each instrument and draw a labelled sketch.
2. Write its principle, main parts and use.
3. In the software, note how each instrument records a response.

## Observations

| Instrument | Principle / parts | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Actophotometer** | Square or round arena with light beams falling on photocells; each beam cut by the animal is counted | Locomotor activity – CNS stimulants (↑ count) and depressants (↓ count) |
| **Rota-rod** | Rod rotating at a set speed; animal must keep its balance; fall-off time recorded | Muscle relaxant and motor coordination (diazepam reduces time on rod) |
| **Analgesiometer** – Eddy's hot plate | Plate kept at a constant temperature; time to paw licking or jumping | Centrally acting analgesics (morphine) |
| **Analgesiometer** – tail-flick (radiant heat) | Heated wire under the tail; time to flick the tail | Centrally acting analgesics |
| **Electroconvulsiometer** | Delivers a measured electric current (mA) for a short time through ear or corneal electrodes | Maximal electroshock seizures – screening of antiepileptics |
| **Elevated plus maze** | Plus-shaped platform raised above the floor: two open and two closed arms | Anxiolytic drugs (↑ time in open arms) |
| **Organ bath** with kymograph or transducer | Tissue in a warmed, aerated physiological salt solution; contractions recorded | Isolated ileum, uterus, heart – dose–response curves, bioassay |
| **Student's organ bath / Dale's bath** | Inner bath for tissue, outer water jacket with thermostat | Isolated tissue experiments |
| **Sherrington rotating drum (kymograph)** | Smoked paper on a rotating drum; lever writes the response | Recording contractions (older method) |
| **Plethysmometer** | Measures paw volume by fluid displacement | Anti-inflammatory activity (paw oedema) |
| **Inclined plane** | Board tilted at a set angle; animal must stay on it | Muscle relaxants, sedatives |
| **Cook's pole-climbing apparatus** | Animal learns to climb a pole to avoid a shock after a sound signal (conditioned avoidance) | Antipsychotics |

## Result

The principle and uses of common instruments in experimental pharmacology were studied.

## Precautions

1. Calibrate instruments (temperature, current, rpm) before use.
2. Keep the testing room quiet and at constant light and temperature.
3. Electroconvulsiometer: handle electrodes carefully.

## Viva questions

1. **What does an actophotometer measure?** Locomotor activity, using photocells.
2. **Which instrument screens muscle relaxants?** Rota-rod.
3. **Name two analgesiometers.** Eddy's hot plate and tail-flick (radiant heat) apparatus.
4. **What is an organ bath used for?** To keep an isolated tissue alive in warm, aerated salt solution and record its responses to drugs.
5. **What does a plethysmometer measure?** Volume of the rat paw (for anti-inflammatory studies).

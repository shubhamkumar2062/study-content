# Mydriatic effect on the rabbit eye

**Aim:** To study the effect of mydriatic drugs on pupil size and light reflex in the rabbit eye using simulation software.

## Principle

Pupil size depends on two muscles of the iris: the **sphincter pupillae** (parasympathetic, M3 – miosis) and the **dilator pupillae** (sympathetic, α1 – mydriasis).

- **Anticholinergics** (atropine, homatropine, tropicamide) block M3 receptors → mydriasis, **loss of light reflex** and cycloplegia.
- **Sympathomimetics** (phenylephrine, adrenaline, ephedrine) stimulate α1 receptors of the dilator → mydriasis with the **light reflex kept**.

The pupil diameter is measured before and at intervals after instilling the drug, and the light reflex is tested.

## Requirements

Simulation software; in the simulated set-up: rabbit in a holder, atropine sulphate 1% eye drops, phenylephrine eye drops, normal saline, measuring scale (mm), torch, stopwatch.

## Procedure

1. Measure the pupil diameter of both eyes in diffuse light and test the light reflex with a torch.
2. Instil saline into one eye (control) and the mydriatic into the other.
3. Measure pupil diameter and test the light reflex at fixed intervals (e.g. 5, 10, 20, 30 minutes).
4. Repeat with a sympathomimetic mydriatic and compare.

## Result

Atropine produced mydriasis with loss of light reflex; phenylephrine produced mydriasis with the light reflex present; saline had no effect.

## Precautions

1. Measure the pupil under the same lighting each time.
2. Instil the same number of drops each time and keep the other eye as control.
3. Avoid touching the cornea with the dropper.

## Viva questions

1. **What is a mydriatic?** A drug that dilates the pupil.
2. **Why is the light reflex lost with atropine but not with phenylephrine?** Atropine paralyses the sphincter pupillae; phenylephrine only contracts the dilator, so the sphincter still responds to light.
3. **Which mydriatic is shortest acting?** Tropicamide.
4. **When are mydriatics contraindicated?** Angle-closure glaucoma.
5. **What is cycloplegia?** Paralysis of accommodation.

# Screening of anticonvulsants using an electroconvulsiometer

**Aim:** To study the anticonvulsant effect of a drug (for example phenytoin) against **maximal electroshock (MES)** seizures in rats, by simulation.

## Principle

A strong electric current passed briefly through the brain (by ear or corneal electrodes) produces a **maximal seizure** with typical phases. The **tonic hind-limb extension (THLE)** phase is abolished or shortened by drugs effective in **generalised tonic–clonic (grand mal) epilepsy**, such as phenytoin, carbamazepine, phenobarbitone and valproate. Commonly used settings are about **150 mA for 0.2 s in rats** (lower current, about 50 mA, in mice).

```text
 Phases of MES seizure:
 1. Tonic flexion → 2. Tonic extension (hind limbs stretched backwards) →
 3. Clonic convulsions → 4. Stupor → 5. Recovery (or death)
```

## Requirements

Simulation software; in the simulated set-up: rats, electroconvulsiometer with ear (or corneal) electrodes, phenytoin (standard), test drug, saline, stopwatch.

## Procedure

1. Divide rats into control, standard and test groups.
2. Apply the current to the control animal and note the duration of each phase, especially **tonic hind-limb extension**.
3. Give phenytoin (or test drug) by the route shown; wait for the time of peak effect.
4. Apply the same current again and record the duration of each phase.
5. Note whether THLE is abolished or reduced and whether the animal recovers.

```text
 % protection = (Number of animals without THLE ÷ Total animals in group) × 100
 or % reduction in THLE = (Control THLE − Treated THLE) ÷ Control THLE × 100
```

## Result

Phenytoin abolished/reduced the tonic hind-limb extension phase, showing anticonvulsant activity against generalised tonic–clonic seizures.

## Precautions

1. Use the same current strength and duration in all animals.
2. Moisten electrodes with saline for good contact.
3. Test at the time of peak drug effect.
4. Handle the electrodes carefully.

## Viva questions

1. **Which phase is used to judge anticonvulsant activity in MES?** Tonic hind-limb extension.
2. **The MES test is a model of which type of epilepsy?** Generalised tonic–clonic (grand mal).
3. **Which test is a model of absence seizures?** Pentylenetetrazole (PTZ)-induced seizures; ethosuximide protects.
4. **Name a standard drug for MES.** Phenytoin.
5. **Mechanism of phenytoin?** Prolongs the inactivation of voltage-gated sodium channels.

# Effect of drugs on the isolated frog heart

**Aim:** To study the effect of drugs (any two, for example adrenaline and acetylcholine, and the effect of atropine on acetylcholine) on the rate and force of contraction of the isolated frog heart, by simulation.

## Principle

A frog heart, perfused through a **Symes cannula** with oxygenated **frog Ringer solution**, keeps beating outside the body for hours. Its contractions are recorded on a kymograph or transducer. Drugs added to the perfusion fluid change the:

- **rate** (chronotropic effect) and
- **force/amplitude** (inotropic effect) of contraction.

| Drug | Receptor | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adrenaline | β1 | ↑ rate and ↑ force (positive chronotropic and inotropic) |
| Acetylcholine | M2 | ↓ rate and ↓ force; large dose may stop the heart in diastole |
| Atropine | Blocks M2 | Prevents the effect of acetylcholine |
| Propranolol | Blocks β1 | Prevents the effect of adrenaline |
| Digoxin | Na+/K+ ATPase | ↑ force; toxic doses – arrhythmia, systolic arrest |
| Calcium chloride (excess) | — | ↑ force |
| Potassium chloride (excess) | — | ↓ force and rate, diastolic arrest |

## Requirements

Simulation software; in the simulated set-up: pithed frog, dissection set, Symes cannula, frog Ringer solution, heart lever, kymograph or recorder, drugs (adrenaline, acetylcholine, atropine), syringes.

## Procedure

1. In the simulation, the frog is pithed, the heart exposed and the Symes cannula tied into the sinus venosus/ventricle for perfusion with frog Ringer.
2. Attach the apex of the heart to the lever and record a **normal tracing** (baseline).
3. Add a dose of adrenaline into the perfusion; record the response; wash with Ringer until the tracing returns to normal.
4. Add acetylcholine and record; wash.
5. Add atropine, then acetylcholine again, and record (block of ACh effect).
6. Count heart rate (beats/min) and measure amplitude (mm) for each response.

## Observations

| Drug added | Heart rate (beats/min) | Amplitude (mm) | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Normal (Ringer) | | | Baseline |
| Adrenaline | | | |
| Acetylcholine | | | |
| Atropine + acetylcholine | | | |

```text
 % change in amplitude = (Amplitude after drug − Baseline amplitude) ÷ Baseline amplitude × 100
```

## Result

Adrenaline increased the rate and force of contraction; acetylcholine decreased both; atropine blocked the effect of acetylcholine.

## Precautions

1. Keep the heart moist and perfused with Ringer at a constant flow.
2. Avoid air bubbles in the cannula.
3. Wash the heart after each drug and wait for the baseline to return.
4. Add small doses first.

## Viva questions

1. **Name the cannula used.** Symes cannula.
2. **Composition of the perfusion fluid?** Frog Ringer solution (sodium chloride, potassium chloride, calcium chloride, sodium bicarbonate, glucose in water).
3. **Why does acetylcholine slow the heart?** It acts on M2 receptors and opens K+ channels (hyperpolarisation).
4. **What blocks adrenaline's effect on the heart?** β-blockers such as propranolol.
5. **What is a positive inotropic effect?** An increase in the force of contraction.

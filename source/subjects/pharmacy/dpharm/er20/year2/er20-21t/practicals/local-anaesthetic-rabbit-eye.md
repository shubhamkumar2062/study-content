# Effect of local anaesthetics on the rabbit eye

**Aim:** To study the surface (topical) local anaesthetic effect of drugs on the rabbit cornea using simulation software (Ex-Pharm or similar).

## Principle

Touching the cornea normally causes an immediate blink – the **corneal reflex**. A **local anaesthetic** applied to the cornea blocks sodium channels in the sensory nerve endings, so touching the cornea no longer produces a blink. The **onset**, **intensity** (number of touches without a blink) and **duration** of anaesthesia are compared for different drugs. Surface anaesthetics such as lidocaine (lignocaine) and tetracaine produce this effect; procaine penetrates mucous membranes poorly and is a weak surface anaesthetic.

## Requirements

Simulation software; in the simulated set-up: rabbit in a holder, test drugs (lidocaine 2–4% solution, tetracaine/amethocaine drops, procaine solution, normal saline as control), dropper, a fine hair or bristle (for touching the cornea), stopwatch.

## Procedure

1. Select the rabbit and check the corneal reflex in both eyes by touching the cornea gently with the bristle (the animal blinks).
2. Instil the control (saline) into one eye and the test local anaesthetic into the other, holding the lower lid like a cup; close the lids for a minute.
3. Test the corneal reflex every few minutes (e.g. every 5 minutes): touch the cornea a fixed number of times (e.g. 10 touches) and count the blinks.
4. Record the time of onset (no blink), the time of complete anaesthesia and the time when the reflex returns.
5. Repeat with other local anaesthetics and compare.

## Precautions

1. Touch the cornea gently and from the side, not the front, so the animal does not see the bristle.
2. Do not touch the eyelashes or lid margins.
3. Use the same number of touches each time.
4. Keep one eye as control.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the corneal reflex?** Blinking when the cornea is touched.
2. **How do local anaesthetics act?** By blocking voltage-gated sodium channels in nerves.
3. **Why is procaine a poor surface anaesthetic?** It does not penetrate mucous membranes well.
4. **Name two surface anaesthetics used in the eye.** Lidocaine and tetracaine (proparacaine is also used).
5. **Why should a patient avoid rubbing the eye after topical anaesthesia?** The cornea is insensitive and may be injured.

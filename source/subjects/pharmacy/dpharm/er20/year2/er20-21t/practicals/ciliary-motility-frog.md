# Effect of drugs on ciliary motility of the frog buccal cavity

**Aim:** To study the effect of drugs on the ciliary movement of the frog's buccal (palate and oesophageal) mucosa, by simulation.

## Principle

The mucosa of the frog's mouth, palate and oesophagus is lined with **ciliated epithelium**. The cilia beat in one direction and carry particles towards the stomach. The **speed of ciliary movement** is measured by timing how long a small particle (such as a poppy seed or a bit of cork or charcoal) takes to travel a fixed distance.

- **Cholinergic drugs** (acetylcholine, pilocarpine, physostigmine) **increase** ciliary movement → particle moves faster (shorter time).
- **Anticholinergics** (atropine) and **local anaesthetics** (lidocaine) **decrease** it → longer time.

This relates to the clearance of mucus in human airways.

## Requirements

Simulation software; in the simulated set-up: pithed frog, dissection board, pins, frog Ringer solution, poppy seeds, scale, stopwatch, drugs (acetylcholine or pilocarpine, atropine, lidocaine).

## Procedure

1. In the simulation, the pithed frog is pinned on its back; the lower jaw is cut and the buccal cavity and oesophagus are exposed and kept moist with Ringer.
2. Mark two points a fixed distance apart (e.g. 1 cm) on the palate.
3. Place a poppy seed at the upper mark and record the time taken to reach the lower mark. Repeat to get a steady **control time**.
4. Apply a few drops of the drug (e.g. acetylcholine) and after a minute repeat the timing.
5. Wash with Ringer, wait for recovery, then test the next drug (atropine, lidocaine).

```text
 Speed of ciliary movement = Distance ÷ Time
 % change = (Speed after drug − Control speed) ÷ Control speed × 100
```

## Result

Acetylcholine increased the speed of ciliary movement, while atropine and lidocaine decreased it.

## Precautions

1. Keep the mucosa moist with Ringer throughout.
2. Use particles of the same size and place them at the same mark.
3. Wash between drugs and wait for control values to return.

## Viva questions

1. **Which epithelium lines the frog buccal cavity?** Ciliated epithelium.
2. **Effect of acetylcholine on ciliary movement?** Increases it.
3. **Effect of atropine?** Decreases it (anticholinergic).
4. **Why is this experiment relevant to humans?** Airway cilia clear mucus; anticholinergics and some drugs can slow this clearance.
5. **What particle is used?** A poppy seed (or small cork or charcoal particle).

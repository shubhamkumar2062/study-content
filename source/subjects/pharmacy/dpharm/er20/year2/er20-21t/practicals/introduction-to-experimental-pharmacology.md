# Introduction to experimental pharmacology

**Aim:** To understand the purpose, scope and rules of experimental pharmacology, and how the ER-2020 practicals are done using simulation software.

> Under ER-2020, animals are **not** used for any of the listed experiments. All experiments are carried out or demonstrated with software such as **Ex-Pharm** or other suitable simulation programs.

## Principle

**Experimental pharmacology** studies the effects of drugs on living systems (whole animals, isolated organs, tissues or cells) under controlled conditions, before and alongside studies in humans. It helps to:

1. Find new drugs and screen compounds for a wanted action (screening).
2. Work out the **mechanism of action** and the **dose–response** relationship.
3. Compare the potency and efficacy of drugs (bioassay).
4. Detect toxicity before a drug is given to humans (pre-clinical safety).
5. Teach students how drugs act.

### Ethics and the 3 Rs

In India animal experiments are regulated by the **Committee for Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals (CCSEA**, earlier CPCSEA) under the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960. Every institution using animals needs an **Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC)**. The guiding principle is the **3 Rs**:

| R | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Replacement** | Use non-animal methods wherever possible | Computer simulation, cell culture, isolated tissues from slaughterhouses |
| **Reduction** | Use the fewest animals that give a valid result | Good experimental design and statistics |
| **Refinement** | Reduce pain and distress | Anaesthesia, humane end points, good housing |

ER-2020 practicals apply **Replacement** fully by using software.

## Requirements

Computer with simulation software (Ex-Pharm or similar), practical record book, graph paper or spreadsheet.

## Procedure

1. Open the software and read the introduction screen for the experiment.
2. Note the animal or tissue being simulated, the apparatus and the drugs available.
3. Record the **control (baseline) reading** before giving any drug.
4. Select a drug and dose, "administer" it by the route shown and record the response at the stated times.
5. Repeat with a standard drug and with the test drug; compare with control.
6. Tabulate results, draw graphs (dose–response curves) and write conclusions.

## Result

The aims, scope, ethical regulation and simulated methods of experimental pharmacology were studied.

## Precautions

1. Always record a control reading first.
2. Use the dose units shown in the software (mg/kg) and note the route.
3. Do not change more than one variable at a time.

## Viva questions

1. **What is experimental pharmacology?** The study of drug effects on animals, isolated tissues or cells under controlled conditions.
2. **What are the 3 Rs?** Replacement, Reduction and Refinement of animal use.
3. **Which body regulates animal experiments in India?** CCSEA (formerly CPCSEA); each institution has an IAEC.
4. **Why does ER-2020 use software?** To avoid using animals (replacement) while still teaching drug effects.
5. **What is a bioassay?** Measuring the potency of a drug by comparing its biological effect with that of a standard.

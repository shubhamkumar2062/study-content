# Routes of drug administration in animals

**Aim:** To study the different routes of drug administration in laboratory animals and the volumes and needles normally used.

> Learn the techniques from charts, videos and the simulation software. Animals are not used under ER-2020.

## Principle

The route decides the speed and extent of absorption. In experiments the dose is given as **mg per kg body weight**, so the volume injected depends on the weight of the animal. Small rodents cannot take large volumes, and some routes (IV in mice) need skill.

## Requirements

Charts or software, syringes (1 ml tuberculin and 2–5 ml), needles of different gauges, oral feeding needle (gavage cannula), animal holders, practical record book.

## Procedure

1. Study each route, the site and the restraint used (table below).
2. Calculate the dose volume for a given animal weight (calculation section).
3. Record in the practical book.

## Observations

| Route | Abbreviation | Site / method | Animals |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Oral** (per oral) | p.o. | Oral feeding needle (blunt, ball-tipped) passed into the stomach | Mice, rats |
| **Intraperitoneal** | i.p. | Lower abdomen, to one side of the midline, needle at an angle (avoids bladder and gut) | Mice, rats – most common in screening |
| **Subcutaneous** | s.c. | Loose skin at the back of the neck or flank | All |
| **Intramuscular** | i.m. | Thigh muscles | Rats, rabbits |
| **Intravenous** | i.v. | **Tail vein** (mice, rats – warm the tail first to dilate the vein); **marginal ear vein** (rabbits) | All |
| Intradermal | i.d. | Into the skin layers (raises a bleb) | Guinea pigs, rabbits |
| Intracerebroventricular | i.c.v. | Into brain ventricles (specialised) | Rats, mice |
| Topical / instillation | — | Eye drops into the conjunctival sac | Rabbits |
| Inhalation | — | Volatile anaesthetics in a chamber | All |

## Calculation

```text
 Dose to inject (mg) = Dose (mg/kg) × Body weight (kg)
 Volume to inject (ml) = Dose to inject (mg) ÷ Concentration of solution (mg/ml)

 Example: diazepam 4 mg/kg for a 25 g mouse, solution 0.4 mg/ml
   Dose = 4 × 0.025 = 0.1 mg
   Volume = 0.1 ÷ 0.4 = 0.25 ml
```

Keep the volumes small for small animals (for example about 10 ml/kg orally or i.p. in mice is commonly used in screening).

## Result

## Precautions

1. Use the smallest needle that does the job; use a fresh sterile needle for each animal.
2. Remove air bubbles before IV injection.
3. Pull back slightly before i.p. injection to make sure the needle is not in the gut or a vessel.
4. Restrain properly to avoid injury to the animal and the handler.

## Viva questions

1. **Which vein is used for IV injection in rabbits?** The marginal ear vein.
2. **Which vein is used in mice and rats?** The lateral tail vein.
3. **Why is the i.p. route popular in screening?** It is easy, quick and gives fairly fast absorption.
4. **What is a gavage (feeding) needle?** A blunt, ball-tipped needle for giving drugs directly into the stomach.
5. **How is dose expressed in animal experiments?** In mg per kg body weight.

# Pyrogen testing by the rabbit method

**Aim:** To study the official rabbit pyrogen test for parenteral preparations, by simulation.

## Principle

**Pyrogens** are fever-producing substances, mainly **bacterial endotoxins** (lipopolysaccharides from the cell walls of gram-negative bacteria). They may be present in water for injection or injections even after sterilisation, because heat-stable endotoxins are not destroyed by autoclaving. When injected, they cause **fever, chills** and even shock. The pharmacopoeial **rabbit test** measures the **rise in body temperature** of rabbits after IV injection of the test solution. Rabbits are used because their temperature response to pyrogens resembles that of humans. The **bacterial endotoxin test (LAL test)**, using *Limulus* amoebocyte lysate, is an in vitro alternative that is now widely used.

## Requirements

Simulation software; in the simulated set-up: healthy adult rabbits of similar weight (kept in standard conditions and on the same diet), rabbit holders, clinical thermometer or electronic rectal probes, pyrogen-free (depyrogenated) syringes and needles (glassware heated, e.g. at 250 °C for at least 30 minutes), test solution warmed to about 37 °C.

## Procedure

1. Use healthy rabbits that have not been used for a pyrogen test recently and that have been trained to the holders; withhold food overnight before the test (water allowed).
2. Insert the thermometer or probe into the rectum to the fixed depth given in the pharmacopoeia; record temperatures at intervals before injection to get the **control (initial) temperature** of each rabbit.
3. Inject the test solution (usually 10 ml per kg body weight, or the dose in the monograph) slowly into the **marginal ear vein** within about 30 minutes of the control reading.
4. Record the temperature at regular intervals (e.g. every 30 minutes) for 3 hours after injection.
5. The **response** of each rabbit = highest temperature after injection − its control temperature (a fall is taken as zero).
6. Judge the result against the limits in the current pharmacopoeia.

**Interpretation (commonly taught pharmacopoeial scheme; check the current IP for exact limits):** the test starts with 3 rabbits. If no rabbit shows a rise of 0.5 °C or more, the sample passes. If any rabbit shows such a rise, the test is continued with 5 more rabbits, and the sample passes only if the number of rabbits with a rise and the total of the rises stay within the pharmacopoeial limits.

## Result

The sample was found to be **pyrogen-free / not pyrogen-free** according to the pharmacopoeial limits.

## Precautions

1. All glassware, syringes and needles must be pyrogen-free.
2. Rabbits must be calm and restrained gently; excitement raises temperature.
3. Keep the room temperature constant and avoid noise.
4. Insert the probe to the same depth each time.
5. Warm the injection to about body temperature and inject slowly.

## Viva questions

1. **What are pyrogens?** Fever-producing substances, mainly bacterial endotoxins (lipopolysaccharides of gram-negative bacteria).
2. **Why are rabbits used?** Their fever response to pyrogens is similar to that of humans.
3. **Which vein is used for injection?** The marginal ear vein.
4. **How is glassware made pyrogen-free?** Dry heat at a high temperature (e.g. 250 °C for at least 30 minutes).
5. **What is the in vitro alternative?** The bacterial endotoxin test (LAL test).
6. **Does autoclaving destroy pyrogens?** No, endotoxins are heat-stable; they are removed by dry heat depyrogenation, distillation or ultrafiltration.

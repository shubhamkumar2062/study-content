# Techniques of blood collection from animals

**Aim:** To study the methods used to collect blood from laboratory animals for pharmacological and toxicological studies.

> Learn from charts, videos and software. No animals are used under ER-2020.

## Principle

Blood samples are needed to measure drug levels (pharmacokinetics), blood sugar, cholesterol, liver and kidney function, and blood counts. The method depends on the species, the volume needed, how often samples are taken and whether the animal is to survive. The total volume taken must be limited to protect the animal's health, as laid down in ethics guidelines.

## Requirements

Charts or software, capillary tubes, syringes and fine needles, collection tubes with anticoagulant (EDTA, heparin, citrate) or plain tubes for serum, local anaesthetic, warming lamp, cotton, practical record book.

## Procedure

1. Study each method, its site, the volume obtained and the animals in which it is used (table).
2. Note the anticoagulant needed for each test.
3. Record in the practical book.

## Observations

| Method | Site and technique | Animals | Remarks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Retro-orbital (orbital sinus) puncture** | Fine capillary tube inserted at the inner corner of the eye into the venous sinus, under anaesthesia | Mice, rats | Small to moderate volume; skill needed; repeated sampling possible but now discouraged |
| **Tail vein / tail tip** | Tail warmed to dilate vein; needle prick or small snip of tail tip | Mice, rats | Small volumes, e.g. blood glucose |
| **Saphenous vein** | Hind leg vein pricked after shaving | Mice, rats, guinea pigs | Repeated small samples, little stress |
| **Marginal ear vein / central ear artery** | Vein dilated by warming or xylene rubbing; needle or small cut | Rabbits | Common method in rabbits |
| **Cardiac puncture** | Needle through the chest wall into the heart under deep anaesthesia | Mice, rats, guinea pigs, rabbits | Large volume; **terminal** procedure only |
| **Jugular vein** | Under anaesthesia, sometimes by a catheter | Rats, rabbits | Repeated sampling with cannula |
| Decapitation / terminal bleeding | At end of study under ethics approval | Rodents | Terminal |

### Choice of tube

| Test | Tube |
| --- | --- |
| Blood counts | EDTA |
| Plasma drug levels | Heparin or EDTA |
| Clotting tests | Sodium citrate |
| Serum chemistry (liver, kidney tests) | Plain tube (allow to clot) |

## Result

The common methods of blood collection from laboratory animals were studied.

## Precautions

1. Use anaesthesia where required; terminal methods only under approval.
2. Do not exceed the permitted blood volume.
3. Use sterile equipment and apply pressure to stop bleeding.
4. Label tubes with animal number, time and group.

## Viva questions

1. **Which method gives the largest volume of blood?** Cardiac puncture (terminal).
2. **Which method is used in rabbits?** Marginal ear vein (or central ear artery).
3. **What is retro-orbital puncture?** Collection from the orbital venous sinus behind the eye with a capillary tube.
4. **Which anticoagulant is used for blood counts?** EDTA.
5. **Why is the tail warmed before sampling?** To dilate the tail vein.

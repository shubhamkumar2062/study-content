# Unit 13: Bioethics and ICMR guidelines, Consumer Protection Act and Disaster Management Act

This unit joins syllabus chapter 18 (bioethics and the ICMR National Ethical Guidelines), chapter 19 (introduction to the Consumer Protection Act) and chapter 20 (introduction to the Disaster Management Act). Each is a short topic, usually asked as a short note.

## Part A: Bioethics

## 13.1 Basic concepts

**Bioethics** is the study of ethical issues arising from **medicine, health care and biological research** – for example, research on human beings, consent, confidentiality, end-of-life care, organ donation and new technologies.

Research on humans is needed to find better treatments, but participants must never be used merely as a means. Bioethics tries to balance **scientific progress** with the **rights, safety and dignity** of individuals.

## 13.2 History

| Year | Event | Lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1947 | **Nuremberg Code** – after the trial of doctors who did cruel experiments on prisoners during the Second World War | **Voluntary consent** is absolutely essential; research must avoid unnecessary suffering |
| 1964 | **Declaration of Helsinki** by the World Medical Association (revised several times since) | Ethical principles for medical research; independent ethics committee review; the participant's welfare comes before science |
| 1932–1972 | **Tuskegee study** (USA) – treatment was withheld from men with syphilis to study the disease | Exposed abuse of poor and vulnerable people; led to stronger rules |
| 1979 | **Belmont Report** (USA) | Three principles: **respect for persons, beneficence, justice** |
| 1996 | **ICH Good Clinical Practice** guideline | International standard for clinical trials |
| 1980 onwards | **ICMR** in India: policy statement (1980), ethical guidelines (2000, 2006) and the **National Ethical Guidelines, 2017** | Indian framework for research on humans |

## 13.3 Principles of bioethics

The four widely used principles:

| Principle | Meaning | Application in research |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Autonomy (respect for persons)** | Respect each person's right to decide for himself | Informed, voluntary consent; right to withdraw; extra protection for those who cannot decide |
| **Beneficence** | Do good; maximise benefits | Research must have value; benefits should outweigh risks |
| **Non-maleficence** | Do no harm | Minimise risks; stop the study if harm appears |
| **Justice** | Fair distribution of benefits and burdens | Fair selection of participants; vulnerable groups not exploited; benefits shared |

Related values: **confidentiality and privacy**, **truthfulness**, and **accountability**.

## 13.4 ICMR National Ethical Guidelines for Biomedical and Health Research involving Human Participants, 2017 (brief overview)

The Indian Council of Medical Research issued these guidelines in **2017**. They apply to **all biomedical, social and behavioural health research** on human participants, their biological material or their data in India.

### General principles

1. **Essentiality** – research is undertaken only when necessary, after considering alternatives.
2. **Voluntariness** – participation is voluntary, with informed consent and the right to withdraw.
3. **Non-exploitation** – participants are not exploited; they are compensated fairly for injury.
4. **Social responsibility** – research should not harm social harmony or relationships.
5. **Privacy and confidentiality** – participants' identity and records are protected.
6. **Risk minimisation** – risks reduced to the minimum.
7. **Professional competence** – research done by qualified, trained people.
8. **Maximisation of benefit** – benefits to participants and society are maximised.
9. **Institutional arrangements** – institutions have proper systems (ethics committees, records).
10. **Transparency and accountability** – research and results are open; conflicts of interest declared.
11. **Totality of responsibility** – everyone involved shares responsibility for participants' welfare.
12. **Environmental protection** – research should not harm the environment.

### Main areas covered

- **Ethics committees** – composition (medical and non-medical members, a legal expert, a social scientist, a lay person, both genders), review procedures, monitoring.
- **Informed consent** – written, in a language the participant understands; special procedures for children (assent plus parental consent), the illiterate (impartial witness) and those who cannot consent.
- **Vulnerable groups** – children, pregnant women, prisoners, the mentally ill, economically and socially disadvantaged people need extra protection.
- **Compensation** for research-related injury.
- Specific chapters on **clinical trials**, public health research, social and behavioural research, **genetic and genomic research**, **biological materials and biobanking**, and research during disasters.
- **Post-research access** to beneficial interventions where possible.

## Part B: Consumer Protection Act (introduction)

## 13.5 Overview

The **Consumer Protection Act, 2019** replaced the Consumer Protection Act, 1986 and came into force in **2020**. It gives quick, inexpensive remedies to consumers and covers new areas such as **e-commerce**, **product liability** and **misleading advertisements**.

### Who is a consumer

A person who **buys goods or hires services for consideration** (payment, promised payment or partly paid), including online purchases, **for personal use**. A person who buys for **resale or commercial purposes** is not a consumer. A patient who pays for medicines or health services is a consumer.

### Rights of consumers

1. **Right to safety** – protection from hazardous goods and services.
2. **Right to be informed** – about quality, quantity, potency, purity, standard and price.
3. **Right to choose** – access to a variety of goods at competitive prices.
4. **Right to be heard** – consumer interests considered at proper forums.
5. **Right to seek redressal** – against unfair trade practices and exploitation.
6. **Right to consumer awareness (education)**.

### Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Defect | Fault or shortcoming in quality, quantity, potency, purity or standard of goods |
| Deficiency | Fault or inadequacy in the quality or manner of a service |
| Unfair trade practice | Deceptive or unfair methods to promote sale (false claims, selling spurious goods, refusing to issue a bill) |
| Misleading advertisement | Advertisement that falsely describes a product or gives a false guarantee |
| Product liability | Responsibility of the manufacturer, service provider or seller to compensate for harm caused by a defective product |

### Authorities

| Body | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Consumer Protection Councils (Central, State, District) | Advisory; promote consumer rights |
| **Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA)** | Investigates violations of consumer rights, orders **recall** of unsafe goods, acts against **misleading advertisements** (including against endorsers), imposes penalties |
| **Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions** – District, State and National | Decide complaints; jurisdiction by the value of goods or services (limits set by rules and revised from time to time) |
| Mediation cells | Settle disputes by mediation |

- Complaints can be filed **electronically** and **where the consumer lives**.
- Commissions can order replacement, refund, compensation and removal of defects; penalties apply for selling **adulterated or spurious goods** and for misleading advertisements.

### Relevance to pharmacy

- Selling **expired, spurious or substandard** medicines, **overcharging** above MRP, or **not issuing a bill** can lead to consumer complaints.
- Wrong dispensing that harms a patient may be treated as **deficiency in service**.
- Always give a proper **bill** with batch and expiry details and follow the MRP.

## Part C: Disaster Management Act (introduction)

## 13.6 Overview

The **Disaster Management Act, 2005** provides for effective management of disasters and connected matters. It was invoked across India during the **COVID-19 pandemic** (2020) to issue nationwide orders.

### Definition

A **disaster** is a catastrophe, mishap, calamity or grave occurrence in any area, from **natural or man-made causes** (or by accident or negligence), causing substantial loss of life, human suffering, damage to property or the environment, which is **beyond the coping capacity** of the affected community. Examples: earthquakes, floods, cyclones, landslides, epidemics, industrial and chemical accidents.

### Authorities

| Level | Authority | Chairperson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| National | **National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA)** – lays down policies, plans and guidelines | **Prime Minister** |
| National | National Executive Committee – implements the national plan | Union Home Secretary |
| State | **State Disaster Management Authority (SDMA)** | **Chief Minister** |
| District | **District Disaster Management Authority (DDMA)** – plans and coordinates at district level | **District Collector / District Magistrate** |
| Response | **National Disaster Response Force (NDRF)** – specialised response | – |
| Training | **National Institute of Disaster Management (NIDM)** – training and research | – |

### Main provisions

- **Disaster management plans** at national, state and district levels, and by each ministry and department.
- Phases: **prevention, mitigation, preparedness, response, relief, rehabilitation and reconstruction**.
- Powers to **requisition** resources, issue directions and orders that everyone must follow.
- **National and State Disaster Response Funds** and Mitigation Funds.
- **Offences**: obstructing officials, making **false claims** for relief, **misappropriating relief material**, giving **false warnings** causing panic, and refusing to obey orders are punishable.

### Role of pharmacists in disasters

1. Keep **emergency stocks** of essential medicines, ORS, antiseptics, vaccines, and maintain the **cold chain**.
2. Manage **donated medicines** – sort, check expiry and labels, store properly, reject unsuitable donations.
3. **Rational distribution** and counselling in relief camps; prevent hoarding and overpricing.
4. Support **disease prevention** (water purification, immunisation, outbreak control).
5. Help in **documentation** and supply logistics; follow official orders (for example, on sale of masks, sanitisers and drugs during an epidemic).

## Remember

- Bioethics landmarks: **Nuremberg Code 1947**, **Declaration of Helsinki 1964**, **Belmont Report 1979** (respect for persons, beneficence, justice).
- Four principles: **autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice**.
- **ICMR National Ethical Guidelines, 2017**: 12 general principles (essentiality, voluntariness, non-exploitation, privacy, risk minimisation…), ethics committees, informed consent, vulnerable groups.
- **Consumer Protection Act, 2019**: six consumer rights; **CCPA**; three-tier **Commissions**; product liability; misleading ads.
- **Disaster Management Act, 2005**: **NDMA (PM)**, **SDMA (CM)**, **DDMA (Collector)**, **NDRF**, **NIDM**; used during COVID-19.

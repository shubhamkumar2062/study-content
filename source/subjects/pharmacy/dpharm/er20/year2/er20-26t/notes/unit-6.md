# Unit 6: Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954 and Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960

This unit joins syllabus chapters 5 and 6. The first law stops false and harmful advertising of medicines; the second protects animals, including animals used in experiments in pharmacy colleges and industry.

## Part A: Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954

## 6.1 Objectives

1. To **control the advertisement of drugs** in certain cases.
2. To **prohibit advertisements of remedies alleged to have magic qualities**.
3. To protect the public, especially the poor and less educated, from being misled into **self-medication** for serious diseases or for socially sensitive conditions.
4. To provide for matters connected with these.

The **Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Rules, 1955** give the procedures.

## 6.2 Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Advertisement | Any notice, circular, label, wrapper or other document, and any announcement made orally or by producing or transmitting light, sound or smoke (this covers print, radio, TV, signboards and similar media) |
| Drug | Medicines for human or animal use; substances for diagnosis, treatment or prevention of disease; articles (other than food) to affect the body's structure or function; articles meant as components of these |
| Magic remedy | Any **talisman, mantra, kavacha, charm or any other thing** claimed to have **miraculous powers** to cure, diagnose, prevent or mitigate any disease, or to affect the structure or function of the body |
| Registered medical practitioner | A person registered under the relevant medical law (including Indian systems of medicine) |
| Taking part in publication | Includes printing, publishing or distributing an advertisement |

## 6.3 Prohibited advertisements

### Advertisements of drugs for certain purposes

No person shall take part in publishing any advertisement referring to a drug in terms suggesting or likely to lead to its use for:

1. **Procuring miscarriage** in women or **preventing conception**;
2. **Maintaining or improving sexual capacity**;
3. **Correcting menstrual disorders** in women; or
4. **Diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment or prevention** of any disease, disorder or condition listed in the **Schedule** to the Act (or any other the Government adds by rules).

The Schedule lists serious or chronic conditions for which self-treatment is dangerous, for example **cancer, diabetes, epilepsy, cataract, glaucoma, blindness, deafness, leprosy, obesity** and many others.

### Misleading advertisements

No person shall take part in publishing an advertisement that:

- directly or indirectly gives a **false impression** about the true character of the drug;
- makes a **false claim** for the drug; or
- is otherwise **false or misleading** in any material particular.

### Magic remedies

No person shall take part in publishing an advertisement referring to any **magic remedy** that directly or indirectly claims to be effective for any of the purposes above (for example, an amulet "guaranteed to cure diabetes").

### Import and export

Import into or export from India of any document containing such a prohibited advertisement is also prohibited, and such documents may be seized under customs law.

## 6.4 Classes of exempted advertisements

The prohibitions do **not** apply to:

1. A **signboard or notice** displayed by a registered medical practitioner on his premises, saying that treatment for a disease or condition is available there.
2. A **treatise or book** dealing with these matters from a **bona fide scientific or social standpoint**.
3. An advertisement about a drug sent **confidentially** in the prescribed manner **only to registered medical practitioners**.
4. An advertisement about a drug printed or published by the **Government**.
5. An advertisement about a drug printed or published by any person **with the previous sanction of the Government**.

Advertisements to medical practitioners must be sent in a sealed cover marked "for the use only of registered medical practitioners" (or to a hospital or laboratory), as the rules describe.

## 6.5 Enforcement, offences and penalties

- A **gazetted officer** authorised by the State Government may enter and search any place, examine records, and **seize** any document or article believed to break the Act.
- Contravening the Act is punishable with **imprisonment, fine or both** on first conviction, and with **longer imprisonment, fine or both** on a subsequent conviction. The amounts have been under review; **check the current Act**.
- Where a **company** commits an offence, every person in charge of its business at that time is also deemed guilty, unless he proves it happened without his knowledge or that he tried to prevent it.
- Pharmacists must not display or distribute such advertisements in the shop; doing so is also an offence and professional misconduct.

## Part B: Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960

## 6.6 Objectives

1. To **prevent the infliction of unnecessary pain or suffering** on animals.
2. To establish the **Animal Welfare Board of India** to advise on animal welfare.
3. To **control and supervise experiments on animals** (Chapter IV of the Act), so that experiments are done only when necessary and with minimum suffering.

The **Breeding of and Experiments on Animals (Control and Supervision) Rules, 1998** (amended later) give the detailed procedure for institutions using animals.

## 6.7 Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Animal | Any living creature **other than a human being** |
| Captive animal | An animal (not domestic) that is captured or kept in captivity |
| Domestic animal | An animal tamed or being tamed to serve some human purpose |
| Board | The Animal Welfare Board of India |
| Owner | Includes any person in possession or custody of the animal |
| Establishment | A place where experiments on animals are performed, or animals are bred for experiments |

## 6.8 CPCSEA – brief overview

The **Committee for the Purpose of Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals (CPCSEA)** is constituted by the Central Government under the Act.

**Functions:**

1. To ensure that animals are **not subjected to unnecessary pain or suffering** before, during or after experiments.
2. To **register establishments** that perform experiments or breed animals for experiments.
3. To lay down **standards** for animal houses, care, feeding, veterinary care and experiments.
4. To approve experiments on **large animals** and certain experiments referred to it.
5. To **inspect** establishments and their records.
6. To **suspend or revoke registration** where rules are broken.

### The 3Rs (and a fourth R)

| Principle | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Replacement** | Use non-animal methods wherever possible | Cell culture, computer models, tissue preparations |
| **Reduction** | Use the minimum number of animals that gives valid results | Good statistical design |
| **Refinement** | Reduce pain and distress | Anaesthesia, analgesia, humane end-points |
| Rehabilitation | Care for animals after experiments (stressed by CPCSEA) | Aftercare of large animals |

## 6.9 Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC)

Every registered establishment must have an **IAEC** to review and approve its animal experiments.

### Composition (as laid down by CPCSEA)

| Member | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Chairperson and Member Secretary | Usually senior scientists of the institution |
| Biological scientist | Expertise in the area of research |
| Scientists from different biological disciplines | Two scientists |
| Veterinarian | Animal health and welfare |
| Scientist in charge of the animal house | Facilities and care |
| Scientist from outside the institution | Independent opinion |
| Non-scientific, socially aware member | Public view |
| **CPCSEA nominee(s)** | Main nominee (and link nominee) appointed by CPCSEA |

### Functions of the IAEC

1. Review and approve **protocols** before experiments start (justification, species, numbers, procedures, pain relief).
2. Make sure the **3Rs** are applied.
3. Inspect the **animal house** and procedures periodically.
4. Forward proposals on large animals to CPCSEA.
5. Keep minutes and send reports to CPCSEA.

## 6.10 Breeding, stocking, experiments, transfer and records

### Breeding and stocking of animals

- Animals must be **bred** only by **registered breeders** or in registered establishments.
- Animal houses must meet standards for space, temperature, humidity, light, ventilation, cleanliness, food and water, and veterinary care.
- Different species are housed separately; sick animals are isolated.

### Performance of experiments

1. Only for the **advancement of knowledge** that will be useful for saving or prolonging life, alleviating suffering or fighting disease (human, animal or plant).
2. Done by or under the supervision of **qualified persons**.
3. Done under **anaesthesia** where pain is likely, unless the experiment's purpose would be defeated.
4. **Smaller animals** (for example, mice, rats, guinea pigs) used where they will serve; large animals only when essential and with CPCSEA approval.
5. Experiments for **merely acquiring manual skill** are to be avoided (replaced by models and videos where possible).
6. Animals in severe pain that cannot be relieved should be **humanely killed (euthanasia)**.

### Transfer and acquisition of animals

- Animals for experiments must be acquired **only from registered breeders** or registered establishments.
- Transfer between establishments must be **recorded** with the details the rules require (species, number, source, purpose).

### Records

Each establishment must keep records of animals acquired, bred, used in experiments, transferred and disposed of, and of IAEC approvals, in the forms laid down, and make them available for inspection.

### Power to suspend or revoke registration

If CPCSEA is satisfied, after giving the establishment an opportunity to be heard, that the rules or its directions have been broken, it may **suspend or revoke the registration** of the establishment, and may **prohibit** it from performing experiments.

### Offences and penalties

Contravening an order of the CPCSEA or the conditions it imposes is punishable with a **fine**; cruelty to animals generally is punishable under other sections with fine and, for repeat offences, imprisonment. The fine amounts in the 1960 Act are small and changes have been proposed; **check the current Act**.

## Remember

- DMR Act **1954**: no ads for **miscarriage/contraception, sexual capacity, menstrual disorders, or diseases in the Schedule**; no **misleading** ads; no **magic remedies** (talisman, mantra, kavacha, charm).
- **Exempted**: RMP signboard, scientific books, confidential ads to RMPs, Government ads, ads with Government sanction.
- PCA Act **1960**: CPCSEA controls experiments; every establishment has an **IAEC** with a **CPCSEA nominee**.
- **3Rs** = Replacement, Reduction, Refinement.
- Animals only from **registered breeders**; records kept; CPCSEA can **suspend or revoke** registration.

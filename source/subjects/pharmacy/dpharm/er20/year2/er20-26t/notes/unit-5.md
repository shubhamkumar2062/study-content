# Unit 5: Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985 and Poisons Act, 1919

This unit joins two short syllabus chapters (chapter 4, NDPS Act, and chapter 7, Poisons Act). Both laws control substances that are dangerous when misused. The NDPS Act is asked far more often.

## Part A: NDPS Act, 1985 and Rules

## 5.1 Background

Before 1985, narcotics were controlled by the **Opium Act, 1857**, the **Opium Act, 1878** and the **Dangerous Drugs Act, 1930**. These laws had light punishments and did not cover psychotropic substances. India had also signed three United Nations conventions:

| Convention | Subject |
| --- | --- |
| Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs, 1961 | Opium, coca, cannabis and their products |
| Convention on Psychotropic Substances, 1971 | Synthetic drugs that act on the mind (sedatives, stimulants, hallucinogens) |
| Convention against Illicit Traffic in Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances, 1988 | Drug trafficking, money from it, and precursor chemicals |

The **NDPS Act, 1985** replaced the old Acts and put India's obligations under these conventions into law. It has been amended several times (including in 1989, 2001 and 2014). The **NDPS Rules, 1985** (made by the Central Government) and State NDPS rules give the procedures.

## 5.2 Objectives

1. To **consolidate and amend** the law on narcotic drugs.
2. To make **stringent provisions** for the control and regulation of operations relating to narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances.
3. To provide for the **forfeiture of property** derived from illicit traffic.
4. To control **controlled substances** (precursor chemicals) used to make narcotics.
5. To carry out India's obligations under the **international conventions**.
6. To allow **medical and scientific use** while preventing misuse, and to provide for treatment of addicts.

## 5.3 Important definitions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Addict | A person who has become dependent on any narcotic drug or psychotropic substance |
| Cannabis (hemp) | **Charas** (separated resin of the cannabis plant), **ganja** (flowering or fruiting tops, excluding seeds and leaves when not with the tops), and any mixture or drink made from them |
| Coca leaf | Leaf of the coca plant, except a leaf from which all alkaloids have been removed |
| Coca derivative | Crude cocaine, ecgonine and its derivatives, cocaine and its salts, and preparations containing more than the specified amount of cocaine |
| Opium | The coagulated juice of the opium poppy, and any mixture of it (with exceptions for preparations with very low morphine content) |
| Opium poppy | The plant *Papaver somniferum*, and any other plant species from which opium or its alkaloids can be extracted, as notified |
| Poppy straw | All parts of the opium poppy after harvesting (except seeds), whether in original form or cut or powdered |
| Manufactured drug | All coca derivatives, medicinal cannabis, opium derivatives and poppy straw concentrate, and any other narcotic substance the Government declares |
| Narcotic drug | Coca leaf, cannabis, opium, poppy straw and all manufactured drugs |
| Psychotropic substance | Any substance, natural or synthetic, or any salt or preparation of it, listed in the **Schedule to the Act** (for example, many barbiturates, benzodiazepines and amphetamines) |
| Controlled substance | A substance the Government declares controlled because it is used to make narcotic drugs or psychotropic substances (precursors such as acetic anhydride, ephedrine and pseudoephedrine) |
| Illicit traffic | Cultivating, producing, possessing, selling, buying, transporting, importing, exporting or financing any of these against the Act |
| Small quantity / commercial quantity | Quantities fixed by Government notification for each drug; punishment depends on them |
| Essential narcotic drugs | Narcotic drugs the Central Government notifies for medical and scientific use, regulated uniformly across India (introduced by the 2014 amendment to improve access to pain relief, for example morphine) |

## 5.4 Authorities and officers

| Level | Authority | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Central | **Narcotics Commissioner** | Supervises cultivation of opium poppy and production of opium; issues licences for it |
| Central | **Narcotics Control Bureau (NCB)** | Coordinates enforcement against illicit traffic; works with States and international agencies |
| Central | **Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Consultative Committee** | Advises the Central Government on administration of the Act |
| Central | National Fund for Control of Drug Abuse | Money for fighting illicit traffic, treatment and rehabilitation of addicts |
| State | State Government and its officers (excise, police, drugs control) | Regulate possession, transport, sale and use within the State |
| Officers | Officers of central and state departments empowered by notification | Search, seizure and arrest under the Act |

## 5.5 Prohibition, control and regulation

### Prohibition

No person shall:

- **cultivate** any coca plant, or gather any part of it;
- **cultivate** the opium poppy or any cannabis plant; or
- **produce, manufacture, possess, sell, purchase, transport, warehouse, use, consume, import, export** or transship any narcotic drug or psychotropic substance,

**except for medical or scientific purposes** and in the manner and to the extent the Act, rules, licences and permits allow.

### Control by the Central Government

The Central Government regulates (through rules and licences):

1. Cultivation of the opium poppy and production of opium (in notified areas, by licensed cultivators).
2. **Manufacture of manufactured drugs** (for example, morphine, codeine) and psychotropic substances.
3. Import, export and transshipment of narcotics and psychotropics (import and export certificates).
4. Essential narcotic drugs (possession, transport, sale and use, uniformly in all States).
5. Controlled substances (precursors): manufacture, possession, sale, import and export.

### Control by State Governments

States regulate:

1. Possession, transport, purchase, sale and consumption of **poppy straw**, opium (where allowed), and **manufactured drugs** other than essential narcotic drugs.
2. Cultivation of cannabis for purposes other than ganja and charas, where allowed.
3. Licences to pharmacies, hospitals and practitioners to stock and dispense such drugs.

### What this means for a pharmacist

- A pharmacy needs the appropriate **licence or permit** to stock narcotic drugs (for example, codeine or morphine preparations).
- Sell only on a **valid prescription**; keep the drug locked; keep **records of receipt and issue** with the patient's and prescriber's details; report stocks as required; produce records to officers.
- Psychotropics such as benzodiazepines are also controlled under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act (Schedule H, H1 or X).

## 5.6 Offences and penalties

Punishment for most offences depends on the **quantity** involved:

| Quantity involved | Punishment (since the 2001 amendment) |
| --- | --- |
| **Small quantity** | Rigorous imprisonment up to **1 year**, or fine up to **₹10,000**, or both |
| More than small but less than commercial | Rigorous imprisonment up to **10 years** and fine up to **₹1 lakh** |
| **Commercial quantity** | Rigorous imprisonment of **not less than 10 years, up to 20 years**, and fine of **not less than ₹1 lakh, up to ₹2 lakh** (the court may impose more) |

Other important provisions:

- **Consumption** of a narcotic drug or psychotropic substance is itself an offence, with a lighter punishment, heavier for drugs like cocaine, morphine and heroin.
- **Repeat offenders** convicted again for large quantities face enhanced punishment; for certain very large quantities the court may impose the death penalty (made discretionary by the 2014 amendment).
- **Financing illicit traffic and harbouring offenders** are punished like the main offence.
- Offences relating to **controlled substances** (precursors) are punishable.
- **Property** derived from illicit traffic can be frozen and forfeited.
- Most offences are **cognizable and non-bailable**; special courts try them.
- **Addicts** who volunteer for treatment at a recognised centre can get immunity from prosecution for consumption and small-quantity possession, if conditions are met.
- Licensed persons who **contravene licence conditions** (for example, a pharmacy failing to keep records) are also punishable.

Since amounts and quantities are changed by notification and amendment, **check the current Act and notifications**.

## Part B: Poisons Act, 1919

## 5.7 Introduction and objective

The **Poisons Act, 1919** is a short, old Central Act. Its objective is to **regulate the import, possession and sale of poisons**. Most of the detail is in **rules made by each State Government**, so the actual list of poisons and conditions differs between States.

## 5.8 Definition

- **Poison** – any substance that the **State Government declares** to be a poison by notification for the purposes of the Act (for example, arsenic compounds, strychnine, cyanides, some pesticides, as listed in State rules).
- The Act does not apply to medicines dispensed in the normal way under other laws that already control them, as the rules provide.

## 5.9 Main provisions

### Possession of any poison

The State Government may make rules to **regulate the possession** of any specified poison in any local area, fixing the maximum quantity a person may possess without a licence.

### Possession for sale and sale of any poison

- Possession for sale and sale of any poison, by wholesale or retail, may be allowed **only under a licence** granted under State rules.
- State rules usually require the seller to:
  1. sell only to adults known to him or properly identified;
  2. enter every sale in a **poison register**: date, name and address of buyer, name and quantity of poison, purpose of purchase, and the buyer's signature;
  3. keep poisons in a separate locked cupboard, in containers labelled **"POISON"** with the name of the substance;
  4. not sell to children or to persons who appear intoxicated or unsound of mind.

### Import of poisons

The **Central Government** may regulate or prohibit the **import of any poison** into India by sea, air or land, and may allow import only under a licence.

### Search and seizure

A magistrate may issue a warrant to search a place where a poison is believed to be kept against the Act, and seize the poison.

### Penalties

Breaking the Act or rules is punishable with **fine or imprisonment for a short term**, with heavier punishment for a second offence. Poisons possessed against the Act may be confiscated. Exact amounts are in the 1919 Act and State rules; **check the current rules of your State**.

## 5.10 NDPS Act and Poisons Act compared

| Point | NDPS Act, 1985 | Poisons Act, 1919 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Substances | Narcotic drugs, psychotropics, precursors | Substances notified as poisons |
| Main concern | Addiction and trafficking | Accidental or deliberate poisoning |
| Detail made by | Mostly Central Act and rules, plus State rules | Mostly State rules |
| Punishment | Very strict, quantity-based | Light |
| International basis | Three UN conventions | None |

## Remember

- NDPS Act **1985** replaced the **Opium Acts (1857, 1878)** and the **Dangerous Drugs Act (1930)**; based on UN conventions of **1961, 1971 and 1988**.
- **Narcotic drug** = coca leaf, cannabis, opium, poppy straw and manufactured drugs; **psychotropic** = substances in the Schedule to the Act.
- **Charas** = resin; **ganja** = flowering tops.
- Authorities: **Narcotics Commissioner**, **NCB**, Consultative Committee, State Governments.
- Punishment depends on **small vs commercial quantity**; commercial quantity: **10–20 years** RI.
- **Essential narcotic drugs** (2014 amendment) improve access to pain relief.
- Poisons Act **1919**: import (Centre), possession and sale (State rules, licence, poison register).

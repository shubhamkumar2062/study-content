# Unit 9: FSSAI and food supplements, Medical Termination of Pregnancy Act, and Clinical Establishments Act

This unit joins three short syllabus chapters (chapter 8, FSSAI; chapter 11, MTP Act; chapter 16, Clinical Establishments Act). Each is a 2-hour topic, usually asked as a short answer or a short note.

## Part A: Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 and food supplements

## 9.1 Overview of the Act

The **Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 (FSS Act)** brought many older food laws under one Act, for example the **Prevention of Food Adulteration Act, 1954**, the Fruit Products Order and the Milk and Milk Products Order. It moved from many departments and multiple standards to **one authority and one set of standards**.

### Objectives

1. To consolidate the laws relating to food.
2. To establish the **Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI)**.
3. To lay down **science-based standards** for articles of food.
4. To regulate their **manufacture, storage, distribution, sale and import**.
5. To ensure availability of **safe and wholesome food** for human consumption.

### FSSAI

- Set up under the Act in **2008**, under the **Ministry of Health and Family Welfare**, with its head office in New Delhi.
- It frames regulations and standards, licenses and registers food businesses (through the State and Central licensing authorities), accredits laboratories, runs surveillance, and creates awareness.
- **Commissioners of Food Safety** in the States and **Food Safety Officers** in districts enforce the Act (inspection, sampling, prosecution).

### Licensing of food businesses

| Type | Who |
| --- | --- |
| **Registration** | Small (petty) food business operators below the turnover/capacity limit set by the regulations |
| **State licence** | Medium-sized businesses |
| **Central licence** | Large manufacturers, importers, businesses operating in many States, and specified categories |

Applications are made online on FSSAI's portal. A pharmacy that sells food supplements, infant foods or nutritional products needs **FSSAI registration or licence** in addition to its **drug licence**.

## 9.2 Food supplements (health supplements and nutraceuticals)

Food supplements are regulated by the **Food Safety and Standards (Health Supplements, Nutraceuticals, Food for Special Dietary Use, Food for Special Medical Purpose, Functional Food and Novel Food) Regulations, 2016**, which have since been revised (a revised version was notified in 2022). Check the current version before applying any detail.

### What they are

| Category | Meaning (simplified) | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Health supplement | Concentrated source of nutrients (vitamins, minerals, amino acids, etc.) to supplement the normal diet | Multivitamin tablets |
| Nutraceutical | Food or food ingredient with a physiological benefit, such as plant extracts | Omega-3 capsules, plant extract capsules |
| Food for special dietary use | For people with special needs due to a physical or physiological condition | Low-protein foods |
| Food for special medical purpose | Used under medical supervision for patients with specific diseases | Nutrition formula for kidney patients |
| Probiotic / prebiotic food | Contain beneficial microbes / substances that support them | Probiotic drinks |

### Key rules

1. They are **food, not medicine** – they must **not claim to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent a disease**.
2. They must not contain **drugs** listed under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act (for example, Schedule H drugs) or hormones/steroids.
3. Ingredients must be from the **permitted lists**, and vitamins and minerals within the **limits** set (related to recommended daily allowances).
4. They are taken **orally** in forms such as tablets, capsules, powders, syrups and drinks; injectable forms are not food.

### Manufacture

- Manufacturer needs an **FSSAI licence** for the category and must follow **Good Manufacturing Practices and Good Hygienic Practices**.
- Raw materials and products are tested; records of batches are kept.
- Product approval is needed for ingredients or products not covered by the standards.

### Storage

- Store as stated on the label (cool, dry place; away from sunlight; refrigerated if needed).
- Follow **first-expiry-first-out**; remove products past "use by" / "best before" date.
- Keep them separate from medicines in a pharmacy so they are not confused with drugs.

### Sale

- Sold only by registered or licensed food business operators.
- Must not be sold or promoted **as medicines**; no false health claims at the counter.
- Pharmacist should advise customers that a supplement does not replace prescribed treatment.

### Labelling (main points)

```text
Name of the food and its CATEGORY (e.g. "HEALTH SUPPLEMENT" / "NUTRACEUTICAL")
List of ingredients; amount of nutrients per serving and % of daily allowance
"NOT FOR MEDICINAL USE"
Recommended usage and "Not to exceed the recommended daily usage"
Warnings and precautions; "Keep out of reach of children" where required
Statement that it is not a substitute for a varied diet
Allergen information; veg (green) or non-veg (brown) symbol
FSSAI logo and licence number; name and address of manufacturer/importer
Batch/lot number, date of manufacture, best before / use by, net quantity
```

## Part B: Medical Termination of Pregnancy Act and Rules

## 9.3 Basic understanding

Before this Act, abortion was a crime under the Indian Penal Code except to save the woman's life. Unsafe abortions by untrained persons caused many deaths. The **Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP) Act, 1971** allowed **safe, legal termination** by qualified doctors in approved places, on specified grounds. The **MTP Rules** and **Regulations** (revised in 2003 and later) give the procedures. The Act was significantly amended by the **MTP (Amendment) Act, 2021**.

## 9.4 Salient features (as amended in 2021)

### Who may terminate

Only a **registered medical practitioner (RMP)** who has the **experience or training in gynaecology and obstetrics** laid down by the rules.

### When (gestation limits)

| Gestation | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Up to **20 weeks** | Opinion of **one** RMP |
| **20 to 24 weeks** | Opinion of **two** RMPs, only for **special categories** of women listed in the rules (for example, survivors of rape or incest, minors, women whose marital status changed during pregnancy, women with disabilities, and cases of foetal malformation) |
| Beyond 24 weeks | Only for **substantial foetal abnormalities**, on the opinion of a **State-level Medical Board** |
| Any time | To **save the woman's life** in an emergency, by an RMP acting in good faith (no second opinion needed) |

### Grounds

1. Continuing the pregnancy would risk the woman's **life** or cause grave injury to her **physical or mental health**.
2. There is a substantial risk that the child would have serious **physical or mental abnormalities**.
3. Pregnancy caused by **rape** (presumed to cause grave injury to mental health).
4. Pregnancy resulting from **failure of a contraceptive** used by **any woman or her partner** (the 2021 amendment widened this from "married woman or her husband").

### Consent

- The **woman's own consent** is needed; no one else's consent is required for an adult woman.
- For a **minor** (below 18 years) or a woman with a mental illness, the **written consent of the guardian** is needed.

### Where

- In a **hospital run by the Government**, or a **place approved** by the Government or the district-level committee.
- **Medical methods** (drugs) can be used in early pregnancy within the limit set by the rules, by an RMP, with access to a facility for complications.

### Confidentiality

The name and particulars of the woman must **not be revealed** except to a person authorised by law; breaking this is an offence (added in 2021).

### Medical Boards (2021)

Every State and Union Territory must set up a **Medical Board** (gynaecologist, paediatrician, radiologist or sonologist and other members) to decide on terminations beyond 24 weeks for foetal abnormalities.

### Offences

- Termination by a person who is **not an RMP**, or in a **place not approved**, is punishable with **rigorous imprisonment** (the Act specifies not less than two years, extendable to seven years).
- Revealing the woman's identity is punishable with imprisonment, fine or both.

### Role of the pharmacist

- Drugs used for medical termination (such as mifepristone and misoprostol) are **prescription-only** medicines.
- Sell them **only against a valid prescription** of an RMP; counsel on use and danger signs (heavy bleeding, fever); never sell them over the counter.

## Part C: Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act, 2010

## 9.5 Objectives and scope

1. To provide for the **registration and regulation of clinical establishments** in the country.
2. To prescribe **minimum standards** of facilities and services they provide.
3. To improve the quality of health care and to collect data for health planning.

**Clinical establishment** includes a hospital, maternity home, nursing home, dispensary, clinic, sanatorium, or any institution offering diagnosis, treatment or care in any recognised system of medicine, and a **diagnostic laboratory or imaging centre**, whether run by the Government, a trust or a private owner. Establishments run by the armed forces are excluded.

The Act applies in the States that have adopted it (including Himachal Pradesh) and in the Union Territories; other States have their own laws.

## 9.6 Authorities

| Authority | Role |
| --- | --- |
| National Council for Clinical Establishments | Classifies establishments, frames minimum standards, keeps the national register |
| State / UT Council | Compiles the State register, reviews implementation |
| **District Registering Authority** (headed by the District Collector) | Grants **provisional** and **permanent** registration, inspects, cancels registration |

## 9.7 Main provisions

1. **No one may run a clinical establishment without registration.**
2. **Provisional registration** is given on application; **permanent registration** after it is shown that the minimum standards are met (after a period for public objections).
3. Conditions of registration:
   - meet the **minimum standards** of facilities, staff and services;
   - **display the rates** charged for each service in a conspicuous place, and charge within the range set by the Government;
   - provide treatment needed to **stabilise any emergency** patient;
   - maintain **medical records** (electronic where required) and send reports and statistics;
   - follow **standard treatment guidelines** issued by the Government.
4. Registration can be **cancelled** for breaking conditions, after a hearing; appeals lie to higher authorities.
5. Running without registration and other breaches are punishable with **monetary penalties** that increase for repeat offences (check the current Act for amounts).

## 9.8 Aspects related to pharmacy

- A **hospital pharmacy** in a clinical establishment must hold the proper **drug licence** and be run by a **registered pharmacist**.
- Minimum standards include **storage of drugs** (temperature, security), availability of **emergency medicines**, records of **narcotic and Schedule H1/X drugs**, and proper disposal of expired drugs and biomedical waste.
- Drug procurement should follow the hospital formulary; a **Pharmacy and Therapeutics Committee** is good practice.
- Rates of medicines charged to patients must follow the displayed rate list and the **MRP / ceiling price** rules.
- Medication records must be part of the patient's medical record.

## Remember

- FSS Act **2006**, FSSAI set up in **2008** under the Health Ministry; replaced the PFA Act 1954 and other food orders.
- Supplements are **food, not medicine**: no disease claims; label **"NOT FOR MEDICINAL USE"** and the category name; FSSAI licence number.
- MTP Act **1971**, amended **2021**: up to **20 weeks** one RMP; **20–24 weeks** two RMPs for special categories; beyond **24 weeks** only via **Medical Board** for foetal abnormalities.
- Minor or mentally ill woman: **guardian's written consent**; identity kept **confidential**.
- Clinical Establishments Act **2010**: registration (provisional, then permanent), **minimum standards**, **display rates**, emergency stabilisation; District Registering Authority.

# Unit 8: Code of Pharmaceutical Ethics

This unit covers syllabus chapter 10 (5 hours). Law tells a pharmacist what he **must** do; ethics tells him what he **ought** to do. The Pharmacy Council of India's Code of Ethics for Pharmacists and the Pharmacy Practice Regulations, 2015 (Unit 2) set out these duties. Long questions on "code of ethics in relation to the job, trade, medical profession and profession" are very common.

## 8.1 Definitions

- **Ethics** – the study of right and wrong conduct; a set of moral principles that guide behaviour.
- **Professional ethics** – the standards of conduct that members of a profession accept as their duty towards the people they serve, their colleagues and society.
- **Pharmaceutical ethics** – the moral principles and standards of conduct that guide pharmacists in their practice, so that the patient's welfare comes first.
- **Code of ethics** – a written set of these principles, adopted by a professional body, which members are expected to follow. Breaking it can be treated as **professional misconduct**.

### Law and ethics compared

| Law | Ethics |
| --- | --- |
| Made by the State | Developed by the profession and society |
| Compulsory; enforced by courts | Voluntary but enforced by the professional council for members |
| Sets the **minimum** standard | Sets a **higher** standard |
| Breach → fine, imprisonment | Breach → warning, suspension or removal from register; loss of reputation |
| Example: sell Schedule H drugs only on prescription | Example: counsel a patient patiently and keep his information private |

## 8.2 Ethical principles

| Principle | Meaning for a pharmacist | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Autonomy** | Respect the patient's right to make informed decisions | Explain options; do not force a brand |
| **Beneficence** | Act for the patient's good | Check the prescription for interactions |
| **Non-maleficence** | Do no harm | Refuse to sell an abortion pill without a valid prescription |
| **Justice** | Treat all patients fairly | Serve every patient equally regardless of status |
| **Veracity** | Be truthful | Tell the patient the correct price and correct information |
| **Confidentiality** | Keep patient information private | Do not discuss an HIV patient's medicines with others |
| **Fidelity** | Keep promises and be loyal to the patient's interest | Keep stock of a regular patient's life-saving medicine as agreed |
| **Accountability** | Take responsibility for one's actions | Report and correct a dispensing error |

## 8.3 Ethical problem solving

A pharmacist often faces situations where two duties conflict (for example, a patient asks for a prescription medicine without a prescription because he cannot afford a doctor). A simple, structured approach:

```text
1. Identify the problem        -> What exactly is the ethical question?
2. Collect the facts           -> Patient, prescription, law, risks
3. Identify the people involved-> Patient, family, prescriber, employer, society
4. Identify the principles     -> Which duties are in conflict (beneficence vs law)?
5. List the options            -> Every possible action
6. Evaluate each option        -> Consequences, legality, professional code
7. Decide and act              -> Choose the option that best protects the patient
                                  within the law
8. Review and document         -> Record what was done and learn from it
```

**Example:** a customer requests a sleeping pill (Schedule H1) without a prescription for his anxious mother. The law forbids sale; beneficence suggests helping. The pharmacist refuses the sale, explains why, advises consulting a doctor (or a nearby government hospital), and gives non-drug advice on sleep. He documents the request if it seems suspicious.

## 8.4 Registration and ethics

- Only a **registered pharmacist** may dispense; registration makes the pharmacist **accountable** to the State Pharmacy Council.
- Infamous conduct in a professional respect can lead to **removal of the name** from the register (Unit 2).
- Ethical practice is therefore both a moral and a legal duty.

## 8.5 Code of ethics: pharmacist in relation to his job

1. **Scope of pharmaceutical services** – the pharmacist should provide all the services expected: dispensing, counselling, drug information, and should not limit his role to selling.
2. **Conduct of the pharmacy** – the pharmacy should be clean, well organised and look like a health establishment; drugs stored properly; the licence and registration displayed; a pharmacist present during working hours.
3. **Handling of prescriptions**:
   - receive the prescription personally and read it carefully;
   - do **not change or substitute** any ingredient without the prescriber's consent (except as the law allows);
   - if the prescription seems wrong (dose, interaction, allergy), **contact the prescriber**, not the patient's alarm;
   - do not comment on the prescriber's choice in front of the patient;
   - keep the prescription's contents **confidential**.
4. **Handling of drugs** – dispense drugs of **good quality**, correct strength and in **proper containers** with clear labels; never sell expired, spurious or substandard drugs.
5. **Apprentice pharmacists** – train trainees properly, give them guidance and supervise their work; make them good professionals.

## 8.6 Code of ethics: pharmacist in relation to his trade

1. **Price structure** – charge **fair prices**; follow the MRP and ceiling prices; do not overcharge.
2. **Fair trading practice** – do not undercut competitors unfairly or attract customers by unethical schemes; do not entice customers away from other pharmacists.
3. **Purchase of drugs** – buy only from **licensed, reliable sources**; never deal in spurious, smuggled or stolen drugs.
4. **Hawking of drugs** – **hawking** (selling drugs by moving from place to place, door to door) is unethical and must be avoided.
5. **Advertising and displays** – window displays and advertisements should be **dignified and truthful**; must not promote misuse or make false claims; must not display prohibited (DMR Act) advertisements.

## 8.7 Code of ethics: pharmacist in relation to the medical profession

1. **Limitation of professional activity** – the pharmacist should **not diagnose diseases or prescribe** medicines (except first aid in an emergency or OTC advice within his scope); he should refer patients to a doctor.
2. **Clandestine arrangements** – he must **not enter into secret arrangements** with doctors, such as paying or receiving **commissions** for prescriptions, or "prescription steering" to his shop.
3. **Liaison with doctors** – keep good professional relations; discuss prescription problems directly and politely with the prescriber; give **drug information** when asked.
4. **Recommendation of doctors** – he should not recommend a particular doctor for personal gain; if asked, he may suggest qualified practitioners.

## 8.8 Code of ethics: pharmacist in relation to his profession

1. **Professional vigilance** – keep up to date with knowledge (continuing education) and the law; uphold the **dignity** of the profession.
2. **Law-abiding citizen** – obey all laws relating to drugs and pharmacy and help authorities enforce them.
3. **Relationship with professional organisations** – join and support professional associations (for example, the Indian Pharmaceutical Association) and take part in their activities.
4. **Decorum and propriety** – behave with honesty and dignity; do nothing that brings discredit to the profession.
5. **Relations with colleagues** – do not criticise fellow pharmacists unfairly; help them in professional matters.
6. **Public health** – take part in health awareness, immunisation drives, disaster relief and rational drug use campaigns.

## 8.9 Pharmacist's oath (meaning)

A pharmacist's oath is a solemn promise made when entering the profession. Its main promises, in summary:

```text
I will keep the welfare of the patient and the public first.
I will use my knowledge and skill for the good of patients and never to harm them.
I will dispense medicines of good quality and give correct advice about them.
I will keep the information of patients confidential.
I will follow the laws and the code of ethics of the profession.
I will keep my knowledge up to date throughout my career.
I will treat all patients with respect, without discrimination.
I will not take part in any unfair or dishonest practice.
I will uphold the dignity and honour of the profession.
```

Different bodies use slightly different wording; learn the meaning, not exact words.

## 8.10 Common ethical situations in pharmacy

| Situation | Ethical action |
| --- | --- |
| Customer asks for antibiotics without prescription | Refuse politely, explain resistance, refer to a doctor |
| Doctor's prescription has a dose ten times higher than normal | Contact the prescriber before dispensing |
| Medical representative offers a gift to push a brand | Refuse; recommend on merit only |
| Patient asks about a cheaper equivalent | Give honest information, within the law on substitution |
| Neighbour asks what medicines a patient bought | Do not disclose; confidentiality |
| Expired stock found | Remove from sale, record, return or destroy as per rules |

## Remember

- Ethics sets a **higher standard** than law; breach can mean removal from the register.
- Principles: **autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice, veracity, confidentiality**.
- Job: scope of service, conduct of pharmacy, **handling of prescriptions** (no change without prescriber), handling of drugs, apprentices.
- Trade: **fair price**, fair trading, purchase from licensed sources, **no hawking**, dignified advertising.
- Medical profession: **no diagnosing/prescribing**, **no clandestine arrangements**, good liaison.
- Profession: vigilance, law-abiding, professional bodies, decorum; **pharmacist's oath**.

# How to use this guide

Pharmacy Law and Ethics (ER20-26T) is a second-year D.Pharm theory subject of **75 hours**, with no practical. The syllabus has **21 short chapters**. Chapter 3 (Drugs and Cosmetics Act and Rules) alone has 23 hours, so this guide splits it into two units; many 1- and 2-hour chapters are joined so that each unit is a sensible study block.

| Guide unit | Syllabus chapter(s) | Hours in syllabus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. General principles of law, history and Acts | 1 | 2 |
| 2. Pharmacy Act 1948 and Pharmacy Practice Regulations 2015 | 2 | 5 |
| 3. Drugs and Cosmetics Act – objectives, definitions, schedules, import, administration | 3 (part) | 23 (shared) |
| 4. Drugs and Cosmetics Act – manufacture, schedules, sale, records, banned drugs | 3 (part) | (shared) |
| 5. NDPS Act 1985 and Poisons Act 1919 | 4 and 7 | 2 + 2 |
| 6. Drugs and Magic Remedies Act 1954 and Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act 1960 | 5 and 6 | 2 + 2 |
| 7. NPPA, DPCO 2013, Pharmaceutical Policy 2002 and NLEM | 9 | 5 |
| 8. Code of Pharmaceutical Ethics | 10 | 5 |
| 9. FSSAI and food supplements, MTP Act, Clinical Establishments Act | 8, 11 and 16 | 2 + 2 + 2 |
| 10. CDSCO and IPC, good regulatory practices, medical devices | 12, 13 and 21 | 1 + 3 + 2 |
| 11. New drug development, clinical trials, BCS, ANDA/NDA, brands, patents, EUA | 14 | 7 |
| 12. Blood bank and Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules 2016 | 15 and 17 | 2 + 2 |
| 13. Bioethics and ICMR guidelines, Consumer Protection Act, Disaster Management Act | 18, 19 and 20 | 2 + 1 + 1 |

Suggested order: Units 2, 3, 4 and 8 first (they carry the most marks), then 5, 7 and 11, then the rest. Read a unit, close the notes, write the "Remember" points from memory, then answer that unit's questions.

## Exam pattern (ER-2020 theory paper)

D.Pharm ER-2020 examinations are yearly. In Himachal Pradesh the board (HPTSB) conducts them under the PCI ER-2020 scheme. The theory paper is **80 marks, 3 hours**; another 20 marks come from sessional (internal) assessment. The usual paper looks like this; **confirm the exact pattern with your college**, because boards sometimes change the format.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Objective questions (MCQs / one-word / fill in the blanks) – all compulsory | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| II | Short answers – attempt 10 of 11 | 10 × 3 = 30 |
| III | Long answers – attempt 6 of 7 | 6 × 5 = 30 |

In this guide **long** questions are 5-mark answers and **short** questions are 3-mark answers, to match this pattern. **Very-short** (2-mark) questions are included for quick revision and sessional tests.

## Probability ratings

- **High** – asked in most years; prepare the full answer.
- **Medium** – asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** – rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

Ratings are an estimate from syllabus weight and the kind of questions usually set in D.Pharm papers. They are not a promise.

## Laws change – how to handle it in the exam

- Fines, form numbers, schedule contents and fees are revised often. This guide gives figures only where they are well established, and otherwise says "check the current rules".
- In the exam, write the **name of the Act and year**, the **objective**, the **authority** and the **key provision** correctly; that earns most marks. If you mention an amendment (for example, MTP Amendment 2021, NDCT Rules 2019, Medical Devices Rules 2017), give its year.
- If your teacher gives you a newer figure from an official notification, use it.

## Scoring tips for Pharmacy Law and Ethics

1. **Start every answer with the full name and year of the law**, then its **objectives**. Examiners look for this first.
2. **Definitions carry marks.** Learn the exact meaning of drug, cosmetic, misbranded, adulterated, spurious, patent or proprietary medicine, magic remedy, narcotic drug, psychotropic substance, ceiling price, scheduled formulation, registered pharmacist.
3. **Use tables** for constitution of PCI and State Councils, DTAB vs DCC, licence forms, schedules with labels, BMW colour codes, BCS classes, NDA vs ANDA, brand vs generic.
4. **Draw a simple flow chart** for the administration of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, the drug development process, or BMW handling – it makes the answer clear and quick to mark.
5. **For a 5-mark answer**, write about one and a half pages: definition/objective, a table or classification, 5–6 explained points, and a one-line conclusion.
6. **For a 3-mark answer**, write 5–6 crisp points or a small table.
7. **Ethics answers**: use the four headings – in relation to his **job**, **trade**, **medical profession** and **profession** – with 3–4 points under each.
8. **Do not invent numbers.** A correct principle without a figure is better than a wrong figure.

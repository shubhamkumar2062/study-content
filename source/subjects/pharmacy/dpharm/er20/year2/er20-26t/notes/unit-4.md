# Unit 4: Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and Rules, 1945 – II (manufacture, schedules, sale, records, banned drugs)

This unit continues syllabus chapter 3. It covers licences for manufacture, the schedules named in the syllabus (C and C1, G, H, H1, K, P, M, N and X), licences for sale, the records a pharmacy must keep, and drugs banned in India. Examiners love tables of licence forms and schedule labels, so learn them well.

## 4.1 Manufacture of drugs

### Prohibition of manufacture and sale of certain drugs

No person may manufacture for sale, sell, stock, exhibit or distribute:

1. Any drug that is **not of standard quality**, or is **misbranded, adulterated or spurious**.
2. Any **patent or proprietary medicine** whose label does not show the true formula or list of active ingredients with quantities.
3. Any drug that claims to **prevent or cure a disease in Schedule J**.
4. Any drug made in breach of the Act or its Rules, or **without a licence**.
5. Any drug whose manufacture or sale has been **prohibited by the Central Government** (section 26A, see 4.6).

### Types of manufacturing licence

| Licence | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Form 25 | Manufacture for sale of drugs **other than** those in Schedules C, C(1) and X |
| Form 28 | Manufacture for sale of drugs in **Schedules C and C(1)** (excluding Schedule X) |
| Form 25A | **Loan licence** for drugs other than C, C(1) and X |
| Form 28A | **Loan licence** for drugs in Schedules C and C(1) |
| Form 25B | **Repacking** licence (drugs other than C, C(1) and X) |
| Form 29 | Licence to manufacture drugs for **examination, test or analysis** |
| Separate forms | Manufacture of Schedule X drugs, cosmetics, homoeopathic medicines and ASU drugs each have their own licences |

The State licensing authority grants most licences. For Schedule C/C(1) products such as vaccines, sera, blood products and large volume parenterals, the licence needs the approval of the **Central Licence Approving Authority (DCGI)**. Form numbers are revised from time to time, so confirm with the current rules.

### Conditions for grant of a manufacturing licence

1. **Premises, plant and equipment** that meet **Schedule M** (GMP).
2. **Competent technical staff**: manufacture under the active direction of a person with a degree in pharmacy, pharmaceutical chemistry or chemistry (or as the rules list) with practical experience.
3. A **testing unit** of its own with a competent analytical chemist, or arrangement with an approved laboratory where the rules allow.
4. Proper storage facilities for raw materials and finished products.
5. Inspection of the premises by drug inspectors before grant.

### Conditions of a manufacturing licence (after grant)

1. Keep **manufacturing and analytical records** as given in **Schedule U**, for the period prescribed.
2. Test every batch of raw material and finished product before release.
3. Allow inspectors to enter, inspect and take samples at any time.
4. Supply samples to the authority when asked.
5. Report changes in technical staff or premises.
6. Comply with Schedule M continuously; recall defective batches.
7. Pay the licence or **retention fee** on time. Under the present rules most licences no longer expire after a fixed period but remain valid while the retention fee is paid at the set interval; check the current rule.

### Manufacture for examination, test or analysis

A small quantity of a drug may be made for **test, examination or analysis** (for example, for stability studies or to develop a product) under a test licence (Form 29). The quantity is limited, the drug cannot be sold, and records of making, use and disposal must be kept.

### Manufacture of a new drug

A **new drug** (not yet approved in India, or a new indication, dosage form, route or combination of an approved drug) can be manufactured for sale only after permission from the **Central Licensing Authority** under the **New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019** (Unit 11). After that, the State licence is endorsed for the product.

### Loan licence

A **loan licence** is given to a person (the loan licensee) who **does not own** a manufacturing unit but uses the premises, equipment and technical staff of another licensed manufacturer to make his products. Both are responsible for quality. It allows small marketing companies to get products made without building a factory.

### Repacking licence

**Repacking** means breaking up a drug from a bulk container into smaller packs for sale or distribution (for example, repacking a bulk powder or tablets into small packs). It needs a separate licence (Form 25B), suitable premises and a competent person, and repackers must label packs correctly and keep records. Some products (for example, Schedule C/C(1) items) may not be repacked under this licence.

## 4.2 Schedules named in the syllabus

| Schedule | What it covers | Examples | Label / special rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **C** | Biological and special products, mostly parenteral | Vaccines, sera, toxoids, antigens, insulin, heparin, sterile injections | Form 28 licence; cold storage and special testing |
| **C(1)** | Other special products, mostly non-parenteral | Digitalis and ergot preparations, some antibiotics and vitamins for oral use, ophthalmic preparations | Form 28 licence; storage as labelled |
| **G** | Drugs that should be taken under medical supervision | Several antihistamines and oral antidiabetics, as listed | Label: **"Caution: It is dangerous to take this preparation except under medical supervision"** |
| **H** | Prescription drugs | Most antibiotics, antihypertensives, steroids, as listed | Symbol **Rx** and warning: **"Schedule H drug – Warning: To be sold by retail on the prescription of a Registered Medical Practitioner only"** |
| **H1** | Specified 3rd/4th generation antibiotics, anti-TB drugs and habit-forming drugs (added in 2013) | Cefepime, meropenem, moxifloxacin, isoniazid, rifampicin, ethambutol, pyrazinamide, alprazolam, zolpidem, buprenorphine | **Rx** in red and a warning in a box with a red border: dangerous to take except on medical advice, not to be sold without prescription. Separate **H1 register** |
| **K** | Exemptions from certain provisions | Household remedies (for example, simple pain relievers, antacids, balms as listed) sold by shops in villages; drugs supplied by a doctor to his own patients; drugs supplied by hospitals to their patients | Conditions in the schedule must be met |
| **P** | Life period (expiry) of drugs and storage conditions | Shelf life of listed drugs, such as antibiotics | Expiry not to exceed the stated period unless approved |
| **M** | Good Manufacturing Practices | Premises, sanitation, staff, equipment, documentation, quality control | Must be followed by every drug factory |
| **N** | Minimum equipment of a pharmacy | Premises, dispensing equipment, reference books | Must be followed by pharmacies that compound and dispense |
| **X** | Narcotic and psychotropic drugs with high misuse potential | Amphetamine, methylphenidate, barbiturates such as amobarbital, pentobarbital and secobarbital | **XRx** in red and warning; special licences, prescriptions in duplicate, separate records, locked storage |

### Schedule H1 in detail

- Introduced to control **misuse of antibiotics (to slow antimicrobial resistance)** and of habit-forming drugs.
- The retailer must record in a **separate register**: name and address of the prescriber, name of the patient, name of the drug and quantity supplied.
- The register must be kept for **three years** and be open to inspection.

### Schedule X in detail

- Needs **special licences** for manufacture and sale (retail and wholesale).
- Prescription must be in **duplicate**; the retailer keeps one copy for the period laid down (two years).
- Drugs must be kept under **lock and key** in a separate place.
- Records of purchase and sale are kept separately and checked by inspectors.
- Manufacturers must report stock and sales to the authorities as the rules require.

### Schedule M (GMP) in brief

```text
Premises      -> location away from pollution, proper drains, separate areas for
                 raw materials, production, packing, quarantine, finished goods
Personnel     -> qualified and trained staff, health checks, hygiene, clean clothing
Equipment     -> suitable, cleaned, calibrated, maintained, with logs
Materials     -> tested, quarantined, labelled, stored properly
Production    -> written procedures, batch records, line clearance, in-process checks
Quality control -> independent QC department; testing, stability, retained samples
Documentation -> master formula, batch records, SOPs, distribution and complaint records
Other         -> self-inspection, recall procedure, validation, complaints handling
```

### Schedule N in brief

- **Premises:** a pharmacy should be clean, separated from living quarters, with proper light and ventilation, and with the area the rules require.
- **Equipment:** prescription balance and weights, measures, mortars and pestles, spatulas, funnels, glass and porcelain vessels, refrigerator, water bath, hot plate, ointment slab, and other dispensing items listed.
- **Books:** the current Indian Pharmacopoeia and the National Formulary of India, and other standard references.
- **Staff:** a registered pharmacist in charge of compounding and dispensing.

## 4.3 Sale of drugs

### Types of sale licence

| Licence | Who it is for |
| --- | --- |
| **Form 20** | Retail sale of drugs other than those in Schedules C, C(1) and X |
| **Form 21** | Retail sale of drugs in Schedules C and C(1), excluding X |
| **Form 20B** | Wholesale of drugs other than C, C(1) and X |
| **Form 21B** | Wholesale of drugs in Schedules C and C(1) |
| **Form 20F / 20G** | Retail / wholesale of Schedule X drugs |
| **Form 20A / 21A** | **Restricted licences** for dealers who do not employ a pharmacist (for example, general stores in villages or itinerant vendors), for selling only certain drugs in sealed packs |

Licences are issued by the **State licensing authority**. Form numbers and fees are revised from time to time; check the current rules.

### Conditions for a retail sale licence

1. **Premises** of adequate area (the rules prescribe a minimum floor area), clean and well lit, with proper storage.
2. A **refrigerator** where drugs need cold storage.
3. Sale under the **personal supervision of a registered pharmacist**.
4. Prescription drugs (H, H1, X) sold **only on a prescription** of a registered medical practitioner.
5. Drugs stored as labelled; expired drugs kept separately and not sold.
6. Licence displayed prominently.

### Conditions for a wholesale licence

1. Adequate premises and storage, including cold storage where needed.
2. Business under the charge of a **competent person** – a registered pharmacist, or a person with the educational qualification and experience in dealing with drugs that the rules prescribe.
3. Sale only to licensed retailers, hospitals, institutions and doctors, with proper invoices.

### Restricted licence

Given to a person who does not have a pharmacist (for example, a shop in a village where no pharmacy exists). It allows sale of **only certain drugs**, usually household remedies in **original sealed packs** from licensed manufacturers, without opening or compounding.

## 4.4 Records to be kept in a pharmacy

| Record | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Purchase records | Invoices with name of supplier, drug, batch number, quantity, expiry |
| Sale records (cash memo / bill) | For Schedule C, C(1), H, H1 and X drugs: name of patient and prescriber, drug, quantity, batch number |
| Prescription records | Prescriptions dispensed are stamped with the pharmacy's name and date to prevent reuse; prescription register for compounded medicines |
| **Schedule H1 register** | Prescriber, patient, drug and quantity; kept **three years** |
| **Schedule X records** | Duplicate prescription copy and separate register |
| NDPS records | For narcotic drugs, as the NDPS rules require (Unit 5) |
| Expiry and returns records | Expired or recalled stock, returned to supplier or destroyed properly |
| Temperature log | For refrigerated products such as vaccines and insulin |

Records must be kept for the periods the rules lay down and be shown to the inspector on demand.

## 4.5 Labelling of drugs (main points)

Labels of drugs for sale must show: name of the drug (generic name prominently), content of active ingredients, quantity, **batch number**, **manufacturing licence number**, **date of manufacture and expiry**, name and address of the manufacturer, storage conditions where needed, the schedule symbol and warning (Rx, XRx, caution), "for external use only" or "not for injection" where relevant, and the maximum retail price.

## 4.6 Drugs prohibited for manufacture and sale in India

- The Central Government can **prohibit** the manufacture, sale or distribution of any drug or cosmetic if it is satisfied, after consulting the DTAB, that the drug **involves risk to people or animals**, has **no therapeutic value claimed**, or has **no therapeutic justification** for its ingredients (section 26A).
- Many **irrational fixed-dose combinations (FDCs)** have been banned this way, for example a large group of FDCs banned in 2018.
- Examples of single drugs or uses banned in the past include **nimesulide formulations for children below 12 years**, **sibutramine** and **gatifloxacin** formulations for systemic use.
- A drug may also be restricted to certain uses instead of a full ban.
- The list changes often and some bans are challenged in court; **always check the current list on the CDSCO website**.

## Remember

- Manufacture licences: **25** (general), **28** (C and C1), **25A/28A** (loan), **25B** (repacking), **29** (test).
- Sale licences: **20/21** retail, **20B/21B** wholesale, **20F/20G** Schedule X, **20A/21A** restricted.
- Schedule **G** = caution label; **H** = Rx; **H1** = Rx in red + register for 3 years; **X** = XRx, duplicate prescription, lock and key.
- Schedule **M** = GMP; **N** = pharmacy equipment and books; **K** = exemptions; **P** = shelf life; **C/C1** = biologicals and special products.
- **Loan licence** = using another firm's factory; **repacking** = bulk to small packs.
- Section **26A** lets the Government ban drugs and irrational FDCs.

# Unit 10: Government regulators (CDSCO, IPC), good regulatory practices and medical devices

This unit joins syllabus chapter 12 (role of CDSCO and IPC), chapter 13 (good regulatory practices) and chapter 21 (medical devices). Together they describe **who** regulates medicines in India, **how** a pharmacy or company stays compliant, and how **medical devices** are now controlled.

## Part A: Government pharma regulators

## 10.1 Overview

```text
Ministry of Health and Family Welfare
   |-- Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS)
   |      |-- CDSCO (headed by the Drugs Controller General of India, DCGI)
   |-- Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission (IPC), Ghaziabad (autonomous)
   |-- FSSAI (food), National Medical Commission, etc.
Department of Pharmaceuticals (Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers)
   |-- NPPA (prices)
Pharmacy Council of India (statutory body under the Pharmacy Act)
State Governments -> State Drugs Control Departments (Drugs Controller, inspectors, labs)
```

## 10.2 Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO)

CDSCO is the **national regulatory authority** for drugs, cosmetics and medical devices, working under the DGHS. It is headed by the **Drugs Controller General of India (DCGI)**, who is the **Central Licensing Authority** under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act and its rules. Its headquarters is in New Delhi, with zonal and sub-zonal offices, **port offices** at airports and seaports, and central drug testing laboratories.

### Functions of CDSCO

1. **Approval of new drugs** and permission to conduct **clinical trials** (New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019).
2. **Regulation of imports**: registration of foreign manufacturers and products, import licences, and checks at ports.
3. **Licensing of special products** as the Central Licence Approving Authority: vaccines, sera, blood and blood products, large volume parenterals, recombinant and some biological products.
4. **Regulation of medical devices** (classes C and D, and all imports) under the Medical Devices Rules, 2017.
5. **Laying down standards** and amending the Act and Rules, with the DTAB's advice; **banning** unsafe drugs and irrational FDCs.
6. **Coordination with States** through the Drugs Consultative Committee, for uniform enforcement; joint inspections of factories.
7. **Testing** of samples in central laboratories (for example, the Central Drugs Laboratory, Kolkata).
8. **Export support**: issuing no-objection certificates where needed and certificates such as the WHO-GMP certificate of pharmaceutical product (with State authorities).
9. **Pharmacovigilance** and safety actions (with IPC), and registration of ethics committees for clinical trials.
10. Online services through the **SUGAM portal**.

### State Drug Control Organisations

Each State has a **Drugs Control Department** headed by a **State Drugs Controller** (the controlling authority). It issues **manufacturing licences** for most drugs and cosmetics and **all sale licences**, appoints **drug inspectors**, runs **State drug-testing laboratories**, and prosecutes offenders.

## 10.3 Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission (IPC)

The IPC is an **autonomous institution** of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, located at **Ghaziabad (Uttar Pradesh)**.

### Functions of IPC

1. **Publishes the Indian Pharmacopoeia (IP)** – the official book of standards for drugs in India – with regular new editions and addenda (editions include 1955, 1966, 1985, 1996, 2007, 2010, 2014, 2018 and 2022; check for any newer edition).
2. Publishes the **National Formulary of India (NFI)**, a guide to rational use of medicines for health professionals.
3. Prepares and supplies **IP Reference Substances (IPRS)** used for testing drugs.
4. Acts as the **National Coordination Centre for the Pharmacovigilance Programme of India (PvPI)** – collects adverse drug reaction reports from hospitals and the public, analyses them and recommends safety actions to CDSCO.
5. Runs the **Materiovigilance Programme of India (MvPI)** for adverse events from medical devices.
6. Trains regulators, analysts and pharmacists on pharmacopoeial standards and pharmacovigilance.

| Regulator | Main task |
| --- | --- |
| CDSCO | Approves, licenses and controls (enforcement) |
| IPC | Sets standards (IP), reference substances, pharmacovigilance |
| State Drugs Control | Licenses manufacture and sale locally, inspects, prosecutes |
| NPPA | Fixes and enforces prices |
| PCI | Pharmacy education and the profession |

## Part B: Good regulatory practices

## 10.4 What good regulatory practice means

**Good regulatory practice (GRP)** for a pharmacy or company means doing everything the law requires, **on time, and with written proof**: correct licences, renewals, records, readiness for inspection, and the right permissions for import and export. Good documentation is the key – "if it is not written, it was not done".

## 10.5 Documentation, licences and renewals

| Setting | Main licences and registrations | Key documents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Community pharmacy | Retail drug licence (Forms 20/21, and 20F for Schedule X); pharmacist's registration; NDPS licence where narcotics are stocked; FSSAI registration for supplements | Purchase invoices, sale bills, prescription records, Schedule H1 register, Schedule X records, expiry and temperature logs |
| Hospital pharmacy | Drug licence in the hospital's name; NDPS licence; blood centre licence if applicable | Formulary, indents, issue records, narcotic register, ADR reports, Pharmacy and Therapeutics Committee minutes, SOPs |
| Pharma manufacturing | Manufacturing licences (and loan licences); product permissions; new drug approval where needed | Site master file, master formula and batch records (Schedule U), SOPs, validation, QC records, change control, complaints and recalls |
| Wholesale business | Wholesale licences (Forms 20B/21B, 20G); competent person | Invoices showing batch and expiry, records of buyers' licences, cold chain records, recall records |

### Renewals and retention

- Licences now generally remain valid while the **retention fee** is paid at the set interval; the pharmacist's **registration** must be renewed with the State Council.
- Any change (premises, pharmacist, products, ownership) must be **intimated** to, or approved by, the licensing authority.
- Keep a **compliance calendar** of due dates.

## 10.6 e-Governance

- **SUGAM portal (CDSCO)** – online applications for import, new drugs, clinical trials, registration of ethics committees and other central permissions; status tracking.
- **Online National Drugs Licensing System** – used by many States for online manufacture and sale licences.
- Online systems for **medical device** licensing, **pharmacovigilance** reporting (PvPI forms and mobile app), FSSAI licences, and NPPA price complaints.
- Benefits: transparency, faster decisions, less paperwork, nationwide data.

## 10.7 Inspections

| Type | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Pre-licence inspection | Check premises and staff before granting a licence |
| Routine inspection | Periodic check of compliance |
| For-cause inspection | After a complaint, a failed sample or an adverse event |
| Joint inspection | Central and State inspectors together (for example, for vaccines or exports) |

**Being inspection-ready:** licences displayed and valid, pharmacist present, records complete and up to date, expired drugs segregated, cold chain logs maintained, SOPs followed. After an inspection, answer every observation with a **corrective and preventive action (CAPA)** report within the time given.

## 10.8 Import and export of drugs and medical devices

| Activity | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Import of drugs | Registration of foreign site and products, import licence, port clearance by CDSCO port office |
| Import for test or analysis | Test licence; limited quantity; records |
| Import of medical devices | Import licence from the Central Licensing Authority under the Medical Devices Rules |
| Export of drugs | Manufacturing licence; export NOC where the product is not approved in India; **WHO-GMP / COPP** certificate as the importing country asks |
| Export of medical devices | Licence and certificates (free sale, quality management) as required |
| Narcotics | Import and export certificates under the NDPS Act |

## Part C: Medical devices

## 10.9 Basic aspects

- A **medical device** is an instrument, apparatus, appliance, implant, material, software or similar article used for diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, treatment or alleviation of disease or injury, or for supporting life, that does **not** achieve its main action by pharmacological, immunological or metabolic means.
- Medical devices are regulated as "drugs" under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act through the **Medical Devices Rules, 2017**, which came into force on **1 January 2018**. From **1 April 2020** the Government brought **all medical devices** under regulation, with registration first and licensing made compulsory in phases (classes A and B, then C and D, by October 2023).
- In vitro diagnostic devices (IVDs) are covered by the same rules.

## 10.10 Categorization (risk-based classes)

| Class | Risk | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | Low | Tongue depressors, alcohol swabs, simple bandages |
| **B** | Low to moderate | Hypodermic needles, suction equipment |
| **C** | Moderate to high | Lung ventilators, bone fixation plates |
| **D** | High | Heart valves, coronary stents, implantable defibrillators |

Classification is based on intended use, duration of contact, whether invasive or implantable, and whether active (powered). The CDSCO publishes lists classifying devices.

## 10.11 Manufacture and sale

### Manufacture

1. **Licensing authority:** classes **A and B** by the **State Licensing Authority**; classes **C and D** by the **Central Licensing Authority (DCGI)**. All **imports** are licensed centrally.
2. **Notified bodies** (accredited third-party agencies) audit class A and B manufacturers.
3. Manufacturers must follow a **quality management system** (laid down in the rules, in line with international standards such as ISO 13485) and the essential principles of safety and performance.
4. New devices without a predicate may need **clinical investigation** with permission.
5. **Labelling:** name, model, batch or lot, date of manufacture and expiry (where relevant), "sterile" and method where applicable, licence number, manufacturer's address, and instructions for use.
6. **Post-market surveillance**: report adverse events, handle complaints, recall defective devices; adverse events can be reported to the **Materiovigilance Programme of India**.

### Sale

- Sale of medical devices needs registration or licence with the State authority as the rules now require; dealers must buy from licensed manufacturers or importers and keep records.
- Devices must be stored as labelled; sterile devices kept intact; expired devices removed.
- Prices of some devices (coronary stents, knee implants) have been capped under the DPCO powers.

## Remember

- **CDSCO** (under DGHS) headed by **DCGI** = Central Licensing Authority: new drugs, clinical trials, imports, vaccines and blood products, class C/D devices.
- **IPC, Ghaziabad**: publishes **IP** (2022 edition) and **NFI**, IP reference substances, runs **PvPI** and **MvPI**.
- Good regulatory practice = valid licences, timely renewals/retention fees, complete records, inspection readiness, **CAPA**, online systems (**SUGAM**).
- Medical Devices Rules **2017** (in force **2018**); classes **A (low), B, C, D (high)**; A/B by State, C/D and imports by Centre.

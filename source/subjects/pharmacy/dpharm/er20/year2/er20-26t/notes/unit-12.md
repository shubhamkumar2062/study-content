# Unit 12: Blood bank and Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016

This unit joins syllabus chapter 15 (blood bank – basic requirements and functions) and chapter 17 (Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016). Both deal with materials from the human body that can spread infection if handled badly. The colour-coding table of biomedical waste is a favourite exam question.

## Part A: Blood bank (blood centre)

## 12.1 Definition and legal basis

A **blood bank** is a place or organisation where blood is **collected from donors, tested, processed (into components), stored and issued** for transfusion. Recent amendments to the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules call it a **blood centre**.

- **Whole human blood and blood components are "drugs"** under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, so a blood bank needs a **licence**.
- The requirements are laid down in the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules (the part dealing with blood banks and the related part of **Schedule F**).
- The licence is granted by the **State Licensing Authority** with the **approval of the Central Licence Approving Authority (DCGI)**, after a joint inspection.
- National policy and standards are guided by the **National Blood Transfusion Council (NBTC)** and State councils, under the **National Blood Policy**. Commercial (paid) blood donation is banned; donation must be **voluntary and non-remunerated**.

## 12.2 Basic requirements

### Premises

A blood bank should have separate, clean, well-lit and air-conditioned areas for:

| Area | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Registration and medical examination | Donor registration, history, check-up |
| Blood collection room | With donor couches |
| Refreshment / rest room | Donor recovery after donation |
| Laboratories | Blood grouping and cross-matching; testing for transfusion-transmissible infections |
| Component preparation room | If components are made |
| Storage | Quarantine (untested) and released (tested) blood kept apart |
| Sterilisation and washing room | For reusable items and waste treatment |
| Records and office | Documentation |

The rules prescribe the **minimum floor area**; check the current figure.

### Equipment

Donor couches, blood collection monitors and mixers, haemoglobin meter, weighing scale, BP apparatus, **blood bank refrigerators (2–6 °C)** with temperature recorders and alarms, **deep freezers** for plasma, **refrigerated centrifuge** (for components), **platelet incubator with agitator (20–24 °C)**, laminar air flow, tube sealer, serological and ELISA equipment, emergency medicines and oxygen for donor reactions.

### Personnel

1. **Medical officer** in charge, qualified as the rules require (for example, a postgraduate in transfusion medicine or pathology, or an MBBS doctor with the prescribed experience).
2. **Technical supervisor** (qualified in medical laboratory technology with experience).
3. **Registered nurse(s)** and laboratory technicians.
4. Additional qualified staff for component preparation and apheresis.

### Donor selection (main criteria)

| Criterion | Requirement (as per national donor selection guidelines; check for updates) |
| --- | --- |
| Age | 18 to 65 years |
| Weight | At least **45 kg** (for 350 mL collection); at least 55 kg for 450 mL |
| Haemoglobin | Not less than **12.5 g/dL** |
| Interval | At least **3 months for men** and **4 months for women** between whole blood donations |
| Health | Normal temperature, pulse and blood pressure; free from serious diseases; not on medicines that make him unfit; no high-risk behaviour |

### Mandatory testing of every unit

1. **Blood grouping** – ABO and Rh(D); screening for irregular antibodies.
2. **Transfusion-transmissible infections**: **HIV 1 and 2**, **hepatitis B (HBsAg)**, **hepatitis C**, **syphilis** and **malaria**.
3. **Cross-matching** with the recipient's blood before issue.

Units that test positive are **discarded** safely as biomedical waste, and the donor is referred for counselling.

### Storage of blood and components

| Product | Storage temperature | Shelf life |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole blood / red cells (CPDA-1) | 2–6 °C | 35 days |
| Red cells in additive solution (SAGM) | 2–6 °C | 42 days |
| Platelet concentrate | 20–24 °C with **continuous agitation** | 5 days |
| Fresh frozen plasma | −30 °C or colder | Up to 1 year |
| Cryoprecipitate | −30 °C or colder | Up to 1 year |

### Labelling and records

- Each bag is labelled with the **unit number**, blood group, date of collection and expiry, anticoagulant, volume, test results ("tested non-reactive"), storage conditions and the licence number.
- Records: donor register, test results, component preparation, stock, issue and cross-match records, transfusion reaction reports, discard records and temperature charts, kept for the period the rules require.

## 12.3 Functions of a blood bank

1. **Donor recruitment and motivation** – voluntary blood donation camps and awareness.
2. **Donor screening and collection** of blood under aseptic conditions.
3. **Testing** for blood group and infections.
4. **Component separation** – packed red cells, platelets, fresh frozen plasma, cryoprecipitate – so one donation helps several patients.
5. **Storage** at correct temperatures with cold chain.
6. **Cross-matching and issue** of compatible blood to hospitals.
7. **Haemovigilance** – recording and reporting transfusion reactions.
8. **Quality control** of reagents, equipment and products.
9. **Record keeping** and reporting to authorities.
10. **Safe disposal** of discarded units and waste.

### Role of the pharmacist

Pharmacists may work in blood banks in storage, inventory and cold-chain management, issue of blood products, documentation and quality assurance, and in handling plasma-derived medicines (albumin, clotting factors), which are also drugs.

## Part B: Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016

## 12.4 Basic aspects

- Notified in **2016** by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**, replacing the 1998 rules; amended later (for example, in 2018 and 2019).
- **Bio-medical waste (BMW)** means any waste produced during **diagnosis, treatment or immunisation** of human beings or animals, or research activities related to these, or in the production or testing of biologicals, or in health camps.
- **Applies to** all persons who generate, collect, receive, store, transport, treat, dispose or handle BMW: hospitals, nursing homes, clinics, dispensaries, blood banks, laboratories, veterinary hospitals, animal houses, research institutes, AYUSH hospitals, vaccination and health camps and similar.
- **Does not apply** to radioactive waste, hazardous chemicals, municipal solid waste, battery waste and similar wastes covered by other rules.
- The **prescribed authority** is the **State Pollution Control Board** (or Pollution Control Committee in a Union Territory).

## 12.5 Segregation and colour coding

| Category | Container | Type of waste | Treatment and disposal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Yellow** | Non-chlorinated yellow plastic bags or containers | Human and animal anatomical waste; soiled waste (dressings, cotton, plaster casts contaminated with blood or body fluids); **expired or discarded medicines**; cytotoxic drug waste; chemical waste; discarded linen; microbiology and lab waste (after pre-treatment) | **Incineration**, plasma pyrolysis or deep burial (deep burial only where permitted, for example in remote areas) |
| **Red** | Non-chlorinated red plastic bags or containers | **Contaminated recyclable** plastic waste: tubing, bottles, IV sets, catheters, urine bags, syringes (without needles), gloves | Autoclaving or microwaving / hydroclaving, then **shredding**, and sent to registered recyclers – **never incinerated or landfilled** |
| **White (translucent)** | **Puncture-proof, leak-proof, tamper-proof** container | **Sharps**: needles, syringes with fixed needles, scalpels, blades, broken metal sharps | Autoclaving or dry heat sterilisation, then shredding or mutilation or **encapsulation** |
| **Blue** | Cardboard boxes with blue colour marking (puncture-proof) | **Glassware** (broken or discarded, contaminated) and **metallic body implants** | Disinfection (soaking or autoclaving), then sent for recycling |

Labels carry the **biohazard symbol** (and the **cytotoxic symbol** for cytotoxic waste).

```text
Generation point (ward, lab, pharmacy)
        |  segregate AT SOURCE into yellow / red / white / blue
        v
Pre-treatment of lab and microbiology waste on site (disinfection/sterilisation)
        |
        v
Bar-coded bags -> central storage (untreated waste not kept beyond 48 hours)
        |
        v
Common Bio-medical Waste Treatment Facility (CBWTF) -> treatment -> final disposal
```

## 12.6 Duties of the occupier (hospital, clinic, laboratory)

1. **Segregate at source** into the colour-coded containers; never mix BMW with general waste.
2. **Pre-treat** laboratory, microbiological waste and blood bags on site by disinfection or sterilisation as guided by WHO or national guidelines.
3. **Phase out chlorinated plastic bags and gloves** (blood bags excepted) as the rules require.
4. Use a **bar-code system** for bags and containers going out of the premises.
5. Hand over waste to a **CBWTF** (or treat it in its own authorised facility); **store untreated waste for no more than 48 hours**.
6. Provide **training** to all health workers and **immunise** them (for example, against hepatitis B and tetanus).
7. Keep **records** of generation, treatment and disposal; submit an **annual report** to the prescribed authority; report major accidents.
8. Obtain **authorisation** from the State Pollution Control Board.
9. Treat **liquid waste** separately so that it meets discharge standards.

## 12.7 Disposal of pharmaceutical and medical waste

### Pharma manufacture

- **Expired, rejected or returned medicines** and contaminated packaging are destroyed through **authorised incineration** (high-temperature, with records), or as the State Pollution Control Board allows; never dumped.
- **Cytotoxic and hormone** wastes are incinerated at high temperature in authorised facilities.
- Chemical and solvent wastes from synthesis fall under the **Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules**; effluents must be treated before discharge.
- Waste from **biological products** (vaccines, biotechnology) is decontaminated and handled as biomedical waste.

### Pharmacies

- Separate **expired, damaged and recalled medicines** from saleable stock, label them "not for sale", record them, and **return them to the supplier/manufacturer** for destruction (take-back), or hand them to an authorised agency.
- Sharps from in-store services (for example, blood glucose testing) go into a **puncture-proof container** and to a CBWTF.
- Never throw medicines in municipal bins or drains.
- **Counsel patients** on safe disposal of unused medicines.

### Homes

- Expired or unused medicines, used insulin needles and syringes, and soiled dressings are **domestic hazardous waste**.
- Do **not flush** medicines (except where specifically instructed) or burn them in the open.
- Put used needles in a **hard plastic or metal container** with a lid; return medicines to **take-back points** or pharmacies where available, or hand them separately to municipal collectors.

### Hospitals

- Full BMW system as described above; expired medicines and cytotoxic waste go into the **yellow** category for **incineration**; sharps in **white** containers; recyclable contaminated plastics in **red**; glass vials and ampoules in **blue**.

## Remember

- Blood and components are **drugs**; blood bank licence from the **State authority with DCGI's approval**; voluntary donation only.
- Donor: **18–65 years, ≥45 kg, Hb ≥12.5 g/dL**, interval **3 months (men), 4 months (women)**.
- Mandatory tests: **HIV 1/2, HBsAg, HCV, syphilis, malaria**, plus grouping and cross-match.
- Red cells **2–6 °C**; platelets **20–24 °C with agitation, 5 days**; FFP **−30 °C or colder**.
- BMW Rules **2016** under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**; authority = **State Pollution Control Board**.
- **Yellow** = anatomical, soiled, expired drugs (incineration); **Red** = recyclable plastics (autoclave, shred, recycle); **White** = sharps (puncture-proof); **Blue** = glass and metal implants.
- Untreated waste stored **≤48 hours**; bar-coding; annual report; segregation **at source**.

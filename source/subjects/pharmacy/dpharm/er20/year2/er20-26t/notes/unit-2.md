# Unit 2: Pharmacy Act, 1948 and Pharmacy Practice Regulations, 2015

This unit covers syllabus chapter 2: the Act that created the Pharmacy Council of India and the State Pharmacy Councils, education regulations, registration of pharmacists, offences, and the Pharmacy Practice Regulations, 2015. It is one of the most frequently examined chapters.

## 2.1 Objectives of the Pharmacy Act, 1948

The Act was passed on the advice of the Chopra Committee. Its main aims are:

1. To **regulate the profession and practice of pharmacy** in India.
2. To make sure that only **properly qualified persons** practise as pharmacists.
3. To set up the **Pharmacy Council of India (PCI)** to lay down **uniform minimum standards of pharmacy education**.
4. To set up **State Pharmacy Councils** (and Joint State Councils) to **register pharmacists** and keep their registers.
5. To punish unqualified persons who dispense medicines or falsely claim to be registered.

The PCI was constituted in **1949**. The Act extends to the whole of India.

## 2.2 Important definitions

| Term | Meaning (in simple words) |
| --- | --- |
| Central Council | The Pharmacy Council of India |
| State Council | A State Pharmacy Council set up under the Act (includes a Joint State Council where one exists) |
| Joint State Council | One council formed by agreement between two or more States |
| Register | The register of pharmacists kept by a State Council |
| Registered pharmacist | A person whose name is entered in the register of a State for the time being |
| Central Register | The all-India register of pharmacists kept by the PCI |
| Agreement | An agreement between States to form a Joint State Council |

## 2.3 Pharmacy Council of India

### Constitution (who the members are)

The PCI has three kinds of members: **elected**, **nominated** and **ex officio**.

| Type | Members |
| --- | --- |
| Elected by the University Grants Commission | Six teachers of pharmacy colleges, with at least one teacher each of pharmaceutical chemistry, pharmaceutics (pharmacy), pharmacology and pharmacognosy |
| Nominated by the Central Government | Six members, of whom at least four hold a degree or diploma in pharmacy or pharmaceutical chemistry and practise pharmacy |
| Elected by the medical council | One member (the Act names the Medical Council of India, which was replaced by the National Medical Commission in 2020) |
| Ex officio | Director General of Health Services; Drugs Controller (General) of India; Director of the Central Drugs Laboratory |
| Nominated by UGC and AICTE | One representative each |
| Elected by each State Council | One registered pharmacist from each State |
| Nominated by each State Government | One registered pharmacist from each State |

- The members elect a **President** and a **Vice-President** from among themselves.
- Elected and nominated members hold office for **five years** and may be re-elected or re-nominated.
- The Council appoints a **Registrar** (who acts as secretary) and other staff.
- An **Executive Committee** (President, Vice-President and some members) handles day-to-day work.

### Functions of the PCI

1. **Education Regulations** – prescribes the minimum standard of education for registration as a pharmacist: course of study, duration, practical training, subjects, examinations, staff, equipment and accommodation of institutions.
2. **Approval of courses and institutions** – an authority conducting a course may apply for approval; the Council approves it if it meets the regulations. Only an approved qualification leads to registration.
3. **Withdrawal of approval** – if an institution stops meeting the standards, the Council can withdraw approval after giving it a chance to explain (with the Central Government's role as the Act provides).
4. **Inspection** – appoints inspectors to inspect institutions and examinations, and reports to the Council.
5. **Qualifications from outside India** – decides whether foreign pharmacy qualifications can be accepted.
6. **Central Register of Pharmacists** – keeps an all-India register based on the State registers.
7. Advises the Central and State Governments on pharmacy education and practice; frames regulations such as the **Pharmacy Practice Regulations, 2015**.

## 2.4 Education Regulations

Under the Act the PCI has framed several Education Regulations for the diploma; the present one is **Education Regulations 2020 (ER-2020)**.

| Feature | ER-2020 (D.Pharm) |
| --- | --- |
| Entry qualification | 10+2 with physics, chemistry and biology or mathematics (as the regulations lay down) |
| Duration | Two academic years |
| Practical training | Not less than 500 hours, spread over at least three months, in a hospital or community pharmacy approved under the regulations |
| Subjects | Pharmaceutics, pharmaceutical chemistry, pharmacognosy, human anatomy and physiology, social pharmacy (first year); pharmacology, community pharmacy and management, biochemistry and clinical pathology, pharmacotherapeutics, hospital and clinical pharmacy, pharmacy law and ethics (second year) |
| Examinations | Yearly; theory and practical with sessional marks |
| Exit examination | PCI has notified a D.Pharm exit examination before registration; check its current status and dates |

The regulations also set staff qualifications, laboratory equipment, library and building requirements for an institution.

## 2.5 State Pharmacy Councils and Joint State Councils

### Constitution of a State Pharmacy Council

| Type | Members |
| --- | --- |
| Elected | Six registered pharmacists, elected by registered pharmacists of the State |
| Nominated by the State Government | Five members, of whom at least three hold a degree or diploma in pharmacy or pharmaceutical chemistry, or are registered pharmacists |
| Elected by the State Medical Council | One member |
| Ex officio | Chief administrative medical officer of the State; officer in charge of drugs control in the State; Government Analyst under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act (or as the Act names) |

- Members elect a **President** and **Vice-President**; term is **five years**.
- The Council appoints a **Registrar**, who keeps the register of pharmacists.

### Functions of a State Council

1. Prepare and maintain the **register of pharmacists** of the State.
2. Register qualified persons and issue certificates of registration.
3. Renew registrations and keep the register up to date.
4. Remove names for misconduct or other reasons, and restore names when justified.
5. Appoint **pharmacy inspectors** to inspect premises where drugs are compounded or dispensed.
6. Send copies of the register to the PCI.

### Joint State Pharmacy Council

Two or more States (or a State and a Union Territory) may make an **agreement** to form one Joint Council for all of them, for a fixed period that can be renewed. Its constitution is similar to a State Council, with members from each State in the agreement.

## 2.6 Registration of pharmacists

### Who can be registered

A person may apply for registration in a State if he or she:

1. Has reached the age set by the Act (18 years);
2. Resides or carries on business or profession of pharmacy in that State; and
3. Holds an **approved qualification** (D.Pharm or higher) and meets the conditions of the Education Regulations, including practical training (and the exit examination where it applies).

The first registers (when the Act started) also allowed persons with a fixed number of years of practical experience in compounding to be registered. That was a one-time provision.

### Steps

```text
Application to Registrar of State Council (with fee, documents)
          |
          v
Registrar checks qualification -> name entered in register
          |
          v
Certificate of registration issued -> renewal at intervals with fee
          |
          v
Name sent to PCI for the Central Register
```

### Removal from the register

The Executive Committee of a State Council may remove a name if the pharmacist:

- has been convicted of an offence that the Council considers involves moral turpitude or makes the person unfit to practise;
- has been found guilty of **infamous conduct** in a professional respect (professional misconduct) after an inquiry; or
- has got registration by fraud or by mistake; or fails to pay the renewal fee.

The person is given a chance to be heard. An **appeal** lies to the State Government within the period set by the Act. A name may be **restored** later if the Council is satisfied.

## 2.7 Offences and penalties

| Offence | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| Falsely claiming to be registered | Using the title "registered pharmacist" or similar words when not registered |
| Dispensing by an unregistered person | Compounding, preparing, mixing or dispensing any medicine on a medical practitioner's prescription by anyone other than a registered pharmacist (a medical practitioner may supply medicines to his own patients) |
| Failure to surrender the certificate | Not returning the certificate after removal from the register |
| Obstructing an inspector | Refusing entry or information to a pharmacy inspector |
| Employing unregistered persons | Employer allowing dispensing by an unregistered person is also punishable |

Punishment is a **fine, imprisonment, or both**, with heavier punishment for repeat offences. The amounts set in the 1948 Act have been changed by amendments, so **check the current Act for the exact figures**.

## 2.8 Pharmacy Practice Regulations, 2015

The PCI framed these regulations in **2015** under the Pharmacy Act. They lay down how a registered pharmacist must practise.

### Main points

1. **Definitions** – pharmacist, pharmacy practice, patient counselling, professional misconduct and so on.
2. **Duties of a pharmacist** – dispense only against a valid prescription where the law requires one; check the prescription for errors and interactions; counsel the patient on dose, use, storage and side-effects; keep records; maintain confidentiality.
3. **Display** – the registration certificate should be displayed in the pharmacy; the pharmacist should be identifiable (name badge, apron) as the regulations require.
4. **Patient medication records** and dispensing records to be kept for the period laid down.
5. **Continuing education** – pharmacists are expected to update their knowledge through continuing pharmacy education programmes.
6. **Code of ethics** – duties towards patients, prescribers, the public and the profession (detailed in Unit 8).
7. **Professional misconduct** – for example: allowing an unregistered person to dispense; dispensing a prescription-only medicine without prescription; substituting without consent; selling spurious or expired medicines; giving false certificates; advertising in an undignified way; accepting gifts or commissions to promote particular products; breaching confidentiality.
8. **Punishment** – a State Council may warn, suspend registration for a period, or remove the name, after an inquiry.

### Pharmacist roles named in the regulations

| Setting | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Community pharmacy | Dispensing, counselling, OTC advice, health promotion |
| Hospital pharmacy | Procurement, storage, inpatient and outpatient dispensing, drug information |
| Clinical pharmacy | Medication review, adverse drug reaction monitoring, rational use |
| Other | Drug information centres, public health programmes |

## Remember

- Pharmacy Act **1948**; PCI constituted **1949**; aim: regulate the profession and set **minimum standards of education**.
- PCI has **elected, nominated and ex officio** members; term **five years**; President and Vice-President elected by members.
- PCI frames **Education Regulations** (now ER-2020), approves courses and institutions, can withdraw approval, keeps the **Central Register**.
- State Councils keep the **register of pharmacists**, appoint pharmacy inspectors, remove names for misconduct (appeal to State Government).
- Only a **registered pharmacist** may dispense on prescription; claiming falsely to be registered is an offence.
- **Pharmacy Practice Regulations, 2015**: duties, records, counselling, professional misconduct and punishment.

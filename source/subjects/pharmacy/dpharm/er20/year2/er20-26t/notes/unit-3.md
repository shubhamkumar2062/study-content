# Unit 3: Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and Rules, 1945 – I (objectives, definitions, schedules, import, administration)

Syllabus chapter 3 is the biggest chapter in this subject (23 of 75 hours). In this guide it is split into two units. This unit covers the objectives, legal definitions, the list of schedules, import of drugs and the authorities that run the Act. Unit 4 covers manufacture, the important schedules in detail, sale, records and banned drugs.

## 3.1 Objectives of the Act

The Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 (with the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945 made under it) aims to:

1. Regulate the **import, manufacture, distribution and sale** of drugs and cosmetics in India.
2. Make sure that drugs sold are **safe, effective and of standard quality**, and that cosmetics are safe.
3. Prevent the sale of **misbranded, adulterated and spurious** drugs and cosmetics.
4. Regulate the **manufacture of Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani (ASU)** drugs for sale.
5. Set up **advisory and testing bodies** (DTAB, DCC, CDL) and appoint **analysts and inspectors** to enforce the law.

The Act was first passed as the Drugs Act, 1940; it was later amended to cover cosmetics and renamed. It has been amended many times (for example, the 2008 amendment made punishments for spurious drugs much heavier). Medical devices are now regulated under the **Medical Devices Rules, 2017** made under this same Act (Unit 10), and new drugs and clinical trials under the **New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019** (Unit 11).

## 3.2 Important definitions

### Drug

"Drug" is defined very widely. It includes:

1. All **medicines for internal or external use** of human beings or animals, and all substances used for the diagnosis, treatment, mitigation or prevention of any disease or disorder, including preparations applied to the body to repel insects such as mosquitoes;
2. Substances (other than food) intended to **affect the structure or any function** of the human body, or to destroy vermin or insects that cause disease;
3. Substances intended to be used as **components of a drug**, including empty gelatin capsules; and
4. **Devices** notified by the Central Government after consulting the DTAB (this is how medical devices came under the Act).

### Cosmetic

Any article meant to be **rubbed, poured, sprinkled or sprayed on, introduced into, or otherwise applied to the human body** for **cleansing, beautifying, promoting attractiveness or altering the appearance**, including any article meant to be a component of a cosmetic. **Soap is excluded.**

### Misbranded drug

A drug is misbranded if:

- it is coloured, coated, powdered or polished so that damage is hidden, or it is made to appear of better value than it is;
- it is **not labelled** in the prescribed manner; or
- its label or container carries any **false or misleading statement or claim**.

### Adulterated drug

A drug is adulterated if:

- it contains any **filthy, putrid or decomposed** substance;
- it was prepared, packed or stored under **insanitary conditions** where it may have become contaminated;
- its container is made of a **poisonous or harmful** substance that may make the contents harmful;
- it contains a **colour other than one prescribed**;
- it contains any **harmful or toxic** substance; or
- any substance has been **mixed** with it so as to reduce its quality or strength.

### Spurious drug

A drug is spurious if:

- it is imported or made under a **name that belongs to another drug**;
- it is an **imitation of, or a substitute for, another drug** or resembles it so as to deceive, or carries the name of another drug;
- its label carries the name of a manufacturer that is **fictitious or does not exist**;
- it has been **substituted** wholly or partly by another drug or substance; or
- it claims to be the product of a manufacturer who did not actually make it.

| Type | Key idea | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Misbranded | Wrong or misleading label or appearance | Tablet label without batch number or with a false claim |
| Adulterated | Quality spoiled by contamination or mixing | Syrup made in dirty conditions, containing fungus |
| Spurious | Fake identity | Chalk tablets sold in a famous brand's packing |
| Not of standard quality | Fails the official standard | Tablet with 80% of the labelled content |

Similar definitions exist for **misbranded, adulterated and spurious cosmetics**.

### Other definitions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Manufacture | Any process of making, altering, ornamenting, finishing, packing, labelling, breaking up or otherwise treating a drug or cosmetic with a view to its sale or distribution. Compounding or dispensing in the ordinary course of retail business is **not** manufacture |
| Patent or proprietary medicine | (for modern medicine) a drug presented in a form ready for use whose formula is not in the Indian Pharmacopoeia or another recognised pharmacopoeia; (for ASU) a ready-to-use drug whose formula is not in the authoritative ASU books |
| Ayurvedic, Siddha or Unani drug | A drug made exclusively according to the formulae in the authoritative books listed in the First Schedule |
| Government Analyst | An analyst appointed by the Central or State Government under the Act |
| Inspector | A drug inspector appointed under the Act |
| Standard quality | A drug that complies with the standards in the Second Schedule to the Act |

## 3.4 Import of drugs

### Classes of drugs and cosmetics prohibited from import

No person may import:

1. Drugs or cosmetics that are **not of standard quality**.
2. **Misbranded, adulterated or spurious** drugs, and misbranded or spurious cosmetics.
3. Drugs or cosmetics whose import has been **prohibited by notification** (for example, drugs not in the public interest or banned in the country of origin as the rules say).
4. Any drug that claims to **cure or prevent any disease listed in Schedule J**, or any other disease the Government notifies.
5. **Patent or proprietary medicines** that do not show the true formula or list of active ingredients and their quantities on the label.
6. Any drug or cosmetic that is **not labelled** as the rules require.
7. Cosmetics containing harmful ingredients that the Government has prohibited, and cosmetics tested on animals where the rules forbid such import.
8. Drugs imported without a **licence** where a licence is required.

### Import under licence or permit

```text
Foreign manufacturer and its products
        |  Registration certificate (Form 41) from the Central licensing authority (DCGI)
        v
Indian importer
        |  Import licence: Form 10 (drugs other than Schedule X)
        |                  Form 10A (Schedule X drugs)
        v
Imported through notified ports, checked by CDSCO port officers and customs
```

| Purpose | Permission |
| --- | --- |
| Commercial import | Registration certificate for the foreign site and products, plus an import licence (Form 10 / 10A) |
| Import for examination, test or analysis | A test licence (Form 11), for small quantities, not for sale |
| Import for personal use | Small quantities for the importer's own treatment are allowed under the rules, with a permit where required |
| Import of a new drug | Permission under the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019 |
| Import of medical devices | Licence under the Medical Devices Rules, 2017 |

Applications are now made online through the **SUGAM portal** of CDSCO. Form numbers and fees are revised from time to time; **check the current rules** before applying.

Conditions of an import licence include: storage under proper conditions, keeping records of import and sale, allowing inspection, testing samples if asked, and labelling with the importer's name and licence number.

## 3.5 Administration of the Act and Rules

```text
                   Central Government (Ministry of Health)
                    |               |                |
                 DTAB             DCC             CDL, Kolkata
          (technical advice)  (uniform working)  (appellate testing)
                    |
      CDSCO headed by Drugs Controller General of India
      (central licensing: imports, new drugs, Schedule C/C1 and blood products, devices)
                    |
           State Drugs Control Departments
      (licensing authorities, controlling authority, drug inspectors,
       State drug-testing laboratories with Government Analysts)
```

### Drugs Technical Advisory Board (DTAB)

- **Function:** advises the Central and State Governments on **technical matters** arising from the Act, and carries out other functions given to it.
- **Members:** ex officio members such as the **Director General of Health Services (Chairman)**, the Drugs Controller General of India, the Director of the Central Drugs Laboratory, the heads of national research institutes (for example, the Central Research Institute, Kasauli and the Indian Veterinary Research Institute), and the Presidents of the pharmacy and medical councils; plus nominated and elected members, including State drug control officers, pharmacy and medicine teachers, an industry representative and Government Analysts.
- Nominated and elected members hold office for **three years**.
- The DTAB is consulted before schedules are changed or a drug is banned.

### Central Drugs Laboratory (CDL), Kolkata

- Tests samples of drugs and cosmetics sent by **customs officers** (imports) and by **courts**.
- Acts as the **appellate laboratory**: when a party disputes a State Government Analyst's report, the court can send a sample to CDL, and its report is conclusive.
- Other central laboratories do the same work for particular products (for example, CRI Kasauli for vaccines and sera).

### Drugs Consultative Committee (DCC)

- **Members:** two representatives of the Central Government and **one representative of each State Government**.
- **Function:** advises the Central and State Governments and the DTAB on matters needed to secure **uniform administration of the Act across India**.
- It meets when the Central Government calls it and decides its own procedure.

### Government Analysts

- Appointed by the Central or State Government with qualifications set by the rules.
- They test samples sent by inspectors (or by others under the Act) and give a **report in the prescribed form**, stating whether the sample is of standard quality.
- Their report is evidence in court; the accused can challenge it, and the sample can then go to CDL.

### Licensing authorities

| Authority | Licences issued |
| --- | --- |
| Central Licensing Authority (DCGI) | Import, new drugs and clinical trials; approval of licences for Schedule C/C(1) products such as vaccines, sera, blood products, large volume parenterals and some biologicals; class C and D medical devices |
| State Licensing Authority | Manufacture licences for most drugs and cosmetics; all sale licences (retail and wholesale); class A and B medical devices |

### Controlling authority

The officer appointed by the State Government to control the licensing authority's work and the drug inspectors; in most States this is the **Drugs Controller** of the State.

### Drug Inspectors

**Qualifications:** a degree in pharmacy, pharmaceutical sciences or medicine (with the specialisations and experience the rules prescribe); for some inspections (for example, of biologicals) further experience is required.

**Powers** (within the area assigned):

1. Inspect any premises where drugs or cosmetics are made, stored, sold or distributed, and the materials and records there.
2. **Take samples** (paying the price and giving a receipt; sample divided into portions – one sent to the Government Analyst, one given to the person, others kept).
3. Search any place, person or vehicle where he has reason to believe an offence is being committed.
4. **Stop and seize** drugs, cosmetics, records and materials that he believes break the Act, and order that stock not be disposed of.
5. Examine records and registers, and require production of documents.

**Duties:** inspect licensed premises regularly as the rules require, check that licence conditions are followed, investigate complaints, send samples for testing, start prosecutions with the authority's sanction, and report to the controlling authority. An inspector must not disclose information obtained except in the course of duty.

## 3.6 Penalties (overview)

The Act provides graded punishments. The heaviest are for manufacturing or selling **spurious or adulterated drugs**: where such a drug is likely to cause death or grievous hurt, the 2008 amendment provides imprisonment of **not less than ten years, which may extend to life**, and a heavy fine. Lesser offences (not-of-standard-quality drugs, selling without a licence, failure to keep records) carry shorter imprisonment and fines. Special courts may try offences relating to spurious and adulterated drugs. Because amounts are revised, **check the current Act for exact figures**.

## Remember

- Objective: control **import, manufacture, distribution and sale** so that drugs are of standard quality.
- "Drug" includes components (empty capsules) and **notified devices**; "cosmetic" excludes **soap**.
- **Misbranded** = wrong label/appearance; **adulterated** = contaminated/mixed; **spurious** = fake identity.
- First Schedule = ASU books; Second Schedule = standards; Schedule **Y** replaced by NDCT Rules, 2019.
- Import licences: **Form 10** (non-X), **Form 10A** (Schedule X), registration certificate **Form 41**, test licence **Form 11**.
- **DTAB** = technical advice; **DCC** = uniform administration; **CDL Kolkata** = appellate laboratory.
- Drug inspectors can **inspect, sample, search and seize**.

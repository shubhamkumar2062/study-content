# SOAP note for a clinical case of asthma

**Aim:** To prepare and discuss a SOAP note for a clinical case of bronchial asthma and to check inhaler technique.

## Principle

Asthma control depends on regular inhaled corticosteroid, correct inhaler technique, trigger avoidance and adherence. Over-use of the reliever (salbutamol) is a sign of poor control. The SOAP note documents control, finds the cause of poor control and plans treatment changes and counselling.

## Requirements

Case details, prescription, placebo metered-dose inhaler and spacer, peak flow meter, spirometry report, references (GINA summary, STG), SOAP sheet.

## Procedure

1. Record symptoms: daytime symptoms, night waking, reliever use per week, activity limitation, attacks in the last year.
2. Record triggers, smoking or smoke exposure, other diseases (rhinitis, GERD), drugs (beta-blockers, NSAIDs).
3. Record PEF or FEV1, oxygen saturation, eosinophils.
4. **Ask the patient to demonstrate the inhaler** and note errors.
5. Assess control (well, partly, poorly controlled) and identify DRPs.
6. Write the plan: step up/down, technique correction, action plan, counselling.

## Observations (worked hypothetical case)

**Case:** Rahul, 19 years, college student, uses a salbutamol inhaler 5–6 times a week and wakes at night with cough twice a week. Prescribed a budesonide inhaler 3 months ago but "uses it only when breathless". Lives near a construction site. Demonstration: does not shake the inhaler, breathes in fast, no breath-hold.

| SOAP | Findings |
| --- | --- |
| **S** | Daytime symptoms more than twice a week, night waking, reliever use 5–6 times/week, ICS used only when breathless; dust exposure |
| **O** | PEF 70% of personal best; SpO2 97%; poor MDI technique observed |
| **A** | **Poorly controlled asthma**. DRPs: (1) **non-adherence to ICS** (controller used as reliever); (2) **poor inhaler technique**; (3) SABA over-use; (4) trigger exposure |
| **P** | Correct technique and add a spacer; daily ICS (or switch to ICS–formoterol as maintenance and reliever, as decided by the prescriber); review in 4–6 weeks before stepping up further; written asthma action plan; mask in dusty areas; influenza vaccine. Monitor reliever use, night symptoms, PEF diary |

**Counselling:** explain reliever versus preventer; preventer every day even when well; shake, breathe out, slow deep breath while pressing, hold 10 seconds; rinse mouth after ICS; seek care if reliever needed more often or not working.

## Result

Poor asthma control due to ICS non-adherence and wrong technique was identified; technique was corrected and a plan with an action plan was prepared.

## Precautions

Use placebo inhalers for demonstration; refer urgently if signs of a severe attack (cannot speak, cyanosis, silent chest).

## Viva questions

1. **Sign of poor asthma control?** Reliever use more than twice a week, night waking, activity limitation.
2. **Why rinse after ICS?** Prevent oral thrush.
3. **Breath-hold after MDI?** About 10 seconds.
4. **Why should LABA not be used alone in asthma?** Increases risk of severe attacks without treating inflammation.
5. **Use of a spacer?** Removes need for coordination; more drug to lungs, less in mouth.

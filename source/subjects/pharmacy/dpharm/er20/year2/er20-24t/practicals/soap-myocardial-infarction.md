# SOAP note for a clinical case of myocardial infarction

**Aim:** To prepare and discuss a SOAP note for a patient recovering from acute myocardial infarction, with emphasis on secondary prevention and discharge counselling.

## Principle

After MI, long-term drugs reduce re-infarction and death: antiplatelets, beta-blocker, ACE inhibitor/ARB, high-intensity statin (and aldosterone antagonist if heart function is low), with life-style change and cardiac rehabilitation ("ABCDE"). The pharmacist checks that each is present, at a suitable dose, and that the patient understands why.

## Requirements

Discharge summary or hypothetical case, ECG, troponin, echocardiography and lipid reports, prescription, references, SOAP sheet.

## Procedure

1. Summarise the event: date, type (STEMI/NSTEMI), reperfusion done (PCI/thrombolysis), complications.
2. Record history: risk factors, other diseases, allergies, current drugs.
3. Record vital signs, ejection fraction, creatinine, potassium, lipids, sugar.
4. Check the discharge prescription against the ABCDE list.
5. Look for interactions (e.g. clopidogrel with omeprazole, NSAIDs with antiplatelets).
6. Write assessment, plan, monitoring and discharge counselling.

## Observations (worked hypothetical case)

**Case:** Mr K, 48 years, smoker, admitted with anterior STEMI; primary PCI with stent done. Discharge drugs: aspirin, ticagrelor, metoprolol, ramipril, atorvastatin (high intensity), sublingual nitrate. He asks whether he can stop "blood thinners" after a month, and takes diclofenac for back pain.

| SOAP | Findings |
| --- | --- |
| **S** | Chest pain 5 days ago; smoker 20/day; back pain treated with diclofenac; wants to stop antiplatelets early |
| **O** | BP 124/78 mmHg; pulse 66/min; troponin was raised; EF 40%; LDL 168 mg/dL; creatinine and potassium normal |
| **A** | Post-STEMI with stent, reduced EF. Prescription covers ABCDE. DRPs: (1) **risk of premature stopping of dual antiplatelets** (stent thrombosis); (2) **NSAID use** with antiplatelets (bleeding, CV risk); (3) continued smoking |
| **P** | Continue aspirin + ticagrelor for the period advised (often 12 months), then aspirin long term; continue metoprolol, ramipril, atorvastatin; consider aldosterone antagonist for low EF (prescriber). Replace diclofenac with paracetamol. Smoking cessation support. Cardiac rehabilitation referral. Monitor BP, pulse, lipids at 6–8 weeks, liver enzymes, potassium and creatinine |

**Counselling:** never stop antiplatelets without the cardiologist's advice; report black stools, blood in urine or unusual bruising; carry SL nitrate – if chest pain is not relieved after one dose in 5 minutes call emergency; stop smoking completely; walk daily as advised; heart-healthy diet; avoid over-the-counter pain-killers like diclofenac and ibuprofen.

## Result

A post-MI SOAP note was prepared; NSAID use and the risk of stopping dual antiplatelet therapy were identified and corrected, and discharge counselling was planned.

## Precautions

1. Avoid NSAIDs in post-MI patients.
2. Check heart rate and BP before beta-blocker titration.
3. Maintain confidentiality; advice to prescriber only.

## Viva questions

1. **Marker for MI?** Cardiac troponin.
2. **Expand ABCDE.** Aspirin/ACE inhibitor, Beta-blocker/BP, Cholesterol/Cigarettes, Diet/Diabetes, Exercise/Education.
3. **Why is DAPT important after stent?** Prevents stent thrombosis.
4. **Name two thrombolytics.** Streptokinase, tenecteplase (or alteplase).
5. **Most common cause of early death after MI?** Arrhythmias.

# SOAP note for a clinical case of stroke

**Aim:** To prepare and discuss a SOAP note for a patient after an ischaemic stroke, focusing on secondary prevention.

## Principle

After ischaemic stroke the risk of another stroke is high. Secondary prevention includes an antiplatelet (or an anticoagulant if atrial fibrillation), a statin, BP and diabetes control, smoking cessation and rehabilitation. Haemorrhagic stroke must be excluded by CT before any antithrombotic.

## Requirements

Case details, CT/MRI report, ECG (atrial fibrillation), lipid profile, sugar, prescription, references, SOAP sheet.

## Procedure

1. Record onset, type of stroke, deficits (weakness, speech, swallowing), treatment given (thrombolysis).
2. Record risk factors: BP, diabetes, AF, smoking, lipids, previous TIA.
3. Record BP, ECG, lipids, HbA1c, swallowing assessment.
4. Check the prescription: antithrombotic choice, statin, antihypertensive, dosage form suitable for swallowing.
5. Identify DRPs, plan and counselling for patient and caregiver.

## Observations (worked hypothetical case)

**Case:** Mr V, 68 years, ischaemic stroke with right-sided weakness; CT showed infarct, no bleed. ECG shows atrial fibrillation. Discharge prescription: aspirin, atorvastatin, amlodipine. He has difficulty swallowing large tablets.

| SOAP | Findings |
| --- | --- |
| **S** | Weakness right side, slurred speech improving; difficulty swallowing tablets; caregiver is his daughter |
| **O** | BP 150/92 mmHg; pulse irregular; ECG atrial fibrillation; LDL 142 mg/dL; HbA1c 6.1% |
| **A** | Ischaemic stroke with atrial fibrillation. DRPs: (1) **anticoagulant indicated but not prescribed** – aspirin alone is inadequate for AF-related stroke prevention; (2) BP above target; (3) dosage-form problem (dysphagia) |
| **P** | Suggest oral anticoagulant (apixaban, dabigatran or warfarin, timing as decided by the neurologist) in place of aspirin; continue statin; optimise BP; dosage forms suitable for swallowing difficulty (crushable tablets or liquids where appropriate – not for modified-release forms); physiotherapy and speech therapy. Monitor BP, bleeding, INR if warfarin, kidney function |

**Counselling (patient and caregiver):** take the blood thinner exactly as advised; report black stools, blood in urine, gum bleeding or severe headache; with warfarin keep INR appointments and a consistent diet, check before new medicines; BE FAST warning signs – go to hospital immediately; control BP, stop smoking.

## Result

A post-stroke SOAP note was prepared; missing anticoagulation for atrial fibrillation, uncontrolled BP and a dosage-form problem were identified and a plan was proposed.

## Precautions

No antithrombotic before CT excludes bleeding; check whether a tablet can be crushed; caregiver education is essential.

## Viva questions

1. **First investigation in suspected stroke?** CT scan of the brain.
2. **Thrombolytic in ischaemic stroke and time window?** Alteplase within about 4.5 hours.
3. **Most important risk factor?** Hypertension.
4. **Why anticoagulant in AF?** AF causes clots in the heart that embolise to the brain.
5. **Expand BE FAST.** Balance, Eyes, Face, Arm, Speech, Time.

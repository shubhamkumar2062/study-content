# SOAP note for a clinical case of anaemia (iron deficiency anaemia)

**Aim:** To prepare and discuss a SOAP note for a clinical case of anaemia; iron deficiency anaemia is taken as the example (the same format suits megaloblastic anaemia).

## Principle

Iron deficiency anaemia is microcytic and hypochromic, confirmed by low ferritin. Management has two parts: replace iron (oral ferrous salts; IV iron if needed) and **find and treat the cause** (blood loss, hookworm, diet, pregnancy). Poor adherence because of GI upset and wrong timing with tea or calcium are common problems.

## Requirements

Case details, CBC with indices, serum ferritin, stool examination, prescription, references, SOAP sheet.

## Procedure

1. Record symptoms (fatigue, breathlessness, pica), menstrual history, diet, worm infestation, NSAID use, pregnancy.
2. Record pallor, koilonychia, Hb, MCV, MCH, ferritin, TIBC, stool for ova and occult blood.
3. Classify anaemia by severity and type; look for the cause.
4. Review the iron product (elemental iron content), timing and interactions.
5. Identify DRPs; plan therapy, monitoring and counselling.

## Observations (worked hypothetical case)

**Case:** Ms R, 17 years, student, tiredness, breathlessness on climbing stairs and craving to eat chalk. Heavy periods. Vegetarian. Started ferrous sulfate tablets but takes them with milk tea at breakfast; complains of constipation and black stools and wants to stop.

| SOAP | Findings |
| --- | --- |
| **S** | Fatigue, exertional breathlessness, pica, heavy periods, vegetarian diet; iron taken with tea; constipation; worried by black stools |
| **O** | Pallor, koilonychia; Hb 8.6 g/dL; MCV 68 fL; ferritin low; TIBC high; stool shows hookworm ova |
| **A** | Moderate iron deficiency anaemia due to menorrhagia, hookworm and low dietary iron. DRPs: (1) **reduced absorption** – iron taken with tea/milk; (2) **adverse effects** (constipation, black stools) leading to likely non-adherence; (3) **cause untreated** – hookworm, heavy periods |
| **P** | Take iron between meals or on an empty stomach with water/lemon juice (with food if upset); fluids and fibre for constipation; deworming with albendazole; gynaecology review for heavy periods; iron-rich diet; continue iron about 3 months after Hb is normal. Monitor Hb after 3–4 weeks (expect rise of about 1–2 g/dL) |

**Counselling:** black stools are expected and harmless; avoid tea, coffee and milk within about an hour of the tablet; vitamin C helps; continue the full course; keep tablets away from children; eat green leafy vegetables, pulses, jaggery, ragi.

## Result

A SOAP note for iron deficiency anaemia was prepared; incorrect administration, adverse effects threatening adherence and untreated causes were identified and addressed.

## Precautions

Always look for the cause of iron deficiency; IV iron only where anaphylaxis can be treated; in megaloblastic anaemia never give folic acid alone if B12 deficiency is possible.

## Viva questions

1. **Best test for iron stores?** Serum ferritin.
2. **Expected Hb rise with oral iron?** About 1–2 g/dL in 3–4 weeks.
3. **What improves iron absorption?** Vitamin C.
4. **Antidote for iron poisoning?** Desferrioxamine.
5. **Common cause of IDA in Indian adolescents?** Heavy periods, poor diet, hookworm.

# Patient counselling exercises using role plays

**Aim:** To practise and document patient counselling on the disease, medicines, life-style changes and monitoring parameters through role plays based on real or hypothetical cases (minimum five cases).

## Principle

Counselling is a two-way discussion that helps patients understand and follow their therapy. Good counselling uses simple language, open questions, demonstration where needed, checking understanding (**teach-back**) and written information. Role play lets students practise both as pharmacist and as patient, and the session is documented.

## Requirements

Case scenarios, prescriptions, demonstration devices (placebo inhalers, spacer, insulin pen, glucometer, eye drops), patient information leaflets, counselling documentation form, a quiet counselling area.

## Procedure

1. **Prepare:** read the case and prescription; list the key messages (about the disease, each drug, life-style, monitoring, warning signs).
2. **Open:** greet, introduce yourself, confirm the patient's identity, explain the purpose and time needed, ensure privacy.
3. **Assess:** use open questions – "What did the doctor tell you about this medicine?", "How do you take it at home?" (the three prime questions: what is it for, how to take it, what to expect).
4. **Counsel:** explain the disease simply; for each drug – name, purpose, dose and timing, how to take, duration, common side effects and what to do, serious side effects to report, storage, interactions (food, alcohol, other medicines).
5. **Demonstrate** devices and ask the patient to show back.
6. **Life-style:** diet, exercise, smoking, alcohol, sleep as relevant.
7. **Monitoring:** what to check, how often, and follow-up dates.
8. **Close:** teach-back ("Can you tell me how you will take this?"), give written leaflet, invite questions, give contact details.
9. **Document** on the form: patient details, topics covered, devices demonstrated, barriers found, patient's understanding, follow-up.

## Observations: suggested five role-play cases with key points

| Case | Key counselling points |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Newly diagnosed hypertension on amlodipine | Silent disease; daily tablet even when well; ankle swelling possible; salt below 5 g/day, exercise, weight loss; home BP log; follow-up in 4 weeks |
| 2. Type 2 diabetes starting insulin pen | Storage (2–8 °C, not frozen), priming, injection sites and rotation, hypoglycaemia recognition and Rule of 15, foot care, glucometer use |
| 3. Asthma with MDI and spacer | Reliever vs preventer, technique with spacer, rinse after ICS, triggers, action plan |
| 4. Tuberculosis on FDC tablets | Full course, daily dosing, orange urine harmless, report jaundice/vision change, contraception, cough hygiene, nutrition |
| 5. Depression starting an SSRI | Not addictive, 2–4 weeks for effect, early nausea, do not stop suddenly, avoid alcohol, report suicidal thoughts |
| Optional: warfarin after stroke / glaucoma eye drops | Bleeding signs, INR, consistent diet / drop technique, nasolacrimal occlusion, lifelong use |

## Result

Counselling sessions were conducted for at least five cases using role play, and the content, patient understanding and follow-up were documented.

## Precautions

Respect privacy and confidentiality; use the patient's language and avoid jargon; do not give information beyond your role – refer to the doctor when needed; be non-judgemental, especially for stigmatised conditions (HIV, TB, mental illness).

## Viva questions

1. **What is teach-back?** Asking the patient to explain or demonstrate in their own words to confirm understanding.
2. **Three prime questions?** What did the doctor tell you the medicine is for? How did the doctor tell you to take it? What did the doctor tell you to expect?
3. **Barriers to counselling?** Language, low literacy, hearing or vision problems, lack of time, privacy, stigma.
4. **Why written leaflets?** Patients forget much of verbal advice; leaflets reinforce it.
5. **What is discharge counselling?** Counselling at hospital discharge about medicines, devices, diet, warning signs and follow-up.

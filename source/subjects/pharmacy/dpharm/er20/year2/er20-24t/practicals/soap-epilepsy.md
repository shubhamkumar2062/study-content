# SOAP note for a clinical case of epilepsy

**Aim:** To prepare and discuss a SOAP note for a clinical case of epilepsy.

## Principle

Seizure control depends on the right antiepileptic for the seizure type, regular adherence, avoidance of triggers and attention to interactions and special groups (women who may become pregnant). Breakthrough seizures are most often due to missed doses, sleep loss, alcohol or interacting drugs.

## Requirements

Case details, seizure diary, EEG/MRI report, drug levels (if available), prescription, references, SOAP sheet.

## Procedure

1. Record seizure type, frequency, last seizure, triggers, adherence, other drugs (including contraceptives), pregnancy plans.
2. Record examination, EEG/imaging, sodium, liver tests, drug level.
3. Check suitability of the drug for seizure type and patient (age, sex), dose and interactions.
4. Identify DRPs, write plan, safety advice and counselling.

## Observations (worked hypothetical case)

**Case:** Ms A, 24 years, generalised tonic–clonic seizures, seizure-free for one year on sodium valproate. Recently married, planning pregnancy; started a combined oral contraceptive. Had one seizure last week after night duty and missing two doses.

| SOAP | Findings |
| --- | --- |
| **S** | Breakthrough seizure after sleep loss and two missed doses; wants pregnancy soon; on combined pill |
| **O** | Neurological exam normal; EEG generalised discharges; liver tests normal; weight gain 6 kg in a year |
| **A** | Generalised epilepsy with breakthrough seizure. DRPs: (1) **non-adherence and sleep deprivation**; (2) **valproate in a woman planning pregnancy** – high teratogenic risk; (3) no folic acid |
| **P** | Refer to neurologist for a planned switch to a safer drug (e.g. levetiracetam or lamotrigine) **before** conception, done gradually; start folic acid; note: lamotrigine levels fall with oestrogen-containing pills (dose review if switched). Sleep hygiene, avoid alcohol; pill box and phone reminders. Monitor seizure diary |

**Counselling:** never stop or change the drug suddenly; take at fixed times; if a dose is missed take it when remembered unless near the next dose; regular sleep; avoid alcohol; no driving, swimming alone or working at heights until seizure-free as advised; inform the doctor before pregnancy; family first aid (recovery position, nothing in mouth, call help if longer than 5 minutes).

## Result

A SOAP note for generalised epilepsy was prepared; non-adherence, sleep loss and valproate use in a woman planning pregnancy were identified, and a safe plan was suggested.

## Precautions

Never stop antiepileptics abruptly; check interactions with enzyme inducers; switching drugs only under specialist care.

## Viva questions

1. **Drug of choice for absence seizures?** Ethosuximide (or valproate).
2. **Why avoid valproate in pregnancy?** Neural tube defects and developmental problems.
3. **First drug in status epilepticus?** IV lorazepam or diazepam.
4. **Two adverse effects of phenytoin?** Gum hypertrophy, hirsutism (also ataxia).
5. **Which antiepileptic reduces the effect of oral contraceptives?** Carbamazepine (also phenytoin, phenobarbitone).

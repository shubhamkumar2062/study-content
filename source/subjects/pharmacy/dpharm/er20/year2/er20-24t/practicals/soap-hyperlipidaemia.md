# SOAP note for a clinical case of hyperlipidaemia

**Aim:** To prepare and discuss a SOAP note for a clinical case of hyperlipidaemia (dyslipidaemia).

## Principle

Raised LDL-cholesterol causes atherosclerosis. Management is based on the patient's overall cardiovascular risk: life-style change for all and a statin for those at higher risk or with very high LDL; fibrates for very high triglycerides. Secondary causes (hypothyroidism, diabetes, drugs) must be found and treated.

## Requirements

Case details, fasting lipid profile, sugar, TSH, liver function, prescription, references, SOAP sheet.

## Procedure

1. Record history: diet, alcohol, exercise, smoking, family history of early heart disease, other diseases and drugs.
2. Record BMI, waist, BP and the lipid profile; calculate non-HDL cholesterol (total – HDL).
3. Look for secondary causes.
4. Assess cardiovascular risk and decide the goal.
5. Review the prescribed lipid-lowering drug for suitability, interactions and adverse effects.
6. Write the assessment, plan and counselling.

## Observations (worked hypothetical case)

**Case:** Mr D, 45 years, office worker, BMI 28. Fasting lipids: total cholesterol 262, LDL 178, HDL 36, triglycerides 240 mg/dL. Complains of tiredness, weight gain and feeling cold. Prescribed atorvastatin. Drinks alcohol at weekends.

| SOAP | Findings |
| --- | --- |
| **S** | Tiredness, cold intolerance, weight gain; weekend alcohol; fried food, no exercise |
| **O** | BMI 28; BP 132/84; lipids as above; non-HDL 226 mg/dL; TSH 12 mIU/L (high) |
| **A** | Mixed dyslipidaemia. DRP: **secondary cause not treated** – hypothyroidism (raises LDL and increases statin muscle-toxicity risk) |
| **P** | Suggest levothyroxine for hypothyroidism and recheck lipids after the thyroid is normal; continue statin as per risk assessment. Non-drug: less saturated fat, no trans fat, more fibre, reduce alcohol (triglycerides), 150 minutes of exercise per week, weight loss. Monitor: lipids 6–8 weeks after changes, liver enzymes if symptoms, muscle symptoms |

**Counselling:** take the statin regularly (atorvastatin any time of day); report unexplained muscle pain, weakness or dark urine; continue diet and exercise – drugs are not a replacement; limit alcohol; regular lipid checks.

## Result

A SOAP note for mixed dyslipidaemia was prepared; untreated hypothyroidism was identified as a secondary cause, and a combined drug and life-style plan was suggested.

## Precautions

Fasting sample for triglycerides; statins avoided in pregnancy; check interactions (gemfibrozil, some macrolides, azole antifungals raise statin levels).

## Viva questions

1. **Main lipid target?** LDL-cholesterol.
2. **Low HDL level?** Below 40 mg/dL.
3. **Serious adverse effect of statins?** Myopathy/rhabdomyolysis.
4. **Drug for very high triglycerides?** Fenofibrate (and omega-3 fatty acids).
5. **Two secondary causes?** Hypothyroidism, diabetes (also nephrotic syndrome, alcohol, thiazides).

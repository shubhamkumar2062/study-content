# Dose calculation of selected drugs in paediatrics and geriatrics under various pathological conditions

**Aim:** To calculate doses of selected drugs for children and elderly patients, including dose adjustment in kidney disease, using simulated cases (minimum four cases).

## Principle

Children differ from adults in body size and in drug handling, and elderly patients often have reduced kidney function, lower body weight and more sensitivity to drugs. Doses are therefore calculated by **body weight (mg/kg)** or **body surface area (BSA)**, and drugs cleared by the kidney are adjusted using **creatinine clearance (CrCl)**. Age-based formulae (Young's, Clark's) are older methods, used only for learning or when weight-based data are not available.

## Requirements

Simulated case sheets, calculator, drug references (National Formulary of India, paediatric formulary, product information), BSA nomogram or formula.

## Formulae

| Method | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Weight-based | Dose = dose per kg × body weight (kg); do not exceed the adult dose |
| Clark's rule | Child dose = (weight in pounds ÷ 150) × adult dose |
| Young's rule (1–12 years) | Child dose = [age (years) ÷ (age + 12)] × adult dose |
| BSA (Mosteller) | BSA (m²) = √[height (cm) × weight (kg) ÷ 3600] |
| BSA-based | Child dose = (child's BSA ÷ 1.73) × adult dose |
| Cockcroft–Gault CrCl (mL/min) | [(140 – age) × weight (kg)] ÷ [72 × serum creatinine (mg/dL)]; × 0.85 for women |

## Procedure

1. Read the case: age, sex, weight, height, diagnosis, serum creatinine, other drugs.
2. Choose the method (weight, BSA or CrCl adjustment) recommended for the drug.
3. Calculate step by step with units; round to a measurable dose (tablet strength or mL of syrup).
4. Check the result against the maximum dose and the reference.
5. Convert the dose into practical instructions (mL, number of tablets, frequency).

## Observations / calculations (simulated cases)

**Case 1 – Paracetamol syrup for a febrile child.** Child 3 years, 14 kg. Usual dose 15 mg/kg per dose, every 6 hours as needed (no more than 4 doses in 24 hours). Syrup 120 mg/5 mL.
Dose = 15 × 14 = **210 mg**. Volume = 210 ÷ 120 × 5 = **8.75 mL**, give about **8.5–9 mL** (the nearest practical measure, using an oral syringe) every 6 hours as needed.

**Case 2 – Amoxicillin for pneumonia in a child.** Child 2 years, 12 kg; prescriber orders 45 mg/kg/day in 3 divided doses (check against current paediatric guidance). Suspension 250 mg/5 mL.
Daily dose = 45 × 12 = **540 mg/day** → per dose = 540 ÷ 3 = **180 mg** → volume = 180 ÷ 250 × 5 = **3.6 mL** three times daily.

**Case 3 – Age and BSA comparison (learning exercise).** Child 8 years, 25 kg, 125 cm; adult dose of a drug 500 mg.
Young's rule: 8 ÷ (8 + 12) × 500 = **200 mg**.
Clark's rule: 25 kg = 55 lb → 55 ÷ 150 × 500 = **183 mg**.
BSA = √(125 × 25 ÷ 3600) = √0.868 = **0.93 m²** → 0.93 ÷ 1.73 × 500 = **269 mg**.
Note how the methods differ; weight- or BSA-based doses from references are preferred.

**Case 4 – Elderly patient with reduced kidney function.** Woman 78 years, 50 kg, serum creatinine 1.4 mg/dL.
CrCl = [(140 – 78) × 50] ÷ (72 × 1.4) = 3100 ÷ 100.8 = **30.8 mL/min**; × 0.85 = **about 26 mL/min**.
Interpretation: moderate–severe reduction. Drugs cleared by the kidney need dose reduction or avoidance – for example **nitrofurantoin should be avoided** at this level, **metformin** needs dose reduction or stopping depending on eGFR, and many antibiotics need longer dosing intervals; check each drug in the reference.

**Case 5 (optional) – Elderly and hepatic disease.** Discuss why benzodiazepines such as diazepam are avoided in an elderly patient with cirrhosis (long-acting, accumulate, cause confusion and falls, can precipitate encephalopathy) and lorazepam or oxazepam in reduced dose is preferred if needed.

## Result

Doses for children were calculated by weight, age and BSA methods, and creatinine clearance was calculated to guide dose adjustment in an elderly patient with reduced kidney function.

## Precautions

1. Always use the child's actual weight; never exceed the adult dose.
2. Use oral syringes, not household spoons, for liquid doses.
3. Check calculations twice and with a reference; watch decimal points and units (mg vs mL).
4. In the elderly start low and go slow; review all drugs for kidney dosing and interactions.

## Viva questions

1. **Preferred basis for paediatric doses?** Body weight (mg/kg) or BSA.
2. **Young's rule?** Child dose = age ÷ (age + 12) × adult dose.
3. **Average adult BSA used in calculations?** 1.73 m².
4. **Formula for CrCl?** Cockcroft–Gault: (140 – age) × weight ÷ (72 × serum creatinine), × 0.85 for women.
5. **Why are elderly patients more sensitive to drugs?** Reduced kidney and liver function, less body water and muscle, more body fat, more drugs and diseases, more sensitive brain (falls, confusion).

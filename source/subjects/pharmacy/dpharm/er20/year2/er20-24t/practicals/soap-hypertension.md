# SOAP note for a clinical case of hypertension

**Aim:** To prepare and discuss a SOAP (Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan) note for a clinical case of essential hypertension and to identify drug-related problems and counselling needs.

## Principle

A SOAP note is a structured record of a patient's problem. **Subjective** data are what the patient reports, **objective** data are measured findings, the **assessment** is the pharmacist's judgement of the problem and the therapy, and the **plan** lists drug and non-drug measures, monitoring and counselling. In hypertension the aim is to reach the target BP (commonly below 140/90 mmHg, lower in diabetes or kidney disease if tolerated), protect target organs and improve adherence.

## Requirements

Case sheet or hypothetical case, prescription, laboratory reports, BP apparatus (sphygmomanometer and stethoscope or digital monitor), weighing scale, measuring tape, references (National Formulary of India, standard treatment guidelines), SOAP format sheet.

## Procedure

1. Read the case and list the patient's details (age, sex, occupation, weight, height).
2. Record the **subjective** data: chief complaints, duration, past history, medication history, allergies, diet (salt), smoking, alcohol, exercise, family history.
3. Record the **objective** data: BP (two readings, seated, after 5 minutes' rest), pulse, BMI, waist, laboratory values (creatinine, potassium, sugar, lipids, urine protein), ECG.
4. Classify the BP and identify risk factors and target-organ damage.
5. Review each prescribed drug for indication, dose, interactions, contraindications and adverse effects; list drug-related problems (DRPs).
6. Write the **assessment**: diagnosis, control status and DRPs.
7. Write the **plan**: drug therapy changes (as suggested to the prescriber), non-drug measures, monitoring parameters and follow-up, and counselling points.
8. Discuss the case with the teacher and record corrections.

## Observations (worked hypothetical case)

**Case:** Mr R, 52 years, clerk, comes for refill. Diagnosed with hypertension 1 year ago, on enalapril. Complains of a dry irritating cough for 2 months and occasional morning headache. Smokes 10 cigarettes a day; eats pickles daily; no exercise. Father had a stroke.

| SOAP | Findings |
| --- | --- |
| **S** | Dry cough 2 months, morning headache; smoker; high salt intake; sedentary; family history of stroke; takes medicine "only when headache comes" |
| **O** | BP 158/98 and 154/96 mmHg; pulse 82/min; weight 84 kg, height 1.70 m, BMI about 29 kg/m²; serum creatinine and potassium normal; fasting glucose 98 mg/dL; LDL 150 mg/dL |
| **A** | Stage 1 essential hypertension, **uncontrolled**. DRPs: (1) adverse drug reaction – dry cough due to the ACE inhibitor; (2) **non-adherence** (takes drug only when symptomatic); (3) untreated risk factors – smoking, obesity, salt, raised LDL |
| **P** | Suggest to prescriber: change enalapril to an **ARB** (e.g. losartan or telmisartan) – no cough; add amlodipine if BP stays above target. Consider a statin after cardiovascular risk assessment. Non-drug: salt below about 5 g/day, avoid pickles, brisk walk 30 min on most days, weight loss, stop smoking (refer to cessation support). Monitor: home BP log, review in 2–4 weeks, creatinine and potassium after starting/changing ARB. Counsel as below |

**Counselling points given:** hypertension is usually silent – take tablets daily even without headache; same time each day; do not double a missed dose; rise slowly; report dizziness, swelling of lips or face; avoid pain-killers (NSAIDs) and decongestants without advice; keep a BP diary.

## Result

A SOAP note for a case of uncontrolled essential hypertension was prepared; ACE-inhibitor cough and non-adherence were identified as drug-related problems, and a therapy plan and counselling were proposed.

## Precautions

1. Measure BP correctly – correct cuff size, arm at heart level, patient seated and rested, no smoking or caffeine 30 minutes before.
2. Keep patient information confidential.
3. Recommendations are made to the prescriber; the pharmacist does not change therapy alone.
4. Use generic names and current guidelines.

## Viva questions

1. **What does SOAP stand for?** Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan.
2. **Which antihypertensive causes dry cough?** ACE inhibitors (enalapril, ramipril) – due to bradykinin.
3. **Why switch to an ARB?** ARBs give the same benefit without cough.
4. **Target BP for most adults?** Below 140/90 mmHg; often below 130/80 in diabetes or kidney disease if tolerated.
5. **Name two first-line drug groups.** ACE inhibitors/ARBs, CCBs, thiazides (any two).
6. **Salt advice?** Less than about 5 g salt per day.

# SOAP note for a clinical case of tuberculosis

**Aim:** To prepare and discuss a SOAP note for a clinical case of pulmonary tuberculosis treated under the National TB Elimination Programme (NTEP).

## Principle

Drug-sensitive TB is treated with daily weight-band fixed-dose combinations: 2HRZE in the intensive phase and 4HRE in the continuation phase. Success depends on adherence, monitoring for hepatotoxicity and other adverse effects, nutrition, and testing for HIV and diabetes. Rifampicin interacts with many drugs.

## Requirements

Case details, sputum/CBNAAT report, chest X-ray, LFT, HIV and blood sugar results, weight, NTEP treatment card, references, SOAP sheet.

## Procedure

1. Record symptoms (cough, fever, weight loss), contact history, previous TB treatment, other diseases and drugs.
2. Record weight (for weight band), sputum/CBNAAT result (rifampicin sensitivity), X-ray, LFT, HIV, sugar.
3. Check regimen, weight-band dose and phase; check interactions (oral contraceptives, antiretrovirals, warfarin).
4. Identify DRPs and adverse effects; write plan, monitoring and counselling.

## Observations (worked hypothetical case)

**Case:** Mr J, 34 years, labourer, 46 kg. New sputum-positive pulmonary TB, rifampicin-sensitive. In the 6th week of treatment he complains of tingling in the feet and has stopped tablets for 4 days because "the urine is red". Drinks alcohol daily. Blood sugar 214 mg/dL (random).

| SOAP | Findings |
| --- | --- |
| **S** | Cough improving; tingling feet; stopped tablets for 4 days fearing red urine; daily alcohol |
| **O** | Weight 46 kg (weight band checked); LFT normal; random glucose 214 mg/dL, HbA1c 8%; HIV negative |
| **A** | New pulmonary TB, intensive phase. DRPs: (1) **treatment interruption** due to misunderstanding of harmless rifampicin colour; (2) **isoniazid neuropathy** – pyridoxine needed; (3) **untreated diabetes** (worsens TB outcome); (4) alcohol – raises hepatotoxicity risk |
| **P** | Restart treatment at once (adherence support, treatment supporter); add pyridoxine; refer for diabetes management; alcohol cessation counselling; nutrition support (Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana); follow-up sputum at end of intensive phase as per programme; monitor LFT symptoms, vision, weight monthly |

**Counselling:** orange-red urine, sweat and tears are harmless; complete the full 6 months; take daily at the same time; report yellow eyes, vomiting, loss of appetite, blurred vision, rash; stop alcohol; cover mouth when coughing; screen family contacts.

## Result

A SOAP note for pulmonary TB was prepared; treatment interruption, isoniazid neuropathy, undiagnosed diabetes and alcohol use were identified and managed.

## Precautions

Check weight-band dose; never give a single anti-TB drug alone; notify the case; maintain confidentiality and avoid stigma.

## Viva questions

1. **NTEP regimen for drug-sensitive TB?** 2HRZE/4HRE daily.
2. **Why pyridoxine?** Prevents isoniazid-induced peripheral neuropathy.
3. **Most hepatotoxic first-line drug?** Pyrazinamide.
4. **Which test detects rifampicin resistance quickly?** CBNAAT (GeneXpert) or TrueNat.
5. **Ethambutol toxicity?** Optic neuritis (red-green colour blindness).

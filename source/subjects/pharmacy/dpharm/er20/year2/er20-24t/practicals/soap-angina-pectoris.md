# SOAP note for a clinical case of angina pectoris

**Aim:** To prepare and discuss a SOAP note for a clinical case of stable angina pectoris.

## Principle

Stable angina is chest pain due to temporary myocardial ischaemia on exertion. Therapy aims to relieve attacks (sublingual nitrate), prevent attacks (beta-blocker or CCB, long-acting nitrate) and prevent MI and death (aspirin, statin, risk-factor control). The SOAP note helps check that all three aims are covered and that the patient can use the nitrate correctly.

## Requirements

Case sheet, prescription, ECG and lipid reports, BP apparatus, references (NFI, standard treatment guidelines), demonstration sublingual tablet bottle or spray, SOAP sheet.

## Procedure

1. Note patient details and the nature of chest pain (site, radiation, duration, trigger, relief).
2. Record risk factors: smoking, hypertension, diabetes, lipids, family history, obesity.
3. Record vital signs, ECG (rest/stress), lipid profile, sugar.
4. Check whether the prescription has (a) a reliever, (b) an anti-anginal preventer, (c) aspirin and a statin.
5. Look for interactions (nitrate with sildenafil) and contraindications (beta-blocker in asthma).
6. Write assessment, plan and counselling; discuss with the teacher.

## Observations (worked hypothetical case)

**Case:** Mrs S, 60 years, diabetic, gets chest tightness on climbing stairs that goes after 3–4 minutes' rest. Prescribed isosorbide mononitrate and sublingual nitroglycerin. She sometimes swallows the sublingual tablet. She keeps the tablets in a pill box in her purse.

| SOAP | Findings |
| --- | --- |
| **S** | Exertional chest tightness for 3 months, relieved by rest; type 2 diabetes for 8 years on metformin; non-smoker; tablets kept in a pill box |
| **O** | BP 146/90 mmHg; pulse 88/min; ECG: ST depression on stress test; LDL 132 mg/dL; HbA1c 7.8% |
| **A** | Stable angina; BP and diabetes not at target. DRPs: (1) **no antiplatelet and no statin** (indication without drug); (2) **wrong technique** – swallowing the SL tablet; (3) **improper storage** of nitroglycerin; (4) no beta-blocker for symptom prevention |
| **P** | Suggest: low-dose aspirin, high-intensity statin, beta-blocker (e.g. metoprolol) to prevent attacks and lower heart rate; ACE inhibitor for diabetes with high BP. Keep the nitrate-free interval for isosorbide mononitrate. Non-drug: graded walking, diet, weight control. Monitor: frequency of attacks, pulse, BP, lipids, HbA1c |

**Counselling:** at onset of pain sit down and put one tablet **under the tongue** (do not swallow); if not better in 5 minutes take another and call an ambulance; headache is common; keep tablets in the **original airtight glass bottle**, away from heat and light; never with sildenafil-type drugs; report pain at rest or more frequent attacks.

## Result

The SOAP note identified missing preventive therapy and incorrect nitrate use and storage, and a plan with counselling was prepared.

## Precautions

1. Unstable or rest pain must be referred urgently.
2. Check BP and pulse before advising beta-blockers.
3. Recommendations go to the prescriber.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is the SL nitrate placed under the tongue?** Rapid absorption, bypasses first-pass metabolism.
2. **What is nitrate tolerance?** Loss of effect with continuous use; prevented by a nitrate-free interval.
3. **Drug of choice in variant angina?** Calcium channel blocker.
4. **Why add aspirin and a statin?** To prevent MI and death.
5. **Main interaction of nitrates?** PDE-5 inhibitors (sildenafil) – severe hypotension.

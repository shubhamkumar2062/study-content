# Unit 4: Endocrine disorders – diabetes mellitus and thyroid disorders

Syllabus chapter 2(c), 5 hours. India has one of the largest numbers of people with diabetes in the world, so this is a very important topic for the exam and for community pharmacy practice.

## 4.1 Diabetes mellitus

### Definition

**Diabetes mellitus** is a group of metabolic disorders characterised by **chronic hyperglycaemia** (high blood glucose) resulting from a **defect in insulin secretion, insulin action, or both**, with disturbed metabolism of carbohydrate, fat and protein.

### Classification

| Type | Cause | Typical patient |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Type 1** | Autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells → absolute insulin deficiency | Children and young adults; thin; prone to ketoacidosis; insulin is essential |
| **Type 2** (about 90% or more) | Insulin resistance + progressive fall in insulin secretion | Adults (now younger too); overweight, inactive, family history |
| **Gestational diabetes** | Glucose intolerance first found in pregnancy | Pregnant women; higher later risk of type 2 |
| **Other specific types** | Pancreatic disease, drugs (steroids, thiazides, some antipsychotics), Cushing's syndrome, genetic forms (MODY) | — |

### Risk factors for type 2 diabetes

Family history, obesity (especially abdominal – Indians develop diabetes at lower body weight), physical inactivity, unhealthy diet, age above 30–35 years, history of gestational diabetes, polycystic ovary syndrome, hypertension, dyslipidaemia.

### Pathogenesis

```text
Type 1:  Genetic risk + trigger (virus?) → autoimmune attack on beta cells
         → no insulin → glucose cannot enter cells → hyperglycaemia,
           fat breakdown → ketones → diabetic ketoacidosis

Type 2:  Obesity + inactivity + genes → insulin resistance (muscle, liver, fat)
         → beta cells work harder → slowly fail
         → liver releases more glucose + less uptake by muscle → hyperglycaemia
```

### Clinical manifestations

- Classical "3 P's": **polyuria** (frequent urination), **polydipsia** (excess thirst), **polyphagia** (excess hunger), plus weight loss (especially type 1), tiredness, blurred vision.
- Slow wound healing, repeated infections (skin, urinary, fungal), tingling of feet.
- Many type 2 patients have **no symptoms** and are found on screening.

### Diagnostic criteria (any one; repeat to confirm if no symptoms)

| Test | Diabetes | Prediabetes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fasting plasma glucose (8 hours fasting) | **126 mg/dL or more** | 100–125 mg/dL (impaired fasting glucose) |
| 2-hour plasma glucose after 75 g oral glucose (OGTT) | **200 mg/dL or more** | 140–199 mg/dL (impaired glucose tolerance) |
| HbA1c | **6.5% or more** | 5.7–6.4% |
| Random plasma glucose with classical symptoms | **200 mg/dL or more** | — |

**HbA1c** (glycated haemoglobin) reflects average blood glucose over the last **2–3 months** and is used to monitor control.

### Complications

| Acute | Chronic microvascular | Chronic macrovascular |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hypoglycaemia** (usually from drugs) | **Retinopathy** (blindness) | Coronary artery disease, MI |
| **Diabetic ketoacidosis** (mainly type 1) | **Nephropathy** (proteinuria → kidney failure) | Stroke |
| Hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state (type 2, elderly) | **Neuropathy** (numb, painful feet; autonomic problems) | Peripheral artery disease → **diabetic foot**, gangrene |

### Goals of therapy (common targets; individualise)

- HbA1c **below 7%** for most adults (less strict in elderly or those prone to hypoglycaemia).
- Fasting glucose about 80–130 mg/dL; after meals below about 180 mg/dL.
- BP below about 130/80; LDL-cholesterol at target with statins; no smoking.

### Non-drug management

1. **Medical nutrition therapy:** regular meals, controlled carbohydrate portions, more fibre (whole grains, pulses, vegetables), less sugar, sweets, refined flour and fried foods; limit alcohol.
2. **Physical activity:** at least about 150 minutes a week of moderate exercise (brisk walking) + some muscle-strengthening.
3. **Weight loss** in overweight patients – even 5–10% improves control.
4. **Stop smoking.**
5. **Self-monitoring of blood glucose** with a glucometer; regular eye, foot, kidney and lipid checks.

### Drug management

#### Type 1 diabetes

**Insulin is essential for life.** Common plan: **basal-bolus** – a long-acting insulin once daily plus a rapid-acting insulin before meals; or premixed insulin twice daily; or an insulin pump.

| Insulin type | Examples | Onset / duration (approx.) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rapid-acting | Insulin lispro, aspart, glulisine | 10–15 min / 3–5 h |
| Short-acting | Regular (soluble) insulin – the only one used IV | 30 min / 6–8 h |
| Intermediate | NPH (isophane) | 1–2 h / 12–18 h |
| Long-acting | Glargine, detemir, degludec | 1–2 h / about 24 h or longer, no peak |
| Premixed | 30/70 regular + NPH | — |

#### Type 2 diabetes

**Metformin** is the usual **first drug** (unless contraindicated), together with lifestyle change. Other drugs are added according to the patient:

| Group | Examples | How it works | Notes / adverse effects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Biguanide** | Metformin | ↓ liver glucose output, ↑ insulin sensitivity | GI upset, B12 deficiency; no weight gain; no hypoglycaemia alone; avoid in severe kidney disease (lactic acidosis) |
| Sulfonylureas | Glimepiride, gliclazide, glipizide | ↑ insulin release | **Hypoglycaemia**, weight gain; cheap |
| Meglitinides | Repaglinide | ↑ insulin release (short) | Taken with meals |
| DPP-4 inhibitors (gliptins) | Sitagliptin, vildagliptin, teneligliptin | ↑ incretin effect | Weight neutral; low hypoglycaemia risk |
| **SGLT2 inhibitors** (gliflozins) | Dapagliflozin, empagliflozin | ↑ glucose loss in urine | Genital fungal infections, dehydration; protect heart and kidney |
| **GLP-1 receptor agonists** | Liraglutide, semaglutide, dulaglutide | ↑ insulin, ↓ glucagon, ↓ appetite | Weight loss; nausea; injections (oral semaglutide exists) |
| Thiazolidinediones | Pioglitazone | ↑ insulin sensitivity | Weight gain, oedema, avoid in heart failure |
| Alpha-glucosidase inhibitors | Acarbose, voglibose | Slow carbohydrate digestion | Flatulence |
| Insulin | — | Replaces insulin | When oral drugs fail, very high sugar, pregnancy, acute illness |

**Choice by patient:** heart disease or heart failure or kidney disease → add an **SGLT2 inhibitor or GLP-1 agonist** with proven benefit; need to lose weight → GLP-1 agonist or SGLT2 inhibitor; cost a concern → sulfonylurea. **Gestational diabetes:** diet and exercise first, then **insulin** (metformin in selected cases).

### Hypoglycaemia (a must-know emergency)

Blood glucose **below about 70 mg/dL**. Symptoms: sweating, tremor, palpitations, hunger, anxiety; then confusion, abnormal behaviour, fits, unconsciousness. Causes: missed meal, extra exercise, too much insulin or sulfonylurea, alcohol, kidney failure. **Beta-blockers can hide the warning symptoms** (except sweating).

**Rule of 15:** if conscious, give about **15 g of fast sugar** (glucose tablets, 3 teaspoons of sugar in water, half a glass of fruit juice), recheck after 15 minutes, repeat if still low, then a snack. If unconscious: **IV dextrose** or **glucagon** injection; nothing by mouth.

### Counselling points

- Take metformin with or after food; sulfonylureas before breakfast; do not skip meals.
- Insulin: correct storage (refrigerator 2–8 °C, never freeze; the vial or pen in use may be kept at room temperature for the time given on the leaflet), rotate injection sites, prime pens, roll cloudy insulin gently.
- Recognise and treat hypoglycaemia; always carry sugar and a diabetes card.
- **Foot care:** inspect feet daily, wear proper footwear, never walk barefoot, cut nails straight.
- Sick-day rules: never stop insulin during illness; check sugar more often; drink fluids.
- Yearly eye, kidney and foot check-up.

## 4.2 Thyroid disorders

The thyroid gland makes **thyroxine (T4)** and **triiodothyronine (T3)** under the control of **TSH** from the pituitary (which in turn is controlled by TRH from the hypothalamus). Iodine is needed to make the hormones.

```text
Hypothalamus → TRH → Pituitary → TSH → Thyroid → T4, T3
       ↑_______________ negative feedback ________________|
```

| Test | Hypothyroidism (primary) | Hyperthyroidism (Graves') |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TSH | **High** | **Low** (suppressed) |
| Free T4 / T3 | Low | High |

### 4.2.1 Hypothyroidism

**Definition:** a clinical state resulting from **deficient production of thyroid hormones**, slowing down body metabolism. Severe form in adults = **myxoedema**; in infants = **congenital hypothyroidism** (cretinism) causing mental and growth retardation if not treated early.

**Causes:** **Hashimoto's (autoimmune) thyroiditis** – most common where iodine intake is adequate; **iodine deficiency** (still seen in some hill areas); after thyroid surgery or radioactive iodine; drugs – **lithium, amiodarone**, antithyroid drugs; pituitary disease (secondary).

**Clinical manifestations:** tiredness, **weight gain**, **cold intolerance**, constipation, dry coarse skin, hair loss, hoarse voice, slow pulse, puffy face, slow speech and thinking, depression, heavy periods and infertility, high cholesterol; goitre in some.

**Non-drug management:** use of iodised salt (prevention of iodine-deficiency disease); diet and exercise for weight.

**Drug management:** **levothyroxine (L-thyroxine) daily for life**. Start with a low dose and increase slowly in the **elderly and in heart disease** (to avoid angina); dose is adjusted by **TSH every 6–8 weeks** until normal, then yearly. Dose often needs to **increase in pregnancy**.

**Counselling:** take on an **empty stomach**, about 30–60 minutes before breakfast, with water; keep **4 hours apart from iron, calcium, antacids** and soy which reduce absorption; do not stop; report palpitations, chest pain, tremor or weight loss (signs of too much dose); benefits appear over weeks.

### 4.2.2 Hyperthyroidism (thyrotoxicosis)

**Definition:** a clinical state resulting from **excess thyroid hormone**, speeding up body metabolism.

**Causes:** **Graves' disease** (autoimmune; antibodies stimulate the TSH receptor) – most common; toxic multinodular goitre; toxic adenoma; thyroiditis; excess thyroxine intake; iodine excess.

**Clinical manifestations:** **weight loss despite good appetite**, **heat intolerance**, sweating, **palpitations and fast pulse** (may cause atrial fibrillation), tremor, anxiety, irritability, insomnia, frequent stools, light periods, muscle weakness, goitre. Graves' disease specially: **exophthalmos** (bulging eyes) and pretibial myxoedema. **Thyroid storm** is a life-threatening worsening (high fever, very fast pulse, confusion) after infection or surgery.

**Management:**

| Option | Details |
| --- | --- |
| **Antithyroid drugs (thionamides)** | **Carbimazole / methimazole** (first choice), **propylthiouracil** (preferred in the first trimester of pregnancy and in thyroid storm); usually 12–18 months. Adverse effects: rash, liver injury, and rare but serious **agranulocytosis** |
| **Beta-blocker** | **Propranolol** – quick relief of palpitations, tremor and anxiety while antithyroid drugs act |
| **Radioactive iodine (I-131)** | Destroys thyroid tissue; not in pregnancy or breastfeeding; often leads to hypothyroidism later |
| **Surgery (thyroidectomy)** | Large goitre, suspicion of cancer; patient made euthyroid first |
| Iodide (Lugol's solution) | Short use before surgery and in thyroid storm |

**Counselling (antithyroid drugs):** report **sore throat, fever or mouth ulcers immediately** (possible agranulocytosis – a blood count is needed); report yellow eyes or dark urine; take regularly; thyroid tests are repeated to adjust the dose; avoid excess iodine; protect the eyes (sunglasses, lubricating drops) in Graves' eye disease.

### Hypo- vs hyperthyroidism – quick comparison

| Feature | Hypothyroidism | Hyperthyroidism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weight | Gain | Loss |
| Temperature | Cold intolerance | Heat intolerance, sweating |
| Pulse | Slow | Fast, palpitations |
| Bowel | Constipation | Frequent stools |
| Skin | Dry, coarse | Warm, moist |
| Mood | Slow, depressed | Anxious, irritable |
| TSH (primary disease) | High | Low |
| Main drug | Levothyroxine | Carbimazole + propranolol |

## Remember

- Diagnosis of diabetes: FPG 126 or more, 2-h OGTT 200 or more, HbA1c 6.5% or more, random 200 or more with symptoms (mg/dL).
- Type 1 → insulin always. Type 2 → lifestyle + metformin first; add SGLT2 inhibitor or GLP-1 agonist in heart or kidney disease.
- Target HbA1c below 7% for most; treat hypoglycaemia with 15 g fast sugar and recheck after 15 minutes.
- Hypothyroidism: high TSH; levothyroxine empty stomach, apart from iron and calcium; start low in the elderly.
- Hyperthyroidism: low TSH; carbimazole (PTU in early pregnancy and thyroid storm) + propranolol; watch for sore throat (agranulocytosis).

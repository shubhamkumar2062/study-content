# Unit 2: Cardiovascular disorders

Syllabus chapter 2(a), 8 hours: hypertension, angina pectoris and myocardial infarction, hyperlipidaemia and congestive heart failure. For every disease learn the same five headings: definition, causes and risk factors (etiopathogenesis), clinical manifestations, non-drug management, drug management, plus counselling points.

## 2.1 Hypertension

### Definition

**Hypertension** is a persistent rise of arterial blood pressure, usually taken as a systolic BP of **140 mmHg or more** and/or a diastolic BP of **90 mmHg or more**, confirmed on two or more readings on separate occasions.

### Classification (JNC 7, widely used in Indian textbooks; adults)

| Category | Systolic (mmHg) | | Diastolic (mmHg) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | below 120 | and | below 80 |
| Prehypertension | 120–139 | or | 80–89 |
| Stage 1 hypertension | 140–159 | or | 90–99 |
| Stage 2 hypertension | 160 or more | or | 100 or more |

Newer American guidelines (2017) call 130–139/80–89 "stage 1", so you may see both systems. A sudden very high BP (often 180/120 or more) is a **hypertensive crisis**: an **emergency** if organs are being damaged (brain, heart, kidney, eyes), an **urgency** if not.

### Types and causes

| Type | Share | Causes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary (essential)** | About 90–95% | No single cause; genes + environment |
| **Secondary** | About 5–10% | Kidney disease, renal artery stenosis, primary aldosteronism, Cushing's syndrome, phaeochromocytoma, thyroid disease, coarctation of aorta, pregnancy, drugs (oral contraceptives, NSAIDs, steroids, decongestants) |

**Risk factors:** older age, family history, obesity, high salt intake, low potassium intake, physical inactivity, alcohol, smoking, stress, diabetes, dyslipidaemia.

### Pathogenesis (in simple words)

BP = **cardiac output × peripheral resistance**. Hypertension develops when either rises and stays high:

```text
Salt retention, obesity, stress
      ↓
↑ Sympathetic activity   ↑ Renin → angiotensin II → aldosterone
      ↓                          ↓                  ↓
↑ Heart rate & force     Vasoconstriction    Na+ and water retention
      ↓                          ↓                  ↓
      └──────→  ↑ Cardiac output and ↑ peripheral resistance  ←──────┘
                                  ↓
                             ↑ Blood pressure
```

### Clinical manifestations

Usually **no symptoms** ("silent killer"); found on routine check. Some patients have headache (often in the morning at the back of the head), giddiness, palpitations, tiredness or nose bleeds. **Target organ damage:** left ventricular hypertrophy and heart failure, coronary artery disease, stroke, kidney damage (proteinuria, chronic kidney disease), hypertensive retinopathy, peripheral arterial disease.

### Goals of therapy

Reduce the risk of stroke, heart attack, heart and kidney failure. A common target is **below 140/90 mmHg**, and a lower target (often below 130/80) for many high-risk patients such as those with diabetes or kidney disease, if tolerated.

### Non-drug management (for every patient)

| Measure | Advice |
| --- | --- |
| Salt restriction | Less than about 5 g salt per day; avoid pickles, papad, chips, processed foods |
| DASH-type diet | Fruits, vegetables, low-fat dairy, whole grains; less saturated fat |
| Weight reduction | Aim for a healthy BMI |
| Physical activity | Brisk walking about 30 minutes on most days |
| Alcohol | Limit or stop |
| Smoking | Stop completely |
| Stress | Yoga, meditation, adequate sleep |

### Drug management

**First-line groups:** (1) ACE inhibitors (enalapril, ramipril) or ARBs (losartan, telmisartan); (2) calcium channel blockers (amlodipine); (3) thiazide or thiazide-like diuretics (hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone, indapamide). **Beta-blockers** (metoprolol, atenolol, bisoprolol) are used when there is another reason (angina, after MI, heart failure, fast heart rate).

| Condition | Preferred drug(s) |
| --- | --- |
| Diabetes, proteinuria, chronic kidney disease | ACE inhibitor or ARB |
| Elderly, isolated systolic hypertension | CCB or thiazide |
| Angina or after MI | Beta-blocker, ACE inhibitor |
| Heart failure | ACE inhibitor/ARB, beta-blocker, diuretic, aldosterone antagonist |
| Pregnancy | Methyldopa, labetalol, nifedipine (ACE inhibitors and ARBs are contraindicated) |
| Benign prostatic hyperplasia | Alpha-blocker may be added (prazosin) |
| Resistant hypertension | Add spironolactone |

**Stepwise approach:** start one drug (or two drugs in low dose for higher BP), increase the dose or add a second drug from another group; common effective pairs are ACE inhibitor/ARB + CCB or + thiazide. Do **not** combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB. **Hypertensive emergency** is treated in hospital with IV drugs (for example labetalol, nitroglycerin, sodium nitroprusside), lowering BP gradually rather than suddenly.

### Important adverse effects

| Drug group | Adverse effects |
| --- | --- |
| ACE inhibitors | Dry cough, angioedema, high potassium, first-dose hypotension |
| ARBs | High potassium, dizziness (no cough) |
| CCBs (amlodipine) | Ankle oedema, flushing, headache |
| Thiazides | Low potassium, high uric acid (gout), high blood sugar |
| Beta-blockers | Slow pulse, tiredness, cold hands; avoid in asthma |

### Counselling points

Take medicines daily even when you feel well; check BP regularly and keep a record; reduce salt; rise slowly from bed (postural dizziness); report a persistent dry cough or swelling of the lips (ACE inhibitor); avoid pain-killers (NSAIDs) and decongestants without advice.

## 2.2 Angina pectoris

### Definition

**Angina pectoris** is chest pain or discomfort caused by temporary **myocardial ischaemia** – the heart muscle gets less oxygen than it needs – without permanent damage to the muscle.

### Etiopathogenesis

Most cases are due to **atherosclerosis** of the coronary arteries. Risk factors: age, male sex, family history, smoking, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidaemia, obesity, inactivity.

```text
Atherosclerotic plaque narrows coronary artery
            ↓
Exercise, emotion, cold, heavy meal → ↑ O2 demand
            ↓
Demand > supply → ischaemia → chest pain (angina)
            ↓
Rest / nitrate → demand falls → pain goes
```

| Type | Feature |
| --- | --- |
| **Stable (classical) angina** | Brought on by exertion, relieved by rest or sublingual nitrate within minutes |
| **Unstable angina** | New, worsening, or at rest; plaque rupture with partial clot; an emergency (part of acute coronary syndrome) |
| **Variant (Prinzmetal) angina** | At rest, often at night; due to coronary spasm |

### Clinical manifestations

Tight, heavy, squeezing or burning pain behind the sternum, spreading to the left arm, jaw, neck or back; lasts a few minutes; with breathlessness, sweating. ECG may show ST depression during pain or on a stress test.

### Non-drug management

Stop smoking, control BP, sugar and cholesterol, regular graded exercise, weight loss, avoid heavy meals and sudden exertion, stress control. Coronary revascularisation (angioplasty with stent, or bypass surgery) when drugs fail.

### Drug management

| Purpose | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| **Relief of an attack** | Sublingual **nitroglycerin** (glyceryl trinitrate) tablet or spray, or isosorbide dinitrate |
| **Prevent attacks** | **Beta-blockers** (first choice in stable angina; not in variant angina), **CCBs** (first choice in variant angina), long-acting nitrates (isosorbide mononitrate, with a nitrate-free period to avoid tolerance), ivabradine, ranolazine, nicorandil, trimetazidine |
| **Prevent MI and death** | Low-dose **aspirin** (clopidogrel if aspirin is not tolerated), a **statin**, an ACE inhibitor in diabetes or reduced heart function |

### Counselling (sublingual nitrate)

Sit down, put one tablet under the tongue; if pain is not better in 5 minutes, take another and call for emergency help (the pain may be a heart attack). Expect headache and flushing. Keep tablets in the original airtight glass bottle away from heat and light. **Never use sildenafil or tadalafil with nitrates** (severe fall in BP).

## 2.3 Myocardial infarction (MI)

### Definition

**Myocardial infarction** is **death (necrosis) of part of the heart muscle** caused by prolonged ischaemia, usually because a **thrombus forms on a ruptured atherosclerotic plaque** and blocks a coronary artery. MI and unstable angina together are called **acute coronary syndrome (ACS)**.

| Type | ECG |
| --- | --- |
| STEMI | ST-segment elevation; usually complete block of the artery |
| NSTEMI | No ST elevation; troponin raised |

### Clinical manifestations

Severe crushing central chest pain lasting **more than 20–30 minutes**, not relieved by rest or nitrate, spreading to arm or jaw; sweating, nausea, vomiting, breathlessness, fear. Elderly and diabetics may have "silent" MI with little pain.

**Diagnosis:** ECG changes and a rise in cardiac biomarkers, especially **troponin I or T** (CK-MB is also used).

**Complications:** arrhythmias (most common cause of early death), heart failure, cardiogenic shock, rupture, pericarditis, clot in the heart, re-infarction.

### Management

**Immediate ("time is muscle"):**

1. Call emergency help; rest; oxygen if oxygen level is low.
2. **Aspirin** chewed as early as possible (a loading dose of about 150–325 mg is commonly used) plus a second antiplatelet (clopidogrel or ticagrelor).
3. **Nitroglycerin** sublingual/IV for pain (unless BP is low); morphine for severe pain.
4. **Reperfusion** in STEMI: primary PCI (angioplasty) if available quickly; otherwise **thrombolysis** (streptokinase, tenecteplase, alteplase, reteplase) – the earlier the better.
5. **Anticoagulant** – heparin or enoxaparin.

**Long-term secondary prevention ("ABCDE")**

| Letter | Measure |
| --- | --- |
| A | Aspirin (+ second antiplatelet for up to a year), ACE inhibitor or ARB |
| B | Beta-blocker; BP control |
| C | Cholesterol – high-intensity statin; cigarettes – stop |
| D | Diet and diabetes control |
| E | Exercise (cardiac rehabilitation), education |

Aldosterone antagonist (eplerenone, spironolactone) if heart function is reduced.

### Counselling

Never stop antiplatelets without the doctor's advice (stent clot risk); report black stools or bleeding; carry sublingual nitrate; join cardiac rehabilitation; complete smoking cessation; resume work and sexual activity as advised.

## 2.4 Hyperlipidaemia (dyslipidaemia)

### Definition

**Hyperlipidaemia** is a raised level of lipids in blood – total cholesterol, LDL-cholesterol and/or triglycerides – often with low HDL-cholesterol. Because it is a major cause of atherosclerosis, "dyslipidaemia" is the preferred term.

### Lipid values (fasting, mg/dL; commonly used cut-offs)

| Lipid | Desirable | High |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total cholesterol | below 200 | 240 or more |
| LDL-cholesterol ("bad") | below 100 (lower in high-risk patients) | 160 or more |
| HDL-cholesterol ("good") | 60 or more is protective | below 40 is low |
| Triglycerides | below 150 | 200 or more; 500 or more risks pancreatitis |

### Causes

- **Primary (genetic):** for example familial hypercholesterolaemia (LDL receptor defect).
- **Secondary:** diabetes, obesity, hypothyroidism, nephrotic syndrome, chronic kidney disease, alcohol, liver disease (cholestasis), drugs (thiazides, beta-blockers, steroids, some antiretrovirals, oral contraceptives), diet high in saturated and trans fat.

### Pathogenesis and manifestations

High LDL enters the artery wall, is oxidised and taken up by macrophages (foam cells) → fatty streak → plaque → narrowing and rupture → angina, MI, stroke, peripheral artery disease. Usually no symptoms; signs in severe cases: **xanthomas** (yellow lumps on tendons or skin), **xanthelasma** (yellow patches on eyelids), **corneal arcus** in young people; very high triglycerides can cause pancreatitis.

### Non-drug management

Diet low in saturated fat and free of trans fat; more fibre, fruits, vegetables, nuts; weight loss; regular exercise (raises HDL); stop smoking; limit alcohol (especially for high triglycerides); treat secondary causes.

### Drug management

| Group | Examples | Main effect | Key adverse effects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Statins (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors)** – first choice | Atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin | ↓ LDL most | Muscle pain, rarely rhabdomyolysis; raised liver enzymes; contraindicated in pregnancy |
| Cholesterol absorption inhibitor | Ezetimibe | ↓ LDL (added to statin) | Diarrhoea |
| Fibrates | Fenofibrate, gemfibrozil | ↓ triglycerides, ↑ HDL | Muscle injury with statins (gemfibrozil more) |
| Bile acid sequestrants | Cholestyramine, colesevelam | ↓ LDL | Constipation, bloating; bind other drugs |
| Niacin (nicotinic acid) | — | ↓ TG, ↑ HDL | Flushing, high sugar, liver injury |
| Omega-3 fatty acids | Fish oil | ↓ triglycerides | Fishy burps |
| PCSK9 inhibitors | Evolocumab, alirocumab | Large ↓ LDL | Injection-site reactions; costly |

### Counselling

Take simvastatin at night (short-acting statins work best at night; atorvastatin and rosuvastatin any time); report unexplained muscle pain or dark urine; avoid large amounts of grapefruit juice with some statins; continue diet and exercise; lipids are checked after starting therapy.

## 2.5 Congestive heart failure (CHF)

### Definition

**Heart failure** is a clinical syndrome in which the heart **cannot pump enough blood to meet the needs of the body**, or can do so only at high filling pressures. "Congestive" refers to the congestion of lungs and body tissues with fluid.

### Causes

Coronary artery disease and previous MI (most common), long-standing hypertension, valve disease, cardiomyopathy, arrhythmias, anaemia, thyroid disease. **Precipitating factors:** infection, uncontrolled BP, stopping medicines, excess salt or fluid, NSAIDs, some CCBs (verapamil, diltiazem), arrhythmias.

| Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) | Weak contraction; EF about 40% or less |
| Preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) | Stiff ventricle, poor filling |
| Left-sided | Lung congestion → breathlessness |
| Right-sided | Body congestion → oedema, raised neck veins, liver enlargement |

### Pathogenesis

```text
Damaged heart → ↓ cardiac output
        ↓
Body "compensates": ↑ sympathetic activity, ↑ renin–angiotensin–aldosterone
        ↓
Vasoconstriction + Na+/water retention + remodelling of heart
        ↓
More load on a weak heart → further damage (vicious cycle)
```

Most modern drugs work by **blocking this harmful compensation**.

### Clinical manifestations

Breathlessness on exertion, **orthopnoea** (breathless lying flat), **paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea**, fatigue, ankle and leg oedema, weight gain from fluid, raised jugular venous pressure, enlarged liver, crackles in the lungs, fast heart rate. NYHA class I–IV grades how much activity causes symptoms. BNP or NT-proBNP and echocardiography help diagnosis.

### Non-drug management

Salt restriction; fluid restriction in advanced cases; **daily weight** (a gain of about 2 kg in 2–3 days means fluid retention – contact the doctor); stop smoking and alcohol; vaccination against influenza and pneumococcus; regular light exercise when stable; avoid NSAIDs.

### Drug management (HFrEF)

| Drug group | Examples | Benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ACE inhibitor / ARB / ARNI** | Enalapril, ramipril; losartan; sacubitril–valsartan | Reduce death and admissions |
| **Beta-blocker** (start low, go slow) | Carvedilol, bisoprolol, metoprolol succinate | Reduce death |
| **Aldosterone antagonist** | Spironolactone, eplerenone | Reduce death; watch potassium |
| **SGLT2 inhibitor** | Dapagliflozin, empagliflozin | Reduce death and admissions, with or without diabetes |
| **Loop diuretic** | Furosemide, torsemide | Relieve congestion and oedema (symptoms) |
| Digoxin | — | Fewer admissions; controls rate in atrial fibrillation; narrow therapeutic index |
| Ivabradine, hydralazine + isosorbide dinitrate | — | In selected patients |

**Digoxin toxicity:** nausea, vomiting, loss of appetite, yellow–green vision, arrhythmias; more likely with low potassium, kidney failure, elderly. Acute heart failure is treated in hospital with oxygen, IV furosemide, nitrates and inotropes (dobutamine) if needed.

### Counselling

Weigh yourself every morning; limit salt; take diuretic in the morning; report swelling, breathlessness or rapid weight gain; report dizziness; do not use pain-killers like ibuprofen; know signs of low potassium (cramps, weakness) and of digoxin toxicity.

## Remember

- Hypertension: 140/90 or more; mostly essential; silent; first-line = ACE inhibitor/ARB, CCB, thiazide; ACE inhibitor/ARB in diabetes and kidney disease; methyldopa/labetalol/nifedipine in pregnancy.
- Angina: SL nitroglycerin for attack; beta-blocker (stable) or CCB (variant) for prevention; aspirin + statin to protect.
- MI: troponin rise; aspirin early, reperfusion (PCI or thrombolysis), then ABCDE secondary prevention.
- Lipids: statins first; LDL is the main target; fibrates for high triglycerides.
- CHF (reduced EF): ACE inhibitor/ARB/ARNI + beta-blocker + aldosterone antagonist + SGLT2 inhibitor reduce death; loop diuretics relieve symptoms; daily weight.

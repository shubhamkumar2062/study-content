# Unit 1: Introduction to pharmacotherapeutics, rational use of medicines, evidence-based medicine, essential medicines and standard treatment guidelines

This unit is syllabus chapter 1 (8 hours). It gives the ideas you will use in every later unit: what pharmacotherapeutics is, how to choose and use medicines rationally, how evidence is graded, and how essential medicines lists and standard treatment guidelines help the whole country use medicines well.

## 1.1 Pharmacotherapeutics: meaning

**Pharmacotherapeutics** is the branch of pharmacy and medicine that deals with the **safe, effective and economical use of drugs to prevent and treat disease** in individual patients. It joins three things:

- knowledge of the **disease** (cause, how it develops, signs and symptoms),
- knowledge of the **drug** (pharmacology, dose, side effects, interactions), and
- knowledge of the **patient** (age, weight, kidney and liver function, pregnancy, other diseases, other medicines, beliefs and ability to pay).

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Pharmacology | Study of how drugs act on the body and how the body handles drugs |
| Pharmacotherapy | The actual treatment of a disease with drugs |
| Pharmacotherapeutics | The study and practice of choosing and using drug therapy for a patient |
| Etiopathogenesis | Etiology (cause) + pathogenesis (how the disease develops) |
| Clinical manifestations | Symptoms (what the patient feels) + signs (what the examiner finds) |

## 1.2 Scope of pharmacotherapeutics

1. **Choosing the right drug** for a diagnosed disease, using guidelines and evidence.
2. **Individualising therapy** – adjusting drug and dose for children, the elderly, pregnancy, lactation, kidney or liver disease.
3. **Monitoring therapy** – checking that the drug works (efficacy) and does no harm (safety) using clinical signs and laboratory tests.
4. **Detecting and preventing drug-related problems** – adverse drug reactions, interactions, duplication, wrong dose, poor adherence.
5. **Patient counselling** – explaining the disease, the medicines, life-style changes and warning signs.
6. **Supporting other healthcare workers** – the pharmacist gives drug information to doctors and nurses.
7. **Public health** – rational use, antimicrobial stewardship, national programmes (TB, malaria, HIV, anaemia).

## 1.3 Objectives

- To understand the etiopathogenesis of common diseases.
- To learn evidence-based drug therapy and non-drug measures for each disease.
- To prepare individualised therapeutic plans based on diagnosis.
- To assess clinical outcomes of therapy (goals reached or not).
- To identify drug-related problems and suggest interventions.
- To plan and give patient and discharge counselling.

## 1.4 Assessing a patient: subjective and objective data

In practice the pharmacist collects two kinds of information. This is the basis of the **SOAP note** used in the practical (ER20-24P).

| Part | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **S** – Subjective | What the patient (or relative) says | Chief complaint, history of present illness, past medical and medication history, allergies, social history (smoking, alcohol), family history |
| **O** – Objective | What can be measured or observed | Vital signs (BP, pulse, temperature, respiratory rate), weight and BMI, physical examination, laboratory values, ECG, X-ray |
| **A** – Assessment | Your judgement | Diagnosis, severity, whether present therapy is working, drug-related problems |
| **P** – Plan | What will be done | Drug therapy, non-drug measures, monitoring, counselling, follow-up |

**Drug-related problems (DRPs)** commonly looked for:

| DRP | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Indication without drug | Diabetic with protein in urine but no ACE inhibitor |
| Drug without indication | Antibiotic for a viral cold |
| Wrong drug | Drug that the patient is allergic to |
| Dose too low / too high | Same adult dose given to a child |
| Adverse drug reaction | Dry cough with enalapril |
| Drug interaction | Warfarin with a drug that raises its effect |
| Non-adherence | Patient stops antihypertensive when he "feels well" |

## 1.5 Rational use of medicines (RUM)

**Definition (WHO, Nairobi conference, 1985):** rational use of medicines means that patients receive medicines **appropriate to their clinical needs**, in **doses that meet their own individual requirements**, for an **adequate period of time**, and at the **lowest cost** to them and their community.

### The "rights" of rational use

```text
Right patient  →  Right diagnosis  →  Right drug (effective, safe, affordable)
      →  Right dose  →  Right route  →  Right time and duration
      →  Right information to the patient  →  Right monitoring
```

### Steps of rational prescribing (WHO "Guide to Good Prescribing")

1. Define the patient's problem (diagnosis).
2. Specify the therapeutic objective (what do you want to achieve?).
3. Choose a suitable treatment – check that your **P-drug** (personal drug of choice, chosen in advance on efficacy, safety, suitability and cost) suits this patient.
4. Start the treatment – write a complete, legible prescription.
5. Give information, instructions and warnings.
6. Monitor (and stop) the treatment.

### Common types of irrational use

| Type | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Polypharmacy | Too many drugs per prescription, often duplicates |
| Overuse of antibiotics | For viral fever, cough, diarrhoea |
| Overuse of injections | When an oral drug would work |
| Irrational fixed-dose combinations | Combinations with no proven benefit |
| Not following guidelines | Older or costly drug when a guideline drug exists |
| Self-medication | Prescription-only drugs bought without a prescription |
| Wrong dose or duration | Antibiotic stopped after two days |
| Overuse of tonics and vitamins | Without a deficiency |

### Causes of irrational use

- **Patients:** belief that injections and costly drugs work better, demand for antibiotics, poor knowledge.
- **Prescribers:** lack of training, lack of time, pressure from patients, influence of drug promotion, fear of losing patients.
- **Health system:** shortage of drugs, no guidelines, poor regulation, unqualified sellers.
- **Industry:** aggressive promotion and incentives.

### Consequences

Treatment failure, adverse effects, antimicrobial resistance, waste of money, loss of patient trust, and shortage of essential drugs.

### Strategies to promote rational use

1. **Educational** – training of prescribers and pharmacists, public education.
2. **Managerial** – essential medicines lists, standard treatment guidelines, drug and therapeutics committees, prescription audits.
3. **Regulatory** – banning irrational combinations, prescription-only schedules (Schedule H, H1, X), control of promotion.
4. **Economic** – generic medicines (for example Jan Aushadhi stores), price control.

**Role of the pharmacist:** dispense only valid prescriptions, check doses and interactions, counsel, report adverse reactions (Pharmacovigilance Programme of India), refuse antibiotics without prescription, advise on generic substitution where allowed.

## 1.6 Evidence-based medicine (EBM)

**Definition:** evidence-based medicine is the **careful, open and sensible use of the best evidence available today** when deciding how to treat an individual patient. It combines three things:

```text
          Best research evidence
                   /\
                  /  \
                 / EBM\
                /______\
   Clinical expertise    Patient values and preferences
```

### Five steps of EBM (the "5 A" approach)

1. **Ask** – frame a clear clinical question (PICO: Patient/Problem, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome).
2. **Acquire** – search for the evidence (PubMed, Cochrane Library, guidelines).
3. **Appraise** – check the quality, validity and relevance of the evidence.
4. **Apply** – use it for the patient, together with expertise and the patient's wishes.
5. **Assess** – evaluate the result and improve next time.

### Hierarchy (levels) of evidence

| Level (strongest first) | Type of study |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Systematic reviews and meta-analyses of randomised controlled trials (RCTs) |
| 2 | Individual well-designed RCTs |
| 3 | Cohort studies |
| 4 | Case–control studies |
| 5 | Case series and case reports |
| 6 | Expert opinion, editorials, animal and laboratory studies |

```text
            /\        Systematic reviews / meta-analyses
           /  \       Randomised controlled trials
          /    \      Cohort studies
         /      \     Case-control studies
        /        \    Case series / case reports
       /__________\   Expert opinion, animal studies
```

**Advantages of EBM:** decisions based on proof rather than habit; better outcomes; avoids useless or harmful treatments; supports guidelines. **Limitations:** good evidence is not available for every question; trials may not include children, the elderly or pregnant women; needs time and skill to appraise; may ignore individual differences if applied blindly.

## 1.7 Essential medicines and the Essential Medicines List

**Definition (WHO):** essential medicines are those that **satisfy the priority health-care needs of the population**. They are selected with regard to disease prevalence and public health relevance, evidence of efficacy and safety, and comparative cost-effectiveness. They should be available at all times, in adequate amounts, in appropriate dosage forms, with assured quality and at a price the individual and the community can afford.

- WHO published its **first Model List of Essential Medicines in 1977** and revises it every two years.
- WHO also publishes a separate list for children.
- The WHO list now classifies antibiotics as **Access, Watch and Reserve (AWaRe)** to guide antibiotic use.

### National List of Essential Medicines (NLEM), India

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| First NLEM | 1996 |
| Latest | NLEM 2022 – 384 medicines |
| Prepared by | Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, through an expert committee |
| Levels of care | P – primary, S – secondary, T – tertiary |
| Price control | Medicines in the NLEM come under price control (Drugs Prices Control Order, 2013, through the NPPA) |

### Criteria for selecting an essential medicine

1. Relevant to the disease pattern of the country.
2. Proven efficacy and safety.
3. Cost-effective compared with other drugs of the same class.
4. Stable under storage conditions; suitable dosage forms available.
5. Usually a single-ingredient medicine; fixed-dose combinations only when clearly better (for example antitubercular or antiretroviral combinations).
6. Usually listed by **generic name**.

### Benefits of an essential medicines list

- Better access and lower cost; easier procurement and storage.
- Basis for training, standard treatment guidelines and drug information.
- Promotes rational prescribing and generic medicines.
- Improves quality of care and reduces waste.

## 1.8 Standard Treatment Guidelines (STGs)

**Definition:** standard treatment guidelines are **systematically developed statements** that help prescribers decide on the most appropriate treatment for a specific clinical condition. They list the preferred drug (first choice and alternatives), dose, duration, non-drug measures, and when to refer.

**Typical contents of an STG for one disease:** definition, diagnostic criteria, non-drug management, drug treatment (first-line, second-line), doses and duration, precautions, patient counselling, referral criteria.

**Examples in India:** the national programme guidelines (National TB Elimination Programme, National Center for Vector Borne Diseases Control for malaria, National AIDS Control Programme for HIV), the ICMR **Standard Treatment Workflows**, and state STGs.

### Advantages of STGs

| For | Benefit |
| --- | --- |
| Patients | Consistent, effective and affordable treatment |
| Prescribers | Ready reference; protection when treatment follows accepted practice |
| Pharmacists | Basis for checking prescriptions, stocking and counselling |
| Health managers | Predictable drug needs; easier procurement; audit tool |
| Country | Less irrational use and less antimicrobial resistance |

### Disadvantages / limitations

- Need regular updating as evidence changes.
- Cannot cover every patient; clinical judgement is still needed.
- Useless if not distributed and followed.

### Relationship between EML, STGs and RUM

```text
Evidence (EBM)  →  Standard Treatment Guidelines  →  Essential Medicines List
       ↓                         ↓                             ↓
           all three support Rational Use of Medicines
```

The STG tells **which drug** to use for each disease; the EML ensures that **those drugs are available**; EBM supplies the **proof** behind both.

## 1.9 Individualised therapy and outcome assessment

The same disease may need different drugs in different patients. Factors to consider:

- **Age:** children need weight-based doses; elderly have lower kidney function and more side effects (falls, confusion).
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** avoid drugs harmful to the fetus (for example ACE inhibitors, warfarin, valproate).
- **Kidney and liver disease:** reduce doses of drugs cleared by these organs.
- **Other diseases:** beta-blockers may worsen asthma; thiazides may raise blood sugar.
- **Other drugs:** interactions.
- **Genetics, allergies, cost and patient preference.**

**Outcome assessment** compares the result with the goal set at the start – for example BP below target, HbA1c below target, sputum smear negative after TB treatment, fewer seizures – and records any adverse effects. If the goal is not met, check adherence first, then dose, then the choice of drug.

## Remember

- Pharmacotherapeutics = disease + drug + patient knowledge used together.
- RUM (WHO 1985): right drug, right dose, right duration, lowest cost.
- WHO's six steps of good prescribing; the P-drug concept.
- EBM = best evidence + clinical expertise + patient values; 5 A's; meta-analysis of RCTs is the strongest evidence.
- WHO first EML 1977; India NLEM first 1996, latest 2022 (384 medicines), listed by generic name, with P/S/T levels.
- STGs say which drug, dose and duration to use; EML makes the drug available; both promote RUM.
- SOAP = Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan.

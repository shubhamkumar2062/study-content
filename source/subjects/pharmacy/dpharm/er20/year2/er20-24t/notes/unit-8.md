# Unit 8: Infectious diseases I – tuberculosis, pneumonia, urinary tract infections and hepatitis

Syllabus chapter 2(g) has 12 hours and eight diseases, so this guide splits it into two units. This unit covers **tuberculosis, pneumonia, urinary tract infections and viral hepatitis**; Unit 9 covers gonorrhoea, syphilis, malaria, HIV and viral infections. General principles of antimicrobial use (culture before antibiotics, narrowest effective drug, full course) apply to every infection; see also Unit 12 on antimicrobial resistance.

## 8.1 Tuberculosis (TB)

### Definition

**Tuberculosis** is a chronic infectious disease caused by ***Mycobacterium tuberculosis*** (an acid-fast bacillus, AFB), most often affecting the **lungs (pulmonary TB)**, but able to affect any organ (**extrapulmonary TB** – lymph nodes, pleura, bones and spine, brain/meninges, abdomen, kidneys).

### Etiopathogenesis

- **Spread:** by **airborne droplet nuclei** when a patient with pulmonary TB coughs, sneezes or talks.
- **Risk factors:** close contact with a TB patient, **HIV infection** (strongest risk), diabetes, malnutrition, smoking, alcohol, overcrowding, silicosis, immunosuppressive drugs (steroids, anti-TNF), old age.

```text
Inhaled bacilli → alveolar macrophages
        ↓
Cell-mediated immunity forms a granuloma (Ghon focus); caseous necrosis
        ↓
Most people: infection contained → latent TB infection (not infectious)
        ↓  weak immunity (HIV, diabetes, malnutrition, old age)
Reactivation → active TB → cavities in lungs → infectious sputum
```

### Clinical manifestations

- **Cough for 2 weeks or more** (key symptom for testing), with sputum, sometimes blood (haemoptysis).
- **Fever, especially evening rise; night sweats; loss of weight and appetite**; tiredness; chest pain.
- Extrapulmonary: swollen neck nodes, back pain and deformity (spine), headache and neck stiffness (meningitis).

**Diagnosis:** sputum smear microscopy for AFB (Ziehl–Neelsen staining); **rapid molecular tests (CBNAAT/GeneXpert, TrueNat)**, which also detect **rifampicin resistance**; culture and drug-susceptibility testing; chest X-ray; tuberculin skin test or IGRA for latent infection.

### Management under the National TB Elimination Programme (NTEP)

**Principles:** use a **combination** of drugs (prevents resistance), for an **adequate duration**, with **daily fixed-dose combinations (FDCs)** dosed by **weight band**, under **supervision and adherence support**, with free diagnosis and drugs. Every case is notified (Ni-kshay portal), and patients receive nutritional support through the Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana.

**Drug-sensitive TB (new and previously treated):**

```text
Intensive phase:     2 months  H + R + Z + E   (isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide, ethambutol)
Continuation phase:  4 months  H + R + E
Written as:  2HRZE / 4HRE   (daily, weight-band FDCs)
```

The continuation phase may be extended in some forms of extrapulmonary TB (for example TB of the brain or spine). **Drug-resistant TB** (rifampicin-resistant or MDR-TB) is treated with newer, mostly **all-oral regimens** containing drugs such as **bedaquiline**, linezolid and fluoroquinolones, decided by the programme's resistance testing.

### First-line drugs – key adverse effects

| Drug | Main adverse effects | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Isoniazid (H)** | Hepatitis, **peripheral neuropathy** | Give **pyridoxine (vitamin B6)** to prevent neuropathy |
| **Rifampicin (R)** | Hepatitis, **orange-red urine, sweat and tears**, flu-like illness | Strong **enzyme inducer** – reduces effect of **oral contraceptives**, warfarin, many antiretrovirals |
| **Pyrazinamide (Z)** | Hepatitis, **raised uric acid, joint pain** | Most hepatotoxic of the four |
| **Ethambutol (E)** | **Optic neuritis** – blurred vision, red-green colour blindness | Check vision; care in young children |
| Streptomycin | Ototoxicity, kidney damage | Injectable, rarely used now |

**TB preventive therapy** is given to close contacts (especially children under 5 and people with HIV) after active TB is excluded – isoniazid for 6 months or shorter rifamycin-based regimens.

**Prevention:** BCG vaccine at birth (protects mainly against severe childhood TB), early diagnosis, cough hygiene, ventilation, contact tracing.

### Counselling points

1. **Complete the full course** even when you feel better; missed doses cause relapse and drug resistance.
2. Orange-red urine and tears with rifampicin are harmless (may stain soft contact lenses).
3. Use another contraceptive method – rifampicin makes the pill ineffective.
4. Report **yellow eyes, dark urine, vomiting, loss of appetite** (hepatitis), **change in vision** (ethambutol), **tingling of feet** (isoniazid).
5. Cover mouth when coughing; dispose of sputum safely; good ventilation at home.
6. Eat a nutritious diet; avoid alcohol; get tested for HIV and diabetes.

## 8.2 Pneumonia

### Definition

**Pneumonia** is an **acute infection of the lung parenchyma** (alveoli), with inflammation and filling of the alveoli with fluid and cells (consolidation).

### Classification and organisms

| Type | Common organisms |
| --- | --- |
| **Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP)** | ***Streptococcus pneumoniae*** (most common), *Haemophilus influenzae*; atypicals – *Mycoplasma pneumoniae*, *Chlamydophila*, *Legionella*; respiratory viruses (influenza, SARS-CoV-2) |
| **Hospital-acquired (HAP)** – 48 hours or more after admission; **ventilator-associated (VAP)** | Gram-negative bacilli – *Pseudomonas*, *Klebsiella*, *E. coli*, *Acinetobacter*; *Staphylococcus aureus* including MRSA |
| Aspiration pneumonia | Mouth anaerobes and Gram-negatives (stroke, alcohol, unconscious patients) |
| In HIV | *Pneumocystis jirovecii* (see Unit 9), TB |

**Risk factors:** extremes of age, smoking, COPD, diabetes, heart failure, alcohol, malnutrition, immunosuppression, recent viral infection, indoor air pollution; in children – malnutrition, no vaccination, no breastfeeding.

### Clinical manifestations

Fever with chills, **cough with purulent or rust-coloured sputum**, **breathlessness**, **pleuritic chest pain**, fast breathing; crackles and bronchial breathing over the affected area; in elderly – confusion and falls may be the only signs. **Chest X-ray** shows consolidation. Children: fast breathing and chest indrawing are danger signs.

**Severity:** the **CURB-65** score (Confusion, Urea raised, Respiratory rate 30 or more, BP low – systolic below 90 or diastolic 60 or less, age 65 or more) helps decide home treatment (0–1), hospital (2) or ICU care (3 or more).

### Management

**Non-drug:** rest, fluids, oxygen if saturation is low, paracetamol for fever and pain, stop smoking, chest physiotherapy in selected patients.

**Antibiotics (choose empirically, then adjust to culture):**

| Setting | Typical choices |
| --- | --- |
| CAP, outpatient, otherwise healthy | **Amoxicillin**; or a macrolide (azithromycin, clarithromycin) or doxycycline |
| CAP with other diseases | Amoxicillin–clavulanate + macrolide (respiratory fluoroquinolones such as levofloxacin are used sparingly in India because they may mask TB) |
| CAP in hospital | **IV ceftriaxone (or cefotaxime) + azithromycin** |
| HAP/VAP | Anti-pseudomonal drugs – piperacillin–tazobactam, cefepime, meropenem ± vancomycin/linezolid for MRSA, guided by local resistance |
| Influenza pneumonia | Add oseltamivir |

Duration is usually about **5–7 days** for uncomplicated CAP if the patient improves. **Prevention:** pneumococcal and influenza vaccines; Hib and PCV in the childhood schedule; stop smoking.

**Counselling:** finish the course; drink fluids; return if breathlessness worsens or fever persists after 48–72 hours; take azithromycin as directed and report palpitations; get vaccinated.

## 8.3 Urinary tract infections (UTI)

### Definition

**UTI** is the presence and multiplication of microorganisms in the urinary tract causing infection – usually defined in culture as **10⁵ colony-forming units per mL or more** of a single organism in a clean-catch midstream urine sample.

| Type | Site / meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Lower UTI – cystitis** | Bladder |
| **Upper UTI – acute pyelonephritis** | Kidney |
| Uncomplicated | Healthy, non-pregnant women with normal urinary tract |
| Complicated | Men, pregnancy, diabetes, catheter, stones, obstruction, abnormal tract |
| Asymptomatic bacteriuria | Bacteria without symptoms – treat **only in pregnancy** (and before urological surgery) |

### Etiopathogenesis

- ***Escherichia coli*** causes most cases; others – *Klebsiella*, *Proteus* (stones), *Enterococcus*, *Staphylococcus saprophyticus* (young women), *Pseudomonas* (catheter, hospital).
- Usually **ascending infection** from bowel flora around the urethra.
- **Risk factors:** **female sex** (short urethra), sexual activity, pregnancy, menopause, diabetes, urinary catheter, stones, enlarged prostate, poor hygiene, holding urine for long.

### Clinical manifestations

- **Cystitis:** burning on urination (dysuria), frequency, urgency, lower abdominal pain, cloudy or smelly urine, sometimes blood; usually no high fever.
- **Pyelonephritis:** **high fever with chills, loin (flank) pain**, nausea, vomiting, with or without bladder symptoms.
- Elderly: confusion; children: fever, poor feeding.

**Diagnosis:** urine routine (pus cells, nitrite, leucocyte esterase on dipstick), **urine culture and sensitivity** (before antibiotics in complicated cases, pregnancy and pyelonephritis).

### Management

**Non-drug:** drink plenty of water, do not hold urine, empty the bladder after intercourse, wipe front to back, avoid irritant douches; remove or change catheters where possible; cranberry products have limited evidence.

**Drug treatment:**

| Type | Usual choices |
| --- | --- |
| Uncomplicated cystitis (women) | **Nitrofurantoin** for about 5 days, **fosfomycin** single dose, or cotrimoxazole where local resistance is low; urinary alkaliniser for symptoms |
| Pyelonephritis (mild, outpatient) | Oral ciprofloxacin or an oral cephalosporin per sensitivity |
| Pyelonephritis (severe) / complicated UTI | **IV ceftriaxone**, aminoglycoside, or carbapenem for resistant organisms, then switch per culture; usually 7–14 days |
| UTI in pregnancy | Nitrofurantoin (avoid near term), cephalexin, amoxicillin–clavulanate; **avoid fluoroquinolones and tetracyclines**; cotrimoxazole avoided in the first trimester and near term |

**Rising resistance** of *E. coli* to fluoroquinolones and cephalosporins (ESBL producers) in India makes **culture-guided therapy** important.

**Counselling:** complete the course; take nitrofurantoin **with food** (it may colour urine brown); drink water; return if fever or flank pain develops; prevention habits as above.

## 8.4 Viral hepatitis

### Definition

**Hepatitis** means **inflammation of the liver**. **Viral hepatitis** is caused by hepatitis viruses **A, B, C, D and E**. (Other causes: alcohol – Unit 6, drugs such as paracetamol overdose, isoniazid, and autoimmune disease.)

| Virus | Spread | Chronic? | Vaccine | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A (HAV)** | **Faeco-oral** (contaminated food and water) | No | Yes | Mostly children; self-limiting |
| **B (HBV)** – DNA virus | **Blood, sexual, mother to child** | Yes (especially if infected at birth) | **Yes** | Cirrhosis and liver cancer |
| **C (HCV)** | **Blood** (unsafe injections, transfusions, drug users), less often sexual | **Yes, in most** | No | Cirrhosis, liver cancer; **curable** with modern drugs |
| **D (HDV)** | Blood | Yes | Prevented by HBV vaccine | **Needs HBV** to infect |
| **E (HEV)** | **Faeco-oral** (water) | Usually no | Not in routine use | **Severe, high mortality in pregnant women** |

### Clinical manifestations

**Acute hepatitis:** a prodrome of fever, malaise, loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting, aversion to food and smoking, right upper abdominal discomfort; then **jaundice** (yellow eyes and skin), dark urine, pale stools, itching. Most recover; a few develop **acute liver failure** (confusion, bleeding). **Chronic hepatitis B or C** is often silent for years until cirrhosis or cancer.

**Laboratory:** raised ALT and AST (often very high in acute viral hepatitis), raised bilirubin; virus markers – **IgM anti-HAV**, **HBsAg** (B infection), HBeAg and HBV DNA (activity), **anti-HCV** antibody and HCV RNA, IgM anti-HEV.

### Management

**General (acute hepatitis A and E, early B):** rest, adequate calories and fluids, avoid alcohol and liver-toxic drugs (avoid high paracetamol doses, sedatives), no special diet needed; hospital care if liver failure.

**Hepatitis B (chronic, with active disease):** oral antivirals **tenofovir (TDF or TAF)** or **entecavir** – long-term, usually lifelong; monitor HBV DNA and kidney function (tenofovir). Pregnant women with high viral load may receive tenofovir to prevent mother-to-child spread; newborns of infected mothers get **hepatitis B vaccine birth dose + hepatitis B immunoglobulin**.

**Hepatitis C:** **direct-acting antivirals (DAAs)**, for example **sofosbuvir + velpatasvir** or **sofosbuvir + daclatasvir**, usually for **12 weeks** – cure rates are above 90%. India provides free testing and treatment under the **National Viral Hepatitis Control Programme**.

**Prevention:** safe water and food, hand washing (A, E); **hepatitis B vaccination** (birth dose and infant schedule; health-care workers), screening of blood, safe injections and sterile equipment, safe sex, no sharing of razors or needles (B, C, D); hepatitis A vaccine.

**Counselling:** no alcohol; avoid self-medication and herbal remedies; complete antiviral courses and never stop hepatitis B drugs suddenly (flare); household contacts should be vaccinated against hepatitis B; use condoms; do not share razors or toothbrushes.

## Remember

- TB: cough 2 weeks or more; CBNAAT detects rifampicin resistance; NTEP 2HRZE/4HRE daily FDCs by weight band; pyridoxine with isoniazid; rifampicin – orange urine, contraceptive failure; ethambutol – vision.
- Pneumonia: *S. pneumoniae* commonest in CAP; CURB-65; amoxicillin or macrolide outpatient, ceftriaxone + azithromycin in hospital; vaccinate.
- UTI: *E. coli*; 10⁵ CFU/mL; nitrofurantoin or fosfomycin for cystitis; treat asymptomatic bacteriuria only in pregnancy; avoid fluoroquinolones in pregnancy.
- Hepatitis: A and E faeco-oral, B/C/D blood; B and C become chronic; tenofovir/entecavir for B; sofosbuvir-based DAAs cure C in 12 weeks; HBV vaccine birth dose.

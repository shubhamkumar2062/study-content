# How to use this guide

Pharmacotherapeutics (ER20-24T) is a second-year D.Pharm subject of 75 theory hours, with a linked practical, ER20-24P (25 hours). The syllabus has two chapters: chapter 1 (introduction, rational use of medicines, evidence-based medicine, essential medicines and standard treatment guidelines) and chapter 2, which lists about 40 diseases in 13 body-system groups, (a) to (m). In this guide each group is a unit, except that:

- **infectious diseases (2g, 12 hours)** are split into two units (8 and 9), because they hold eight diseases;
- **musculoskeletal disorders and dermatology (2h and 2i, 3 hours each)** are joined in Unit 10;
- **ophthalmology and antimicrobial resistance (2k and 2l, 2 hours each)** are joined in Unit 12.

## How to study a disease

The syllabus asks the same things for every disease: **definition, etiopathogenesis, clinical manifestations, non-pharmacological management and pharmacological management**. Learn every disease in that order, and add **counselling points**, because examiners and the practical (SOAP notes and counselling) both expect them. A good habit: after reading a disease, close the notes and write the five headings with three or four points under each.

Study one unit every two or three days. Draw each flow diagram (pathogenesis) once from memory, learn the comparison tables (asthma vs COPD, RA vs OA, UC vs Crohn's, hypo- vs hyperthyroidism, bacterial vs viral conjunctivitis), then answer the question bank without looking.

## Exam pattern (ER-2020 theory paper)

D.Pharm ER-2020 examinations are yearly and are conducted by the state examining body (in Himachal Pradesh, HPTSB) under the PCI ER-2020 regulations. The theory paper is **80 marks, 3 hours**; another 20 marks come from sessional (internal) assessment. The usual ER-2020 paper looks like this; confirm the exact format with your college, because boards sometimes change it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Objective questions (MCQs / one-word / fill in the blanks) – all compulsory | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| II | Short answers – attempt 10 of 11 | 10 × 3 = 30 |
| III | Long answers – attempt 6 of 7 | 6 × 5 = 30 |

In this guide the **long** questions are 5-mark answers and the **short** questions are 3-mark answers, to match this pattern. A few **very-short** (2-mark) questions are included for quick revision and sessional tests.

## Probability ratings

- **High** – asked in most years or a core topic with heavy syllabus weight; prepare the full answer.
- **Medium** – asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** – rare, but useful for objective questions and as a backup choice.

Ratings are an estimate from the syllabus weight (hours) and the kind of questions usually set in D.Pharm papers. They are not a promise.

## Scoring tips for Pharmacotherapeutics

1. **Use the five headings in every long answer:** definition → causes/risk factors → pathogenesis (a small flow diagram) → signs and symptoms → non-drug management → drug management (a table of drug groups with examples) → counselling. This structure alone earns most of the marks.
2. **Name drugs by group with two examples each** (for example "ACE inhibitors – enalapril, ramipril"). Generic names only.
3. **Mention the first-line drug** clearly: metformin in type 2 diabetes, ICS in asthma, SSRIs in depression, methotrexate in RA, permethrin in scabies, levodopa–carbidopa in Parkinson's, PPIs in GERD.
4. **Quote national programme regimens correctly:** TB 2HRZE/4HRE (NTEP), malaria – chloroquine + 14-day primaquine for vivax and ACT for falciparum, HIV – TLD.
5. **Give the key numbers:** BP 140/90; FPG 126, HbA1c 6.5%; Hb 13/12/11 g/dL; IOP 10–21 mmHg; CD4 below 200 for AIDS; FEV1/FVC below 0.70 in COPD; alteplase within 4.5 hours in stroke.
6. **Always finish with 3–4 counselling points** – they show pharmacist-level thinking and examiners reward them.
7. **Draw comparison tables** for "differentiate" questions; they are quicker to write and easier to mark.
8. **For a 3-mark short answer**, write 5–6 crisp points or a small table; do not write an essay.
9. Avoid writing doses unless you are sure; a wrong dose costs more than leaving it out.
10. The practical record (SOAP notes, counselling, dose calculations) uses the same knowledge – practise writing one SOAP note for each common disease.

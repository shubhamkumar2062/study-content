# Unit 9: Infectious diseases II – gonorrhoea, syphilis, malaria, HIV and opportunistic infections, viral infections (SARS, COVID-19)

This unit is the second half of syllabus chapter 2(g). Many of these infections are treated under **national programmes** (malaria – National Center for Vector Borne Diseases Control; HIV and sexually transmitted infections – National AIDS Control Programme), and the pharmacist must follow the programme regimens and counsel carefully because **stigma, adherence and partner treatment** decide success.

## 9.1 Sexually transmitted infections (STIs) – general points

- Spread mainly by unprotected sexual contact; one STI raises the risk of others, including HIV.
- India uses **syndromic management** in many clinics: treatment is based on the syndrome (urethral discharge, vaginal discharge, genital ulcer, lower abdominal pain) using **pre-packed STI kits**, so that patients are treated at the first visit.
- **Always:** treat the **partner(s)**, advise abstinence until both complete treatment, promote condoms, offer **HIV and syphilis testing**.

## 9.2 Gonorrhoea

### Definition and cause

**Gonorrhoea** is a sexually transmitted infection caused by ***Neisseria gonorrhoeae***, a **Gram-negative intracellular diplococcus**, which infects the mucous membranes of the urethra, cervix, rectum, throat and eye.

### Clinical manifestations

| In men | In women |
| --- | --- |
| **Purulent urethral discharge**, burning on urination, 2–7 days after exposure | Often **no symptoms**; vaginal discharge, burning urination, bleeding between periods |
| Complications: epididymitis, urethral stricture | Complications: **pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)**, ectopic pregnancy, **infertility** |

Others: proctitis, pharyngitis; **disseminated infection** (fever, joint pain, skin pustules); **ophthalmia neonatorum** – severe eye infection in newborns of infected mothers.

**Diagnosis:** Gram stain of discharge (Gram-negative diplococci inside pus cells), culture (also tests sensitivity), nucleic acid amplification tests.

### Management

- **Drug of choice:** a single dose of an injectable third-generation cephalosporin – **ceftriaxone IM** (cefixime orally is an alternative in some guidelines) – **plus azithromycin** (or doxycycline) to cover **chlamydia**, which often co-exists. Follow the national STI guideline for the exact regimen.
- Penicillin, tetracycline and fluoroquinolones are **no longer reliable** because of widespread resistance.
- Treat partners; test for HIV and syphilis; test of cure if symptoms persist.
- **Newborn:** eye prophylaxis and prompt treatment of ophthalmia neonatorum.

**Counselling:** complete the treatment; no sex until you and your partner(s) are treated and symptoms resolve; use condoms; return if discharge continues.

## 9.3 Syphilis

### Definition and cause

**Syphilis** is a chronic, multi-stage sexually transmitted infection caused by the spirochaete ***Treponema pallidum***; it can also pass from mother to fetus (**congenital syphilis**) and rarely through blood.

### Stages and manifestations

| Stage | Time after infection | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary** | About 3 weeks (9–90 days) | **Painless, hard ulcer (chancre)** at the site of entry; painless swollen nodes; heals on its own |
| **Secondary** | 6 weeks – 6 months | Fever, malaise, **non-itchy rash including palms and soles**, mucous patches in mouth, condylomata lata (moist warty lesions), patchy hair loss |
| **Latent** | Years | No symptoms; only blood tests positive (early latent – infectious, late latent) |
| **Tertiary** | Many years | **Gummas** (destructive lesions of skin, bone), **cardiovascular** (aortitis, aneurysm), **neurosyphilis** (general paralysis, tabes dorsalis, dementia) |
| **Congenital** | — | Stillbirth, abortion; in babies: rash, snuffles, enlarged liver; later Hutchinson's teeth, saddle nose |

**Diagnosis:** dark-ground microscopy of chancre fluid; **non-treponemal tests** (VDRL, RPR – used for screening and to follow treatment response) and **treponemal tests** (TPHA, FTA-ABS – confirm infection; stay positive for life). All pregnant women are screened.

### Management

| Stage | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Early syphilis (primary, secondary, early latent) | **Benzathine penicillin G 2.4 million units IM, single dose** (given as two injections, one in each buttock) |
| Late latent / unknown duration / tertiary (not neuro) | Benzathine penicillin G **once weekly for 3 weeks** |
| Neurosyphilis | IV aqueous (crystalline) penicillin for about 2 weeks |
| Penicillin allergy (not pregnant) | Doxycycline (longer course) |
| **Pregnancy** | **Penicillin only** – desensitise if allergic (doxycycline is contraindicated) |

Test sensitivity to penicillin as per local practice and keep **anaphylaxis treatment ready**. **Jarisch–Herxheimer reaction:** fever, chills, headache and muscle pain within hours of the first dose, due to release of treponemal products; it settles in a day – treat with paracetamol and reassure.

**Follow-up:** repeat VDRL/RPR – a fourfold fall in titre shows response. **Counselling:** partner treatment; no sex until healed; condoms; return for follow-up tests; HIV test.

## 9.4 Malaria

### Definition and cause

**Malaria** is a **febrile illness** caused by **protozoa of the genus *Plasmodium***, transmitted by the bite of an infected **female *Anopheles* mosquito**. Species: ***P. vivax*** and ***P. falciparum*** (both common in India), *P. malariae*, *P. ovale*, *P. knowlesi*.

### Life cycle (simplified)

```text
Mosquito bite injects sporozoites
        ↓
Liver stage (hepatic schizogony) – no symptoms
   (P. vivax / P. ovale leave dormant HYPNOZOITES → relapse months later)
        ↓
Merozoites enter red blood cells → multiply → RBCs burst
        ↓                         (every 48 h: vivax, falciparum, ovale;
Fever paroxysm, anaemia             72 h: malariae)
        ↓
Some form gametocytes → taken up by a mosquito → cycle continues
```

### Clinical manifestations

- **Paroxysms:** **cold stage** (shivering, rigors) → **hot stage** (high fever, headache, vomiting) → **sweating stage** (fever falls); body aches, weakness.
- Enlarged spleen and liver, anaemia, mild jaundice.
- **Severe (complicated) malaria** – mostly ***P. falciparum*** (vivax can also be severe): **cerebral malaria** (altered consciousness, seizures, coma), severe anaemia, **hypoglycaemia**, jaundice, acute kidney injury, breathing difficulty (ARDS), shock, bleeding, high parasite count. High risk in children, pregnant women and non-immune travellers.

**Diagnosis:** **blood smear microscopy** (thick smear to detect, thin smear to identify species) or **rapid diagnostic test (RDT)**. Treat only confirmed cases.

### Drug management (Indian national guideline – summary)

| Situation | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| ***P. vivax*** | **Chloroquine for 3 days** + **primaquine daily for 14 days** (radical cure – kills hypnozoites, prevents relapse) |
| ***P. falciparum*** (uncomplicated) | **Artemisinin-based combination therapy (ACT)** for 3 days: **artesunate + sulfadoxine–pyrimethamine (AS+SP)**; in the **north-eastern states** **artemether–lumefantrine (AL)** because of SP resistance; plus a **single dose of primaquine** (kills gametocytes – reduces spread) |
| Mixed infection | ACT + 14-day primaquine |
| **Severe malaria** | **Injectable artesunate (IV/IM)** for at least 24 hours (alternatives: IM artemether, IV quinine), then a full oral ACT course; manage glucose, fluids, fits, anaemia |
| Pregnancy | Treatment as per the national guideline for each trimester; **primaquine is not given** in pregnancy (or to infants) |

**Never use artemisinin alone (monotherapy)** – it encourages resistance. **Primaquine** can cause **haemolysis in G6PD deficiency** – check where possible and stop if urine turns dark.

**Other drugs:** quinine (tinnitus, low sugar – "cinchonism"), mefloquine (neuropsychiatric effects), doxycycline (used with quinine and for prophylaxis; not in children under 8 or pregnancy), atovaquone–proguanil.

### Prevention

- **Long-lasting insecticidal nets (LLINs)**, indoor residual spraying, removing stagnant water, larvivorous fish.
- Personal protection: repellents, full-sleeved clothes, screens.
- **Chemoprophylaxis** for travellers to high-risk areas (doxycycline or mefloquine as advised).

### Counselling points

Complete all days of treatment even if fever goes; take tablets after food (less vomiting – repeat the dose if vomited within 30 minutes); report dark urine or yellow eyes during primaquine; return immediately if drowsy, fitting, unable to eat or very weak (signs of severe malaria); sleep under a treated bed net.

## 9.5 HIV infection and opportunistic infections

### Definition and cause

**HIV infection** is a chronic infection with the **human immunodeficiency virus** (a retrovirus: HIV-1, the common type, and HIV-2) that destroys **CD4+ T-lymphocytes**, leading to progressive immune deficiency. **AIDS** (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome) is the advanced stage – **CD4 count below 200 cells/µL** or an **AIDS-defining illness**.

### Transmission

Unprotected sexual contact (most common), **mother to child** (pregnancy, delivery, breastfeeding), infected blood and blood products, **sharing needles** (injecting drug use), needle-stick injury. **Not** spread by touching, hugging, sharing food or utensils, or mosquitoes.

### Pathogenesis and stages

```text
HIV binds CD4 (+ CCR5/CXCR4) → enters cell → reverse transcriptase makes DNA
→ integrase inserts it into host DNA → new virus made (protease matures it)
        ↓
Acute infection (2–4 weeks): flu-like illness, high viral load
        ↓
Clinical latency (years): few symptoms, CD4 slowly falls
        ↓
AIDS: CD4 < 200 → opportunistic infections and cancers
```

**Clinical features of advanced disease:** weight loss, chronic diarrhoea, prolonged fever, oral thrush, swollen nodes, skin lesions, recurrent infections; **TB is the most common opportunistic infection in India**.

**Diagnosis:** antibody (and antigen) tests – in India **three tests with different principles** confirm infection in adults; **DNA PCR** in infants under 18 months; **CD4 count** and **viral load** for monitoring.

### Antiretroviral therapy (ART)

**Principle:** **"Treat all"** – ART is started for **every person diagnosed with HIV, regardless of CD4 count**, as early as possible, as a **combination of three drugs** (to prevent resistance), taken **lifelong**. ART is free at ART centres under the National AIDS Control Programme. The goal is an **undetectable viral load** – such a person does not pass HIV sexually ("Undetectable = Untransmittable").

| Class | Examples | Key adverse effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **NRTIs** (nucleoside/nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitors) | **Tenofovir (TDF)**, **lamivudine (3TC)**, emtricitabine, zidovudine (AZT), abacavir | Tenofovir – kidney damage, bone loss; zidovudine – **anaemia**; abacavir – hypersensitivity |
| **NNRTIs** | Efavirenz, nevirapine | Efavirenz – dizziness, vivid dreams; nevirapine – rash, hepatitis |
| **Integrase inhibitors (INSTIs)** | **Dolutegravir (DTG)**, raltegravir | Insomnia, weight gain; few interactions |
| **Protease inhibitors (PIs)** | Lopinavir/ritonavir, atazanavir/ritonavir, darunavir/ritonavir | GI upset, lipid and sugar changes, many interactions |

**Preferred first-line regimen in India for most adults and adolescents: TLD = tenofovir + lamivudine + dolutegravir**, one tablet once daily. Adherence must be very high (about 95% or more of doses).

**Special situations:**
- **Prevention of parent-to-child transmission:** ART for all pregnant women with HIV; **nevirapine syrup prophylaxis for the infant**; safe delivery; infant testing.
- **Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP):** start **as soon as possible, within 72 hours**, continue for **28 days** (after needle-stick injury or sexual exposure).
- **Pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP):** tenofovir-based drugs for high-risk HIV-negative people.
- **Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS):** worsening of an existing infection soon after starting ART as immunity recovers.
- **Drug interactions:** rifampicin lowers dolutegravir levels (dose adjustment needed); antacids and iron/calcium reduce dolutegravir absorption.

### Common opportunistic infections

| Infection | Treatment / prophylaxis |
| --- | --- |
| **Tuberculosis** | Standard anti-TB treatment + ART; TB preventive therapy for those without active TB |
| ***Pneumocystis jirovecii* pneumonia (PCP)** | **Cotrimoxazole** (high dose) ± steroids; **cotrimoxazole prophylaxis** in low CD4 or advanced disease (also prevents toxoplasmosis) |
| Oral / oesophageal **candidiasis** | Clotrimazole lozenges, nystatin, **fluconazole** |
| **Cryptococcal meningitis** | **Amphotericin B + flucytosine** (or fluconazole), then fluconazole maintenance |
| Cerebral toxoplasmosis | Pyrimethamine + sulfadiazine (+ folinic acid) or high-dose cotrimoxazole |
| CMV retinitis | Ganciclovir / valganciclovir |
| Herpes zoster, herpes simplex | **Acyclovir** |
| *Mycobacterium avium* complex | Clarithromycin/azithromycin + ethambutol |

### Counselling points

Take ART **every day at the same time, for life**; never share or stop drugs; use condoms; report side effects instead of stopping; take cotrimoxazole prophylaxis as advised; keep follow-up for CD4 and viral load; tell the doctor or pharmacist about all other medicines (interactions); maintain confidentiality and offer support against stigma.

## 9.6 Viral infections – SARS and COVID-19 (SARS-CoV-2)

### Definitions

- **SARS (severe acute respiratory syndrome)** is a severe viral pneumonia caused by **SARS-CoV**, a coronavirus that caused an outbreak in **2002–2003**, mainly in Asia; it was controlled by isolation and quarantine.
- **COVID-19 (coronavirus disease 2019)** is the illness caused by **SARS-CoV-2**, a new coronavirus first reported in late **2019**; WHO declared it a **pandemic in March 2020**.

### Etiopathogenesis of COVID-19

- Spread by **respiratory droplets and aerosols** (coughing, talking, especially in closed, crowded spaces), and less often contaminated surfaces.
- The virus uses its **spike protein** to bind the **ACE2 receptor** on cells of the respiratory tract and other organs.
- Severe disease involves an excessive immune response (**"cytokine storm"**), lung injury (ARDS) and a tendency to form **blood clots**.
- **Risk factors for severe disease:** old age, diabetes, hypertension, heart, lung and kidney disease, obesity, immunosuppression, being unvaccinated.

### Clinical manifestations

Fever, dry cough, sore throat, tiredness, body aches, headache, **loss of smell or taste**, diarrhoea; breathlessness and **low oxygen saturation** in moderate–severe disease. Many infections are mild or asymptomatic. Complications: pneumonia, ARDS, clots (stroke, pulmonary embolism), heart inflammation, secondary infections (including mucormycosis, seen in India especially with uncontrolled diabetes and steroid misuse), and long-lasting symptoms ("long COVID").

**Diagnosis:** **RT-PCR** of nasal/throat swab (gold standard), **rapid antigen test**; chest CT/X-ray, inflammatory markers in moderate–severe cases.

### Management (broad, following national clinical guidance)

| Severity | Management |
| --- | --- |
| **Mild** (no breathlessness, normal oxygen) | **Home isolation**, rest, fluids, **paracetamol** for fever, monitor temperature and **oxygen saturation with a pulse oximeter**; seek care if saturation falls (below about 94%), breathlessness, chest pain or confusion |
| **Moderate** (low oxygen) | Hospital; **oxygen**; **corticosteroids (dexamethasone)** for patients needing oxygen; **prophylactic anticoagulation** (enoxaparin/heparin); prone positioning |
| **Severe** | ICU; high-flow oxygen or ventilation; steroids, anticoagulation; antivirals (**remdesivir**) in selected patients; anti-IL-6 (**tocilizumab**) in selected cases |

**Not useful / not recommended:** antibiotics for the virus itself (only for proven bacterial infection), hydroxychloroquine, ivermectin, convalescent plasma; **steroids should not be used in mild disease** (harm, mucormycosis risk). Oral antivirals (for example nirmatrelvir–ritonavir, molnupiravir) have been used in some high-risk patients – follow current guidance.

**Prevention:** **vaccination** (India used vaccines such as Covishield and Covaxin), masks in crowded indoor places, hand hygiene, ventilation, staying home when ill.

### Other common viral infections (for practicals)

| Infection | Specific treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Influenza | Oseltamivir (best within 48 hours of onset), annual vaccine |
| Herpes simplex, varicella–zoster (chickenpox, shingles) | Acyclovir, valacyclovir |
| Dengue | **No antiviral**; fluids and **paracetamol**; **avoid aspirin and NSAIDs** (bleeding) |
| Common cold | Symptomatic; antibiotics are not needed |

**Counselling (COVID-19):** isolate as advised; check oxygen regularly; take steroids only if prescribed and monitor sugar in diabetics; complete vaccination; seek help early for warning signs.

## Remember

- Gonorrhoea: Gram-negative diplococci; ceftriaxone + azithromycin (cover chlamydia); treat partners.
- Syphilis: painless chancre → rash on palms/soles → latent → tertiary; VDRL/RPR + TPHA; benzathine penicillin G 2.4 MU IM single dose for early syphilis; penicillin only in pregnancy; Jarisch–Herxheimer reaction.
- Malaria: vivax – chloroquine 3 days + primaquine 14 days; falciparum – ACT (AS+SP; AL in north-east) + single-dose primaquine; severe – injectable artesunate; no artemisinin monotherapy; G6PD and primaquine.
- HIV: treat all; TLD first-line; PEP within 72 h for 28 days; cotrimoxazole prophylaxis; TB is the commonest OI in India.
- COVID-19: SARS-CoV-2 binds ACE2; mild – home care, paracetamol, pulse oximetry; steroids and anticoagulants only for patients needing oxygen; vaccinate.

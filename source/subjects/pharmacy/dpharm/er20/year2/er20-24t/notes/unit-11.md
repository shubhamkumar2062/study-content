# Unit 11: Psychiatric disorders – depression, anxiety and psychosis

Syllabus chapter 2(j), 4 hours. Mental illnesses are common, under-treated and surrounded by stigma. The pharmacist's role is to explain that the drugs work **slowly**, are **not addictive** (except benzodiazepines), must **not be stopped suddenly**, and to watch for side effects and **suicide risk**.

## 11.1 Depression (major depressive disorder)

### Definition

**Depression** is a **mood disorder** with a **persistent low mood and/or loss of interest or pleasure** in usual activities, together with other symptoms, lasting **at least 2 weeks**, that cause distress or impair daily functioning. It is different from ordinary short-term sadness.

### Etiopathogenesis

- **Biological:** **monoamine hypothesis** – reduced activity of **serotonin (5-HT), noradrenaline and dopamine** in the brain; also stress-hormone (HPA axis) over-activity and reduced neuroplasticity (BDNF); **genetic** tendency (family history).
- **Psychosocial:** stressful life events (loss, unemployment, debt), childhood adversity, loneliness, chronic illness, postpartum period.
- **Medical causes:** hypothyroidism, Parkinson's disease, stroke, cancer, chronic pain, alcohol and substance use.
- **Drugs:** corticosteroids, reserpine, beta-blockers (rarely), oral contraceptives (some women), isotretinoin, interferon, alcohol.

### Clinical manifestations

The two core symptoms are **low mood** and **loss of interest (anhedonia)**. Others (remember "SIG E CAPS"):

| Letter | Symptom |
| --- | --- |
| S | **Sleep** disturbance (insomnia, early-morning waking, or excess sleep) |
| I | Loss of **interest** |
| G | **Guilt**, feelings of worthlessness |
| E | Low **energy**, fatigue |
| C | Poor **concentration**, indecision |
| A | **Appetite** change, weight loss or gain |
| P | **Psychomotor** slowing or agitation |
| S | **Suicidal** thoughts |

Severity: mild, moderate, severe (with or without psychotic features). Screening tools: PHQ-9, Hamilton rating scale.

### Management

**Non-drug:** psychoeducation; **psychotherapy** – cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), interpersonal therapy (first choice for mild depression, combined with drugs in moderate–severe); regular exercise, sleep hygiene, avoiding alcohol; social support; **electroconvulsive therapy (ECT)** for severe, suicidal, psychotic or treatment-resistant depression.

**Drug management:**

| Class | Examples | Key adverse effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **SSRIs** (first choice) | **Fluoxetine, sertraline, escitalopram**, paroxetine | Nausea, headache, insomnia or drowsiness, **sexual dysfunction**, low sodium (elderly), bleeding risk with NSAIDs/anticoagulants; agitation early in treatment |
| SNRIs | Venlafaxine, duloxetine | As SSRIs + raised BP |
| **Tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs)** | Amitriptyline, imipramine, nortriptyline | **Anticholinergic** (dry mouth, constipation, urinary retention, blurred vision), sedation, postural hypotension, weight gain, **cardiotoxic in overdose (dangerous)** |
| Atypical | Mirtazapine (sedation, weight gain), bupropion (no sexual side effects; lowers seizure threshold), trazodone, vortioxetine | — |
| MAO inhibitors | Phenelzine, moclobemide | **Cheese (tyramine) reaction** – hypertensive crisis; many interactions; rarely used |

**Principles of antidepressant therapy:**
1. Benefit begins in **2–4 weeks**, full effect in **4–8 weeks**; side effects appear earlier – warn the patient.
2. Start low (especially in elderly), adjust after an adequate trial (4–6 weeks at a proper dose).
3. After remission, **continue for at least 6–12 months** to prevent relapse (longer for recurrent depression).
4. **Taper slowly** when stopping – sudden stopping causes a **discontinuation syndrome** (dizziness, "electric shock" sensations, irritability, flu-like symptoms), especially with paroxetine and venlafaxine.
5. **Serotonin syndrome** (agitation, sweating, tremor, high temperature, muscle jerks) can occur when serotonergic drugs are combined (SSRI + MAOI, tramadol, linezolid, triptans).
6. Young people (under about 25) may have **more suicidal thoughts early in treatment** – close monitoring; dispense limited quantities if suicide risk is high.
7. In **bipolar disorder**, antidepressants alone can trigger mania – mood stabilisers (lithium, valproate) are needed.

**Counselling:** "These medicines are not habit-forming and take a few weeks to work – keep taking them"; do not stop without advice; avoid alcohol; report worsening mood or thoughts of self-harm immediately (helpline / doctor); take SSRIs in the morning if they disturb sleep.

## 11.2 Anxiety disorders

### Definition

**Anxiety** is a normal emotional response to threat. An **anxiety disorder** exists when anxiety is **excessive, persistent, out of proportion to the situation and interferes with daily life**.

### Types

| Disorder | Features |
| --- | --- |
| **Generalised anxiety disorder (GAD)** | Excessive, uncontrollable worry about many everyday matters for 6 months or more, with restlessness, fatigue, poor concentration, irritability, muscle tension, sleep disturbance |
| **Panic disorder** | Recurrent sudden **panic attacks** – palpitations, chest pain, breathlessness, sweating, trembling, fear of dying – peaking within minutes; fear of further attacks |
| Social anxiety disorder (social phobia) | Fear of being judged in social or performance situations |
| Specific phobia | Fear of a particular object or situation (heights, animals) |
| Related conditions | Obsessive–compulsive disorder (OCD), post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) |

### Etiopathogenesis

Genetic tendency; imbalance of **GABA** (reduced inhibition), **serotonin** and **noradrenaline**; over-active amygdala (fear centre); stressful life events, personality, childhood trauma; **medical causes** (hyperthyroidism, arrhythmias, low sugar, asthma) and **substances** (caffeine, nicotine, alcohol withdrawal, salbutamol, steroids, thyroxine excess) must be excluded.

### Clinical manifestations

**Psychological:** worry, fear, restlessness, irritability, poor concentration, sleep problems. **Physical (autonomic):** palpitations, fast breathing, sweating, tremor, dry mouth, butterflies in the stomach, frequent urination, diarrhoea, headache, muscle tension.

### Management

**Non-drug:** **CBT** (first-line, with lasting benefit), relaxation and breathing exercises, yoga, meditation, regular exercise, reduce caffeine and alcohol, sleep hygiene.

**Drug management:**

| Drug | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **SSRIs** (escitalopram, sertraline, paroxetine) and **SNRIs** (venlafaxine, duloxetine) | **First-line long-term treatment** for GAD, panic and social anxiety; take 2–4 weeks or more to act; continue about 6–12 months after improvement |
| **Benzodiazepines** (alprazolam, lorazepam, clonazepam, diazepam) | **Short-term only (2–4 weeks)** for severe distress, or while SSRI starts working; **risk of dependence, tolerance, withdrawal**, sedation, falls, impaired driving; dangerous with alcohol and opioids |
| Buspirone | GAD; not sedating, no dependence; slow onset |
| Pregabalin | GAD in some guidelines |
| **Propranolol** | Controls physical symptoms (tremor, palpitations) in **performance anxiety**; avoid in asthma |
| Hydroxyzine | Sedating antihistamine, short-term |

**Counselling:** benzodiazepines are for short use only; do not drive or drink alcohol; do not stop them suddenly after regular use (withdrawal – anxiety, insomnia, even seizures); SSRIs may increase anxiety for the first week or two before helping; practise breathing exercises during panic.

## 11.3 Psychosis (with emphasis on schizophrenia)

### Definition

**Psychosis** is a mental state in which a person **loses contact with reality**, shown by **hallucinations** (perceiving things that are not there, commonly hearing voices) and **delusions** (fixed false beliefs not in keeping with the person's culture), often with disorganised thinking and behaviour. **Schizophrenia** is the commonest chronic psychotic disorder; psychosis also occurs in bipolar mania, severe depression, dementia, delirium and drug abuse (cannabis, amphetamines, alcohol withdrawal) and with some drugs (high-dose steroids, levodopa).

### Etiopathogenesis

- **Dopamine hypothesis:** **excess dopamine activity in the mesolimbic pathway** produces positive symptoms; reduced dopamine in the mesocortical pathway may cause negative and cognitive symptoms. Glutamate (NMDA) and serotonin systems are also involved.
- **Genetic** risk (family history), problems in brain development (birth complications), **cannabis use**, urban stress, migration, social adversity.

### Clinical manifestations of schizophrenia

| Group | Features |
| --- | --- |
| **Positive symptoms** | **Hallucinations** (voices commenting or commanding), **delusions** (persecution, reference, grandeur, thought insertion/broadcasting), disorganised speech and behaviour, agitation |
| **Negative symptoms** | Flat emotions, poverty of speech, lack of motivation, social withdrawal, self-neglect |
| **Cognitive symptoms** | Poor attention, memory and planning |

Lack of insight is common; onset usually in late teens to early 30s; relapse often follows stopping medicines.

### Management

**Non-drug:** psychoeducation of patient and family, supportive therapy, CBT for psychosis, **family therapy**, social skills and vocational training, community support, adherence support; hospital admission for risk to self or others; ECT in selected cases (catatonia, severe illness).

**Drug management – antipsychotics:**

| Group | Examples | Main adverse effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Typical (first-generation)** – D2 blockers | **Haloperidol**, chlorpromazine, fluphenazine, trifluoperazine | **Extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS)**: acute dystonia, parkinsonism, akathisia, **tardive dyskinesia**; high prolactin; sedation and hypotension (chlorpromazine) |
| **Atypical (second-generation)** – D2 + 5-HT2A blockers | **Risperidone, olanzapine, quetiapine, aripiprazole**, ziprasidone | **Metabolic effects** – weight gain, diabetes, raised lipids (most with olanzapine and clozapine); less EPS; risperidone – high prolactin |
| **Clozapine** | — | For **treatment-resistant schizophrenia**; risk of **agranulocytosis** – regular blood counts required; seizures, myocarditis, constipation, drooling |
| Long-acting (depot) injections | Fluphenazine decanoate, haloperidol decanoate, paliperidone palmitate | For poor adherence |

**Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS):** rare, life-threatening – high fever, **severe muscle rigidity**, confusion, unstable BP, high CPK; stop the antipsychotic, cool, hydrate, dantrolene/bromocriptine in hospital.

**Management of EPS:** acute dystonia and parkinsonism – anticholinergics (**trihexyphenidyl, IM promethazine or biperiden**); akathisia – propranolol or dose reduction; tardive dyskinesia – may be permanent; prevention by lowest effective dose and atypical drugs.

**Principles:** improvement in agitation within days, but positive symptoms take weeks; use **one antipsychotic at the lowest effective dose**; after a first episode continue for **at least 1–2 years**, longer after relapses; **monitor weight, waist, blood sugar, lipids, BP** (atypicals), and prolactin-related effects.

**Acute agitation:** oral or IM haloperidol, olanzapine or lorazepam (often haloperidol + promethazine IM in Indian practice).

**Counselling (often to the family):** the illness is treatable and relapse is common if medicines are stopped; continue medicines even when well; report stiffness, abnormal movements, high fever, or (with clozapine) sore throat and fever; healthy diet and exercise to limit weight gain; avoid alcohol and cannabis; do not drive if drowsy.

## 11.4 Quick comparison

| Feature | Depression | Anxiety | Psychosis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core problem | Low mood, loss of interest | Excessive fear and worry | Loss of contact with reality |
| Neurotransmitters | ↓ 5-HT, NA | ↓ GABA, 5-HT/NA imbalance | ↑ Dopamine (mesolimbic) |
| First-line drugs | SSRIs | SSRIs/SNRIs (benzodiazepines short-term) | Atypical or typical antipsychotics |
| Onset of drug benefit | 2–4 weeks | 2–4 weeks (benzodiazepines quick) | Days to weeks |
| Key risk | Suicide, discontinuation syndrome | Benzodiazepine dependence | EPS, metabolic effects, NMS, agranulocytosis (clozapine) |
| Psychotherapy | CBT, interpersonal therapy | CBT | Family therapy, CBT for psychosis |

## Remember

- Depression: 2 weeks of low mood/anhedonia; SSRIs first; effect in 2–4 weeks; continue 6–12 months after remission; taper; watch suicide risk and serotonin syndrome.
- Anxiety: CBT + SSRI/SNRI long term; benzodiazepines only 2–4 weeks; propranolol for performance anxiety.
- Psychosis: hallucinations and delusions; dopamine excess; typical antipsychotics → EPS; atypicals → weight gain and diabetes; clozapine for resistant cases with blood counts; NMS is an emergency.
- Never stop psychiatric medicines suddenly; involve the family in counselling.

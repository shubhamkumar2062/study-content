# Unit 4: Central drug store – storage, cold chain, expiry, disposal and documentation

This unit covers the second half of syllabus chapter 4: inventory management of the central drug store (storage conditions, methods of storage, distribution), maintaining the cold chain and the devices used (refrigerator, ice-lined refrigerator, walk-in cold rooms), FEFO and FIFO methods, removal, handling and disposal of expired drugs (including narcotics and cytotoxic drugs), and documentation of purchase and inventory.

## 4.1 Central drug store (CDS)

The **central drug store** is the main storage point of the hospital where all medicines, surgical items and consumables are received, checked, stored and issued to the dispensing units, sub-stores and wards. It is under the control of a pharmacist.

### Location and layout

- On the **ground floor**, near a loading area and lift, with easy approach for supply vehicles and easy access to the main pharmacy.
- Enough space for receipt, quarantine, storage, issue and records; room for expansion.
- Clean, dry, well-lit, well-ventilated, protected from sunlight, rain and pests; fire-safety equipment.

```text
 ┌───────────────┬───────────────┬──────────────────┬──────────────┐
 │ Receiving &   │  Quarantine   │  Main storage     │  Issue /     │
 │ unloading bay │  (awaiting    │  (racks, bins,    │  dispatch    │
 │ → checking    │  checking/QC) │  pallets)         │  counter     │
 ├───────────────┼───────────────┼──────────────────┼──────────────┤
 │ Cold room /   │ NDPS & H1     │ Flammables       │ Expired /    │
 │ refrigerators │ (double-lock) │ (separate, cool) │ rejected     │
 │               │               │                  │ (segregated) │
 ├───────────────┴───────────────┴──────────────────┴──────────────┤
 │ Office: records, computer, pharmacist in charge                  │
 └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
 Flow: receive → check → quarantine → store → issue (one direction)
```

## 4.2 Storage conditions

Medicines must be stored as stated on the label or in the pharmacopoeia, because heat, light, moisture and air make them lose potency or form harmful products.

| Label statement | Meaning (temperature) |
| --- | --- |
| Store in a freezer | about −25 °C to −10 °C |
| Store in a refrigerator / cold | 2 °C to 8 °C (do not freeze unless stated) |
| Store in a cool place | not exceeding about 25 °C (IP: between 8 °C and 25 °C) |
| Store at room temperature | the normal temperature of a working area, kept below 30 °C |
| Protect from light | keep in the original amber or opaque pack |
| Protect from moisture | keep in a tightly closed container with desiccant if supplied |

**General rules of good storage:**

1. Shelves and racks of steel; goods kept **off the floor** (on pallets) and away from walls and ceiling to allow air flow and cleaning.
2. Heavy and bulky items (IV fluids) on lower shelves; small costly items in locked cabinets.
3. Room temperature and humidity monitored with a thermometer–hygrometer, recorded twice daily.
4. Separate storage for:
   - **narcotics and psychotropics** – double-locked steel cupboard;
   - **Schedule H1 and high-alert drugs** – marked area, red labels;
   - **inflammable substances** (spirit, ether, acetone) – cool, ventilated room away from heat;
   - **corrosives and strong acids/alkalis** – low shelves, spill trays;
   - **external preparations and disinfectants** – apart from oral and injectable products;
   - **look-alike/sound-alike drugs** – not placed side by side;
   - **expired, damaged and recalled goods** – in a separate, labelled "Not for use" area.
5. Strict **pest control** and cleaning schedules.
6. Only authorised staff in the store; keys controlled.

## 4.3 Methods of storage (arrangement of stock)

| Method | Description | Merit / demerit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Alphabetical** (by generic name) | Products arranged A to Z | Easy to find; but drugs of a class are scattered |
| **Pharmacological / therapeutic class** | Antibiotics together, antihypertensives together, etc. | Good for knowledge-based picking; needs trained staff |
| **Dosage form** | Tablets, capsules, liquids, injections, externals, IV fluids separately | Suits different storage needs; usually combined with alphabetical order within each form |
| **Frequency of use** | Fast-moving items near the issue counter | Saves time |
| **Manufacturer-wise** | Products of one company together | Easy returns; rarely used in hospitals |
| **Bin location / coded system** | Each place has a number recorded in the computer | Precise, suits large stores and software |

In practice a **combination** is used – for example dosage form first, then alphabetical by generic name, with a bin card on each place. Each item should have one fixed location shown on the stock record.

## 4.4 Distribution from the central store

1. Sub-stores, wards and departments send a **written or electronic indent** (requisition) on fixed days, signed by the sister-in-charge or head of department.
2. The pharmacist checks the indent against past use and stock, and approves quantities.
3. Items are picked using **FEFO**, packed, and issued with an **issue voucher**; the receiver signs.
4. Stock register/computer is updated; urgent indents are handled at any time.
5. Narcotic and H1 drugs are issued only on separate special indents with entries in their registers.

## 4.5 FEFO and FIFO

| | FIFO – First In, First Out | FEFO – First Expiry, First Out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rule | The stock received first is issued first | The stock with the earliest expiry date is issued first, whatever its date of receipt |
| Basis | Date of receipt | Date of expiry |
| Use | Items with no expiry or similar expiry | **Preferred for medicines** |
| Example | Batch A received in January is issued before batch B received in March | Batch B (received March, expiry next June) is issued before batch A (received January, expiry the year after) |

To make FEFO work: place newly received stock **behind** older stock, write expiry dates boldly on the outer carton, use computer alerts, and check expiry monthly.

## 4.6 Cold chain

**Cold chain** is the system of people, equipment and procedures that keeps temperature-sensitive products (vaccines, insulin, some biologicals, oxytocin, some antibiotics and eye drops) within the recommended temperature range – usually **+2 °C to +8 °C** – at every step from the manufacturer to the patient.

```text
Manufacturer → State store (walk-in coolers / freezers)
   → Regional / district store (ILR, deep freezer)
   → Hospital / PHC (ILR / pharmacy refrigerator)
   → Session site / ward (vaccine carrier with conditioned ice packs)
   → Patient
   At every step: temperature recorded, no exposure to heat or freezing
```

**Why it matters:** heat reduces potency of all vaccines (damage is cumulative and irreversible); freezing destroys **freeze-sensitive** vaccines such as hepatitis B, pentavalent, DPT, Td/TT, IPV and PCV. Damaged vaccine looks normal, so the patient may get no protection.

### Devices used for cold storage

| Device | Temperature | Use and features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Domestic / pharmacy refrigerator** | +2 to +8 °C (main compartment) | Insulin, some injections; the freezer compartment is not for vaccines; pharmacy-grade models have alarms and stable temperature |
| **Ice-lined refrigerator (ILR)** | +2 to +8 °C | Top-opening; a lining of water-filled tubes/ice packs around the cabinet keeps the inside cold for long power cuts; used for vaccines at hospitals and PHCs; vaccines kept in baskets, freeze-sensitive vaccines in the upper part away from the walls |
| **Deep freezer** | about −15 to −25 °C | Freezing ice packs; storage of vaccines that may be frozen (for example OPV) at higher stores |
| **Walk-in cooler / cold room (WIC)** | +2 to +8 °C | Large insulated room for bulk vaccine storage at state/regional level; has two cooling units, alarm and generator back-up |
| **Walk-in freezer (WIF)** | about −15 to −25 °C | Large-scale frozen storage |
| **Cold box** | +2 to +8 °C with ice packs | Transport and short-term storage (several days) |
| **Vaccine carrier** | +2 to +8 °C with conditioned ice packs | Carrying vaccines to session sites for a day |

### Good cold-chain practice

1. Keep a **thermometer** (or data logger) inside every unit; record temperature **twice daily** on a log sheet; act if outside 2–8 °C.
2. Do not open the door often; do not store food or other items; keep the unit 10 cm away from the wall and out of sunlight.
3. Use **conditioned ice packs** (left at room temperature until ice starts melting) in carriers to avoid freezing vaccines.
4. Check the **vaccine vial monitor (VVM)**: if the inner square is lighter than the outer circle, use; if it matches or is darker, discard.
5. Suspect freezing? Do the **shake test** for freeze-sensitive vaccines.
6. Have a written **contingency plan** for power failure (where to shift stock).
7. Follow FEFO and the "earliest expiry, opened vial first" rule as allowed by policy.

## 4.7 Expired drugs – removal, handling and disposal

### Removal and handling

1. **Monthly expiry check** of all shelves; prepare a **near-expiry list** (for example items expiring in the next 3 months) and circulate it so that wards use them first or the stock is transferred to a department that can use it.
2. Return near-expiry stock to the supplier where the purchase contract allows **replacement**.
3. Remove expired items from shelves immediately; mark "EXPIRED – NOT FOR USE"; keep in a separate locked area.
4. Enter details (name, batch, quantity, value, expiry) in the **expired drugs register**.
5. A **condemnation (write-off) committee** inspects and approves write-off; then disposal is done as per law.

### Disposal methods (general)

Disposal must follow the **Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016** and the hospital policy. Under these rules, discarded or expired medicines and cytotoxic drugs go into the **yellow category**.

| Type of pharmaceutical waste | Usual method |
| --- | --- |
| Expired / discarded medicines (general) | Return to manufacturer/supplier, or send to the common bio-medical waste treatment facility for **incineration** |
| Solids, semi-solids, powders | Incineration; encapsulation or inertisation where incineration is not available |
| Liquids (non-hazardous, small amounts) | Dilution and disposal only where permitted; otherwise incineration |
| Aerosol cans | Not burned (explosion risk); sent to the treatment facility |
| Antibiotics, cytotoxic drugs | Never to drains or landfill; incineration at high temperature |

### Disposal of narcotics

- Expired or unusable NDPS drugs remain under control until destroyed.
- They are listed, kept locked, and destroyed by a **committee** (for example the medical superintendent's nominee, pharmacist in charge and another officer), in the manner allowed by the NDPS Act and state rules, with information to or presence of the authority where required.
- A **destruction certificate** is prepared and signed, and entries made in the NDPS register so that the stock balance is closed.
- Partly used ampoules on wards: the unused part is discarded in the presence of a witness and both sign the register; empty ampoules are returned to the pharmacy when the refill is collected.

### Disposal of cytotoxic drugs

- Cytotoxic (anticancer) drugs and everything contaminated with them (vials, syringes, gloves, gowns) are collected in **yellow bags/containers labelled "Cytotoxic"**.
- Expired cytotoxic drugs are **returned to the manufacturer/supplier** or sent for **incineration at very high temperature (above 1200 °C)**, as required by the BMW Rules.
- Handlers wear gloves, gown, mask and eye protection; a **spill kit** is kept ready.
- Cytotoxics are never put into drains, ordinary garbage or low-temperature burning.

## 4.8 Documentation – purchase and inventory

Good records show what was bought, what is in stock, where it went, and whether laws were followed. They are needed for audits and accreditation.

### Purchase documents

| Document | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Annual requirement / indent to purchase | Quantity needed, approved by authority |
| Tender documents, comparative statement, approval minutes | Proof of fair selection of supplier |
| Supply / rate contract | Terms, prices, period |
| Purchase order (PO) | Order placed on the supplier |
| Delivery challan and invoice (bill) | What the supplier sent and its value |
| Goods received note (GRN) / inspection report | What was received, checked and accepted |
| Quality test reports | Manufacturer's certificate, lab test results |
| Payment records | Bills passed and paid |

### Inventory documents

| Document | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| **Stock register / stock ledger** (or software) | Item-wise receipts, issues and balance with batch and expiry |
| **Bin card** | Card at the storage place showing receipts, issues and balance |
| **Indent book / issue voucher** | Requests from wards and issues made |
| **Narcotic (NDPS) register** | Item-wise receipt, issue and daily balance with signatures |
| **Schedule H1 register** | Prescriber, patient, drug and quantity (kept 3 years) |
| **Expiry / near-expiry register** | Items due to expire and action taken |
| **Cold chain temperature log** | Twice-daily temperature readings |
| **Physical stock verification report** | Periodic counting compared with records; differences explained |
| **Condemnation register** | Items written off and how they were disposed |

**Physical verification** is done periodically (for example every quarter for A-class items and yearly for others). Any **discrepancy** (shortage or excess) is investigated and reported to the authority.

## Remember

- Central drug store: ground floor, one-way flow (receive → check → quarantine → store → issue), separate areas for NDPS, flammables, cold, expired.
- Refrigerator 2–8 °C; freezer about −25 to −10 °C; IP cool place 8–25 °C.
- Storage methods: alphabetical, therapeutic class, dosage form, frequency of use, bin-coded; usually combined.
- FEFO (by expiry) is preferred over FIFO (by receipt) for medicines.
- Cold chain 2–8 °C; freeze-sensitive vaccines (HepB, pentavalent, DPT, Td, IPV, PCV) must never freeze; VVM and shake test.
- ILR keeps 2–8 °C through long power cuts; deep freezer and WIF about −15 to −25 °C; WIC 2–8 °C.
- Expired and cytotoxic drugs: yellow category (BMW Rules 2016); cytotoxics returned to maker or incinerated above 1200 °C.
- Narcotics destroyed by a committee with a destruction certificate and register entries.

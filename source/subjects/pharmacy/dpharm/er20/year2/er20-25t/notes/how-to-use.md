# How to use this guide

Hospital and Clinical Pharmacy is a 75-hour second-year D.Pharm theory subject (3 hours a week) with a 25-hour practical (ER20-25P). The syllabus lists 12 chapters (the PCI table numbers them 1, 2 and 4 to 13; the committees chapter carries the infection control committee as well). In this guide the large chapters are split and the very short ones are joined, so there are 12 units:

| Guide unit | Syllabus chapter | Hours in syllabus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Hospital pharmacy – organisation, standards, NQAS, NABH | 1 | 6 |
| 2. Committees (P&T, Infection Control) and hospital formulary | 2 | 4 |
| 3. Drug lists, purchase procedures and inventory control | 4 (first half) | 14 shared |
| 4. Central drug store, cold chain, expiry, disposal, documentation | 4 (second half) | 14 shared |
| 5. Drug distribution | 5 | 7 |
| 6. Compounding, IV admixtures and TPN | 6 | 4 |
| 7. Radiopharmaceuticals and computers in hospital pharmacy | 7 and 8 | 2 + 2 |
| 8. Clinical pharmacy – development, clinical terms, daily activities | 9 (first part) | 12 shared |
| 9. Pharmaceutical care, drug-related problems, MTM, HMR | 9 (second part) | 12 shared |
| 10. Clinical laboratory tests | 10 | 10 |
| 11. Poisoning and drug/poison information centres | 11 | 6 |
| 12. Pharmacovigilance, medication errors and drug interactions | 12 and 13 | 2 + 6 |

Study one unit every two or three days. Read the notes, redo every worked example with your own numbers, draw the flow charts once from memory, and then answer the question bank without looking. Units 3, 4, 8, 9 and 10 carry the most hours – give them extra time.

## Exam pattern (ER-2020 theory paper)

D.Pharm ER-2020 examinations are yearly. The theory paper is **80 marks, 3 hours**; another 20 marks come from sessional (internal) assessment. The usual ER-2020 board paper looks like this. Confirm the exact split with your college, because boards sometimes change the format.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Objective questions (MCQs / one-word / fill in the blanks) – all compulsory | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| II | Short answers – attempt 10 of 11 | 10 × 3 = 30 |
| III | Long answers – attempt 6 of 7 | 6 × 5 = 30 |

In this guide the **long** questions are 5-mark answers and the **short** questions are 3-mark answers, to match this pattern. A few **very-short** (2-mark) questions are included for quick revision and for sessional tests.

The practical examination (ER20-25P) is assessed separately; the practical guides in this pack follow the eight experiments in the syllabus.

## Probability ratings

- **High** – asked in most years; prepare the full answer with flow charts, tables and examples.
- **Medium** – asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** – rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

Ratings are an estimate from the syllabus weight and the kind of questions usually set in D.Pharm papers. They are not a promise.

## Scoring tips for Hospital and Clinical Pharmacy

1. **Begin every answer with a definition.** Hospital formulary, pharmaceutical care, unit dose, pharmacovigilance, medication error, drug interaction – examiners look for it first.
2. **Use flow charts** for processes: tender procedure, unit-dose distribution, IV admixture preparation, pharmaceutical care cycle, drug information query, management of poisoning.
3. **Use tables for comparisons:** FIFO vs FEFO, floor stock vs unit dose, ABC vs VED, primary vs secondary vs tertiary sources, Type A vs Type B ADRs, obstructive vs restrictive PFT, MTM vs HMR.
4. **Show calculations step by step** – EOQ, reorder level, inventory turnover, ABC cut-offs, drops per minute, creatinine clearance, child dose, radioactive decay, TPN calories. Write the formula, put in the numbers with units, and box the answer.
5. **Quote standard numbers correctly:** cold chain 2–8 °C; Schedule H1 register kept 3 years; Naranjo ≥9 definite, 5–8 probable; Na 135–145 and K 3.5–5.0 mmol/L; INR 2–3 for warfarin; FEV1/FVC below 0.70 = obstruction; Tc-99m half-life about 6 hours.
6. **Name examples:** high-alert drugs, reserve antibiotics, LASA pairs in Tall Man letters, antidotes with their poisons, enzyme inducers and inhibitors.
7. **For a 5-mark long answer**, aim for about one and a half pages: definition, classification or table, flow chart, 4–6 explained points, advantages and disadvantages, and the pharmacist's role.
8. **For a 3-mark short answer**, write 5–6 crisp points or a small table; do not write an essay.
9. **Always add "role of the pharmacist"** where it fits (committees, AMR, NABH, PV, medication errors) – it is the heart of this subject.

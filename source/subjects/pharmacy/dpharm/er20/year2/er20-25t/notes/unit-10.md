# Unit 10: Clinical laboratory tests – significance and interpretation

This unit covers syllabus chapter 10: clinical laboratory tests used to evaluate disease states and drug therapy – haematological tests, liver function tests, renal function tests, thyroid function tests, tests for cardiac disorders, fluid and electrolyte balance, and pulmonary function tests. Worked examples cover creatinine clearance, anion gap and maintenance fluids.

> **Reference ranges** below are typical adult values used in Indian textbooks. Each laboratory prints its own range next to the result, depending on its method and units; always interpret against that range and the patient's clinical picture. Values in children and pregnancy differ.

## 10.1 Why pharmacists need lab tests

1. **Diagnosis and severity** – to understand the disease being treated.
2. **Choice and dose of drug** – kidney and liver function decide doses (aminoglycosides, metformin, vancomycin).
3. **Monitoring effectiveness** – blood glucose and HbA1c, INR for warfarin, TSH for levothyroxine, lipids for statins.
4. **Detecting toxicity / ADRs** – low white cells (carbimazole, clozapine), raised liver enzymes (anti-TB drugs), high potassium (ACE inhibitors, spironolactone), low platelets (heparin).
5. **Therapeutic drug monitoring** – serum levels of digoxin, phenytoin, lithium, aminoglycosides.

## 10.2 Haematological tests

### Complete blood count (CBC)

| Test | Typical adult range | Raised in | Lowered in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Haemoglobin (Hb) | Men about 13–17 g/dL; women about 12–15 g/dL | Polycythaemia, dehydration, high altitude | **Anaemia**, bleeding, bone-marrow suppression |
| RBC count | Men about 4.5–5.5; women about 4.0–5.0 million/µL | Polycythaemia, dehydration | Anaemia, haemolysis |
| Haematocrit (PCV) | Men about 40–50%; women about 36–46% | Dehydration, polycythaemia | Anaemia, over-hydration |
| Total WBC (TLC) | about 4,000–11,000/µL | **Leucocytosis** – bacterial infection, inflammation, steroids, leukaemia | **Leucopenia** – viral infections, drugs (carbimazole, clozapine, cytotoxics), marrow failure |
| Platelets | about 1.5–4.5 lakh/µL | Inflammation, bleeding, after splenectomy | **Thrombocytopenia** – dengue, heparin, cytotoxics, ITP |
| ESR (Westergren) | Men about 0–15; women about 0–20 mm in 1st hour | Infection, TB, inflammation, rheumatoid arthritis, pregnancy, anaemia | Polycythaemia, sickle cell disease |
| Reticulocyte count | about 0.5–2.5% | Bleeding, haemolysis, response to iron/B12 treatment | Marrow failure, untreated deficiency |

### Differential leucocyte count (DLC)

| Cell | Typical % | Raised in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neutrophils | 40–75 | Bacterial infections, steroids, stress |
| Lymphocytes | 20–40 | Viral infections, TB, lymphocytic leukaemia |
| Monocytes | 2–10 | TB, chronic infections, malaria |
| Eosinophils | 1–6 | Allergy, asthma, parasitic worms, drug reactions |
| Basophils | 0–1 | Myeloproliferative disorders |

### Red cell indices (help classify anaemia)

| Index | Range | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MCV (mean corpuscular volume) | about 80–100 fL | Low = **microcytic** (iron deficiency, thalassaemia); high = **macrocytic** (vitamin B12/folate deficiency, methotrexate, alcohol) |
| MCH | about 27–32 pg | Low in iron deficiency (hypochromic) |
| MCHC | about 32–36 g/dL | Low in iron deficiency |

### Coagulation tests

| Test | Typical value | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bleeding time | about 2–7 min | Platelet function (prolonged by aspirin) |
| Prothrombin time (PT) | about 11–15 s (lab-specific) | Extrinsic pathway; liver function; **warfarin** monitoring |
| **INR** | about 1.0 in people not on anticoagulants | Standardised PT; usual target **2–3** for most warfarin indications (higher for some mechanical valves) |
| aPTT | about 25–40 s (lab-specific) | Intrinsic pathway; **unfractionated heparin** monitoring (kept about 1.5–2.5 times control) |

## 10.3 Liver function tests (LFT)

| Test | Typical range | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total bilirubin | about 0.3–1.2 mg/dL | Raised → **jaundice** (visible above about 2–3 mg/dL) |
| Direct (conjugated) bilirubin | up to about 0.3 mg/dL | Raised in liver-cell disease and **obstruction** (cholestasis) |
| Indirect (unconjugated) | difference of the two | Raised in **haemolysis** (pre-hepatic jaundice), Gilbert's syndrome |
| ALT (SGPT) | up to about 40 U/L | More **liver-specific**; raised in hepatitis (viral, drug-induced) |
| AST (SGOT) | up to about 40 U/L | Also in heart and muscle; AST:ALT more than 2 suggests alcoholic liver disease |
| ALP (alkaline phosphatase) | about 40–130 U/L in adults | Raised in **cholestasis** and bone disease (normal growth in children, pregnancy) |
| GGT | up to about 50 U/L | Raised in cholestasis, alcohol use, enzyme-inducing drugs |
| Total protein / albumin | about 6–8 / 3.5–5.0 g/dL | Low albumin → chronic liver disease, malnutrition, nephrotic syndrome (affects protein-bound drugs like phenytoin) |
| PT / INR | see above | Prolonged → reduced synthetic function of liver |

**Patterns:**

```text
Hepatocellular (hepatitis):   ALT, AST ↑↑↑  ALP mildly ↑  bilirubin ↑
Cholestatic (obstruction):    ALP, GGT ↑↑↑  conjugated bilirubin ↑  ALT mild ↑
Haemolytic (pre-hepatic):     unconjugated bilirubin ↑  enzymes normal
Chronic liver failure:        albumin ↓  INR ↑  bilirubin ↑
```

**Drugs that can damage the liver:** paracetamol (overdose), isoniazid, rifampicin and pyrazinamide (anti-TB therapy), valproate, methotrexate, amoxicillin–clavulanate (cholestasis), statins (enzyme rise). The pharmacist watches LFTs in patients on these drugs.

## 10.4 Renal function tests (RFT/KFT)

| Test | Typical range | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blood urea | about 15–40 mg/dL | Raised in kidney failure, dehydration, GI bleeding, high-protein diet, catabolic states |
| BUN (blood urea nitrogen) | about 7–20 mg/dL | Same as urea (BUN ≈ urea ÷ 2.14) |
| Serum creatinine | about 0.6–1.2 mg/dL (men); 0.5–1.1 (women) | Better marker of kidney function; depends on muscle mass (may look "normal" in thin elderly despite poor function) |
| Creatinine clearance / eGFR | eGFR above about 90 mL/min/1.73 m² is normal | Used to stage chronic kidney disease and **adjust doses** |
| Serum uric acid | about 3.5–7.2 mg/dL (men) | Gout, kidney failure, cytotoxic therapy (tumour lysis), thiazides |
| Urine routine | Specific gravity about 1.005–1.030; no protein, glucose, ketones, cells, casts | Protein → kidney disease; glucose → diabetes; ketones → DKA, starvation |
| Urine albumin | microalbuminuria about 30–300 mg/day | Early diabetic or hypertensive kidney damage |

### Creatinine clearance – Cockcroft–Gault equation

```text
CrCl (mL/min) = [(140 − age in years) × body weight in kg] / (72 × serum creatinine in mg/dL)
               × 0.85 for females
```

**Worked example 1.** A 60-year-old man weighing 70 kg has serum creatinine 2.0 mg/dL.

```text
CrCl = [(140 − 60) × 70] / (72 × 2.0) = (80 × 70) / 144 = 5600 / 144 ≈ 38.9 mL/min
For a woman with the same values: 38.9 × 0.85 ≈ 33 mL/min
```

A CrCl of about 39 mL/min is moderately reduced function: drugs cleared by the kidney (aminoglycosides, vancomycin, many beta-lactams, acyclovir, enoxaparin, metformin, digoxin) may need a lower dose or longer dosing interval – check a renal dosing reference.

**Nephrotoxic drugs:** aminoglycosides, amphotericin B, NSAIDs (especially with ACE inhibitors/ARBs and diuretics – the "triple whammy"), vancomycin, cisplatin, radiocontrast media, lithium.

## 10.5 Thyroid function tests

| Test | Typical range | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **TSH** | about 0.4–4.0 mIU/L | Best single screening test (sensitive to small changes) |
| Free T4 | about 0.8–1.8 ng/dL | Active, unbound hormone |
| Free T3 / total T3 | lab-specific | Raised in T3-toxicosis |
| Total T4 | about 5–12 µg/dL | Changes with binding protein levels (pregnancy, oral contraceptives raise it) |
| Thyroid antibodies (anti-TPO) | negative | Positive in Hashimoto's and Graves' disease |

**Interpretation:**

| Condition | TSH | Free T4 / T3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary hypothyroidism | **High** | **Low** |
| Subclinical hypothyroidism | High | Normal |
| Primary hyperthyroidism (Graves') | **Low** (suppressed) | **High** |
| Secondary (pituitary) hypothyroidism | Low or normal | Low |

**Drug points:** levothyroxine dose is adjusted by TSH, checked about 6–8 weeks after a change; amiodarone and lithium can cause thyroid dysfunction; carbimazole needs a WBC check if fever or sore throat develops (agranulocytosis).

## 10.6 Tests associated with cardiac disorders

### Cardiac markers (myocardial infarction)

| Marker | Rises after onset of chest pain | Returns to normal | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cardiac troponin (I or T)** | within about 3–6 hours | after about 7–14 days | Most specific and sensitive; high-sensitivity assays detect earlier |
| **CK-MB** | about 4–6 hours | about 2–3 days | Useful to detect re-infarction |
| LDH | about 12–24 hours | about 10–14 days | Older, less specific test |
| Myoglobin | within 1–3 hours | about 1 day | Early but not specific |

### Other cardiac tests

| Test | Typical / target values | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lipid profile | Total cholesterol below 200 mg/dL desirable; LDL below 100 mg/dL (lower targets in high-risk patients); HDL above 40 (men) / 50 (women) mg/dL; triglycerides below 150 mg/dL | Risk of atherosclerosis; statin monitoring |
| BNP / NT-proBNP | BNP below about 100 pg/mL usually excludes heart failure | Diagnosis and follow-up of heart failure |
| hs-CRP | low values preferred | Inflammation, cardiovascular risk |
| Electrocardiogram (ECG) | — | ST elevation in MI, arrhythmias, QT prolongation (drugs: macrolides, fluoroquinolones, antipsychotics), hyper/hypokalaemia changes |
| Echocardiography (2D echo) | Ejection fraction normally about 55–70% | Heart failure, valve disease |
| Serum digoxin | lab therapeutic range (narrow) | Toxicity more likely with low potassium, high calcium, kidney failure |

## 10.7 Fluid and electrolyte balance

### Body fluids

- **Total body water** ≈ 60% of body weight in an adult man (less in women and the elderly, more in infants).
- About **two-thirds is intracellular (ICF)** and **one-third extracellular (ECF)**; of the ECF about three-quarters is interstitial fluid and one-quarter plasma.

```text
70 kg man → total body water ≈ 42 L
   ICF ≈ 28 L        ECF ≈ 14 L
                     ├─ interstitial ≈ 10.5 L
                     └─ plasma       ≈ 3.5 L
```

**Dehydration** – thirst, dry mouth, poor skin turgor, low urine output, low BP, raised urea; treated with ORS or IV fluids. **Fluid overload** – oedema, breathlessness, raised JVP; treated by restricting fluids and diuretics.

**Maintenance fluids in children (Holliday–Segar "100/50/20" rule) – worked example 2.** For a 20 kg child:

```text
First 10 kg  × 100 mL/kg/day = 1000 mL
Next 10 kg   ×  50 mL/kg/day =  500 mL
Total maintenance            = 1500 mL/day (≈ 62 mL/h)
(above 20 kg add 20 mL/kg/day for each extra kg)
```

### Electrolytes

| Electrolyte | Typical range | Low (hypo-) – causes, drugs | High (hyper-) – causes, drugs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sodium | 135–145 mmol/L | Diuretics (thiazides), SIADH (carbamazepine, SSRIs), vomiting, diarrhoea → confusion, seizures | Dehydration, diabetes insipidus, hypertonic saline → thirst, confusion |
| Potassium | 3.5–5.0 mmol/L | Loop and thiazide diuretics, salbutamol, insulin, vomiting, diarrhoea → weakness, arrhythmias, raises digoxin toxicity | Kidney failure, ACE inhibitors/ARBs, spironolactone, potassium supplements → **dangerous arrhythmias** |
| Chloride | 98–106 mmol/L | Vomiting (with alkalosis) | Saline excess, dehydration |
| Bicarbonate | 22–28 mmol/L | Metabolic acidosis (diabetic ketoacidosis, kidney failure, diarrhoea) | Metabolic alkalosis (vomiting, diuretics) |
| Calcium (total) | 8.5–10.5 mg/dL | Hypoparathyroidism, vitamin D deficiency, low albumin → tetany, cramps | Hyperparathyroidism, cancer, thiazides, excess vitamin D |
| Magnesium | 1.7–2.4 mg/dL | Diuretics, PPIs (long-term), alcoholism | Kidney failure, antacid excess |
| Phosphate | 2.5–4.5 mg/dL | Refeeding syndrome, antacids | Kidney failure |

**Anion gap – worked example 3.** Na 140, Cl 100, HCO3 14 mmol/L.

```text
Anion gap = Na − (Cl + HCO3) = 140 − (100 + 14) = 26 mmol/L
Typical reference about 8–12 mmol/L → high anion gap metabolic acidosis
(causes: diabetic ketoacidosis, lactic acidosis, kidney failure,
 poisoning with methanol, ethylene glycol, salicylates)
```

## 10.8 Pulmonary function tests (PFT)

### Spirometry

| Measurement | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **FVC** (forced vital capacity) | Total volume blown out forcefully after a full breath in |
| **FEV1** (forced expiratory volume in 1 second) | Volume blown out in the first second |
| **FEV1/FVC ratio** | Normally about 0.75–0.80 in young adults (less with age) |
| **PEFR** (peak expiratory flow rate) | Fastest flow; measured at home with a peak flow meter (asthma diary) |
| Reversibility test | FEV1 repeated after an inhaled bronchodilator; an increase of 12% or more **and** 200 mL or more supports asthma |

**Interpretation:**

| Pattern | FEV1 | FVC | FEV1/FVC | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Obstructive** | Reduced | Normal or reduced | **Reduced (below 0.70)** | Asthma (reversible), COPD (not fully reversible), bronchiectasis |
| **Restrictive** | Reduced | **Reduced** | **Normal or high** | Pulmonary fibrosis (also drug-induced: amiodarone, methotrexate, bleomycin), obesity, chest-wall disease |

### Arterial blood gases (ABG) and oxygen saturation

| Parameter | Typical range |
| --- | --- |
| pH | 7.35–7.45 |
| PaCO2 | 35–45 mmHg (raised = respiratory acidosis, as in COPD exacerbation or opioid overdose) |
| PaO2 | about 80–100 mmHg on room air |
| HCO3 | 22–26 mmol/L |
| SpO2 (pulse oximeter) | about 95–100% in healthy people |

## Remember

- Always read results against the lab's own reference range and the patient's condition.
- Anaemia by MCV: low = iron deficiency; high = B12/folate (or methotrexate).
- INR target usually 2–3 for warfarin; aPTT for heparin.
- LFT patterns: ALT/AST high = hepatocellular; ALP/GGT high = cholestatic; albumin low + INR high = poor synthesis.
- Cockcroft–Gault: CrCl = (140 − age) × weight / (72 × SCr) × 0.85 for women; adjust renally cleared drugs.
- TSH high + free T4 low = primary hypothyroidism; TSH low + T4 high = hyperthyroidism.
- Troponin = most specific MI marker; CK-MB returns to normal sooner.
- Na 135–145, K 3.5–5.0 mmol/L; diuretics → low K; ACE inhibitors/spironolactone → high K; anion gap = Na − (Cl + HCO3).
- Obstructive: FEV1/FVC below 0.70; restrictive: FVC low, ratio normal/high; reversibility ≥12% and ≥200 mL suggests asthma.

# Wound dressing (simulated cases and role play)

**Aim:** To demonstrate the aseptic dressing of a wound in two simulated cases and to select a suitable dressing material.

## Principle

A **wound** is a break in the continuity of skin or tissue. Dressing a wound keeps it clean, absorbs discharge, keeps a moist healing surface where suitable, prevents infection and protects it from injury. Dressing is done by an **aseptic (no-touch) technique** so that no new organisms enter the wound. The choice of dressing depends on the type of wound and the amount of exudate.

## Requirements

Sterile dressing pack (gauze, cotton balls, forceps, gallipot, drape), sterile gloves, normal saline, antiseptic as per hospital policy (for example povidone-iodine), dressing materials (sterile gauze pads, paraffin gauze, transparent film, foam or hydrocolloid as available), adhesive tape or bandage, scissors, waste bags (yellow for soiled dressings), sharps container, mannequin or role-play volunteer, documentation sheet.

## Common dressing materials

| Dressing | Suitable for |
| --- | --- |
| Dry sterile gauze | Clean surgical wounds with little exudate; cover dressing |
| Paraffin gauze (tulle) | Superficial burns and abrasions – does not stick |
| Transparent film | Clean shallow wounds, IV cannula sites; allows viewing |
| Hydrocolloid | Light to moderate exudate, pressure ulcers |
| Hydrogel | Dry wounds, to add moisture |
| Foam | Moderate to heavy exudate |
| Alginate | Heavy exudate, slight bleeding |

## Procedure

1. Check the order and the patient's identity; explain the procedure and get consent; ensure privacy; give pain relief beforehand if needed.
2. Wash hands; put on clean gloves; position the patient comfortably and expose only the wound area.
3. Remove the old dressing gently (moisten with saline if stuck); look at it for amount and type of discharge; discard in the yellow bag; remove gloves and wash hands.
4. Open the sterile pack on a clean trolley without touching the inside; pour saline into the gallipot; put on sterile gloves.
5. **Assess the wound:** site, size, depth, colour of the wound bed, exudate, smell, edges, signs of infection (redness, swelling, heat, pus, fever).
6. **Clean the wound** with saline-soaked swabs using forceps, from the cleanest area to the dirtiest – for a clean incision, from the wound outward, one stroke per swab; discard each swab after one stroke.
7. Apply antiseptic only if prescribed by the hospital policy; dry the surrounding skin.
8. Apply the chosen dressing so that it covers the wound with a margin; secure with tape or bandage (not too tight).
9. Dispose of waste: soiled dressings in yellow bags, sharps in the sharps container; remove gloves; wash hands.
10. **Document:** date, wound appearance and size, dressing used, patient's response, next dressing date.

## Observations (simulated cases)

| Case | Wound description | Dressing chosen | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Post-operative abdominal incision, day 3 | Clean, dry, edges joined, no redness | Dry sterile gauze pad / film | Clean wound with minimal exudate |
| 2. Superficial burn on forearm (scald) | Red, moist, small blisters | Paraffin gauze covered with sterile gauze and light bandage | Non-adherent; protects and allows healing |

Role play: one student acts as the pharmacist/dresser, one as the patient; the observer checks steps against a checklist (hand hygiene, aseptic technique, cleaning direction, waste disposal, documentation).

## Result

Aseptic wound dressing was demonstrated for two simulated cases with appropriate dressing selection, waste disposal and documentation.

## Precautions

1. Hand hygiene before and after every step; maintain asepsis.
2. One swab, one stroke; never go back from a dirty area to a clean one.
3. Do not use cotton wool directly on an open wound.
4. Report signs of infection to the doctor; a swab for culture may be needed.
5. Do not apply bandages too tightly.

## Viva questions

1. **What are the signs of wound infection?** Redness, swelling, heat, pain, pus, foul smell, fever.
2. **Which solution is commonly used for cleaning wounds?** Sterile normal saline.
3. **Which dressing is non-adherent for burns?** Paraffin gauze (tulle).
4. **In which bag are soiled dressings discarded?** Yellow bag.
5. **Why clean from clean to dirty?** To avoid carrying organisms into the cleaner area.

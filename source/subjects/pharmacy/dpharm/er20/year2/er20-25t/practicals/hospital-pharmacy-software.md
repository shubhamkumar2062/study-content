# Use of hospital pharmacy software and digital health tools

**Aim:** To use hospital pharmacy software (or a training/demo version) for prescription processing, stock management and reports, and to become familiar with common digital health tools.

## Principle

Hospital pharmacy software links prescriptions, patient profiles, stock and billing. It checks doses and interactions, keeps legal registers, raises reorder and expiry alerts and prepares reports. Digital health tools such as electronic health records, online ADR reporting, drug databases and telemedicine platforms support safe medicine use.

## Requirements

Computers with a hospital information system (HIS) pharmacy module, the hospital's own software, or a demonstration/training version; internet access; sample prescriptions and stock data; printer.

## Procedure

1. **Log in** with your own user name and password (never share passwords).
2. **Patient registration / search:** find the patient by hospital number or name; check allergies and weight in the profile.
3. **Prescription entry or receipt (CPOE):** open the electronic order; check drug, dose, frequency, route and duration; note any **interaction or dose alert** and how it is handled.
4. **Dispensing:** select batch by FEFO; print labels; record quantity issued; generate the bill or free-supply entry.
5. **Ward indent:** receive an electronic indent from a ward; approve quantities; issue and update stock.
6. **Stock management:** view current stock of an item; generate the **reorder report** (items at or below reorder level), **near-expiry report** and **ABC analysis** of consumption.
7. **Purchase:** create a purchase order; enter a goods received note (GRN) with batch, expiry and quantity.
8. **Controlled drugs:** view the electronic narcotic or Schedule H1 register and running balance.
9. **Digital health tools (demonstration):**
   - online/app-based ADR reporting under PvPI;
   - drug information and interaction databases;
   - electronic health record view (discharge summary, lab results);
   - the ABHA (Ayushman Bharat Health Account) concept for linking records;
   - teleconsultation platforms such as the government's eSanjeevani service.
10. Log out; never leave a logged-in screen unattended.

## Observations (sample exercise)

| Task | Output / remark |
| --- | --- |
| Prescription checked | Ceftriaxone 1 g IV BD, patient CrCl normal – no alert |
| Interaction alert seen | Clarithromycin + simvastatin – major; pharmacist informed prescriber |
| Batch selected | Batch with earliest expiry (FEFO) |
| Reorder report | 6 items at or below reorder level → purchase indent raised |
| Near-expiry report | 3 items expiring within 3 months → transfer/return planned |
| ABC report | A-class items: ___ % of items, ___ % of value |
| GRN entry | Batch, expiry, quantity, rate entered and verified |

**Check of a software reorder calculation:** average daily use 40 units, lead time 7 days, safety stock 120 units.

```text
Reorder level = (40 × 7) + 120 = 400 units
The software should flag the item when stock falls to 400 units.
```

## Result

Hospital pharmacy software was used for prescription processing, dispensing, indents, stock and expiry reports, purchase entries and controlled drug registers, and common digital health tools were demonstrated.

## Precautions

1. Keep passwords secret; log out after use.
2. Respect patient confidentiality; do not copy or share patient data.
3. Do not ignore alerts without checking; document the reason for any override.
4. Verify that stock in the software matches physical stock (periodic verification).
5. Keep back-up and a manual procedure for system failure.

## Viva questions

1. **What is CPOE?** Computerised prescriber order entry – doctors enter orders electronically.
2. **Name two reports a pharmacy system generates.** Reorder report, near-expiry report (also ABC, consumption, narcotic register).
3. **What is an EHR?** An electronic health record – a shareable digital record of a person's health information.
4. **What is alert fatigue?** Ignoring warnings because the system shows too many of them.
5. **Why is an audit trail important?** It records who did what and when, which is essential for controlled drugs and accountability.

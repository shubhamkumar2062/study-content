# Vaccination and injection techniques (IV, IM, SC) using mannequins

**Aim:** To demonstrate on mannequins the techniques of intramuscular, subcutaneous, intradermal and intravenous injection and of vaccination (five activities).

> Follow the current national immunisation schedule and your institution's protocol; the details below are for practice on mannequins.

## Principle

The **route** decides the speed and site of drug action. Each route has a correct **site, needle size and angle**. Safe injection practice protects the patient (right drug, dose, site, asepsis), the health worker (no needle-stick injury) and the community (safe disposal).

| Route | Usual angle | Common sites | Typical needle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intradermal (ID) | 10–15°, bevel up | Inner forearm; upper arm for BCG | 26–27 G, short |
| Subcutaneous (SC) | 45° (90° with short needle and skin pinched) | Abdomen, outer upper arm, front of thigh | 25–27 G |
| Intramuscular (IM) | 90° | Deltoid (small volumes), vastus lateralis (thigh – infants), ventrogluteal, dorsogluteal | 21–23 G, about 2.5 cm (shorter for infants) |
| Intravenous (IV) | 15–30° | Veins of the forearm, back of hand, antecubital fossa | Needle or cannula 18–24 G |

## Requirements

Injection-training mannequins (arm, thigh, buttock), syringes and needles of different sizes, IV cannula and set, ampoules and vials of water for injection (practice), alcohol swabs, cotton, tourniquet, gloves, kidney tray, sharps container, vaccine carrier with ice packs (demonstration), immunisation card.

## Procedure

### Common steps (all routes)

1. Check the prescription/immunisation schedule: right patient, drug, dose, route, time; check expiry and appearance; check allergy.
2. Wash hands; wear gloves; prepare the drug aseptically (swab vial top, draw up, expel air).
3. Explain the procedure; position the patient; select and expose the site.
4. Clean the site as per protocol and allow it to dry.
5. Inject at the correct angle and depth; inject slowly; withdraw the needle quickly; press gently with dry cotton (do not massage for insulin or vaccines unless advised).
6. **Do not recap**; discard the needle and syringe immediately in the sharps container.
7. Observe the patient (for vaccines, about 30 minutes for reactions); document.

### Activity 1 – Intramuscular injection

1. Deltoid: locate the dense part of the muscle about 2–3 finger-breadths below the acromion; use for small volumes.
2. Vastus lateralis: middle third of the outer thigh – preferred site in infants.
3. Stretch the skin, insert the needle at 90° with a quick dart, inject slowly.
4. **Z-track technique** (for irritant drugs such as iron): pull the skin to one side before injecting, release after withdrawing so the track is sealed.

### Activity 2 – Subcutaneous injection

1. Pinch a fold of skin of the abdomen (away from the navel), upper arm or thigh.
2. Insert at 45° (or 90° with short insulin needles); inject; release the fold; withdraw.
3. Rotate sites (for example insulin) to avoid lumps of fat.

### Activity 3 – Intradermal injection

1. Stretch the skin of the inner forearm (or upper arm for BCG).
2. Insert the needle bevel up at 10–15°, just under the skin surface.
3. Inject slowly – a small pale bleb (wheal) should appear; do not massage.

### Activity 4 – Intravenous injection / cannulation

1. Apply a tourniquet above the site; ask the patient to open and close the fist; select a straight, visible vein.
2. Clean the site; anchor the vein by stretching the skin below it.
3. Insert the needle or cannula bevel up at 15–30°; look for "flashback" of blood.
4. Release the tourniquet; inject slowly or connect the IV set; secure the cannula with a sterile dressing and label with the date.

### Activity 5 – Vaccination (immunisation session)

1. Keep vaccines in a vaccine carrier with conditioned ice packs; check the label, expiry and VVM; do not use frozen freeze-sensitive vaccine.
2. Check the child's immunisation card and age; screen for contraindications.
3. Give each vaccine by its correct route and site as per the national schedule – for example **BCG intradermally** in the left upper arm, **oral polio vaccine** as drops by mouth, **hepatitis B and pentavalent vaccines intramuscularly** in the anterolateral thigh of infants, **measles–rubella vaccine subcutaneously** in the right upper arm.
4. Record on the card and register; tell the parent about common reactions (fever, pain) and the next due date; observe for 30 minutes.

## Result

Intramuscular, subcutaneous, intradermal and intravenous injection techniques and vaccination were demonstrated correctly on mannequins.

## Precautions

1. Strict asepsis; one needle and syringe per injection.
2. Never recap used needles; dispose at once in sharps containers.
3. Avoid the sciatic nerve region (use the correct gluteal landmarks or other sites).
4. Check for air in syringes and IV lines.
5. Keep adrenaline and an anaphylaxis kit ready during vaccination and injections.

## Viva questions

1. **What angle is used for an IM injection?** 90°.
2. **Which IM site is preferred in infants?** Vastus lateralis (anterolateral thigh).
3. **What sign shows a correct intradermal injection?** A small pale bleb (wheal) at the site.
4. **What is the Z-track technique?** Displacing the skin before IM injection so the needle track is sealed – used for irritant drugs such as iron.
5. **Which syringe is used in immunisation?** Auto-disable syringe.

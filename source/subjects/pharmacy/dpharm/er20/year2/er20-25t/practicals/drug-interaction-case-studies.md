# Case studies on drug–drug interactions

**Aim:** To identify, interpret and report drug–drug interactions in two clinical cases and to recommend how to manage them.

> The cases below are worked examples. Your teacher will give you other cases; use an interaction reference to check each pair of drugs.

## Principle

A drug interaction occurs when one drug (the **precipitant**) changes the effect of another (the **object drug**). Interactions may be **pharmacokinetic** (absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion) or **pharmacodynamic** (additive, synergistic or opposing effects). Their clinical significance depends on **severity** (major, moderate, minor), **documentation**, **onset** and **patient factors**. The pharmacist screens every prescription, explains the mechanism, and suggests a safe alternative, dose change, timing change or monitoring.

## Requirements

Case sheets, interaction reference (book or database), National Formulary of India or similar reference, interaction reporting form, calculator.

## Procedure

1. List all drugs (prescription, OTC, herbal) with dose and start date.
2. Check every pair against an interaction reference.
3. For each interaction note: drugs involved, mechanism, expected effect, severity, onset and documentation.
4. Decide whether the interaction is clinically important in **this** patient.
5. Recommend management – avoid, substitute, change dose, separate doses, monitor.
6. Communicate to the prescriber and document; counsel the patient.

## Observations

**Case 1.** A 62-year-old man taking **simvastatin 40 mg** at night for high cholesterol is prescribed **clarithromycin 500 mg twice daily** for 7 days for a chest infection. After 4 days he complains of muscle pain and weakness; urine is dark.

| Item | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Object / precipitant | Simvastatin / clarithromycin |
| Type and mechanism | Pharmacokinetic – clarithromycin strongly inhibits the CYP3A4 enzyme that metabolises simvastatin → simvastatin levels rise |
| Effect | Myopathy, possibly rhabdomyolysis (dark urine, kidney damage) |
| Severity | Major – combination generally avoided |
| Recommendation | Stop simvastatin during the antibiotic course (or choose an antibiotic without this effect, such as amoxicillin if suitable); check CK and kidney function urgently; restart statin after the course if CK normal |
| Counselling | Report muscle pain or dark urine at once while on statins; tell every doctor about the statin |

**Case 2.** A 25-year-old woman on a **combined oral contraceptive pill** is started on anti-TB treatment containing **rifampicin**.

| Item | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Object / precipitant | Oral contraceptive (ethinylestradiol + progestin) / rifampicin |
| Type and mechanism | Pharmacokinetic – rifampicin is a strong **enzyme inducer**, increasing metabolism of the hormones → lower levels |
| Effect | Contraceptive failure (unplanned pregnancy), breakthrough bleeding |
| Onset | Delayed – induction builds up over 1–2 weeks and persists for some weeks after stopping rifampicin |
| Severity | Major |
| Recommendation | Use a reliable non-hormonal method (for example a copper IUCD or barrier method) during rifampicin therapy and for the period advised after stopping it |
| Counselling | Explain why the pill may fail; use the advised method consistently |

### Worked format for reporting (example)

```text
Patient: Mr A, 62 y        Ward: Medicine         Date: __/__/__
Drugs: simvastatin 40 mg HS; clarithromycin 500 mg BD

Recommendation: withhold simvastatin during course; check CK, creatinine
Accepted by prescriber: Yes / No     Outcome: _______________
Pharmacist: ________________
```

## Result

Two clinically significant drug–drug interactions were identified, their mechanisms explained and management recommended and documented.

## Precautions

1. Include OTC and herbal products in the drug list.
2. Not every listed interaction matters for every patient – judge clinical significance.
3. Give practical alternatives, not only warnings.
4. Remember delayed onset and offset of enzyme induction.
5. Document the prescriber's decision and the outcome.

## Viva questions

1. **Name two enzyme inducers.** Rifampicin, carbamazepine (also phenytoin, phenobarbital).
2. **Name two enzyme inhibitors.** Clarithromycin, ketoconazole (also erythromycin, fluconazole, cimetidine, grapefruit juice).
3. **Give a pharmacodynamic interaction.** Sildenafil + nitrates → severe hypotension.
4. **Give a useful interaction.** Probenecid raises penicillin levels; levodopa with carbidopa.
5. **Why are narrow therapeutic index drugs important in interactions?** Small changes in level can cause toxicity or failure (warfarin, digoxin, lithium, phenytoin).

# Orthopaedic and surgical aids

**Aim:** To identify common orthopaedic and surgical aids – knee cap, lumbo-sacral (LS) belt, abdominal belt, walker and walking stick – and to demonstrate their selection, fitting and use.

## Principle

Orthopaedic aids **support, immobilise or relieve load** on joints, muscles and the spine, and mobility aids help patients walk safely by widening the base of support. Pharmacists in hospitals and community pharmacies supply these aids, so they must know the indications, how to choose the correct size, how to fit them and how to teach the patient.

## Requirements

Knee caps (elastic knee supports) of different sizes, LS belt, abdominal belt (binder), adjustable walker, walking stick (single-point and tripod/quadripod), measuring tape, size charts of the manufacturer, a chair.

## Procedure

### Knee cap (knee support)

1. Uses: mild knee pain, osteoarthritis, minor sprains, support during activity; gives compression and warmth.
2. Measure the circumference around the middle of the knee cap (or as the size chart directs) with the leg straight; choose the size from the chart.
3. Slip it on from the foot upward; centre the opening (if any) over the patella; smooth out wrinkles.
4. It should be snug but not tight – check that there is no numbness, tingling, swelling below or colour change of the foot.
5. Remove at night unless advised; wash as directed.

### Lumbo-sacral (LS) belt

1. Uses: low back pain, lumbar disc problems, support after spinal injury or surgery, heavy lifting.
2. Measure the waist (or hip) circumference as the size chart directs.
3. Apply in standing or lying position; the rigid back stays (supports) lie over the lower spine; fasten the front Velcro starting at the bottom, firm but allowing normal breathing.
4. Advise: wear during activity, not continuously for long periods (muscles may weaken); combine with exercises advised by the physiotherapist.

### Abdominal belt (abdominal binder)

1. Uses: support after abdominal surgery or caesarean section, after delivery, hernia support (as advised), to reduce pain on coughing and movement.
2. Measure the circumference at the widest part of the abdomen; select the size.
3. Apply lying down; centre on the abdomen, overlap and fasten from below upward; it should be firm without restricting breathing.
4. Check the skin daily for redness; keep the wound dressing clean underneath.

### Walker

1. Uses: elderly patients, weakness after illness or surgery, poor balance, partial weight-bearing after leg injury.
2. Adjust height: with the patient standing upright and arms hanging, the walker handles should be at the level of the wrist crease; the elbows bend slightly (about 15–30°) when holding it.
3. Walking: move the walker forward about one step's length → step forward with the weak leg → then the strong leg; keep all four legs on the ground before stepping.
4. Sitting: back up until the chair touches the legs, reach back for the armrests, lower slowly – do not lean on the walker while sitting down.

### Walking stick (cane)

1. Uses: mild balance problems, mild weakness or pain in one leg.
2. Height: handle at the level of the wrist crease with the arm hanging; elbow slightly bent when holding.
3. Hold the stick in the hand **opposite** the weak or painful leg.
4. Move the stick and the weak leg forward together, then the strong leg.
5. On stairs: "up with the good, down with the bad" – the strong leg leads going up; the stick and weak leg lead going down.
6. Check the rubber tip regularly; tripod/quadripod sticks give more support.

## Result

The orthopaedic and surgical aids were identified, the correct size was selected, and their fitting and use were demonstrated.

## Precautions

1. Always measure; do not guess the size.
2. Too tight supports reduce circulation; too loose ones give no support.
3. Check rubber tips and locks on walkers and sticks.
4. Do not use support belts as a substitute for exercise and medical advice.
5. Remove supports and seek advice if pain, swelling, numbness or skin damage occurs.

## Viva questions

1. **In which hand is a walking stick held?** In the hand opposite the weak or painful leg.
2. **How is walker height adjusted?** Handles at the wrist crease with arms hanging; elbows slightly bent when holding.
3. **What is an LS belt used for?** Support of the lower back in low back pain and after spinal injury or surgery.
4. **Why should support belts not be worn all the time?** Long continuous use can weaken the supporting muscles.
5. **What is the rule for stairs with a stick?** Up with the good leg, down with the bad leg.

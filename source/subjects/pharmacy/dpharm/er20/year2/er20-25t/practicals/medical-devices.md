# Needles, syringes, catheters, IV sets and other medical devices

**Aim:** To identify and demonstrate the use of needles, syringes, IV cannulae, IV administration sets, urinary catheters and urine bags, Ryle's (nasogastric) tube, urine pots, colostomy bags and oxygen masks.

## Principle

Hospital pharmacies stock and issue many medical devices. The pharmacist must recognise each device, know its sizes and uses, store it correctly (sterile, dry, before expiry) and help nurses and patients use it safely. Most of these devices are **sterile and single-use**; reuse spreads infection.

## Requirements

Samples of hypodermic needles of different gauges, syringes (1 mL insulin, 2, 5, 10 mL, auto-disable syringe), IV cannulae of different sizes, IV infusion sets (macro-drip and micro-drip), Foley urinary catheter, urine collection bag, Ryle's tube, sterile urine container (urine pot), colostomy bags (one-piece and two-piece), nasal cannula, simple oxygen mask, venturi mask, non-rebreather mask, mannequin.

## Identification and use

| Device | Key features | Use and handling points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hypodermic needle** | Size by **gauge** (larger number = thinner) and length | Thin needles (26–27 G) for intradermal/SC; 21–23 G for IM; never recap a used needle; discard in a puncture-proof sharps container |
| **Syringe** | Barrel, plunger, Luer slip or Luer lock tip; graduated in mL | Choose a size close to the volume; **insulin syringe** graduated in units (match syringe to insulin strength); **auto-disable syringe** locks after one use (immunisation) |
| **IV cannula** | Plastic catheter over a needle; size in gauge with standard colour coding (for example 18 G green, 20 G pink, 22 G blue, 24 G yellow) | Larger bore for blood and rapid fluids; smaller for children and routine drugs; fix with dressing and label date |
| **IV infusion set** | Spike, drip chamber, roller clamp, injection port, Luer connector | Macro-drip about 20 drops/mL (adults); micro-drip 60 drops/mL (children, precise drugs); prime to remove air; calculate drops per minute |
| **Foley urinary catheter** | Soft tube with an inflatable balloon; size in French (Fr) units; two-way or three-way | Urinary retention, monitoring output, surgery; inserted aseptically; balloon inflated with sterile water as stated on the catheter |
| **Urine collection bag** | Bag with tubing, non-return valve and drain tap; graduated | Keep below bladder level and off the floor; empty regularly; closed system to prevent infection |
| **Ryle's (nasogastric) tube** | Long tube with markings, passed through the nose into the stomach | Feeding, giving drugs, draining stomach contents; length estimated nose → ear → xiphisternum; position confirmed by aspirate testing or X-ray before use; flush before and after drugs |
| **Urine pot (container)** | Sterile screw-capped container | Mid-stream urine for culture and routine tests; label and send promptly |
| **Colostomy bag** | Pouch fixed over a stoma; one-piece or two-piece (flange + bag); drainable or closed | Measure stoma and cut the opening to fit, protect surrounding skin, empty or change as needed |
| **Nasal cannula** | Two prongs in the nostrils | Low-flow oxygen (about 1–4 L/min) |
| **Simple face mask** | Covers nose and mouth | Moderate oxygen (about 5–10 L/min) |
| **Venturi mask** | Colour-coded adaptors giving fixed oxygen concentrations | Patients needing controlled oxygen, e.g. COPD |
| **Non-rebreather mask** | Mask with reservoir bag and valves | High oxygen (about 10–15 L/min) in emergencies; reservoir kept inflated |

## Procedure

1. Identify each device and note its size markings, sterility and expiry date on the pack.
2. Demonstrate on the mannequin: drawing up a dose with a syringe; priming an IV set and counting drops; securing a cannula; connecting a urine bag to a catheter and positioning it; measuring and marking a Ryle's tube length; fitting a colostomy bag; selecting and fitting an oxygen device and setting the flow.
3. Show safe disposal: sharps in a puncture-proof white container; contaminated plastics (tubing, bags, catheters) in the red bag; soiled dressings in the yellow bag, as per the Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules.

## Observations / calculations

**Drip rate check:** 500 mL normal saline over 4 h with a macro-drip set (20 drops/mL).

```text
Drops/min = (500 × 20) / (4 × 60) = 10,000 / 240 ≈ 42 drops/min
```

## Result

Common hospital medical devices were identified, their sizes and uses noted, and their correct use and disposal demonstrated.

## Precautions

1. Check sterility (intact pack) and expiry before use.
2. One needle, one syringe, one patient – never reuse.
3. Do not recap used needles; dispose immediately in sharps containers.
4. Keep urine bags below bladder level; never disconnect a closed system unnecessarily.
5. Confirm Ryle's tube position before every feed or drug.
6. Oxygen is a drug – give only at the prescribed flow; no smoking or flames nearby.

## Viva questions

1. **Which is thinner, a 22 G or an 18 G needle?** The 22 G needle (larger gauge number = thinner).
2. **What is an auto-disable syringe?** A syringe that locks after a single use; used in immunisation.
3. **How is the length of a Ryle's tube estimated?** Nose tip → ear lobe → xiphisternum.
4. **What are the drops per mL of a micro-drip set?** 60 drops/mL.
5. **Which oxygen device gives the highest concentration?** Non-rebreather mask with reservoir bag.

# Types of bandages and dressing materials

**Aim:** To identify common surgical dressing materials and bandages – sterile gauze, absorbent cotton, roller, crepe, triangular and tubular bandages – and to demonstrate the basic bandaging turns.

## Principle

**Dressings** are applied directly on a wound to protect it, absorb discharge and help healing. **Bandages** hold dressings or splints in place, give support or compression, and limit movement. Surgical dressing materials such as absorbent gauze and absorbent cotton wool have pharmacopoeial standards. A bandage must be firm enough to do its job without cutting off circulation.

## Requirements

Sterile gauze swabs and pads, absorbent cotton wool, roller bandages (cotton), crepe (elastic) bandages of different widths, triangular bandage, tubular bandage, adhesive tape, safety pins or clips, scissors, mannequin or volunteer.

## Identification of materials

| Material | Description | Main uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sterile absorbent gauze** | Cotton cloth of open weave, sterilised and packed as swabs or pads | Primary dressing on wounds, cleaning wounds, packing |
| **Absorbent cotton wool** | Cleaned, bleached cotton fibres that absorb fluid | Padding, cleaning skin before injections (with antiseptic), absorbent layer over gauze (never directly on an open wound – fibres stick) |
| **Roller bandage (cotton)** | Long strip rolled up | Holding dressings in place |
| **Crepe bandage** | Elastic cotton bandage that stretches and recovers | Support and compression of sprains and strains, holding dressings on joints, controlling swelling |
| **Triangular bandage** | Large triangle of cloth | Arm sling, head bandage, holding large dressings, first aid |
| **Tubular bandage** | Seamless elastic tube | Quick support for limbs and fingers, holding dressings |
| **Adhesive tape** | Plaster tape | Fixing dressings and cannulae |

## Procedure – basic bandaging

1. Explain the procedure; make the patient comfortable with the part supported in the position it should stay in.
2. Wash hands; place a dressing over any wound first.
3. Hold the roll with the rolled part upward ("head" up) and unroll it close to the skin.
4. Start with **two circular turns** below the injury to anchor the bandage.
5. Bandage from **distal (far) to proximal (near the heart)**, covering one-half to two-thirds of the previous turn each time, with even tension.
6. Use the correct turn:
   - **Circular** – overlapping circles, for anchoring and for the wrist or forehead;
   - **Spiral** – for parts of even width (arm, finger);
   - **Reverse spiral** – for parts that widen (forearm, leg);
   - **Figure-of-eight** – for joints (ankle, elbow, knee);
   - **Spica** – figure-of-eight over a joint where a limb meets the trunk (shoulder, groin, thumb);
   - **Recurrent** – for the head or a stump.
7. Finish with a circular turn and fix with tape or clip (not over the wound or on the inner side of a limb).
8. **Check circulation** beyond the bandage: colour, warmth, capillary refill (pressed nail returns to pink within about 2 seconds), numbness or tingling.

**Arm sling with triangular bandage:** place the bandage with the point under the elbow, the forearm across the chest, bring the lower end up over the forearm and tie at the side of the neck; fold and pin the point at the elbow.

## Observations

| Bandage practised | Body part | Type of turn | Circulation check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crepe | Ankle | Figure-of-eight | Toes pink, warm |
| Roller | Forearm | Reverse spiral | Fingers pink, warm |
| Triangular | Forearm | Arm sling | Fingers pink, warm |

## Result

Dressing materials and bandages were identified, and common bandaging turns were demonstrated correctly with circulation checked afterwards.

## Precautions

1. Never apply a bandage too tight; recheck circulation after some time, especially with crepe bandages and swelling.
2. Do not put cotton wool directly on an open wound.
3. Use sterile gauze for wounds; keep the packet sealed until use.
4. Leave fingers and toes exposed to check circulation.
5. Do not tie knots over a wound or bony points.

## Viva questions

1. **Which turn is used for joints?** Figure-of-eight.
2. **Why bandage from distal to proximal?** To support venous return and avoid trapping fluid below the bandage.
3. **What is a crepe bandage used for?** Support and compression of sprains and strains and holding dressings on joints.
4. **Why is cotton wool not placed directly on wounds?** Fibres stick to the wound and are hard to remove.
5. **How do you check that a bandage is not too tight?** Colour, warmth, capillary refill and sensation of the part beyond it.

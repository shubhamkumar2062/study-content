# How to use this guide

Quality Control and Standardization of Herbals (BP806ET) is a **semester-8 elective** of the PCI B.Pharm course. It has no practical paper. It builds on Pharmacognosy (semesters 4 and 5) and Herbal Drug Technology (semester 6), so revise the evaluation of crude drugs and Schedule T from those subjects first. This subject is mostly **guidelines, definitions, lists and tables** — WHO, EU, ICH and Indian law — plus the standard quality control tests and chromatographic methods. Marks come from writing the right name of each guideline, its headings in order, and one or two examples for each point.

The five units follow the five units of the PCI syllabus exactly. Unit 4 is the most mixed (stability testing, chromatography, new drug and export dossiers, and the Drugs and Cosmetics Act); study it on two days — science on one day, documents and law on the next.

Study one unit a day: read the notes, write the "Remember" list from memory, then answer the question bank. Each unit has very short (2-mark), short answer (5-mark) and long essay (10-mark) questions, rated by how often that kind of question appears in PCI-syllabus pharmacy papers.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ questions — all 20 | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

The app's full mock paper follows this pattern, and a 20-question, 20-minute quick MCQ test is also given.

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with headings, a table or flow chart, and examples.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from common patterns and syllabus weight, not a promise of what will be asked.

**Where the marks usually come from**

| Unit | Topics that keep coming back |
| --- | --- |
| 1 WHO quality control | WHO guidelines for quality control of herbal drugs (all parameters), sampling, ash and extractive values, foaming and swelling index, contaminants and their limits, evaluation methods and adulteration of crude drugs, basic tests for drugs |
| 2 Quality assurance | WHO GACP for medicinal plants, WHO GMP for herbal medicines, GLP principles, Schedule T, specification of a herbal material |
| 3 EU and ICH | Traditional herbal registration (Directive 2004/24/EC), well-established use, EU extract types, ICH guidelines applied to herbals, WHO research guidelines for safety and efficacy |
| 4 Stability and chromatography | Stability testing of herbal products and climatic zones, HPTLC fingerprinting, HPLC and GC uses, method validation, dossier for new drug and export registration, Chapter IV-A provisions |

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. Name the guideline in full on the first line ("WHO guidelines on Good Agricultural and Collection Practices for medicinal plants") — it shows the examiner you know the source.
2. Write guideline answers as **numbered headings** in the order of the document (for GACP: identification → seeds → cultivation → harvest → collection → processing → packaging → storage → personnel → documentation). Each heading with one line earns more than a long paragraph.
3. Draw a **flow chart** wherever there is a sequence: sampling and quartering, stability protocol, HPTLC steps, field-to-factory traceability, pharmacovigilance reporting.
4. Learn the **numbers that are safe to quote**: WHO sampling (all up to 5; 5 from 6–50; 10% above 50), foreign-matter sample sizes (500/250/50 g), total ash at 500–600 °C, foaming index = 1000/a, WHO lead 10 mg/kg and cadmium 0.3 mg/kg, ICH 40 °C/75% RH accelerated, India zone IVb 30 °C/75% RH, EU traditional use 30 years (15 in the EU). If you are not sure of a number, describe the rule without it.
5. Give **plant examples** with every marker or test: sennosides (senna), withaferin A (ashwagandha), curcumin (turmeric), glycyrrhizin (liquorice), swelling index (isabgol), bitterness value (chirata, gentian).
6. Do not confuse: quality assurance vs GMP vs quality control; GAP vs GACP vs GMP vs GLP vs GCP; standardised vs quantified extracts; active vs analytical markers; well-established use vs traditional use; ASU DTAB (33C) vs ASU DCC (33D); Schedule T (GMP) vs Schedule E(1) (poisonous ASU drugs).
7. Finish regulatory answers with one line on **why it matters to India** — exports, Ministry of AYUSH, WHO-GMP certification.

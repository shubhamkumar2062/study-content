# Unit 3: EU and ICH guidelines and research guidelines for safety and efficacy

Indian herbal products are sold in Europe, the USA and many other countries, and Indian companies that export must meet the standards of those markets. The European Union has the most developed system for herbal medicines, and the ICH guidelines set the technical rules that regulators worldwide expect. This unit covers the EU framework, the ICH guidelines that apply to herbal products, and the WHO research guidelines for proving that a herbal medicine is safe and effective.

## 3.1 The European Union framework for herbal medicinal products

### Bodies and laws

| Item | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **European Medicines Agency (EMA)** | EU agency for scientific evaluation of medicines |
| **Committee on Herbal Medicinal Products (HMPC)** | Scientific committee of EMA for herbal medicines; prepares EU herbal monographs, the list of herbal substances for traditional use, and quality and safety guidelines |
| **Directive 2001/83/EC** | The basic EU law for medicinal products for human use |
| **Directive 2004/24/EC** | Amended Directive 2001/83/EC to create the **simplified registration of traditional herbal medicinal products** |
| **European Pharmacopoeia (Ph. Eur.)** | Legally binding quality standards, including general and individual monographs for herbal drugs, preparations and extracts |

### EU definitions

- **Herbal substance** — whole, fragmented or cut plants, plant parts, algae, fungi or lichens, in unprocessed form, usually dried but sometimes fresh. Defined by the plant part used and the full botanical name.
- **Herbal preparation** — obtained by treating a herbal substance: extraction, distillation, expression, fractionation, purification, concentration or fermentation. Includes powders, tinctures, extracts, essential oils, expressed juices and exudates.
- **Herbal medicinal product** — any medicinal product containing as active ingredients only herbal substances, herbal preparations, or both.

In the EU the **whole herbal substance or preparation is treated as the active substance**, even when individual constituents with known activity are measured.

### Three routes to the EU market

| Route | Evidence needed | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Full marketing authorisation** (stand-alone application) | Own quality, non-clinical and clinical data, as for any new drug | New herbal products with new claims |
| **Well-established use** (bibliographic application) | Published scientific literature showing **well-established medicinal use** in the EU for at least 10 years, with recognised efficacy and acceptable safety; full quality data | Senna for constipation, ispaghula, St John's wort extracts with clinical evidence |
| **Traditional use registration** (simplified, Directive 2004/24/EC) | **Evidence of traditional use for at least 30 years, including at least 15 years within the EU**; bibliographic review of safety; efficacy is accepted as plausible from long use; **full quality data still required** | Products for minor, self-limiting conditions |

Conditions for traditional use registration:

1. Indications suitable for use **without a doctor's supervision** for diagnosis, prescription or monitoring.
2. **Oral, external or inhalation** use only.
3. A specified strength and posology.
4. The 30-year/15-year period of traditional use.
5. Data on traditional use show the product is **not harmful** under the stated conditions, and its pharmacological effects or efficacy are **plausible** from long use.

Labels of such products state that the product is a traditional herbal medicinal product for use in the stated indications **exclusively based on long-standing use**.

### EU herbal monographs and the list

- **EU herbal monographs** (earlier "Community herbal monographs") summarise the HMPC's view on a herbal substance or preparation: well-established and/or traditional use, indications, posology, contraindications, warnings, interactions, adverse effects.
- **The EU list** of herbal substances, preparations and combinations for traditional use: an applicant whose product matches a list entry need not prove traditional use or safety separately.

### EU quality guidelines for herbal products

EMA/HMPC quality guidelines cover:

1. **Quality of herbal medicinal products and traditional herbal medicinal products** — what must be described in the dossier for the herbal substance, herbal preparation and finished product.
2. **Specifications: test procedures and acceptance criteria** for herbal substances, herbal preparations and herbal medicinal products.
3. **Good agricultural and collection practice (GACP)** for starting materials of herbal origin.
4. **Stability testing** of herbal products, with markers and fingerprints.
5. **Declaration of herbal substances and preparations** on labels.

#### What the quality dossier must cover

| Stage | Information |
| --- | --- |
| **Herbal substance** | Botanical name, part, geographical source, cultivated or wild, harvest, drying, storage; GACP; specification with identity (macroscopy, microscopy, chromatographic fingerprint), purity (foreign matter, ash, water), contaminants (pesticides, fumigants, heavy metals, mycotoxins, microbial quality, radioactivity where relevant) |
| **Herbal preparation** | Manufacturing process, **extraction solvent**, **drug–extract ratio (DER)**, excipients added; specification with fingerprint, marker content, residual solvents |
| **Finished product** | Composition, manufacture, controls, validation, container, **stability** |

**Drug–extract ratio (DER)** — ratio of the quantity of herbal substance used to the quantity of extract obtained. A DER of 4–6:1 means 4 to 6 kg of dried herb gives 1 kg of native extract.

#### Types of extracts (Ph. Eur. classification)

| Type | Definition | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Standardised extract** | Adjusted to a defined content of constituents with **known therapeutic activity**, using excipients or by mixing batches; content stated as a single value with tolerance | Senna leaf dry extract adjusted to a stated content of hydroxyanthracene glycosides |
| **Quantified extract** | Adjusted to a defined **range** of constituents (often active markers) whose contribution to activity is accepted but not proven alone, by mixing batches | Ginkgo leaf extract with stated ranges of flavone glycosides and terpene lactones |
| **Other extracts** | Not adjusted; defined by production process (herb, solvent, DER) and by specification, using **analytical markers** for control | Many extracts of herbs whose actives are unknown |

#### Markers in EU usage

**Markers** are chemically defined constituents or groups of constituents used for control purposes, whether or not they have therapeutic activity.

- **Active markers** — contribute to the therapeutic activity.
- **Analytical markers** — used only to check identity, quality and consistency.

## 3.2 ICH guidelines and herbal drugs

The **International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use (ICH)** brings together regulators and industry to agree technical guidelines. ICH has **no guideline written only for herbal products**, but its quality, safety, efficacy and multidisciplinary guidelines are applied to herbal products and extracts, especially in dossiers for regulated markets.

| ICH guideline | Subject | How it is used for herbals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Q1A(R2)** | Stability testing of new drug substances and products | Storage conditions and test frequency for extracts and finished herbal products |
| **Q1B** | Photostability testing | Light-sensitive constituents (hypericin, curcuminoids, chlorophyll) |
| **Q2** | Validation of analytical procedures | Validating HPLC/HPTLC methods for markers, fingerprints, pesticides |
| **Q3C** | Residual solvents (classes 1, 2, 3) | Solvents left in extracts — hexane, methanol, ethanol, acetone |
| **Q3D** | Elemental impurities | Risk assessment for lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury |
| **Q6A** | Specifications for chemical substances and products | Approach to setting tests and acceptance criteria, adapted for herbal products |
| **Q7** | GMP for active pharmaceutical ingredients | Manufacture of herbal extracts used as active ingredients |
| **Q8, Q9, Q10** | Pharmaceutical development, quality risk management, pharmaceutical quality system | Designing robust extraction processes; risk-based control of contamination |
| **M4 (CTD)** | Common Technical Document format | Modules 1–5 for registration dossiers |
| **S-series** | Non-clinical safety studies (genotoxicity, reproductive toxicity, safety pharmacology and others) | Safety studies of new herbal drugs and phytopharmaceuticals |
| **E6** | Good clinical practice | Clinical trials of herbal medicines |

### ICH stability conditions (Q1A(R2))

| Study | Storage condition | Minimum period at submission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long-term | 25 °C ± 2 °C / 60% RH ± 5% RH, or 30 °C ± 2 °C / 65% RH ± 5% RH | 12 months |
| Intermediate | 30 °C ± 2 °C / 65% RH ± 5% RH | 6 months |
| Accelerated | 40 °C ± 2 °C / 75% RH ± 5% RH | 6 months |

India is in the hot and very humid **climatic zone IVb**, for which WHO guidance uses **30 °C / 75% RH** as the long-term condition (details in Unit 4).

### Common Technical Document (CTD)

```text
Module 1  Regional administrative information (country-specific)
Module 2  Summaries: quality overall summary, non-clinical and clinical overviews
Module 3  Quality (herbal substance, preparation, finished product)
Module 4  Non-clinical study reports
Module 5  Clinical study reports
```

## 3.3 Research guidelines for evaluating the safety and efficacy of herbal medicines

WHO (through its Regional Office for the Western Pacific) published **"Research guidelines for evaluating the safety and efficacy of herbal medicines"**, and later the **"General guidelines for methodologies on research and evaluation of traditional medicine"** (2000). Their aim is to help countries ask for the right amount of evidence — not ignoring long traditional experience, but not accepting claims without proof.

### General principles

1. **Quality comes first.** The material tested must be botanically identified and standardised; otherwise results cannot be repeated or applied to the marketed product.
2. **Traditional use is evidence.** A herb used for a long time without reports of harm, in the same preparation and dose, may need fewer new toxicity studies.
3. **New is new.** A new indication, a new preparation (for example a concentrated extract instead of a decoction), a new route, a higher dose or a new combination requires fresh safety and efficacy data.
4. **Ethics** — research on humans follows the Declaration of Helsinki, approval by an ethics committee and informed consent.

### Evaluation of safety

```text
 Literature and traditional-use review
        |
 Quality and characterisation of the test material
        |
 Acute toxicity (single dose)
        |
 Repeated-dose (sub-acute / sub-chronic / chronic) toxicity
   duration linked to the intended duration of clinical use
        |
 Special studies when indicated:
   local tolerance, sensitisation, genotoxicity/mutagenicity,
   carcinogenicity, reproductive and developmental toxicity
        |
 Herb-drug interaction studies (enzymes such as CYP3A4, P-glycoprotein)
        |
 Post-marketing surveillance (pharmacovigilance, Unit 5)
```

- Studies are done in **GLP-compliant** laboratories (Unit 2).
- Observations include body weight, food intake, clinical signs, haematology, biochemistry (liver and kidney function), organ weights and histopathology.
- Special concern for herbs with known toxic groups — pyrrolizidine alkaloids (liver), aristolochic acids (kidney, cancer), and for products containing metals or minerals.

### Evaluation of efficacy

**Pre-clinical (pharmacological) studies** — in vitro tests and animal models that support the traditional claim and suggest a mechanism (anti-inflammatory, hypoglycaemic, hepatoprotective models).

**Clinical trials** — in phases, under good clinical practice:

| Phase | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| I | Safety and tolerability in a small number of volunteers or patients; may be shortened or combined when long traditional use exists |
| II | Efficacy and dose-finding in a small number of patients |
| III | Confirmation of efficacy and safety in larger, randomised, controlled trials |
| IV | Post-marketing studies and surveillance |

Special issues in herbal clinical trials:

- **Placebo design** — herbal products have strong colour, taste and smell, which makes blinding difficult.
- **Individualised treatment** — Ayurveda and traditional Chinese medicine tailor prescriptions to the person (for example by *prakriti*); trial designs must allow for this or test a fixed formula.
- **Outcome measures** — validated modern measures, alongside traditional assessment where relevant.
- **Product consistency** — every batch used in the trial must be standardised and retained.
- **Concomitant medicines** — record them, because of interactions.

### Evidence in India

- **Classical ASU medicines** made as per authoritative books are licensed on the basis of the textual reference; proprietary ASU medicines need evidence of safety and effectiveness; **phytopharmaceutical drugs** need non-clinical and clinical data like new drugs (Unit 4).
- Clinical trials in India follow the **New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019**, ICMR ethical guidelines and good clinical practice; the AYUSH research councils (CCRAS, CCRUM, CCRS) carry out and guide research on ASU medicines.

## Remember

- HMPC is the EMA committee for herbal medicines; Directive 2004/24/EC created traditional herbal registration.
- EU routes: full application; well-established use (at least 10 years in the EU, bibliographic); traditional use (30 years, of which 15 in the EU; oral, external or inhalation; no medical supervision needed; full quality data still required).
- EU extract types: standardised (known actives, fixed value), quantified (active markers, range), other (analytical markers).
- Active versus analytical markers; DER = herb : extract.
- ICH has no herbal-only guideline: apply Q1A(R2), Q1B, Q2, Q3C, Q3D, Q6A approach, Q7, Q8–Q10, M4 CTD, S-series, E6.
- ICH stability: long-term 25/60 or 30/65; intermediate 30/65; accelerated 40/75 for 6 months.
- WHO research guidelines: quality first; traditional use counts as evidence; new preparations/indications need new data; acute → repeated-dose → special toxicity; clinical phases I–IV with ethics and GCP.

# Unit 2: Quality assurance in the herbal industry — GAP, GACP, GMP, cGMP and GLP

Testing at the end cannot put quality into a herbal medicine. If the wrong plant was collected, if it was dried on the roadside and grew mould, or if a mixer was not cleaned after the previous batch, no laboratory test can rescue the product. Quality must be **built in** at every step: in the field (good agricultural and collection practices), in the factory (good manufacturing practices) and in the test laboratory (good laboratory practice). This unit covers these systems as they apply to herbal medicines and the traditional systems of medicine, with special attention to the two WHO guidelines named in the syllabus.

## 2.1 Quality assurance, GMP and quality control

```text
            QUALITY ASSURANCE (QA)
   the total system that makes sure products are of the
   quality required for their intended use
                     |
           GOOD MANUFACTURING PRACTICE (GMP)
   the part of QA that makes sure products are consistently
   produced and controlled to suitable quality standards
                     |
              QUALITY CONTROL (QC)
   the part of GMP concerned with sampling, specifications,
   testing, and release or rejection of materials and products
```

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Quality assurance** | Organised arrangements covering design, development, production, control, distribution and complaint handling, so that every product reaching the patient is fit for use |
| **GMP** | Rules for premises, equipment, personnel, materials, processes and records in manufacturing |
| **cGMP** | "Current" GMP — the expectation that manufacturers use up-to-date technology and systems, not just the minimum written years ago |
| **GAP / GACP** | Good agricultural (and collection) practices for growing and collecting medicinal plants |
| **GLP** | Good laboratory practice — a quality system for non-clinical safety studies (toxicity and similar tests) |
| **GCP** | Good clinical practice — for clinical trials (Unit 3) |

### Why QA is harder for herbal products

- The starting material is a **living, variable** plant, not a pure chemical.
- **Contamination risks** begin in the field — pesticides, heavy metals from soil, microbes from manure and water, mould during drying.
- **Identification** needs botanical skill; look-alike species and trade names cause confusion.
- Herbal powders produce **dust**, which carries cross-contamination, microbes and a fire/explosion risk.
- Materials are bulky and **hygroscopic**; insects and rodents are a constant threat in stores.
- Specifications often rely on **fingerprints and markers** instead of a single assay.

## 2.2 Good agricultural practice (GAP) and WHO guidelines on GACP for medicinal plants

**WHO guidelines on Good Agricultural and Collection Practices (GACP) for medicinal plants** (2003) give guidance for both cultivated and wild-collected plants. Their aims are to:

1. Improve the **quality, safety and efficacy** of herbal medicines at the source.
2. Guide countries in making their own GACP guidelines and monographs.
3. Encourage **sustainable** cultivation and collection that protects plant species and the environment, and respects the rights of local communities.

In India, the **National Medicinal Plants Board (NMPB)** has issued GACP guidelines for medicinal plants based on the WHO guidelines.

### Main sections of WHO GACP

| Section | Key points |
| --- | --- |
| **Identification and authentication** | Botanical name (genus, species, author) and family; local names; **voucher specimen** deposited in a herbarium; identity confirmed by a botanist |
| **Seeds and propagation material** | Authentic species and variety; pure, free from pests and diseases; organic seed where possible |
| **Cultivation — site selection** | Soil and water free from heavy metals, pesticide residues and other contaminants; past land use known |
| **Climate and soil** | Suitable for the species, since climate and soil affect constituent content |
| **Irrigation and fertilisation** | Water of suitable quality; manure well composted to avoid microbial load; no human excreta |
| **Crop maintenance and protection** | Minimum pesticide use; integrated pest management; only approved agents, at permitted doses, recorded |
| **Harvest** | At the best season and time of day for constituent content; dry weather; clean tools; damaged and foreign plants removed; no contact with soil |
| **Collection (wild plants)** | Permission from authorities; no endangered species; sustainable methods leaving enough plants to regenerate; trained collectors |
| **Primary processing** | Prompt inspection and sorting; washing only if allowed; drying (sun, shade, or dryer) on clean surfaces, protected from animals and dust; specific treatments (peeling, slicing, fermentation) |
| **Packaging and labelling** | Clean, dry containers; label with botanical name, part, origin, date, batch |
| **Storage and transport** | Dry, ventilated, pest-proof stores; separate from other goods; fumigation only with permitted agents and recorded |
| **Equipment** | Clean, easy to maintain; does not contaminate |
| **Personnel** | Trained; healthy; personal hygiene; protective clothing when handling toxic or allergenic plants |
| **Documentation** | Records of seed source, site, cultivation inputs, pesticides used, harvest date, processing, batch numbers — so every batch can be traced |
| **Quality assurance** | Agreements on quality between producer and buyer; audits |
| **Ethical and legal issues** | Intellectual property, benefit sharing with local communities, protection of threatened species |

```text
 Seed -> Site -> Cultivation -> Harvest/Collection -> Primary processing
      -> Packaging -> Storage -> Transport -> Manufacturer (quarantine)
   (documents travel with every batch: traceability from field to factory)
```

**Benefits of GACP** — consistent raw material, lower contamination, fewer rejected batches, traceability, acceptance in export markets, and conservation of wild resources.

## 2.3 Good manufacturing practice for herbal medicines

### WHO guidelines on GMP for herbal medicines

WHO published supplementary **guidelines on good manufacturing practices (GMP) for herbal medicines** (2007). They do not replace the main WHO GMP text; they add the points that are specific to herbal materials. The headings follow general GMP, with herbal-specific detail:

| Area | Herbal-specific requirements |
| --- | --- |
| **Quality assurance** | Herbal materials and preparations handled as "starting materials"; supplier qualification and audits of farms and collectors where possible |
| **Sanitation and hygiene** | Plant material carries soil and microbes; separate areas for receipt, cleaning and storage of raw herbs |
| **Qualification and validation** | Validation of cleaning, extraction, drying and sterilising or decontaminating processes |
| **Complaints and recalls** | Records allow tracing back to the herbal batch and its source |
| **Contract production and analysis** | Written contracts; clear responsibilities |
| **Self-inspection** | Regular internal audits |
| **Personnel** | Trained in botany/pharmacognosy as well as GMP; protection from dust and allergenic or toxic plants |
| **Premises** | Raw herbs stored apart from processed materials and finished products; well ventilated, pest-proof; **dust extraction** where powders are cut, ground, sieved or mixed; separate quarantine areas |
| **Equipment** | Easy to clean; dedicated equipment where cross-contamination is a risk |
| **Materials** | Quarantine on receipt; sampling; **reference samples and voucher specimens** retained; storage that protects from moisture, light, insects and rodents |
| **Documentation** | Specifications for herbal materials, herbal preparations and finished products; batch processing records; standard operating procedures |
| **Good practices in production** | Defined processing (cutting, drying, extraction); fixed solvents and ratios; mixing of batches only when documented; in-process controls |
| **Good practices in quality control** | Identity by macroscopy, microscopy and fingerprint; tests for contaminants; stability; retention of samples |

### What a specification for a herbal material should state

1. **Botanical name** (genus, species, variety, author) and family.
2. **Source** — country or region; cultivated or wild; time of collection; pre-treatment (drying, fumigation).
3. **Part used** and whether fresh or dried, whole or powdered.
4. **Description** — macroscopic and microscopic features.
5. **Identity tests** — chemical tests and a **TLC/HPTLC fingerprint** against a reference.
6. **Assay** — content of active constituents or markers, with limits.
7. **Purity tests** — foreign matter, total and acid-insoluble ash, moisture, extractive values.
8. **Contaminant tests** — pesticides, heavy metals, microbes, aflatoxins, residual fumigants or solvents, radioactivity where relevant.
9. **Other tests** — particle size, swelling index, volatile oil.
10. **Storage conditions** and re-test period.

For **herbal preparations (extracts)** the specification adds the solvent used, the **drug-to-extract ratio**, residual solvent limits and, where relevant, the content range of markers.

### cGMP — keeping GMP current

"Current" means the manufacturer keeps its quality system up to date with modern methods: validated processes, computerised records with audit trails, risk-based thinking, environmental monitoring, change control, deviation and CAPA (corrective and preventive action) systems, and periodic product quality review. Inspectors judge a plant against what is current practice today.

## 2.4 GMP in the traditional systems of medicine (India)

In India, Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani (ASU) medicines are manufactured under licence, and the licence requires compliance with **Schedule T** of the **Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945** — the GMP for ASU medicines (studied in detail in Herbal Drug Technology, sixth semester). Its main headings:

```text
Schedule T, Part I (GMP requirements)
  premises and location  -> water supply -> waste disposal
  container cleaning     -> stores (raw material, packing, finished goods, quarantine)
  working space          -> health, clothing and sanitation of workers
  medical services       -> machinery and equipment
  batch manufacturing records -> distribution records
  record of market complaints and adverse reactions -> quality control
Schedule T, Part II
  list of machinery and minimum manufacturing area for each ASU dosage form
```

Points that bring Indian ASU units in line with WHO GMP:

- **Raw material store** with separate sections for herbs, minerals and animal products; poisonous drugs (listed in Schedule E(1)) kept separately and under lock.
- **Authentication** of every raw herb by a qualified person before release.
- **Quality control** section, or approved testing laboratories, to test raw materials and finished products against pharmacopoeial standards.
- **Batch manufacturing records** that allow full traceability.
- **WHO-GMP certification** of ASU units, needed by many export markets, is granted after inspection against WHO GMP for herbal medicines.

## 2.5 Good laboratory practice (GLP)

**GLP** is a quality system covering the **organisational process and the conditions** under which **non-clinical health and environmental safety studies** are planned, performed, monitored, recorded, archived and reported. It ensures that the toxicity data submitted to regulators are reliable and can be reconstructed. The internationally accepted text is the **OECD Principles of GLP**. In India, laboratories are inspected and certified by the **National GLP Compliance Monitoring Authority (NGCMA)** under the Department of Science and Technology.

### OECD principles of GLP

| Principle | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Test facility organisation and personnel** | Management responsibilities; a **study director** responsible for each study; qualified, trained staff |
| **Quality assurance programme** | An independent QA unit inspects studies and audits reports |
| **Facilities** | Suitable size and design; separate areas for test systems (animals), test items, archives and waste |
| **Apparatus, materials and reagents** | Calibrated, maintained, labelled with identity and expiry |
| **Test systems** | Proper housing and care of animals or other systems; records of receipt and health |
| **Test and reference items** | Characterised; identity, batch, purity, stability and storage recorded |
| **Standard operating procedures** | Written SOPs for every routine activity |
| **Performance of the study** | Written **study plan (protocol)** approved by the study director; raw data recorded promptly and signed |
| **Reporting of results** | Final report with methods, results and a QA statement |
| **Storage and retention of records** | Archives for protocols, raw data, specimens and reports |

### Why GLP matters for herbal drugs

- Toxicity studies of herbal extracts and phytopharmaceuticals submitted for approval must be done in **GLP-compliant** laboratories to be accepted by regulators, including in export markets.
- The **test item** must be a fully characterised herbal extract — botanical identity, extraction method, drug-to-extract ratio, fingerprint and marker content — so that the safety result applies to a defined product.
- GLP also guides quality control laboratories in documentation, calibration and data integrity, even when formal certification is not required.

## 2.6 Documents that support quality assurance

| Document | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| **Standard operating procedure (SOP)** | Step-by-step instructions for a routine task (cleaning a pulveriser, sampling herbs) |
| **Master formula record** | Approved formula and method for a product: ingredients, quantities, process, equipment, in-process checks |
| **Batch manufacturing record** | What actually happened for one batch, signed at each step |
| **Specifications** | Tests and limits for raw materials, intermediates, finished products |
| **Certificate of analysis** | Test results for a batch released by QC |
| **Validation protocols and reports** | Evidence that processes and methods do what they claim |
| **Deviation, change control and CAPA records** | Investigation and correction of departures from procedure |

## Remember

- QA ⊃ GMP ⊃ QC. cGMP = current, up-to-date GMP.
- WHO GACP for medicinal plants (2003): identification with voucher specimen, seed, site, cultivation, harvest, collection, primary processing, packaging, storage, personnel, documentation, ethics.
- WHO GMP for herbal medicines (2007) is supplementary to main GMP: separate raw-herb storage, dust control, voucher and reference samples, fingerprint-based specifications, contaminant testing.
- Herbal material specification: botanical name, source, part, description, identity (TLC fingerprint), assay, purity, contaminants, storage.
- ASU GMP in India = Schedule T (Part I requirements, Part II machinery and area); poisonous ASU ingredients in Schedule E(1).
- GLP = OECD principles for non-clinical safety studies: study director, QA unit, SOPs, study plan, raw data, archives. India: NGCMA under DST.

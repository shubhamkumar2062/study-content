# Last-night revision sheet

If you only have an hour, learn these five answers with headings and a diagram: **WHO guidelines for quality control of herbal drugs**, **WHO GACP for medicinal plants**, **stability testing of herbal medicines**, **HPTLC fingerprinting and markers**, and **WHO pharmacovigilance guidelines for herbal medicines**. Add **EU traditional herbal registration** as the sixth if time allows.

## Unit 1 — Basic tests and WHO quality control

- Quality = **identity + purity + content**, plus safety and batch consistency.
- WHO **Basic tests for drugs**: substances, plant materials, dosage forms; simple tests to confirm identity and detect gross degradation; **never replace** pharmacopoeial tests.
- WHO **QC methods for medicinal plant materials** (1998) → **for herbal materials** (2011): a methods manual, not a pharmacopoeia.
- **Sampling**: ≤5 containers — all; 6–50 — 5; >50 — 10% (rounded up to a multiple of 10); top, middle, bottom; quartering; retain a reference sample.
- **Foreign matter** sample: 500 g roots/bark, 250 g leaves/flowers/seeds/fruit, 50 g cut material.
- **Ash**: total (500–600 °C), acid-insoluble (silica/sand), water-soluble (low in exhausted drug), sulphated.
- **Extractives**: hot (reflux 1 h) and cold (6 h + 18 h maceration); 25 ml filtrate dried at 105 °C.
- **Water**: toluene distillation (water only); LOD at 100–105 °C (water + volatiles).
- **Bitterness**: vs quinine hydrochloride (1 g in 2000 ml = 1 unit). **Foaming index** = 1000/a. **Swelling index** = ml per g. **Tannins** with hide powder. **Haemolytic activity** vs saponin.
- **Contaminants**: pesticides (GC; limits from ADI), arsenic and toxic metals (AAS/ICP), microbes, aflatoxins (HPLC-fluorescence), radioactivity.
- **Limits**: WHO gives no single global limits, only national examples. WHO-suggested **lead 10 mg/kg, cadmium 0.3 mg/kg**. Microbial example: teas/topical aerobic bacteria 10^7/g, other internal herbal materials 10^5/g; *Salmonella*, *Shigella* absent. Aflatoxin example (Germany): B1 2 µg/kg, total 4 µg/kg.
- Limits from data: about 20 batches, mean ± 3 SD.
- **Evaluation**: organoleptic, microscopic (stomatal index = S×100/(E+S), palisade ratio, vein-islet and vein-termination number, lycopodium spore method), physical, chemical, chromatographic, biological.
- **Adulteration**: inferior varieties, exhausted drug, look-alike plants, artificial material, synthetic principles, wrong parts, heavy matter, spoiled drug.

## Unit 2 — Quality assurance

- QA ⊃ GMP ⊃ QC; cGMP = current GMP.
- **WHO GACP (2003)**: identification + voucher specimen, seeds, site, climate/soil, irrigation/fertiliser, crop protection, harvest, wild collection (permits, sustainability), primary processing (drying), packaging/labelling, storage/transport, equipment, personnel, documentation, QA, ethics. NMPB GACP in India.
- **WHO GMP for herbal medicines (2007)** — supplementary: separate raw-herb store, dust extraction, quarantine, voucher/reference samples, fingerprint specs, contaminant testing, validated processes.
- Herbal specification: botanical name, source, part, description, identity (TLC), assay, purity, contaminants, storage. Extracts add solvent, DER, residual solvents.
- **Schedule T**: Part I GMP headings; Part II machinery and minimum area. Schedule E(1) poisonous ASU drugs.
- **GLP** (OECD): study director, QA unit, facilities, apparatus, test systems, test items, SOPs, study plan, reporting, archives. India: NGCMA (DST).

## Unit 3 — EU, ICH and research guidelines

- EMA → **HMPC**; Directive 2001/83/EC; **Directive 2004/24/EC** (traditional registration).
- Routes: full; **well-established use** (≥10 years in EU, literature); **traditional use** (30 years incl. 15 in EU; oral/external/inhalation; no medical supervision; full quality data).
- EU herbal monographs and the EU list.
- Extracts: **standardised** (known actives, fixed value), **quantified** (active markers, range), **other** (analytical markers). DER = herb : extract.
- ICH: Q1A(R2) stability, Q1B photostability, Q2 validation, Q3C residual solvents, Q3D elemental impurities, Q6A approach, Q7, Q8–Q10, M4 CTD, S-series, E6 GCP. No herbal-only ICH guideline.
- WHO research guidelines: quality first; traditional use is evidence; new preparation/indication needs new data; acute → repeated-dose → special toxicity; GLP; clinical phases I–IV, ethics, GCP; placebo and individualised-treatment problems.

## Unit 4 — Stability, chromatography, dossiers, law

- Herbal stability: marker + **fingerprint** + physical + microbial + dosage-form tests.
- Zones: I 21/45 · II 25/60 · III 30/35 · IVa 30/65 · **IVb 30/75 (India)**. Accelerated 40/75 for 6 months (0, 3, 6). Long-term 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 18, 24 months then yearly. Rule 161-B shelf life of ASU drugs.
- Fingerprint = pattern of bands/peaks; Rf = spot ÷ solvent front.
- HPTLC: application → development (twin trough) → derivatisation → densitometry. HPLC: C18, PDA, marker assay. GC: oils, pesticides, solvents. LC–MS/GC–MS: unknowns and adulterants.
- ICH Q2 validation: specificity, linearity, range, accuracy, precision, LOD, LOQ, robustness.
- India: classical ASU (textual reference), proprietary ASU (safety + effectiveness, Rule 158-B), **phytopharmaceutical** (≥4 defined compounds; CDSCO, NDCT Rules 2019).
- Export: licence, GMP/WHO-GMP, COPP/free sale certificate, dossier (CTD), CoA, stability, heavy metal/pesticide/microbial/aflatoxin reports, biodiversity/CITES clearance, Pharmexcil.
- Chapter IV-A: 33C ASU DTAB, 33D ASU DCC, 33E misbranded, 33EE adulterated, 33EEA spurious, 33EEC prohibition. Rule 161 labelling.

## Unit 5 — Regulation, pharmacovigilance, pharmacopoeias, markers

- Regulation: quality, safety, efficacy, labelling, post-marketing. USA DSHEA 1994 (supplements) vs botanical drug; Canada NHP; Australia listed/registered; China TCM volume.
- **WHO PV guidelines for herbal medicines (2004)**: include herbs in national systems; report botanical name, part, preparation, batch, source, sample; product analysis; WHO–UMC causality; Uppsala Monitoring Centre. Issues: identity (aristolochia), adulteration, interactions (St John's wort), multi-herb products.
- India: PvPI (IPC); AYUSH pharmacovigilance (AIIA New Delhi) + misleading advertisements.
- Pharmacopoeias: API (Part I single drugs, Part II formulations, PCIM&H), UPI, SPI, HPI, IP, IHP (IDMA + RRL Jammu), BHP, Ph. Eur., USP, Chinese, Japanese; WHO monographs advisory; Commission E/ESCOP therapeutic.
- Indian limits: Pb 10 ppm, Hg 1 ppm, As 3 ppm, Cd 0.3 ppm.
- **Markers**: active, analytical, negative. Senna–sennosides; turmeric–curcumin; ashwagandha–withaferin A; liquorice–glycyrrhizin; brahmi–bacosides; kalmegh–andrographolide; vasaka–vasicine; guggul–guggulsterones; pepper–piperine.
- **Biological markers**: bioassays, isoenzymes, DNA (RAPD, RFLP, AFLP, SSR, SCAR, barcoding ITS2/rbcL/matK). DNA = identity, not quality.

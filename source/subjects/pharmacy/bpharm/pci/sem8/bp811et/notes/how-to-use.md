# How to use this guide

Advanced Instrumentation Techniques is **one of the semester-8 electives** of the PCI B.Pharm course. It has no practical; the whole subject is the 75-mark theory paper. It builds directly on Instrumental Methods of Analysis (BP701T, semester 7): you are expected to remember UV, IR, fluorimetry, flame photometry, HPLC and GC from that subject, because Unit 3 asks how those instruments are calibrated and Unit 5 couples HPLC and GC to a mass spectrometer. Study one unit at a time: read the notes, draw each block diagram from memory, redo the worked calculations, then answer the question bank. Each unit has very short (2-mark), short answer (5-mark) and long essay (10-mark) questions, rated by how often that kind of question appears in PCI-syllabus pharmacy papers.

The five units follow the five units of the PCI syllabus exactly. Unit 1 joins NMR and mass spectrometry and Unit 2 joins thermal methods with X-ray diffraction, as the syllabus does; both are long, so their notes are longer. Unit 4 joins radioimmunoassay with extraction techniques.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ questions | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with a diagram and applications.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from common patterns and syllabus weight, not a promise of what will be asked.

**Where the marks usually come from**

| Unit | Topics that keep coming back |
| --- | --- |
| 1 NMR and MS | Principle of NMR, chemical shift and the factors affecting it, spin–spin coupling and coupling constant, relaxation, FT-NMR instrumentation, ¹³C NMR (decoupling, DEPT); MS principle, ionisation (EI, CI, FAB, MALDI), fragmentation, quadrupole and TOF analysers |
| 2 Thermal and XRD | TGA, DTA and DSC (principle, instrumentation, factors, applications), DTA vs DSC; origin of X-rays, crystal systems and Miller indices, Bragg's law, rotating crystal, single crystal and powder methods, polymorphism |
| 3 Calibration and validation | Definitions, ICH Q2 validation parameters, qualification (DQ, IQ, OQ, PQ), calibration of balance, UV-visible, IR, fluorimeter, flame photometer, HPLC and GC |
| 4 RIA and extraction | Principle, components, methods and limitations of RIA; LLE and SPE (principle, steps, comparison) |
| 5 Hyphenated techniques | GC-MS/MS and LC-MS/MS instrumentation and interfaces, ESI and APCI, MS/MS scan modes and MRM, HPTLC-MS |

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. Use one plan for every technique question: **principle → block diagram → each part explained → factors or limitations → applications in pharmacy**. Examiners give separate marks to the diagram.
2. Learn the key equations with every symbol: ν = γB0/2π, δ = Δν/ν(spectrometer) × 10⁶, nλ = 2d sinθ, d = a/√(h² + k² + l²), t = L√(m/2zeV), LOD = 3.3σ/S, LOQ = 10σ/S, K = Corg/Caq.
3. Practise the worked calculations: chemical shift from Hz, J from line positions, m/z of fragments and isotope ratios, % mass loss in TGA, d-spacing from 2θ, recovery and RSD, minimum weight of a balance, fraction extracted after n extractions. Show the formula, substitution and unit.
4. Comparisons are favourite short questions: ¹H vs ¹³C NMR, EI vs CI, quadrupole vs TOF, DTA vs DSC, TGA vs DSC, powder vs single crystal, calibration vs validation, RIA vs IRMA, LLE vs SPE, ESI vs APCI, GC-MS vs LC-MS. Answer them as tables.
5. For Unit 3, write the standard material for each instrument (holmium, potassium dichromate, KCl, toluene in hexane, polystyrene, quinine sulfate, NaCl/KCl standards, caffeine) and what each one checks.
6. Learn the standard numbers: TMS δ 0, ¹H range 0–12 ppm and ¹³C 0–220 ppm, EI at 70 eV, Cl 3 : 1 and Br 1 : 1, tropylium m/z 91, Cu Kα about 1.54 Å, indium mp 156.6 °C, ¹²⁵I half-life about 60 days, assay range 80–120%.
7. End with pharmacy applications (structure, impurity, polymorph, bioanalysis, quality control); the examiner looks for that link.

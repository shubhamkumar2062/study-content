# Unit 4: Safety data generation and ICH guidelines for pharmacovigilance

Safety information about a medicine is built up in layers: animal studies before it is given to humans, carefully watched clinical trials, and then years of real-world use. Each stage has its own rules for collecting and reporting safety data, and the most widely used rules are the E2 guidelines of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH). This unit follows a drug from the laboratory to the market and then covers expedited reporting, individual case safety reports, periodic safety update reports, post-approval reporting, pharmacovigilance planning and good clinical practice in safety studies.

## 4.1 Safety data generation — the life cycle

```text
Pre-clinical (animal, in vitro)  →  Phase I  →  Phase II  →  Phase III  →  Approval  →  Phase IV / PMS
   toxicity profile, safe          first in    efficacy in   large trials,              real-world use,
   starting dose                   humans,     patients,     common ADRs                rare, delayed and
                                   tolerability dose range                              special-group ADRs
```

## 4.2 Pre-clinical phase

Pre-clinical safety studies identify target organs of toxicity, reversibility, a safe starting point for human trials and parameters to monitor in patients. They follow Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) and the ICH safety (S) guidelines; animal use in India is controlled by the CCSEA (formerly CPCSEA).

| Study | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Single-dose (acute) toxicity | Effects after one administration; signs and target organs |
| Repeated-dose toxicity (sub-acute, sub-chronic, chronic) | Effects of continued use; duration linked to planned clinical use |
| Safety pharmacology | Effects on vital functions — CNS, cardiovascular (including QT prolongation, hERG testing) and respiratory systems (ICH S7A, S7B) |
| Genotoxicity | Gene mutation (Ames test), chromosomal damage (in vitro and in vivo micronucleus) |
| Carcinogenicity | Lifetime studies for drugs used long term or with concern |
| Reproductive and developmental toxicity | Fertility, embryo-fetal development (teratogenicity), pre- and post-natal development |
| Toxicokinetics | Exposure in the toxicity studies, linking findings to blood levels |
| Local tolerance, immunotoxicity, phototoxicity, dependence | As needed for route and class |

**Limits:** species differences (thalidomide's teratogenicity was not seen in the rodent tests used at the time), high doses, idiosyncratic reactions are not predictable in animals.

## 4.3 Clinical phase

| Phase | Subjects | Safety focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Small number of healthy volunteers (or patients for cytotoxic drugs) | Tolerability, pharmacokinetics, dose-limiting effects |
| II | Some hundreds of patients | Short-term safety, dose–response, common ADRs |
| III | Several hundreds to thousands of patients, often randomised controlled | Common and less common ADRs, comparison with standard treatment, benefit–risk for approval |

**Safety tools during trials**

- **Investigator's Brochure (IB)** — contains the **reference safety information** used to decide whether a reaction is "expected".
- **Adverse event collection** at every visit; **serious adverse events (SAEs)** reported by the investigator to the sponsor quickly.
- **SUSARs** (suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions) reported to regulators on an expedited basis.
- **Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB)** — independent experts who review unblinded safety data and can recommend stopping a trial.
- **Development Safety Update Report (DSUR, ICH E2F)** — annual review of the safety of a drug under development.

## 4.4 Post-approval phase — post-marketing surveillance (PMS)

**PMS** is the monitoring of a medicine after it is marketed, to find ADRs not seen in trials and to confirm the benefit–risk balance in real use.

**Methods:** spontaneous reports, solicited reports from patient support programmes, literature monitoring, **post-authorisation safety studies (PASS)**, phase IV trials, registries, drug utilisation studies, periodic reports (PSUR/PBRER), risk management plans.

**Possible outcomes:** new warnings or contraindications, dose changes, restricted use or prescriber, additional monitoring, boxed warning, suspension or withdrawal (for example, rofecoxib, cerivastatin).

## 4.5 ICH — organisation and objectives

The **International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use (ICH)** was founded in **1990** by the regulators and industry associations of the **European Union, Japan and the United States**. In **2015** it was reorganised as an international non-profit association under Swiss law and took its present name (earlier it was the International *Conference* on Harmonisation). Many other regulators have since joined as members or observers.

**Founding parties**

| Region | Regulator | Industry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EU | European Commission / EMA | EFPIA |
| Japan | MHLW / PMDA | JPMA |
| USA | FDA | PhRMA |

**Structure:** Assembly (decision-making body of all members), Management Committee, MedDRA Management Committee, Secretariat in Geneva, and expert working groups that draft guidelines.

**Objectives**

1. Harmonise technical requirements for registering medicines.
2. Avoid duplication of animal and human testing.
3. Make development faster and cheaper without lowering quality, safety or efficacy.
4. Enable better use of resources and faster access of patients to new medicines.
5. Provide common standards for electronic exchange of data (MedDRA, E2B, eCTD).

**Guideline categories:** **Q** (quality), **S** (safety — non-clinical), **E** (efficacy — clinical, including the pharmacovigilance E2 series and GCP E6), **M** (multidisciplinary — MedDRA, CTD).

**ICH process steps:** 1 consensus building → 2 draft agreed for consultation → 3 regulatory consultation and revision → 4 adoption of the final harmonised guideline → 5 implementation in each region.

**The E2 (pharmacovigilance) series**

| Guideline | Topic |
| --- | --- |
| E2A (1994) | Clinical safety data management: definitions and standards for expedited reporting |
| E2B | Data elements for electronic transmission of individual case safety reports (current version E2B(R3)) |
| E2C | Periodic safety update reports; revised as E2C(R2) **Periodic Benefit–Risk Evaluation Report (PBRER)** in 2012 |
| E2D | Post-approval safety data management (2003; revised as E2D(R1) in 2025) |
| E2E (2004) | Pharmacovigilance planning |
| E2F (2010) | Development Safety Update Report (DSUR) |

## 4.6 Expedited reporting (ICH E2A)

**Key definitions (E2A):** adverse event, adverse drug reaction (in clinical trials, a noxious and unintended response to a medicinal product related to any dose, where a causal relationship is at least a reasonable possibility), **unexpected** ADR (not consistent with the IB or label), **serious** (death, life-threatening, hospitalisation or prolongation, persistent or significant disability, congenital anomaly, medically important).

**What must be expedited:** all suspected ADRs that are **both serious and unexpected**.

| Type of case | Timeline (from day 0 = first receipt of the minimum information) |
| --- | --- |
| Fatal or life-threatening, unexpected ADR | As soon as possible, **no later than 7 calendar days**; a complete follow-up report within **8 more days** |
| Other serious, unexpected ADR | As soon as possible, **no later than 15 calendar days** |
| Other information that changes the benefit–risk (e.g. a significant increase in rate of an expected serious ADR, a finding in animal studies) | Also expedited, usually within 15 days |

**Not usually expedited:** non-serious ADRs, serious **expected** ADRs (summarised in periodic reports), events judged not related.

**Other E2A points**

- Blinded cases: the blind is usually broken for the individual SUSAR before reporting.
- Reports should use a standard format (CIOMS I form or electronic E2B).
- Minimum information: identifiable patient, identifiable reporter, suspect product, reaction.

## 4.7 Individual case safety reports (ICSR) — ICH E2B

An **ICSR** is the format and content for reporting one or more suspected adverse reactions in **one patient** at a specific time.

**E2B** defines the standard **data elements** so that reports can be sent electronically between companies, regulators and the WHO. E2B(R3) uses the HL7 ICSR XML message format.

**Main sections of an ICSR**

1. Case administrative information — unique case identifier, dates of receipt, type of report (spontaneous, study, literature), seriousness criteria.
2. Primary source (reporter) — qualification, country.
3. Sender and receiver information.
4. Literature reference and study identification (if any).
5. Patient characteristics — initials, age, sex, weight, medical history, past drug history, death details.
6. Reaction/event — verbatim and MedDRA terms, onset, outcome.
7. Results of tests and procedures.
8. Drug information — suspect, concomitant and interacting drugs; dose, route, dates, indication, action taken, dechallenge/rechallenge.
9. Narrative case summary, reporter's and sender's comments, causality.

**Validity:** a case is valid only with the four minimum criteria. Missing details are sought by **follow-up**; each follow-up is sent as a new version of the same case.

## 4.8 Writing case narratives

A **case narrative** is a clear, complete, chronological story of the case that can be understood without the rest of the report.

**Structure**

1. Opening: report source and type; patient age, sex (and relevant weight).
2. Relevant medical history, risk factors, allergies.
3. Concomitant medicines.
4. Suspect drug: indication, dose, route, start date.
5. The event: onset (time from first dose), signs and symptoms, diagnosis.
6. Relevant investigations with values, units and dates.
7. Treatment of the event.
8. Action taken with the drug; dechallenge and rechallenge results.
9. Outcome and date (recovered, recovering, not recovered, recovered with sequelae, fatal, unknown).
10. Reporter's assessment of seriousness and causality; sender's comment.

**Qualities of a good narrative:** accurate (matches the data fields), complete, objective (no blame or speculation), chronological, concise, no unexplained abbreviations, protects identity (initials only), consistent with MedDRA coding.

## 4.9 Periodic safety update reports (PSUR) — ICH E2C

A **PSUR** is a periodic report from the MAH that presents worldwide safety experience of a medicine over a defined period after approval, with an evaluation of whether the benefit–risk balance has changed.

**Key concepts**

- **International Birth Date (IBD)** — the date of the first marketing approval anywhere in the world; reporting periods are aligned to it.
- **Data lock point (DLP)** — the cut-off date for data included in a report.
- **Reference safety information** — the company core safety information against which expectedness is judged.

**Classical PSUR contents**

1. Introduction
2. Worldwide marketing authorisation status
3. Actions taken for safety reasons in the period
4. Changes to reference safety information
5. Patient exposure
6. Individual case histories (line listings and summary tabulations)
7. Studies (new, ongoing, published)
8. Other information (efficacy, late-breaking data)
9. Overall safety evaluation
10. Conclusion

**PBRER — E2C(R2), 2012.** The PSUR was converted into the **Periodic Benefit–Risk Evaluation Report**, which:

- evaluates **benefits as well as risks** and gives an integrated benefit–risk analysis;
- considers **cumulative** data, not just the interval;
- has sections on signals (new, ongoing, closed), signal and risk evaluation, benefit evaluation and integrated benefit–risk analysis;
- is modular, so sections can be reused in the DSUR and RMP.

**Frequency:** set by each regulator; in India the rules require PSURs every six months for the first two years after approval and annually for the next two years (see Unit 5).

## 4.10 Post-approval expedited reporting — ICH E2D

**Sources of post-approval reports**

| Unsolicited | Solicited |
| --- | --- |
| Spontaneous reports from professionals and consumers | Reports from organised data collection: patient support programmes, surveys, registries, post-approval studies |
| Literature reports | Treated like study reports — need a causality assessment |
| Internet and digital media, other sources | |

**Rules**

- Spontaneous reports are considered to carry an **implied suspicion** of causality.
- **Serious, unexpected** ADRs from any source are expedited **within 15 calendar days**.
- Literature must be screened regularly; the date the MAH becomes aware of a published case is day 0.
- Special situations to be collected: lack of efficacy, overdose, abuse, misuse, medication errors, exposure during pregnancy and breastfeeding, occupational exposure.
- Good case management practices: follow-up, duplicate detection, data quality.

## 4.11 Pharmacovigilance planning — ICH E2E

E2E describes how to plan PV activities, especially around the time of approval, for new drugs or new indications.

**Two parts**

1. **Safety specification** — summary of what is known and not known:
   - **important identified risks** (proven by adequate evidence);
   - **important potential risks** (suspected, not confirmed);
   - **missing information** (populations not studied — children, pregnancy, hepatic impairment).
2. **Pharmacovigilance plan** — how each issue will be addressed:
   - **routine pharmacovigilance** (spontaneous reporting, PSURs, signal detection) for all products;
   - **additional activities** (PASS, registries, active surveillance, targeted studies) for specific concerns, with milestones.

E2E is the basis of the **Risk Management Plan (RMP)**, which adds **risk minimisation measures** — routine (labelling, pack size, legal status) and additional (educational material, controlled access, pregnancy prevention programmes).

## 4.12 Good clinical practice in pharmacovigilance studies

**GCP (ICH E6)** is the international ethical and scientific quality standard for designing, conducting, recording and reporting trials with human subjects. The current version is E6(R3), adopted by ICH in 2025.

**Core principles applied to safety studies**

1. Rights, safety and well-being of participants come first (Declaration of Helsinki).
2. Approval by an **ethics committee** and **informed consent** before participation.
3. A scientifically sound **protocol**, followed carefully.
4. Qualified investigators and trained staff.
5. Accurate, verifiable, protected data (data integrity, confidentiality).
6. Prompt recording and reporting of **adverse events and SAEs** by the investigator to the sponsor, and of SUSARs by the sponsor to regulators and ethics committees.
7. Monitoring, audits and quality management by the sponsor.

**Non-interventional (observational) safety studies** are not trials, but similar principles apply through **Good Pharmacoepidemiology Practice** and regulatory guidance on PASS: a written protocol, ethics review where needed, data protection, reporting of adverse reactions found during the study, and publication of results.

## Remember

- Pre-clinical → phases I–III → approval → phase IV/PMS; trials are too small to see rare ADRs.
- ICH founded **1990** (EU, Japan, USA; regulators + industry); renamed **Council** in **2015**; guidelines Q, S, E, M.
- E2A definitions and expedited reporting; E2B ICSR data elements; E2C PSUR → PBRER (R2, 2012); E2D post-approval; E2E PV planning; E2F DSUR; E6 GCP.
- Expedited: serious + unexpected. Fatal/life-threatening **7 days** (+8 for follow-up); other serious unexpected **15 days**.
- Minimum valid case: patient, reporter, suspect drug, reaction.
- PSUR keys: IBD, data lock point, reference safety information.
- E2E: safety specification (identified risks, potential risks, missing information) + PV plan (routine + additional) → RMP.

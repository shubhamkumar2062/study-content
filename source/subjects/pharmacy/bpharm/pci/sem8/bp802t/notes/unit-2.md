# Unit 2: Preventive Medicine — Prevention and Control of Diseases

Preventive medicine applies the levels of prevention (Unit 1) to particular diseases. For every disease in this unit, learn the same frame; examiners reward answers that follow it:

```text
Agent → Mode of spread / risk factors → Key clinical features → Diagnosis
     → Prevention and control (agent, host, environment) → Role of the pharmacist
```

No drug doses are given here; treatment is mentioned only as the type of therapy used in national guidelines.

## 2.1 General principles of prevention and control of communicable diseases

**Chain of infection**

```text
Agent → Reservoir/source → Portal of exit → Mode of transmission → Portal of entry → Susceptible host
```

Breaking any link stops spread.

| Measure | Aimed at | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Control of the reservoir | Source | Early diagnosis, treatment, isolation, notification, treating carriers, controlling animal reservoirs |
| Interrupting transmission | Route | Safe water, sanitation, food hygiene, vector control, hand washing, masks, condoms, safe injections, disinfection |
| Protecting the susceptible host | Host | Immunisation, chemoprophylaxis, nutrition, health education, personal protection |

**Other key terms**

- **Incubation period** — time from infection to first symptoms; decides the quarantine period.
- **Isolation** — separating a sick person; **quarantine** — restricting healthy contacts for the incubation period.
- **Notification** — reporting cases to health authorities (in India through the Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme, Unit 3).
- **Surveillance** — continuous watch on disease occurrence to act early.
- **Epidemic, endemic, pandemic** — sudden excess in an area; constant presence in an area; epidemic over many countries.
- **Disinfection** — concurrent (during illness) and terminal (after the patient leaves).

**General principles for non-communicable diseases (NCDs):** reduce shared risk factors (tobacco, alcohol, unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, obesity, air pollution) through primordial and primary prevention; screen adults for early disease; ensure regular treatment and follow-up; rehabilitate.

## 2.2 Cholera

- **Agent:** *Vibrio cholerae*, a comma-shaped Gram-negative bacterium. Serogroups O1 (classical and El Tor biotypes) and O139 cause epidemics.
- **Spread:** faecal–oral, through contaminated water and food, especially where sanitation is poor; floods, camps and slums carry high risk.
- **Incubation:** a few hours to about 5 days.
- **Features:** sudden painless watery diarrhoea ("rice-water stools"), vomiting, rapid dehydration, muscle cramps; can kill within hours if fluids are not replaced.
- **Diagnosis:** stool culture; rapid tests during outbreaks.

**Prevention and control**

1. **Rehydration is the main treatment** — oral rehydration salts (ORS) for most patients; intravenous fluids for severe dehydration. Antibiotics shorten illness in severe cases (as per guidelines). Zinc is given to children with diarrhoea.
2. Safe drinking water — boiling, chlorination of wells and tanks.
3. Sanitation — latrines, safe excreta disposal, no open defecation.
4. Food hygiene — cooked hot food, washed hands, no cut fruit from roadside stalls in outbreaks.
5. Hand washing with soap.
6. Notification and outbreak investigation; disinfection of stools and vomit.
7. Oral cholera vaccines are available for high-risk areas and outbreaks.
8. Health education.

**Pharmacist:** stock ORS and zinc, teach how to prepare ORS correctly, advise against anti-motility drugs in children, refer severe dehydration urgently.

## 2.3 SARS (Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome)

- **Agent:** SARS coronavirus (SARS-CoV). The outbreak began in southern China in 2002 and spread to several countries in 2003; it was brought under control by public health measures. The related virus SARS-CoV-2 caused COVID-19.
- **Spread:** respiratory droplets and close contact; contaminated surfaces; "super-spreading" events in hospitals.
- **Features:** fever, cough, breathlessness, muscle pain; atypical pneumonia; severe cases need ventilation.
- **Diagnosis:** clinical picture with contact history; RT-PCR on respiratory samples.

**Prevention and control**

1. Early detection and **isolation** of cases; **quarantine** and follow-up of contacts.
2. Infection prevention and control in hospitals — masks (N95 for health workers), gloves, gowns, eye protection, hand hygiene, isolation wards.
3. Respiratory etiquette; ventilation of rooms.
4. Screening of travellers during outbreaks; international reporting under the International Health Regulations.
5. Health education to avoid panic and stigma.

**Pharmacist:** supply masks, hand rub and PPE; counsel on cough etiquette; refer suspected cases; no unproven medicines; rational use during shortages.

## 2.4 Ebola virus disease

- **Agent:** Ebola virus (family Filoviridae). Fruit bats are thought to be the natural reservoir.
- **Spread:** to humans from infected animals (bushmeat); then person to person through **direct contact with blood, body fluids, secretions**, contaminated needles and bedding, and unsafe burials. Health workers are at high risk.
- **Incubation:** 2 to 21 days.
- **Features:** sudden fever, weakness, muscle pain, headache, sore throat; then vomiting, diarrhoea, rash, kidney and liver failure, internal and external bleeding; high fatality.
- **Diagnosis:** RT-PCR on blood in high-level biosafety labs.
- **Large outbreaks:** the West African epidemic of 2014–2016 was the largest so far.

**Prevention and control**

1. Isolation of patients in special units; strict barrier nursing with full PPE.
2. **Contact tracing** — contacts are monitored for 21 days.
3. Safe and dignified burials; disinfection of contaminated material.
4. Avoid handling or eating bushmeat; avoid contact with sick animals.
5. Screening at airports and ports for travellers from affected countries.
6. A vaccine is available for the Zaire species and is used for contacts and health workers in outbreaks.
7. Supportive treatment (fluids, electrolytes, oxygen); specific antibody treatments exist for the Zaire species.

**Pharmacist:** infection control supplies, waste disposal advice, travel advice, myth busting.

## 2.5 Influenza

- **Agent:** influenza viruses A, B and C (family Orthomyxoviridae). Type A is subtyped by surface proteins haemagglutinin (H) and neuraminidase (N), for example H1N1, H3N2, H5N1 (avian).
- **Antigenic drift** — small changes every season → seasonal epidemics; **antigenic shift** — major change (new H or N) → pandemics, such as the H1N1 pandemic of 2009.
- **Spread:** droplets, aerosols and hands; short incubation (1–4 days).
- **Features:** sudden fever, chills, headache, body ache, sore throat, cough; complications such as pneumonia in the elderly, very young, pregnant women and people with chronic disease.

**Prevention and control**

1. Seasonal influenza vaccine for high-risk groups (vaccine is updated each year to match circulating strains).
2. Cough and sneeze etiquette, masks for the sick, hand washing.
3. Stay home when ill; avoid crowds during outbreaks.
4. Antivirals (neuraminidase inhibitors such as oseltamivir) for high-risk patients and severe cases as per guidelines.
5. Surveillance of influenza-like illness and severe acute respiratory infection; control of bird flu in poultry.

**Pharmacist:** vaccine awareness, advice against antibiotics for simple flu, symptomatic care, warning signs for referral (breathlessness, chest pain, drowsiness).

## 2.6 Acute respiratory infections (ARI) and pneumonia

ARI are a leading cause of illness and death in children under five, mostly through **pneumonia**.

- **Upper ARI** — common cold, pharyngitis, otitis media.
- **Lower ARI** — bronchitis, bronchiolitis, pneumonia.
- **Agents:** viruses (respiratory syncytial virus, influenza, measles); bacteria (*Streptococcus pneumoniae* — the commonest bacterial cause of pneumonia, *Haemophilus influenzae* type b, *Staphylococcus aureus*).

**Risk factors:** low birth weight, malnutrition, no breastfeeding, overcrowding, indoor smoke from solid fuel, parental smoking, cold weather, missed vaccines (measles, pertussis, Hib, pneumococcal), vitamin A deficiency.

**Recognising pneumonia in a child (IMNCI approach)**

| Age | Fast breathing (breaths per minute) |
| --- | --- |
| Under 2 months | 60 or more |
| 2 to 12 months | 50 or more |
| 12 months to 5 years | 40 or more |

Chest indrawing, inability to drink, convulsions, lethargy or stridor = **danger signs** → urgent referral.

**Pneumonia in adults:** fever, cough with sputum, pleuritic chest pain, breathlessness; higher risk in the elderly, smokers, diabetics, alcohol users and people with HIV.

**Prevention and control**

1. Immunisation — measles, pertussis (DPT/pentavalent), Hib, pneumococcal conjugate vaccine; influenza and pneumococcal vaccines for at-risk adults.
2. Exclusive breastfeeding and good nutrition; vitamin A.
3. Clean cooking fuels and ventilated kitchens; no smoking near children.
4. Early case management with antibiotics by trained health workers as per national protocols; oxygen for severe cases.
5. Hand washing; less crowding.
6. Health education of mothers on danger signs.

**Pharmacist:** do not give antibiotics or cough syrups for a simple cold; recognise fast breathing and danger signs and refer; ensure full antibiotic course when prescribed.

## 2.7 Malaria

- **Agent:** *Plasmodium* species — *P. falciparum* (most dangerous, cerebral malaria), *P. vivax* (commonest in many parts of India, relapses from liver stages), *P. malariae*, *P. ovale*; *P. knowlesi* is a monkey parasite that can infect humans.
- **Vector:** female *Anopheles* mosquito (bites at night). In India, *An. culicifacies* is a major rural vector and *An. stephensi* an urban vector.
- **Life cycle:** sexual cycle in the mosquito; asexual cycle in humans (liver stage, then red blood cell stage causing fever).
- **Features:** fever with chills and rigors, sweating, headache, anaemia, enlarged spleen. Falciparum can cause cerebral malaria, severe anaemia, kidney failure, and is dangerous in pregnancy.
- **Diagnosis:** blood smear microscopy (thick and thin films) or rapid diagnostic tests (RDT) — every fever in a malarious area should be tested.

**Treatment principle (no doses):** as per the national drug policy, artemisinin-based combination therapy (ACT) for falciparum; chloroquine plus primaquine for vivax (primaquine for the liver stage; avoided in pregnancy, infants and G6PD deficiency); severe malaria needs parenteral artemisinin derivatives in hospital.

**Prevention and control**

1. Early diagnosis and complete treatment (cuts the parasite reservoir).
2. **Vector control** — indoor residual spraying, long-lasting insecticidal nets, larvivorous fish (*Gambusia*, guppy) in tanks and wells, source reduction (removing stagnant water), larvicides, fogging in outbreaks.
3. **Personal protection** — nets, repellents, full-sleeve clothing, screened windows.
4. Surveillance and active case detection.
5. Chemoprophylaxis for some travellers as per guidelines.
6. Community participation and health education.

The national programme for malaria is described in Unit 4.

## 2.8 Dengue

- **Agent:** dengue virus (flavivirus), four serotypes (DENV-1 to 4). Infection by one serotype gives lasting immunity to it only; a second infection with another serotype raises the risk of severe dengue.
- **Vector:** *Aedes aegypti* (mainly) and *Aedes albopictus* — **day-biting** mosquitoes that breed in clean stored water: coolers, tyres, pots, flower vases, water tanks.
- **Features:** high fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle and joint pain ("breakbone fever"), rash. **Warning signs:** abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding, restlessness, falling platelets with rising haematocrit. **Severe dengue** = plasma leakage with shock, severe bleeding or organ damage.
- **Diagnosis:** NS1 antigen in the first days; IgM antibody later; platelet count and haematocrit for monitoring.

**Prevention and control**

1. **Source reduction** — empty, cover and clean water containers every week; puncture or remove tyres; "dry day" once a week.
2. Larvicides in water that cannot be removed; fogging during outbreaks.
3. Personal protection in the daytime — repellents, full clothes, nets for daytime sleepers and patients.
4. Early detection and fluid management; no specific antiviral.
5. Community campaigns; National Dengue Day (16 May) awareness.

**Pharmacist:** advise **paracetamol for fever and avoid aspirin, ibuprofen and other NSAIDs** (bleeding risk); plenty of oral fluids; recognise warning signs and refer; myth busting about unproven "platelet-raising" products.

## 2.9 Chikungunya

- **Agent:** chikungunya virus (an alphavirus). The name comes from a Makonde word describing the bent-over posture of patients.
- **Vector:** *Aedes aegypti* and *Aedes albopictus* (same as dengue).
- **Features:** sudden fever and **severe joint pain**, often in hands and feet; rash; joint pain can last weeks or months. Deaths are rare.
- **Diagnosis:** RT-PCR early; IgM antibody later.
- **Treatment:** supportive — rest, fluids, paracetamol; avoid aspirin until dengue is excluded; physiotherapy for chronic joint pain.
- **Prevention:** exactly as for dengue — *Aedes* control, source reduction, personal protection.

## 2.10 Lymphatic filariasis

- **Agents:** *Wuchereria bancrofti* (most cases in India) and *Brugia malayi* — thread-like nematodes living in lymphatic vessels.
- **Vectors:** *Culex quinquefasciatus* (bancroftian filariasis; breeds in dirty water, drains, cesspits); *Mansonia* (brugian; breeds in ponds with water plants).
- **Microfilariae** circulate mostly at night (**nocturnal periodicity**), so blood smears are taken at night; antigen tests can be done during the day.
- **Features:** often no symptoms for years; then filarial fever, lymphangitis, **lymphoedema and elephantiasis** of limbs, **hydrocele**; disability and stigma.

**Prevention and control (elimination strategy)**

1. **Mass drug administration (MDA)** — the whole eligible population in endemic districts takes anti-filarial tablets once a year under supervision (DEC plus albendazole, or a triple-drug regimen adding ivermectin in selected districts); this stops transmission.
2. **Morbidity management and disability prevention (MMDP)** — limb hygiene, skin care and exercise for lymphoedema; surgery for hydrocele.
3. Vector control — drainage, cleaning drains, larvicides, removing water plants for *Mansonia*.
4. Personal protection — nets and repellents.
5. Surveys to confirm the stop of transmission.

**Pharmacist:** support MDA by explaining that tablets must be swallowed in front of the drug administrator, that mild reactions (fever, body ache) can occur because parasites are killed, and that pregnant women, very young children and the seriously ill are excluded.

## 2.11 Hypertension

**Hypertension** is persistently raised blood pressure, generally **140/90 mm Hg or more** on repeated measurements (some guidelines use lower cut-offs).

- **Types:** primary (essential, no single cause) — most cases; secondary (kidney disease, endocrine disorders, drugs, pregnancy).
- **Risk factors:** age, family history, obesity, **high salt intake**, low potassium intake, alcohol, tobacco, physical inactivity, stress, diabetes.
- **Complications:** stroke, heart attack, heart failure, kidney failure, retinopathy. It is a "silent killer" because most people have no symptoms.
- **Rule of halves** (classic observation): only about half of people with hypertension know it, half of them are treated, and half of those are controlled.

**Prevention and control**

| Level | Measures |
| --- | --- |
| Primordial / population | Less salt in processed food, school health education, tobacco control, parks and walking spaces |
| Primary | Reduce salt (WHO advises under 5 g per day for adults), more fruits and vegetables, weight control, regular exercise, no tobacco, limit alcohol, stress management |
| Secondary | Opportunistic and population screening of adults (at Ayushman Arogya Mandirs); regular treatment with antihypertensives; follow-up |
| Tertiary | Stroke and cardiac rehabilitation, kidney care |

**Pharmacist:** BP measurement and record card, counselling on lifelong adherence (do not stop when BP is normal), lifestyle advice, watch for side effects, avoid NSAIDs and decongestants without advice.

## 2.12 Diabetes mellitus

- **Types:** type 1 (insulin deficiency, autoimmune, usually young); type 2 (insulin resistance with relative deficiency, adults, linked to obesity — most cases in India); gestational diabetes; other specific types.
- **Diagnosis (standard criteria):** fasting plasma glucose 126 mg/dl or more; 2-hour value 200 mg/dl or more in a 75 g oral glucose tolerance test; HbA1c 6.5% or more; or random glucose 200 mg/dl or more with symptoms.
- **Risk factors:** family history, obesity (especially abdominal), physical inactivity, unhealthy diet, age, history of gestational diabetes, Asian Indian ethnicity (diabetes at lower body weight).
- **Complications:** heart attack, stroke, kidney failure, blindness (retinopathy), nerve damage, foot ulcers and amputations, infections.

**Prevention and control**

1. Primordial and primary — healthy diet (whole grains, vegetables, less sugar and refined food), regular physical activity, weight control, no tobacco.
2. Secondary — screening of adults; early treatment with diet, exercise, oral drugs or insulin; HbA1c monitoring.
3. Tertiary — eye, kidney and foot checks; foot care education; rehabilitation after amputation.

**Pharmacist:** glucose testing, insulin storage and injection technique, hypoglycaemia recognition and correction, foot care advice, adherence counselling, sick-day advice.

## 2.13 Cancer

**Cancer** is uncontrolled growth of abnormal cells that can invade and spread. In India, **oral, lung, breast and cervical cancers** are among the commonest; tobacco is the largest single preventable cause.

**Risk factors:** tobacco (smoked and chewed), alcohol, infections (human papillomavirus — cervical cancer; hepatitis B and C — liver cancer; *H. pylori* — stomach cancer), unhealthy diet and obesity, physical inactivity, radiation, occupational exposures (asbestos, benzene), air pollution, family history.

**Warning signs ("CAUTION")**

```text
C  Change in bowel or bladder habits
A  A sore that does not heal
U  Unusual bleeding or discharge
T  Thickening or lump (breast or elsewhere)
I  Indigestion or difficulty in swallowing
O  Obvious change in a wart or mole
N  Nagging cough or hoarseness
```

**Prevention and control**

| Level | Measures |
| --- | --- |
| Primary | Tobacco and alcohol control, healthy diet, physical activity, HPV and hepatitis B vaccination, safe sex, workplace protection |
| Secondary | Screening of adults for **oral** (visual examination), **breast** (clinical breast examination) and **cervical** cancer (visual inspection with acetic acid, Pap smear, HPV test); early diagnosis and treatment |
| Tertiary | Surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy; palliative care and pain relief; rehabilitation |

**Pharmacist:** tobacco cessation, awareness of warning signs, support for chemotherapy patients (side effects, safe handling), palliative care and access to opioid analgesics under rules.

## 2.14 Drug addiction — drug and substance abuse

**Key definitions**

- **Drug (substance) abuse** — use of a substance in a way that harms health or social life, or not as medically directed.
- **Dependence** — a cluster of behavioural and physical features: craving, loss of control, continued use despite harm, tolerance and withdrawal. **Psychological dependence** = compulsive craving; **physical dependence** = withdrawal symptoms on stopping.
- **Tolerance** — needing more of the drug for the same effect.
- **Withdrawal** — unpleasant symptoms when the drug is stopped.

**Commonly misused substances**

| Group | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Depressants | Alcohol, benzodiazepines, barbiturates |
| Opioids | Heroin (smack), morphine, codeine-containing cough syrups, tramadol |
| Stimulants | Cocaine, amphetamines, nicotine, caffeine |
| Cannabis | Ganja, charas, bhang |
| Hallucinogens | LSD |
| Inhalants | Glue, petrol, whitener, thinner (common in street children) |
| Tobacco | Cigarettes, bidi, gutka, khaini |

**Causes:** peer pressure, curiosity, stress, family problems, easy availability, unemployment, mental illness, early use of tobacco and alcohol.

**Effects:** physical damage (liver, lungs, brain), overdose deaths, HIV and hepatitis from shared needles, accidents, crime, job loss, family breakdown, poverty.

**Prevention and control**

1. **Supply reduction** — enforcement of the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act, 1985; control of prescription drugs (Schedule H, H1 and X rules).
2. **Demand reduction** — school and college awareness, life-skills education, family counselling, community campaigns.
3. **Harm reduction** — needle–syringe programmes and opioid substitution therapy for people who inject drugs.
4. **Treatment and rehabilitation** — detoxification, de-addiction centres, counselling, self-help groups (such as Alcoholics Anonymous and Narcotics Anonymous), vocational rehabilitation, relapse prevention.

**Role of the pharmacist (often asked)**

1. Dispense Schedule H1 and X drugs only on valid prescription; keep registers; watch for forged or repeated prescriptions.
2. Recognise misuse of cough syrups, sedatives and painkillers; refuse sale and counsel.
3. Educate schools and communities.
4. Refer to de-addiction centres; support recovering patients.
5. Store controlled drugs securely; report thefts.

## Remember

- Break the chain of infection: source, route, host.
- Cholera: *V. cholerae* O1/O139, rice-water stools, **ORS** is the main treatment, safe water and sanitation prevent it.
- SARS and Ebola: isolation, contact tracing, PPE; Ebola contacts watched for 21 days.
- Influenza: drift → epidemics, shift → pandemics; vaccine updated each year.
- Child pneumonia: fast breathing 60/50/40; chest indrawing = refer.
- Malaria → *Anopheles* (night); dengue and chikungunya → *Aedes* (day, clean water); filariasis → *Culex* (dirty water).
- Dengue: paracetamol only, avoid aspirin/NSAIDs; NS1 test early.
- Filariasis: annual MDA plus MMDP.
- Hypertension ≥ 140/90; salt < 5 g/day; rule of halves.
- Diabetes: FPG ≥ 126, OGTT 2 h ≥ 200, HbA1c ≥ 6.5%.
- Cancer: tobacco is the biggest preventable cause; screen for oral, breast, cervical cancer.
- Substance abuse: NDPS Act 1985; supply, demand and harm reduction.

# How to use this guide

Social and Preventive Pharmacy is a theory subject with no numericals. Marks come from **well-organised descriptive answers**: definitions, classifications, tables and the "objectives → functioning → outcome → pharmacist's role" frame for national programmes. Study one unit a day: read the notes, make your own one-page chart of each programme or disease, then answer the question bank aloud or in writing.

The five units follow the five units of the PCI syllabus for BP802T. No unit was split or joined. Unit 2 (diseases) and Units 3–4 (national programmes) carry the most marks.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

The question bank has long (10-mark), short (5-mark) and a few very short (2-mark) questions for each unit, and at least 10 MCQs per unit.

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare a full, structured answer.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from common patterns and syllabus weight, not a promise of what will be asked.

**Where the marks usually come from**

| Unit | Topics that keep coming back |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Health and disease | WHO definition and dimensions of health, levels of prevention, balanced diet, vitamin deficiencies, malnutrition (PEM), urbanisation and poverty, personal hygiene |
| 2 Preventive medicine | Malaria, dengue, cholera, filariasis, hypertension, diabetes, cancer, drug addiction; any one disease as "prevention and control" |
| 3 Programmes I | NACP, NTEP, UIP, Pulse Polio, NLEP, NMHP, IDSP, NPCB&VI, NPPCD |
| 4 Programmes II | RMNCH+A schemes, family welfare and contraceptive methods, NTCP and COTPA, malaria programme history and elimination framework, NPHCE, role of WHO |
| 5 Community services | Functions of PHC, primary health care principles and elements, rural sanitation, NUHM, school health services, health education methods |

**A note on dates and targets**

Programme names, launch years and targets change. The years in these notes were checked on official sources (mohfw.gov.in, nhm.gov.in, programme sites and government press releases). Before the exam, check whether any programme has been renamed or given a new target on mohfw.gov.in or nhm.gov.in. If you are not sure of a year in the exam hall, write the objective in words — a wrong date costs more than a missing one.

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. **Programme answers:** use headings — Introduction (year, nodal agency) → Objectives → Components/strategies (as a numbered list or table) → Outcome → Role of pharmacist. Even if you forget a detail, the structure earns marks.
2. **Disease answers:** agent → spread → features → diagnosis → prevention and control (source, route, host) → pharmacist's role. Add a small vector or chain-of-infection diagram.
3. **Draw simple diagrams:** epidemiological triad, natural history of disease, levels of prevention, chain of infection, rural health set-up, continuum of care, poverty–disease cycle.
4. **Tables score well:** vitamin deficiencies, marasmus vs kwashiorkor, levels of prevention, COTPA sections, contraceptive methods, IDSP S/P/L forms, population norms of SC/PHC/CHC.
5. **Always end with the pharmacist's role** — this is a pharmacy paper, and examiners look for it.
6. **Learn a few key numbers exactly:** SC 5,000/3,000, PHC 30,000/20,000, CHC 1,20,000/80,000; SAM criteria; child fast-breathing cut-offs 60/50/40; hypertension 140/90; diabetes FPG 126 mg/dl; quit line 1800-11-2356; Tele-MANAS 14416.
7. **No drug doses are needed.** Name the type of treatment used in national guidelines (ORS, ACT, MDT, ART) and focus on prevention.

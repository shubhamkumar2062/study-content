## 4.1 National Health Mission — the umbrella

- The **National Rural Health Mission (NRHM)** was launched on **12 April 2005** to strengthen rural health care. It created the **ASHA** (Accredited Social Health Activist), generally one per 1,000 population (relaxed in tribal, hilly and desert areas).
- The **National Urban Health Mission (NUHM)** was approved in **May 2013** (Unit 5).
- Together they form the **National Health Mission (NHM)**. Most programmes in Units 3 and 4 are funded and run through NHM, with state and district health societies, village health, sanitation and nutrition committees, and untied funds for facilities.

## 4.2 National health intervention programme for mother and child (RMNCH+A)

**Background:** Maternal and child health services were brought together under the **Reproductive and Child Health (RCH)** approach, and in **2013** the **RMNCH+A** strategy (Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, Child and Adolescent health) was adopted to address the main causes of death and illness along the **continuum of care**. A nutrition component was later added (often written RMNCAH+N).

**Objectives**

- Reduce the maternal mortality ratio (MMR), infant mortality rate (IMR), neonatal mortality and under-five mortality.
- Reduce the total fertility rate; meet unmet need for contraception.
- Improve the health and nutrition of adolescents; reduce anaemia.

**Continuum of care**

```text
Adolescence → Pre-pregnancy → Pregnancy → Delivery → Newborn → Infancy and childhood
   (and all stages are linked across home → community → facility)
```

**Functioning — main schemes and interventions**

| Life stage | Key interventions |
| --- | --- |
| Reproductive health | Spacing and permanent family planning methods; home delivery of contraceptives by ASHA; post-partum family planning |
| Maternal health | **Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY, launched 12 April 2005)** — conditional cash transfer to promote institutional delivery among poor women; **Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram (JSSK, June 2011)** — free delivery including caesarean section, free drugs, diagnostics, diet, blood and transport for pregnant women and sick infants; **Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA)** — free, specialist antenatal check-up on a fixed day every month; at least four antenatal visits; iron–folic acid and calcium; Td vaccination; identification of high-risk pregnancy; birth preparedness |
| Delivery | Skilled birth attendance; delivery points; quality improvement of labour rooms; emergency obstetric care |
| Newborn health | Essential newborn care, early initiation of breastfeeding within one hour, newborn care corners at delivery points, special newborn care units, home-based newborn care visits by ASHA, kangaroo mother care for low birth weight babies |
| Child health | Universal Immunization Programme (Unit 3); exclusive breastfeeding for six months; ORS and zinc for diarrhoea; management of pneumonia; vitamin A; deworming; **Rashtriya Bal Swasthya Karyakram (RBSK)** — screening of children from birth to 18 years for the "4 Ds": defects at birth, deficiencies, diseases and developmental delays, with referral to District Early Intervention Centres; nutrition rehabilitation centres for severe acute malnutrition |
| Adolescent health | **Rashtriya Kishor Swasthya Karyakram (RKSK)** — nutrition, sexual and reproductive health, mental health, injuries and violence, substance misuse, non-communicable diseases; adolescent-friendly health clinics; peer educators; weekly iron–folic acid supplementation; menstrual hygiene |
| Cross-cutting | **Anemia Mukt Bharat** (iron–folic acid for all age groups, testing and treatment of anaemia), maternal and child death reviews, ASHA incentives, Mother and Child Protection card |

**Outcome:** institutional deliveries have risen sharply; MMR, IMR and under-five mortality have fallen markedly over two decades. Remaining problems include anaemia, low birth weight, child undernutrition and differences between states.

**Pharmacist's role:** supply and counsel on iron–folic acid, calcium and ORS–zinc; promote breastfeeding and immunisation; recognise danger signs in pregnancy (bleeding, convulsions, severe headache, swelling) and in newborns (poor feeding, fast breathing, cold body, jaundice in the first day) and refer; avoid unsafe drugs in pregnancy and lactation; counsel on contraceptives.

## 4.3 National Family Welfare Programme

**Background:** India was the **first country in the world to start a national family planning programme, in 1952**, during the First Five Year Plan. The approach shifted over time from a "target-based" clinic approach to a broader **family welfare** approach linking family planning with maternal and child health, and later to a **target-free, voluntary, rights-based** approach. The **National Population Policy, 2000** guides it.

**Objectives**

- Help couples have the number of children they want, when they want — meeting the **unmet need** for contraception.
- Stabilise population growth consistent with sustainable development.
- Improve maternal and child health through spacing of births.

**Functioning — "basket of choices"**

| Type | Methods |
| --- | --- |
| Spacing (temporary) | Condoms; combined oral contraceptive pills; centchroman (non-hormonal weekly pill); injectable contraceptive (medroxyprogesterone acetate); intrauterine devices — copper IUCD, including post-partum and post-abortion insertion; emergency contraceptive pill |
| Permanent (terminal) | Female sterilisation (tubectomy, laparoscopic or mini-lap); male sterilisation (vasectomy, including no-scalpel vasectomy) |

Other components:

1. **Home delivery of contraceptives** and pregnancy test kits by ASHAs.
2. Compensation to acceptors of sterilisation for loss of wages; a family planning indemnity scheme.
3. **Mission Parivar Vikas** in high-fertility districts (check the current coverage on nhm.gov.in).
4. Comprehensive abortion care under the Medical Termination of Pregnancy Act.
5. Logistics through the family planning logistics management information system.
6. IEC — "small family" messages, birth spacing, delaying the first child.

**Outcome:** the total fertility rate has fallen and is now close to replacement level nationally, though some states remain above it; male participation (vasectomy) is still low.

**Pharmacist's role:** confidential counselling on contraceptive choice and correct use (missed-pill rules, emergency contraception within the time limit, condoms for dual protection against STIs), side effects, and referral for IUCD or sterilisation.

## 4.4 National Tobacco Control Programme (NTCP)

**Background:** Launched by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare in **2007–08** (initially in 42 districts of 21 states/UTs), now in all states and UTs. India's main tobacco law is the **Cigarettes and Other Tobacco Products Act (COTPA), 2003**. India is also a party to the **WHO Framework Convention on Tobacco Control (FCTC)**. The **national tobacco quit line** is **1800-11-2356** (toll-free).

**Objectives**

- Create awareness about the harmful effects of tobacco use.
- Reduce the production and supply of tobacco products.
- Ensure effective implementation of COTPA, 2003.
- Help people quit tobacco.
- Implement the strategies of the WHO FCTC.

**Main provisions of COTPA, 2003 (sections commonly asked)**

| Section | Provision |
| --- | --- |
| 4 | Ban on smoking in public places |
| 5 | Ban on direct and indirect advertising, promotion and sponsorship of tobacco products |
| 6(a) | Ban on sale to and by persons under 18 years |
| 6(b) | Ban on sale within 100 yards of educational institutions |
| 7 | Mandatory specified health warnings (pictorial) on tobacco packs |

**Functioning**

1. National, state and district tobacco control cells.
2. **Tobacco cessation centres** in district hospitals and medical colleges; quit line; mobile-phone-based quitting support.
3. Training of health and social workers, police and teachers; school programmes; "tobacco-free educational institutions" guidelines.
4. Enforcement squads to fine violators; monitoring of advertising.
5. Mass media campaigns; World No Tobacco Day (31 May).
6. Ban on electronic cigarettes (Prohibition of Electronic Cigarettes Act, 2019).

**Outcome:** national surveys show a fall in tobacco use among adults, but India still has very large numbers of smokeless tobacco users and high rates of oral cancer.

**Pharmacist's role:** brief advice using the **5 A's** (Ask, Advise, Assess, Assist, Arrange follow-up) and the **5 R's** for those not yet ready (Relevance, Risks, Rewards, Roadblocks, Repetition); nicotine replacement therapy counselling; referring to quit line and cessation centres; not selling tobacco from the pharmacy.

## 4.5 National Malaria Prevention Programme — history and present

**Historical stages**

| Year | Stage |
| --- | --- |
| 1953 | **National Malaria Control Programme (NMCP)** — indoor residual spraying with DDT; large fall in cases |
| 1958 | Converted to the **National Malaria Eradication Programme (NMEP)** |
| 1970s | Resurgence of malaria (insecticide and drug resistance, operational and financial problems) |
| 1977 | **Modified Plan of Operation** (with a *P. falciparum* containment programme) — aimed to prevent deaths and reduce illness rather than eradicate |
| Early 2000s | Malaria brought with other vector-borne diseases under the **National Vector Borne Disease Control Programme (NVBDCP)**; now run by the **National Center for Vector Borne Diseases Control (NCVBDC)** |
| 2016 | **National Framework for Malaria Elimination 2016–2030** |

NVBDCP covers six diseases: **malaria, dengue, chikungunya, lymphatic filariasis, Japanese encephalitis and kala-azar**.

**Present objectives (National Framework for Malaria Elimination 2016–2030)**

- **Interrupt indigenous transmission of malaria in all states and UTs by 2027.**
- **Eliminate malaria (zero indigenous cases) throughout the country by 2030**, and keep malaria-free areas free of transmission.
- A National Strategic Plan (2023–2027) builds on the framework. Check ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in for the current plan.

**Functioning**

1. **Early diagnosis and complete treatment** — RDTs and microscopy by ASHAs and health workers at village level; ACT for falciparum and chloroquine plus primaquine for vivax as per national drug policy; severe malaria management in hospitals.
2. **Integrated vector management** — indoor residual spraying in high-risk areas, long-lasting insecticidal nets, larvivorous fish, source reduction, minor engineering, insecticide resistance monitoring.
3. **Surveillance** — case-based surveillance, stratification of districts by risk (categories 0 to 3), outbreak response.
4. **Behaviour change communication** and community participation.
5. Inter-sectoral work with urban bodies, irrigation, construction and mining.
6. Monitoring of drug resistance (therapeutic efficacy studies).

**Outcome:** malaria cases and deaths have fallen sharply since 2015; many districts report zero cases; remaining burden is concentrated in forested and tribal areas.

**Pharmacist's role:** test-before-treat (no antimalarial without a test); full course counselling; G6PD caution with primaquine; promoting nets and repellents; reporting cases; checking antimalarial quality and storage.

## 4.6 National Programme for the Health Care of the Elderly (NPHCE)

**Background:** Launched by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare in **2010–11** to address the health problems of elderly people (60 years and above). It is run under the NCD flexible pool of NHM.

**Objectives**

- Provide easy access to promotive, preventive, curative and rehabilitative services for the elderly through a **community-based primary health care approach**.
- Identify health problems of the elderly and provide appropriate management.
- Build the capacity of medical and paramedical staff and of family carers.
- Provide referral services to district hospitals and regional medical institutions.

**Functioning — services at each level**

| Level | Services |
| --- | --- |
| Sub-centre / Ayushman Arogya Mandir | Health education, home visits for the bedridden, simple assistive devices, link with PHC |
| PHC | Geriatric clinic on fixed days, screening for common conditions |
| CHC | Geriatric clinic on fixed days, rehabilitation (physiotherapy) unit |
| District hospital | Geriatric OPD, geriatric ward beds, physiotherapy, laboratory support |
| **Regional Geriatric Centres** (medical colleges) | Dedicated geriatric OPD and ward, training, research |
| National Centres for Ageing | Tertiary care, training and research |

**Common health problems of the elderly:** hypertension, diabetes, heart disease, arthritis and falls, cataract and hearing loss, dementia and depression, urinary problems, polypharmacy and adverse drug reactions, loneliness and financial dependence.

**Pharmacist's role:** medication review to reduce **polypharmacy** and inappropriate drugs, simple dosing schedules, pill organisers, large-print labels, fall-risk drug counselling (sedatives, antihypertensives), home delivery, vaccination awareness (influenza, pneumococcal).

## 4.7 Social health programmes

"Social health programme" is best answered as the **community-based (social) arm of national health programmes** — schemes that use community workers, other sectors and social support to improve health.

| Programme / scheme | Social health feature |
| --- | --- |
| **ASHA** under NHM | Woman from the village, chosen by the village, acts as health activist: mobilises for immunisation and institutional delivery, home visits for newborns, depot holder for ORS, contraceptives, IFA and paracetamol, links people to services |
| Village Health, Sanitation and Nutrition Committees; Village Health and Nutrition Days | Community planning and monitoring; monthly service day at Anganwadi |
| **Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS)** / POSHAN Abhiyaan | Supplementary nutrition, growth monitoring, pre-school education, immunisation, health check-ups and referral through Anganwadi workers |
| School meals (PM POSHAN, earlier Mid-Day Meal) | Hot cooked meal in government schools; improves nutrition and attendance |
| **Ayushman Bharat** — Ayushman Arogya Mandirs (primary care) and PM-JAY (insurance for hospital care) | Reduces out-of-pocket spending for the poor |
| **Jan Aushadhi Kendras** (Pradhan Mantri Bhartiya Janaushadhi Pariyojana) | Quality generic medicines at low prices, run by pharmacists |
| Swachh Bharat Mission | Toilets, end of open defecation, solid waste management (Unit 5) |
| Rogi Kalyan Samitis | Patient welfare committees managing hospital funds with community members |

**Pharmacist's role:** work with ASHAs and Anganwadi workers, support health camps, run Jan Aushadhi stores, guide patients to free schemes.

## 4.8 Role of WHO in Indian national programmes

**WHO** is the United Nations agency for health, founded in **1948** (its constitution came into force on 7 April, now World Health Day), with headquarters in **Geneva**. India is in the **South-East Asia Region**, whose **Regional Office is in New Delhi**; there is also a WHO Country Office for India.

**Functions of WHO (general)**

1. Directing and coordinating international health work; setting norms and standards (for example, essential medicines list, treatment guidelines, International Classification of Diseases).
2. Technical cooperation with countries.
3. Surveillance and response to epidemics; the **International Health Regulations (2005)**.
4. Research, health statistics and publications.
5. Prequalification of medicines and vaccines; standards for biologicals.
6. Promoting primary health care and universal health coverage.

**WHO's role in Indian programmes**

| Programme | WHO contribution |
| --- | --- |
| Smallpox | Global eradication campaign; India free of smallpox in the 1970s |
| Polio | Part of the Global Polio Eradication Initiative; the National Polio Surveillance Project with the government ran AFP surveillance; certification of polio-free status (2014) |
| Immunisation | Technical support for UIP, measles–rubella surveillance and campaigns, AEFI systems |
| Tuberculosis | DOTS strategy and End TB strategy; guidelines for diagnosis and treatment; consultants supporting NTEP |
| Leprosy | Multidrug therapy recommended by a WHO study group; MDT drugs supplied free through WHO for many years |
| Malaria and vector-borne diseases | Global technical strategy, drug policy advice, elimination certification process |
| HIV/AIDS, hepatitis | Treatment and testing guidelines |
| NCDs and tobacco | Global action plans; the Framework Convention on Tobacco Control; WHO-supported surveys |
| Mental health | Mental health action plans, mhGAP guidance for non-specialists |
| Blindness | Global initiative to eliminate avoidable blindness |
| Emergencies | Outbreak response (SARS, Ebola, pandemic influenza, COVID-19), preparedness |
| Medicines | Essential medicines concept (India's National List of Essential Medicines follows it), pharmacovigilance, good manufacturing practice standards |

# Unit 2: Formulation of skin care, hair care and oral care products

This unit asks how a product is "built": what each ingredient does (the **building blocks**), how the product is made, and what its strengths and weaknesses are. The formulas given are **typical examples** to show the building blocks; real products vary, and percentages are shown as usual ranges, not fixed recipes. Cold cream, vanishing cream, shampoo, hair dye and toothpaste were also met in Industrial Pharmacy I (BP502T, Unit 5); this unit goes further into the product types the syllabus names.

## 2.1 General principles of formulating a cosmetic

Every cosmetic formula is planned around:

1. **Purpose and claim** – cleanse, moisturise, protect, colour, or a cosmeceutical benefit.
2. **Skin or hair type and user** – oily, dry, sensitive, baby, climate (hot, humid India needs light, non-greasy products).
3. **Vehicle** – solution, gel, o/w or w/o emulsion, suspension, stick, paste, bar.
4. **Building blocks** – base (water, oils), actives, surfactants or emulsifiers, thickeners, humectants, emollients, preservatives, antioxidants, chelating agents, pH adjusters, colour and perfume.
5. **Safety** – permitted ingredients and limits under the Cosmetics Rules and BIS standards, low irritation, patch testing.
6. **Stability** – no separation, colour or odour change, microbial growth or loss of active over shelf life.
7. **Aesthetics** – feel, spread, smell, look, foam.
8. **Pack** – compatible material, protection from air, light and contamination (tubes and pumps are better than open jars).
9. **Cost and manufacturing ease.**

## 2.2 Face wash

A face wash is a cleanser made for facial skin, which is thinner and more sensitive than the body and often oily (T-zone). It removes sebum, sweat, dirt, make-up and pollution without stripping the skin.

**Types:** clear gel, foaming cream, lotion (milky, non-foaming), foam (pump), scrub (with exfoliating particles), medicated or cosmeceutical (salicylic acid, tea tree oil, niacinamide).

**Building blocks of a gel face wash (typical example)**

| Ingredient | Role | Typical range (% w/w) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sodium laureth sulphate or a milder anionic (sodium cocoyl glycinate) | Primary cleanser | 8–15 |
| Cocamidopropyl betaine | Mildness, foam | 3–8 |
| Glycerin | Humectant, reduces tightness | 2–5 |
| Carbomer (neutralised) or acrylate copolymer | Gel body, suspends beads | 0.5–1.5 |
| Disodium EDTA | Chelating agent | 0.05–0.1 |
| Preservative (phenoxyethanol etc.) | Microbial protection | As permitted |
| Actives (aloe, salicylic acid, neem extract) | Benefit | As claimed |
| Citric acid / sodium hydroxide | pH to about 5.5–6.5 | q.s. |
| Perfume, colour, purified water | Appeal, vehicle | q.s. to 100 |

**Method:** disperse carbomer in water and hydrate → add glycerin, EDTA → add surfactants slowly with gentle stirring to avoid foam → neutralise to form the gel → add preservative, actives, perfume below about 40 °C → adjust pH → fill.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- |
| Milder and nearer skin pH than soap | Strong surfactants can still dry and irritate |
| Easy to add actives (anti-acne, brightening) | Needs preservative (water-based) |
| Clear, attractive gels; pleasant foam | Short contact time limits benefit of actives |
| Rinses off well; suits oily skin | Scrub particles can irritate; plastic microbeads are being replaced by natural or biodegradable particles |

## 2.3 Moisturising cream

A moisturiser restores and keeps water in the stratum corneum. It works by three mechanisms, usually together:

- **Occlusion** – an oily film reduces transepidermal water loss (petrolatum, mineral oil, dimethicone, waxes).
- **Humectancy** – attracts and holds water (glycerin, sodium PCA, hyaluronic acid, urea).
- **Emolliency** – smooths and softens the surface (esters, plant oils, fatty alcohols).
- Cosmeceutical moisturisers also add **barrier repair** lipids (ceramides, cholesterol, fatty acids).

**Typical o/w moisturising cream**

| Phase | Ingredient | Role | Typical range (% w/w) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oil | Liquid paraffin / caprylic-capric triglyceride | Emollient, occlusive | 5–15 |
| Oil | Cetostearyl alcohol | Body, co-emulsifier | 2–5 |
| Oil | Glyceryl stearate + PEG stearate (or cetearyl glucoside) | Emulsifier | 2–5 |
| Oil | Dimethicone | Smooth feel, occlusion | 1–3 |
| Water | Glycerin | Humectant | 3–8 |
| Water | Disodium EDTA | Chelating | 0.05–0.1 |
| Water | Xanthan gum or carbomer | Stabiliser | 0.1–0.5 |
| Cool-down | Preservative, perfume, actives (niacinamide, aloe) | Protection, benefit | As needed |
| – | Purified water | Vehicle | q.s. to 100 |

**Method (hot process):** heat oil phase and water phase separately to about 70–75 °C → add one phase to the other with high-shear mixing (homogeniser) → cool with slow stirring → add heat-sensitive items below about 40 °C → adjust pH (about 5–6) → fill.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- |
| Hydrates and repairs barrier; good for dry skin | Heavy or greasy creams can block pores (comedogenic) |
| Good vehicle for actives | Emulsions can separate on storage |
| O/w creams spread easily and wash off | Need preservatives |
| Can be tailored: light lotion for oily skin, rich cream for dry skin | Some users react to perfume or emulsifier |

## 2.4 Cold cream

A **cold cream** is a cleansing and emollient cream that gives a **cooling effect** as its water evaporates from the skin. The classic product is a **beeswax–borax cream**: borax reacts with the free fatty acids (cerotic acid) of beeswax to form **sodium soap in situ**, which emulsifies the system. Classic cold cream is mainly **water-in-oil**; modern versions may be o/w with added emulsifiers.

**Typical formula (classic)**

| Ingredient | Role | Typical amount (% w/w) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| White beeswax | Emulsifier precursor, body | 12–18 |
| Liquid paraffin | Oil, cleansing, emollient | 45–55 |
| Borax | Forms sodium soap with beeswax acids | 0.5–1 |
| Purified water | Cooling | 25–35 |
| Perfume, preservative, antioxidant | Stability, appeal | q.s. |

**Method:** melt beeswax and liquid paraffin at about 70–75 °C → dissolve borax in water at the same temperature → add the water phase slowly to the oil phase with stirring → stir while cooling → add perfume near 40 °C → fill.

**Uses:** removing make-up and dirt (cleansing cream), night and massage cream, for dry skin.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- |
| Cooling, soothing feel | Greasy; not liked in hot humid weather |
| Cleans oil-soluble make-up well | Can block pores in oily skin |
| Good emollience for dry, winter skin | Borax content is restricted in some countries (especially for broken skin and children) |
| Simple, cheap ingredients | W/o creams are hard to wash off |

## 2.5 Vanishing cream

A **vanishing cream** is an o/w cream based on **stearic acid**, partly neutralised by an alkali (potassium hydroxide, sodium hydroxide or triethanolamine) to form a **stearate soap**, which emulsifies the remaining free stearic acid. When rubbed in, the water evaporates and a thin, dry, non-greasy film of stearic acid crystals is left, so the cream seems to "vanish". It gives a matt look and holds powder, so it is used as a **day cream and foundation base**.

**Typical formula**

| Ingredient | Role | Typical amount (% w/w) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stearic acid | Main body; partly saponified | 15–20 |
| Potassium hydroxide (or triethanolamine) | Forms emulsifying soap | Enough to neutralise roughly one fifth to one quarter of the acid |
| Glycerin | Humectant, stops drying out | 5–10 |
| Cetyl alcohol | Texture | 0–2 |
| Purified water | Vehicle | to 100 |
| Perfume, preservative | Appeal, protection | q.s. |

**Method:** melt stearic acid (with cetyl alcohol) at about 75 °C → dissolve alkali and glycerin in hot water at the same temperature → add water phase to melted acid with constant stirring → keep stirring while cooling; the cream thickens and develops a **pearly sheen** (from stearic acid crystals) → perfume near 40 °C → fill.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- |
| Non-greasy, matt finish; good in hot climate | Little moisturising for dry skin |
| Good base under make-up and powder | Alkaline soap can irritate sensitive skin; pH higher than skin |
| Easily washable | Can "soap" (white streaks) when rubbed |
| Simple to make | Texture changes on storage (graininess from crystal growth) |

**Cold cream vs vanishing cream**

| Point | Cold cream | Vanishing cream |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emulsion type | Mainly w/o | o/w |
| Key ingredients | Beeswax, liquid paraffin, borax | Stearic acid, alkali, glycerin |
| Emulsifier | Sodium soap of beeswax acids | Potassium (or TEA) stearate |
| Feel | Oily, cooling | Dry, non-greasy |
| Use | Cleansing, night, massage | Day cream, foundation base |

## 2.6 Application of these products in cosmeceuticals

These bases are the vehicles for cosmeceutical actives:

| Base | Cosmeceutical use | Examples of actives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Face wash | Anti-acne, oil control, brightening washes | Salicylic acid, tea tree oil, niacinamide, glycolic acid, neem |
| Moisturising cream | Anti-ageing, barrier repair, brightening | Retinol, peptides, ceramides, hyaluronic acid, niacinamide, vitamin C derivatives, sunscreens (day creams) |
| Cold cream | Night repair creams for dry skin | Vitamin E, plant oils, retinyl palmitate |
| Vanishing cream | Day creams, fairness and matt foundations | Niacinamide, liquorice extract, UV filters, oil-absorbing powders |

Points to watch: active must be **stable** in the base (vitamin C and retinol oxidise; use antioxidants, airless packs, stable derivatives); the **pH** must suit the active (AHAs act at acidic pH); the active must reach the target layer (penetration enhancers or vesicles); the claim decides whether it remains a cosmetic.

## 2.7 Antiperspirants and deodorants: actives and mechanism

(Also part of Unit 5; the full topic is given there.) Briefly:

- **Antiperspirants** reduce sweat. Actives are **aluminium salts**: aluminium chlorohydrate, aluminium zirconium chlorohydrate (glycine complexes) and aluminium chloride. They dissolve in sweat and form a **gel plug of aluminium hydroxide** in the upper sweat duct, blocking sweat flow for a time.
- **Deodorants** do not stop sweat; they reduce **odour** by killing or slowing skin bacteria (ethanol, antimicrobials), absorbing odour (zinc ricinoleate), neutralising acids (sodium bicarbonate) or masking it (perfume).

## 2.8 Conditioning shampoo

A shampoo cleans hair and scalp (detail of a basic shampoo is in BP502T). A **conditioning (2-in-1) shampoo** cleans and conditions in one step.

**How it works:** it contains **cationic polymers** (polyquaternium-10, guar hydroxypropyltrimonium chloride) and often **silicone** (dimethicone) emulsion. In the bottle these stay dispersed with the anionic surfactant. When the shampoo is **diluted** with water on the head, the polymer and surfactant separate out as a **coacervate** that deposits on the negatively charged hair, carrying silicone with it. Hair is left softer, smoother and easier to comb with less static.

**Typical building blocks**

| Ingredient | Role | Typical range (% w/w) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sodium laureth sulphate | Primary surfactant | 8–14 |
| Cocamidopropyl betaine | Co-surfactant, mildness | 2–5 |
| Polyquaternium-10 or cationic guar | Conditioning (coacervate) | 0.1–0.5 |
| Dimethicone (emulsion) | Smoothness, shine | 0.5–2 |
| Glycol distearate | Pearlescence, suspends silicone | 1–2 |
| Sodium chloride | Thickener | 0.5–2 |
| Disodium EDTA, citric acid | Chelation, pH about 5–6.5 | q.s. |
| Preservative, perfume, water | – | q.s. to 100 |

## 2.9 Hair conditioner

A rinse-off conditioner is used after shampoo to make wet hair easy to comb, reduce static and frizz, and add shine.

**Building blocks**

| Ingredient | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **Cationic surfactant** – cetrimonium chloride, behentrimonium chloride (about 1–3%) | Positively charged; binds to negatively charged damaged hair; lubricates; reduces static |
| **Fatty alcohols** – cetyl, stearyl, cetostearyl alcohol (about 3–6%) | Form a lamellar gel network with the cationic surfactant; give creamy body |
| **Silicones** – dimethicone, amodimethicone | Smoothness, shine, heat protection |
| Oils and proteins (hydrolysed keratin, panthenol) | Nourishing claims, moisture |
| Acid (citric) | Low pH (about 4–5) closes cuticle scales |
| Preservative, perfume, water | – |

Types: rinse-off, leave-in (lighter, sprays), deep conditioning masks, and conditioning shampoos (above).

**Method:** melt fatty alcohol with cationic surfactant at about 75 °C, add to hot water with stirring, cool slowly (the gel network forms), add silicone, perfume, preservative below 40 °C.

## 2.10 Anti-dandruff shampoo

Dandruff is visible flaking of the scalp linked with the yeast **Malassezia**, sebum and individual sensitivity (Unit 5). An anti-dandruff shampoo is a cleansing shampoo with an **anti-dandruff active**.

| Active | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Zinc pyrithione | Antifungal; most common cosmetic active |
| Ketoconazole | Antifungal azole (medicated; licensed as a drug in India) |
| Selenium sulphide | Antifungal and slows cell turnover |
| Piroctone olamine, climbazole | Antifungal |
| Salicylic acid | Keratolytic; loosens scales |
| Coal tar | Slows cell turnover (medicated) |
| Tea tree oil, neem | Herbal antimicrobial claims |

**Formulation points:** insoluble actives (zinc pyrithione, selenium sulphide) must be **suspended** evenly with a suspending agent (acrylate polymers, glycol distearate) and deposited on the scalp (cationic polymers help); keep a contact time of a few minutes before rinsing; pH compatible with the active; conditioning agents to offset dryness.

## 2.11 Hair oils

Hair oils are very popular in India for scalp massage, shine, preventing breakage and as carriers for herbal extracts.

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Vegetable oils | Coconut oil (penetrates hair well because of its lauric acid triglyceride content and reduces protein loss), almond, sesame (til), mustard, castor, olive |
| Mineral oil based | Light liquid paraffin (light, non-sticky, stable, odourless) |
| Herbal (Ayurvedic) oils | Amla, bhringraj, brahmi, hibiscus, neem, methi extracted in a base oil |
| Light non-sticky oils | Blends of mineral oil with silicones or light esters |

**Building blocks:** base oil(s), herbal extract (by heating herbs in oil – taila paka – or by adding oil-soluble extract), **antioxidant** (BHT, tocopherol) to prevent rancidity, perfume, oil-soluble colour, sometimes vitamin E.

**Evaluation points:** colour, odour, viscosity, acid value and peroxide value (rancidity), saponification value, absence of free water and sediment, stability.

## 2.12 Para-phenylenediamine (PPD) based hair dye: chemistry and formulation

PPD dyes are **permanent (oxidation) dyes**. The colour is formed **inside** the hair shaft, so it lasts until the hair grows out.

**Chemistry:**

```text
Ammonia (alkaline pH ~9–10) swells hair and opens the cuticle
      ↓
Small colourless precursors (PPD + coupler) enter the cortex
      ↓
H2O2 oxidises PPD → reactive quinonediimine
      ↓
Quinonediimine + coupler → large coloured indo dye (dimer/trimer)
      ↓
Molecule is too large to wash out → permanent colour
```

- **Primary intermediates (precursors):** p-phenylenediamine, p-toluenediamine, p-aminophenol.
- **Couplers (modifiers):** resorcinol, m-aminophenol, 1-naphthol, m-phenylenediamine; each gives a different shade with PPD (brown, black, red tones).
- **Oxidising agent (developer):** hydrogen peroxide; also lightens natural melanin a little.
- **Alkaliser:** ammonia or ethanolamine.
- PPD can also self-couple to a coloured product (Bandrowski's base).
- **Antioxidant** (sodium sulphite) in the dye part stops early oxidation in the pack.

**Formulation – two-part system mixed just before use**

| Part 1: dye cream / gel | Part 2: developer |
| --- | --- |
| PPD and couplers | Hydrogen peroxide (stabilised) |
| Ammonia or ethanolamine | Stabiliser and acid to keep pH low |
| Surfactants and fatty alcohols (cream base) | Thickener (cream developer) |
| Antioxidant (sodium sulphite), chelating agent | Water |
| Conditioning agents, perfume | |

**Safety:** PPD is a strong **skin sensitiser** that can cause allergic contact dermatitis. Labels carry a warning and directions to do a **skin (patch) test about 48 hours before each use**; the product must not be used on eyebrows or eyelashes. "Black henna" (henna mixed with PPD) has caused severe skin reactions.

## 2.13 Oral care products

A toothpaste's basic building blocks (abrasive, humectant, binder, foaming agent, sweetener, flavour, preservative, therapeutic agent) are given in BP502T. Here are the special types the syllabus names.

### Toothpaste for bleeding gums

Bleeding gums usually mean **gingivitis** from plaque. Such toothpastes add:

- **Antiplaque / antibacterial agents:** stannous fluoride, zinc citrate, cetylpyridinium chloride; herbal agents such as neem, clove, miswak, tulsi.
- **Astringents** that tighten gum tissue: zinc salts, herbal tannins.
- **Anti-inflammatory herbal agents:** aloe, turmeric, chamomile.
- A **mild abrasive** and **mild surfactant** to avoid hurting inflamed gums; soft brushing advised.
- Triclosan was once used with a copolymer, but its use has fallen because of safety concerns.
- Persistent bleeding needs a dentist, since a toothpaste cannot treat periodontitis.

### Toothpaste for sensitive teeth

Sensitivity is explained by the **hydrodynamic theory**: when dentine tubules are open, fluid movement inside them (from cold, heat, air or sweets) stimulates the nerve.

| Approach | Agent | How it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nerve desensitising | Potassium nitrate, potassium citrate | Potassium ions diffuse along tubules and reduce nerve excitability |
| Tubule occlusion | Strontium chloride or acetate | Precipitates in tubules |
| | Stannous fluoride | Forms a protective layer |
| | Arginine with calcium carbonate | Plugs tubules with a calcium-rich layer |
| | Calcium sodium phosphosilicate | Deposits hydroxyapatite-like mineral |
| Supporting | Fluoride, low-abrasion silica, mild surfactant (or SLS-free) | Strengthens enamel, avoids further wear |

Benefit builds up over regular use for a couple of weeks.

### Teeth whitening

| Type | Agent / method | How it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Surface stain removal | Higher-cleaning abrasives (silica, calcium carbonate, alumina), enzymes (papain), pyrophosphates | Remove and prevent extrinsic stains |
| Chemical bleaching | Hydrogen peroxide or carbamide peroxide (releases hydrogen peroxide) in gels, strips, trays | Oxidises stain molecules inside enamel and dentine |
| Optical | Blue covarine | Blue film makes yellow teeth look whiter at once |
| Others | Activated charcoal (abrasive, may wear enamel) | Mechanical |

Problems: sensitivity, gum irritation, enamel wear with harsh abrasives; strong bleaching should be supervised by a dentist.

### Mouthwash

A mouthwash (mouth rinse) is a solution used to rinse the mouth to freshen breath, reduce plaque and gingivitis, or deliver fluoride.

| Ingredient | Role | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Antibacterial | Antiplaque, anti-gingivitis | Chlorhexidine gluconate (prescription-type, can stain teeth), cetylpyridinium chloride, essential oils (thymol, eucalyptol, menthol, methyl salicylate) |
| Fluoride | Anti-caries | Sodium fluoride |
| Solvent | Dissolves flavour and actives | Water, ethanol (alcohol-free versions use propylene glycol or glycerin) |
| Humectant | Body, sweetness | Glycerin, sorbitol |
| Surfactant | Solubilises flavour, cleaning | Polysorbates, poloxamers |
| Flavour and sweetener | Taste | Peppermint, sodium saccharin |
| Astringent, buffer, colour, preservative | Feel and stability | Zinc chloride, sodium benzoate |

Mouthwash is used **after** brushing, swished for about 30–60 seconds and spat out, not swallowed.

## Remember

- Moisturisers work by occlusion, humectancy, emolliency and barrier repair.
- Cold cream: beeswax + liquid paraffin + borax → sodium soap in situ → mainly w/o, cooling, cleansing.
- Vanishing cream: stearic acid partly neutralised by KOH or TEA → o/w, matt, pearly sheen, foundation base.
- Conditioning shampoo: cationic polymer + silicone deposit by coacervation on dilution.
- Conditioner: cationic surfactant + fatty alcohol gel network + silicone at low pH.
- Anti-dandruff actives: zinc pyrithione, ketoconazole, selenium sulphide, piroctone olamine, climbazole, salicylic acid, coal tar.
- PPD dye = precursor + coupler + H2O2 + ammonia → indo dye inside cortex; patch test 48 h before use.
- Sensitive teeth: potassium nitrate (nerve), strontium salts, stannous fluoride, arginine (tubule occlusion).
- Whitening: abrasives, peroxides, blue covarine. Mouthwash: chlorhexidine, CPC, essential oils, fluoride.

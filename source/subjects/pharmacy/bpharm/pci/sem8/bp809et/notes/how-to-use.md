# How to use this guide

Cosmetic Science is **one of the semester-8 electives** of the PCI B.Pharm course. It has no practical; the whole subject is the 75-mark theory paper. The subject is descriptive with almost no calculations (SPF is the only formula you must write). Marks come from clear definitions, ingredient tables ("building blocks"), mechanisms and labelled diagrams. Study one unit a day: read the notes, write each formula table and mechanism once from memory, draw the diagrams (skin layers, hair cross-section, PPD dye reaction, antiperspirant plug, instrument principles), then answer the question bank. Each unit has very short (2-mark), short answer (5-mark) and long essay (10-mark) questions, rated by how often that kind of question appears in PCI-syllabus papers.

The five units follow the five units of the PCI syllabus. The syllabus lists antiperspirants and deodorants in both Unit II and Unit V; in this guide Unit 2 gives a short summary and the full topic is in Unit 5. Indian cosmetic law (Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and Cosmetics Rules, 2020) is covered in Unit 1 with the definition of cosmetics. Cold cream, vanishing cream, shampoo, toothpaste, hair dye and sunscreen basics were first met in Industrial Pharmacy I (BP502T, Unit 5); this guide builds on them, so revise both together.

Formulas in the notes are **typical examples** to show the role of each ingredient. In the exam, a table of ingredient, role and example (with an approximate range if you know it) earns full marks; you do not need to memorise exact quantities.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ questions | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with a table, a diagram and examples.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from common patterns and syllabus weight, not a promise of what will be asked.

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. Start with a one-line definition, then a **building-blocks table** (ingredient – role – example). This one habit covers most formulation questions in Units 2 and 5.
2. For every formulation, add the **method** in 4–5 arrows and 3–4 **evaluation tests**; examiners look for all three parts.
3. Draw: skin layers, hair shaft cross-section, hair growth cycle, PPD oxidation scheme, antiperspirant plug, sebumeter and TEWL probe principle, stress–strain curve of hair.
4. Learn the exact facts the paper can test: UVB 290–320 nm, UVA 320–400 nm; SPF = MED protected ÷ MED unprotected at 2 mg/cm²; water's dielectric constant about 80 (corneometer); TEWL in g/m²/h; skin surface pH about 4.5–5.5; soap pH 9–10, syndet 5.5–7; BIS toilet soap TFM grades 76/70/60%; henna – lawsone; clove – eugenol; turmeric – curcumin.
5. In law questions, quote the Indian definition from the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and name the Cosmetics Rules, 2020; do not invent rule or form numbers.
6. For comparison questions (cold vs vanishing cream, soap vs syndet, physical vs chemical sunscreen, antiperspirant vs deodorant, irritant vs allergic dermatitis) use a two-column table with at least six points.
7. In problem-based questions (dandruff, acne, hair fall, dry skin) write: definition → causes → cosmetic approach with named actives → when to see a doctor.

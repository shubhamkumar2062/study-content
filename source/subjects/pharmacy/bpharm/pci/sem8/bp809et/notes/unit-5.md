# Unit 5: Cosmetic problems of skin, hair and scalp; antiperspirants and deodorants

The last unit connects the products of Unit 2 with the everyday problems that send people to the chemist's counter: oily or dry skin, pimples, dandruff, hair fall, dark spots, wrinkles, prickly heat and body odour. For each problem, know the cause in simple biology and the cosmetic approach. Where a problem becomes a disease (severe acne, alopecia areata, eczema), the pharmacist should advise a doctor.

## 5.1 Skin types: oily and dry skin

| Feature | Oily skin | Dry skin | Normal skin | Combination skin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sebum | High | Low | Balanced | Oily T-zone (forehead, nose, chin), normal or dry cheeks |
| Look | Shiny, enlarged pores | Dull, rough, flaky, fine lines | Smooth, even | Mixed |
| Feel | Greasy within hours of washing | Tight after washing, may itch | Comfortable | Mixed |
| Problems | Blackheads, acne, make-up slides off | Scaling, cracking, early fine lines, sensitivity | Few | Both |
| Care | Gentle foaming cleanser, oil-free light moisturiser (gel), non-comedogenic products, salicylic acid or niacinamide | Mild syndet or cream cleanser, rich moisturiser with humectant and occlusive, avoid hot water | Basic care | Treat zones differently |

**Causes of oily skin:** genetics, hormones (androgens increase sebum, for example in puberty), hot humid climate, some cosmetics, stress.

## 5.2 Causes leading to dry skin

Dry skin (xerosis) means the stratum corneum has too little water, usually because the **barrier** (lipids) or **natural moisturising factor (NMF)** is reduced, so water is lost faster than it is replaced.

| Group | Causes |
| --- | --- |
| Environmental | Low humidity, cold and windy weather, air conditioning and heating, sun exposure |
| Washing habits | Frequent bathing, hot water, harsh alkaline soaps and strong surfactants that remove lipids |
| Age | Older skin makes less sebum and lipids |
| Constitutional | Genetic tendency, atopic skin (eczema-prone) |
| Disease | Hypothyroidism, diabetes, kidney disease, ichthyosis, psoriasis |
| Drugs | Retinoids, diuretics, some anti-acne agents |
| Nutrition | Deficiency of essential fatty acids or vitamins |
| Occupational | Wet work, solvents and detergents |

## 5.3 Skin moisturisation

Water content of the stratum corneum keeps skin soft and flexible. Normal skin holds water through three systems: **intercellular lipids** (ceramides, cholesterol, fatty acids in layers), **NMF** inside corneocytes (amino acids, PCA, lactate, urea, salts), and **sebum** on the surface.

**Moisturiser actions (see Unit 2):**

| Action | Ingredient type | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Occlusion – reduce TEWL | Occlusives | Petrolatum, mineral oil, lanolin, dimethicone, waxes |
| Attract and hold water | Humectants | Glycerin, urea, lactic acid / sodium lactate, sodium PCA, hyaluronic acid, sorbitol |
| Smooth the surface | Emollients | Esters, plant oils, fatty alcohols |
| Repair the barrier | Physiological lipids | Ceramides, cholesterol, free fatty acids |
| Remove scaling | Mild keratolytics | Urea (higher strength), lactic acid, salicylic acid |

**Good moisturising practice:** apply soon after bathing on damp skin; use lukewarm water and mild cleansers; reapply in dry weather. Benefit is measured by corneometer (up) and TEWL (down) – Unit 4.

## 5.4 Comedogenic and dermatitis: basic terms

### Comedogenic

A **comedone** is a plugged hair follicle: an **open comedone (blackhead)** has a dark surface from oxidised material and melanin, a **closed comedone (whitehead)** is covered by skin.

**Comedogenic** describes an ingredient or product that tends to block pores and produce comedones. **Non-comedogenic** products are formulated and tested to avoid this. (Acnegenic means causing inflammatory acne lesions.)

- Ingredients often reported as comedogenic (depends on concentration and the whole formula): some fatty acid esters (isopropyl myristate, isopropyl palmitate), some lanolin derivatives, cocoa butter, coconut oil, some heavy oils and waxes, some D&C red colours.
- **Testing:** in the past the rabbit ear model was used; now human tests (repeated application under occlusion on the upper back, then follicular biopsy or examination) are used; animal testing of cosmetics is banned in India.
- Advice for acne-prone skin: oil-free, light gel formulas, non-comedogenic labels, removing make-up at night.

### Dermatitis

**Dermatitis** means inflammation of the skin: redness, itching, swelling, scaling, sometimes blisters and oozing (eczema is often used for the same picture).

| Type | Cause | Cosmetic link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Irritant contact dermatitis** | Direct damage by a substance; no allergy needed; anyone with enough exposure | Strong surfactants, soaps, solvents, acids and alkalis, frequent washing |
| **Allergic contact dermatitis** | Type IV (delayed) hypersensitivity to an allergen in a sensitised person | Fragrances, preservatives (isothiazolinones, formaldehyde releasers, parabens rarely), **PPD in hair dyes**, nickel, lanolin, some sunscreens |
| **Photo-contact dermatitis** | Reaction needing light plus a chemical | Some fragrances and sunscreen filters |
| **Atopic dermatitis** | Inherited barrier defect and allergy tendency | Needs mild cleansers, moisturisers |
| **Seborrhoeic dermatitis** | Inflammation in oily areas linked to Malassezia | Severe form of dandruff on scalp, face folds |

Prevention: patch test before using new hair dyes, choose fragrance-free products for sensitive skin, avoid overwashing; persistent dermatitis needs medical treatment.

## 5.5 Cosmetic problems of hair and scalp: dandruff

**Dandruff** is excessive shedding of scalp skin as visible white or greyish flakes, often with itching, without obvious inflammation (a severe, inflamed form is seborrhoeic dermatitis).

**Causes (three factors):**

1. **Malassezia** yeasts (*Malassezia globosa*, *M. restricta*) that live on the scalp.
2. **Sebum** – the yeast breaks down sebum triglycerides with lipase, releasing irritant **free fatty acids** (such as oleic acid).
3. **Individual sensitivity** – some people react with irritation and faster cell turnover, so scalp cells clump and shed as flakes.

Other contributing factors: infrequent washing, stress, cold dry weather, hormones, some hair products.

**Cosmetic management:** regular washing with **anti-dandruff shampoo** (zinc pyrithione, piroctone olamine, climbazole, selenium sulphide, ketoconazole as a medicated product), keratolytics (salicylic acid) for thick scales, coal tar (medicated), tea tree oil and neem (herbal); leave the shampoo on the scalp for a few minutes; continue use once or twice a week to prevent return.

## 5.6 Hair fall: causes

Hair fall (hair loss) above normal daily shedding has many causes. Most act by shortening anagen or pushing hairs into telogen early (Unit 1 hair cycle).

| Type / cause | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Androgenetic alopecia** (male and female pattern hair loss) | Hereditary; dihydrotestosterone (from testosterone by 5α-reductase) makes scalp follicles smaller (miniaturisation); receding hairline and crown thinning in men, diffuse thinning at the parting in women |
| **Telogen effluvium** | Sudden diffuse shedding about 2–3 months after a trigger: high fever, illness, surgery, childbirth, crash dieting, severe stress, some drugs |
| **Nutritional** | Deficiency of iron, protein, zinc, biotin, vitamin D; poor diet |
| **Hormonal and medical** | Thyroid disorders, polycystic ovary syndrome, after pregnancy, scalp infections (tinea capitis) |
| **Alopecia areata** | Autoimmune; round bald patches |
| **Drug-induced** | Anticancer drugs (anagen effluvium), some anticoagulants, retinoids |
| **Cosmetic and mechanical** | Tight hairstyles (traction alopecia), harsh chemical treatments (bleaching, perming, straightening), heat styling, rough combing → hair breakage |

**Cosmetic approach:** gentle shampoos and conditioners to reduce breakage, avoiding harsh treatments and tight styles, scalp oils and herbal tonics (amla, bhringraj) as traditional support, good nutrition. Medical treatment (for example minoxidil, which is a drug) is for a doctor to advise; hair fall that is sudden, patchy or heavy needs medical evaluation.

## 5.7 Cosmetic problems of skin

### Blemishes

Blemishes are marks or spots that spoil an even skin appearance: **hyperpigmentation** (dark spots), post-inflammatory marks after acne or injury, **melasma** (brown patches on cheeks, forehead, linked with sun and hormones), **freckles** and sun spots (lentigines), and acne spots and scars.

- **Cause:** extra melanin production or uneven distribution, triggered by UV light, inflammation, hormones and some drugs.
- **Cosmetic approach:** **daily sunscreen** (most important), skin-lightening cosmeceuticals that reduce melanin (niacinamide – blocks transfer of melanin to keratinocytes; kojic acid, arbutin, liquorice extract, vitamin C – reduce tyrosinase activity or melanin formation), exfoliation (AHAs), and cover-up make-up. Hydroquinone and topical steroids are drugs and must not be in cosmetics; mercury compounds are not allowed in skin-lightening products.

### Wrinkles

Wrinkles are creases and folds in the skin.

| Type of ageing | Cause | Changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Intrinsic (chronological)** | Age, genes, hormones (menopause) | Thin skin, fine wrinkles, less collagen and elastin, less sebum and dryness |
| **Extrinsic (photoageing)** | UV light (mainly UVA), smoking, pollution | Deep coarse wrinkles, leathery texture, pigment spots, broken elastic fibres (solar elastosis) |

Other contributors: repeated facial expressions (expression lines), loss of facial fat, poor sleep, dehydration of skin, free radicals (oxidative damage).

**Cosmetic approach:** sunscreen (prevention), moisturisers (plump fine lines), retinoids (retinol), antioxidants (vitamins C and E), peptides, AHAs, niacinamide; make-up and light-reflecting particles for temporary effect.

### Acne

Acne (acne vulgaris) is a disorder of the **pilosebaceous unit**, common in adolescents.

**Four main factors:**

```text
1. Increased sebum (androgens at puberty)
2. Abnormal keratinisation → follicle plugged → comedone (blackhead / whitehead)
3. Bacteria (Cutibacterium acnes, formerly Propionibacterium acnes) multiply in the plug
4. Inflammation → papules, pustules, nodules, cysts; may scar
```

Aggravating factors: hormonal changes, stress, comedogenic cosmetics, squeezing pimples, some drugs, possibly diet.

**Cosmetic approach:** mild cleansers twice daily, non-comedogenic oil-free moisturisers, salicylic acid (unblocks pores), niacinamide, tea tree oil, neem, clay masks; benzoyl peroxide and topical antibiotics or retinoids are medicines. Moderate to severe acne needs a dermatologist because it can scar.

### Prickly heat

**Prickly heat (miliaria)** is an itchy, prickling rash of tiny red or clear bumps in hot, humid weather.

- **Cause:** blockage of **eccrine sweat ducts** (by swelling of the skin surface in heat and sweat, tight clothing, heavy creams); sweat leaks into the surrounding skin and causes inflammation. Common in Indian summers, in babies and in people who sweat heavily.
- **Cosmetic approach:** keep skin cool and dry, loose cotton clothing, avoid heavy occlusive creams; **prickly heat powders** with talc or starch (absorb sweat), zinc oxide (soothing, mildly astringent), menthol and camphor (cooling, relieve itching), mild antiseptics; calamine lotion.

### Body odour

Fresh sweat is almost odourless. **Body odour** forms when **skin bacteria** (mainly *Corynebacterium* and *Staphylococcus* species) break down secretions of the **apocrine glands** (armpit and groin) and sebum into smelly volatile compounds: short-chain and branched fatty acids, sulphur-containing thioalcohols and steroid derivatives.

Contributing factors: puberty (apocrine glands become active), heat, exercise, stress, hairy armpits (more surface for bacteria), synthetic clothing, diet (garlic, onion, spices), poor hygiene, some diseases.

Control: regular washing, removing underarm hair, cotton clothing, and **deodorants and antiperspirants**.

## 5.8 Antiperspirants and deodorants: actives and mechanism of action

| Point | Antiperspirant | Deodorant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aim | Reduces the **amount of sweat** | Reduces or masks **odour** |
| Main actives | Aluminium and aluminium–zirconium salts | Antimicrobials, odour absorbers, perfume |
| Acts on | Sweat glands (ducts) | Bacteria and odour molecules |
| Regulatory (USA) | OTC drug (affects a body function) | Cosmetic |
| Often | Combined with deodorant actives | Can be sold alone |

### Antiperspirant actives

- **Aluminium chlorohydrate (ACH)** – the most common; less acidic and less irritant than aluminium chloride.
- **Aluminium zirconium chlorohydrate–glycine complexes** (for example aluminium zirconium tetrachlorohydrex gly) – more effective; glycine buffers acidity; used mainly in sticks and roll-ons (not in aerosols).
- **Aluminium chloride (hexahydrate)** – strong but more irritant; used in products for heavy sweating, usually applied at night to dry skin.
- Activated (enhanced) aluminium salts with smaller polymer species work better.

**Mechanism of action:**

```text
Aluminium salt applied to armpit → dissolves in sweat
        ↓
Aluminium ions hydrolyse; pH rises inside the duct
        ↓
Aluminium hydroxide / polymeric gel precipitates with mucopolysaccharides
        ↓
Gel PLUG forms in the upper (distal) part of the eccrine sweat duct
        ↓
Sweat cannot reach the surface → less wetness (and less odour)
        ↓
Plug is gradually cleared by skin renewal → regular reapplication needed
```

Aluminium salts also have some **antibacterial** action, adding a deodorant effect. Sweat reduction from cosmetic antiperspirants is partial, not complete. Possible problems: skin irritation (especially on freshly shaved or broken skin), stinging, yellow stains on clothes.

### Deodorant actives

| Approach | Actives | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Antimicrobial | Ethanol, triclosan (use now limited), chlorhexidine, quaternary ammonium compounds, farnesol, plant extracts | Reduce odour-forming bacteria |
| Odour absorbers | Zinc ricinoleate, cyclodextrins | Trap odour molecules |
| Neutralisers | Sodium bicarbonate, zinc oxide | Neutralise acidic odour compounds |
| Enzyme inhibitors | Some esters (triethyl citrate) | Slow bacterial enzymes that release odour |
| Masking | Perfume | Covers odour |

### Product forms

Aerosol sprays (alcohol base or anhydrous suspension of salt in silicone with propellant), roll-ons (aqueous or alcohol solutions or emulsions), sticks (waxes and volatile silicones with suspended salt), creams, gels and powders.

**Formulation points:** suspending the salt evenly in anhydrous products; avoiding nozzle blocking in aerosols; quick-drying, non-sticky feel; skin mildness (emollients); no white marks or clothing stains; efficacy tested by **gravimetric sweat collection** (pads weighed before and after sweating in a hot room) and odour by **trained sniffers**.

## Remember

- Oily skin: high sebum, shine, acne; dry skin: low barrier lipids and NMF, tightness, flaking.
- Moisturisation = occlusives + humectants + emollients + barrier lipids.
- Comedogenic = blocks pores to form comedones; non-comedogenic products avoid this.
- Dermatitis: irritant (direct damage) vs allergic (type IV, e.g. PPD, fragrance, preservatives).
- Dandruff = Malassezia + sebum + individual sensitivity; zinc pyrithione and friends.
- Hair fall: androgenetic (DHT, miniaturisation), telogen effluvium, nutrition, thyroid, alopecia areata, drugs, traction and chemicals.
- Blemishes: excess melanin → sunscreen + niacinamide, kojic acid, arbutin, vitamin C.
- Wrinkles: intrinsic vs photoageing; sunscreen, retinol, antioxidants, peptides.
- Acne: sebum + plugging + C. acnes + inflammation.
- Prickly heat = blocked eccrine ducts → powders with talc, zinc oxide, menthol, camphor.
- Body odour = bacteria acting on apocrine secretions.
- Antiperspirant = aluminium salts → gel plug in sweat duct; deodorant = antimicrobial, absorber, perfume.

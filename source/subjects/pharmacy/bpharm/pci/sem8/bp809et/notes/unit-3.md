# Unit 3: Sun protection, herbs in cosmetics and analytical cosmetics

This unit has three parts. Sun protection explains how sunlight harms skin and how sunscreens are classified and rated (SPF). The herbal part covers six plants named in the syllabus, two each for skin, hair and mouth. The last part is quality control: Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) specifications and the analytical tests used for shampoo, skin cream and toothpaste.

## 3.1 Sunlight and the skin

Sunlight reaching the earth contains ultraviolet (UV), visible and infrared light. UV is the part that matters most for skin.

| Band | Wavelength | Reaches earth? | Effects on skin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UVC | about 200–290 nm | Mostly absorbed by ozone | (Germicidal; not a skin problem from sunlight) |
| UVB | about 290–320 nm | Yes, mostly at midday and in summer | Sunburn (erythema), delayed tanning, DNA damage, skin cancer; also vitamin D formation |
| UVA | about 320–400 nm (UVA2 320–340, UVA1 340–400) | Yes, all day, through clouds and glass | Penetrates into dermis; immediate tanning, photoageing (wrinkles, loss of elasticity), pigmentation, photosensitivity reactions, adds to cancer risk |
| Visible and infrared | above 400 nm | Yes | Visible (blue) light can worsen pigmentation; infrared adds heat |

**Harmful effects of sun exposure:**

- **Acute:** sunburn, tanning, photosensitivity and phototoxic reactions (with some drugs, perfumes), prickly heat.
- **Chronic:** photoageing (wrinkles, coarse skin, uneven colour, broken capillaries), pigmentation (melasma, freckles, sun spots), immune suppression, actinic keratoses and skin cancers.

**Natural defences:** melanin, thickening of the stratum corneum after exposure, antioxidants in skin, sweat components such as urocanic acid.

**Sun protection measures:** avoid peak sun, protective clothing, hats and sunglasses, shade, and **sunscreens** applied in enough amount and reapplied.

## 3.2 Classification of sunscreens

### By mechanism

| Class | Mechanism | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Chemical (organic) sunscreens / UV absorbers** | Molecules with conjugated aromatic systems absorb UV energy and release it as harmless heat | See table below |
| **Physical (inorganic) sunscreens / sunblocks** | Particles reflect and scatter (and partly absorb) UV and visible light | Zinc oxide, titanium dioxide (micronised or nano grades look less white) |
| **Natural / botanical agents** | Weak UV absorption and antioxidant support; cannot replace proper filters | Aloe, green tea, turmeric (curcumin), Pongamia (karanja) oil |
| **Systemic / supportive** | Antioxidants that limit sun damage | Vitamins C and E (topical), oral antioxidants (claims only) |

### Chemical absorbers by the range they cover

| Range | Chemical group | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UVB | p-Aminobenzoic acid (PABA) and esters | PABA, padimate O (PABA now little used due to allergy and staining) |
| UVB | Cinnamates | Octyl methoxycinnamate (octinoxate), cinoxate |
| UVB | Salicylates | Octyl salicylate (octisalate), homosalate |
| UVB | Others | Octocrylene, ensulizole (phenylbenzimidazole sulphonic acid) |
| UVA (and some UVB) | Benzophenones | Oxybenzone (benzophenone-3), sulisobenzone |
| UVA | Dibenzoylmethanes | Avobenzone (butyl methoxydibenzoylmethane) – needs photostabilising (for example with octocrylene) |
| UVA | Anthranilates | Meradimate |
| Broad spectrum | Newer filters | Bemotrizinol, bisoctrizole, ecamsule (approval varies by country) |

### By formulation type

Lotions and creams (o/w or w/o), gels, sprays, sticks, powders and tinted products. **Water-resistant** sunscreens use film-forming polymers and w/o bases. A good sunscreen also has antioxidants, a pleasant non-greasy feel and stability under light.

**Ideal sunscreen:** absorbs or blocks both UVB and UVA (broad spectrum); photostable; non-irritant and non-sensitising; does not penetrate into the body; water and sweat resistant; spreads evenly; cosmetically acceptable; compatible with other ingredients; stable in the pack.

**Physical vs chemical sunscreens**

| Point | Physical (inorganic) | Chemical (organic) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Action | Reflect, scatter, absorb | Absorb and convert to heat |
| Spectrum | Broad (UVB + UVA, some visible) | Depends on the filter; usually combined |
| Feel | Can leave white cast | Light, invisible |
| Allergy risk | Very low | Some cause contact allergy or photoallergy |
| Photostability | Stable | Some degrade in light (avobenzone) |
| Suitable for | Sensitive skin, children | Daily wear, cosmetic products |

## 3.3 Sun Protection Factor (SPF) and UVA protection

**SPF** measures protection against **sunburn**, which is caused mainly by UVB.

```text
         MED of skin protected by sunscreen
SPF = ───────────────────────────────────────
         MED of unprotected skin
```

**MED (minimal erythemal dose)** is the smallest UV dose that causes just-visible redness 16–24 hours after exposure.

**In vivo SPF test (on volunteers):**

1. Select volunteers with skin types that burn (fair skin types).
2. Mark test sites on the back; apply the product at a standard amount of **2 mg/cm²**.
3. Expose small spots to increasing doses of UV from a solar simulator.
4. Read the MED on protected and unprotected sites after 16–24 hours.
5. SPF = ratio for each person; report the mean with statistics.

Water resistance is tested by measuring SPF again after set periods of immersion in water.

**In vitro SPF:** the product is spread on a roughened plate (PMMA) or tape and its UV absorbance is measured by spectrophotometry. A simple solution method used in student practicals is the **Mansur equation**:

```text
SPF = CF × Σ (290→320 nm) EE(λ) × I(λ) × Abs(λ)
CF = correction factor (10); EE × I = erythemal effect × solar intensity (tabulated);
Abs = absorbance of the diluted product at each wavelength (5 nm steps)
```

**What SPF numbers mean:** SPF 15 blocks about 93% of UVB, SPF 30 about 97% and SPF 50 about 98%. Higher numbers add only a little more protection. In real use people apply much less than 2 mg/cm², so the protection obtained is lower than the label.

**UVA protection** is rated separately:

- **PA system** (PA+, PA++, PA+++, PA++++) based on **persistent pigment darkening (PPD)** – used in Asia, including many Indian products.
- **UVA protection factor (UVA-PF)** and the **critical wavelength** test (a broad-spectrum product absorbs up to at least about 370 nm).
- EU recommends that UVA protection is at least one third of the labelled SPF.

## 3.4 Role of herbs in cosmetics

Herbal ingredients are popular in India because of Ayurveda, consumer preference for "natural" products, and their mild multi-functional actions (antioxidant, antimicrobial, soothing, colouring). They must still meet safety, stability and standardisation requirements: identity, active marker content, microbial load and pesticide and heavy metal limits.

### Skin care: Aloe

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Source | Leaf gel (inner parenchyma) of *Aloe vera* (Aloe barbadensis), family Liliaceae / Asphodelaceae |
| Constituents | Polysaccharides (acemannan, glucomannans), amino acids, vitamins, enzymes, salicylic acid; the yellow latex contains anthraquinones (aloin), which are removed from gel used in cosmetics |
| Actions | Moisturising (humectant polysaccharides), soothing and cooling, anti-inflammatory, wound and burn healing, mild antimicrobial |
| Uses | After-sun gels, moisturisers, face washes, shaving gels, hair gels, sunburn relief |
| Formulation points | Gel is mostly water and spoils easily, so needs preservation; colour and viscosity may change; often used as stabilised juice or powder |

### Skin care: Turmeric

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Source | Dried rhizome of *Curcuma longa*, family Zingiberaceae |
| Constituents | Curcuminoids (curcumin, a yellow polyphenol), volatile oil (turmerones) |
| Actions | Antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, skin brightening claims, wound healing |
| Uses | Traditional ubtan and haldi face packs, fairness and anti-blemish creams, anti-acne products, soaps |
| Formulation points | Curcumin is poorly water soluble and sensitive to light and alkali; it stains skin and fabric yellow; colourless tetrahydrocurcumin is used in some products |

### Hair care: Henna

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Source | Dried leaves of *Lawsonia inermis*, family Lythraceae |
| Constituent | **Lawsone** (2-hydroxy-1,4-naphthoquinone) |
| Action | Lawsone binds to hair keratin (and skin), giving a lasting orange-red to reddish-brown colour; also conditions and adds body to hair |
| Uses | Natural hair dye, conditioning hair packs, mehndi on skin; mixed with indigo (*Indigofera*) for brown to black shades |
| Points | Colour develops better in a slightly acidic paste left for some hours; quality depends on lawsone content. **"Black henna" adulterated with PPD** can cause severe allergic reactions |

### Hair care: Amla

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Source | Fruit of *Phyllanthus emblica* (*Emblica officinalis*), Indian gooseberry, family Phyllanthaceae (Euphorbiaceae in older texts) |
| Constituents | Vitamin C, tannins (emblicanins, gallic and ellagic acid), flavonoids |
| Actions | Antioxidant, astringent; traditionally used to strengthen hair, reduce hair fall and early greying, and darken hair |
| Uses | Amla hair oils, shampoos, hair packs (with shikakai and reetha), conditioners |
| Points | Tannins can darken with iron and age; extract is acidic and astringent |

### Oral care: Neem

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Source | Leaves, bark and twigs of *Azadirachta indica*, family Meliaceae |
| Constituents | Limonoids (azadirachtin, nimbin, nimbidin), flavonoids, tannins |
| Actions | Antibacterial against plaque bacteria, antifungal, anti-inflammatory, astringent |
| Uses | Neem twigs as traditional tooth brush (datun); herbal toothpastes, tooth powders and mouthwashes for gum health and bad breath; also anti-acne and anti-dandruff products |
| Points | Bitter taste masked with flavour and sweetener |

### Oral care: Clove

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Source | Dried flower buds of *Syzygium aromaticum*, family Myrtaceae |
| Constituent | Clove oil, rich in **eugenol** |
| Actions | Local anaesthetic (relieves toothache), antiseptic, antibacterial, flavouring |
| Uses | Toothpastes, tooth powders, mouthwashes, dental pain relief preparations; eugenol is used with zinc oxide in dentistry |
| Points | Undiluted oil can irritate gums and mucosa; used in small amounts |

**Advantages of herbs in cosmetics:** acceptance and tradition, multiple actions, mildness, marketing appeal. **Limitations:** variable composition, need for standardisation, microbial contamination, colour and odour, stability, possible allergy, limited scientific proof for some claims, and claims must not become disease claims.

## 3.5 Analytical cosmetics: BIS specifications

The **Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS)** publishes Indian Standards (IS) for cosmetics such as toothpaste, skin creams, shampoos, soaps, hair oils, talcum powder and lipsticks. Each standard gives:

- **Requirements** – description, permitted ingredients, physical and chemical limits (pH, solids, active matter), heavy metal limits (lead, arsenic and others), microbial limits, packaging and labelling.
- **Methods of test** – how each requirement is checked.

Under the Cosmetics Rules, 2020, cosmetics must conform to the relevant Indian Standard where one exists. Learn the **parameters and the principle of each test**; exact IS numbers and limits should be taken from the current standard.

### Shampoo: tests and analytical methods

| Test | Method / principle | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Description | Visual: colour, clarity, odour, homogeneity | Appearance |
| pH | pH meter on a dilute aqueous solution | Mildness to scalp and eyes, stability |
| Total non-volatile matter (solids) | Weigh before and after drying at about 105 °C | Concentration; avoids over-dilution |
| Active matter (anionic surfactant) | **Two-phase titration**: anionic surfactant titrated with a cationic surfactant using a mixed indicator (methylene blue / chloroform system) | Cleansing strength; label claim |
| Foam height and stability | Cylinder shake test or Ross–Miles method (solution falls from a fixed height; foam measured at start and after 5 minutes) | Lather |
| Viscosity | Brookfield viscometer | Pourability, consumer acceptance |
| Surface tension | Stalagmometer or tensiometer | Detergency |
| Wetting time | Time for a canvas disc to sink in the solution | Wetting ability |
| Dirt dispersion | Drops of Indian ink shaken in shampoo solution; ink should stay in the foam-free water layer, not the foam | Dirt is not redeposited on hair |
| Cleaning (detergency) | Removal of artificial sebum from hair tresses, by weight | Cleaning power |
| Heavy metals | Limit tests or atomic absorption spectroscopy for lead and arsenic | Safety |
| Eye and skin irritation | Non-animal methods and human patch tests (animal testing of cosmetics is banned in India) | Safety |
| Microbial limits | Total viable count; absence of specified pathogens | Hygiene |
| Stability | Storage at different temperatures and freeze–thaw | Shelf life |

### Skin cream: tests and analytical methods

| Test | Method / principle | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Description | Colour, odour, texture, homogeneity | Appearance |
| pH | pH meter on a dispersion of the cream in water | Skin compatibility |
| Thermal stability | Store at a raised temperature for a set time; check for oil or water separation | Emulsion stability |
| Emulsion type | Dilution test, dye test (water-soluble dye colours o/w continuous phase), conductivity (o/w conducts) | Identity |
| Total fatty substance / non-volatile matter | Extraction and weighing; drying | Composition |
| Viscosity and spreadability | Viscometer; spreadability by glass slide method (time or diameter) | Ease of application |
| Globule size | Microscopy | Stability |
| Heavy metals | Lead, arsenic, mercury limits (mercury is not allowed as a skin-lightening agent) | Safety |
| Microbial limits | Plate count, pathogens absent | Hygiene |
| Irritation and sensitisation | Human patch test | Safety |
| Washability and greasiness | Practical assessment | Consumer acceptance |

### Toothpaste: tests and analytical methods

| Test | Method / principle | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Description and consistency | Visual; paste extruded from tube should form a continuous ribbon | Appearance, extrudability |
| pH | pH meter on a slurry in water | Safety for mouth tissues |
| Fineness of abrasive | Wet sieving; weigh residue | Avoid gritty feel and damage |
| Sharp and hard-edged abrasive particles | Microscopy, or scratch test on a soft metal surface | Prevent enamel damage |
| Abrasivity | Relative dentine abrasivity (RDA) and enamel abrasivity methods | Cleaning vs wear |
| Fluoride content | **Fluoride ion-selective electrode**; total and free (soluble) fluoride; the Indian standard limits total fluoride to not more than 1000 ppm | Anti-caries effect and safety |
| Heavy metals | Lead and arsenic limit tests / AAS | Safety |
| Foaming | Foam volume of a slurry | Consumer acceptance |
| Volatile matter / moisture | Loss on drying | Drying out in the tube |
| Stability | Storage at raised temperature: no separation, gas, tube corrosion or swelling | Shelf life |
| Microbial limits | Total count, pathogens absent | Hygiene |
| Cleaning efficiency | Stain removal on standard surfaces | Performance |

## Remember

- UVB 290–320 nm (sunburn); UVA 320–400 nm (ageing, tanning, deep damage); UVC absorbed by ozone.
- Chemical filters absorb (PABA, cinnamates, salicylates, benzophenones, avobenzone, octocrylene); physical filters reflect and scatter (ZnO, TiO2).
- SPF = MED protected ÷ MED unprotected; applied at 2 mg/cm²; SPF 15 ≈ 93%, 30 ≈ 97%, 50 ≈ 98% UVB blocked.
- UVA rating: PA+ to PA++++ (persistent pigment darkening), UVA-PF, critical wavelength.
- Aloe (acemannan, soothing), turmeric (curcumin, antioxidant), henna (lawsone, dye), amla (vitamin C, tannins, hair tonic), neem (antibacterial, datun), clove (eugenol, analgesic).
- BIS standards give requirements and methods of test; cosmetics must meet them under the Cosmetics Rules, 2020.
- Shampoo: pH, solids, active matter by two-phase titration, foam (Ross–Miles), dirt dispersion, wetting, heavy metals.
- Cream: pH, thermal stability, emulsion type, spreadability, heavy metals, microbial count.
- Toothpaste: abrasive fineness and sharp particles, fluoride by ion-selective electrode (≤1000 ppm total in India), pH, heavy metals, stability.

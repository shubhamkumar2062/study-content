# Unit 2: Isolated Tissue Bioassays

Isolated tissue preparations allow the pharmacologist to study a drug's effect on a single tissue or receptor type, free from the integrating influences of the whole animal: reflexes, counter-regulatory hormones and metabolism by other organs are all excluded. This unit covers the technical basis of the organ bath, the tissues most commonly used and the calculations for each type of bioassay.

## 2.1 Principle of isolated tissue preparations

When a tissue is removed from the body and kept in a nutrient solution at the correct temperature with continuous oxygen supply, it retains its pharmacological responsiveness for one to several hours. Drugs added to this bathing solution diffuse into the tissue and produce measurable effects: a change in the tension of a muscle preparation is the most common endpoint.

The **signal** (usually isometric or isotonic muscle tension) is converted by a **transducer** to an electrical signal, amplified and recorded on a chart recorder or a data-acquisition computer. The resulting trace is an objective, permanent record of the response.

## 2.2 The organ bath

### Components and design

```text
Organ bath cross-section:

  Gas inlet (95% O2 / 5% CO2 or 100% O2) ───► bubbles through Krebs solution
  Jacketed outer chamber ─── thermostated water maintains 37°C (or species-specific temp)
  Inner bath ─── contains Krebs-Henseleit solution (25–75 mL)
  Tissue suspended vertically between two hooks
    Lower hook ── fixed to base
    Upper hook ── connected to isometric force transducer via thread
  Drug inlet ── usually by syringe into the bath
  Drain tap ── for rapid washout
```

### Types of organ baths
- **Simple organ bath**: a single-walled glass cylinder, temperature maintained by immersion in a water bath. Adequate for routine work.
- **Trendelenburg bath**: a U-shaped or angled bath in which the tissue is stretched longitudinally; used for intestinal preparations.
- **Jacketed organ bath**: double-walled; thermostated water circulates in the jacket. Maintains temperature precisely. The standard design for most modern work.

### Krebs-Henseleit solution composition (approximate, in mmol/L)

| Ion / Compound | Concentration (mmol/L) |
| --- | --- |
| NaCl | 118 |
| KCl | 4.7 |
| CaCl2 | 2.5 |
| MgSO4 | 1.2 |
| KH2PO4 | 1.2 |
| NaHCO3 | 25 |
| Glucose | 11 |

Purpose: provides the osmotic environment, ionic gradients and metabolic substrate identical to interstitial fluid. The bicarbonate/CO2 buffer (gassed with 95% O2 / 5% CO2) keeps pH at 7.4.

**Oxygenation**: continuous bubbling of carbogen (95% O2 / 5% CO2) supplies oxygen and removes carbon dioxide. The carbonate/bicarbonate buffer prevents the pH from drifting. Without adequate oxygenation, tissues lose contractility within minutes.

**Temperature control**: most mammalian tissues are studied at 37°C; frog tissues at 25°C or room temperature.

### Transducers
An **isometric force transducer** measures the change in tension when the muscle length is held constant (more common and more reproducible). An **isotonic transducer** measures the change in length when the load is held constant (used for recording lever movements). Modern preparations almost universally use isometric transducers with a computerised data acquisition system.

## 2.3 Commonly used isolated tissue preparations

### Frog rectus abdominis muscle

| Feature | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Species | Frog (Rana tigrina or Rana hexadactyla) |
| Tissue | Rectus abdominis muscle strip |
| Receptor | Nicotinic acetylcholine receptor (N-M type) |
| Response | Contraction (increase in tension) |
| Medium | Frog Ringer's solution at 25°C |
| Uses | Bioassay of acetylcholine and other nicotinic agonists; study of neuromuscular blocking agents |

Atropine does not block the contraction in this preparation, confirming it is a nicotinic response. Tubocurarine blocks it competitively.

### Guinea pig ileum

| Feature | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Species | Guinea pig (Cavia porcellus) |
| Tissue | 2–3 cm segment of ileum |
| Receptors | H1 (histamine), Kinin B2 (bradykinin), 5-HT3 (serotonin), μ-opioid, muscarinic |
| Response | Contraction |
| Medium | Krebs solution at 37°C, 95% O2 / 5% CO2 |
| Uses | Bioassay of histamine (most sensitive preparation); bradykinin, serotonin, opioid (naloxone-reversible relaxation); Schultz-Dale reaction for anaphylaxis |

The guinea pig ileum is the **most versatile and widely used isolated preparation**. Because it responds to so many autacoids it can be used in a cascade system to identify unknown substances.

**Schultz-Dale reaction**: an actively sensitised guinea pig ileum contracts violently when challenged with the specific antigen (antigen-antibody reaction on mast cells releases histamine and other mediators within the wall of the gut).

### Rabbit duodenum

| Feature | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Species | Rabbit |
| Tissue | 3–5 cm segment of duodenum |
| Receptors | α1-adrenergic (contraction), β2-adrenergic (relaxation) |
| Response | Spontaneous rhythmic contractions that increase with α-agonists and decrease with β-agonists |
| Medium | Krebs solution at 37°C |
| Uses | Bioassay of adrenaline and noradrenaline; demonstration of α/β-adrenoceptor activities |

Adrenaline at low doses may first inhibit (β2 predominant at low concentrations), then stimulate (α1 predominant at higher concentrations) spontaneous contractions. Noradrenaline predominantly contracts. Phentolamine blocks the contractile component; propranolol blocks the relaxant component.

### Rabbit jejunum

| Feature | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Species | Rabbit |
| Tissue | 3–5 cm segment of jejunum |
| Receptors | Oxytocin receptor (smooth muscle), prostaglandin TP receptor |
| Response | Contraction |
| Uses | Bioassay of oxytocin; prostaglandin bioassay (PGE2 relaxes, PGF2α contracts) |

### Rat uterus (oestrogen-sensitised)

| Feature | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Species | Rat in oestrus or primed with oestrogen |
| Tissue | Uterine horn |
| Receptors | Oxytocin receptor, PGF2α receptor, oestrogen-sensitised smooth muscle |
| Response | Phasic contractions (frequency and amplitude) |
| Medium | De Jalon solution (a low-calcium Ringer) at 30°C |
| Uses | Bioassay of oxytocin (most sensitive preparation; used for WHO oxytocin standardisation); ergometrine; prostaglandins |

Oestrogen priming upregulates oxytocin receptors and increases the sensitivity of the uterus to oxytocin.

## 2.4 Experimental procedure for an isolated tissue bioassay

```text
Step 1: Sacrifice the animal humanely; remove the tissue immediately.
Step 2: Place tissue in cold, oxygenated Krebs solution to minimise hypoxic damage.
Step 3: Trim the tissue to the required size (avoid crushing the ends used for hooks).
Step 4: Mount the tissue in the organ bath; attach to fixed hook below and transducer thread above.
Step 5: Apply a resting tension (preload) appropriate to the tissue:
         - Guinea pig ileum: 0.5–1 g tension
         - Rat uterus: 0.5 g
         - Frog rectus: 0.2 g
Step 6: Equilibrate for 30–60 minutes in Krebs at 37°C with continuous oxygenation.
Step 7: During equilibration, wash the bath at intervals (every 15 min) with fresh Krebs.
Step 8: Confirm viability: add a reference agonist (e.g., histamine 10 ng/mL) and check for a reproducible contraction.
Step 9: Wash out and allow tension to return to baseline.
Step 10: Carry out the bioassay.
```

## 2.5 Cumulative dose-response curves

In a cumulative (non-equilibrium) concentration-response experiment, increasing concentrations of an agonist are added to the bath **without washing between doses**, so the agonist accumulates until a maximum response is reached. The logarithm of the concentration is plotted on the x-axis and the response (expressed as a percentage of maximum) on the y-axis. This gives a sigmoidal curve from which EC50 and Emax are read.

For the **classical bioassay**, non-cumulative dose-response is used: each dose is washed out completely before the next is added.

**Washing between doses**: at least two or three complete volume changes of fresh Krebs solution are needed to wash out a drug. Inadequate washing leaves residual agonist and shifts the baseline upward.

## 2.6 Bioassay of acetylcholine on frog rectus abdominis (three-point assay worked example)

The **three-point assay** uses three doses in a cycle: low dose of standard (S1), unknown (U), high dose of standard (S2), then S1 again. The sequence SUSSUS or S1US2S1US2 is repeated three or more times.

```text
Worked example:
  S1 = 10 ng ACh → mean contraction = 15 mm
  U  = volume V of unknown → mean contraction = 22 mm
  S2 = 40 ng ACh → mean contraction = 32 mm

  The linear log-dose response equation between S1 and S2:
    log(10) = 1.000  → response 15 mm
    log(40) = 1.602  → response 32 mm
    Slope b = (32 - 15) / (1.602 - 1.000) = 17 / 0.602 = 28.24 mm per log unit

  Log-dose for unknown (from interpolation on the line):
    Log(dose_U) = 1.000 + (22 - 15) / 28.24 = 1.000 + 0.248 = 1.248
    Dose of standard equivalent to U = antilog(1.248) = 17.7 ng

  If V = 0.1 mL was added:
    Concentration of ACh in unknown = 17.7 ng / 0.1 mL = 177 ng/mL
```

## 2.7 Bioassay of histamine on guinea pig ileum (bracketing method)

Standard histamine doses bracket the response of the unknown sample.

```text
Doses:    S_low = 20 ng/mL → response 15 mm
          Unknown U        → response 24 mm
          S_high = 80 ng/mL → response 36 mm

Log-dose for unknown:
  Log(20) = 1.301; Log(80) = 1.903
  b = (36 - 15) / (1.903 - 1.301) = 21 / 0.602 = 34.9 mm per log unit
  Log(dose_U) = 1.301 + (24 - 15) / 34.9 = 1.301 + 0.258 = 1.559
  Dose equivalent = antilog(1.559) = 36.2 ng/mL

This is the potency of the unknown expressed as ng histamine per mL.
```

## 2.8 Bioassay of oxytocin on rat uterus

The rat uterus responds to oxytocin with rhythmic contractions. The response measured is usually the **height of the contraction** in mm, or the **area under the tension-time curve** per unit time.

A calibration curve is constructed with 0.5, 1 and 2 mIU/mL oxytocin (using the International Standard). The unknown preparation is tested in the same range, and its potency in mIU/mL is read from the calibration curve by interpolation.

## 2.9 Estimation of pA2 (Schild plot) for competitive antagonists

The **pA2** is the negative logarithm of the molar concentration of an antagonist that requires a **doubling** of agonist concentration to produce the original response. It is a measure of antagonist potency.

**Procedure**:
1. Construct an agonist concentration-response curve without antagonist → note EC50 (call it D).
2. Add a fixed concentration of antagonist, allow equilibration, then repeat the agonist CRC → note the new EC50 (call it D').
3. Calculate the dose ratio r = D' / D.
4. Repeat for at least three antagonist concentrations (B).

**Schild equation**:
```text
log(r - 1) = log(B) - pA2

Plotting log(r - 1) vs log(B) gives a straight line with slope 1 and y-intercept = -pA2.
```
If the slope is exactly 1, the antagonism is competitive and reversible (Schild equation is satisfied). pA2 is then the x-intercept of the Schild plot.

## 2.10 Four-point parallel-line assay: matching bioassay calculation

```text
Standard doses: S1 = 10 units, S2 = 30 units (dose ratio = 3)
Unknown doses:  U1, U2 with the same dose ratio of 3

Mean responses:
  S1 → 18 mm, S2 → 30 mm
  U1 → 24 mm, U2 → 36 mm

Slope of standard line: b_S = (30 - 18) / (log30 - log10) = 12 / 0.477 = 25.16 mm per log unit
Slope of unknown line:  b_U = (36 - 24) / (log ratio)  = 12 / 0.477 = 25.16 (parallel, good)

Mean log-dose of standard: (log10 + log30)/2 = (1.000 + 1.477)/2 = 1.238
Mean response of standard: (18 + 30)/2 = 24 mm

Mean log-dose of unknown:  (logU1 + logU2)/2 = midpoint of unknown
Mean response of unknown:  (24 + 36)/2 = 30 mm

Log-potency ratio M = (mean response_U - mean response_S) / b_average
                    = (30 - 24) / 25.16 = 0.238

Potency ratio R = antilog(0.238) = 1.73

If the unknown was labelled as 10 units/mL, the true potency = 10 × 1.73 = 17.3 units/mL.
```

## Remember

1. The organ bath maintains the tissue in oxygenated Krebs at 37°C; NaHCO3 is the buffer, glucose is the substrate.
2. The frog rectus abdominis tests nicotinic ACh receptors; atropine does not block but tubocurarine does.
3. The guinea pig ileum is the most versatile preparation — it responds to histamine, bradykinin, 5-HT and opioids.
4. The oestrogen-sensitised rat uterus is the standard preparation for oxytocin bioassay.
5. Wash the bath thoroughly between doses; residual agonist gives falsely high baselines.
6. In the three-point assay cycle SUSU, interpolate the unknown response on the standard log-dose line.
7. The four-point parallel-line assay is valid only if the two lines are parallel and linear.
8. pA2 = -log(molar concentration of antagonist that doubles the agonist EC50); higher pA2 = more potent antagonist.
9. A Schild slope of 1 confirms competitive (reversible) antagonism.
10. Equilibrate the tissue for at least 30 minutes before starting the bioassay.

# Unit 5: In-vivo Models, Toxicity Studies and Pharmacokinetics

This unit integrates the experimental models with the broader drug development pipeline. It covers cardiovascular and GI in-vivo preparations, the design of toxicity studies required by regulatory agencies, and the pharmacokinetic measurements that link animal data to clinical dosing.

## 5.1 In-vivo cardiovascular models

### 5.1.1 Pithed rat preparation

The **pithed rat** is a rat in which the brain and spinal cord have been destroyed by inserting a metal rod through the orbit, while keeping the cardiovascular system intact via artificial ventilation and an indwelling arterial cannula. All cardiovascular reflexes are ablated, so the preparation responds only to direct receptor stimulation by injected drugs without interference from reflex compensation.

**Uses**:
- Direct vasopressor and vasodepressor responses.
- Cardiac rate and contractility responses to catecholamines.
- Identification of adrenoceptor subtypes (alpha-1, alpha-2, beta-1, beta-2).
- Testing of sympathomimetics and sympatholytics without baroreflex confound.

### 5.1.2 Blood pressure recording methods

**Direct method (intra-arterial, anaesthetised rat)**:

```text
Equipment: surgical preparation → carotid or femoral artery cannulation
           → heparinised saline-filled polyethylene catheter → pressure transducer
           → amplifier → data recorder.
Measured: systolic, diastolic and mean arterial pressure (MAP); also pulse pressure.
Anaesthetic: urethane 1.25 g/kg i.p. (long-acting), or pentobarbitone 40 mg/kg i.p.
Advantage: continuous, accurate, real-time waveform.
Disadvantage: invasive, requires anaesthesia, single-use animal.
```

**Indirect method (tail cuff plethysmography, conscious rat)**:

```text
Equipment: inflatable cuff on the tail → photoplethysmographic detector (blood flow through tail).
Procedure: warm the rat (38°C for 5 min to dilate tail vessels), place in a restrainer,
           inflate the tail cuff above systolic BP, then release; record the pressure
           at which pulsations reappear (systolic BP).
Advantage: non-invasive, serial measurements in the same animal (chronic studies).
Disadvantage: only systolic BP; stressed animal (restraint); less accurate than direct.
```

**Uses of blood pressure recording**: evaluation of antihypertensive drugs (ACE inhibitors, calcium antagonists, ARBs, diuretics), vasopressors, inotropes; dose-response studies; drug interaction studies.

### 5.1.3 Antihypertensive drug evaluation

A chronically hypertensive rat model is used for antihypertensive screening. The **spontaneously hypertensive rat (SHR)** is a genetically hypertensive strain; blood pressure is reliably high (200+ mmHg) from 10–12 weeks of age. Drugs that lower BP in SHR are likely antihypertensive in humans. Weekly tail cuff measurements over 4 weeks give a time-course of effect.

## 5.2 In-vivo GI models

### 5.2.1 Castor oil-induced diarrhoea

**Principle**: ricinoleic acid (from castor oil hydrolysis by pancreatic lipase) activates EP3 prostaglandin receptors in the gut wall, increasing secretion and decreasing absorption of water and electrolytes, causing diarrhoea. Antidiarrhoeal drugs reduce the number of loose stools.

```text
Procedure:
1. Fast mice for 18 hours.
2. Administer test drug or vehicle 30 min before castor oil (0.5 mL/mouse p.o.).
3. Place each mouse in an individual cage lined with filter paper.
4. Count loose stools for 4 hours.
5. Loperamide (3 mg/kg p.o.) is the reference antidiarrhoeal.
```

Opioids (loperamide), NSAIDs (reduce prostaglandins), atropine (reduce secretion) all reduce castor oil diarrhoea.

### 5.2.2 Charcoal meal motility test

**Principle**: activated charcoal suspended in gum arabic (10% charcoal, 5% gum arabic) is given orally. The distance the charcoal meal travels from the pylorus to the leading edge as a fraction of the total small intestinal length is the **propulsion ratio**.

```text
Procedure:
1. Fast mice for 18 hours.
2. Administer test drug or vehicle.
3. Give charcoal meal (0.3 mL/mouse p.o.) 30 min later.
4. After 20 min, sacrifice, remove the intestine, measure total length and charcoal travel distance.
5. Propulsion (%) = (distance charcoal travelled / total intestinal length) × 100.
6. Antispasmodics and opioids reduce propulsion %; prokinetics increase it.
```

Atropine, loperamide and dicyclomine reduce propulsion. Metoclopramide increases propulsion.

### 5.2.3 Gastric secretion — Shay rat model

**Principle**: pyloric ligation in an anaesthetised rat prevents the emptying of gastric secretions, causing them to accumulate. After 4 hours, the stomach is opened and the gastric juice is collected for volume measurement and acid titration.

```text
Procedure:
1. Fast rat for 24 hours; water allowed until 2 hours before surgery.
2. Anaesthetise; perform mid-line laparotomy; ligate the pylorus.
3. Administer drug or vehicle intragastrically or intraduodenally just before closing the abdomen.
4. After 4 hours, sacrifice; collect gastric juice.
5. Measure volume (mL); titratable acidity (meq/L) with 0.01N NaOH to pH 7.0; calculate total acid output = volume × acidity.
6. Antiulcer drugs (omeprazole, ranitidine, sucralfate) reduce acid volume and total acid output.
```

**Parameters measured**: gastric juice volume, free and total acid (titratable), pH, pepsin activity. The model screens proton pump inhibitors, H2 blockers, antacids and cytoprotective agents.

## 5.3 Toxicity studies

### 5.3.1 Acute toxicity — LD50 determination

**Lorke's method (staged approach)**:

```text
Stage 1: 3 mice per group, administered doses of 10, 100 and 1000 mg/kg.
  Observe for 24 hours; record deaths.
  
Stage 2: Based on Stage 1 results, choose 4 doses in the range that includes the LD50.
  1 mouse per dose in Stage 2 (uses only 4 additional animals).
  Observe 24 hours.

LD50 calculation:
  Take the geometric mean of the highest dose that produced 0 deaths (D0)
  and the lowest dose that produced 100% deaths (D1):
  LD50 = √(D0 × D1)
```

**Limit test**: if a single dose of 2000 mg/kg (or 5000 mg/kg for some agencies) produces no deaths in 10 mice, the compound is considered to have an LD50 above this limit and is classified as practically non-toxic (GHS Category 5).

**Clinical signs after acute dosing**: motor activity, posture, gait, vocalization, eye signs (lacrimation, miosis), skin and fur condition, respiratory rate, cardiovascular signs. Necropsy at the end to identify gross organ damage.

### 5.3.2 Sub-acute toxicity (28-day and 90-day studies)

**Duration**: 28 days (4 weeks) for sub-acute; 90 days (13 weeks) for sub-chronic.

**Species**: rat and one non-rodent species (usually dog).

**Doses**: three dose levels (low, mid, high) + vehicle control group, 10 animals/sex/group.

**Parameters monitored**:

| Parameter | Frequency |
| --- | --- |
| Body weight | Weekly |
| Food and water intake | Weekly |
| Clinical observations | Daily |
| Ophthalmology | Before and at end |
| Haematology (CBC, differential, platelets) | Weeks 2 and 4 (or end) |
| Clinical biochemistry (liver enzymes, creatinine, electrolytes, glucose, lipids) | Weeks 2 and 4 |
| Urinalysis | End of study |
| Organ weights | At termination (absolute and relative to body weight) |
| Gross pathology | At termination |
| Histopathology | At termination (all animals at high dose and controls; targeted organs at other doses) |

**NOAEL** (No Observed Adverse Effect Level): the highest dose at which no adverse effects are observed. It is used to calculate the **safety factor** when setting the first-in-human dose: human first dose = NOAEL × (1/safety factor × body weight adjustment).

### 5.3.3 Chronic toxicity

Studies lasting 6–24 months (rodents for carcinogenicity; 12 months in non-rodents). Parameters are the same as sub-acute plus tumour incidence (rodents), carcinogenicity assessment (histopathology of all major organs) and special attention to endocrine, reproductive and immune organs.

### 5.3.4 Special toxicity studies

**Teratogenicity (Segment II reproductive toxicity)**:
- Administered to pregnant rats (or rabbits) from day 6 to day 15 of gestation (organogenesis).
- Caesarean section on day 20 (rat); examine foetuses for external, visceral and skeletal malformations.
- Endpoints: maternal body weight, uterine content (number of implantations, resorptions, live foetuses), foetal body weight, foetal malformations.

**Genotoxicity**:
- **Ames test (bacterial reverse mutation)**: Salmonella typhimurium strains with defective histidine operons; a mutagen restores wild-type growth on histidine-free medium. Positive = mutagenic. Metabolic activation added (rat liver S9 fraction) to detect pro-mutagens.
- **Micronucleus test**: mice receive the test substance; bone marrow is sampled 24–72 hours later; polychromatic erythrocytes are examined for micronuclei (fragments of chromosomes or whole chromosomes that failed to segregate at mitosis). An increase in micronuclei = clastogenic.

## 5.4 ICH guidelines for toxicity studies

The International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) publishes guidance documents to harmonise drug registration requirements across the US, EU and Japan.

| ICH category | Focus | Key guidelines |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q (Quality) | Chemistry, manufacturing, stability | Q1A–Q1F (stability); Q2 (validation of analytical procedures) |
| S (Safety) | Preclinical toxicology | S1 (carcinogenicity); S2 (genotoxicity); S3 (toxicokinetics); S4 (single and repeat dose); S5 (reproductive toxicity); S6 (biotechnology-derived drugs); S7 (pharmacological safety) |
| E (Efficacy) | Clinical trial design and conduct | E6 (GCP); E9 (statistical principles); E14 (QT studies) |

For a new drug application, **S4A** (duration of chronic toxicity to support clinical trials) and **S2(R1)** (genotoxicity testing) are specifically cited in regulatory submissions.

**OECD guidelines**: the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development publishes test guidelines for environmental and pharmaceutical toxicology (OECD 423, 425 for acute toxicity; OECD 407, 408 for 28-day and 90-day repeat dose; OECD 474 for micronucleus test). These are accepted in India by CDSCO.

## 5.5 Pharmacokinetic studies in animals

### 5.5.1 Single-dose PK study design

```text
Study design: Wistar rat (200–250 g), group of 6 per route.
Route: intravenous (IV) for absolute bioavailability; oral (PO) for bioavailability and clearance.
Sampling: tail vein or jugular cannula.
Sample time points (oral, typical): pre-dose, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12 and 24 hours.
Bioanalytical method: HPLC with UV, fluorescence or mass spectrometry detection.
Matrix: plasma (centrifuge blood at 3000 rpm; store at -80°C until analysis).
```

### 5.5.2 Calculation of PK parameters

**AUC (area under the plasma concentration-time curve)** by the **trapezoidal method**:

```text
AUC(0→t) = Σ [(C_i + C_{i+1})/2 × (t_{i+1} - t_i)]

For the terminal phase (from last measured point to infinity):
  AUC(t→∞) = C_last / λ_z

Total AUC(0→∞) = AUC(0→t) + AUC(t→∞)

Where λ_z = terminal elimination rate constant from the slope of log(C) vs time.
```

**Key PK parameters**:

| Parameter | Symbol | Calculation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maximum plasma concentration | Cmax | Directly from the data (highest observed C) |
| Time to Cmax | Tmax | Directly from the data |
| Terminal half-life | t½ | 0.693 / λz |
| Volume of distribution | Vd | Dose (IV) / AUC(IV) × t½ / 0.693, or Dose / (λz × AUC) |
| Total body clearance | CL | Dose (IV) / AUC (IV) |
| Bioavailability | F | [AUC(PO) / Dose(PO)] / [AUC(IV) / Dose(IV)] × 100% |

### 5.5.3 Bioavailability and bioequivalence studies in animals

**Bioavailability**: the fraction of the administered dose that reaches the systemic circulation in unchanged form. Absolute bioavailability compares AUC after oral and intravenous dosing (same dose). Relative bioavailability compares two formulations by the same route.

**Bioequivalence**: two formulations are bioequivalent if their 90% confidence intervals for the AUC and Cmax ratios fall within 80–125% (the regulatory acceptance window). Animal studies guide formulation development before human bioequivalence studies.

### 5.5.4 Microdialysis

**Principle**: a probe with a semi-permeable dialysis membrane is implanted in the tissue of interest (brain, muscle, interstitial fluid). Perfusion fluid is pumped through the probe, and small molecules diffuse across the membrane by concentration gradient into the perfusate (dialysate). Sequential samples of dialysate give the free (unbound) concentration of the drug in the tissue compartment over time, without repeatedly sampling blood or tissue.

Used to measure brain extracellular fluid drug concentrations, neurotransmitter concentrations, and metabolite profiles without killing the animal.

### 5.5.5 In-vitro-in-vivo correlation (IVIVC)

**Concept**: IVIVC is a predictive mathematical model relating the in-vitro dissolution profile (percentage drug dissolved vs time) to the in-vivo absorption profile (percentage drug absorbed vs time) for a modified-release formulation. If a good IVIVC (Level A: point-to-point) is established, it can replace bioequivalence studies for certain formulation changes.

Levels of IVIVC: Level A (highest; point-to-point correlation between dissolution and absorption); Level B (mean dissolution time vs mean residence time; not accepted as a surrogate for bioequivalence); Level C (single dissolution time point vs single PK parameter).

### 5.5.6 Allometric scaling

**Principle**: physiological parameters such as CL and Vd scale predictably with body weight across species according to a power function:

```text
Parameter = a × (Body weight)^b

Where:
  For clearance: b ≈ 0.75 (CL scales as metabolic rate)
  For volume:    b ≈ 1.0 (Vd scales directly with body weight)
  a = allometric coefficient (species-dependent)

Scaling CL from rat to human:
  CL(human) = CL(rat) × (BW_human / BW_rat)^0.75
  = CL(rat) × (70 / 0.25)^0.75
```

Allometric scaling provides a first estimate of human PK parameters from animal data, used to design first-in-human (Phase I) dose and dosing interval.

## 5.6 Drug discovery pipeline

```text
Target identification and validation
  ↓
Hit identification (high-throughput screening, virtual screening)
  ↓
Lead identification (hits with desired biological activity and acceptable selectivity)
  ↓
Lead optimisation (medicinal chemistry: improve potency, selectivity, ADME, reduce toxicity)
  ↓
Preclinical development:
  Pharmacology (in-vitro and in-vivo efficacy models)
  ADME studies (absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion in animals)
  Toxicology (acute, sub-acute, genotoxicity, safety pharmacology)
  ↓
IND (Investigational New Drug) application to the regulator (CDSCO in India, FDA in USA)
  ↓
Phase I clinical trial: healthy volunteers, safety, pharmacokinetics, dose range (20–80 subjects)
  ↓
Phase II clinical trial: patients, proof of concept, dose selection (100–300 subjects)
  ↓
Phase III clinical trial: large-scale efficacy and safety vs standard treatment (1000–3000 subjects)
  ↓
NDA (New Drug Application) or ANDA (Abbreviated NDA for generics) submission
  ↓
Regulatory review and approval
  ↓
Phase IV (post-marketing surveillance): safety in a broader population, pharmacovigilance
```

**IND filing** requires: pharmacology summary, ADME summary, toxicology summary, clinical protocol, investigator qualifications and manufacturing information.

**Post-marketing surveillance** detects rare adverse effects (< 1 in 1000) that are not identified in clinical trials.

## Remember

1. Pithed rat: brain and spinal cord destroyed but cardiovascular system intact; removes all reflexes; pure receptor pharmacology.
2. Direct BP recording: carotid artery cannula, continuous waveform; indirect: tail cuff, only systolic BP, non-invasive.
3. Shay rat model: pyloric ligation accumulates gastric juice over 4 hours; measures volume and total acidity for antiulcer drug screening.
4. Lorke's LD50: Stage 1 uses 3 mice/group at 10, 100, 1000 mg/kg; Stage 2 uses 1 mouse/group at 4 doses; LD50 = √(highest dose with 0% mortality × lowest dose with 100% mortality).
5. NOAEL: highest dose in a sub-acute study with no adverse effects; used to calculate the first human dose.
6. Ames test: bacteria; mutations measured as colony growth (histidine reversion); detects chemical mutagens.
7. Micronucleus test: in-vivo bone marrow; micronuclei in polychromatic erythrocytes indicate clastogenicity.
8. AUC by trapezoidal method: sum of trapezoids under the plasma concentration curve.
9. Bioavailability F = AUC(PO)/Dose(PO) ÷ AUC(IV)/Dose(IV) × 100%.
10. Allometric scaling: CL scales as (BW)^0.75; Vd scales as (BW)^1.0.
11. Drug discovery: target → hit → lead → optimise → preclinical → IND → Phase I–III → NDA → post-marketing.

# Unit 1: Nutraceuticals, functional foods and public health nutrition

This unit sets up the language of the whole subject. It defines functional foods, nutraceuticals and dietary supplements, classifies nutraceuticals, links them to common diseases, and then steps back to look at nutrition in the community — mothers and children, the elderly and nutrition education. It ends with seven well-known products used as nutraceuticals or functional foods: spirulina, soyabean, ginseng, garlic, broccoli, ginkgo and flaxseed.

## 1.1 Definitions

### Nutraceutical

The word **nutraceutical** joins "nutrition" and "pharmaceutical". It was coined by **Stephen DeFelice** in 1989. A nutraceutical is **a food, or a part of a food, that gives medical or health benefits, including the prevention or treatment of disease**, over and above basic nutrition.

In practice the term is used for **bioactive substances taken out of foods** (or made to match them) and given in a measured amount — lycopene capsules, fish-oil capsules, green tea extract, glucosamine tablets. FSSAI describes nutraceuticals as naturally occurring ingredients that are extracted, isolated and purified from food or non-food sources and that, taken in measured amounts, give a physiological benefit and help maintain good health.

### Functional food

A **functional food** looks and is eaten like an ordinary food, but it gives a health benefit **beyond basic nutrition** and may lower the risk of some diseases. The idea started in **Japan** in the 1980s; Japan later set up a legal class of such products called **FOSHU** ("Foods for Specified Health Use").

Functional foods can be:

- **Natural foods** rich in a useful component — oats (beta-glucan), tomato (lycopene), green tea (catechins), flaxseed (omega-3, lignans).
- **Fortified or enriched foods** — iodised salt, double-fortified salt (iron and iodine), vitamin A and D fortified milk and oil, iron-fortified rice and wheat flour.
- **Modified foods** — omega-3 enriched eggs, plant-sterol spreads, low-fat foods with added fibre.
- **Foods with live organisms or their food** — probiotic curd and drinks, prebiotic (inulin) biscuits.

### Dietary supplement

A **dietary supplement** is a product **taken by mouth** that **adds** a dietary ingredient — vitamin, mineral, herb or other botanical, amino acid, enzyme, fatty acid, or a concentrate or extract of these — to the normal diet. It is usually sold in **dosage forms**: tablets, capsules, soft gels, powders, sachets, liquids. The term comes from US law (the **Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act, DSHEA, 1994**). Indian law (FSSAI) uses the term **health supplement** for a similar group of products meant to supplement the normal diet with concentrated sources of nutrients.

### How the terms differ

| Point | Functional food | Nutraceutical | Dietary / health supplement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Form | Ordinary food | Isolated or concentrated bioactive | Tablet, capsule, powder, liquid |
| How eaten | As part of normal meals | In measured amounts | In measured amounts, by label directions |
| Main aim | Health benefit beyond nutrition | Physiological benefit, health maintenance | Add nutrients missing from the diet |
| Examples | Oats, probiotic curd, fortified salt | Lycopene, EGCG, omega-3 concentrate | Multivitamin, calcium with vitamin D, protein powder |

Related terms worth knowing:

- **Medical food / food for special medical purpose (FSMP)** — specially made food for dietary management of a patient under medical supervision (for example, formulas for inborn errors such as phenylketonuria).
- **Food for special dietary use (FSDU)** — food made for people with particular needs (weight management, sportspersons, pregnancy, elderly, coeliac disease).
- **Phytochemical / phytonutrient** — a non-nutrient plant compound with a health effect (carotenoids, flavonoids, sulphur compounds).
- **Probiotic, prebiotic, synbiotic** — live helpful microbes, non-digestible food that feeds them, and the two combined.

## 1.2 Classification of nutraceuticals

Nutraceuticals can be grouped in several ways. Write at least three in an exam answer.

### A. By source

| Source | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| **Plants** | Lycopene (tomato), curcumin (turmeric), resveratrol (grapes), isoflavones (soya), sulforaphane (broccoli), allicin (garlic), ginsenosides (ginseng) |
| **Animals** | Omega-3 fatty acids EPA and DHA (fish oil), conjugated linoleic acid (milk, meat), glucosamine and chitosan (shellfish), chondroitin (cartilage), collagen peptides, lactoferrin |
| **Microbes** | Probiotics (*Lactobacillus*, *Bifidobacterium*, *Saccharomyces boulardii*), spirulina (cyanobacterium), yeast beta-glucan, fermented products |
| **Minerals** | Calcium, iron, zinc, selenium, magnesium |

### B. By chemical nature

1. **Isoprenoid derivatives (terpenoids)** — carotenoids (beta-carotene, lycopene, lutein), saponins, tocopherols and tocotrienols, limonene, phytosterols.
2. **Phenolic compounds** — flavonoids (quercetin, catechins, anthocyanins), isoflavones, lignans, stilbenes (resveratrol), phenolic acids.
3. **Carbohydrates and their derivatives** — dietary fibre (pectin, beta-glucan, psyllium), oligosaccharides (fructo-oligosaccharides, inulin), ascorbic acid.
4. **Fatty acids and structural lipids** — omega-3 (alpha-linolenic acid, EPA, DHA), omega-6 (gamma-linolenic acid), conjugated linoleic acid, lecithin.
5. **Amino-acid based** — amino acids, peptides, proteins (soy protein, whey), glutathione, isothiocyanates (formed from glucosinolates).
6. **Microbes** — probiotics.
7. **Minerals** — calcium, selenium, zinc, potassium, copper.

### C. By mechanism of action

- **Antioxidants** — vitamin C, vitamin E, carotenoids, flavonoids, polyphenols.
- **Anti-inflammatory** — omega-3 fatty acids, curcumin, gamma-linolenic acid.
- **Lipid lowering** — soluble fibre, phytosterols, soy protein, omega-3.
- **Antimicrobial / gut flora modifying** — garlic, probiotics, prebiotics.
- **Anticancer (chemopreventive)** — sulforaphane, lycopene, EGCG, isoflavones.
- **Bone and joint support** — calcium, vitamin D, glucosamine, chondroitin.

### D. By how the product is presented

- **Dietary supplements** — capsules and tablets of vitamins, minerals, herbs, fish oil.
- **Functional foods** — fortified, enriched and probiotic foods.
- **Medicinal foods** — products for dietary management of disease.
- **Farmaceuticals** — useful compounds produced by modified crops or animals (a newer group).

### E. By certainty of benefit (traditional vs non-traditional)

- **Traditional nutraceuticals** — natural foods with no change made (tomato, garlic, green tea, fish).
- **Non-traditional nutraceuticals** — foods that were fortified, or bred or engineered to raise a nutrient (iodised salt, vitamin A rice, omega-3 eggs).

## 1.3 Health problems in which nutraceuticals help

Nutraceuticals **support** health and may lower the risk of disease. They do not replace medicines, and under Indian food law their labels may not claim to prevent, treat or cure a disease.

| Problem | Nutraceuticals and functional foods used | How they are thought to work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Weight control / obesity** | Soluble fibre (glucomannan, psyllium, guar gum), green tea catechins, conjugated linoleic acid, high-protein foods, chitosan | Fibre fills the stomach and slows emptying, giving fullness; catechins and caffeine slightly increase energy use; protein increases satiety |
| **Diabetes** | Soluble fibre, fenugreek seed, bitter gourd, cinnamon, chromium, alpha-lipoic acid, oats beta-glucan | Slow sugar absorption, lower post-meal glucose, improve insulin sensitivity, reduce oxidative stress |
| **Cancer** (risk reduction) | Lycopene, sulforaphane, green tea EGCG, curcumin, resveratrol, soy isoflavones, flax lignans, selenium, fibre | Antioxidant action, induction of detoxifying (phase II) enzymes, blocking cell growth, promoting apoptosis, anti-inflammatory action |
| **Heart disease** | Omega-3 fatty acids, plant sterols and stanols, oat beta-glucan, soy protein, garlic, flavonoids, coenzyme Q10 | Lower LDL cholesterol and triglycerides, stop LDL oxidation, reduce platelet clumping, improve the lining of blood vessels |
| **Hypertension** | Potassium-rich foods, garlic, flavanols (cocoa, tea), omega-3, beetroot nitrate, fermented milk peptides | Relax blood vessels (nitric oxide), act like mild ACE inhibitors (milk peptides), balance sodium |
| **Stress and anxiety** | Adaptogens (ginseng, ashwagandha), L-theanine (tea), B vitamins, magnesium, omega-3 | Help the body cope with stress, calm the nervous system |
| **Osteoarthritis** | Glucosamine, chondroitin sulphate, collagen, omega-3, curcumin, boswellia | Supply building blocks of cartilage, reduce inflammation |
| **Osteoporosis** | Calcium, vitamin D, vitamin K, soy isoflavones | Bone mineralisation, reduce bone loss |
| **Eye health** | Lutein, zeaxanthin, omega-3 (DHA) | Filter blue light and protect the macula |
| **Gut problems** | Probiotics, prebiotics, psyllium | Restore helpful flora, normalise bowel movement |

## 1.4 Public health nutrition

**Public health nutrition** applies nutrition science to **whole populations** rather than to single patients. Its aim is to **prevent** malnutrition and diet-related disease and to **promote** good health through policy, programmes and education.

India carries a **double burden of malnutrition**:

- **Undernutrition** — stunting and underweight in children, low birth weight, wasting.
- **Micronutrient deficiency ("hidden hunger")** — iron-deficiency anaemia, iodine deficiency disorders (goitre, cretinism), vitamin A deficiency (night blindness, xerophthalmia), zinc and vitamin D deficiency.
- **Overnutrition** — obesity, type 2 diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, especially in cities.

### Main public health strategies

1. **Food fortification** — iodised salt, double-fortified salt, fortified rice, oil and milk (FSSAI's "+F" logo marks fortified foods).
2. **Supplementation** — iron and folic acid, vitamin A, calcium for target groups through government programmes.
3. **Dietary diversification** — encouraging pulses, vegetables, fruits, millets, milk.
4. **Food distribution** — Public Distribution System, supplementary nutrition.
5. **Nutrition education** — changing habits through counselling and media.
6. **Hygiene and safe water** — repeated infections cause and worsen malnutrition.

### Important Indian programmes

| Programme | Main target |
| --- | --- |
| **Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS)** | Children under six, pregnant and nursing mothers — supplementary nutrition, growth monitoring, immunisation, preschool education through anganwadis |
| **PM POSHAN** (formerly the Mid-Day Meal scheme) | Hot cooked meals for school children |
| **POSHAN Abhiyaan** (National Nutrition Mission) | Reduce stunting, undernutrition, anaemia and low birth weight |
| **Anaemia Mukt Bharat** | Prevent and treat anaemia in children, adolescents and women |
| **National Iodine Deficiency Disorders Control Programme** | Universal use of iodised salt |
| **Vitamin A prophylaxis programme** | Prevent vitamin A deficiency in young children |

## 1.5 Maternal and child nutrition

### Pregnancy and lactation

During pregnancy the mother's needs rise for **energy, protein, iron, folic acid, calcium, iodine and DHA**.

- **Folic acid** before conception and in early pregnancy lowers the risk of **neural tube defects** (spina bifida).
- **Iron** need rises sharply because of the growing blood volume, placenta and baby; anaemia in pregnancy increases low birth weight and maternal death.
- **Iodine** is needed for the baby's brain and thyroid development.
- **Calcium and vitamin D** for the baby's bones.
- **Omega-3 DHA** for brain and retina development.
- In lactation, extra energy, protein and fluids support milk production.

Poor maternal nutrition gives **low birth weight** babies, who are more likely to become stunted, and later in life to develop diabetes and heart disease (the idea of foetal origins of adult disease).

### Infancy and childhood

- **Exclusive breastfeeding for the first six months**, then **complementary feeding** with continued breastfeeding up to two years or beyond. **Colostrum** is rich in antibodies and should be given.
- Breast milk gives protection against infection, the right balance of nutrients, and bonding.
- Common problems: **protein–energy malnutrition** (marasmus — severe wasting; kwashiorkor — oedema, skin and hair changes, enlarged fatty liver), anaemia, vitamin A deficiency, rickets, iodine deficiency.
- **Growth monitoring** (weight-for-age, height-for-age, weight-for-height) picks up malnutrition early.
- Infant foods are regulated separately and are **outside** the FSSAI nutraceutical rules (which do not apply to infants up to 24 months).

## 1.6 Nutrition and ageing

Ageing changes both the body's needs and its ability to eat and absorb food.

| Change with age | Nutritional effect |
| --- | --- |
| Lower muscle mass and activity | Less energy needed, but protein need stays high to slow **sarcopenia** |
| Poor teeth, dry mouth, reduced taste and smell | Lower food intake, monotonous diet |
| Less stomach acid (atrophic gastritis) | Poor absorption of **vitamin B12**, iron, calcium |
| Thinner skin, less time outdoors | Less **vitamin D** made in skin → bone loss |
| Reduced thirst | **Dehydration** |
| Many medicines | Drug–nutrient interactions, loss of appetite |
| Loneliness, depression, low income | Skipped meals |

Nutritional priorities: adequate protein, calcium and vitamin D (osteoporosis), vitamin B12, fibre and water (constipation), antioxidant-rich fruits and vegetables, omega-3 for heart and brain, lutein for eyes, and soft, easy-to-eat, energy-dense foods when appetite is poor. Nutraceuticals commonly used by the elderly include calcium–vitamin D, glucosamine, omega-3 and multivitamins.

## 1.7 Nutrition education in the community

Nutrition education is the planned use of communication to help people **choose and eat healthy diets** using foods they can afford.

**Steps**: assess the community's nutrition problems → set simple goals → choose the message and method → carry it out → evaluate the change.

**Methods**:

- **Individual** — counselling of mothers in clinics and anganwadis, home visits.
- **Group** — talks, demonstrations (cooking a low-cost nutritious recipe), group discussions.
- **Mass media** — radio, television, posters, social media, campaigns such as "Eat Right India" by FSSAI.
- **Through schools** — midday meals with nutrition lessons, school gardens.

**Key messages**: breastfeeding and correct complementary feeding; use of iodised salt; iron-rich foods with vitamin C; seasonal fruits and green leafy vegetables; less salt, sugar and fat; handwashing and safe water; regular growth monitoring.

The **pharmacist** helps by counselling on correct use of supplements, warning about unproven claims and supplement–drug interactions, and supporting national programmes.

## 1.8 Products used as nutraceuticals or functional foods

For each product learn: **source (with family)**, **marker compound and its chemical nature**, and **medicinal uses / health benefits**. A *marker compound* is a chemically defined constituent used to identify and standardise a product; it may or may not be the active one.

### Spirulina

- **Source**: dried biomass of the blue-green alga (cyanobacterium) *Arthrospira platensis* and *A. maxima* (formerly *Spirulina*), grown in warm, alkaline water.
- **Marker compounds**: **C-phycocyanin** — a blue **protein–pigment complex (phycobiliprotein)**; also **gamma-linolenic acid** (omega-6 fatty acid), beta-carotene (carotenoid), chlorophyll.
- **Nutritional value**: very high protein (more than half its dry weight, with all essential amino acids), iron, B vitamins.
- **Uses and benefits**: protein and micronutrient supplement in malnutrition; antioxidant and anti-inflammatory (phycocyanin); immune support; may help lower blood lipids; natural blue food colour.
- **Caution**: contamination with toxins from other algae (microcystins) and heavy metals; its vitamin B12 is mostly an inactive analogue, so it is not a reliable B12 source.

### Soyabean

- **Source**: seeds of *Glycine max*, family **Fabaceae**.
- **Marker compounds**: **isoflavones** — **genistein**, **daidzein** and glycitein (present as glycosides genistin and daidzin) — chemically **phenolic compounds (3-phenylchromones)** that act as **phytoestrogens**; also saponins, lecithin (phospholipid), phytosterols, high-quality protein.
- **Uses and benefits**: soy protein lowers LDL cholesterol; isoflavones may reduce menopausal hot flushes and bone loss; possible lower risk of hormone-related cancers; soy foods (tofu, soy milk) as protein for vegetarians; lecithin as emulsifier.
- **Caution**: allergy; isoflavone supplements are used with care in hormone-sensitive conditions.

### Ginseng

- **Source**: roots of *Panax ginseng* (Asian ginseng) and *Panax quinquefolius* (American ginseng), family **Araliaceae**.
- **Marker compounds**: **ginsenosides** (panaxosides) — **triterpenoid saponins**, mostly of the **dammarane** type (for example Rb1, Rg1).
- **Uses and benefits**: **adaptogen** (raises resistance to physical and mental stress); reduces fatigue; improves alertness and physical performance; mild blood-glucose lowering; immune modulation.
- **Caution**: insomnia, nervousness and raised blood pressure with long use; may change the effect of warfarin and antidiabetic drugs.

### Garlic

- **Source**: bulbs of *Allium sativum*, family **Liliaceae** (now placed in Amaryllidaceae).
- **Marker compounds**: **alliin** (S-allyl-L-cysteine sulphoxide), a **sulphur-containing amino acid**. When the clove is crushed the enzyme **alliinase** turns it into **allicin** (a thiosulphinate), which breaks down to **diallyl disulphide, diallyl trisulphide** and **ajoene**. Aged garlic extract contains **S-allyl cysteine**.
- **Uses and benefits**: modest lowering of cholesterol and blood pressure; reduces platelet aggregation; antimicrobial; antioxidant; possible lower risk of stomach and colon cancer.
- **Caution**: bleeding risk with anticoagulants; can lower blood levels of some anti-HIV drugs.

### Broccoli

- **Source**: flower heads of *Brassica oleracea* var. *italica*, family **Brassicaceae** (Cruciferae). Broccoli sprouts are a rich source.
- **Marker compounds**: the **glucosinolate glucoraphanin**, which the enzyme **myrosinase** (released on chewing or chopping) converts to **sulforaphane**, an **isothiocyanate**; also indole-3-carbinol (from glucobrassicin), vitamin C, folate, lutein, fibre.
- **Uses and benefits**: sulforaphane **induces phase II detoxifying enzymes** (through the Nrf2 pathway) and is studied for **cancer prevention**; antioxidant; good source of vitamins and fibre.
- **Note**: heavy cooking destroys myrosinase and lowers sulforaphane formation; light steaming keeps more of it.

### Ginkgo

- **Source**: leaves of *Ginkgo biloba*, family **Ginkgoaceae** (the "living fossil" tree). Standardised leaf extracts are used.
- **Marker compounds**: **flavonol glycosides** (of quercetin, kaempferol, isorhamnetin) and **terpene trilactones** — **ginkgolides A, B, C** (diterpenes) and **bilobalide** (a sesquiterpene). Extracts are standardised to these two groups and limited in ginkgolic acids, which are allergenic.
- **Uses and benefits**: memory and cognitive decline, poor peripheral circulation (intermittent claudication), tinnitus, vertigo; antioxidant; **ginkgolide B antagonises platelet-activating factor**.
- **Caution**: bleeding risk with anticoagulants and antiplatelet drugs.

### Flaxseed (linseed)

- **Source**: seeds of *Linum usitatissimum*, family **Linaceae**.
- **Marker compounds**: **alpha-linolenic acid** (ALA, the plant **omega-3 fatty acid**) in the oil; the **lignan secoisolariciresinol diglucoside (SDG)**, which gut bacteria convert to the **mammalian lignans enterodiol and enterolactone** (phytoestrogens); **mucilage** (soluble fibre).
- **Uses and benefits**: lowers cholesterol; supports heart health; laxative (mucilage, fibre); lignans studied for hormone-related cancers and menopausal symptoms; anti-inflammatory.
- **Note**: whole seeds pass undigested, so **ground** flaxseed is used; raw seeds contain small amounts of cyanogenic glycosides.

## Remember

- Nutraceutical = food or part of food with medical or health benefit (DeFelice, 1989); functional food = ordinary food with a benefit beyond nutrition (idea from Japan's FOSHU); dietary supplement = oral product that adds a dietary ingredient (US DSHEA 1994); India uses "health supplement".
- Classify nutraceuticals by source, chemical nature, mechanism, product type and traditional vs non-traditional.
- Weight control — fibre; diabetes — fibre, fenugreek, chromium; cancer — lycopene, sulforaphane, EGCG; heart — omega-3, plant sterols, oat beta-glucan; osteoarthritis — glucosamine, chondroitin; stress — adaptogens.
- India faces a double burden: undernutrition with hidden hunger, and obesity. Strategies: fortification, supplementation, diversification, education.
- Pregnancy: folic acid (neural tube defects), iron, iodine, calcium, DHA. Infants: exclusive breastfeeding for six months.
- Elderly: protein for sarcopenia, vitamin B12, calcium and vitamin D, fibre and water.
- Spirulina — phycocyanin; soya — genistein, daidzein; ginseng — ginsenosides; garlic — alliin → allicin; broccoli — glucoraphanin → sulforaphane; ginkgo — ginkgolides, bilobalide; flaxseed — ALA and SDG lignan.

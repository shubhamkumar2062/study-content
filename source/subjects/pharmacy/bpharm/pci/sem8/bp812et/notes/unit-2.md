# Unit 2: Phytochemicals as nutraceuticals and foods as functional foods

**Phytochemicals** are non-nutrient chemicals made by plants. They are not needed to prevent a deficiency disease, but many of them protect cells, lower the risk of chronic disease and are sold as nutraceuticals. This unit studies the main groups named in the syllabus — carotenoids, sulphides, polyphenolics (resveratrol), flavonoids, prebiotics and probiotics, phytoestrogens and tocopherols — and then proteins, vitamins, minerals, cereals, vegetables and beverages used as functional foods. For each group learn **where it occurs**, its **chemical nature** and its **medicinal benefits**.

## 2.1 Carotenoids

### Chemical nature

Carotenoids are **tetraterpenoids (C40)** — eight isoprene units joined into a long chain with many **conjugated double bonds**. The conjugated chain absorbs blue light, so carotenoids are **yellow, orange or red**. They are **fat-soluble**. Two groups:

| Group | Structure | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Carotenes** | Pure hydrocarbons (only C and H) | Alpha-carotene, beta-carotene, lycopene |
| **Xanthophylls** | Contain oxygen (hydroxyl, keto or epoxy groups) | Lutein, zeaxanthin, beta-cryptoxanthin, astaxanthin |

```text
Beta-carotene:  ring ─ long conjugated polyene chain ─ ring
                 (beta-ionone)      (C40)          (beta-ionone)
   cleavage in the middle → 2 molecules of retinal → vitamin A

Lycopene: open chain, no rings → cannot form vitamin A
```

### Alpha- and beta-carotene

- **Occurrence**: carrots, sweet potato, pumpkin, mango, papaya, green leafy vegetables (the green of chlorophyll hides the orange colour), red palm oil, spirulina.
- **Features**: beta-carotene has a **beta-ionone ring at both ends**; alpha-carotene has one beta- and one epsilon-ring. Both are **provitamin A** — beta-carotene gives the most vitamin A because it splits into two retinal molecules.
- **Benefits**: source of **vitamin A** (vision, epithelium, immunity) in vegetarian diets; antioxidant — quenches **singlet oxygen**; supports skin protection against light.
- **Caution**: large-dose beta-carotene supplements were linked with more lung cancer in **smokers** in clinical trials, so high-dose supplements are not advised for smokers. Food sources are safe.

### Lycopene

- **Occurrence**: **tomato** and tomato products (paste, ketchup), watermelon, pink guava, pink grapefruit, papaya.
- **Features**: **acyclic (open-chain)** carotene with 11 conjugated double bonds; red; **no provitamin A activity**; one of the **strongest singlet-oxygen quenchers** among carotenoids. In raw tomato it is mostly the all-trans form; heating with oil converts some to cis isomers and breaks the cell walls, so **cooked tomato products in oil give better absorption** than raw tomatoes.
- **Benefits**: antioxidant; studied for lower risk of **prostate cancer** and heart disease; skin protection.

### Xanthophylls — lutein and zeaxanthin

- **Occurrence**: lutein in **marigold flowers** (the commercial source), spinach, kale, other green leafy vegetables, egg yolk, maize; zeaxanthin in maize and orange peppers.
- **Features**: hydroxylated carotenoids; they are the only carotenoids concentrated in the **macula of the retina** (the "macular pigment").
- **Benefits**: filter harmful **blue light** and act as antioxidants in the eye; studied for lower risk of **age-related macular degeneration** and cataract.
- **Other xanthophylls**: beta-cryptoxanthin (oranges, papaya; provitamin A), astaxanthin (algae, salmon, shrimp — pink colour).

## 2.2 Sulphides (organosulphur compounds of Allium)

- **Occurrence**: **garlic**, onion, leek, chives (genus *Allium*).
- **Formation**: intact garlic contains **alliin**. Crushing brings it into contact with **alliinase**, giving **allicin** (diallyl thiosulphinate) within seconds. Allicin is unstable and changes into oil-soluble sulphides — **diallyl sulphide (DAS)**, **diallyl disulphide (DADS)**, **diallyl trisulphide (DATS, also called allitridin)** — and into **ajoenes** and vinyldithiins.

```text
Alliin ──alliinase (on crushing)──► Allicin ──(standing, heat, oil)──►
        Diallyl sulphide + Diallyl disulphide + Diallyl trisulphide + Ajoene
```

- **Chemical nature**: small organic molecules with two allyl groups (CH2=CH–CH2–) joined by one, two or three sulphur atoms; volatile, strong smelling, lipid-soluble.
- **Benefits**:
  - Lower total and LDL cholesterol modestly (inhibit cholesterol synthesis).
  - Reduce platelet aggregation (ajoene) — anti-thrombotic.
  - Mild lowering of blood pressure (hydrogen sulphide release relaxes blood vessels).
  - **Cancer prevention**: diallyl sulphides **modify carcinogen-metabolising enzymes** (block activation of carcinogens, induce phase II detoxification) and DATS induces apoptosis in cancer cells in experiments.
  - Antimicrobial (allicin), antioxidant.
- **Tip**: crush garlic and wait a few minutes before cooking so that allicin can form; heating whole cloves first destroys alliinase.

## 2.3 Polyphenolics — resveratrol

- **Occurrence**: skins of **red grapes**, red wine, peanuts, mulberries, blueberries; the commercial source is the root of Japanese knotweed (*Polygonum cuspidatum*).
- **Chemical nature**: a **stilbene** — **3,5,4′-trihydroxystilbene**, two phenol rings joined by an ethylene bridge. It occurs as **trans** (more active) and cis forms; plants make it as a **phytoalexin** in response to fungal attack and UV stress.
- **Benefits**:
  - Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory.
  - Cardioprotective — proposed as part of the explanation for the "**French paradox**" (low heart disease despite fatty diet, linked to red wine).
  - Activates **sirtuins (SIRT1)** and AMP-activated kinase in experiments, so it is studied for **anti-ageing** and metabolic effects.
  - Chemopreventive activity in laboratory studies.
- **Limitation**: very low oral **bioavailability** — it is rapidly conjugated in gut and liver.

## 2.4 Flavonoids

### Basic chemistry

Flavonoids are **polyphenols with a C6–C3–C6 skeleton** (two benzene rings A and B joined by a three-carbon unit that usually forms ring C, a pyran ring). Subclasses differ in ring C:

| Subclass | Ring C feature | Example in syllabus | Main source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Flavones** | 4-keto, 2,3 double bond | Apigenin, luteolin | Parsley, celery, chamomile |
| **Flavonols** | Flavone + 3-OH | **Quercetin**, kaempferol; **rutin** (quercetin glycoside) | Onion, apple, buckwheat, tea |
| **Flavanones** | 2,3 bond saturated | Naringenin; **naringin** (its glycoside), hesperidin | Grapefruit, citrus |
| **Flavan-3-ols** | No 4-keto, 3-OH | **Catechins**, EGCG | Green tea, cocoa, apples |
| **Anthocyanidins** | Flavylium cation | **Cyanidin**, delphinidin, pelargonidin | Berries, grapes, purple cabbage |
| **Isoflavones** | B ring at C-3 | Genistein, daidzein | Soya |

### Rutin

- **Occurrence**: buckwheat, citrus peel, apple, tea, *Sophora japonica* flower buds.
- **Nature**: **quercetin-3-O-rutinoside** (flavonol glycoside; rutinose = rhamnose + glucose). Also called vitamin P in old texts.
- **Benefits**: strengthens **capillaries** and reduces their fragility and leakiness (used in varicose veins, haemorrhoids, easy bruising); antioxidant; anti-inflammatory.

### Naringin

- **Occurrence**: **grapefruit** (gives its **bitter** taste), bitter orange, pomelo.
- **Nature**: **flavanone glycoside** — naringenin-7-O-neohesperidoside; gut bacteria release the aglycone **naringenin**.
- **Benefits**: antioxidant; lipid lowering in studies; used to make the sweetener neohesperidin dihydrochalcone.
- **Note**: grapefruit's **furanocoumarins** (with some contribution from naringin) **inhibit intestinal CYP3A4**, raising blood levels of drugs such as some statins and calcium-channel blockers — the classic food–drug interaction.

### Quercetin

- **Occurrence**: **onion** (red onion is rich), apples, capers, berries, tea, broccoli.
- **Nature**: **flavonol aglycone** (3,5,7,3′,4′-pentahydroxyflavone); occurs in foods mostly as glycosides.
- **Benefits**: strong antioxidant and **metal chelator**; anti-inflammatory; stabilises mast cells (anti-allergic); studied for blood pressure lowering and heart protection.

### Anthocyanidins (and anthocyanins)

- **Occurrence**: **blueberries, blackberries, black grapes, jamun, purple cabbage, red onion, black rice**, cherries.
- **Nature**: anthocyanidins are the **aglycones** (cyanidin, delphinidin, pelargonidin, malvidin, peonidin, petunidin), based on the **flavylium cation**; with sugars attached they are **anthocyanins**, the water-soluble red–blue–purple pigments. Their colour changes with **pH** (red in acid, blue/purple near neutral) and they are unstable to heat and light.
- **Benefits**: antioxidant; improve blood vessel function; studied for eye health (night vision, bilberry), memory and anti-diabetic effects; natural food colours.

### Catechins

- **Occurrence**: **green tea** (richest), black tea (partly converted to theaflavins and thearubigins during fermentation), cocoa and dark chocolate, apples, grapes.
- **Nature**: **flavan-3-ols** — catechin, epicatechin, epicatechin gallate, epigallocatechin, and **epigallocatechin-3-gallate (EGCG)**, the main green tea catechin. Polymers form **proanthocyanidins** (condensed tannins).
- **Benefits**: antioxidant; lower LDL cholesterol; slight increase in fat burning (used in weight-management products); studied for cancer prevention; antimicrobial (oral health).
- **Caution**: concentrated green tea extracts in large amounts have been linked to liver injury.

### Flavones

- **Occurrence**: **apigenin** in parsley, celery and chamomile; **luteolin** in celery, peppers, thyme.
- **Benefits**: antioxidant, anti-inflammatory; apigenin has a mild calming effect (chamomile tea).

## 2.5 Prebiotics and probiotics

| Term | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Probiotic** | Live microorganisms which, when taken in adequate amounts, give a health benefit to the host | *Lactobacillus acidophilus*, *L. rhamnosus* GG, *L. casei*, *Bifidobacterium* species, *Saccharomyces boulardii* (yeast) |
| **Prebiotic** | Non-digestible food ingredient that is **selectively used by helpful gut bacteria** and gives a health benefit | **Fructo-oligosaccharides (FOS)**, **inulin**, galacto-oligosaccharides, lactulose, resistant starch |
| **Synbiotic** | Probiotic + prebiotic in one product | Curd with inulin |

### Fructo-oligosaccharides (FOS) and inulin

- **Occurrence**: chicory root (main commercial source of inulin), onion, garlic, banana, wheat, asparagus, Jerusalem artichoke; FOS are also made from sucrose by enzymes.
- **Chemical nature**: **fructans** — chains of fructose units joined by **β(2→1) links**, usually with a glucose at one end. FOS are short chains (about 2–10 units); inulin has longer chains. Human enzymes cannot split β(2→1) links, so they reach the colon intact.
- **Benefits**:
  - **Bifidogenic** — selectively feed bifidobacteria and lactobacilli.
  - Fermentation gives **short-chain fatty acids** (acetate, propionate, butyrate) → lower colon pH, energy for colon cells, fewer harmful bacteria.
  - Better **calcium and magnesium absorption**; relief of constipation; low-calorie sugar and fat replacer in foods.
- **Caution**: gas and bloating in large amounts.

### Lactobacillus (probiotic)

- **Nature**: Gram-positive, rod-shaped, non-spore-forming, **lactic acid bacteria**; ferment sugars to lactic acid. Found in curd, yoghurt, buttermilk, idli and dosa batter, pickles, and in the human gut and vagina.
- **Requirements for a good probiotic**: human origin or safe history, survive stomach acid and bile, stick to the gut lining, stay alive in the product until its expiry, proven benefit for the **specific strain**.
- **Mechanisms**: compete with pathogens for sites and nutrients; make lactic acid and **bacteriocins**; strengthen the gut barrier; modulate immunity.
- **Benefits**: prevention and treatment of **antibiotic-associated diarrhoea** and infective diarrhoea in children; **lactose intolerance** (bacteria supply lactase); irritable bowel syndrome; vaginal health; possibly allergy and immunity.
- Indian rules require probiotic foods to use listed strains and to contain a minimum number of live organisms per daily serving (stated on the label in colony-forming units).

## 2.6 Phytoestrogens

**Phytoestrogens** are plant compounds whose structure looks like **17β-oestradiol** (a phenol ring at a similar distance from another hydroxyl), so they bind **oestrogen receptors**, mostly **ERβ**. They act as **weak oestrogens** — and, when the body's own oestrogen is high, partly as anti-oestrogens (they behave like natural selective oestrogen receptor modulators).

| Class | Members | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Isoflavones** | **Genistein**, **daidzein**, glycitein, biochanin A, formononetin | **Soyabean** and soy foods, red clover, chickpea |
| **Lignans** | Secoisolariciresinol, matairesinol → **enterodiol, enterolactone** (mammalian lignans made by gut bacteria) | **Flaxseed** (richest), sesame, whole grains, berries |
| **Coumestans** | Coumestrol | Alfalfa and clover sprouts |

- **Daidzein** is converted by some people's gut bacteria to **equol**, which is more oestrogenic — so response to soy differs between people.
- **Benefits**: reduce **menopausal hot flushes**; slow **bone loss** after menopause; lower LDL cholesterol (soy); studied for lower risk of breast and prostate cancer (populations eating soy daily have lower rates).
- **Cautions**: concentrated supplements in women with oestrogen-sensitive cancers are used only with medical advice; may affect thyroid function when iodine intake is low.

(The syllabus prints "Geebustin" — read this as **genistein**.)

## 2.7 Tocopherols (vitamin E)

- **Chemical nature**: **vitamin E** is a family of eight compounds — four **tocopherols** (α, β, γ, δ) and four **tocotrienols**. Each has a **chromanol ring** (with the active phenolic –OH) and a 16-carbon side chain — **saturated (phytyl) in tocopherols** and **with three double bonds in tocotrienols**. The forms differ in the number and position of methyl groups on the ring.
- **α-Tocopherol** has the highest vitamin activity and is kept in the body by the liver's α-tocopherol transfer protein; natural **RRR-α-tocopherol** (d-α-tocopherol) is more active than synthetic all-rac (dl) forms. Esters (acetate, succinate) are more stable for products.
- **Occurrence**: **vegetable oils** (wheat germ oil is the richest; sunflower, safflower, rice bran, palm oil — palm and rice bran are rich in tocotrienols), nuts (almonds), seeds, green leafy vegetables.
- **Action**: the main **lipid-soluble, chain-breaking antioxidant** in membranes and lipoproteins. It gives a hydrogen atom to a lipid peroxyl radical, stopping the chain of lipid peroxidation; the resulting **tocopheroxyl radical** is stable and is recycled back by **vitamin C** (and then glutathione).
- **Benefits**: protects cell membranes and LDL from oxidation; immune function; skin; used as a **food and pharmaceutical antioxidant** (preservative for oils).
- **Deficiency** is rare (fat malabsorption, premature infants): haemolytic anaemia, neuropathy.
- **Caution**: high-dose supplements can increase bleeding tendency with anticoagulants.

## 2.8 Proteins, vitamins, minerals, cereals, vegetables and beverages as functional foods

### Proteins and peptides

- **Whey protein** — rich in branched-chain amino acids (leucine); muscle building, satiety; lactoferrin and immunoglobulins in it support immunity.
- **Casein- and whey-derived bioactive peptides** — some fermented-milk peptides inhibit ACE and lower blood pressure.
- **Soy protein** — lowers LDL cholesterol; complete plant protein.
- **Collagen peptides** — skin and joint support.

### Vitamins and minerals as functional ingredients

- **Fortified foods**: iodised salt (iodine), double-fortified salt (iron + iodine), fortified rice and wheat flour (iron, folic acid, vitamin B12), fortified milk and edible oil (vitamins A and D).
- **Antioxidant vitamins** C and E, and minerals selenium and zinc (cofactors of antioxidant enzymes).
- **Calcium + vitamin D** in fortified milk and juices for bone health.

### Cereals

| Cereal / product | Functional component | Benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Oats** | **Beta-glucan** (soluble fibre, β(1→3),(1→4)-glucan); avenanthramides (antioxidant) | Lowers LDL cholesterol; slows glucose rise; satiety |
| **Wheat bran** | **Insoluble fibre** (cellulose, hemicellulose, lignin), ferulic acid | Increases stool bulk, relieves constipation, lower colon cancer risk |
| **Rice bran** | **Gamma-oryzanol**, **tocotrienols**, fibre; rice bran oil | Cholesterol lowering, antioxidant |
| **Barley** | Beta-glucan | Cholesterol and glucose lowering |
| **Millets** (ragi, jowar, bajra) | Fibre, calcium (ragi), iron, polyphenols | Low glycaemic response, mineral supply |
| **Whole grains** in general | Fibre, lignans, vitamins, minerals | Lower risk of heart disease, diabetes, colon cancer |

### Vegetables and fruits

- **Tomato** — lycopene. **Carrot** — beta-carotene. **Broccoli, cabbage** — sulforaphane, indoles. **Spinach** — lutein, folate. **Onion, garlic** — quercetin, sulphides, FOS. **Beetroot** — nitrate (blood pressure), betalains. **Citrus** — vitamin C, hesperidin, naringin. **Amla** — vitamin C and tannins. **Berries, jamun** — anthocyanins.

### Sea foods

- **Fatty fish** (salmon, sardine, mackerel, Indian oil sardine) and fish oil — **omega-3 fatty acids EPA and DHA**: lower triglycerides, anti-inflammatory, antiplatelet, brain and eye development.
- Shellfish — glucosamine and chitosan (from shells); **seaweeds** — iodine, fucoidan, alginates (fibre); fish protein, taurine, selenium, zinc.
- Krill oil — omega-3 with astaxanthin.

### Beverages

| Beverage | Key components | Benefits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Green tea** | Catechins (EGCG), caffeine, **L-theanine** | Antioxidant, cholesterol lowering, alertness with calm (theanine) |
| **Black tea** | Theaflavins, thearubigins, caffeine | Antioxidant, vascular function |
| **Coffee** | **Caffeine**, **chlorogenic acid**, trigonelline | Alertness, antioxidant; regular coffee drinking is associated with lower risk of type 2 diabetes |
| **Cocoa** | Flavanols (epicatechin), theobromine | Improved blood vessel function, lower blood pressure |
| **Red wine / grape juice** | Resveratrol, anthocyanins, procyanidins | Antioxidant (grape juice preferred — alcohol harms) |
| **Fermented milk drinks** | Probiotics, peptides | Gut health, mild blood pressure lowering |

## Remember

- Carotenoids = C40 tetraterpenoids; carotenes (alpha, beta, lycopene) have no oxygen, xanthophylls (lutein, zeaxanthin) do. Beta-carotene → 2 retinal (provitamin A); lycopene is acyclic, no vitamin A, best singlet-oxygen quencher, better absorbed from cooked tomato in oil; lutein and zeaxanthin protect the macula.
- Garlic: alliin → (alliinase) allicin → diallyl sulphide, disulphide, trisulphide (allitridin), ajoene — cholesterol, platelets, cancer prevention.
- Resveratrol = 3,5,4′-trihydroxystilbene, red grape skin, phytoalexin, SIRT1, French paradox, poor bioavailability.
- Flavonoids C6–C3–C6: rutin (quercetin rutinoside, capillaries), naringin (grapefruit flavanone glycoside, bitter), quercetin (onion flavonol), anthocyanidins (berries, flavylium, pH colour), catechins (green tea EGCG), flavones (apigenin, luteolin).
- FOS and inulin = β(2→1) fructans, bifidogenic, short-chain fatty acids; *Lactobacillus* = lactic acid bacteria, strain-specific benefit.
- Phytoestrogens: isoflavones (genistein, daidzein → equol), lignans (flax SDG → enterolactone), coumestans; weak ERβ agonists.
- Vitamin E: 4 tocopherols + 4 tocotrienols, chromanol ring, α-tocopherol most active, chain-breaking antioxidant recycled by vitamin C.
- Functional foods: oats beta-glucan, wheat bran insoluble fibre, rice bran oryzanol, fish omega-3, tea catechins and theanine, coffee chlorogenic acid.

# Unit 4: Free radicals in disease, antioxidants and functional foods for chronic disease

Unit 3 showed how free radicals form and what they damage. This unit applies that knowledge to **diseases** — diabetes, inflammation, ischaemia–reperfusion injury, cancer, atherosclerosis, brain disorders, kidney and muscle damage — and to **ageing**. It then describes the body's **antioxidant defences** (enzymes and small molecules), two **synthetic antioxidants** used in foods (BHT, BHA), and finally the **functional foods** used to prevent chronic disease.

A good long answer on any disease in this unit follows the same plan:

```text
Where do extra ROS come from?  →  What do they damage?  →  What is the result?
→  Evidence (markers)  →  Antioxidant / dietary approach
```

## 4.1 Free radicals in diabetes mellitus

**Sources of extra ROS in high blood glucose (hyperglycaemia):**

1. **Glucose auto-oxidation** — glucose and its products oxidise in the presence of metals, forming superoxide, H2O2 and reactive dicarbonyls.
2. **Advanced glycation end products (AGEs)** — formed by non-enzymatic glycation of proteins plus oxidation; AGEs binding to their receptor **RAGE** switch on NADPH oxidase and inflammatory signalling (NF-κB).
3. **Polyol (sorbitol) pathway** — aldose reductase turns excess glucose into sorbitol using **NADPH**; less NADPH means less regeneration of reduced **glutathione**, so antioxidant defence falls.
4. **Protein kinase C activation** (through diacylglycerol) increases NADPH oxidase activity.
5. **Hexosamine pathway** — altered gene expression and inflammation.
6. **Mitochondrial overproduction of superoxide** — thought to link all four pathways above.

**Effects:**

- **β-cells** of the pancreas have **low levels of antioxidant enzymes** (catalase, glutathione peroxidase), so they are easily damaged by ROS → reduced insulin secretion. Experimental diabetes is produced in animals with **alloxan** and **streptozotocin**, which destroy β-cells partly through ROS.
- ROS interfere with insulin signalling → **insulin resistance**.
- **Complications**: endothelial damage → **micro-vascular** (retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy) and **macro-vascular** disease (coronary, stroke); cataract (oxidised lens proteins); poor wound healing.

**Evidence**: higher MDA, AGEs and protein carbonyls; lower glutathione and vitamin C in diabetic patients.

**Diet**: good glucose control is the main step. **Alpha-lipoic acid** is used in some countries for diabetic neuropathy; fibre-rich diets, polyphenol-rich foods and omega-3 support overall control.

## 4.2 Free radicals in inflammation

- At a site of infection or injury, **neutrophils and macrophages** gather and switch on **NADPH oxidase** (the **respiratory burst**), producing superoxide; **myeloperoxidase** converts H2O2 and chloride into **hypochlorous acid (HOCl)**; inducible nitric oxide synthase makes NO•, which forms **peroxynitrite** with superoxide.
- These oxidants **kill microbes** — useful — but in **chronic inflammation** they spill out and damage the host's own tissue.
- ROS activate the transcription factor **NF-κB**, increasing inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6), COX-2 and adhesion molecules — so inflammation and oxidative stress **feed each other** (a vicious circle).
- Examples: **rheumatoid arthritis** (•OH breaks down hyaluronic acid in synovial fluid, cartilage damage), **inflammatory bowel disease**, asthma, chronic hepatitis, periodontitis.
- **Dietary help**: omega-3 fatty acids (anti-inflammatory mediators), curcumin, flavonoids (quercetin), vitamin C and E.

## 4.3 Ischaemia–reperfusion injury

**Ischaemia** is the loss of blood supply to a tissue; **reperfusion** is the return of blood (for example after clot-busting treatment or angioplasty in a heart attack, organ transplantation, or after surgery). Paradoxically, **much of the damage happens when oxygen returns**.

```text
ISCHAEMIA (no oxygen)
 ATP → ADP → AMP → adenosine → inosine → HYPOXANTHINE accumulates
 Ca²⁺ rises in cell → protease converts xanthine DEHYDROGENASE
                       into xanthine OXIDASE
                    │
REPERFUSION (oxygen returns suddenly)
 Hypoxanthine + O2 ──xanthine oxidase──► xanthine + O2•⁻ + H2O2
 Xanthine + O2   ──xanthine oxidase──► uric acid + O2•⁻ + H2O2
                    │
 O2•⁻ + H2O2 + Fe²⁺ (Fenton) → •OH
 + damaged mitochondria leak more superoxide
 + neutrophils arrive → respiratory burst
                    │
 Lipid peroxidation, protein and DNA damage, Ca²⁺ overload,
 opening of mitochondrial pores → cell death (myocardial stunning,
 arrhythmias, graft failure)
```

- Seen in **heart** (myocardial infarction treatment), **brain** (stroke), **kidney** and **liver** transplants, intestine.
- Protective approaches studied: **allopurinol** (xanthine oxidase inhibitor), antioxidants, iron chelators, and organ-preservation solutions containing glutathione and allopurinol; **ischaemic preconditioning** (short ischaemic episodes before a long one).

## 4.4 Free radicals in cancer

Free radicals take part in all three stages of carcinogenesis:

| Stage | Role of ROS |
| --- | --- |
| **Initiation** | Oxidative **DNA damage** (8-oxo-dG, strand breaks) → **mutations** in oncogenes (*ras*) and tumour-suppressor genes (*p53*) |
| **Promotion** | ROS act as signals that increase cell growth (activation of NF-κB, AP-1, MAP kinases); tumour promoters such as phorbol esters raise ROS |
| **Progression** | Genetic instability, angiogenesis, invasion and metastasis |

- **Chronic inflammation** drives cancer through ROS: *Helicobacter pylori* gastritis → stomach cancer; hepatitis B and C → liver cancer; ulcerative colitis → colon cancer.
- Carcinogens such as tobacco smoke, asbestos and ionising radiation act partly through radicals.
- **Two sides**: cancer cells often have high ROS, and many **anticancer drugs and radiotherapy** kill cells by generating ROS (doxorubicin, bleomycin). So high-dose antioxidant supplements during chemotherapy or radiotherapy are not routinely advised.
- **Dietary prevention**: fruits and vegetables rich in antioxidants and phytochemicals (lycopene, sulforaphane, EGCG, curcumin, resveratrol), fibre, and avoiding tobacco and alcohol. Large trials of single antioxidant supplements have not shown clear cancer prevention, and beta-carotene raised lung cancer risk in smokers — whole foods are preferred.

## 4.5 Free radicals in atherosclerosis

The **oxidative modification hypothesis**:

```text
LDL enters the artery wall (intima)
        │  ROS from endothelium, macrophages, smooth muscle
        ▼
Oxidised LDL (ox-LDL): lipid peroxidation + modified apoB-100
        │
 • Taken up by macrophage SCAVENGER receptors (not down-regulated)
   → cholesterol-loaded FOAM CELLS → FATTY STREAK
 • Damages endothelium, reduces nitric oxide (endothelial dysfunction)
 • Attracts more monocytes (chemotactic), causes inflammation
 • Stimulates smooth-muscle growth → fibrous PLAQUE
        │
 Plaque rupture → thrombosis → heart attack, stroke
```

- **Risk factors** that increase ROS: smoking, diabetes, hypertension (angiotensin II activates NADPH oxidase), high LDL, obesity.
- Superoxide destroys **nitric oxide**, the vessel-relaxing and anti-platelet molecule.
- **Protection by diet**: polyphenols (tea, cocoa, red grapes), vitamin E within LDL, omega-3 fatty acids, soluble fibre and plant sterols (lower LDL), garlic, a Mediterranean-type diet.

## 4.6 Free radicals in brain metabolism and pathology

**Why the brain is very vulnerable:**

1. Uses a **large share of the body's oxygen** for its small weight → much mitochondrial ROS.
2. Rich in **PUFAs** (DHA, arachidonic acid) — easily peroxidised.
3. Contains **iron** in several regions (iron can drive the Fenton reaction).
4. Relatively **low catalase** and modest glutathione peroxidase activity.
5. **Auto-oxidation of dopamine** and its metabolism by **MAO** produce H2O2 and quinones.
6. **Excitotoxicity** — excess glutamate → Ca²⁺ influx → nitric oxide synthase and mitochondrial ROS.
7. Neurons hardly divide, so damage builds up.

**Diseases:**

| Disease | Role of ROS |
| --- | --- |
| **Parkinson's disease** | Dopamine oxidation and MAO-B → H2O2; iron in substantia nigra; complex I defect; lower glutathione; toxins (MPTP → MPP⁺) act through mitochondrial ROS |
| **Alzheimer's disease** | Beta-amyloid with copper and iron produces H2O2 and lipid peroxidation; protein oxidation; mitochondrial dysfunction |
| **Stroke** | Ischaemia–reperfusion and excitotoxicity |
| **Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis** | Some familial cases are caused by mutations in **Cu/Zn-SOD (SOD1)** |
| Others | Huntington's disease, epilepsy, brain trauma |

## 4.7 Free radicals in kidney and muscle damage, and other disorders

**Kidney damage**:

- **Drug nephrotoxicity** — cisplatin, aminoglycosides (gentamicin), ciclosporin and radiocontrast agents act partly through ROS.
- **Ischaemia–reperfusion** in renal transplants and shock.
- **Diabetic nephropathy** and glomerulonephritis (neutrophils, AGEs).
- **Rhabdomyolysis** — myoglobin released from damaged muscle; its haem iron catalyses lipid peroxidation in kidney tubules → acute kidney injury.

**Muscle damage**:

- **Strenuous or unaccustomed exercise** raises oxygen use and ROS → muscle soreness, fatigue and damage (markers such as creatine kinase rise). Moderate regular exercise, in contrast, **increases** the body's antioxidant enzymes (adaptation).
- **Muscular dystrophies**, **ischaemia–reperfusion** of limbs, and ageing (sarcopenia).
- **Vitamin E and selenium deficiency** cause muscle disease in animals (white muscle disease).

**Other disorders involving free radicals**:

- **Eye**: cataract (lens protein oxidation), **age-related macular degeneration**, retinopathy of prematurity (high oxygen in premature babies).
- **Lungs**: smoking-related **COPD**, asthma, **acute respiratory distress syndrome**, oxygen toxicity, paraquat poisoning.
- **Liver**: alcoholic liver disease (CYP2E1), paracetamol overdose, carbon tetrachloride, iron overload.
- **Skin**: UV damage, photo-ageing, skin cancer.
- **Blood**: haemolysis in **G6PD deficiency** (low NADPH → low glutathione), thalassaemia with iron overload.
- **Infection** — HIV, malaria (red cell oxidative damage).

## 4.8 Free radical theory of ageing

Proposed by **Denham Harman in 1956**, the theory states that **ageing results from the gradual, lifelong build-up of damage to cells and tissues caused by free radicals** made during normal metabolism.

**Supporting points:**

- Oxidative damage to DNA, proteins (carbonyls) and lipids **increases with age**; the age pigment **lipofuscin** (oxidised lipid–protein material) builds up in neurons and heart muscle.
- Animal species with **higher metabolic rates** and more ROS production tend to live shorter lives (link with the older "rate of living" theory).
- **Caloric restriction**, which lowers ROS production, extends lifespan in many laboratory animals.
- **Mitochondrial theory** (an extension): mitochondrial DNA is damaged by ROS → faulty respiratory chain → still more ROS — a vicious circle.

**Limitations:**

- Antioxidant supplements have **not extended lifespan** in human trials.
- Some long-lived animals (for example naked mole-rats) have high oxidative damage.
- ROS are also useful signals; ageing is now seen as having many causes (telomere shortening, protein misfolding, inflammation, genetic programmes), with oxidative damage one contributor.

## 4.9 Antioxidants

An **antioxidant** is **any substance that, at low concentration compared with an oxidisable substrate, significantly delays or prevents the oxidation of that substrate** (a definition given by Halliwell).

### Ways antioxidants act

| Type | Action | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Preventive** | Stop radicals forming — remove peroxides, bind metals | Catalase, glutathione peroxidase, transferrin, ferritin, ceruloplasmin, albumin |
| **Radical scavenging / chain-breaking** | React with radicals and stop the chain | Vitamin E, vitamin C, carotenoids, uric acid, flavonoids, BHT, BHA |
| **Repair** | Remove and replace damaged molecules | DNA repair enzymes, proteasomes, methionine sulphoxide reductase |
| **Adaptation** | Increase antioxidant enzymes when stressed | Nrf2 pathway (switched on by sulforaphane) |

### A. Endogenous enzymatic antioxidants

```text
             O2•⁻
              │  SOD
              ▼
             H2O2 ──── catalase ────► H2O + O2
              │
              └── glutathione peroxidase (Se) ──► H2O
                     2 GSH  →  GSSG
                                │ glutathione reductase
                                │ (NADPH from pentose phosphate pathway)
                                ▼
                              2 GSH
```

**Superoxide dismutase (SOD)**

- Reaction: **2 O2•⁻ + 2 H⁺ → H2O2 + O2** (dismutation — one superoxide is oxidised, the other reduced).
- Forms: **Cu/Zn-SOD (SOD1)** in the cytosol; **Mn-SOD (SOD2)** in mitochondria (essential for life in animals); **extracellular SOD (SOD3, Cu/Zn)** in tissue fluids and vessel walls.
- The **first line of defence** against superoxide; it must work together with catalase or peroxidases, because its product H2O2 is still harmful.

**Catalase**

- Reaction: **2 H2O2 → 2 H2O + O2**.
- A **haem (iron-porphyrin)** enzyme found mainly in **peroxisomes**; very high in liver and red blood cells, low in the brain and pancreatic β-cells.
- Handles **high concentrations** of H2O2; one of the fastest enzymes known.

**Glutathione peroxidase (GPx)**

- Reaction: **H2O2 + 2 GSH → GSSG + 2 H2O**, and **ROOH + 2 GSH → ROH + GSSG + H2O** — it also reduces **lipid hydroperoxides**.
- A **selenium-containing enzyme** (selenocysteine at its active site) — this is why **selenium** is an antioxidant nutrient.
- Removes **low levels** of H2O2 all the time, in cytosol and mitochondria.
- Works with **glutathione reductase** (a FAD enzyme that uses NADPH) to regenerate GSH. Other helpers: glutathione S-transferases, peroxiredoxins and thioredoxin.

### B. Endogenous non-enzymatic antioxidants

**Glutathione (GSH)**

- **Tripeptide γ-glutamyl-cysteinyl-glycine**; its active part is the **thiol (–SH) of cysteine**; the γ-link makes it resistant to ordinary peptidases. Made in all cells, with high levels in the liver.
- Functions: substrate of **GPx**; directly scavenges •OH and singlet oxygen; regenerates **vitamins C and E**; keeps protein thiols reduced; conjugates drugs and toxins (**glutathione S-transferases**) — e.g. it detoxifies the reactive metabolite of paracetamol (**N-acetylcysteine** is used in paracetamol poisoning to restore GSH).
- The **GSH : GSSG ratio** is a key index of the cell's redox state.

**Alpha-lipoic acid**

- An **8-carbon fatty acid with a dithiolane ring** (two sulphur atoms); made in mitochondria and found in meat (organ meats), spinach, broccoli.
- Normally a **cofactor** of **pyruvate dehydrogenase** and **α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase**.
- Its reduced form, **dihydrolipoic acid (DHLA)**, and the oxidised form together make a strong **redox couple**: they scavenge several ROS, **chelate metals**, and **regenerate** other antioxidants — **vitamin C, vitamin E (via vitamin C), glutathione** and coenzyme Q10 — so lipoic acid is called the "**antioxidant of antioxidants**" or a network antioxidant.
- Soluble in **both water and fat**, so it acts in cytosol and membranes.
- Studied and used for **diabetic neuropathy**, insulin sensitivity.

**Melatonin**

- **N-acetyl-5-methoxytryptamine**, an **indoleamine** made from **tryptophan** (via serotonin) mainly by the **pineal gland** at night; also in the gut and in some foods.
- **Amphiphilic** — crosses membranes and the **blood–brain barrier** easily.
- Directly scavenges **•OH**, peroxynitrite and other radicals; its breakdown products are themselves antioxidants (a cascade); **increases** the activity of SOD, catalase and GPx; does not cause redox cycling (does not become a pro-oxidant).
- Better known for sleep and circadian rhythm; production falls with age.

**Other endogenous small molecules**: **uric acid** (a major plasma antioxidant), **bilirubin**, **coenzyme Q10** (ubiquinol in membranes), **albumin** (thiol groups), metal-binding proteins **transferrin, ferritin, ceruloplasmin, lactoferrin, metallothionein**.

### C. Dietary (exogenous) antioxidants

Vitamin C (water-phase scavenger, recycles vitamin E), vitamin E (lipid-phase chain-breaker), carotenoids (singlet oxygen quenchers), flavonoids and other polyphenols, selenium and zinc (enzyme cofactors), copper and manganese (SOD cofactors).

### D. Synthetic antioxidants

**Butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT)** and **butylated hydroxyanisole (BHA)** are **hindered phenols** added to fats, oils, fat-containing foods, cosmetics, and pharmaceutical products (vitamin A and E preparations, oily injections, ointments) to **prevent rancidity**.

| Feature | **BHT** | **BHA** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chemical name | 2,6-di-tert-butyl-4-methylphenol | Mixture of 2- and 3-tert-butyl-4-hydroxyanisole (methoxyphenol) |
| Structure | Phenol with **two** bulky tert-butyl groups beside the –OH | Phenol with **one** tert-butyl group and a methoxy group |
| Solubility | Fat-soluble, insoluble in water | Fat-soluble, insoluble in water |
| Heat stability ("carry-through" in baking and frying) | Moderate; somewhat volatile | Good in animal fats and baked goods |

**Mechanism** — chain-breaking: the phenolic –OH gives a **hydrogen atom** to a lipid peroxyl radical (LOO•), forming LOOH and a **phenoxyl radical**. The bulky tert-butyl groups and the ring **stabilise** this radical (resonance and steric hindrance) so it cannot continue the chain:

```text
LOO•  +  ArOH (BHT/BHA)  →  LOOH  +  ArO•  (stable, unreactive)
ArO•  +  LOO•            →  non-radical products
```

- They work **synergistically** with each other and with **citric acid** (metal chelator) and ascorbyl palmitate.
- Other synthetic food antioxidants: **propyl gallate**, **tert-butylhydroquinone (TBHQ)**; ascorbyl palmitate (semi-synthetic).
- **Safety**: permitted only within limits set by food regulations; their safety has been debated, so there is a trend to replace them with natural antioxidants (tocopherols, rosemary extract).

### Antioxidant network and the pro-oxidant problem

```text
 LOO• ──► vitamin E ──► tocopheroxyl radical
                         │ recycled by
                         ▼
                    vitamin C ──► ascorbyl radical
                         │ recycled by
                         ▼
                    glutathione / lipoic acid ──► GSSG
                         │ recycled by
                         ▼
                 glutathione reductase + NADPH
```

Antioxidants protect best as a **team**. A single antioxidant at high dose, in the presence of free iron or copper, can act as a **pro-oxidant** (for example, vitamin C reduces Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺, feeding the Fenton reaction). This is one reason why mixed antioxidants from foods are preferred to mega-dose single supplements.

## 4.10 Functional foods for chronic disease prevention

| Chronic disease | Functional foods and their active components |
| --- | --- |
| **Coronary heart disease** | Oats and barley (beta-glucan), plant sterol/stanol-enriched foods, soy protein, nuts (almonds, walnuts), fatty fish (EPA, DHA), flaxseed (ALA, lignans), green and black tea, cocoa (flavanols), garlic, olive oil (oleic acid, polyphenols), red grapes |
| **Hypertension** | Fruits and vegetables rich in potassium, low-fat dairy and fermented milk peptides, beetroot (nitrate), cocoa, garlic, low-salt foods (DASH-type diet) |
| **Type 2 diabetes** | Whole grains, millets, legumes, oats, psyllium, fenugreek, bitter gourd, cinnamon, nuts, coffee and tea (associated with lower risk) |
| **Cancer** | Tomato (lycopene), cruciferous vegetables (sulforaphane, indoles), garlic and onion (organosulphur compounds), green tea (EGCG), turmeric (curcumin), soy (isoflavones), flaxseed (lignans), berries (anthocyanins, ellagic acid), whole-grain fibre, probiotic curd |
| **Obesity** | High-fibre foods, high-protein foods, green tea, low-energy-density foods |
| **Osteoporosis** | Calcium- and vitamin D-fortified milk and juices, soy isoflavones, green leafy vegetables (vitamin K), ragi |
| **Gut health and colon cancer** | Probiotic curd and drinks, prebiotic fibres (inulin), wheat bran |
| **Eye disease (cataract, AMD)** | Green leafy vegetables, egg yolk, maize (lutein, zeaxanthin), fish (DHA) |
| **Cognitive decline** | Fish (DHA), berries (anthocyanins), green tea, nuts |
| **Urinary tract infections** | Cranberry (proanthocyanidins that stop bacteria sticking) |

The overall message: a **varied diet** rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, pulses, nuts and fish, with probiotic foods, gives the widest range of protective compounds. Functional foods support, but do not replace, medical treatment and a healthy lifestyle.

## Remember

- Diabetes: glucose auto-oxidation, AGEs–RAGE, polyol pathway (uses NADPH), PKC; β-cells are low in catalase and GPx (alloxan, streptozotocin).
- Inflammation: respiratory burst (NADPH oxidase), HOCl (myeloperoxidase), peroxynitrite; NF-κB vicious circle.
- Ischaemia–reperfusion: hypoxanthine builds up and xanthine dehydrogenase becomes xanthine oxidase; on reperfusion O2 gives superoxide; allopurinol protects.
- Cancer: ROS cause DNA mutations (initiation), act as growth signals (promotion); many anticancer drugs work through ROS.
- Atherosclerosis: oxidised LDL → scavenger receptors → foam cells → fatty streak → plaque.
- Brain is vulnerable: high oxygen use, PUFAs, iron, low catalase, dopamine oxidation; Parkinson's, Alzheimer's, ALS (SOD1).
- Kidney: cisplatin, gentamicin, myoglobin; muscle: strenuous exercise, dystrophies.
- Free radical theory of ageing: Harman, 1956; lipofuscin, mitochondrial theory, caloric restriction; supplements have not extended lifespan.
- SOD: 2 O2•⁻ + 2H⁺ → H2O2 + O2 (Cu/Zn cytosol, Mn mitochondria); catalase: 2 H2O2 → 2 H2O + O2 (haem, peroxisomes); GPx: H2O2 + 2 GSH → GSSG + 2 H2O (selenium).
- Glutathione = γ-Glu-Cys-Gly; lipoic acid = network antioxidant (DHLA) and PDH cofactor; melatonin = pineal indoleamine, crosses BBB.
- BHT and BHA = hindered phenols, chain-breaking antioxidants for fats and oils; synergy with citric acid.

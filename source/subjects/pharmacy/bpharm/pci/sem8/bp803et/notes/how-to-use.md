# How to use this guide

Pharma Marketing Management is **one of the semester-8 electives** of the PCI B.Pharm course. It has no practical; the whole subject is the 75-mark theory paper. The subject is descriptive: there are very few calculations, and marks come from well-organised answers with definitions, classifications, diagrams and pharmaceutical examples. Study one unit a day: read the notes, draw each diagram once from memory (marketing environment, BCG matrix, product life cycle, channel chain), then answer the question bank. Each unit has very short (2-mark), short answer (5-mark) and long essay (10-mark) questions, rated by how often that kind of question appears in PCI-syllabus papers.

The five units follow the five units of the PCI syllabus exactly. Unit 4 joins two syllabus topics (marketing channels and the professional sales representative) and Unit 5 joins pricing with emerging concepts, as the syllabus itself does.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ questions | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with a diagram or table and pharmaceutical examples.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from common patterns and syllabus weight, not a promise of what will be asked.

| Unit | Topics that keep coming back |

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. Open every answer with a one-line definition, then a classification table or list. Examiners in marketing papers reward structure.
2. Draw where you can: the marketing environment circles, Porter's five forces, the product life cycle curve, the BCG grid, the channel chain from manufacturer to patient, the buyer decision process. A neat diagram is worth marks even in a short answer.
3. Always bring the answer back to medicines: name the doctor, chemist, stockist, C&F agent, OTC versus prescription products. A general business-studies answer scores less than one with pharmacy examples.
4. For comparison questions (marketing vs selling, push vs pull, skimming vs penetration, vertical vs horizontal systems), use a two-column table with at least six points.
5. Learn the few exact facts that this paper can test: DPCO is issued under section 3 of the Essential Commodities Act, 1955; current order DPCO 2013; ceiling price = simple average PTR of brands with at least 1% market share plus 16% retailer margin; annual WPI revision; 10% annual limit on MRP increase for non-scheduled drugs; NPPA set up on 29 August 1997.
6. Do not write market-size or growth figures unless you remember them with their source and year; a wrong number costs more than no number.
7. In long PSR questions, cover all the syllabus words in order — duties, purpose of detailing, selection and training, supervising, call norms, motivating, evaluating, compensation, future prospects — with two or three points each.

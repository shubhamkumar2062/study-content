# Unit 3: Registration of Indian drug products in overseas markets

India exports medicines to almost every country in the world, mostly generics. To sell in another country, an Indian company must satisfy two sets of rules: **Indian export rules** (licences, certificates, customs) and the **registration rules of the importing country** (a dossier reviewed by that country's regulator). This unit covers the export procedure, the technical documents that make up a registration dossier, Drug Master Files, and the three common dossier formats: CTD, eCTD and the ASEAN Common Technical Dossier (ACTD).

Export procedures and document lists change through notices of the Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT), CDSCO and importing countries. Learn the principles for the exam; in real work check the current notices.

## 3.1 Why overseas registration is needed

- No country allows a medicine to be sold unless its own regulator has authorised it (registration, marketing authorisation, approval).
- The importing regulator wants proof of **quality** (GMP, specifications, stability for its climate), **safety and efficacy** (for generics, bioequivalence with its own reference product) and **correct labelling** in its language.
- Markets are often grouped as **regulated** (USA, EU, UK, Japan, Australia, Canada — strict review and inspections), **semi-regulated / emerging** (many countries of Asia, Africa, Latin America, the CIS, the Gulf) and markets that rely heavily on WHO certificates and **WHO prequalification**.

## 3.2 Procedure for export of pharmaceutical products from India

### a. Requirements on the Indian side

| Requirement | Issued by | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Importer-Exporter Code (IEC) | DGFT (Ministry of Commerce) | Needed by every exporter |
| Registration-cum-Membership Certificate (RCMC) | Pharmexcil (Pharmaceuticals Export Promotion Council of India) | Registration with the export promotion council for pharmaceuticals; needed to claim export benefits |
| Manufacturing licence | State Licensing Authority (with central approval for specified products) | Legal manufacture of the product in India |
| WHO-GMP certificate | State Licensing Authority / CDSCO after inspection | Shows the site follows WHO GMP |
| Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product (COPP) | CDSCO (zonal offices), in the WHO certification scheme format | Required by many importing regulators |
| Free sale certificate, market standing, non-conviction and similar certificates | Licensing authorities | Asked by some importing countries |
| Export NOC | CDSCO (port office) | For export of drugs not approved for sale in India, or of unapproved new drugs and certain other categories; check current rules |

Other points: export packs may need special labelling (for example "For export only"), and DGFT has rules on track-and-trace barcoding of export packs; drugs controlled under the NDPS Act need separate export authorisation from the Narcotics Commissioner.

### b. Steps in exporting a product

```text
Market selection (demand, competition, regulatory route, patents)
   → Appoint local agent / importer / marketing authorisation holder
   → Study importing country's registration rules (dossier format, BE
     reference product, stability zone, labelling, fees)
   → Prepare dossier (CTD / ACTD / country format) + samples + certificates
     (COPP, GMP, free sale, analysis certificates)
   → Submit to importing regulator → queries → possible GMP inspection
     of the Indian site → product registration (licence) granted
   → Import permit / import licence for each shipment (importer's side)
   → Commercial export: purchase order, manufacture, release by QA,
     export documents, customs clearance, shipment
   → Post-registration: variations, renewals, pharmacovigilance
```

### c. Shipping and commercial documents

| Document | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Commercial invoice and packing list | Value, quantity and packing details |
| Shipping bill | Customs export declaration in India |
| Bill of lading / airway bill | Transport contract and receipt |
| Certificate of origin | Shows goods were made in India (for duty purposes) |
| Certificate of analysis (CoA) | Batch test results |
| Insurance certificate, letter of credit documents | Commercial and payment security |
| Import permit of buyer's country | Some countries need it for each consignment |

## 3.3 Technical documentation

The **technical documentation** (dossier) is the full set of scientific and administrative documents submitted to the importing regulator.

| Part | Typical contents |
| --- | --- |
| Administrative | Application form, fees, power of attorney for local agent, manufacturing licence, GMP certificate, COPP, free sale certificate, labels and leaflet in the local language, product information (summary of product characteristics) |
| Quality (CMC) | Drug substance: manufacturer, synthesis, specifications, impurities, stability (often via a DMF or certificate); drug product: composition, pharmaceutical development, manufacturing process and controls, process validation, excipient control, finished product specifications and test methods with validation, container closure, stability in the right climatic zone |
| Non-clinical | For generics: usually literature-based overview; for new products: full reports |
| Clinical | For generics: bioequivalence study report against the reference product accepted by that country (or a biowaiver justification); for new products: full clinical data |

Supporting documents commonly requested: **Site Master File** (description of the manufacturing site and its quality systems), validation master plan, batch manufacturing records, analytical method validation reports, and samples with reference standards.

### Stability and climatic zones

Stability conditions depend on the climate of the importing country (ICH and WHO zones: zone I temperate to zone IV hot and humid). A product for a hot humid country needs data under the zone IV conditions accepted by that regulator. Check the importing country's guideline for the exact conditions and duration.

## 3.4 Drug Master File (DMF)

**Definition:** a DMF is a confidential, detailed document submitted to a regulator by a manufacturer (usually of an API, excipient or packaging material) giving information about facilities, processes and materials used in making a drug component. It lets the supplier protect its confidential know-how while still allowing the regulator to review it in support of a customer's application.

### a. How a DMF works (US system)

```text
API maker (DMF holder) ──files DMF──→ FDA (DMF number allotted)
        │
        └──Letter of Authorisation (LoA)──→ Finished-product company (applicant)
                                             │
                                             └── cites DMF number + LoA in its
                                                 ANDA / NDA ──→ FDA reviews DMF
                                                                 with the application
```

- The FDA **neither approves nor disapproves** a DMF; it reviews it only when an application refers to it.
- The holder must keep it current: amendments for changes and annual reports.
- The applicant gets an "open part" (non-confidential) of the information; the "closed part" goes only to the regulator.
- For generic APIs supporting ANDAs, US law adds a DMF fee and a completeness assessment (GDUFA).

### b. Types of US DMF

| Type | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| Type I | Manufacturing site, facilities, operating procedures and personnel — **no longer used** |
| Type II | Drug substance (API), drug substance intermediate and materials used in their preparation, or drug product |
| Type III | Packaging material |
| Type IV | Excipient, colourant, flavour, essence, or material used in their preparation |
| Type V | FDA-accepted reference information |

Type II is by far the most common; Indian API makers hold a large number of US Type II DMFs.

### c. Contents of a Type II DMF (outline)

1. Administrative: holder name and address, agent, letter of commitment, list of customers authorised.
2. General information of the substance: nomenclature, structure, properties.
3. Manufacture: manufacturer and site, flow chart and description of synthesis, control of materials, critical steps and intermediates, process validation.
4. Characterisation: proof of structure, impurities (organic, inorganic, residual solvents, genotoxic impurities).
5. Control of drug substance: specifications, analytical methods, method validation, batch analyses.
6. Reference standards, container closure system, stability data.

It is now submitted in CTD format (usually Module 3.2.S plus Module 1 and the quality summary) and, for the FDA, electronically.

### d. DMF equivalents in other regions

| Region | System |
| --- | --- |
| EU | **Active Substance Master File (ASMF)**, with an applicant's part and a restricted part; or a **Certificate of Suitability (CEP)** from EDQM showing the API complies with the European Pharmacopoeia monograph |
| Canada | Master files with Health Canada |
| Japan | Master File system |
| WHO prequalification | API master file procedure |

## 3.5 Common Technical Document (CTD)

**Definition:** the CTD is an internationally agreed format for organising the quality, safety and efficacy information in a registration application. It was developed by ICH (guideline **M4**) and is used by the USA, EU, Japan, Canada, Australia, the UK, India and many other countries. It standardises the **format**, not the data requirements: each country still decides what studies it needs.

### a. Structure: the CTD triangle

```text
                         /\
                        /  \
                       / M1 \      Regional administrative information
                      /------\     (not part of the harmonised CTD)
                     /   M2   \    CTD summaries
                    /----------\
                   /  M3 | M4 | M5\  Quality | Non-clinical | Clinical
                  /------+----+----\       study reports
```

| Module | Title | Main contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Administrative and prescribing information (regional) | Application forms, fees, cover letter, proposed labels and product information, country-specific certificates |
| 2 | Common technical document summaries | 2.1 table of contents; 2.2 introduction; 2.3 quality overall summary; 2.4 non-clinical overview; 2.5 clinical overview; 2.6 non-clinical written and tabulated summaries; 2.7 clinical summary |
| 3 | Quality | 3.1 table of contents; 3.2 body of data — 3.2.S drug substance, 3.2.P drug product, 3.2.A appendices, 3.2.R regional; 3.3 literature references |
| 4 | Non-clinical study reports | Pharmacology, pharmacokinetics, toxicology; literature |
| 5 | Clinical study reports | Tabular listing; biopharmaceutic (BA/BE) studies; PK and PD studies; efficacy and safety studies; post-marketing experience; case report forms; literature |

ICH sub-guidelines: **M4Q** (quality), **M4S** (safety), **M4E** (efficacy).

### b. Advantages

1. One dossier, reused for many countries with only Module 1 changed.
2. Saves time and cost of reformatting.
3. Easier review; reviewers know where to find each item.
4. Better exchange of information between regulators.
5. Basis for electronic submission (eCTD).

## 3.6 Electronic Common Technical Document (eCTD)

**Definition:** the eCTD is the electronic form of the CTD, defined by ICH (developed under the ICH M2 work on electronic standards). It lets the dossier be submitted, reviewed and kept up to date electronically through the whole life of the product.

### a. Key features

| Feature | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Folder structure | Fixed folders m1 to m5 matching the CTD modules |
| XML backbone | An index file that lists every document, its location and its life-cycle status |
| Documents | Mostly PDF files with bookmarks and hyperlinks |
| Sequences | Each submission is a numbered sequence (0000 for the first, 0001, 0002 …) |
| Life-cycle operations | Each document is marked **new**, **replace**, **append** or **delete**, so the regulator sees the current version and the history |
| Checksums | Each file has a checksum so changes or corruption can be detected |
| Regional Module 1 | Each regulator publishes its own Module 1 specification |
| Validation | Regulators check submissions with technical validation criteria before review |

The long-used version is eCTD **3.2.2**; ICH has also issued **eCTD v4.0**, based on a newer messaging standard, which regulators are bringing in step by step.

### b. Advantages over paper

- Faster submission and review; no printing, shipping or storage of paper volumes.
- Clear life-cycle management of variations and renewals.
- Easy searching and hyperlinking between summaries and reports.
- Reduced errors and duplication.

The FDA requires the eCTD format for NDAs, ANDAs, BLAs, commercial INDs and master files (check the FDA eCTD page for exceptions); the EU and other major regulators also use it. In India, CDSCO accepts dossiers in CTD format through SUGAM; check its notices for the current electronic requirements.

## 3.7 ASEAN Common Technical Dossier (ACTD)

The syllabus calls it the ASEAN Common Technical Document; its official name is the **ASEAN Common Technical Dossier (ACTD)**. It is the registration format agreed by the member states of the **Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN)** (for example Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam). It goes together with the **ASEAN Common Technical Requirements (ACTR)**, which describe what data are needed.

### Structure: four parts

| Part | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Part I | Table of contents, administrative data and product information (application form, labels, package insert, product information, certificates) |
| Part II | Quality document |
| Part III | Non-clinical document |
| Part IV | Clinical document |

### CTD vs ACTD

| Point | ICH CTD | ACTD |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Developed by | ICH | ASEAN |
| Organisation | 5 modules | 4 parts |
| Summaries | Separate Module 2 | Overviews and summaries placed within each part |
| Administrative data | Module 1, regional | Part I, common to ASEAN with country additions |
| Generic products | Quality + BE as required by each region | Non-clinical and clinical parts often not required for generics; quality and BE (where the country requires it) are the focus |
| Electronic version | eCTD | Mainly paper/electronic files per country practice |

## 3.8 Practical tips for Indian exporters

1. Check patent and exclusivity status in each target country before development.
2. Use the importing country's reference product for BE, or confirm that a foreign reference is accepted.
3. Generate stability data at the right climatic zone conditions from the start.
4. Keep one "core" CTD dossier and adapt Module 1 or the ACTD Part I for each country.
5. Expect foreign GMP inspections (FDA, EU authorities, WHO prequalification) and keep the site inspection-ready.
6. Maintain the registration: variations, renewals, pharmacovigilance and labelling updates.

## Remember

- Export from India: IEC (DGFT), RCMC (Pharmexcil), manufacturing licence, WHO-GMP, COPP (CDSCO), free sale certificate, export NOC where needed; then the importing country's registration and import permit.
- Technical dossier: administrative + quality + non-clinical + clinical (for generics, BE); Site Master File; stability by climatic zone.
- DMF: confidential, neither approved nor disapproved; LoA lets the applicant cite it; Types II (API), III (packaging), IV (excipients), V (reference information); Type I no longer used. EU equivalents: ASMF and CEP.
- CTD (ICH M4): M1 regional, M2 summaries, M3 quality, M4 non-clinical, M5 clinical; format, not content, is harmonised.
- eCTD: folders m1–m5, XML backbone, sequences 0000…, life-cycle operations new/replace/append/delete, checksums; versions 3.2.2 and 4.0.
- ACTD: ASEAN; four parts (I admin and product information, II quality, III non-clinical, IV clinical) with ACTR.

# Unit 5: Regulatory concepts

Regulatory affairs has its own vocabulary and its own reference books. This unit explains the basic terms, the difference between laws, regulations, guidelines and guidance, and four US publications that every regulatory professional uses: the **Federal Register**, the **Code of Federal Regulations (CFR)**, the **Orange Book** and the **Purple Book**. (The syllabus writes "Code of Federal Regulatory"; the correct name is Code of Federal Regulations.) Indian equivalents are given alongside so the ideas can be applied at home.

## 5.1 Basic regulatory terminology

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Drug | A substance used for diagnosis, treatment, mitigation or prevention of disease, or to affect the structure or function of the body (each law has its own exact definition, e.g. section 3(b) of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940) |
| New drug | In India: a drug not yet approved for the proposed use, or an approved drug with new claims, new dosage form, new route, new combination, etc. (NDCT Rules 2019) |
| Investigational product | Active ingredient or placebo being tested or used as a reference in a trial |
| Active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) | The substance that gives the pharmacological effect |
| New chemical entity / new molecular entity (NCE/NME) | An active moiety never approved before |
| Innovator / reference product | The first approved product, used as the comparator for generics |
| Reference listed drug (RLD) | US term for the approved product a generic copies |
| Generic drug | Same API, dosage form, strength, route and use as the reference, and bioequivalent to it |
| Biological product / biologic | Product made from living systems (vaccines, blood products, proteins, antibodies) |
| Biosimilar | Biologic highly similar to an approved reference biologic with no clinically meaningful differences |
| Interchangeable product | US term: a biosimilar that may be substituted for the reference product without the prescriber's intervention, where state law allows |
| Bioavailability / bioequivalence | Rate and extent of absorption / no significant difference in these between two products |
| Sponsor / applicant | Person or company that runs the trial / submits the application |
| Marketing authorisation holder (MAH) | Company legally responsible for the product on the market |
| Dossier | The complete set of documents submitted for approval |
| Label / labelling | Label = matter on the container; labelling = label plus package insert and all printed matter that goes with the product |
| SmPC / PIL | Summary of product characteristics (for professionals) / patient information leaflet (EU terms) |
| Variation / supplement | Application to change an approved product (EU term / US term) |
| Renewal | Re-approval at the end of a validity period (e.g. EU after five years) |
| Clinical hold | FDA order to delay or stop a clinical investigation |
| Complete response letter | FDA letter listing why an application cannot be approved in its present form |
| Tentative approval | US: an ANDA meets all scientific needs but cannot be approved yet because of patents or exclusivity |
| Orphan drug | Drug for a rare disease, with special incentives |
| Exclusivity | Period during which a regulator will not approve (or accept) competing applications, granted by law on approval |
| Patent | Intellectual property right granted by the patent office for an invention, independent of drug approval |
| GxP | Collective name for good practices: GLP (laboratory), GCP (clinical), GMP (manufacturing), GDP (distribution), GVP (pharmacovigilance) |
| Pharmacovigilance | Detection, assessment, understanding and prevention of adverse effects |
| PSUR / PBRER | Periodic safety update report / periodic benefit-risk evaluation report |
| Regulatory intelligence | Collecting and analysing current regulatory information to guide strategy |

## 5.2 Laws, Acts, regulations, guidelines and guidance

These words describe **levels** of regulatory documents. The higher the level, the stronger its legal force.

```text
        ┌────────────────────────────────────┐
        │ CONSTITUTION                       │
        ├────────────────────────────────────┤
        │ ACT / LAW (statute)                │  passed by Parliament / Congress
        │ e.g. Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940; │  legally binding
        │ US FD&C Act                        │
        ├────────────────────────────────────┤
        │ REGULATIONS / RULES                │  made by the government or agency
        │ e.g. Drugs Rules 1945, NDCT Rules  │  under powers given by the Act;
        │ 2019; US 21 CFR                    │  legally binding
        ├────────────────────────────────────┤
        │ GUIDELINES / GUIDANCE              │  recommendations explaining how
        │ e.g. ICH guidelines, FDA guidance, │  to meet the law; not legally
        │ CDSCO guidance documents           │  binding by themselves
        └────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### a. Law and Act

- A **law** is a rule of conduct made and enforced by the state. An **Act** (statute) is a law passed by the legislature (Parliament in India, Congress in the USA) and signed into force.
- Acts set broad principles, define offences and penalties, create authorities and give the government power to make rules.
- Examples: India — Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940, Pharmacy Act 1948, NDPS Act 1985, Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act 1954. USA — Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act 1938 and amendments (Kefauver–Harris 1962, Hatch–Waxman 1984), Public Health Service Act.

### b. Regulations (rules)

- **Regulations** are detailed, legally binding requirements made by an executive authority under the power given by an Act ("delegated" or "subordinate" legislation).
- They say exactly what must be done: forms, licences, standards, timelines.
- Examples: India — Drugs Rules 1945, NDCT Rules 2019, Medical Devices Rules 2017. USA — regulations in Title 21 CFR (e.g. 21 CFR 312 for IND, 211 for GMP).
- In the USA new regulations are made by **notice-and-comment rulemaking:** the proposed rule is published in the Federal Register, the public comments, and the final rule is published with a date of effect. In India, draft rules are published in the **Gazette of India** for objections and suggestions, and final rules are notified in the Gazette (as GSR notifications).

### c. Guidelines and guidance

- **Guidelines** are recommendations that describe an acceptable way to meet the legal requirements. They are not law; another approach may be used if it satisfies the law and is justified.
- **FDA guidance documents** represent the FDA's "current thinking" on a topic. They are not binding on the FDA or the public; they use words like "should" and "recommend", while regulations use "must" and "shall". Draft guidances are published for comment before being finalised.
- **ICH guidelines** are harmonised technical guidelines adopted by member regulators: Q (quality, e.g. Q1 stability, Q8–Q10), S (safety, e.g. S7 safety pharmacology), E (efficacy, e.g. E6 GCP, E2A safety reporting), M (multidisciplinary, e.g. M4 CTD). Once a regulator adopts an ICH guideline it becomes part of its expectations.
- **WHO guidelines** (e.g. WHO GMP) are used widely, especially by countries without their own detailed guidance.
- **CDSCO** issues guidance documents and notices (for example on clinical trials, BA/BE studies, biosimilars).

### d. Comparison

| Point | Act / law | Regulation / rule | Guideline / guidance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Made by | Legislature | Government / agency under the Act | Agency or international body |
| Binding | Yes | Yes | No (recommendation) |
| Detail | Broad | Detailed | Technical, practical |
| Change | Slow (amendment by legislature) | By notification after consultation | Easily revised |
| Language | Shall / must | Shall / must | Should / recommended |
| Example | D&C Act 1940; FD&C Act | Drugs Rules 1945; 21 CFR 314 | ICH Q1A; FDA BE guidance |

## 5.3 Federal Register

The **Federal Register (FR)** is the **official daily journal of the US government**. It is published every federal working day by the **Office of the Federal Register** (part of the National Archives and Records Administration) with the US Government Publishing Office.

**Contents (sections):**

1. **Rules and regulations:** final rules, with the date they take effect.
2. **Proposed rules:** draft regulations, with the period for public comments.
3. **Notices:** announcements of guidance documents, advisory committee meetings, availability of documents, decisions on applications, withdrawal of approvals.
4. **Presidential documents:** executive orders and proclamations.

**Importance in drug regulation:**

- It is how the public learns of proposed and final FDA regulations, and how comments are invited.
- It gives the legal history ("preamble") explaining the reasons behind each regulation.
- Citations are by volume and page, written like "65 FR 1776".
- Final rules published in the FR are later collected ("codified") into the CFR.

**Indian equivalent:** the **Gazette of India**, in which drug rules, amendments, bans and other notifications are published (for example the NDCT Rules 2019 were notified as G.S.R. 227(E)).

## 5.4 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR)

The **CFR** is the **codification (organised collection) of the general and permanent rules** published in the Federal Register by US federal agencies. It is divided into **50 titles**, each covering a broad area; drug rules are in **Title 21 — Food and Drugs**. Each title is revised once a year (Title 21 as of 1 April), and an electronic version (eCFR) is kept up to date continuously.

**Structure and citation:**

```text
Title → Chapter → Subchapter → Part → Subpart → Section
21 CFR 312.40  =  Title 21, Part 312, Section 40
(Chapter I of Title 21 = Food and Drug Administration, Parts 1–1299)
```

**Important parts of 21 CFR for pharmacy students:**

| Part | Subject |
| --- | --- |
| 11 | Electronic records and electronic signatures |
| 50 | Protection of human subjects (informed consent) |
| 54 | Financial disclosure by clinical investigators |
| 56 | Institutional review boards |
| 58 | Good laboratory practice for non-clinical studies |
| 201 | Labelling |
| 210, 211 | Current good manufacturing practice (general; finished pharmaceuticals) |
| 312 | Investigational new drug application |
| 314 | Applications for FDA approval to market a new drug (NDA, ANDA) |
| 320 | Bioavailability and bioequivalence requirements |
| 600–680 | Biological products |
| 820 | Quality system / quality management for medical devices |

**FR vs CFR:**

| Federal Register | Code of Federal Regulations |
| --- | --- |
| Daily journal, in date order | Organised by subject (title and part) |
| Contains proposed rules, final rules, notices | Contains only final, permanent rules in force |
| Includes preambles explaining rules | Rule text only |
| Like a newspaper of the government | Like an encyclopaedia of the rules |

## 5.5 Orange Book

**Official title:** *Approved Drug Products with Therapeutic Equivalence Evaluations*, published by the FDA (named after its orange cover). It is available online, and its data files are updated monthly.

### a. What it contains

1. A list of drug products approved by the FDA as safe and effective under the FD&C Act (NDA and ANDA products).
2. **Therapeutic equivalence (TE) evaluations** for multi-source prescription products.
3. **Patent information** listed by NDA holders for each product.
4. **Exclusivity information** for each product.
5. Lists of OTC products and of discontinued products.

### b. Therapeutic equivalence codes

| First letter | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | Considered therapeutically equivalent to other pharmaceutically equivalent products | **AB** — bioequivalence actual or potential problems resolved by adequate in vivo/in vitro evidence; AA — products in conventional dosage forms with no known BE problems; AN, AO, AP, AT for aerosols, oils, parenterals, topicals |
| **B** | Not considered therapeutically equivalent at present (BE not shown or problems not resolved) | BC, BD, BE, BN, BP, BR, BS, BT, BX |

### c. Uses

- Pharmacists and states use TE codes to decide **generic substitution** (an AB-rated generic may be substituted).
- Generic companies use it to choose the **RLD** and reference standard, and to find **patents and exclusivities** they must certify against (Paragraph I–IV).
- It is the starting point for planning a generic launch.

### d. Common US exclusivities shown in it (well-established figures)

| Exclusivity | Period |
| --- | --- |
| New chemical entity | 5 years |
| New clinical investigation (e.g. new indication supported by new studies) | 3 years |
| Orphan drug | 7 years |
| Paediatric | Adds 6 months to existing patents/exclusivities |
| First generic with Paragraph IV challenge | 180 days |

## 5.6 Purple Book

**What it is:** the FDA's *Database of Licensed Biological Products*, the biologics counterpart of the Orange Book. It lists biological products licensed under the **Public Health Service Act** by CDER and CBER.

**Contents:**

1. Licensed biological products (BLAs under section 351(a)) and their reference products.
2. **Biosimilar** products (section 351(k)) and whether each is also **interchangeable**.
3. Date of licensure.
4. **Reference product exclusivity** expiry dates and any interchangeability exclusivity.
5. Certain patent information (added by later law).

**Uses:** helps prescribers, pharmacists and companies know which biosimilars exist, which are interchangeable (and so may be substituted where state law allows), and when exclusivity ends.

### Orange Book vs Purple Book

| Point | Orange Book | Purple Book |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Products | Small-molecule drugs (NDA, ANDA) | Biological products (BLA, biosimilars) |
| Law | FD&C Act | Public Health Service Act |
| Equivalence term | Therapeutic equivalence (A/B codes) | Biosimilarity and interchangeability |
| Copies | Generics | Biosimilars |
| Patent and exclusivity data | Yes | Exclusivity dates and certain patent information |

## 5.7 Linking the concepts

```text
Act (FD&C Act / D&C Act 1940)
   └─→ Regulations (21 CFR / Drugs Rules, NDCT Rules) — first published in
       the Federal Register / Gazette of India, then codified (CFR)
          └─→ Guidance (FDA guidance, ICH, CDSCO guidance) — how to comply
                 └─→ Applications (IND, NDA, ANDA, BLA / CT-04, CT-18, CT-21)
                        └─→ Approved products listed in the Orange Book or
                            Purple Book; generics/biosimilars refer to them
```

## Remember

- Act (legislature, binding, broad) → regulations/rules (agency, binding, detailed) → guidelines/guidance (recommendations, "should", not binding).
- Federal Register: daily journal of the US government; rules, proposed rules, notices, presidential documents; India's equivalent is the Gazette of India.
- CFR: 50 titles; Title 21 Food and Drugs; parts 11, 50, 56, 58, 210/211, 312, 314, 320; revised yearly (Title 21 as of 1 April); citation 21 CFR 312.40.
- Orange Book: "Approved Drug Products with Therapeutic Equivalence Evaluations"; TE codes A (AB etc.) and B; patents and exclusivity; data files updated monthly.
- Exclusivity: NCE 5 years, new clinical investigation 3 years, orphan 7 years, paediatric +6 months, first Paragraph IV generic 180 days.
- Purple Book: Database of Licensed Biological Products; PHS Act 351(a) and 351(k); biosimilar and interchangeable; reference product exclusivity.

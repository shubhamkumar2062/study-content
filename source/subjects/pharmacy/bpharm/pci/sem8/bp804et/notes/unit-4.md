# Unit 4: Clinical trials

A **clinical trial** is a systematic study of a drug (or other intervention) in human subjects to find or confirm its clinical, pharmacological or adverse effects, or its absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion, with the aim of establishing safety and efficacy. Because people are exposed to an unproven medicine, trials are controlled by law and by the ethical and scientific standard called **Good Clinical Practice (GCP)**.

Key documents for this unit:

| Document | Scope |
| --- | --- |
| ICH E6 — Guideline for Good Clinical Practice (revised as R2 and, most recently, R3) | International GCP standard |
| ICH E2A | Definitions and expedited reporting of safety data in trials |
| Declaration of Helsinki (World Medical Association) | Ethical principles for research on humans |
| India: New Drugs and Clinical Trials (NDCT) Rules, 2019 | Permission, ethics committees, informed consent, safety reporting, compensation |
| India: ICMR National Ethical Guidelines for Biomedical and Health Research | Ethics guidance |
| USA: 21 CFR Parts 50 (informed consent), 56 (IRBs), 312 (IND) | US rules |

## 4.1 The people involved

```text
          Regulator (CDSCO / FDA …) ── permission, inspections
                     │
Sponsor ── funds, designs, manages ──→ Investigator + site team ←── Subjects
   │        (may delegate to a CRO)          │                        (consent)
   └── Monitor (CRA) checks the site ────────┘
                     │
   Ethics committee / IRB ── approves protocol and consent, watches safety
```

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Sponsor | Person, company or institution that starts, manages and/or finances the trial |
| Contract research organisation (CRO) | Organisation that does some of the sponsor's trial duties under contract |
| Investigator (principal investigator) | Qualified person responsible for conducting the trial at a site |
| Monitor / clinical research associate (CRA) | Person appointed by the sponsor to oversee the trial at sites |
| Ethics committee (EC) / institutional review board (IRB) | Independent body protecting the rights, safety and well-being of subjects |
| Subject / participant | Person taking part, either a patient or a healthy volunteer |

## 4.2 Developing a clinical trial protocol

The **protocol** is the document that describes the objectives, design, methods, statistical considerations and organisation of a trial. Every person involved works from it, and the regulator and ethics committee approve it before the trial starts. Changes are made only by formal **protocol amendments**, which also need approval.

### a. Steps in developing a protocol

```text
Define the research question (from pre-clinical data and earlier phases)
 → Literature review and regulatory/scientific advice
 → Choose design, population, endpoints, sample size
 → Draft with clinicians, statisticians, pharmacologists, regulatory, QA
 → Internal review → regulator + ethics committee review → final version
 → Amendments as needed during the trial
```

### b. Contents of a protocol (ICH E6 outline)

1. **General information:** title, protocol number, version and date; names of sponsor, monitor, investigators, laboratories.
2. **Background:** name and description of the investigational product, summary of non-clinical and clinical findings, known risks and benefits, route and dosage, population, references.
3. **Objectives and purpose:** primary and secondary.
4. **Trial design:**
   - primary and secondary **endpoints**;
   - type of trial: placebo-controlled or active-controlled, parallel or crossover;
   - **randomisation** (to avoid selection bias) and **blinding** (open, single-blind, double-blind);
   - treatments and duration; stopping rules; drug accountability; how randomisation codes are kept and broken.
5. **Selection and withdrawal of subjects:** inclusion criteria, exclusion criteria, withdrawal criteria and follow-up.
6. **Treatment of subjects:** products, schedule, permitted and prohibited concomitant medicines, compliance checks.
7. **Assessment of efficacy:** parameters, how and when measured.
8. **Assessment of safety:** parameters, recording and reporting of adverse events, follow-up.
9. **Statistics:** sample size with power, significance level, analysis sets (for example intention-to-treat), interim analyses, handling of missing data.
10. **Direct access to source data and documents** for monitoring, audit and inspection.
11. **Quality control and quality assurance.**
12. **Ethics:** ethics committee approval, informed consent.
13. **Data handling and record keeping.**
14. **Financing, insurance, compensation and publication policy.**

### c. Features of a good protocol

Clear and answerable question; scientifically sound design; subjects protected; feasible at the planned sites; consistent with the Investigator's Brochure; enough detail for every site to do the same thing.

## 4.3 Institutional Review Board / Independent Ethics Committee

An **ethics committee** (called an IRB in the USA, IEC or EC elsewhere) is an independent body of medical, scientific and non-scientific members. It reviews and approves the protocol, the informed consent documents and the investigators and facilities, and it keeps watch on the trial to protect the subjects.

### a. Formation (constitution)

**India (NDCT Rules 2019, rule 7)** — an ethics committee for clinical trials must have:

- a **minimum of seven members** from medical, non-medical, scientific and non-scientific areas, with at least:
  - one lay person;
  - one woman member;
  - one legal expert;
  - one independent member from a related field (such as a social scientist, a representative of a voluntary organisation, a philosopher, ethicist or theologian);
- **at least 50% of members not affiliated** with the institute;
- a **chairperson** who is not affiliated with the institute, and a **member secretary** who is affiliated;
- members trained as specified by the Central Licensing Authority.

The committee must be **registered** with the Central Licensing Authority (application in Form CT-01; registration granted in Form CT-02) before it can approve trials. An ethics committee that only approves biomedical and health research (not drug trials) registers with the authority named in the rules for that purpose.

**USA (21 CFR 56):** an IRB has at least five members of varied background, including at least one scientist, one non-scientist and one member not affiliated with the institution; members with a conflict of interest do not vote on that study.

### b. Working procedures

1. Written **standard operating procedures** covering membership, meetings, quorum, review, decisions and records.
2. **Initial review** of the protocol, Investigator's Brochure, informed consent form and patient information in the local language, advertisements for recruitment, investigator CVs, insurance and compensation arrangements.
3. **Decision** in a properly constituted meeting: approval, approval with modifications, disapproval, or termination/suspension of an earlier approval. Members with a conflict of interest withdraw.
4. **Expedited review** for minor amendments, where its SOPs allow.
5. **Continuing review** at intervals suited to the risk (at least yearly in most systems): progress reports, amendments, protocol deviations.
6. **Safety review:** serious adverse events, deaths, new safety information; opinion on compensation for injury or death (India).
7. **Site visits** where needed.
8. **Record keeping:** membership lists, SOPs, minutes, correspondence, kept for the period set by the rules.

## 4.4 Informed consent process and procedures

**Informed consent** is the process by which a subject voluntarily confirms willingness to take part in a trial after being told all aspects of the trial relevant to that decision. It is documented by a written, signed and dated consent form. It is a **process**, not only a signature: it continues throughout the trial.

### a. Essential elements of the information given

1. That the study involves research; its purpose and expected duration.
2. Procedures, including which are experimental; trial treatments and chance of random assignment.
3. Foreseeable risks and discomforts; expected benefits (or that there may be none).
4. Alternative treatments available.
5. Confidentiality of records, and who may see them (monitors, auditors, ethics committee, regulators).
6. Compensation and treatment available for trial-related injury.
7. Whom to contact with questions or in case of injury.
8. That participation is **voluntary**, and the subject may **withdraw at any time** without penalty or loss of benefits.
9. Expected payments or expenses, and the approximate number of subjects.
10. Circumstances in which participation may be ended by the investigator.

### b. Procedure

```text
Approved consent form and information sheet (in a language the subject understands)
 → Investigator or delegate explains the trial in plain words
 → Enough time to read, think, ask questions, consult family
 → No pressure or undue influence
 → Subject (or legally acceptable representative) signs and dates;
   person explaining signs; impartial witness if subject cannot read
 → Copy given to the subject
 → Re-consent when important new information arises
```

### c. Special situations

- **Children:** consent of a parent or legally acceptable representative, plus the child's **assent** when the child can understand.
- **Unconscious or mentally incapable subjects:** consent of a legally acceptable representative; emergency situations as allowed by the rules and ethics committee.
- **Illiterate subjects:** an impartial witness is present throughout.
- **India:** the NDCT Rules require an **audio-video recording** of the informed consent process for vulnerable subjects in clinical trials of a new chemical entity or new molecular entity; for trials of anti-HIV and anti-leprosy drugs, only an audio recording is kept. The checklist of consent elements and a model consent form are in the Third Schedule of the rules.

## 4.5 GCP obligations of investigators, sponsors and monitors

### a. Investigator

1. Is qualified by education, training and experience; has enough time, staff and facilities; has adequate number of eligible subjects available.
2. Follows the approved protocol; documents and explains any deviation.
3. Obtains ethics committee approval before starting and keeps the committee informed.
4. Obtains and documents informed consent before any trial procedure.
5. Provides medical care for adverse events during and after participation.
6. **Reports safety events:** serious adverse events promptly to the sponsor (and, in India, to the regulator and ethics committee — see 4.7).
7. Keeps the investigational product properly: storage, dispensing only to subjects, accountability records, return or destruction.
8. Keeps accurate, complete source documents and case report forms; permits monitoring, audit and inspection.
9. Follows randomisation procedures and breaks the blind only as the protocol allows.
10. Submits progress reports and the final report; keeps essential documents for the required period.

### b. Sponsor

1. Designs the trial and implements **quality management** (risk-based approach).
2. Selects qualified investigators and sites; obtains regulatory permission.
3. Provides the Investigator's Brochure and updates it.
4. Supplies investigational product made under GMP, properly labelled and coded.
5. Arranges **monitoring** and, independently, **audits**.
6. Handles data management, statistics, clinical study reports.
7. **Safety:** evaluates all safety information and reports serious unexpected suspected adverse reactions to regulators, investigators and ethics committees within the set timelines.
8. Provides insurance and **compensation** for trial-related injury or death (in India under the formula of the rules).
9. Registers the trial in a public registry (in India the **Clinical Trials Registry – India, CTRI**, before enrolling the first subject).
10. May transfer duties to a CRO, but remains finally responsible for the quality and integrity of the data.

### c. Monitor (clinical research associate)

Purpose of monitoring: to check that the rights and well-being of subjects are protected, that the trial data are accurate, complete and verifiable from source documents, and that the trial follows the protocol, GCP and the law.

Duties:

1. Acts as the main line of communication between sponsor and investigator.
2. Checks that the investigator and site remain suitable.
3. Checks that informed consent was obtained before participation.
4. **Source data verification:** compares case report forms with hospital and clinic records.
5. Checks investigational product storage, dispensing and accountability.
6. Checks that adverse events are reported in time.
7. Checks that essential documents are kept in the site file.
8. Writes a **monitoring report** after each visit or contact and follows up findings.

## 4.6 Managing and monitoring clinical trials

### a. Stages of trial management

```text
PLANNING        Protocol, CRF design, budget, feasibility, site selection,
                regulator + ethics approvals, CTRI registration, insurance
START-UP        Investigator meeting, site initiation visit, drug supplies,
                randomisation system, training
CONDUCT         Recruitment and consent, treatment and visits, data entry
                (CRF/eCRF), monitoring visits, safety reporting, amendments,
                drug accountability, query resolution
CLOSE-OUT       Last subject last visit, close-out visits, database lock,
                unblinding, statistical analysis
REPORTING       Clinical study report (ICH E3), results to regulator and
                ethics committee, publication, archiving of essential documents
```

### b. Types of monitoring

| Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| On-site monitoring | Monitor visits the site and checks source documents |
| Centralised (remote) monitoring | Data reviewed centrally to detect unusual patterns, outliers or missing data |
| Risk-based monitoring | Intensity of monitoring set by the risks to subjects and data in each trial or site |

### c. Quality assurance

- **Audit:** systematic, independent check by the sponsor's QA (separate from monitoring) of trial conduct and documents.
- **Inspection:** done by a regulator, at the site, sponsor or CRO.
- **Essential documents:** documents that allow the conduct of a trial and the quality of its data to be evaluated (protocol, consent forms, approvals, CVs, drug records, monitoring reports, SAE reports).
- Common findings: consent problems, protocol deviations, poor drug accountability, unreported adverse events, data not matching source.

### d. Data and safety monitoring board (DSMB)

An independent group of experts, set up by the sponsor for large or high-risk trials, that reviews accumulating safety (and sometimes efficacy) data at intervals and recommends continuing, modifying or stopping the trial.

## 4.7 Pharmacovigilance: safety monitoring in clinical trials

**Pharmacovigilance** is the science and activities relating to the detection, assessment, understanding and prevention of adverse effects or any other drug-related problem (WHO definition). In a trial it means collecting, assessing and reporting every safety finding so subjects are protected and the safety profile is understood before approval.

### a. Definitions (ICH E2A)

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Adverse event (AE) | Any untoward medical occurrence in a subject given a drug; need not be caused by it |
| Adverse drug reaction (ADR) | A noxious and unintended response to a drug where a causal relationship is at least a reasonable possibility |
| Serious AE / ADR | Results in death, is life-threatening, needs or prolongs hospitalisation, causes persistent or significant disability, or is a congenital anomaly / birth defect; also other medically important events |
| Unexpected ADR | Nature or severity not consistent with the reference safety information (Investigator's Brochure or approved label) |
| SUSAR | Suspected unexpected serious adverse reaction |

Severity (mild, moderate, severe) is different from seriousness (the regulatory criteria above).

### b. Safety monitoring process

```text
Subject reports / investigator detects event
 → Record in source and CRF (onset, severity, seriousness, action, outcome)
 → Investigator assesses causality (e.g. WHO-UMC categories) and seriousness
 → Serious → expedited report (see timelines)
 → Sponsor assesses expectedness and causality → SUSAR reported
   to regulator, ethics committees and all investigators
 → Aggregate review: DSMB, safety update reports, IB update
 → Actions: protocol change, consent update, dose change, trial stop
```

### c. Reporting timelines

| System | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| ICH E2A (adopted by many regulators) | Fatal or life-threatening unexpected ADRs: as soon as possible and within **7 calendar days** of first knowledge (follow-up within a further 8 days); other serious unexpected ADRs: within **15 calendar days** |
| India, NDCT Rules 2019 | Investigator reports every **serious adverse event** to the Central Licensing Authority, the sponsor (or its representative) and the ethics committee **within 24 hours** of occurrence; the report after due analysis goes to the Central Licensing Authority, the ethics committee chairperson and the head of the institution **within 14 days**; reasons must be given for any delay. For a death, the authority sets up an independent expert committee to examine the cause and compensation |
| USA, 21 CFR 312.32 | IND safety reports within 15 calendar days; unexpected fatal or life-threatening suspected reactions within 7 calendar days |

### d. Other safety tools

- **Development Safety Update Report (DSUR, ICH E2F):** yearly summary of safety during development.
- Updated Investigator's Brochure.
- After approval: periodic safety update reports (in India every six months for the first two years and yearly for the next two years), spontaneous reporting through the **Pharmacovigilance Programme of India (PvPI)**, whose national coordination centre is the Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission, Ghaziabad.

### e. Compensation (India)

The NDCT Rules require the sponsor to give free medical management for injury during a trial, and financial compensation for trial-related injury or death, calculated by the formula in the Seventh Schedule after review by the ethics committee and the Central Licensing Authority. Check the current rule text for amounts and procedure.

## Remember

- Protocol = objectives, design (randomised, blinded, controlled), subjects (inclusion/exclusion), treatment, efficacy, safety, statistics, ethics, data, finance.
- Ethics committee (India): ≥7 members incl. lay person, woman, legal expert, independent member; ≥50% non-affiliated; non-affiliated chairperson; registration CT-01 → CT-02. US IRB ≥5 members.
- Informed consent: voluntary, informed, documented, ongoing; children assent; impartial witness; audio-video recording for vulnerable subjects in NCE/NME trials (audio only for anti-HIV and anti-leprosy).
- Investigator: protocol, consent, care, SAE reporting, drug accountability, records. Sponsor: quality system, sites, IB, GMP supplies, monitoring, safety, compensation, registration (CTRI). Monitor: source data verification, reports.
- Trial stages: planning → start-up → conduct → close-out → reporting (ICH E3); audit vs inspection; DSMB.
- Serious = death, life-threatening, hospitalisation, disability, congenital anomaly. ICH: 7 / 15 days. India: SAE 24 hours, analysed report 14 days.

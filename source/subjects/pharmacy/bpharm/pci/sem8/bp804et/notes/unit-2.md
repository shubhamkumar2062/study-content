# Unit 2: Regulatory approval process and regulatory authorities

This unit has two parts. **Part A** follows a drug through the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) system, which the syllabus uses as the model: the Investigational New Drug (IND) application, the New Drug Application (NDA), the Abbreviated New Drug Application (ANDA) for generics, and how changes to an approved product are handled. **Part B** describes the regulators of India, the United States, the European Union, the United Kingdom, Australia, Japan and Canada: how each is organised and what types of applications it receives.

Rules, forms and review times change. The figures in these notes were checked against the official texts (US Code of Federal Regulations, FDA and EMA websites, India's NDCT Rules 2019). In real work always check the current version on the regulator's website.

## Part A: Approval processes (USA as the model)

## 2.1 The overall pathway

```text
Pre-clinical data ─→ IND submitted to FDA ─→ 30-day wait (no clinical hold)
      ─→ Phase I ─→ Phase II ─→ (end-of-Phase-II meeting) ─→ Phase III
      ─→ (pre-NDA meeting) ─→ NDA ─→ filing review (60 days)
      ─→ full review ─→ approval letter or complete response letter
      ─→ marketing + Phase IV, safety reports, supplements, annual reports

Generic copy of an approved drug ─→ ANDA (BE instead of clinical trials) ─→ approval
```

## 2.2 Investigational New Drug (IND) application

**Definition:** an IND is a request to the FDA for permission to give an unapproved drug (or an approved drug for a new, unapproved use in a trial) to humans. Legally it is also an exemption that allows the drug to be shipped across state lines for clinical trials. The rules are in **21 CFR Part 312**.

### a. Types of IND

| Type | Who files / why |
| --- | --- |
| Investigator IND | A physician who both starts and conducts a study (for example to study a new use of an approved drug) |
| Emergency use IND | For an urgent situation where there is no time to file a normal IND |
| Treatment IND | Gives access to a promising drug for serious or life-threatening disease while final trials are going on |
| Commercial (research) IND | Filed by a company that intends to market the drug |

### b. Contents of an IND

1. Cover form **FDA 1571** and table of contents.
2. Introductory statement and general investigational plan.
3. **Investigator's Brochure** (summary of non-clinical and any clinical data for investigators).
4. Clinical protocols.
5. Chemistry, manufacturing and controls (CMC) information.
6. Pharmacology and toxicology data.
7. Previous human experience, if any.
8. Additional information; investigator statement **FDA 1572** for each investigator.

### c. Process and timeline

```text
Sponsor submits IND
      │
      ▼
FDA review (medical, chemistry, pharmacology/toxicology, statistics)
      │
      ├── Safety concern ──→ CLINICAL HOLD (trial may not start / must stop)
      │                       sponsor answers → hold lifted
      │
      └── No hold within 30 days of receipt ──→ IND in effect → trial may start
```

- An IND goes into effect **30 days after the FDA receives it** unless the FDA places a clinical hold (21 CFR 312.40); the FDA can also allow an earlier start.
- An institutional review board (IRB) must also approve each study before it starts.
- During the IND the sponsor files **protocol amendments** (new protocol, changes, new investigator), **information amendments**, **IND safety reports** and **annual reports**.
- Meetings with the FDA: pre-IND, end-of-Phase-II and pre-NDA meetings help agree the development plan.

## 2.3 New Drug Application (NDA)

**Definition:** an NDA is the application asking the FDA to approve a new drug for sale in the USA. It must show that the drug is safe and effective for its proposed use, that the benefits outweigh the risks, that the proposed labelling is appropriate, and that the methods and controls used in manufacture keep its identity, strength, quality and purity. The rules are in **21 CFR Part 314**; the application form is **FDA 356h**.

### a. Types of NDA (sections of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act)

| Route | Used for |
| --- | --- |
| 505(b)(1) "stand-alone" NDA | New molecule; the applicant owns or has right of reference to all the safety and efficacy studies |
| 505(b)(2) NDA | Relies partly on studies the applicant did not do (published literature or FDA's earlier findings), e.g. new salt, new dosage form, new combination, new route |
| 505(j) ANDA | Generic copy, shown bioequivalent to the reference listed drug |
| BLA (Public Health Service Act) | Biological products such as vaccines, blood products, monoclonal antibodies |

### b. Contents

Organised in the CTD format (Unit 3): administrative documents and proposed labelling; summaries; quality (CMC); non-clinical study reports; clinical study reports with statistical analysis; patent information; financial disclosure of investigators; case report tabulations.

### c. NDA review process and timelines

```text
NDA received
   → Filing review: FDA decides within 60 days whether the application is
     complete enough to be reviewed (else "refuse to file")
   → Review by discipline: medical, statistics, clinical pharmacology,
     pharmacology/toxicology, chemistry/manufacturing, microbiology
   → Inspections: manufacturing sites (pre-approval inspection) and
     clinical sites; labelling negotiation; advisory committee if needed
   → Action: APPROVAL LETTER  or  COMPLETE RESPONSE LETTER (deficiencies)
```

- Review goals under the user-fee programme (PDUFA): **standard review 10 months, priority review 6 months**. For a new molecular entity the clock runs from the 60-day filing date. Priority review is for drugs that would be a significant improvement in a serious condition.
- Expedited programmes: fast track, breakthrough therapy, accelerated approval (based on a surrogate endpoint) and priority review.

## 2.4 Abbreviated New Drug Application (ANDA)

**Definition:** an ANDA is the application for approval of a **generic** drug under section 505(j). It is "abbreviated" because it does not repeat the non-clinical and clinical studies; it shows instead that the generic is the same as the reference listed drug (RLD) and bioequivalent to it.

### a. What an ANDA must show

1. Same active ingredient(s), dosage form, strength, route of administration and conditions of use as the RLD.
2. Labelling the same as the RLD (except permitted differences, such as manufacturer and carved-out patented uses).
3. **Bioequivalence** to the RLD (or a justified biowaiver).
4. Full chemistry, manufacturing and controls data, stability and GMP compliance of the sites.
5. **Patent certification** for each patent listed for the RLD in the Orange Book.

### b. Patent certifications

| Certification | Statement | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paragraph I | No patent information has been filed | Approval possible when ready |
| Paragraph II | The patent has expired | Approval possible when ready |
| Paragraph III | The patent will expire on a stated date | Approval effective after expiry |
| Paragraph IV | The patent is invalid or will not be infringed by the generic | Patent owner may sue; if sued in time, approval is generally stayed for up to 30 months; first successful filer may get 180-day exclusivity |

A **section viii statement** is used when the generic leaves out ("carves out") a patented method of use from its label.

### c. ANDA process

```text
ANDA submitted → receipt/filing review (completeness) → review of
bioequivalence, chemistry/manufacturing, labelling, microbiology (sterile)
→ facility inspections → approval (full or tentative) or complete response
```

**Tentative approval:** the ANDA meets all scientific requirements but final approval is blocked by patents or exclusivity. Review goals are set under the generic drug user-fee programme (GDUFA); check the current FDA figures.

### d. NDA vs ANDA

| Point | NDA | ANDA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Section | 505(b) | 505(j) |
| Clinical efficacy trials | Required | Not required |
| Non-clinical studies | Required | Not required |
| Bioequivalence | Not the main evidence | Main evidence |
| Cost and time | Very high, long | Lower, shorter |
| Labelling | Developed by sponsor | Same as RLD |
| Patent certification | Not applicable | Required |

## 2.5 Changes to an approved NDA / ANDA

After approval, the holder must tell the FDA about changes in components, manufacturing site, process, specifications, container closure, labelling and so on. **21 CFR 314.70** sets the categories for NDAs; **314.97** applies the same rules to ANDAs. The category depends on how likely the change is to affect the identity, strength, quality, purity or potency of the drug.

| Change | Filing | When the change may be used | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Major** | **Prior approval supplement (PAS)** | Only after FDA approves the supplement | Change in formulation (including inactive ingredients); change in sterilisation method; change in API synthesis that may alter the impurity profile; new indication (efficacy supplement) |
| **Moderate** | **Supplement – Changes Being Effected in 30 days (CBE-30)** | 30 days after FDA receives it, unless FDA objects | Container closure change not affecting quality; relaxing a specification to match an official compendium |
| **Moderate (FDA-designated)** | **Supplement – Changes Being Effected (CBE-0)** | On receipt by FDA | Adding a specification or tightening controls; strengthening a safety warning in labelling |
| **Minor** | **Annual report** | Immediately; reported in the next annual report | Changes to comply with a compendium; replacing equipment with equipment of the same design and principle; deleting a colouring-only ingredient |

FDA guidance documents (for example the SUPAC guidances for immediate-release, modified-release and semisolid products) explain which change falls in which level and what data (dissolution, stability, BE) are needed.

## Part B: Regulatory authorities and agencies

## 2.6 India — CDSCO

**Law:** Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940; Drugs Rules, 1945; New Drugs and Clinical Trials (NDCT) Rules, 2019; Medical Devices Rules, 2017.

**Organisation:**

```text
Ministry of Health and Family Welfare
        └── Directorate General of Health Services
               └── CDSCO, headed by the Drugs Controller General of India (DCGI)
                    = Central Licensing Authority (CLA)
                    ├── HQ divisions: new drugs, clinical trials, biologicals,
                    │   medical devices, import/registration, pharmacovigilance
                    ├── Zonal, sub-zonal and port offices
                    ├── Central drug testing laboratories
                    └── Subject Expert Committees (advise on applications)
Advisory: Drugs Technical Advisory Board (DTAB), Drugs Consultative Committee (DCC)
States: State Licensing Authorities — manufacturing and sale licences
```

**Types of applications (central):**

| Application | Under |
| --- | --- |
| Permission to conduct a clinical trial of a new drug / investigational new drug (application Form CT-04, permission Form CT-06) | NDCT Rules 2019 |
| Permission to import a new drug for sale (Form CT-18) or manufacture a new drug formulation for sale (Form CT-21) | NDCT Rules 2019 |
| BA/BE study permission, ethics committee registration (Form CT-01, granted in CT-02) | NDCT Rules 2019 |
| Registration certificate and import licence for imported drugs | Drugs Rules 1945 |
| Test licence (small quantities for testing or trials) | Drugs Rules / NDCT Rules |
| Joint approval of licences for vaccines, blood products, large-volume parenterals, r-DNA products | Drugs Rules 1945 |

**Timelines (NDCT Rules 2019):** decision on a clinical trial application within **90 working days**; for a drug discovered and developed in India and to be manufactured in India, **30 working days**, after which permission is deemed granted if there is no communication. Applications are filed online through CDSCO's **SUGAM** portal.

## 2.7 United States — FDA

**Law:** Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (1938, with later amendments); Public Health Service Act (biologics); regulations in Title 21 of the Code of Federal Regulations.

**Organisation:** the FDA is an agency of the Department of Health and Human Services, headed by the Commissioner of Food and Drugs, with headquarters at Silver Spring, Maryland.

| Centre | Regulates |
| --- | --- |
| CDER — Center for Drug Evaluation and Research | Prescription and OTC drugs, generics, most therapeutic biologics such as monoclonal antibodies |
| CBER — Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research | Vaccines, blood products, cell and gene therapies |
| CDRH — Center for Devices and Radiological Health | Medical devices |
| CVM — Center for Veterinary Medicine | Animal drugs and feeds |
| Others | Food, tobacco products; field staff for inspections |

Within CDER, the Office of New Drugs reviews NDAs and the Office of Generic Drugs reviews ANDAs. Independent **advisory committees** of outside experts give non-binding advice.

**Types of applications:** IND, NDA (505(b)(1), 505(b)(2)), ANDA, BLA (including biosimilars), OTC monograph route, supplements, Drug Master Files (Unit 3).

## 2.8 European Union — EMA and national authorities

**Organisation:** the **European Medicines Agency (EMA)**, based in Amsterdam, coordinates scientific evaluation for the EU. It works through scientific committees, mainly:

| Committee | Role |
| --- | --- |
| CHMP — Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use | Gives the opinion on human medicine applications |
| PRAC — Pharmacovigilance Risk Assessment Committee | Safety and risk management |
| CAT — Committee for Advanced Therapies | Gene, cell and tissue therapies |
| COMP — Committee for Orphan Medicinal Products | Orphan designations |
| PDCO — Paediatric Committee | Paediatric investigation plans |
| HMPC — Committee on Herbal Medicinal Products | Herbal medicines |

The **European Commission** grants the legally binding marketing authorisation. Each member state also has its own **national competent authority** (for example in Germany, France), which handles national procedures and clinical trial approvals.

**Procedures for marketing authorisation:**

| Procedure | Use | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Centralised | Compulsory for biotech products, advanced therapies, orphan drugs and new substances for certain diseases (such as cancer, diabetes, HIV); optional for other innovative products | One authorisation valid in all EU member states |
| Decentralised | Product not yet authorised in any member state; applied in several at once with one reference member state | National authorisations in each chosen state |
| Mutual recognition | Product already authorised in one member state; others recognise it | National authorisations |
| National | Product for one member state only | One national authorisation |

**Centralised timeline:** CHMP evaluation takes up to **210 active days** (the clock stops while the applicant answers questions); the European Commission then decides within **67 days** of receiving the CHMP opinion. An EU marketing authorisation is valid for five years and then must be renewed.

**Types of application (by legal basis):** full (stand-alone) application, generic, hybrid, biosimilar, well-established use (based on literature), fixed-combination, and informed-consent applications.

## 2.9 United Kingdom — MHRA

The **Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA)** is an executive agency of the Department of Health and Social Care. Since the UK left the EU it grants UK-wide marketing authorisations itself. The independent **Commission on Human Medicines** advises ministers and the MHRA. The MHRA also regulates clinical trials and medical devices, runs the **Yellow Card** scheme for reporting suspected adverse reactions, and controls GMP and GDP. Routes include national applications, and reliance routes that use decisions of other trusted regulators (check the MHRA website for the current options).

## 2.10 Australia — TGA

The **Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA)** is part of the Australian Government Department of Health and regulates under the **Therapeutic Goods Act 1989**. A product must be entered on the **Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG)** before supply.

| Type | Meaning | Label number |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registered medicines | Higher risk: prescription and many OTC medicines; full evaluation of quality, safety and efficacy | AUST R |
| Listed medicines | Low risk (many complementary medicines); only permitted low-risk ingredients and claims | AUST L |

Prescription medicine applications include new chemical entities, extensions of indications, and generic medicines. The TGA also takes part in work-sharing with other regulators (such as the Access Consortium with Canada, Singapore, Switzerland and the UK). Clinical trials are run under notification (CTN) or approval (CTA) schemes.

## 2.11 Japan — MHLW and PMDA

Japan has a two-tier system:

```text
Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) — grants approval, sets policy
        │ consults
Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (PMDA) — scientific review,
  GMP/GCP inspections, consultations, safety measures, relief services
```

**Law:** the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Act (PMD Act), which replaced the earlier Pharmaceutical Affairs Law. Advice is also given by the Pharmaceutical Affairs Council.

**Types of applications:** new drug applications (new active ingredient, new combination, new route, new indication, new dosage form), generic (follow-on) drug applications, OTC drugs and quasi-drugs. Clinical trial notifications go to the PMDA. Japan uses the CTD, with a Japan-specific Module 1. Foreign data may be accepted with a bridging approach (ICH E5) showing that the results apply to Japanese patients.

## 2.12 Canada — Health Canada

**Health Canada**, through its Health Products and Food Branch, regulates drugs under the **Food and Drugs Act and Food and Drug Regulations**. Within it, the Therapeutic Products Directorate (pharmaceuticals) and the Biologic and Radiopharmaceutical Drugs Directorate (biologics) review submissions.

| Submission | Use |
| --- | --- |
| New Drug Submission (NDS) | New drug, full data |
| Abbreviated New Drug Submission (ANDS) | Generic, comparison with a Canadian reference product |
| Supplemental NDS / ANDS | Changes such as new indication or new strength |
| Clinical Trial Application (CTA) | Permission for a clinical trial |
| DIN application | For products not classed as new drugs |

Approval of an NDS or ANDS gives a **Notice of Compliance (NOC)**, and the product receives a **Drug Identification Number (DIN)** that must appear on the label.

## 2.13 Comparison table

| Country / region | Authority | Main law | New drug application | Generic application | Approval document |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| India | CDSCO (DCGI) | D&C Act 1940; NDCT Rules 2019 | CT-18 / CT-21 with CTD | Application to CLA while still a "new drug"; else State licence | Permission from CLA; State licence |
| USA | FDA (CDER/CBER) | FD&C Act; 21 CFR | NDA / BLA | ANDA | Approval letter |
| EU | EMA + European Commission; national authorities | EU directives and regulations | MAA (centralised/decentralised/MRP/national) | Generic MAA | Marketing authorisation |
| UK | MHRA | Human Medicines Regulations | MAA | Generic MAA | Marketing authorisation |
| Australia | TGA | Therapeutic Goods Act 1989 | Registration on ARTG | Generic registration | ARTG entry (AUST R) |
| Japan | MHLW + PMDA | PMD Act | J-NDA | Generic application | MHLW approval |
| Canada | Health Canada | Food and Drugs Act | NDS | ANDS | NOC + DIN |

Global harmonisation bodies: **ICH** (International Council for Harmonisation — Q, S, E and M guidelines), **WHO** (guidelines, prequalification, certification scheme), **PIC/S** (GMP inspection co-operation).

## Remember

- IND: 21 CFR 312, Form 1571 (sponsor) and 1572 (investigator); four types; in effect 30 days after receipt unless clinical hold.
- NDA: 21 CFR 314, Form 356h; 505(b)(1), 505(b)(2); filing decision within 60 days; review goals 10 months standard, 6 months priority.
- ANDA: 505(j); same API, form, strength, route, use + BE; Paragraph I–IV; 30-month stay; 180-day exclusivity; tentative approval.
- Changes: major = PAS; moderate = CBE-30 or CBE-0; minor = annual report (314.70; 314.97 for ANDA).
- India: CDSCO/DCGI; CT-04 → CT-06; CT-18 import, CT-21 manufacture; 90 working days (30 for India-developed); SUGAM.
- USA: FDA under HHS; CDER, CBER, CDRH, CVM.
- EU: EMA (CHMP, PRAC, CAT, COMP, PDCO); Commission grants; centralised, decentralised, mutual recognition, national; 210 active days + 67 days.
- UK MHRA (Yellow Card); Australia TGA (ARTG, AUST R/AUST L); Japan MHLW + PMDA (PMD Act); Canada Health Canada (NDS, ANDS, NOC, DIN).

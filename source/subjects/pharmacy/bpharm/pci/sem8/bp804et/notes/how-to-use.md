# How to use this guide

Pharmaceutical Regulatory Science is **one of the semester-8 electives** of the PCI B.Pharm course. It has no practical; the whole subject is the 75-mark theory paper. It is a descriptive subject: there are no calculations, and marks come from clear definitions, well-ordered lists, flow charts of approval procedures and tables that compare applications or agencies. Study one unit a day. Read the notes, close them, and write each flow chart from memory (drug discovery and development, IND process, NDA review, ANDA with patent certifications, export procedure, CTD triangle, informed consent, safety reporting, Act → rules → guidance). Then answer that unit's questions and MCQs.

The five units follow the five units of the PCI syllabus exactly. Unit 2 is the largest; it is kept as one unit but split into Part A (IND, NDA, ANDA and post-approval changes) and Part B (regulatory authorities of India, USA, EU, UK, Australia, Japan and Canada). The syllabus names the UK only in its scope line, so the MHRA gets a short section. Several topics also appear in **BP702T Industrial Pharmacy II** (semester 7): IND, NDA, CTD, CDSCO and the NDCT Rules. The facts here match those notes, so revising one helps the other.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ questions | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

The question bank has long (10-mark), short (5-mark) and very short (2-mark) questions. The very short ones are good warm-ups and help with MCQs.

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with a flow chart or table.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from common patterns and syllabus weight, not a promise of what will be asked.

**Where the marks usually come from**

| Unit | Topics that keep coming back |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Drug discovery and development | Stages of drug discovery, pre-clinical studies, phases of clinical trials, innovator vs generic, generic product development, Hatch–Waxman Act |
| 2 Approval process and authorities | IND types, contents and 30-day rule; NDA contents and review; ANDA and Paragraph I–IV; changes to an approved NDA/ANDA (PAS, CBE-30, CBE-0, annual report); CDSCO, FDA, EMA procedures, TGA, PMDA, Health Canada |
| 3 Overseas registration | Export procedure from India, DMF (types, LoA), CTD modules, eCTD features, ACTD and CTD vs ACTD |
| 4 Clinical trials | Protocol contents, ethics committee formation and working, informed consent, GCP duties of investigator, sponsor and monitor, trial management, safety reporting |
| 5 Regulatory concepts | Act vs regulation vs guidance, Federal Register, CFR (Title 21 parts), Orange Book (TE codes), Purple Book |

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. Begin every answer with a one-line definition and expand every abbreviation the first time (IND, NDA, ANDA, CTD, eCTD, ACTD, DMF, CFR, GCP, IRB, SAE, SUSAR, CDSCO, EMA, PMDA, TGA).
2. Draw a flow chart for every procedure. Examiners give a large share of the marks to a neat, correct chart.
3. Use comparison tables: NDA vs ANDA, innovator vs generic, CTD vs ACTD, FR vs CFR, Orange vs Purple Book, Act vs rule vs guidance, the four EU procedures, the change categories.
4. Learn the small set of fixed facts exactly: IND 30 days; NDA filing 60 days, review goals 10 and 6 months; EU 210 active days + 67 days; India 90 and 30 working days; Paragraph I–IV, 30-month stay, 180-day exclusivity; CTD five modules; ACTD four parts; DMF Types II–V; Indian ethics committee at least 7 members; SAE 24 hours and 14 days; ICH 7 and 15 days; 21 CFR 50, 56, 58, 211, 312, 314, 320.
5. Always name the authority and the law together (for example "CDSCO, under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940 and the NDCT Rules 2019"; "FDA, under 21 CFR 312").
6. Forms, fees and timelines are changed by amendments. Write what the notes give for the exam, and add "as per current rules" where suitable; in real work check the regulator's website.
7. End long answers with advantages, significance or a short conclusion.

# How to use this guide

Industrial Pharmacy I is a descriptive subject: most marks come from well-organised lists, flow charts and tables of excipients, methods, defects and tests. Study one unit a day. Read the notes, then close them and write out each flow chart (wet granulation, sugar coating, capsule shell making, parenteral production, lyophilisation, aerosol filling) from memory. Finish with the question bank and the MCQs for that unit.

The notes follow the five units of the PCI syllabus exactly. Unit 2 is the largest (tablets plus liquid orals) and is split into Part A and Part B inside one file; Unit 4 (parenterals and ophthalmics) and Unit 5 (cosmetics, aerosols and packaging) are also in parts. No unit has been merged or moved.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

The question bank has long (10-mark), short (5-mark) and very short (2-mark) questions. The very short ones are good warm-ups and help with MCQs.

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with a flow chart or table.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from common patterns and syllabus weight, not a promise of what will be asked.

**Where the marks usually come from**

| Unit | Topics that keep coming back |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Preformulation | Goals of preformulation, polymorphism, flow properties (angle of repose, Carr's index), solubility and partition coefficient, hydrolysis and oxidation, BCS |
| 2 Tablets and liquid orals | Granulation methods, tablet excipients, processing problems (capping, picking), coating types and defects, IP tests (weight variation, friability, disintegration, dissolution), syrups, suspensions and emulsions |
| 3 Capsules and pellets | Hard shell manufacture, capsule sizes and filling machines, soft gelatin capsules (rotary die, base adsorption, minim/gram factor), capsule QC, pelletisation |
| 4 Parenterals and ophthalmics | Types and requirements of parenterals, vehicles and additives, production area and clean rooms, lyophilisation, containers, QC tests (sterility, pyrogen, LAL, particulate, leaker), eye drops and ointments |
| 5 Cosmetics, aerosols, packaging | Lipstick, shampoo, cold and vanishing creams, toothpaste, hair dyes, sunscreens; aerosol components, propellants, filling and evaluation; packaging materials, interactions and glass tests |

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. Open every answer with a one-line definition, then go to a numbered list; examiners in this subject reward structure.
2. Draw a flow chart for any process (granulation, coating, shell manufacture, rotary die, parenteral production, lyophilisation, pelletisation, aerosol filling). A neat chart can carry half the marks.
3. Use tables for excipients (class, purpose, examples) and for defects (defect, cause, remedy). Give two examples for every class.
4. Learn the official numbers exactly: IP weight variation for tablets (10, 7.5, 5%) and capsules (10, 7.5%), friability (100 revolutions, not more than 1%), disintegration times (15, 30, 60, 3, 5 minutes; enteric 2 h in acid), autoclave 121 °C for 15 minutes, 0.9% NaCl isotonic, sterility test media and temperatures, particulate limits for LVPs.
5. For a "write a note on" question, finish with uses or significance; for a "formulation" question, give a typical ingredient list with the role of each.
6. Compare-type questions (wet vs dry granulation, sugar vs film coating, hard vs soft capsules, flocculated vs deflocculated, cold vs vanishing cream, cold vs pressure filling) are best answered as a table.
7. Practise the small calculations: Carr's index and Hausner ratio, capsule size from fill weight and density, friability %, tonicity adjustment, base adsorption and minim/gram factor.

# Preparation of ascorbic acid injection

**Aim:** To prepare and submit ascorbic acid injection (for example 500 mg in 5 ml, i.e. 10% w/v) in ampoules.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, gloves and a lab coat.

## Principle

Ascorbic acid (vitamin C) is strongly acidic in solution, which is painful on injection, so part of it is converted into sodium ascorbate with sodium bicarbonate to raise the pH. Carbon dioxide is released in this reaction. Ascorbic acid oxidises easily, especially in the presence of air and metal ions, so the solution is prepared with WFI freed from dissolved oxygen, a chelating agent (disodium edetate) and sometimes an antioxidant are added, and the ampoules are flushed and filled under nitrogen. The solution is sterilised as directed; filtration through a 0.22 µm membrane with aseptic filling is the gentler method for this oxidation-prone drug.

## Formula (typical, for 100 ml)

| Ingredient | Quantity | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ascorbic acid | 10 g | Drug |
| Sodium bicarbonate | enough to bring pH to the monograph range (several grams) | pH adjustment (forms sodium ascorbate) |
| Disodium edetate | small quantity (e.g. about 0.01–0.05 g) | Chelating agent |
| Water for Injections (boiled, cooled under nitrogen) | to 100 ml | Vehicle |
| Nitrogen gas | q.s. | Displaces oxygen |

## Requirements

Ascorbic acid IP, sodium bicarbonate, disodium edetate, WFI, nitrogen cylinder with sterile filter on the line, pH meter, membrane filtration assembly (0.22 µm), sterile ampoules, burner, laminar airflow bench if available, amber ampoules or light-protective cartons.

## Procedure

1. Boil WFI and cool it while bubbling nitrogen through it.
2. Dissolve disodium edetate and ascorbic acid in about 80 ml of this water under a nitrogen stream.
3. Add sodium bicarbonate slowly in small portions with gentle stirring, letting the CO2 escape (avoid frothing over).
4. Check pH and adjust within the range in the monograph; make up to 100 ml with nitrogen-saturated WFI.
5. Filter through a sterile 0.22 µm membrane into a sterile receiver.
6. Flush sterile ampoules with nitrogen, fill aseptically (5 ml + overfill), flush the headspace with nitrogen and seal at once.
7. Leaker test, clarity check, label and store protected from light.

## Calculations

**Worked example (stoichiometry, illustrative).** Ascorbic acid (C6H8O6, Mr 176.12) reacts with NaHCO3 (Mr 84.01) 1 : 1. To neutralise 1.0 g of ascorbic acid completely would need 84.01/176.12 = 0.477 g NaHCO3. Because only part of the acid is neutralised to reach the target pH, the amount used is found by titrating with a pH meter, not by full calculation.

**Batch:** 10 ampoules × 5 ml with 0.25 ml overfill = 52.5 ml; with about 15% loss allowance, prepare 60 ml → ascorbic acid 6.0 g.

## Result

Ascorbic acid injection (10% w/v) was prepared under nitrogen, sterilised by filtration, filled into ___ ampoules of 5 ml and found clear and colourless.

## Precautions

1. Keep oxygen out at every step (nitrogen-saturated water, nitrogen flushing).
2. Use metal-free equipment where possible; add edetate to bind metal ions.
3. Add bicarbonate slowly because of effervescence.
4. Protect from light; discard yellow or brown solutions (oxidation).

## Viva questions

1. **Why is sodium bicarbonate added?** To raise pH and reduce pain by forming sodium ascorbate.
2. **Why is nitrogen used?** To displace oxygen and prevent oxidation.
3. **Role of disodium edetate?** Chelates metal ions that catalyse oxidation.
4. **Sign of degradation?** Yellow to brown colour.
5. **Why filtration rather than heat?** Heat speeds oxidation of ascorbic acid.
6. **Pore size of sterilising filter?** 0.22 µm.

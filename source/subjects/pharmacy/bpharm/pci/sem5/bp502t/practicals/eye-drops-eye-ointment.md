# Preparation of eye drops and eye ointment

**Aim:** To prepare and submit (a) chloramphenicol eye drops 0.5% w/v and (b) chloramphenicol eye ointment 1% w/w (or other preparations given by your teacher).

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, gloves and a lab coat. Work aseptically, ideally under a laminar airflow bench.

## Principle

Eye preparations must be sterile, free of irritating particles, close to isotonic and at a tolerable pH. Multi-dose drops contain a preservative. Chloramphenicol loses activity on prolonged heating, so the drops are sterilised by filtration and filled aseptically. The eye ointment base (soft paraffin, liquid paraffin and wool fat) is sterilised by dry heat and the drug is incorporated aseptically.

## Formulae (typical)

**(a) Chloramphenicol eye drops**

| Ingredient | Quantity for 100 ml | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chloramphenicol | 0.5 g | Drug |
| Boric acid | about 1.5 g | Buffer, tonicity |
| Borax | about 0.3 g | Buffer |
| Phenylmercuric nitrate | about 0.002 g | Preservative |
| Purified water (sterile) | to 100 ml | Vehicle |

**(b) Chloramphenicol eye ointment**

| Ingredient | Quantity for 100 g | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chloramphenicol (very fine powder) | 1 g | Drug |
| Liquid paraffin | about 10 g | Softens base, levigating agent |
| Wool fat | about 10 g | Emollient, takes up water |
| Yellow soft paraffin | to 100 g | Base |

## Requirements

Ingredients, beakers, water bath, 0.22 µm membrane filter assembly, sterile dropper bottles, hot-air oven, sterile ointment slab and spatula (or mortar), sterile eye-ointment tubes, laminar airflow bench.

## Procedure

**(a) Eye drops**

1. Dissolve boric acid, borax and phenylmercuric nitrate in about 90 ml of hot purified water; cool to about 60 °C.
2. Add chloramphenicol and stir until dissolved; cool and make up to volume.
3. Filter through a sterile 0.22 µm membrane into a sterile receiver.
4. Fill aseptically (for example 10 ml) into sterile dropper bottles; close at once.

**(b) Eye ointment**

5. Melt the wool fat, liquid paraffin and soft paraffin together; filter hot through coarse filter paper or muslin.
6. Sterilise the base by dry heat (for example 160 °C for 2 hours) in a covered container.
7. Levigate the sterile, very finely powdered chloramphenicol with a little sterile liquid paraffin or base on a sterile slab, then add the remaining cooled base by geometric dilution.
8. Fill into sterile eye-ointment tubes aseptically; close and crimp.

## Calculations

**Tonicity check (worked example with specimen values).** Suppose the sodium chloride equivalents are boric acid E = 0.50 and chloramphenicol E = 0.10 (use the values in your reference table).
NaCl-equivalent of 100 ml = (1.5 × 0.50) + (0.5 × 0.10) + (borax contribution, ignored here) ≈ 0.80 g → about 0.8% NaCl equivalent, which is close to isotonic (0.9%) and well tolerated by the eye.

**Ointment for 20 tubes of 4 g with 10% excess:** 88 g total → chloramphenicol 0.88 g, liquid paraffin 8.8 g, wool fat 8.8 g, yellow soft paraffin 69.5 g.

## Evaluation

Clarity of drops (black and white backgrounds); pH; sterility (if facilities allow); fill volume; for the ointment, homogeneity and absence of gritty particles, leakage of tubes, metal particles test (IP).

## Labels

Eye drops: "Sterile. For external use only. Do not use more than 4 weeks after first opening. Store in a refrigerator if stated by the monograph. Protect from light." Eye ointment: "Sterile eye ointment. For external use only."

## Precautions

1. Work aseptically with sterile containers and tools.
2. Do not heat chloramphenicol drops for long (loss of potency).
3. Use very finely powdered drug in the ointment to avoid eye irritation.
4. Close containers immediately after filling.

## Viva questions

1. **Why must eye preparations be sterile?** The eye has weak defences; infection (e.g. Pseudomonas) can cause loss of sight.
2. **Role of boric acid–borax?** Buffer and tonicity adjustment.
3. **Why add a viscosity agent to some drops?** To prolong contact time with the eye.
4. **Why wool fat in eye ointment base?** It is emollient and helps take up water from tears.
5. **How is the ointment base sterilised?** By dry heat.
6. **Common preservative in eye drops?** Benzalkonium chloride (here phenylmercuric nitrate).

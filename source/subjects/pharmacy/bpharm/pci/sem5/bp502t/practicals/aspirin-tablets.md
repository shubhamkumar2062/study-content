# Preparation and evaluation of aspirin tablets

**Aim:** To prepare aspirin tablets (300 or 325 mg) and evaluate them, including a limit check for free salicylic acid.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles and a lab coat.

## Principle

Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) hydrolyses in the presence of moisture and heat to salicylic acid and acetic acid (vinegar odour). Wet granulation with water is therefore avoided; tablets are made by **dry granulation (slugging)** or direct compression, or by non-aqueous granulation. Alkaline lubricants such as magnesium stearate speed hydrolysis, so **talc** or stearic acid is preferred. Starch acts as disintegrant.

## Formula (typical, per tablet)

| Ingredient | Quantity | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aspirin (crystalline) | 300 mg | Drug |
| Starch (dried) | about 30–40 mg | Disintegrant/diluent |
| Talc | about 10–15 mg | Lubricant/glidant |
| (Optional) microcrystalline cellulose | q.s. | Dry binder, aids compression |

## Requirements

Dried starch, sieves, heavy tablet press with flat punches for slugging, regular punches for final tablets, balance, evaluation equipment (as for paracetamol), ferric chloride solution, test tubes.

## Procedure (slugging method)

1. Dry the starch at about 105 °C and cool in a desiccator.
2. Mix aspirin with half the starch (and MCC if used) and part of the talc.
3. Compress into large, flat slugs on the press at high pressure.
4. Break the slugs in a mortar and pass through a 12/16 mesh sieve to get granules.
5. Add the remaining starch and talc; blend gently.
6. Compress into tablets of the calculated weight.
7. Evaluate and pack in a tightly closed container with desiccant, protected from moisture.

## Evaluation

As for any uncoated tablet: appearance, weight variation, thickness, hardness, friability, disintegration (15 minutes), and dissolution/assay. Also:

**Test for free salicylic acid (limit check by colour):** shake powdered tablets with water and filter; add dilute ferric chloride. Salicylic acid gives a violet colour; compare with a standard containing the permitted amount (as in the monograph). An intense violet colour shows hydrolysis.

**Worked example: uniformity of weight (specimen data).** 20 tablets weigh 7.00 g → average 350 mg (≥250 mg → 5% limit). Range 332.5–367.5 mg; twice the limit 315–385 mg. Readings: one tablet at 330 mg, all others within range → passes.

## Precautions

1. Keep everything dry; work quickly in low humidity.
2. Do not use magnesium stearate or other alkaline excipients.
3. Do not heat aspirin.
4. Store in a tightly closed container with desiccant.

## Viva questions

1. **Why not wet-granulate aspirin with water?** It hydrolyses in moisture and heat.
2. **Why is magnesium stearate avoided?** Its alkalinity catalyses hydrolysis.
3. **What does a vinegar smell from aspirin tablets mean?** Hydrolysis has released acetic acid.
4. **What is slugging?** Dry granulation: large tablets (slugs) are compressed and broken into granules.
5. **Test for salicylic acid?** Violet colour with ferric chloride.
6. **Other ways to make aspirin tablets?** Direct compression; roller compaction; non-aqueous granulation.

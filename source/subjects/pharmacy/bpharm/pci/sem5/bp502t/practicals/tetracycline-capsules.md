# Preparation and evaluation of tetracycline capsules

**Aim:** To prepare and submit tetracycline hydrochloride capsules (250 mg) in hard gelatin capsules and evaluate them.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear gloves, goggles and a lab coat; tetracycline is an antibiotic powder, so avoid inhaling dust.

## Principle

The drug is blended with a diluent, glidant and lubricant to give a free-flowing powder, which is filled into hard gelatin capsules of a size chosen from the fill weight and the tapped density of the blend. Calcium-containing diluents (dicalcium phosphate, calcium carbonate) are **not** used because tetracycline forms chelates with calcium and is then poorly absorbed; lactose or starch is used instead.

## Formula (typical, per capsule)

| Ingredient | Quantity | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tetracycline hydrochloride | 250 mg | Drug |
| Lactose (or starch) | q.s. to fill weight | Diluent |
| Colloidal silicon dioxide or talc | about 0.5–1% | Glidant |
| Magnesium stearate | about 0.5–1% | Lubricant |

## Requirements

Mortar and pestle or blender, sieve (about 60–80 mesh), balance, empty hard gelatin capsules (size 1 or 0), hand-operated capsule filling machine (or tile and spatula), measuring cylinder for tapped density, disintegration apparatus with discs.

## Procedure

1. Determine the tapped density of the drug–diluent blend (see calculation).
2. Choose the capsule size and calculate the diluent needed to fill it.
3. Sift drug and diluent; mix by geometric dilution; add glidant and finally lubricant; mix briefly.
4. Place empty capsules in the filling machine, separate caps, spread the powder evenly over the bodies and level it; tamp lightly; repeat until filled.
5. Replace caps and lock; remove filled capsules.
6. Clean the outside by rolling in a cloth (or tumbling with salt crystals) to remove adhering powder.
7. Evaluate and pack in a well-closed, light-resistant container.

## Calculations

Fill volume needed = fill weight ÷ tapped density.

**Worked example (specimen readings).** Drug blend tapped density = 0.75 g/ml. A size 1 capsule holds about 0.50 ml → can hold 0.50 × 0.75 = 0.375 g = **375 mg**. Drug 250 mg + lubricant/glidant about 4 mg → diluent ≈ 375 − 254 = **121 mg** per capsule. For 50 capsules plus 10% excess for losses (55 capsules): drug 13.75 g, lactose 6.66 g, magnesium stearate and glidant about 0.11 g each.

## Evaluation

| Test | Method | Specimen reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Appearance | Colour, locking, no dents or powder outside | |
| Uniformity of weight | 20 capsules: weigh intact, empty, weigh shells, find contents | see example |
| Disintegration | IP apparatus with discs, water 37 °C | e.g. 8 min (usual limit 30 min) |
| Assay / dissolution | As per monograph | |

**Worked example: uniformity of weight (specimen data).** Average content weight = 372 mg (≥300 mg → 7.5% limit). Range 344.1–399.9 mg; twice the limit (15%) 316.2–427.8 mg. If not more than two contents fall outside the first range and none outside the second, the batch passes.

## Precautions

1. Do not use calcium salts as diluent.
2. Protect from light and moisture (tetracycline degrades to toxic epi- and anhydro- products).
3. Fill uniformly; level the powder before closing.
4. Handle shells with dry, gloved hands.

## Viva questions

1. **Why not dicalcium phosphate as diluent?** Calcium chelates tetracycline and reduces absorption.
2. **How is capsule size chosen?** From fill weight and tapped density of the blend.
3. **Volume of size 0 and 1 capsules?** About 0.68 ml and 0.50 ml.
4. **IP weight variation for capsules?** 10% if less than 300 mg, 7.5% if 300 mg or more.
5. **Why should tetracycline be protected from light and moisture?** Degradation products are less active and can be toxic.
6. **Name automatic capsule filling principles.** Auger, dosator, dosing disc (tamping).

# Film coating of tablets or granules

**Aim:** To film-coat the given tablets (or granules) with a hydroxypropyl methylcellulose (HPMC) film and to evaluate the coated product.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles and a lab coat. Use organic solvents only in a well-ventilated area away from flames.

## Principle

A thin polymer film is sprayed onto tablets rotating in a coating pan while warm air evaporates the solvent. The film masks taste, protects from moisture and light, and improves appearance. A coating solution contains a film-forming polymer, a plasticiser, a solvent and a colourant/opacifier.

## Formula of coating solution (typical, about 100 ml)

| Ingredient | Quantity | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HPMC (low viscosity grade) | about 4–6 g | Film former |
| Polyethylene glycol 400 or 6000 | about 0.5–1 g | Plasticiser |
| Titanium dioxide | about 0.5 g | Opacifier |
| Colour (lake or iron oxide) | q.s. | Colour |
| Purified water (or ethanol–water) | to 100 ml | Solvent |

(For an enteric coat, the teacher may use cellulose acetate phthalate in acetone–ethanol with diethyl phthalate as plasticiser.)

## Requirements

Laboratory coating pan with hot-air blower, spray gun with compressor (or atomiser), core tablets (pre-dusted), beakers, stirrer, sieve, balance, disintegration apparatus.

## Procedure

1. Disperse HPMC in part of hot water, then add the rest as cold water with stirring until clear; add PEG.
2. Disperse titanium dioxide and colour in a little of the solution (homogenise or grind) and add to the bulk; pass through a fine sieve.
3. Weigh 20 core tablets (W1) and record their appearance.
4. Load de-dusted tablets into the pan; warm them with hot air while rotating slowly.
5. Spray the coating solution in short bursts, allowing each layer to dry before the next; adjust pan speed, spray rate and air temperature to avoid sticking or roughness.
6. Continue until the required weight gain (commonly about 2–4%) is reached.
7. Dry the coated tablets in the pan for some minutes with warm air, then cool.
8. Weigh 20 coated tablets (W2) and evaluate.

## Observations and calculations

% weight gain = 100 × (W2 − W1)/W1

**Worked example (specimen readings).** 20 cores = 6.000 g; 20 coated = 6.180 g. Weight gain = 100 × 0.180/6.000 = **3.0%**.

## Precautions

1. De-dust core tablets first.
2. Do not over-wet the bed: spray slowly and let each layer dry, or tablets will stick (picking, twinning).
3. Do not over-heat (blistering, blushing).
4. Keep the spray gun clean and the solution stirred.

## Viva questions

1. **Role of plasticiser?** Makes the film flexible and prevents cracking.
2. **Name two film formers.** HPMC and ethylcellulose (also methacrylate copolymers).
3. **What is orange peel?** Rough coat from a viscous solution drying before it spreads.
4. **Enteric coating polymers?** CAP, HPMCP, Eudragit L/S, shellac.
5. **Advantages of film over sugar coating?** Faster, thinner, little weight gain, keeps logos visible.
6. **IP disintegration time for film-coated tablets?** 30 minutes.

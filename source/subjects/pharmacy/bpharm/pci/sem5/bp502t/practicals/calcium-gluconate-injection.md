# Preparation of calcium gluconate injection

**Aim:** To prepare and submit calcium gluconate injection (10% w/v) in ampoules.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, gloves and a lab coat. Work in the cleanest area available and handle hot ampoules and flames with care.

## Principle

Calcium gluconate is used in calcium deficiency (hypocalcaemia, tetany). Its solubility in cold water is low, so a 10% w/v solution is supersaturated and tends to crystallise on standing. A small quantity of a more soluble calcium salt, usually calcium D-saccharate, is included as a stabiliser (within the proportion allowed by the monograph). The solution is made with Water for Injections, filtered, filled into ampoules, sealed and sterilised by autoclaving.

## Formula (typical)

| Ingredient | Quantity for 100 ml | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calcium gluconate | 10 g | Drug |
| Calcium D-saccharate | small quantity as permitted | Stabiliser (prevents crystallisation) |
| Water for Injections | to 100 ml | Vehicle |

## Requirements

Calcium gluconate IP, stabiliser, Water for Injections, beakers, glass rod, hot plate, sintered glass filter or membrane filter assembly, 10 ml ampoules (cleaned and dry-heat sterilised), syringe or burette for filling, gas–oxygen burner for sealing, autoclave, methylene blue solution for leaker test, black and white inspection boards.

## Procedure

1. Clean ampoules: wash with water and detergent, rinse with purified water and then WFI, dry and sterilise by dry heat.
2. Heat about 80 ml of WFI nearly to boiling; dissolve the stabiliser and then calcium gluconate with stirring.
3. Make up to 100 ml with hot WFI.
4. Filter while hot through a sintered glass or membrane filter to get a clear solution.
5. Fill 10 ml (plus a small overfill) into each ampoule using a syringe with a long needle, keeping the neck dry.
6. Seal by pull sealing (or tip sealing) in the flame.
7. Sterilise in the autoclave at 121 °C for 15 minutes.
8. Carry out the leaker test, clarity test and label.

## Calculations

**Worked example (batch calculation).** Required: 10 ampoules × 10 ml. Add overfill and loss allowance (say 10 ampoules × 10.5 ml = 105 ml, plus about 15% for filter and transfer losses ≈ 120 ml). Calcium gluconate needed = 10 g per 100 ml × 120 ml = **12 g** in 120 ml of solution.

## Result

Calcium gluconate injection 10% w/v was prepared, filled into ___ ampoules of 10 ml each, sterilised by autoclaving and found clear and free from leakers.

## Label

Calcium Gluconate Injection IP, 10% w/v, 10 ml; for intravenous use (slow injection); batch number; date of preparation; storage: do not refrigerate (crystallisation); discard if crystals are seen.

## Precautions

1. Use freshly prepared WFI and sterile, pyrogen-free glassware.
2. Dissolve with heat; filter hot to avoid crystallisation.
3. Do not wet the ampoule neck while filling (charring on sealing).
4. Inspect every ampoule for particles after sterilisation.

## Viva questions

1. **Use of calcium gluconate injection?** Hypocalcaemia, tetany, some cases of hyperkalaemia and magnesium toxicity.
2. **Why is a stabiliser added?** A 10% solution is supersaturated and may crystallise.
3. **Why should it not be refrigerated?** Low temperature encourages crystallisation.
4. **Method of sterilisation?** Autoclaving at 121 °C for 15 minutes.
5. **What is pull sealing?** Neck heated while rotating and the top pulled away, giving a strong seal.
6. **What is the leaker test?** Ampoules immersed in dye under vacuum; leaking ones take up dye.

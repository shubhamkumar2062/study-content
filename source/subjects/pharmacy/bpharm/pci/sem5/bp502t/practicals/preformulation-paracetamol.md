# Preformulation studies on paracetamol (or aspirin)

**Aim:** To carry out preformulation studies on paracetamol (or aspirin or any other drug given by your teacher): organoleptic properties, melting point, solubility, bulk and tapped density, flow properties (angle of repose, Carr's index, Hausner ratio) and loss on drying.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles and a lab coat. All numbers in the worked examples below are specimen readings for practice, not standard values.

## Principle

Preformulation describes the physical and chemical nature of a drug before it is formulated. Flow properties decide whether a powder can be compressed directly or must be granulated. Density gives an idea of packing and compressibility. Solubility and melting point help identify the drug and predict its behaviour.

- Angle of repose: tan θ = h/r
- Carr's index (%) = 100 × (ρt − ρb)/ρt
- Hausner ratio = ρt/ρb

## Requirements

Drug powder (paracetamol IP), funnel with stand, graph paper, 100 ml measuring cylinder, tapped density apparatus (or tapping by hand from a fixed height), balance, melting point apparatus with capillary tubes, test tubes, solvents (water, ethanol, chloroform, ether, 0.1 M HCl, 0.1 M NaOH), hot-air oven, weighing bottle, desiccator.

## Procedure

**A. Organoleptic properties**

1. Note colour, odour, taste (only if the teacher allows) and appearance under a hand lens.

**B. Melting point**

2. Fill a capillary to about 3 mm with dry powder, place in the melting point apparatus and heat slowly near the expected range. Record the temperature range from first melting to complete melting.

**C. Solubility**

3. Add about 10 mg of drug to 1 ml of each solvent, shake, and add solvent in portions until it dissolves. Express the result using the pharmacopoeial descriptive terms.

**D. Bulk and tapped density**

4. Weigh about 25 g (W) of powder, pour gently through a funnel into a 100 ml cylinder and read the bulk volume (Vb).
5. Tap the cylinder (mechanically or by hand from a fixed height) until the volume does not change; read tapped volume (Vt).

**E. Angle of repose (fixed funnel method)**

6. Fix a funnel so its tip is 2 cm above graph paper. Pour powder until the heap just touches the tip.
7. Mark the base circle, measure the diameter at several points and find the radius r; h is the height of the heap.

**F. Loss on drying**

8. Weigh about 1 g in a dried, weighed bottle; dry at 105 °C (or as per the monograph) to constant weight; cool in a desiccator and weigh.

**Worked example (specimen readings).** W = 25 g, Vb = 62.5 ml, Vt = 50 ml. ρb = 0.40 g/ml; ρt = 0.50 g/ml. Carr's index = 100 × (0.50 − 0.40)/0.50 = **20%** (fair). Hausner ratio = 0.50/0.40 = **1.25** (fair). Heap h = 2.0 cm, r = 2.6 cm → tan θ = 0.77, θ ≈ **37.6°** (fair).

**Interpretation:** a powder with fair or poorer flow is usually wet-granulated before compression. Paracetamol crystals flow and compress poorly, which is why it is normally granulated.

Loss on drying (%) = 100 × (weight before − weight after)/weight before.

## Precautions

1. Pour powder gently for bulk volume; do not shake or press it.
2. Keep the funnel height fixed and avoid vibration while forming the heap.
3. Heat slowly (about 1–2 °C per minute) near the melting point.
4. Use dry powder and dry glassware.

## Viva questions

1. **What is preformulation?** Study of drug properties before formulation to design a stable, effective dosage form.
2. **What does a high Carr's index mean?** A more compressible, more cohesive powder with poorer flow.
3. **How can flow be improved?** Granulation, adding a glidant (colloidal silica), larger or more spherical particles, drying.
4. **Why does paracetamol need granulation?** It flows and compresses poorly (tablets cap easily).
5. **What is the difference between bulk and tapped density?** Bulk includes air spaces of loose packing; tapped is after the powder settles by tapping.
6. **Name two instrumental methods for polymorphs.** X-ray powder diffraction and differential scanning calorimetry.

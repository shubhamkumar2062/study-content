# Preparation and evaluation of paracetamol tablets

**Aim:** To prepare and submit paracetamol tablets (500 mg) by wet granulation and to evaluate them.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles and a lab coat.

## Principle

Paracetamol crystals flow poorly and do not bind well on compression, so tablets made by direct compression often cap. Wet granulation with starch paste gives free-flowing, compressible granules. Starch also acts as a disintegrant; magnesium stearate and talc lubricate and help flow.

## Formula (typical, for 100 tablets of about 600 mg)

| Ingredient | Per tablet | For 100 tablets | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paracetamol | 500 mg | 50.0 g | Drug |
| Starch (as 10% paste) | about 25 mg | about 2.5 g | Binder |
| Starch (dry, disintegrant) | about 40 mg | about 4.0 g | Disintegrant |
| Lactose or microcrystalline cellulose | to adjust | q.s. | Diluent |
| Talc | about 1% | about 0.6 g | Glidant/antiadherent |
| Magnesium stearate | about 0.5–1% | about 0.3–0.6 g | Lubricant |

## Requirements

Mortar and pestle or planetary mixer, sieves (about 10/12 and 16/22 mesh), tray dryer or hot-air oven, single punch or rotary tablet press with 12–13 mm punches, balance, beakers, water bath; for evaluation: vernier calliper, hardness tester, friabilator, disintegration test apparatus, dissolution apparatus, UV spectrophotometer.

## Procedure

1. Pass paracetamol, half of the dry starch and the diluent through a sieve and mix well.
2. Prepare starch paste: disperse starch in a little cold water, add hot water with stirring and heat until translucent; cool.
3. Add paste gradually to the powder with mixing until a damp mass forms that holds together when squeezed and breaks cleanly (ball test).
4. Pass the wet mass through a 10/12 mesh sieve.
5. Dry the granules at about 50–60 °C until moisture is low (check loss on drying, about 1–2%).
6. Pass the dried granules through a 16/22 mesh sieve.
7. Add the remaining starch (extragranular), talc and finally magnesium stearate; blend gently for a few minutes only (over-mixing the lubricant slows disintegration).
8. Evaluate the granules (bulk density, Carr's index, angle of repose).
9. Compress on the tablet press, adjusting fill weight and hardness.
10. Evaluate the tablets as below and pack in a well-closed container.

## Evaluation and observations

| Test | Method | Specimen reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Appearance | Visual | White, round, smooth |
| Thickness/diameter | Vernier calliper (10 tablets) | e.g. 4.8 mm / 12.7 mm |
| Uniformity of weight | 20 tablets weighed singly | see example |
| Hardness | Monsanto or similar (5 tablets) | e.g. 5–6 kg |
| Friability | 100 revolutions | see example |
| Disintegration | IP apparatus, water 37 °C | e.g. 6 min |
| Dissolution / assay | As per monograph, UV | |

**Worked example: uniformity of weight (specimen data).** Total of 20 tablets = 12.10 g → average 605 mg. Since average is ≥250 mg, the allowed deviation is 5% → range 574.8–635.3 mg; twice the limit (10%) → 544.5–665.5 mg. If no more than 2 tablets lie outside 574.8–635.3 mg and none outside 544.5–665.5 mg, the batch passes.

**Worked example: friability (specimen data).** 11 tablets (6.65 g) before; 6.62 g after 100 revolutions. Friability = 100 × (6.65 − 6.62)/6.65 = **0.45%** → passes (not more than 1%).

## Precautions

1. Do not over-wet the mass; it gives hard granules and slow disintegration.
2. Dry granules at moderate temperature to the right moisture; too dry granules cap.
3. Add magnesium stearate last and mix briefly.
4. Clean punches and dies before and after compression.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is paracetamol wet-granulated?** Poor flow and compressibility; it tends to cap.
2. **Role of starch paste?** Binder; dry starch acts as disintegrant.
3. **Why add part of the disintegrant after granulation?** Extragranular disintegrant breaks the tablet into granules quickly.
4. **What causes capping?** Trapped air, fines, too-dry granules, worn tooling, high speed.
5. **IP disintegration limit for uncoated tablets?** 15 minutes.
6. **Why is hardness not an official test?** It depends on tablet size and type; it is an in-process control.

# Preparation of cold cream and vanishing cream

**Aim:** To prepare and submit (a) a cold cream (beeswax–borax type) and (b) a vanishing cream (stearic acid type), and to compare them.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles and a lab coat. Potassium hydroxide is corrosive.

## Principle

**Cold cream** is mainly a water-in-oil emulsion. Borax reacts with the free fatty acids of beeswax to form a sodium soap in situ, which emulsifies the water in the oil phase. Evaporation of water gives a cooling effect.

**Vanishing cream** is an oil-in-water emulsion. Part of the stearic acid is saponified by an alkali (potassium hydroxide or triethanolamine) to form stearate soap, which emulsifies the remaining stearic acid. It spreads to an invisible, non-greasy film.

## Formulae (typical, for 100 g)

**(a) Cold cream**

| Ingredient | Quantity | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| White beeswax | about 16 g | Emulsifier source, stiffener |
| Liquid paraffin | about 50 g | Oil phase, emollient |
| Borax | about 1 g | Forms soap with beeswax acids |
| Purified water | to 100 g | Aqueous phase |
| Perfume, preservative | q.s. | |

**(b) Vanishing cream**

| Ingredient | Quantity | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stearic acid | about 18 g | Main oil phase, forms soap |
| Potassium hydroxide | about 0.8–1 g (or triethanolamine about 1–2 g) | Saponifies part of stearic acid |
| Glycerin | about 8 g | Humectant |
| Purified water | to 100 g | Aqueous phase |
| Perfume, preservative (e.g. methyl paraben) | q.s. | |

## Requirements

Beakers, water bath, thermometer, glass rod or mechanical stirrer, balance, porcelain dish, ointment jars.

## Procedure

**(a) Cold cream**

1. Melt beeswax and liquid paraffin together on a water bath at about 70–75 °C.
2. Dissolve borax (and preservative) in water and heat to the same temperature.
3. Add the aqueous phase slowly to the oil phase with continuous stirring.
4. Stir while the cream cools; add perfume at about 40 °C; continue stirring till it sets.
5. Fill into a jar.

**(b) Vanishing cream**

6. Melt stearic acid at about 75 °C.
7. Dissolve potassium hydroxide (or triethanolamine), glycerin and preservative in water; heat to 75 °C.
8. Add the aqueous phase to the melted stearic acid with constant stirring in one direction.
9. Keep stirring as it cools; a white, smooth cream with a pearly sheen forms; add perfume at about 40 °C.
10. Fill into a jar.

## Calculations

**Scaling (worked example).** To make 30 g of vanishing cream from the 100 g formula, multiply each quantity by 0.3: stearic acid 5.4 g, KOH 0.27 g, glycerin 2.4 g, water to 30 g.

**Emulsion type tests**

| Test | Cold cream | Vanishing cream |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dilution with water | Does not mix (w/o) | Mixes (o/w) |
| Water-soluble dye (amaranth) | Scattered coloured droplets | Uniform colour |
| Conductivity | Poor | Conducts |

## Evaluation

Appearance, colour, odour, texture, pH, spreadability, type of emulsion, washability, phase separation on standing or centrifuging.

## Result

Cold cream (w/o) and vanishing cream (o/w) were prepared and the emulsion type of each confirmed.

## Precautions

1. Both phases must be at the same temperature before mixing.
2. Add phases slowly with steady stirring; avoid beating in air.
3. Add perfume after cooling to about 40 °C to prevent loss.
4. Handle KOH with care.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is cold cream cooling?** Water evaporates from the skin.
2. **What emulsifies cold cream?** Sodium soap formed from borax and beeswax fatty acids.
3. **Why is vanishing cream non-greasy?** It is o/w and leaves a thin stearic acid film.
4. **Role of glycerin?** Humectant.
5. **What gives the pearly sheen?** Crystals of stearic acid.
6. **How to test emulsion type?** Dilution, dye and conductivity tests.

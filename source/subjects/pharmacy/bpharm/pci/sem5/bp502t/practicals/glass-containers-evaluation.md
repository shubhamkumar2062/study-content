# Evaluation of glass containers (as per IP)

**Aim:** To evaluate the given glass containers by tests of the Indian Pharmacopoeia: hydrolytic resistance (powdered glass test and water attack test) and related checks, and to identify the type of glass.

> Quantities and procedure details are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and the current IP for exact quantities, strengths of titrant and limits. Wear goggles, gloves and a lab coat; crushed glass is sharp.

## Principle

Glass in contact with water releases alkali (mainly sodium), which raises pH and can degrade drugs. The amount of alkali released under standard autoclaving conditions is measured by titration with dilute acid using methyl red, and the result is compared with IP limits to decide the glass type:

- **Powdered glass test** — on crushed glass of a fixed size; tests the bulk glass; used to tell Type I from Type III.
- **Water attack test** — on whole containers filled with water; tests the treated inner surface; used for Type II.

## Requirements

Containers to be tested, mortar and pestle (hardened steel) or glass crusher, sieves (as specified in the IP), acetone, magnet (to remove iron from crushing), conical flasks of resistant glass, autoclave, burette, standard dilute acid (strength as in IP), methyl red solution, purified water (carbon dioxide-free), balance.

## Procedure

**A. Powdered glass test**

1. Rinse and dry the containers; crush pieces in the mortar and sieve to collect the size fraction specified in the IP.
2. Wash the grains with acetone to remove fine dust; dry; remove iron particles with a magnet.
3. Weigh the specified amount (for example 10 g) into a flask, add the specified volume of purified water, cover and autoclave at 121 °C for the specified time.
4. Cool, decant the water, wash the grains and titrate the combined liquid with the standard acid using methyl red.
5. Do a blank titration with water alone and subtract.

**B. Water attack test (for Type II)**

6. Rinse containers with purified water; fill to about 90% of capacity with CO2-free purified water.
7. Cover and autoclave at 121 °C for the specified time; cool.
8. Pool the contents, take a measured volume and titrate with the standard acid using methyl red; subtract a blank.

**C. Other checks (where facilities allow)**

9. Visual inspection for cracks, bubbles, stones and uneven wall thickness.
10. Light transmission test for amber (light-resistant) glass using a spectrophotometer.
11. Arsenic limit test for glass meant for aqueous parenteral preparations.
12. Thermal shock test (sudden transfer between hot and cold water) and check for annealing with a polariscope.

## Precautions

1. Remove fine dust with acetone so that results reflect true surface attack.
2. Use CO2-free water and resistant-glass flasks for the test.
3. Carry out a blank under identical conditions.
4. Handle crushed glass and hot autoclaved flasks carefully.

## Viva questions

1. **What does the powdered glass test measure?** Alkali released from the bulk of crushed glass under autoclaving.
2. **Which test is used for Type II glass and why?** Water attack test, because its resistance lies in the treated surface, which crushing would destroy.
3. **Composition of Type I glass?** Borosilicate glass.
4. **Why is amber glass used?** It cuts off light that degrades drugs.
5. **Why is the arsenic test done?** Arsenic compounds are used in glass making and could leach into injections.
6. **Indicator used in the titration?** Methyl red.

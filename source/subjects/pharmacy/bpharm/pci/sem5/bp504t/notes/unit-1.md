# Unit 1: Metabolic pathways in higher plants and their determination

Plants make two kinds of compounds. **Primary metabolites** (sugars, amino acids, fatty acids, nucleotides) are needed by every living cell for growth and energy. **Secondary metabolites** (alkaloids, glycosides, terpenoids, phenolics, flavonoids, tannins) are not essential for the life of the cell, are found only in some plants, and help the plant defend itself, attract pollinators or survive stress. Almost all plant drugs are secondary metabolites. This unit shows how they are built from a few starting materials through three main routes — the **shikimic acid pathway**, the **acetate pathways** and the **amino acid pathway** — and how radioactive tracers are used to prove these routes.

## 1.1 Primary and secondary metabolism

| Feature | Primary metabolites | Secondary metabolites |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Occurrence | In all plants and cells | In some species, organs or cells only |
| Role | Growth, energy, reproduction | Defence, attraction, protection, signalling |
| Examples | Glucose, sucrose, starch, amino acids, fatty acids | Morphine, digoxin, menthol, quercetin, tannins |
| Amount | Large | Usually small, varies with season and place |
| Medicinal value | Mostly nutritive or pharmaceutical aids | Most active drugs |

Secondary metabolism branches off from primary metabolism at a few key points:

```text
                    Photosynthesis (CO2 + H2O → glucose)
                                   |
                               Glucose
               ____________________|______________________
              |                    |                      |
   Pentose phosphate pathway   Glycolysis            Glycolysis
              |                    |                      |
   Erythrose-4-phosphate   Phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP)   Pyruvate → Acetyl-CoA
              |____________________|                      |
                       |                        ___________|____________
              SHIKIMIC ACID PATHWAY            |                        |
   (aromatic amino acids, phenylpropanoids,   Malonyl-CoA          Mevalonic acid
    lignans, coumarins, flavonoids*, tannins) (POLYKETIDES:        (TERPENOIDS, steroids,
                                              fatty acids,          carotenoids)
                                              anthraquinones)
                       Krebs cycle and other amino acids → ALKALOIDS
   * flavonoids are "mixed": ring B + C3 from shikimate, ring A from acetate
```

The three building blocks most often used are:

| Building block | Comes from | Gives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acetyl-CoA (C2 unit) | Pyruvate | Polyketides, fatty acids, terpenoids (via mevalonate) |
| Shikimic acid (C6–C3 unit after modification) | PEP + erythrose-4-phosphate | Aromatic amino acids, phenylpropanoids |
| Amino acids | Krebs cycle, glycolysis, shikimate | Alkaloids, cyanogenetic glycosides, glucosinolates |
| Isoprene unit (C5) | Mevalonate or MEP pathway | Terpenoids, steroids, carotenoids |

## 1.2 Shikimic acid pathway

The shikimic acid pathway makes the aromatic ring in plants and microorganisms. Animals do not have it, which is why phenylalanine and tryptophan are essential amino acids for humans and why the herbicide glyphosate (which blocks one enzyme of this pathway) is of low toxicity to animals.

**Steps**

1. **Phosphoenolpyruvate** (PEP, from glycolysis) condenses with **D-erythrose-4-phosphate** (from the pentose phosphate pathway) to give **DAHP** (3-deoxy-D-arabino-heptulosonic acid 7-phosphate), a C7 sugar acid.
2. DAHP cyclises to **3-dehydroquinic acid**.
3. Loss of water gives **3-dehydroshikimic acid**. (This is also the branch point for **gallic acid**, the unit of hydrolysable tannins.)
4. Reduction (NADPH) gives **shikimic acid**.
5. Shikimic acid is phosphorylated (ATP) to **shikimate-3-phosphate**.
6. A second PEP is added to give **5-enolpyruvylshikimate-3-phosphate (EPSP)**. The enzyme EPSP synthase is the site blocked by glyphosate.
7. Loss of phosphate gives **chorismic acid**, the main branch point.

```text
PEP + Erythrose-4-P
        |
      DAHP
        |
3-Dehydroquinic acid
        |
3-Dehydroshikimic acid ─────────────→ Gallic acid → gallotannins
        |
  Shikimic acid
        |  + ATP
Shikimate-3-phosphate
        |  + PEP
       EPSP   (EPSP synthase; blocked by glyphosate)
        |
  CHORISMIC ACID
   _____|___________________
  |                         |
Prephenic acid         Anthranilic acid
  |          |              |
Phenyl-   4-Hydroxyphenyl-  Tryptophan
pyruvic   pyruvic acid
  |          |
Phenylalanine Tyrosine
```

**Products of the shikimic acid pathway**

- **Phenylalanine → cinnamic acid.** The enzyme **phenylalanine ammonia lyase (PAL)** removes ammonia from phenylalanine to give trans-cinnamic acid. This is the entry to **phenylpropanoid (C6–C3)** metabolism:
  - hydroxycinnamic acids: p-coumaric, caffeic, ferulic and sinapic acids;
  - **coumarins** (umbelliferone, scopoletin, psoralens) by ortho-hydroxylation and ring closure;
  - **lignans** (podophyllotoxin) and **lignin** from cinnamyl alcohols (coniferyl, sinapyl alcohol);
  - simple phenylpropenes such as **eugenol** (clove), **anethole** (fennel) and **cinnamaldehyde** (cinnamon).
- **Flavonoids** (mixed origin): p-coumaroyl-CoA (shikimate) + 3 malonyl-CoA (acetate) → **chalcone** (by chalcone synthase) → flavanones → flavones, flavonols (quercetin, rutin), anthocyanins, catechins.
- **Tannins**: gallic acid (hydrolysable tannins); catechins and leucoanthocyanidins (condensed tannins).
- **Alkaloids** from tyrosine (morphine, papaverine, berberine), phenylalanine (ephedrine) and tryptophan (reserpine, vinca alkaloids, ergot alkaloids, quinine).
- **Some quinones**: certain naphthoquinones and anthraquinones (for example in the Rubiaceae) are built partly from chorismate.

## 1.3 Acetate pathways

Acetyl-CoA is used in two quite different ways.

### 1.3.1 Acetate–malonate (polyketide) pathway

1. Acetyl-CoA is carboxylated to **malonyl-CoA** (acetyl-CoA carboxylase).
2. A **starter unit** (usually acetyl-CoA) is joined head-to-tail with several malonyl-CoA units. CO2 is lost at each step, so each addition lengthens the chain by two carbons.
3. If the keto groups are reduced at each step, a **fatty acid** results.
4. If they are not reduced, a **poly-β-keto chain (polyketide)** forms. This folds and cyclises by aldol or Claisen reactions to give aromatic rings.

| Product type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Saturated and unsaturated fatty acids | Palmitic, stearic, oleic, ricinoleic acids |
| Anthraquinones (from an octaketide) | Emodin, aloe-emodin, chrysophanol (senna, aloes, rhubarb, cascara) |
| Phloroglucinols, chromones | Khellin (Ammi visnaga), aloesin |
| Ring A of flavonoids | Quercetin, catechin |
| Microbial polyketides | Tetracyclines, griseofulvin, erythromycin |

### 1.3.2 Acetate–mevalonate pathway (isoprenoid pathway)

1. Two acetyl-CoA → **acetoacetyl-CoA**; one more acetyl-CoA → **3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-CoA (HMG-CoA)**.
2. HMG-CoA is reduced by **HMG-CoA reductase** to **mevalonic acid (MVA)**. This is the enzyme blocked by the statin drugs in humans.
3. Two phosphorylations and a decarboxylation give **isopentenyl pyrophosphate (IPP)**, the "active isoprene" C5 unit. IPP isomerises to **dimethylallyl pyrophosphate (DMAPP)**.
4. DMAPP + IPP (head-to-tail) → **geranyl pyrophosphate (GPP, C10)** → + IPP → **farnesyl pyrophosphate (FPP, C15)** → + IPP → **geranylgeranyl pyrophosphate (GGPP, C20)**.
5. Two FPP joined tail-to-tail → **squalene (C30)** → 2,3-oxidosqualene → triterpenes and steroids. Two GGPP joined tail-to-tail → **phytoene (C40)** → carotenoids.

```text
3 Acetyl-CoA → HMG-CoA → Mevalonic acid → IPP ⇌ DMAPP
                                             |
   GPP (C10) ── monoterpenes: menthol, citral, linalool, iridoids
      |  + IPP
   FPP (C15) ── sesquiterpenes: artemisinin, zingiberene
      |  + IPP              2 × FPP → Squalene (C30) → triterpenes (glycyrrhetinic acid),
   GGPP (C20) ── diterpenes: taxol, forskolin   steroids (diosgenin, digitoxigenin)
      2 × GGPP → Phytoene (C40) → carotenoids (lycopene, β-carotene)
```

**MEP (non-mevalonate) pathway.** IPP and DMAPP are also made inside plastids from **pyruvate + glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate** via **1-deoxy-D-xylulose-5-phosphate (DXP)** and **2-C-methyl-D-erythritol-4-phosphate (MEP)**. In plants, monoterpenes, diterpenes and carotenoids are made mainly through the MEP pathway, while sesquiterpenes, triterpenes and sterols come mainly from the cytosolic mevalonate pathway.

**Isoprene rule.** Wallach noted that terpenoids are built from C5 isoprene units; Ruzicka's "biogenetic isoprene rule" related their structures to the joining of IPP and DMAPP.

| Class | Carbons | Isoprene units | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hemiterpenes | C5 | 1 | Isoprene |
| Monoterpenes | C10 | 2 | Menthol, citral, linalool, geraniol, camphor |
| Sesquiterpenes | C15 | 3 | Artemisinin, zingiberene, caryophyllene |
| Diterpenes | C20 | 4 | Taxol, forskolin, phytol |
| Triterpenes | C30 | 6 | Glycyrrhetinic acid, steroids (C27 after loss of carbons) |
| Tetraterpenes | C40 | 8 | Carotenoids |
| Polyterpenes | Many | Many | Rubber, gutta-percha |

## 1.4 Amino acid pathway (formation of alkaloids)

Most alkaloids are made from a few amino acids. The amino acid usually loses CO2 (decarboxylation) to give an amine, which then condenses with an aldehyde or keto compound. A **Mannich-type reaction** (amine + aldehyde + a nucleophilic carbon) and **Schiff base** formation are the common ring-closing steps.

| Amino acid | Key intermediate | Alkaloid type | Drug examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L-Ornithine (and arginine) | Putrescine → N-methyl-Δ1-pyrrolinium cation | Pyrrolidine, tropane | Hyoscyamine, atropine, hyoscine, cocaine; nicotine (with nicotinic acid) |
| L-Lysine | Cadaverine → Δ1-piperideine | Piperidine, quinolizidine | Lobeline, pelletierine, lupinine, sparteine |
| L-Phenylalanine | Cinnamic acid / benzoic acid derivatives | Phenylethylamine (protoalkaloid) | Ephedrine (with a C2 unit from pyruvate) |
| L-Tyrosine | Dopamine + 4-hydroxyphenylacetaldehyde → (S)-norcoclaurine → (S)-reticuline | Benzylisoquinoline and derived | Morphine, codeine, thebaine, papaverine, berberine, tubocurarine, emetine |
| L-Tryptophan | Tryptamine + secologanin → strictosidine | Monoterpenoid indole (and quinoline) | Reserpine, ajmalicine, vinblastine, vincristine, quinine, strychnine |
| L-Tryptophan | Tryptamine / lysergic acid | Simple indole, ergoline | Physostigmine, ergometrine |
| Anthranilic acid | — | Quinoline, acridine, quinazoline | Skimmianine (Ruta), vasicine |
| L-Histidine | Histamine | Imidazole | Pilocarpine |
| Purine nucleotides | Xanthosine → 7-methylxanthine → theobromine | Purine | Caffeine, theobromine, theophylline |
| Terpenoid or steroid skeleton (N added late, not from an amino acid skeleton) | — | Pseudoalkaloids | Aconitine, solasodine, conessine |

**Important worked routes**

1. **Tropane alkaloids:** ornithine → putrescine → N-methylputrescine → N-methyl-Δ1-pyrrolinium cation → + acetoacetate unit → tropinone → tropine. Tropine + phenyllactic acid (from phenylalanine) → littorine → hyoscyamine. Hyoscyamine → hyoscine (epoxidation). Racemisation of hyoscyamine gives atropine.
2. **Morphine:** two tyrosine molecules → dopamine + 4-hydroxyphenylacetaldehyde → (S)-norcoclaurine → (S)-reticuline → (R)-reticuline → salutaridine → thebaine → codeine → morphine. Reticuline is the branch point for many isoquinolines (berberine via the berberine bridge enzyme).
3. **Monoterpenoid indole alkaloids:** tryptamine (from tryptophan) + **secologanin** (an iridoid from the MEP/terpenoid route) → **strictosidine** (enzyme strictosidine synthase). Strictosidine leads to ajmalicine, reserpine, catharanthine and vindoline; catharanthine + vindoline couple to give the dimeric vinblastine. The quinoline alkaloids of cinchona (quinine) also come from strictosidine by rearrangement of the indole ring.
4. **Caffeine:** xanthosine → 7-methylxanthosine → 7-methylxanthine → theobromine → caffeine, using S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) as the methyl donor.

Amino acids also give **cyanogenetic glycosides** (amygdalin from phenylalanine) and **glucosinolates** (sinigrin in mustard).

## 1.5 Radioactive isotopes in biogenetic (biosynthetic) studies

A biosynthetic pathway is proved by feeding the plant a suspected precursor that carries a "label", then showing that the label appears in a definite position in the final product. The **tracer technique** using isotopes is the main tool.

### 1.5.1 Isotopes used

| Isotope | Type | Half-life | Detected by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 14C | Radioactive, weak β emitter | About 5,730 years | Liquid scintillation counter, autoradiography |
| 3H (tritium) | Radioactive, very weak β | About 12.3 years | Liquid scintillation counter |
| 32P | Radioactive, β | About 14.3 days | Geiger–Müller counter |
| 35S | Radioactive, β | About 87 days | Scintillation counter |
| 13C, 15N, 2H, 18O | Stable (non-radioactive) | — | Mass spectrometry, NMR |

14C is the most widely used because carbon is the backbone of every organic compound and its long half-life needs no correction during an experiment. Stable isotopes are now used more often because 13C-NMR shows exactly which carbon of the product is labelled, without breaking the molecule down.

### 1.5.2 Methods of feeding the labelled precursor

1. **Root feeding:** the plant grown in water culture (hydroponics) takes up the precursor through the roots — used for alkaloids made in roots (tropane alkaloids, nicotine).
2. **Stem feeding / wick method:** a cotton wick passes through the stem and dips in the precursor solution.
3. **Cut shoot (cut stem) feeding:** a cut twig stands in the solution and draws it up by transpiration.
4. **Injection** into stems, capsules (opium poppy), fruits or hollow organs.
5. **Infiltration or floating leaf discs** in the solution.
6. **Spraying or painting** on leaves.
7. **Feeding 14CO2** in a closed chamber; the plant fixes it by photosynthesis, labelling all compounds.
8. **Tissue and cell cultures:** precursor added to the medium — easy to control and sterile.

### 1.5.3 Experimental approaches

- **Precursor–product sequence:** the labelled compound is fed, the plant is left for a time, the product is isolated and purified to constant specific activity, and its radioactivity is measured. Then the product is **chemically degraded step by step** to find which carbon carries the label. If the label sits exactly where the proposed pathway predicts, the precursor is accepted.
- **Competitive feeding:** a labelled precursor and an unlabelled suspected intermediate are fed together. If the intermediate is a true one, it dilutes the label in the product.
- **Sequential analysis:** the plant is exposed to 14CO2 for a short "pulse", then samples are taken at intervals. Compounds that become labelled first are earlier in the pathway. Calvin worked out the path of carbon in photosynthesis this way, and the order of opium alkaloids (thebaine → codeine → morphine) was shown similarly.
- **Double or multiple labelling:** the precursor carries two labels (for example 3H and 14C, or 13C and 15N). If the ratio is the same in the product, the bond between them survived intact.
- **Use of mutants and isolated enzymes:** mutant microorganisms blocked at one step pile up the intermediate before the block. Purified enzymes show each single step in a test tube.
- **Grafting experiments:** a plant that makes an alkaloid is grafted on a stock that does not (or the reverse) to find where the alkaloid is made. Grafting tobacco on tomato roots showed that nicotine is made in the roots and carried to the leaves.

### 1.5.4 Measuring incorporation

```text
% incorporation = (total activity in isolated product ÷ total activity fed) × 100

Specific incorporation = (specific activity of product ÷ specific activity of precursor) × 100

Dilution value = specific activity of precursor ÷ specific activity of product
```

A low dilution (high specific incorporation) suggests the compound is a close precursor. Specific activity is the radioactivity per mole (or per mg) of compound.

### 1.5.5 Detection of radioactivity

- **Geiger–Müller counter** — for stronger β and γ emitters.
- **Liquid scintillation counter** — the sample is dissolved in a scintillation cocktail; light flashes from β particles are counted. Best for 14C and 3H.
- **Autoradiography** — a TLC plate or paper chromatogram is placed on X-ray film; labelled spots darken the film.
- **Radio-TLC scanners and radio-HPLC detectors.**

### 1.5.6 Classical examples

| Study | What was found |
| --- | --- |
| Labelled ornithine fed to Datura / belladonna | Ornithine forms the pyrrolidine ring of tropane alkaloids |
| Labelled tyrosine fed to opium poppy | Tyrosine gives both halves of the benzylisoquinoline skeleton |
| [2-14C]-mevalonic acid fed to many plants | Mevalonate is the precursor of terpenoids and steroids |
| Labelled phenylalanine | Gives cinnamic acid, coumarins, lignans and the B ring of flavonoids |
| 14CO2 pulse feeding of poppy | Order thebaine → codeine → morphine |

### 1.5.7 Limitations

- The precursor may not reach the site of synthesis (poor permeability or transport).
- **Metabolic dilution**: the plant's own large pool of the compound dilutes the label.
- **Randomisation**: the precursor may first be broken down to small units (acetate, CO2) that spread the label everywhere, giving false results.
- Results from one plant may not apply to another, and incorporation in cut shoots may differ from the intact plant.
- Radioactive material needs licensing, safe handling and safe disposal; the work is costly.

## Remember

- Primary metabolites are universal; secondary metabolites are the drugs.
- Shikimic acid pathway: PEP + erythrose-4-P → DAHP → shikimic acid → chorismic acid → phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan. Glyphosate blocks EPSP synthase.
- PAL converts phenylalanine to cinnamic acid → coumarins, lignans, phenylpropenes, flavonoids (with acetate).
- Acetate–malonate pathway → fatty acids and polyketides (anthraquinones, tetracyclines).
- Acetate–mevalonate: HMG-CoA → mevalonic acid → IPP/DMAPP → GPP (C10), FPP (C15), GGPP (C20), squalene (C30), phytoene (C40). MEP pathway works in plastids.
- Alkaloids: ornithine → tropanes; lysine → piperidines; tyrosine → morphine; tryptophan + secologanin → strictosidine → reserpine, vinca alkaloids, quinine.
- Tracer studies: 14C and 3H most used; feeding by roots, stems, injection, 14CO2, cell culture; approaches — precursor–product, competitive feeding, sequential analysis, double labelling, mutants, grafting.
- % incorporation = activity in product × 100 ÷ activity fed.

# Unit 3: Isolation, identification and analysis of phytoconstituents

This unit takes ten important pure compounds from four chemical groups and asks three things about each:

1. **Isolation** — how the compound is taken out of the plant and purified.
2. **Identification** — how you prove what it is: physical constants, colour tests, chromatography and spectra.
3. **Analysis** — how its amount is measured (assay).

The isolation of every compound follows the same logic. Learn the general scheme first; then each compound is just a set of special steps.

## 3.1 General scheme of isolation

```text
Collection and authentication of the plant
          |
Drying and size reduction (coarse powder)
          |
Defatting with petroleum ether/hexane (removes fats, waxes, chlorophyll) — if needed
          |
Extraction (maceration, percolation, Soxhlet, steam distillation, etc.)
          |
Concentration under reduced pressure (rotary evaporator, below 50 °C)
          |
Fractionation — acid/base or solvent partition (liquid–liquid extraction)
          |
Purification — column chromatography, preparative TLC, crystallisation, sublimation
          |
Pure compound → identification (mp, TLC, UV, IR, NMR, MS) and assay (HPLC, titration)
```

**Choice of method by compound type**

| Compound | Key property used | Typical method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Volatile terpenoids (menthol, citral) | Volatility | Steam distillation, then freezing or chemical separation |
| Alkaloids (atropine, quinine, reserpine, caffeine) | Base forms salts with acid | Acid–base (Stas–Otto type) extraction |
| Glycosides (glycyrrhizin, rutin) | Water/alcohol solubility; aglycone obtained by hydrolysis | Hot water or alcohol extraction, hydrolysis, crystallisation |
| Resins (podophyllotoxin, curcumin) | Soluble in alcohol, insoluble in water | Alcohol extraction, precipitation in acidified water, chromatography |

**General identification tools**

| Tool | What it tells |
| --- | --- |
| Melting point, optical rotation | Purity and identity |
| Colour tests | Class of compound (alkaloid, flavonoid, steroid) |
| TLC with a reference standard | Same Rf and colour as the standard |
| UV–visible spectrum | Chromophore (λmax) |
| IR spectrum | Functional groups (OH, C=O, lactone) |
| NMR, mass spectrum | Full structure and molecular weight |

## 3.2 Terpenoids

### 3.2.1 Menthol

- **Nature:** a saturated monocyclic **monoterpene alcohol**, C10H20O. (−)-Menthol (l-menthol) is the natural form, with the strongest cooling effect.
- **Source:** oil of *Mentha arvensis* var. *piperascens* (Japanese or corn mint), which is very rich in menthol; also peppermint oil.

**Isolation (freezing method)**

1. Fresh or partly dried herb is **steam-distilled** to give mint oil.
2. The oil is **chilled** in tanks (below about 0 °C, by stages). Menthol **crystallises** out.
3. The crystals are separated by **centrifugation** or draining.
4. The crude crystals are **recrystallised** (or melted and re-cooled), washed and dried.
5. The remaining liquid is **dementholised oil**. It still contains menthol, menthone and menthyl esters and is sold separately.

Menthol is also made synthetically (from thymol by hydrogenation, or from citronellal), but synthetic products may be racemic.

**Identification**

- Colourless, hexagonal or needle-shaped crystals with the odour of peppermint and a cooling taste.
- **Melting point about 42–44 °C** (l-menthol); laevorotatory.
- Rubbed with an equal weight of **camphor, thymol or chloral hydrate** it **liquefies** (forms a eutectic).
- **TLC** (silica gel; toluene–ethyl acetate 93:7) sprayed with **anisaldehyde–sulphuric acid** and heated → blue to violet spot matching standard menthol.
- IR: broad O–H band near 3300 cm−1, no C=O band.

**Analysis**

- **Gas chromatography (GC-FID)** is the method of choice for menthol in oils and products.
- Classical method: **acetylation** — the oil is heated with acetic anhydride and sodium acetate. Menthol becomes menthyl acetate, and the ester value is found by saponification. This gives **total menthol** (free + esterified); the ester value before acetylation gives the esterified part.

### 3.2.2 Citral

- **Nature:** an **acyclic monoterpene aldehyde**, C10H16O. It is a mixture of two geometric isomers: **geranial (citral a, trans)** and **neral (citral b, cis)**. It has a strong lemon odour.
- **Source:** **lemongrass oil** from *Cymbopogon flexuosus* (East Indian or Cochin lemongrass) and *C. citratus* (West Indian), family **Poaceae** (Gramineae). Lemongrass oil contains about 70–85% citral. Also found in *Litsea cubeba* oil and lemon oil.

**Isolation**

1. **Steam distillation** of the grass gives lemongrass oil.
2. **Bisulphite method** (chemical separation of aldehydes):
   - shake the oil with a saturated solution of **sodium bisulphite**; citral forms a water-soluble (or crystalline) **bisulphite addition compound**;
   - separate it and wash with ether to remove non-aldehydic oil (terpenes, alcohols);
   - **decompose** the adduct with sodium carbonate or dilute alkali, which frees the citral;
   - steam-distil or extract with ether, dry and evaporate.
3. Alternatively, **fractional distillation under reduced pressure**, since citral boils at a different temperature from the other components.

**Identification**

- Pale yellow liquid with a lemon odour.
- Aldehyde tests: forms a **2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazone** (orange-red precipitate), a **semicarbazone** and an oxime with sharp melting points; restores the colour of **Schiff's reagent**.
- **TLC** — anisaldehyde–H2SO4 or 2,4-DNPH spray.
- **GC**, showing two peaks (neral and geranial).
- **IR**: strong conjugated aldehyde C=O band (about 1675 cm−1) and the aldehyde C–H near 2700–2800 cm−1. **UV**: absorption from the conjugated C=C–C=O system.

**Analysis**

- **Hydroxylamine hydrochloride (oximation) method:** citral reacts with hydroxylamine hydrochloride, liberating HCl, which is titrated with standard alkali. The volume used gives the % aldehyde.
- **Bisulphite (or neutral sulphite) absorption method:** a measured volume of oil is shaken with bisulphite solution in a **cassia flask**. Aldehyde is absorbed into the water layer, and the unabsorbed oil rises into the graduated neck. The fall in volume gives the % aldehyde (v/v).
- **GC** for exact composition.
- **Uses:** perfumery, lemon flavour, and starting material for **ionones** and for the synthesis of **vitamin A**.

### 3.2.3 Artemisinin

- **Nature:** a **sesquiterpene lactone with an endoperoxide bridge**, C15H22O5. It has no strong UV chromophore, which matters for analysis.
- **Source:** leaves and flowering tops of *Artemisia annua* (Asteraceae).

**Isolation**

1. Dried, powdered leaves are extracted with a **non-polar solvent** — n-hexane or petroleum ether — at room temperature or by gentle Soxhlet extraction. High heat is avoided.
2. The extract is concentrated. Waxes are removed by dissolving the residue in a little acetonitrile or aqueous alcohol and chilling, so the waxes separate.
3. **Column chromatography** on silica gel, eluting with hexane–ethyl acetate of increasing polarity. Fractions containing artemisinin are found by TLC.
4. **Crystallisation** from cyclohexane or hexane–ethyl acetate gives colourless needles.

Supercritical CO2 extraction is also used industrially.

**Identification**

- Colourless needles; **melting point about 156–157 °C**.
- **Peroxide test:** artemisinin liberates iodine from **potassium iodide** in acetic acid. Iodine turns starch blue.
- **TLC:** silica gel, hexane–ethyl acetate; spray with anisaldehyde–H2SO4 and heat → coloured spot matching the standard. It does not show under UV 254 nm.
- **IR:** lactone C=O near 1735–1740 cm−1, and peroxide bands. **NMR and MS** confirm the structure.

**Analysis**

- **HPLC** with an **evaporative light-scattering detector (ELSD)** or refractive-index detector, because it absorbs UV poorly.
- **HPLC–UV after derivatisation:** treating with alkali converts artemisinin to a UV-absorbing product, which is measured.
- **HPTLC** densitometry after spraying.

## 3.3 Glycosides

### 3.3.1 Glycyrrhetinic acid

- **Nature:** **18β-glycyrrhetinic acid** (glycyrrhetic acid) is the **triterpenoid aglycone** of glycyrrhizin from liquorice. It has an α,β-unsaturated ketone at C-11.
- **Source:** liquorice root (*Glycyrrhiza glabra*, Leguminosae).

**Isolation**

1. Coarse liquorice powder is extracted with **dilute ammonia water** (or hot water). Glycyrrhizin dissolves as its ammonium salt.
2. The extract is concentrated and **acidified** with dilute H2SO4 (or HCl). Crude **glycyrrhizic acid** precipitates and is collected.
3. The glycyrrhizic acid is **hydrolysed** by refluxing with dilute mineral acid for a few hours. This removes the two glucuronic acid units.
4. The insoluble aglycone, **glycyrrhetinic acid**, separates. It is filtered, washed free of acid, extracted with chloroform if needed, and **recrystallised** from ethanol or methanol.

**Identification**

- White crystalline powder, not sweet (glycyrrhizin is sweet).
- **Liebermann–Burchard test:** acetic anhydride + conc. H2SO4 → pink to violet (triterpenoid).
- **TLC:** silica gel, chloroform–methanol; spray anisaldehyde–H2SO4 or vanillin–H2SO4 → violet spot matching the standard. Under UV 254 nm the spot quenches because of the enone.
- **UV:** absorption maximum near 250 nm (the 11-oxo-12-ene system).
- **IR, NMR, MS** for structure.

**Analysis**

- **HPLC** (reverse-phase C18, UV detection near 250 nm) — for glycyrrhizin in liquorice and for glycyrrhetinic acid.
- **UV spectrophotometry** of glycyrrhizin in ammoniacal extract.
- **HPTLC** densitometry.

**Uses:** anti-inflammatory and anti-ulcer; carbenoxolone (its hemisuccinate) heals peptic ulcers.

### 3.3.2 Rutin

- **Nature:** a **flavonol glycoside** — quercetin-3-O-rutinoside. Rutinose = L-rhamnose + D-glucose.
- **Sources:** buckwheat (*Fagopyrum esculentum*, Polygonaceae), flower buds of *Sophora japonica* (Fabaceae), rue (*Ruta graveolens*, Rutaceae), *Eucalyptus macrorhyncha*.
- **Key property:** rutin dissolves in **boiling water and hot alcohol** but very little in **cold water**.

**Isolation**

1. Powdered leaves or flower buds are extracted with **boiling water** (or hot methanol/ethanol under reflux).
2. The hot extract is filtered quickly and allowed to stand in a cold place. **Rutin crystallises** as pale yellow needles on cooling.
3. If methanol was used, it is concentrated and diluted with water to bring out the rutin.
4. Crude rutin is **recrystallised** from hot water or methanol–water, with charcoal to remove colour if needed.

**Identification**

- Pale yellow needles that darken on heating.
- **Shinoda test** (Mg + conc. HCl) → **red**; FeCl3 → green; NaOH → deeper yellow.
- **Acid hydrolysis** gives **quercetin** (yellow aglycone) and two sugars, **glucose and rhamnose**, identified by paper chromatography.
- **TLC:** silica gel, ethyl acetate–formic acid–acetic acid–water; spray with **natural products reagent (NP/PEG)** → orange-yellow fluorescence under UV 365 nm.
- **UV spectrum:** two bands typical of flavonols — band I about 350–360 nm and band II about 255–260 nm. Shift reagents (AlCl3, NaOMe, NaOAc) show where the free OH groups are.

**Analysis**

- **Spectrophotometric (aluminium chloride) method:** rutin forms a yellow complex with AlCl3 that is read in the visible region against a rutin standard curve.
- **HPLC** (C18, UV about 255 or 355 nm).
- **HPTLC** densitometry.

**Uses:** capillary fragility, venous insufficiency, haemorrhoids; antioxidant.

## 3.4 Alkaloids

### 3.4.1 General acid–base extraction of alkaloids

```text
Powdered drug + alkali (NH4OH, Na2CO3 or lime) → free alkaloid bases
          |
Extract with organic solvent (chloroform, ether, toluene)
          |
Shake organic layer with dilute acid (H2SO4 / HCl) → alkaloid salts go into water
          |
Discard organic layer (fats, pigments, neutral compounds)
          |
Make aqueous layer alkaline (NH4OH) → free bases
          |
Re-extract with chloroform → evaporate → crude total alkaloids
          |
Purify: crystallisation as salts, chromatography
```

### 3.4.2 Atropine

- **Nature:** **DL-hyoscyamine**, the racemic ester of tropine with tropic acid. It does not occur in quantity in the fresh plant; it forms by **racemisation of L-hyoscyamine** during extraction.
- **Sources:** *Atropa belladonna*, *Datura* species (*D. stramonium*, *D. metel*), *Hyoscyamus muticus* (Egyptian henbane, rich in hyoscyamine) and *Duboisia* species — all **Solanaceae**.

**Isolation**

1. Powdered leaves or roots are moistened with **sodium carbonate solution or ammonia** to free the alkaloid bases.
2. Extract with **ether or chloroform** (percolation or shaking).
3. Shake the organic extract with **dilute sulphuric acid**; the alkaloid salts pass into the aqueous layer.
4. Make the acid layer alkaline with ammonia and re-extract with **chloroform**.
5. Evaporate → crude hyoscyamine (with hyoscine).
6. **Racemisation:** heat the hyoscyamine in chloroform under reflux, or warm it in alcohol with a trace of alkali. It changes to atropine.
7. **Crystallise** atropine from acetone or as **atropine sulphate** from alcohol. Hyoscine stays in the mother liquor and can be separated as its hydrobromide.

**Identification**

- Colourless crystals; **melting point about 114–116 °C**.
- **Optically inactive** (racemic), whereas hyoscyamine is laevorotatory.
- **Vitali–Morin test:** a little atropine + fuming HNO3, evaporated to dryness on a water bath; the residue dissolved in acetone and a drop of alcoholic KOH added → **violet** colour.
- General alkaloid reagents positive.
- **TLC:** silica gel, chloroform–methanol–ammonia; **Dragendorff's** spray → orange spot matching standard.
- A drop of its solution in the eye of an animal dilates the pupil (mydriatic test, now rarely used).

**Analysis**

- **Non-aqueous titration** of atropine sulphate with **perchloric acid** in glacial acetic acid (crystal violet indicator) — pharmacopoeial type assay.
- **Gravimetric/acid–base titration** of total alkaloids in crude drugs.
- **HPLC** for hyoscyamine and hyoscine together.
- **Acid-dye (ion-pair) colorimetry** with bromocresol green.

### 3.4.3 Quinine

- **Nature:** a **quinoline alkaloid** (a cinchona alkaloid) with a quinoline ring and a quinuclidine ring. It is stereoisomeric with quinidine.
- **Source:** bark of *Cinchona* species — *C. calisaya*, *C. ledgeriana*, *C. officinalis* and *C. succirubra* (*C. pubescens*), family **Rubiaceae**.

**Isolation**

1. Powdered bark is mixed with **lime (calcium hydroxide)** or sodium hydroxide and water to liberate the bases.
2. The mixture is extracted with a hot, water-immiscible solvent — **toluene** (formerly benzene or kerosene).
3. The solvent extract is shaken with **dilute sulphuric acid**; the alkaloids go into the acid layer as sulphates.
4. The acid solution is **neutralised** (about pH 6.5) and cooled. **Quinine sulphate**, the least soluble of the cinchona alkaloid sulphates, **crystallises out**.
5. Recrystallise from hot water, with charcoal for decolourising. The mother liquor contains **quinidine, cinchonine and cinchonidine**, which are separated as other salts.

**Identification**

- White, very bitter needles.
- **Fluorescence test:** a solution in dilute H2SO4 shows a strong **blue fluorescence** under UV light. The fluorescence is quenched by chloride (HCl).
- **Thalleioquin test:** to a solution add bromine water, then excess ammonia → **emerald-green** colour (for quinine and quinidine; quinoline with 6-methoxy group).
- **Erythroquinine test:** bromine water + potassium ferrocyanide + ammonia → red.
- **Herapathite test:** iodine in acetic acid–sulphuric acid → green crystals.
- **TLC** with Dragendorff's or iodoplatinate reagent, and under UV 365 nm (blue fluorescent spot).

**Analysis**

- **Non-aqueous titration** with perchloric acid.
- **Fluorimetry** in dilute sulphuric acid — very sensitive.
- **UV spectrophotometry, HPLC.**
- Total alkaloids in bark by gravimetric or titrimetric methods.

**Uses:** antimalarial (severe and resistant falciparum malaria), bitter flavour in tonic water; quinidine is an antiarrhythmic.

### 3.4.4 Reserpine

- **Nature:** an **indole alkaloid ester** (of reserpic acid with trimethoxybenzoic acid). It is a **weak base**, so it can be extracted into organic solvents at only mildly alkaline or near-neutral pH.
- **Source:** roots of *Rauwolfia serpentina* and other *Rauwolfia* species (Apocynaceae).

**Isolation**

1. Powdered roots are extracted with **methanol or ethanol** (sometimes slightly acidified) by percolation.
2. The extract is concentrated and diluted with water or dilute acid. Fats and pigments are removed with light petroleum.
3. The aqueous solution is adjusted to a mildly alkaline pH (with sodium bicarbonate or dilute ammonia) and extracted with **chloroform**. The weakly basic reserpine moves into the chloroform, while strongly basic alkaloids (ajmaline, serpentine) stay largely behind at this pH.
4. The chloroform extract is concentrated and passed through a **column of alumina or silica**; reserpine-rich fractions are collected.
5. **Crystallise** from methanol or acetone–methanol.

**Identification**

- White to pale yellow crystals that darken on exposure to light (store protected from light).
- Alkaloid reagents positive.
- **Fluorescence:** oxidation with **nitrous acid** (sodium nitrite in acid) gives a yellow-green fluorescent product (3,4-dehydroreserpine). This reaction is the basis of the fluorimetric assay.
- **TLC:** silica gel, chloroform–methanol; Dragendorff's spray, and fluorescence under UV 365 nm.
- **UV** and **IR** spectra matched with standard.

**Analysis**

- **Fluorimetry** after nitrous acid oxidation.
- **UV spectrophotometry** and **HPLC** (for reserpine in tablets and roots).
- Total alkaloids in the root by acid–base titration.

### 3.4.5 Caffeine

- **Nature:** 1,3,7-trimethylxanthine, a **purine alkaloid**. It is a very weak base, fairly soluble in hot water and chloroform, and it **sublimes** on heating.
- **Sources:** tea leaves and **tea dust** (*Camellia sinensis*), coffee seeds, cola seeds, guarana, maté.

**Isolation (laboratory method from tea dust)**

1. Boil tea dust with water containing **calcium carbonate** (or magnesium oxide). The alkali keeps tannins and other acids as insoluble salts and frees caffeine. (Lead acetate was used earlier to precipitate tannins.)
2. Filter hot and cool the filtrate.
3. Extract the filtrate several times with **chloroform** (or dichloromethane) in a separating funnel. Shake gently to avoid emulsions.
4. Dry the organic layer over anhydrous sodium sulphate and evaporate → crude, slightly green caffeine.
5. Purify by **sublimation** or by **recrystallisation** from hot ethanol or acetone.

**Identification**

- White, silky needles; bitter; **melting point about 235–237 °C**; sublimes.
- **Murexide test:** caffeine + a few crystals of potassium chlorate + conc. HCl, evaporated to dryness → reddish residue that turns **purple** with ammonia vapour (purine alkaloids).
- Gives **little or no precipitate with Mayer's reagent** (very weak base), but is precipitated by **tannic acid** and by iodine in acid.
- **TLC:** silica gel, ethyl acetate–methanol (or chloroform–methanol); quenching spot under UV 254 nm; iodine vapour or iodine–KI–HCl spray.
- **UV** maximum at about **273 nm** in water or dilute acid.

**Analysis**

- **UV spectrophotometry** at about 273 nm.
- **HPLC** (C18, UV 273 nm) — for beverages and combination tablets.
- **Non-aqueous titration** with perchloric acid.
- **Gravimetric** — weighing the purified caffeine from a known weight of drug.

## 3.5 Resins

### 3.5.1 Podophyllotoxin

- **Nature:** an **aryltetralin lignan lactone**, the main active part of podophyllum resin.
- **Source:** rhizomes and roots of *Podophyllum hexandrum* (Indian, richer in podophyllotoxin) and *P. peltatum* (American), family **Berberidaceae**.

**Isolation**

1. Powdered rhizome is **percolated with ethanol** (90%).
2. The percolate is concentrated to a syrup and poured slowly, with stirring, into **water acidified with HCl**. **Podophyllum resin (podophyllin)** precipitates. It is collected, washed and dried at low temperature (protected from light).
3. The resin is extracted with **chloroform or ethyl acetate**, and the extract is purified by **column chromatography** (silica gel or alumina).
4. Podophyllotoxin is **crystallised** (often as a solvate) from ethanol or benzene–ethanol.

**Identification**

- White crystals; laevorotatory.
- **TLC:** silica gel, chloroform–methanol; spray with anisaldehyde–H2SO4 or 10% H2SO4 and heat → spot matching standard; quenches under UV 254 nm.
- **UV** absorption near 290 nm (methylenedioxy and trimethoxy aromatic rings).
- **IR:** lactone C=O near 1770 cm−1.
- **NMR and MS** for confirmation.

**Analysis**

- **HPLC** (C18, UV detection) — the standard method for podophyllotoxin in the resin and in rhizomes.
- **Gravimetric** determination of resin content (% alcohol-soluble, water-insoluble resin).
- **HPTLC** densitometry.

### 3.5.2 Curcumin

- **Nature:** a **diarylheptanoid** (diferuloylmethane) — two feruloyl units joined by a methylene. Turmeric contains three **curcuminoids**: **curcumin**, **demethoxycurcumin** and **bisdemethoxycurcumin**. They are yellow-orange, soluble in alcohol and acetone, insoluble in water.
- **Source:** rhizomes of turmeric, *Curcuma longa* L., family **Zingiberaceae**.

**Isolation**

1. Dried, powdered rhizome is first treated with **hexane or light petroleum** to remove the volatile oil and fats.
2. The marc is extracted with **acetone, ethanol or ethyl acetate** (Soxhlet or maceration). The concentrated extract is **turmeric oleoresin**.
3. The oleoresin is washed with hexane, and the crude curcuminoids that separate are collected.
4. Pure curcumin is obtained by **crystallisation** (from ethanol or isopropanol) or by **column chromatography** on silica gel with chloroform–methanol.

**Identification**

- Orange-yellow crystalline powder; **melting point about 180–183 °C**.
- **Alkali test:** turns **reddish-brown** with alkali and returns to yellow with acid.
- **Boric acid test:** with boric acid (and oxalic acid) on drying gives an **orange-red to cherry-red** colour (rosocyanin), turning greenish-blue with alkali.
- With conc. H2SO4 it gives a crimson colour.
- **TLC:** silica gel, chloroform–methanol (about 95:5) → **three yellow spots** visible in daylight: curcumin (highest Rf), demethoxycurcumin and bisdemethoxycurcumin (lowest).
- **UV–visible:** λmax about **420–430 nm** in methanol.

**Analysis**

- **Spectrophotometry** at about 425 nm (total curcuminoids, expressed as curcumin).
- **HPLC** (C18, detection at about 425 nm) separates and measures the three curcuminoids.
- **HPTLC** densitometry.

**Uses:** anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, antiseptic in wound pastes, cholagogue; natural food colour (E100). Its absorption from the gut is poor, so special formulations (with piperine, or as complexes) are made.

## Remember

- Isolation = extraction → fractionation → purification → identification → assay.
- Alkaloids: alkali + organic solvent → acid → alkali → organic solvent.
- Menthol by freezing mint oil; citral by bisulphite adduct; artemisinin by hexane + silica column.
- Glycyrrhetinic acid needs hydrolysis of glycyrrhizin; rutin crystallises from hot water on cooling.
- Atropine forms from hyoscyamine by racemisation; quinine crystallises as the least soluble sulphate; reserpine is a weak base.
- Caffeine: CaCO3 + water + chloroform, then sublimation; murexide test; 273 nm.
- Podophyllin is precipitated by pouring the alcoholic extract into acidified water.
- Curcumin: three yellow TLC spots, boric acid test, 425 nm.

# Unit 4: Industrial production, estimation and utilization of phytoconstituents

Unit 3 dealt with laboratory isolation. This unit asks how eleven plant compounds are produced **on a commercial scale**, how their content is **estimated** (assayed), and how they are **utilised** as medicines. In an exam answer use the same three headings for each compound: **Production → Estimation → Utilization**, and begin with the source.

## 4.1 What changes when a process goes industrial

| Point | Laboratory | Industry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raw material | Small batch of any good sample | Large, uniform supply: cultivated, high-yielding varieties; contracts with farmers |
| Size reduction | Mortar, small mill | Hammer mills, cutter mills, flakers |
| Extraction | Soxhlet, separating funnel | Multistage counter-current percolators, extraction tanks, continuous extractors, supercritical CO2 plants |
| Solvent | Any convenient solvent | Cheap, safe, recoverable solvents (ethanol, methanol, toluene, hexane, acetone); solvent recovery is essential |
| Concentration | Rotary evaporator | Falling-film and climbing-film evaporators, vacuum concentrators |
| Purification | Small columns, crystallisation | Large columns, resin adsorption (macroporous resins), crystallisers, centrifuges, spray/vacuum dryers |
| Quality control | Identity | GMP, validated assay (HPLC), impurity limits, residual solvent limits, stability |

```text
Cultivation / procurement → drying → size reduction → extraction (with solvent recovery)
→ concentration → purification (partition, adsorption, chromatography, crystallisation)
→ drying, milling → QC release (identity, assay, impurities, residual solvents) → packing
```

Ways of obtaining a plant compound commercially:

1. Extraction from the plant (most of the compounds here).
2. **Semi-synthesis** from a plentiful plant precursor (digoxin from lanatoside C, paclitaxel from 10-deacetylbaccatin III, steroids from diosgenin).
3. **Plant cell culture** in bioreactors (paclitaxel, shikonin).
4. **Microbial or engineered-yeast production** (semi-synthetic artemisinin from artemisinic acid).
5. **Total synthesis** (caffeine, atropine are made synthetically too).

## 4.2 Forskolin

- **Source:** tuberous roots of *Coleus forskohlii* Briq. (syn. *Plectranthus barbatus*), family **Lamiaceae**. Native to India; cultivated in Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Gujarat.
- **Chemistry:** a **labdane diterpenoid** (C22H34O7). It activates the enzyme **adenylate cyclase** directly, raising cellular cyclic AMP.

**Industrial production**

1. Roots are harvested, cleaned, sliced and dried; dried roots are powdered.
2. Extraction with an organic solvent (toluene, ethyl acetate or ethanol) in large extractors.
3. The extract is concentrated under vacuum.
4. The concentrate is purified by **column chromatography** (silica) and/or by precipitation and **crystallisation** to give pure forskolin. Standardised extracts (for example 10% or 20% forskolin) are made by blending the purified material.

**Estimation**
- **HPLC** (reverse-phase C18, UV detection at a low wavelength, about 210 nm, since forskolin has only a weak chromophore).
- **HPTLC** densitometry after spraying with anisaldehyde–H2SO4.

**Utilization**
- Eye drops to lower intra-ocular pressure in **glaucoma** (raises cAMP in the ciliary body).
- Bronchodilator, vasodilator and positive inotrope (research and some products). A water-soluble derivative (colforsin) has been used for acute heart failure.
- Widely sold in weight-loss supplements, but evidence is weak.
- An important research tool for raising cAMP in cell studies.

## 4.3 Sennosides

- **Source:** leaflets and pods of *Cassia angustifolia* (Tinnevelly senna) and *C. acutifolia*, family **Leguminosae**. India (Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Rajasthan) is a leading producer and exporter.
- **Chemistry:** dianthrone O-glycosides; sennosides A and B (rhein dianthrone glucosides) are the main ones.

**Industrial production (calcium sennosides)**

1. Dried leaves or pods are powdered.
2. Optional pre-extraction with a non-polar solvent to remove chlorophyll, resin and fats.
3. Extraction with **aqueous methanol** (about 70%) or aqueous ethanol, kept slightly acidic (citric acid) to keep the sennosides stable.
4. The extract is concentrated under vacuum, and impurities are removed (by partition or by adjusting pH and filtering).
5. Sennosides are **precipitated as their calcium salts** by adding **calcium chloride** in methanol and raising the pH with ammonia.
6. The precipitate is filtered, washed with methanol, dried and milled → **calcium sennosides** (a brown powder standardised for sennoside content). Senna extract and concentrate are also made.

**Estimation**
- **Spectrophotometric (pharmacopoeial type) method:**
  - the sennosides are extracted and **hydrolysed and oxidised** with ferric chloride in HCl, giving the aglycones;
  - the aglycones are extracted into ether and treated with **magnesium acetate in methanol**, which forms a red complex;
  - absorbance is read at about **515 nm**, and the result is calculated as sennoside B.
- **HPLC** (C18, buffered mobile phase, UV detection) — measures sennosides A, B, C and D separately.
- **TLC/HPTLC** for identity.

**Utilization**
- **Laxative** tablets, granules and syrups for constipation; bowel preparation before colonoscopy and radiology.
- Ayurvedic and herbal laxative blends. The leaves and pods are exported in bulk.

## 4.4 Artemisinin

- **Source:** leaves of *Artemisia annua*, family **Asteraceae**. High-yielding varieties developed by CSIR-CIMAP (Lucknow) are grown in India.
- **Chemistry:** sesquiterpene lactone endoperoxide.

**Industrial production**

1. Crop harvested before or at the start of flowering, when artemisinin content is highest. Leaves are dried in shade and separated from stems.
2. Extraction with **hexane** or petroleum ether (or **supercritical CO2**; ethanol is also used) in large extractors at moderate temperature.
3. Concentration; dewaxing by chilling in a polar solvent (acetonitrile or aqueous alcohol).
4. Purification by **silica gel column chromatography** and **crystallisation** to pharmaceutical purity.
5. Conversion to derivatives: artemisinin is reduced (sodium borohydride) to **dihydroartemisinin**, which is then made into **artesunate** (with succinic anhydride), **artemether** (methyl ether) or **arteether** (ethyl ether).

**Other production routes:**
- **Semi-synthetic artemisinin:** engineered *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* (baker's yeast) makes artemisinic acid by fermentation, which is converted chemically (photo-oxidation) to artemisinin. This was produced commercially from 2013 to steady supplies.
- **Total synthesis** is known but not economical.

**Estimation**
- **HPLC with ELSD or RI detector**, because artemisinin has no useful UV absorption.
- **HPLC–UV after conversion** with alkali into a UV-absorbing compound.
- **HPTLC** densitometry after spraying.
- **Iodometric peroxide test** to confirm the endoperoxide.

**Utilization**
- **Artemisinin-based combination therapies (ACTs)** for uncomplicated falciparum malaria: artemether + lumefantrine, artesunate + amodiaquine, artesunate + mefloquine, artesunate + sulfadoxine–pyrimethamine (used in India's national programme), dihydroartemisinin + piperaquine.
- **Injectable artesunate** for severe and cerebral malaria (preferred over quinine).
- Under research for some cancers and other parasites.

## 4.5 Diosgenin

- **Source:** tubers of *Dioscorea* species (*D. deltoidea*, *D. floribunda*, *D. composita*), family **Dioscoreaceae**; also fenugreek seeds and *Costus speciosus*.
- **Chemistry:** a steroidal **spirostan sapogenin**, present in the plant as the saponin dioscin.

**Industrial production**

1. Tubers are cleaned, sliced and dried, or used fresh after **fermentation**. Fermenting the wet pulp for a few days lets the plant's own enzymes remove part of the sugar chain, which improves the final yield.
2. **Acid hydrolysis:** the material is heated under reflux with **2–4 N HCl or H2SO4** for several hours. This splits off the sugars and frees the water-insoluble diosgenin.
3. The hydrolysed mass is filtered, washed with water until free of acid, neutralised and dried.
4. The dried residue is extracted with **petroleum ether, hexane or toluene** (Soxhlet or counter-current).
5. The solvent is concentrated; crude diosgenin crystallises. It is **recrystallised** from acetone or ethanol.

**Estimation**
- **Gravimetric** — weight of crude sapogenin from a known weight of tuber (after hydrolysis).
- **Colorimetric** — the sapogenin gives a coloured product with reagents such as anisaldehyde–sulphuric acid, read against a standard curve.
- **HPTLC**, **HPLC** and **GLC** (the most specific methods; also separate diosgenin from yamogenin).

**Utilization**
- Starting material for **steroid drugs**. By the **Marker degradation** diosgenin is converted (via pseudodiosgenin diacetate and oxidation) to **16-dehydropregnenolone acetate (16-DPA)**, the key intermediate for:
  - progesterone and other progestins;
  - **corticosteroids** — cortisone, hydrocortisone, prednisolone, dexamethasone (with microbial hydroxylation steps);
  - androgens (testosterone) and oestrogens;
  - **oral contraceptives** (norethisterone, levonorgestrel are made from steroid intermediates).

```text
Dioscin (saponin) ──acid hydrolysis──→ Diosgenin ──Marker degradation──→ 16-DPA
16-DPA → pregnenolone → progesterone → (microbial 11α-hydroxylation) → cortisone, hydrocortisone
16-DPA → androgens, oestrogens, oral contraceptives
```

## 4.6 Digoxin

- **Source:** leaves of *Digitalis lanata* (Grecian foxglove), family **Scrophulariaceae** (Plantaginaceae). Its leaves contain lanatoside C, which has no counterpart in *D. purpurea*.
- **Chemistry:** cardenolide glycoside; aglycone digoxigenin + three digitoxose units.

**Industrial production**

1. Leaves are harvested and dried rapidly at controlled temperature, or processed fresh, so that enzymes do not destroy the glycosides. Sometimes a **controlled enzymic step** is used deliberately (see below).
2. Extraction with dilute **ethanol or methanol** in water.
3. Removal of tannins and pigments (for example with lead acetate in older processes, or by adsorbents), then extraction of the glycosides into **chloroform** or chloroform–alcohol.
4. The primary glycosides (lanatosides) are converted to digoxin in two steps:
   - **removal of the terminal glucose** by the plant's own enzyme (digilanidase), giving acetyl-digoxin;
   - **removal of the acetyl group** by mild alkaline hydrolysis (for example with calcium hydroxide or potassium bicarbonate in methanol), giving digoxin.

```text
Lanatoside C (acetyl + 3 digitoxose + glucose + digoxigenin)
   ── mild alkali (–acetyl) ──→ Deslanoside
   ── enzyme (–glucose) ──────→ Acetyldigoxin
Both routes, when completed ──→ DIGOXIN (digoxigenin + 3 digitoxose)
```

5. Purification by repeated **column chromatography** and **crystallisation** to a pure, white crystalline product.

**Estimation**
- **HPLC** — the official type method for digoxin raw material and tablets (impurities such as digitoxin and gitoxin are limited).
- **Colorimetric methods:** Baljet (alkaline picrate, orange colour from the lactone ring), Kedde (3,5-dinitrobenzoic acid), xanthydrol reagent (for the 2-deoxy sugar).
- **Biological assay** on animals (formerly).
- In patients, **plasma digoxin** is measured by **immunoassay** for therapeutic drug monitoring.

**Utilization**
- **Congestive heart failure** with poor contraction, and control of ventricular rate in **atrial fibrillation and flutter**.
- Tablets, elixir (paediatric) and injection.
- Narrow therapeutic index: toxicity (nausea, visual disturbances, arrhythmias) increases with low potassium; **digoxin-specific antibody fragments** are the antidote.
- Related products: lanatoside C, deslanoside, acetyldigoxin, digitoxin (from *D. purpurea*).

## 4.7 Atropine

- **Sources:** *Duboisia* species (*D. myoporoides*, *D. leichhardtii* and their hybrids, grown in Australia — the main commercial source of tropane alkaloids, especially hyoscine), *Hyoscyamus muticus* (Egyptian henbane), *Datura* species and *Atropa* species — all **Solanaceae**. Atropine is also made by total synthesis.

**Industrial production**

1. Dried, powdered leaves (or roots) are treated with alkali (ammonia, sodium carbonate) to free the bases.
2. Extraction with an organic solvent (toluene, dichloromethane or ethyl acetate) in large extractors, or extraction with acidified water followed by basification.
3. **Acid–base purification:** alkaloids transferred into dilute acid, basified, re-extracted into solvent.
4. **Racemisation** of L-hyoscyamine to atropine by heating in solvent or with a trace of alkali.
5. **Crystallisation** as atropine base, then conversion to **atropine sulphate** (crystallised from ethanol/acetone). Hyoscine is separated from the mother liquors as hyoscine hydrobromide.

**Estimation**
- Raw drug: **total alkaloids** by acid–base titration after extraction.
- Pure atropine sulphate: **non-aqueous titration** with 0.1 M perchloric acid in glacial acetic acid.
- **HPLC** for hyoscyamine/atropine and hyoscine; **colorimetric** methods (Vitali–Morin colour; acid-dye methods).

**Utilization**
- Atropine sulphate **injection**: preanaesthetic (reduces secretions), sinus bradycardia, **organophosphate and carbamate poisoning** (with pralidoxime).
- **Eye drops**: mydriatic and cycloplegic for eye examination, uveitis, and to prevent synechiae; low-dose eye drops are being used to slow myopia in children.
- Antispasmodic in colic.
- Starting material for derivatives: homatropine, **ipratropium** (inhaled bronchodilator). Hyoscine gives hyoscine butylbromide (antispasmodic) and tiotropium.

## 4.8 Podophyllotoxin

- **Source:** rhizomes and roots of *Podophyllum hexandrum* (Indian podophyllum, from the Himalayas) and *P. peltatum* (American), family **Berberidaceae**. *P. hexandrum* is threatened, so cultivation and alternative sources (some *Juniperus* species, cell culture) are being studied.

**Industrial production**

1. Dried rhizomes are powdered and **percolated with ethanol**.
2. The concentrated percolate is poured into acidified water → **podophyllum resin** precipitates; it is collected and dried.
3. The resin is extracted with chloroform or ethyl acetate and purified by **column chromatography**.
4. Podophyllotoxin is **crystallised**.
5. **Semi-synthesis:** podophyllotoxin → 4'-demethylepipodophyllotoxin → glycosylation with a protected glucose and formation of the ethylidene acetal → **etoposide**; with a thenylidene acetal → **teniposide**. Etoposide phosphate is a water-soluble prodrug.

**Estimation**
- **HPLC** (C18, UV detection) for podophyllotoxin in resin, rhizome and products.
- **Gravimetric** assay of resin content in the rhizome.
- **HPTLC** for identity and amount.

**Utilization**
- **Etoposide**: small-cell lung cancer, testicular cancer, lymphomas, leukaemias (topoisomerase II inhibitor).
- **Teniposide**: childhood leukaemia, brain tumours.
- **Podophyllotoxin 0.5% solution or cream** for anogenital warts (applied by the patient; not in pregnancy).
- The resin is no longer used as a purgative because of its toxicity.

## 4.9 Caffeine

- **Sources:** tea waste and tea dust (*Camellia sinensis*, Theaceae); a by-product of **decaffeinating coffee** (*Coffea arabica*, *C. canephora*, Rubiaceae); cola nuts; and **synthesis** (much of the world's caffeine is now synthetic). In India, tea waste is an important raw material.

**Industrial production from tea waste**

1. Tea waste is extracted with **hot water** in the presence of **lime or sodium carbonate**, which keeps the tannins back as insoluble salts and frees caffeine.
2. The hot extract is filtered and concentrated.
3. Caffeine is extracted with a solvent (chloroform or dichloromethane) or adsorbed on **activated charcoal** and then eluted.
4. The crude caffeine is **decolourised** with charcoal and **recrystallised** from water or alcohol; it can also be purified by **sublimation**.

**From coffee (decaffeination):** green coffee beans are moistened and extracted with **supercritical carbon dioxide**, ethyl acetate or dichloromethane, or by the water process. The caffeine taken out is purified and sold.

**Estimation**
- **UV spectrophotometry** at about 273 nm.
- **HPLC** — for tea, coffee, beverages and combination tablets.
- **Non-aqueous titration** with perchloric acid for pure caffeine.
- **Gravimetric** method (weight of purified caffeine from the drug).

**Utilization**
- **CNS stimulant** (fatigue, drowsiness).
- In **analgesic combinations** (with paracetamol or aspirin) — it enhances the analgesic effect.
- With ergotamine for **migraine**.
- **Caffeine citrate** for **apnoea of prematurity** in newborns.
- Soft drinks and energy drinks, beverages.
- Caffeine is also the starting material for theophylline-type drugs in some syntheses.

## 4.10 Taxol (paclitaxel)

- **Sources:** bark of *Taxus brevifolia* (Pacific yew) originally; needles of *T. baccata*, *T. wallichiana* and other species (for 10-deacetylbaccatin III); family **Taxaceae**.

**Why supply was a problem:** paclitaxel content in bark is very low, the yew grows slowly, and removing the bark kills the tree. Several thousand trees were needed for the early clinical trials. Several solutions were found:

| Route | How it works | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extraction from bark | Methanol extraction → solvent partition (dichloromethane–water) → repeated silica and reverse-phase chromatography → crystallisation | Original route; unsustainable |
| **Semi-synthesis from 10-DAB** | 10-Deacetylbaccatin III is extracted from renewable needles (clippings); protected; the C-13 side chain is attached (for example using a β-lactam); deprotected → paclitaxel | Major commercial route; also gives docetaxel |
| **Plant cell fermentation** | *Taxus* cell suspensions in large bioreactors, with elicitors such as methyl jasmonate to raise yield; paclitaxel recovered from the broth and cells | Commercial route |
| Total synthesis | First achieved in 1994 (Holton; Nicolaou) | Many steps; not commercial |
| Endophytic fungi | Some fungi living in yew make small amounts | Research only |

**Estimation**
- **HPLC** (C18 or special taxane columns, UV detection at about 227 nm) — separates paclitaxel from cephalomannine, baccatin III and 10-DAB. LC–MS for low levels.

**Utilization**
- Paclitaxel infusion for **ovarian, breast and non-small-cell lung cancer** and **Kaposi's sarcoma**. It is poorly soluble in water, so it is given in polyoxyl castor oil–ethanol (which can cause hypersensitivity; patients are premedicated), or as **albumin-bound nanoparticles (nab-paclitaxel)**.
- **Docetaxel** (from 10-DAB): breast, prostate, stomach and lung cancers.
- Paclitaxel-coated stents (to prevent re-blocking of arteries).
- Mechanism: stabilises microtubules and blocks cell division.

## 4.11 Vincristine and vinblastine

- **Source:** leaves of *Catharanthus roseus*, family **Apocynaceae**. Their content is extremely small, so very large quantities of leaves are needed for a small amount of drug.

**Industrial production**

1. Dried leaves are extracted with **acidified aqueous alcohol** (or with an organic solvent after moistening with acid such as tartaric acid), so the alkaloids are taken out as salts.
2. The extract is defatted, basified and extracted into an organic solvent (benzene was used historically; now dichloromethane or toluene) → total alkaloids.
3. The total alkaloid fraction is separated by **column chromatography** (alumina or silica, with stepwise solvent changes) and by **selective crystallisation of salts**. The bisindole fraction is separated into vinblastine and vincristine, and each is converted to its **sulphate**.
4. **Semi-synthesis** improves supply: the plentiful monomers **catharanthine** and **vindoline** are coupled chemically and the product reduced to give vinblastine, which can then be converted to **vincristine**. **Vindesine** and **vinorelbine** are further semi-synthetic members of the group.
5. Plant cell and hairy-root cultures of *Catharanthus* are studied as another source, but yields of the dimeric alkaloids remain low.

**Estimation**
- **HPLC** (C18, UV detection near 220–270 nm) for vinblastine and vincristine in leaves, extracts and injections; LC-MS for trace levels.
- **TLC/HPTLC** with Dragendorff's reagent or UV for identity.
- Total alkaloids in the leaf by acid-base titration (a crude measure only).

**Utilization**
- **Vincristine sulphate** injection: acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (especially in children), Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphoma, Wilms tumour, neuroblastoma. Dose-limiting effect: peripheral neuropathy.
- **Vinblastine sulphate** injection: Hodgkin disease, testicular tumours, Kaposi sarcoma, choriocarcinoma. Dose-limiting effect: bone-marrow suppression.
- **Vinorelbine**: non-small-cell lung cancer and breast cancer. **Vindesine**: leukaemias.
- Mechanism: they bind tubulin and prevent the mitotic spindle from forming, so cells are arrested in metaphase. They are given **intravenously only** and are severe vesicants if they leak out of the vein.

## Remember

- Industrial production = steady raw material + cheap recoverable solvent + large-scale extraction and purification + GMP quality control.
- Four supply routes: direct extraction, semi-synthesis from a plentiful precursor, plant cell culture, and engineered microbial production.
- Forskolin raises cyclic AMP (glaucoma); sennosides are sold as **calcium sennosides**, assayed by the magnesium acetate colour at about 515 nm.
- Artemisinin has no useful UV absorption, so HPLC-ELSD is used; it is always given as a **combination** (ACT).
- Diosgenin → 16-DPA → steroid hormones (Marker degradation).
- Digoxin comes from **lanatoside C** of *D. lanata*, not from *D. purpurea*.
- Atropine is made by **racemising hyoscyamine**; *Duboisia* is the main commercial tropane source.
- Podophyllotoxin → etoposide and teniposide; caffeine comes largely from tea waste, decaffeination and synthesis.
- Paclitaxel supply was solved by **semi-synthesis from 10-deacetylbaccatin III** of yew needles and by plant cell fermentation.
- Vincristine causes neuropathy; vinblastine suppresses the bone marrow; both are intravenous only.

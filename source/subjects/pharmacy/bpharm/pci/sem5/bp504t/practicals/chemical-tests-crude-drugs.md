# Analysis of crude drugs by chemical tests: asafoetida, benzoin, colophony, aloes and myrrh

**Aim:** To identify asafoetida, benzoin, colophony, aloes and myrrh by their characteristic chemical tests.

> Follow your lab manual where details differ. Conc. sulphuric acid, nitric acid and bromine are corrosive and give off fumes: work in a fume cupboard, wear gloves and goggles, and add acids drop by drop.

## Principle

These five are **unorganised drugs** — resins, oleo-gum-resins, a balsamic resin and a dried juice. They have no cells to examine, so identification rests on their behaviour with solvents and on colour reactions of their main constituents:

| Drug | Type | Key constituent tested |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asafoetida | Oleo-gum-resin | Umbelliferone (combined), ferulic acid esters, sulphur compounds |
| Benzoin | Balsamic resin | Benzoic acid (Siam) and cinnamic acid (Sumatra) and their esters |
| Colophony | Resin proper | Abietic acid and related resin acids |
| Aloes | Dried juice | Aloin, a C-glycoside of aloe-emodin anthrone |
| Myrrh | Oleo-gum-resin | Furanosesquiterpenes of the volatile oil |

A gum + resin mixture (asafoetida, myrrh) forms an **emulsion** when triturated with water, because the gum emulsifies the resin and oil. A resin proper (colophony) does not. Benzoin, being a balsam of benzoic/cinnamic acid esters, gives a **sublimate of benzoic acid** on heating. Aloin is a C-glycoside, so the ordinary Borntrager's test fails and the **modified** test must be used.

## Requirements

**Drugs:** asafoetida, benzoin (Sumatra and Siam if available), colophony, aloes (Curacao and Cape if available), myrrh.

**Reagents:** conc. sulphuric acid, conc. and 50% nitric acid, conc. hydrochloric acid, dilute hydrochloric acid, ammonia solution, potassium permanganate solution, borax solution, bromine water, ferric chloride solution, copper acetate solution (dilute), light petroleum, ether, benzene or chloroform, 90% alcohol, acetic anhydride.

**Apparatus:** test tubes and holders, mortar and pestle, watch glasses, dry test tube for sublimation, water bath, spirit lamp, filter papers and funnel, separating funnel or stoppered test tubes, UV lamp.

## Procedure and observations

### 1. Asafoetida

| Test | Procedure | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emulsion test | Triturate a little drug with water in a mortar | Milky white emulsion | Gum + resin + oil (oleo-gum-resin) |
| Sulphuric acid | Touch a fresh fracture with conc. H2SO4 | Red to reddish-brown colour | Resin (asaresinotannol) |
| Nitric acid | Add 50% HNO3 to a little drug | Green colour | Asafoetida |
| **Umbelliferone test** | Boil about 0.5 g with dilute HCl, cool, filter and make the filtrate alkaline with ammonia; view in daylight or UV | **Blue fluorescence** | Umbelliferone set free from its combined form |
| Odour | Smell cautiously | Strong persistent garlic-like odour | Organic sulphur compounds |

A sample showing umbelliferone fluorescence **before** hydrolysis suggests adulteration with galbanum, in which umbelliferone is free.

### 2. Benzoin

| Test | Procedure | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sublimation | Heat a little powder gently in a dry test tube | White crystalline sublimate on the cooler walls | Benzoic acid (and cinnamic acid) |
| **Permanganate test** | Warm about 0.5 g with 5 ml KMnO4 solution | Odour of **benzaldehyde** (bitter almond) | Cinnamic acid → **Sumatra** benzoin. No such odour → Siam benzoin |
| Solubility | Shake with 90% alcohol; then add water to the solution | Dissolves in alcohol; milky emulsion on adding water | Resin, insoluble in water |
| Ferric chloride | Alcoholic solution + FeCl3 | Green colour with Siam benzoin (coniferyl benzoate) | Variety test |

### 3. Colophony

| Test | Procedure | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emulsion test | Triturate with water | **No emulsion** | Resin proper, no gum |
| Solubility | Shake with alcohol, then with water | Soluble in alcohol, insoluble in water | Resin |
| **Copper acetate test** | Dissolve a little in light petroleum, shake with dilute copper acetate solution, allow to separate | **Emerald-green** colour in the petroleum layer | Copper abietate (abietic acid) |
| Liebermann–Burchard type test | Dissolve in acetic anhydride and add a drop of conc. H2SO4 | Purple-violet colour that fades quickly | Resin acids |
| Flame | Heat a small piece on a spatula | Burns with a smoky flame | Resin |

### 4. Aloes

| Test | Procedure | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Modified Borntrager's test** | Boil about 0.5 g with dilute HCl and a little ferric chloride solution for a few minutes; cool, shake with benzene or chloroform; separate the organic layer and shake it with dilute ammonia | **Pink to red** ammoniacal layer | Anthraquinones released from the **C-glycoside** aloin (plain Borntrager's test fails) |
| **Borax (Schouteten's) test** | Warm a little aqueous solution with borax solution; view under UV | Green fluorescence | Aloe-emodin anthranol |
| Bromine test | Add bromine water to an aqueous solution | Pale yellow precipitate | Tetrabromaloin |
| Nitric acid test | Add conc. HNO3 to the powder on a watch glass | Deep reddish-brown with **Curacao**; brownish then fading with **Cape** | Distinguishes varieties |
| Cupraloin test | Aqueous solution + copper sulphate solution, sodium chloride and 90% alcohol; warm | Wine-red colour with Curacao aloes | Isobarbaloin present |

### 5. Myrrh

| Test | Procedure | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emulsion test | Triturate with water | Yellowish-brown emulsion | Oleo-gum-resin |
| **Bromine (or nitric acid) test** | Extract a little drug with ether, evaporate a few drops of the extract on a watch glass and expose the residue to bromine vapour, or add conc. HNO3 | **Violet** colour | Furanosesquiterpenes |
| Sulphuric acid | Touch the powder with conc. H2SO4 | Reddish-violet colour | Resin |
| Odour and taste | Smell; taste cautiously only if permitted | Aromatic odour, bitter acrid taste | Myrrh |

## Result

The given samples were identified as **asafoetida, benzoin, colophony, aloes and myrrh** on the basis of the tests above, the confirmatory ones being the umbelliferone fluorescence (asafoetida), the benzaldehyde odour with permanganate and the benzoic acid sublimate (benzoin), the emerald-green copper acetate test (colophony), the modified Borntrager's test (aloes) and the violet colour with bromine vapour (myrrh).

## Precautions

1. Work in a fume cupboard; conc. acids and bromine give harmful fumes.
2. Add acids drop by drop down the side of a slanted tube, and never add water to conc. acid.
3. Heat test tubes on a water bath wherever possible, mouth pointed away from everyone.
4. Use small quantities of drug; these drugs give strong colours even in traces.
5. Use a fresh fracture of asafoetida for the acid colour tests, since the surface is oxidised.
6. For the modified Borntrager's test, the ferric chloride must be present during boiling; otherwise the C-glycoside is not split and the test fails.
7. Smell by wafting the vapour towards you, never by putting the tube to your nose.

## Viva questions

1. **What is an oleo-gum-resin?** A natural exudate containing resin, volatile oil and gum, for example asafoetida, myrrh and guggul.
2. **Why does asafoetida form an emulsion with water?** Its gum fraction emulsifies the resin and volatile oil.
3. **Why does aloes need the modified Borntrager's test?** Aloin is a C-glycoside; the C–C bond is not broken by acid alone, so ferric chloride is added to oxidise and split it to free anthraquinones.
4. **How do you distinguish Sumatra from Siam benzoin?** Sumatra benzoin contains cinnamic acid and gives the benzaldehyde odour on warming with potassium permanganate; Siam benzoin does not.
5. **What is the copper acetate test for?** Colophony: abietic acid forms green copper abietate, which dissolves in the petroleum layer.
6. **Why does colophony give no emulsion with water?** It is a resin proper, with no gum to act as an emulsifier.
7. **Name the chief chemical constituents of myrrh's volatile oil.** Furanosesquiterpenes such as furanoeudesma-1,3-diene and curzerene.
8. **Which test shows adulteration of asafoetida with galbanum?** Free umbelliferone fluorescence without prior hydrolysis.
9. **Name two adulterants of asafoetida.** Colophony, gum arabic, sand, red clay and stones have all been reported.

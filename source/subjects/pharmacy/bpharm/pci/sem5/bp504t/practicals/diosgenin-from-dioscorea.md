# Isolation and detection of diosgenin from Dioscorea

**Aim:** To isolate diosgenin from the tubers of *Dioscorea* by acid hydrolysis and solvent extraction, find the yield and identify it by chemical tests and TLC.

> Follow your lab manual for exact quantities and acid strength. Refluxing with mineral acid and handling petroleum ether need care: use a fume hood, keep solvents away from flames and wear gloves and goggles.

## Principle

*Dioscorea* tubers (family **Dioscoreaceae**; for example *D. deltoidea*, *D. floribunda*, *D. composita*) contain steroidal saponins, chiefly **dioscin**. Dioscin is a glycoside of the spirostan sapogenin **diosgenin**.

- Saponins are water-soluble because of their sugar chain; the aglycone diosgenin is insoluble in water but soluble in non-polar solvents.
- **Acid hydrolysis** breaks the glycosidic bonds, releasing the sugars into water and leaving diosgenin in the insoluble residue.
- The washed and dried residue is extracted with **petroleum ether or hexane**, which dissolves diosgenin but not the polar plant material.
- Diosgenin crystallises when the concentrated solvent cools.

```text
Dioscin (saponin, water-soluble)
   │  dilute HCl or H2SO4, reflux
   ↓
Diosgenin (insoluble) + sugars (glucose, rhamnose, in solution)
   │  filter, wash to neutral, dry
   ↓
Residue ──petroleum ether (Soxhlet)──→ extract ──concentrate, cool──→ crude diosgenin
   ↓ recrystallise (acetone or ethanol)
Pure diosgenin
```

## Requirements

**Chemicals:** powdered dried *Dioscorea* tuber (20–25 g), dilute hydrochloric acid (about 2 N) or sulphuric acid, sodium bicarbonate or dilute ammonia, petroleum ether (60–80 °C) or hexane, acetone or ethanol, acetic anhydride, conc. H2SO4, chloroform, anisaldehyde–sulphuric acid spray, diosgenin standard (if available).

**Apparatus:** round-bottom flask with reflux condenser, heating mantle or water bath, Buchner funnel and suction, pH paper, oven, Soxhlet apparatus, evaporating dish, TLC plates and chamber, melting point apparatus.

## Procedure

1. **Test for saponin first (optional):** shake a little powder with water in a test tube. A persistent honeycomb froth shows saponins.
2. Weigh about 20 g of powdered tuber into a round-bottom flask. Add about 150–200 ml of dilute HCl (about 2 N).
3. Reflux for 2–4 hours to hydrolyse the saponins.
4. Cool and filter under suction. Wash the residue with water until the washings are no longer acidic (check with pH paper); a little sodium bicarbonate or ammonia in the wash water helps neutralise the last traces of acid.
5. Dry the residue in an oven at about 60–80 °C.
6. Pack the dried residue in a thimble and extract with petroleum ether in a Soxhlet apparatus for about 4–6 hours.
7. Concentrate the extract on a water bath to a small volume and allow it to cool, preferably in a refrigerator. Crude diosgenin separates as a white to pale cream crystalline solid.
8. Filter, dry and weigh. Recrystallise from a small volume of acetone or ethanol if required.

## Identification tests

| Test | Procedure | Observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Liebermann–Burchard** | A little sample in chloroform + 1 ml acetic anhydride; add conc. H2SO4 slowly down the side | Coloured ring changing to green or blue-green (steroid nucleus) |
| **Salkowski** | Sample in chloroform + equal volume conc. H2SO4, shake and allow to separate | Red to reddish-brown colour in the chloroform layer |
| Foam test on the hydrolysed product | Shake with water | **No lasting froth** (sugars removed, so no saponin left) |
| TLC | Silica gel G; spot sample and standard; develop in a non-polar system given in your manual; spray with anisaldehyde–sulphuric acid and heat at about 105 °C | Coloured spot with the same Rf as standard diosgenin |
| Melting point | Capillary method | Compare with the diosgenin standard |

## Precautions

1. Reflux gently and make sure water flows through the condenser before heating.
2. Wash the residue completely free of acid; acid left behind can degrade diosgenin during drying and extraction.
3. Dry the residue fully before Soxhlet extraction; water reduces extraction by petroleum ether.
4. Petroleum ether is highly flammable: heat only on a water bath or mantle, with no naked flame nearby.
5. Add conc. H2SO4 slowly in the colour tests.

## Viva questions

1. **What is dioscin?** The main steroidal saponin of *Dioscorea*; its aglycone is diosgenin.
2. **Why is acid hydrolysis needed?** Diosgenin is bound to sugars; hydrolysis frees the water-insoluble aglycone so it can be taken into a non-polar solvent.
3. **Why is petroleum ether chosen?** It dissolves the non-polar sapogenin and leaves most other plant material behind.
4. **What is the importance of diosgenin?** It is a starting material for steroid hormones; by Marker degradation it gives 16-dehydropregnenolone acetate (16-DPA).
5. **Name two other sources of diosgenin.** Fenugreek seeds and *Costus speciosus*.
6. **Which test shows a steroid nucleus?** Liebermann–Burchard (green-blue) and Salkowski (red chloroform layer).
7. **Why do some manufacturers ferment the tubers first?** The plant's own enzymes partly remove sugars, which improves the yield after acid hydrolysis.

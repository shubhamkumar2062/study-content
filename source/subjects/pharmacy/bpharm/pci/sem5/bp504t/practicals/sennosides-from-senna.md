# Isolation and detection of sennosides from senna

**Aim:** To isolate the sennosides from senna leaf or pods as their calcium salts, find the yield and detect them by Borntrager's test and thin layer chromatography.

> Follow the quantities in your lab manual. Methanol is toxic and flammable; heat it only on a water bath in a fume cupboard.

## Principle

Senna (leaflets or pods of *Cassia angustifolia* Vahl or *C. acutifolia* Delile, family **Leguminosae**) contains **sennosides A, B, C and D**, dianthrone O-glycosides of rhein and aloe-emodin, together with free anthraquinones, flavonoids, mucilage and resins.

- Sennosides are **acidic** glycosides, soluble in aqueous alcohol and in dilute alkali.
- Defatting the drug with a non-polar solvent first removes chlorophyll, fats and resins.
- Extraction with **aqueous methanol** (about 70%) takes out the sennosides; a little acid in the extract keeps them from breaking down.
- The sennosides are then **precipitated as their calcium salts** by adding a calcium salt and adjusting the pH, which separates them from the sugars and other soluble material.
- Identification depends on the anthraquinone nucleus: after hydrolysis and oxidation, the free anthraquinones give the pink-red colour of **Borntrager's test** with ammonia.

```text
Powdered senna ──defat (petroleum ether)──→ marc
   │  extract with 70% aqueous methanol (slightly acidic)
   ↓
Extract ──concentrate under reduced pressure──→ concentrate
   │  add calcium chloride solution, adjust pH with ammonia
   ↓
Calcium sennosides precipitate ──filter, wash with methanol, dry──→ product
```

## Requirements

**Chemicals:** powdered senna leaf or pods, petroleum ether (60–80 °C), methanol, water, dilute hydrochloric acid or citric acid, calcium chloride solution, dilute ammonia solution, ferric chloride solution, benzene or chloroform, silica gel G plates and the solvent system given in your manual, sennoside standard if available.

**Apparatus:** Soxhlet apparatus or percolator, round-bottom flask with reflux condenser, water bath, Buchner funnel with suction, beakers, pH paper, oven or desiccator, TLC chamber.

## Procedure

1. Weigh the powdered drug as directed and defat it with petroleum ether (Soxhlet or by maceration). Discard the petroleum ether extract and air-dry the marc.
2. Extract the marc with about 70% aqueous methanol, kept slightly acidic with a little citric or hydrochloric acid, by refluxing on a water bath. Filter.
3. Repeat the extraction on the residue, and combine the filtrates.
4. Concentrate the combined extract on a water bath (or under reduced pressure) to a small volume.
5. Add calcium chloride solution to the concentrate with stirring, then make it slightly alkaline with dilute ammonia. A brownish precipitate of **calcium sennosides** forms.
6. Allow the precipitate to settle, filter under suction, and wash it with a little methanol to remove colouring matter.
7. Dry the product at a low temperature (in an oven at about 50 °C or in a desiccator), then weigh it.

## Identification tests

| Test | Procedure | Observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Borntrager's test** | Boil a little product with dilute HCl, cool, shake with benzene or chloroform, separate the organic layer and shake it with dilute ammonia | **Pink to red** ammoniacal layer (anthraquinones) |
| **Modified Borntrager's test** | Boil with ferric chloride in dilute HCl before extracting, then proceed as above | Deeper pink-red colour; the oxidation converts anthranols and dianthrones to anthraquinones |
| Solubility | Shake with dilute sodium bicarbonate solution | Dissolves (acidic glycosides) |
| TLC | Spot the sample and standard on silica gel G; develop in the system from your manual; view in UV 365 nm, or spray with alcoholic potassium hydroxide | Spots with the same Rf as standard sennosides; anthraquinones fluoresce and give a red colour with alkali |

**Worked example.** Senna powder = 50.0 g; dried calcium sennosides = 0.65 g.
% yield = 0.65 × 100 / 50 = **1.3% w/w**

The product is a mixture of calcium salts of sennosides A, B, C and D with some other material, so its sennoside content is less than 100%. In industry it is assayed spectrophotometrically and expressed as sennoside B.

## Precautions

1. Defat the drug first, or the chlorophyll and resin make the final product very dark.
2. Keep the extraction temperature low and the extract slightly acidic; sennosides are degraded by heat and by alkali in the presence of air.
3. Add the ammonia slowly and check the pH; too much alkali dissolves the precipitate again.
4. Wash the precipitate with cold methanol only, since warm methanol dissolves some of the product.
5. Dry at a low temperature.

## Viva questions

1. **What are sennosides?** Dianthrone glycosides of senna; sennosides A and B are stereoisomeric rhein dianthrone glucosides.
2. **Why are they precipitated as calcium salts?** The calcium salts are much less soluble, so they separate cleanly from sugars and other soluble matter, and the salt is stable for formulation.
3. **Why is the plain Borntrager's test weak with senna?** Senna contains mainly dianthrones and anthranols; they must first be oxidised to anthraquinones, which is what the modified test does with ferric chloride.
4. **How do sennosides act as a laxative?** They are not absorbed in the small intestine; colonic bacteria convert them to rhein anthrone, which increases peristalsis and fluid secretion in the colon.
5. **How are sennosides assayed officially?** By a spectrophotometric method in which the aglycones form a red complex with magnesium acetate, read at about 515 nm and calculated as sennoside B; HPLC is used for the individual sennosides.
6. **Which part of the plant is richer in sennosides?** The pods generally contain more than the leaflets.
7. **Name an adulterant of senna leaf.** *Cassia auriculata* leaves (Avaram); dog senna and argel leaves are also reported.

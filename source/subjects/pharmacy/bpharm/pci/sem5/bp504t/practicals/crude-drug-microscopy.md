# Morphology, histology, powder characteristics, extraction and detection of cinchona, cinnamon, senna, clove, ephedra, fennel and coriander

**Aim:** To study the macroscopic (morphological) features, the transverse section (histology) and the powder characteristics of cinchona, cinnamon, senna, clove, ephedra, fennel and coriander, and to extract and detect their main constituents by simple chemical tests.

> Follow your lab manual where the details differ. Draw every section and powder as a labelled diagram in your record; in the examination the diagram and labels carry most of the marks.

## Principle

An organised crude drug is identified by three things that together are hard to fake:
- **morphology** — shape, size, colour, surface, fracture, odour and taste of the whole drug;
- **histology** — the arrangement of tissues seen in a thin transverse section (TS), made clear by clearing and staining;
- **powder microscopy** — diagnostic cell types (fibres, stone cells, crystals, trichomes, stomata, oil glands, pollen) that survive powdering.

Lignified walls (fibres, stone cells, xylem) are stained **pink-red with phloroglucinol and conc. HCl**. Starch turns **blue-black with iodine**. Calcium oxalate crystals stay undissolved in acetic acid but dissolve in HCl without effervescence. Oil droplets are stained red with **Sudan III**.

The chemical tests detect the class of the active constituent (alkaloid, anthraquinone, phenolic volatile oil and so on) in a simple extract.

## Requirements

**Drugs:** cinchona bark, cinnamon bark, senna leaf, clove, ephedra stem, fennel fruit, coriander fruit (whole and powdered).

**Reagents:** chloral hydrate solution, phloroglucinol (1% in alcohol), conc. HCl, iodine solution, Sudan III, glycerine, dilute H2SO4, bromine water, dilute ammonia, ferric chloride solution, potassium hydroxide solution, benzene or chloroform, copper sulphate solution, sodium hydroxide solution, ether, 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine reagent.

**Apparatus:** compound microscope, slides and cover slips, razor or blade, pith or potato for holding thin material, watch glasses, spirit lamp, test tubes, water bath, hand lens.

## Procedure

1. **Morphology:** examine the whole drug with the naked eye and a hand lens. Note shape, size, colour of outer and inner surfaces, fracture, odour and taste.
2. **Transverse section:** soften the drug if needed (soak barks and fruits in water; boil hard material briefly). Cut thin sections with a sharp razor, keeping the blade wet.
3. **Clearing and staining:** warm the sections in chloral hydrate to clear them. Stain with phloroglucinol, then add a drop of conc. HCl. Mount in glycerine and examine under low and then high power.
4. **Powder:** take a small quantity of powder on a slide. Mount separately in (a) chloral hydrate after warming, (b) phloroglucinol + HCl, (c) iodine, and (d) Sudan III where an oil is expected. Record the diagnostic elements.
5. **Extraction and detection:** carry out the test for each drug as given in the table at the end.

## Observations

### Cinchona (bark of *Cinchona calisaya*, *C. ledgeriana*, *C. officinalis*, *C. succirubra*; Rubiaceae)

| Feature | Observation |
| --- | --- |
| Morphology | Quills or curved pieces; outer surface greyish-brown with longitudinal wrinkles and transverse cracks, often with lichens; inner surface reddish-brown and finely striated; fracture short in the outer part and fibrous in the inner part; taste intensely bitter, odourless |
| TS | Cork; cortex with idioblasts holding microcrystals (sandy crystals) of calcium oxalate; secondary phloem with **isolated, thick-walled, spindle-shaped phloem fibres** having funnel-shaped pit canals; medullary rays |
| Powder | Lignified **spindle-shaped fibres** (diagnostic), sandy crystals, cork cells, parenchyma with starch; **stone cells are absent** |

### Cinnamon (inner bark of *Cinnamomum zeylanicum*; Lauraceae)

| Feature | Observation |
| --- | --- |
| Morphology | Compound quills of thin bark (about 0.5 mm thick) packed one inside the other; outer surface yellowish-brown with fine wavy lines and small scars; inner surface darker and striated; fracture splintery; odour fragrant, taste aromatic and slightly sweet |
| TS | Outer bark is scraped off, so the section starts with the **pericycle: a continuous band of stone cells with groups of pericyclic fibres**; secondary phloem with oil cells, mucilage cells, phloem fibres and medullary rays containing small **acicular crystals of calcium oxalate** |
| Powder | Stone cells (often thickened more on the inner and side walls), isolated fibres, oil cells, small simple and compound starch grains, needle crystals; no cork |

### Senna (leaflets of *Cassia angustifolia* or *C. acutifolia*; Leguminosae)

| Feature | Observation |
| --- | --- |
| Morphology | Lanceolate leaflets with an acute, mucronate apex and an **asymmetric base**; entire margin; pale yellowish-green (Tinnevelly) or greyish-green (Alexandrian); faint odour, mucilaginous then slightly bitter taste |
| TS | **Isobilateral** leaf: palisade under both epidermises; epidermis with **paracytic stomata** on both surfaces and **unicellular, thick-walled, warty, curved trichomes**; midrib vascular bundle with a sheath of fibres surrounded by a **crystal sheath** of calcium oxalate prisms; cluster crystals in the spongy tissue |
| Powder | Paracytic stomata, warty unicellular trichomes, fibres with crystal sheath, prisms and clusters of calcium oxalate |

### Clove (flower buds of *Syzygium aromaticum*; Myrtaceae)

| Feature | Observation |
| --- | --- |
| Morphology | Dark brown buds about 10–18 mm long; a sub-cylindrical **hypanthium** with four thick sepals and a dome-shaped head of four unopened petals enclosing stamens and a style; pressing with a fingernail squeezes out oil; strong spicy odour and pungent taste |
| TS (hypanthium) | Epidermis with thick cuticle and stomata; **large schizolysigenous oil glands in two or three rows** just beneath the epidermis; parenchyma with bicollateral vascular bundles; a loose spongy (aerenchyma) zone; central column of tissue with vascular strands; cluster crystals of calcium oxalate |
| Powder | Fragments of oil glands, **triangular (tetrahedral) pollen grains**, cluster crystals, a few fibres; **no starch and no stone cells** (stone cells point to clove stalks as an adulterant) |

### Ephedra (young stems of *Ephedra gerardiana*, *E. sinica*, *E. equisetina*; Ephedraceae)

| Feature | Observation |
| --- | --- |
| Morphology | Slender, green to greyish-green, cylindrical, finely ridged and jointed stems with nodes carrying small scale-like leaves; taste slightly bitter and astringent |
| TS | Epidermis with a thick cuticle and **sunken stomata in the furrows** between the ridges; **groups of hypodermal fibres** under the ridges; chlorenchymatous cortex; pericyclic fibres; a ring of collateral vascular bundles; central pith |
| Powder | Epidermis with sunken stomata, abundant lignified fibres, fragments of green cortex, small xylem vessels |

### Fennel (cremocarps of *Foeniculum vulgare*; Umbelliferae)

| Feature | Observation |
| --- | --- |
| Morphology | Oblong, greenish-yellow cremocarps about 5–10 mm long, often split into two mericarps; each mericarp with **five prominent primary ridges**; sweet anise-like odour and taste |
| TS (mericarp) | Epicarp; mesocarp with **six vittae (four on the dorsal side, two on the commissural side)** and lignified reticulate parenchyma; endocarp of narrow cells in groups lying at different angles (**parquetry layer**); testa; large endosperm with fixed oil, aleurone grains and tiny rosette crystals of calcium oxalate; carpophore in the commissure |
| Powder | Brown fragments of vittae, parquetry cells, reticulate lignified mesocarp cells, endosperm with oil globules and aleurone; Sudan III stains the oil red |

### Coriander (cremocarps of *Coriandrum sativum*; Umbelliferae)

| Feature | Observation |
| --- | --- |
| Morphology | Almost spherical, straw-coloured to brown cremocarps; the two mericarps stay joined; **ten wavy primary ridges and eight straight, more prominent secondary ridges**; aromatic odour |
| TS | Epicarp; outer mesocarp in which the dorsal vittae are vestigial; inner mesocarp with a **broad band of lignified sclerenchyma (fibres)** — the diagnostic feature; only **two vittae per mericarp, on the commissural surface**; parquetry endocarp; curved endosperm (concave on the inner side) with aleurone and rosette crystals |
| Powder | Lignified fibre bundles crossing at angles, parquetry cells, endosperm cells with oil and rosette crystals, vittae fragments |

### Extraction and detection

| Drug | Extraction | Test | Observation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cinchona | Heat 0.1 g powder in a dry test tube | **Grahe's test** | Purple-red vapours that condense as a red tar on the cooler part (quinoline alkaloids) |
| Cinchona | Shake powder with dilute H2SO4, filter | Fluorescence in UV light | Blue fluorescence (quinine, quinidine) |
| Cinchona | Same acid extract + bromine water, then excess ammonia | **Thalleioquin test** | Emerald-green colour (quinine) |
| Cinnamon | Extract with chloroform, evaporate, take the residue in alcohol | 2,4-DNPH reagent | Yellow-orange precipitate (cinnamaldehyde, an aldehyde) |
| Senna | Boil with dilute H2SO4, cool, shake with benzene or chloroform, separate the organic layer and shake it with dilute ammonia | **Borntrager's test** | Pink to red ammoniacal layer (anthraquinones) |
| Clove | Alcoholic extract or a drop of oil | Ferric chloride | Blue colour (phenol eugenol) |
| Clove | Oil + strong KOH solution | Potassium eugenate | Needle-shaped crystals |
| Ephedra | Moisten with ammonia, extract with ether, evaporate, dissolve in a little water | Copper sulphate + NaOH, then shake with ether | Violet colour; ether layer purple, water layer blue (ephedrine, Chen–Kao reaction) |
| Fennel / coriander | Section or powder mounted in Sudan III | Volatile oil in vittae | Red-stained oil droplets |

## Result

The given drugs were identified as cinchona, cinnamon, senna, clove, ephedra, fennel and coriander by their morphology, transverse section and powder characteristics, and their main constituents were detected by the tests above.

## Precautions

1. Cut sections as thin as possible and keep the razor wet.
2. Do not let chloral hydrate dry on the slide; add more as needed.
3. Add conc. HCl in small drops; wipe spilled acid off the microscope stage at once.
4. Use a fresh mount for each reagent so that colours are not confused.
5. Heat organic solvents only on a water bath, never over a flame.

## Viva questions

1. **Why are stone cells absent in genuine cinchona bark?** It has no sclereids; its strength comes from isolated spindle-shaped phloem fibres, which are diagnostic.
2. **Why is there no cork in a cinnamon section?** The outer bark (cork and cortex) is scraped off during preparation, so the drug starts at the pericycle.
3. **Name the stomata of senna.** Paracytic (rubiaceous): two subsidiary cells lying parallel to the guard cells.
4. **What does the presence of stone cells in clove powder suggest?** Adulteration with clove stalks.
5. **How do fennel and coriander differ in vittae?** Fennel has six per mericarp (four dorsal, two commissural); coriander has only two, on the commissural side.
6. **Which feature identifies coriander in a section?** A band of lignified sclerenchyma in the inner mesocarp.
7. **What is Grahe's test?** Heating cinchona bark in a dry tube gives purple-red vapours that condense as a red tar.
8. **Why does ephedrine give a poor precipitate with the usual alkaloid reagents?** It is a protoalkaloid with the nitrogen in a side chain, not in a ring; the Chen–Kao copper test is used instead.

# Isolation and detection of caffeine from tea dust

**Aim:** To isolate caffeine from tea dust, find the percentage yield and identify it by chemical tests, melting point and TLC.

> Follow your lab manual for exact quantities. Chloroform and dichloromethane are toxic: work in a fume hood, keep them away from flames and collect the used solvent for recovery.

## Principle

Tea leaves (*Camellia sinensis*, Theaceae) contain caffeine (1,3,7-trimethylxanthine), a purine alkaloid, together with tannins, flavonoids, pigments and gallic acid.

- Caffeine is fairly soluble in **hot water** and in **chloroform**, and very weakly basic.
- When tea is boiled with water containing **calcium carbonate**, the tannins and other acidic compounds form insoluble calcium salts and stay behind, while free caffeine passes into the water.
- Caffeine is then taken out of the cooled water extract into **chloroform** by liquid–liquid extraction, which leaves sugars and salts in the water.
- The crude product is purified by **sublimation** or recrystallisation, since caffeine sublimes on heating.

```text
Tea dust + water + CaCO3 ──boil, filter hot──→ aqueous extract (caffeine)
      │                                       (tannins stay as Ca salts)
      ↓
Aqueous extract ──shake with CHCl3 (3 times)──→ CHCl3 layer
      ↓ dry (Na2SO4), evaporate
Crude caffeine ──sublimation / recrystallisation──→ pure caffeine
```

## Requirements

**Chemicals:** tea dust (10 g), calcium carbonate (powdered), chloroform or dichloromethane, anhydrous sodium sulphate, acetone or ethanol, potassium chlorate, conc. HCl, dilute ammonia, tannic acid solution, Wagner's reagent, Dragendorff's reagent, caffeine standard.

**Apparatus:** 500 ml beaker or round-bottom flask, water bath, funnel and filter paper (or cloth), separating funnel, conical flasks, evaporating dish, watch glasses, melting point apparatus, TLC plates and chamber.

## Procedure

1. Weigh 10 g of tea dust into a beaker with about 4–5 g of calcium carbonate and 150 ml of water.
2. Boil gently for about 20 minutes, stirring now and then and adding water to keep the volume.
3. Filter **while hot** through cloth or a fluted filter paper. Wash the residue with a little hot water and combine the filtrates.
4. Cool the filtrate to room temperature.
5. Transfer it to a separating funnel and extract with 25 ml chloroform. **Swirl and invert gently** rather than shaking hard, to avoid an emulsion. Release pressure often.
6. Run off the lower chloroform layer. Repeat the extraction two more times with fresh chloroform.
7. Combine the chloroform extracts and dry them over anhydrous sodium sulphate.
8. Filter into a weighed dish and evaporate the solvent on a water bath (in a fume hood) to get crude caffeine, a pale greenish-white residue. Weigh it.
9. **Purification:** sublime the crude caffeine by warming it gently under an inverted funnel or a watch glass cooled from above; white needles collect on the cool surface. Or recrystallise from a little hot acetone or ethanol.
10. Dry and weigh the purified caffeine and carry out the tests.

## Identification tests

| Test | Procedure | Observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Murexide test** | Few crystals + a crystal of KClO3 + 1–2 drops conc. HCl; evaporate to dryness on a water bath; expose residue to ammonia vapour | Purple colour (purine alkaloid) |
| Tannic acid | Aqueous solution + tannic acid | White precipitate, soluble in excess |
| Wagner's reagent | Acidified solution + iodine in KI | Reddish-brown precipitate |
| Mayer's reagent | Acidified solution | **No precipitate** (caffeine is an exception among alkaloids) |
| Sublimation | Warm a few crystals on a slide | White needle-shaped sublimate |
| Melting point | Capillary method | Close to that of the caffeine standard (about 235–238 °C) |
| TLC | Silica gel G; spot sample and standard; develop in a system from your manual; view under UV 254 nm or spray with Dragendorff's reagent | Spot with the same Rf as standard caffeine |

## Observations and calculation

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Weight of tea dust taken (W) | 10 g |
| Weight of empty dish (W1) |  |
| Weight of dish + purified caffeine (W2) |  |
| Weight of caffeine (W2 − W1) |  |

% yield of caffeine = (W2 − W1) × 100 / W

**Worked example.** Tea dust = 10.0 g; caffeine obtained = 0.180 g.
% yield = 0.180 × 100 / 10 = **1.8% w/w**

The yield is usually less than the true caffeine content of tea, because some caffeine stays in the water layer and some is lost during sublimation.

Rf = distance travelled by the spot ÷ distance travelled by the solvent front.

## Precautions

1. Filter the decoction while hot; caffeine is far less soluble in cold water.
2. Do not shake the separating funnel vigorously; emulsions are common with tea extracts.
3. Dry the chloroform layer completely before evaporating.
4. Evaporate chloroform only on a water bath in a fume hood.
5. Sublime with gentle heat; strong heating chars the product.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is calcium carbonate added?** To bind tannins and other acids as insoluble calcium salts, so they are not extracted, and to keep caffeine as the free base.
2. **Why is chloroform used for the second extraction?** Caffeine is much more soluble in chloroform than in cold water, while sugars and salts stay in water.
3. **Which test identifies caffeine?** The murexide test, given by purine alkaloids.
4. **Does caffeine give Mayer's test?** No; it is one of the few alkaloids that do not.
5. **Why can caffeine be purified by sublimation?** It passes from solid to vapour on heating and condenses as pure crystals.
6. **What other purine alkaloids occur in tea?** Small amounts of theophylline and theobromine.
7. **What is the industrial source of natural caffeine?** Tea waste and the caffeine removed when coffee is decaffeinated.

# Unit 4: Drugs acting on the endocrine system

This unit is about **steroids and hormones**. It begins with the chemistry every steroid question rests on — nomenclature, stereochemistry and metabolism — and then takes the groups in turn: sex hormones, drugs for erectile dysfunction, oral contraceptives, corticosteroids, and thyroid and antithyroid drugs. The syllabus marks **no drug with an asterisk in this unit**, so no synthesis is asked; the marks are in structure, classification, mechanism and use.

## 4.1 The steroid nucleus and its nomenclature

Every steroid is built on **cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene**: three six-membered rings (A, B, C) fused like phenanthrene, plus a five-membered ring D.

```text
        12  17
   11 ╭────╮ ╭──╮
  C19 │ C  │ │ D│ 16
  1 ╭─┴────┴─┴──┘
    │ A      B
  3 ╰──╮  ╭──╯ 7
       4  6
   Carbons: 1–10 rings A and B, 11–17 rings C and D
   C18 = methyl on C13, C19 = methyl on C10 (the two angular methyls)
   C20 onwards = the side chain at C17
```

**Numbering and naming.** Carbons are numbered as above; the angular methyl groups are C18 (on C13) and C19 (on C10). Parent hydrocarbons give the family names:

| Parent | Carbons | Family | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Estrane | 18 | Oestrogens (aromatic ring A, no C19 methyl) | Oestradiol |
| Androstane | 19 | Androgens | Testosterone |
| Pregnane | 21 | Progestogens and corticosteroids | Progesterone, cortisol |
| Cholane / cholestane | 24 / 27 | Bile acids / sterols | Cholic acid, cholesterol |

**Stereochemistry.** Substituents above the plane of the paper are **β** and drawn with a bold line; those below are **α** and drawn with a dashed line. Ring fusions are described the same way: in most active steroids rings B/C and C/D are *trans*, while the A/B junction may be *trans* (5α series, flat molecule) or *cis* (5β series, bent molecule, typical of bile acids). The **5α/5β difference decides biological activity** — 5α-dihydrotestosterone is the potent androgen, whereas the 5β compound is almost inactive. The natural steroids are almost all of one enantiomeric series, and the angular methyls are β.

**Metabolism of steroids**, in the order examiners expect:

1. Reduction of the **Δ4-3-ketone** of ring A to a 3-hydroxy compound, through 5α- or 5β-reductase.
2. Reduction or oxidation of the **17-keto / 17β-hydroxy** pair (testosterone ⇌ androstenedione).
3. **Hydroxylation**, chiefly at C6β and C16, by cytochrome P450.
4. **Conjugation** of the 3-OH or 17-OH with glucuronic acid or sulphate, giving the water-soluble forms excreted in urine.
5. Oestrogens are additionally hydroxylated at C2 and C16 and methylated by catechol-O-methyltransferase.

Two practical consequences: natural steroids given by mouth are largely destroyed on first pass (hence **17α-alkyl** derivatives such as ethinyloestradiol and methyltestosterone, which resist oxidation at C17), and esterification of a 17β-OH with a long-chain acid (testosterone propionate, enanthate) gives an oily depot injection that releases drug slowly.

## 4.2 Sex hormones

### Androgens

**Testosterone** is 17β-hydroxyandrost-4-en-3-one. It is made in the Leydig cells of the testis under the control of luteinising hormone, and in the tissues is converted by **5α-reductase** to the more potent **dihydrotestosterone**, or by **aromatase** to oestradiol.

SAR notes: the **Δ4-3-ketone in ring A and the 17β-hydroxyl** are needed for full activity; the 17α-position tolerates an alkyl group and this blocks first-pass metabolism (methyltestosterone is orally active); esterifying the 17β-OH prolongs action; removing the C19 methyl (as in **nandrolone**, 19-nortestosterone) shifts the balance towards tissue building (anabolic) and away from masculinising (androgenic) activity.

Uses: replacement in male hypogonadism and delayed puberty, and anabolic steroids in wasting conditions and some anaemias. Misuse in sport is a standard viva question; the harms are liver damage (especially with 17α-alkyl drugs), lipid changes, suppression of the body's own hormone production and infertility.

### Oestrogens

**Oestradiol** is the most potent natural oestrogen; **oestrone** and **oestriol** are weaker metabolites, oestriol being the main oestrogen of pregnancy. Chemically they are unique among steroids: ring **A is aromatic**, so C3 carries a **phenolic hydroxyl**, and there is **no C19 methyl group** (18 carbons, the estrane series).

**Diethylstilbestrol** is a non-steroidal oestrogen: two phenol rings on a stilbene framework. Only the **trans** isomer is active, because it places the two phenolic oxygens about the same distance apart as in oestradiol — the classic example of distance matching a pharmacophore. It is of historical and teaching importance only; its use in pregnancy caused genital tract cancers in daughters, and it is no longer used for that purpose.

SAR: the **phenolic 3-OH and a hydroxyl or ketone about 10.9 Å away at the other end** make up the pharmacophore; ethinylation at 17α (**ethinyloestradiol**) blocks metabolism and gives oral activity; mestranol is its 3-methyl ether, a prodrug.

Uses: hormone replacement around the menopause, failure of ovarian development, and with a progestogen in contraception.

### Progestogens

**Progesterone** is pregn-4-ene-3,20-dione. It prepares the endometrium for implantation, quietens the uterus during pregnancy and raises basal body temperature. Taken by mouth it is destroyed quickly, so synthetic analogues are used: **19-nor compounds** (norethisterone, levonorgestrel, norgestrel) derived from testosterone, and **17α-acetoxyprogesterones** (medroxyprogesterone acetate) derived from progesterone. A 17α-ethinyl group again gives oral activity.

## 4.3 Drugs for erectile dysfunction

**Sildenafil** and **tadalafil** inhibit **phosphodiesterase type 5 (PDE5)**, the enzyme that destroys cyclic GMP in the smooth muscle of the corpus cavernosum.

```text
Sexual stimulation → nitric oxide released from nerve endings and endothelium
        → guanylate cyclase → cGMP rises → smooth muscle relaxes → erection
                 cGMP ──(PDE5)──► GMP   ✗ blocked by sildenafil, tadalafil
```

So the drugs do not create an erection; they allow the natural signal to last. Sildenafil is a pyrazolopyrimidinone that imitates the purine ring of cGMP; tadalafil has a different (β-carboline) skeleton and a much longer action. Sildenafil's weak action on PDE6 in the retina explains the blue-tinged vision some users report. **Giving them with any nitrate is contraindicated**, because both raise cGMP and blood pressure can fall dangerously. Sildenafil is also used in pulmonary arterial hypertension.

## 4.4 Oral contraceptives

Two kinds are examinable: **combined** preparations (an oestrogen, almost always ethinyloestradiol, plus a progestogen such as **levonorgestrel** or **norgestrel**) and **progestogen-only** preparations.

**How they work.** Steady hormone levels act back on the hypothalamus and pituitary, so the **mid-cycle surge of luteinising hormone does not happen and ovulation is prevented**. The progestogen also thickens cervical mucus so sperm cannot pass, and makes the endometrium unsuitable for implantation.

**Mifepristone** is different: it is a **progesterone receptor antagonist** (also a glucocorticoid antagonist) used, under medical supervision and within the limits of Indian law, for medical termination of early pregnancy and in some emergencies. It is a prescription drug regulated under the MTP Act and cannot be sold over the counter in India.

Points worth marks: unwanted effects of combined pills include nausea, breast tenderness, weight gain, raised blood pressure and, importantly, a raised risk of **venous thromboembolism**, which is greater in smokers over 35. Enzyme-inducing drugs (rifampicin, phenytoin, carbamazepine) can make them fail. Benefits include regular, lighter periods and a lower risk of ovarian and endometrial cancer.

## 4.5 Corticosteroids

The adrenal cortex makes two sorts of steroid: **glucocorticoids** (cortisol) that control carbohydrate, protein and fat metabolism and inflammation, and **mineralocorticoids** (aldosterone) that control sodium and potassium.

**The chemistry of the glucocorticoid pharmacophore:**

| Feature | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Δ4-3-ketone in ring A | Essential for all activity |
| 11β-hydroxyl | Essential for glucocorticoid action; cortisone (11-keto) is a prodrug reduced in the liver to hydrocortisone |
| 17α-hydroxyl | Raises glucocorticoid activity |
| C21 hydroxyl of the dihydroxyacetone side chain | Essential; esterifying it gives soluble (succinate, phosphate) or insoluble (acetate) forms |
| Δ1 double bond (1,2-dehydro) | Four times the anti-inflammatory potency with less salt retention — the step from hydrocortisone to **prednisolone** |
| 9α-fluorine | Raises both activities strongly (fludrocortisone) |
| 16α- or 16β-methyl | Almost abolishes the salt-retaining action — this with 9α-F gives **dexamethasone (16α)** and **betamethasone (16β)**, which are epimers of each other |

| Drug | Relative anti-inflammatory potency | Salt retention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hydrocortisone (cortisol) | 1 | Yes |
| Cortisone | 0.8 (prodrug) | Yes |
| Prednisolone | about 4 | Slight |
| Dexamethasone, betamethasone | about 25–30 | Negligible |

**Uses:** replacement in Addison's disease and adrenal insufficiency; suppression of inflammation and immunity in asthma, rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease and skin disease; prevention of transplant rejection; in some leukaemias and lymphomas; and to mature the fetal lung before premature delivery.

**Adverse effects** follow directly from the hormone's actions, and are a favourite short question: moon face and truncal obesity (Cushingoid appearance), high blood sugar, thinning of the skin and easy bruising, osteoporosis, muscle wasting, peptic ulcer, cataract and glaucoma, poor wound healing, susceptibility to infection with the signs masked, mood change, growth retardation in children, and — most importantly — **suppression of the body's own adrenal axis**, so long treatment must never be stopped suddenly but tapered.

## 4.6 Thyroid and antithyroid drugs

The thyroid traps iodide, oxidises it with **thyroid peroxidase**, iodinates tyrosine residues on thyroglobulin (giving MIT and DIT) and couples these to form **T4 (thyroxine)** and **T3 (triiodothyronine)**.

```text
Iodide ──(trap, "iodide pump")──► Thyroid cell
   │ thyroid peroxidase (oxidation and organification)
MIT + DIT ──(coupling)──► T3 and T4 on thyroglobulin
   │ release
 T4 (mostly) ──(5'-deiodinase in tissues)──► T3 (the active hormone)
```

- **L-Thyroxine (levothyroxine, T4)** is the standard replacement in hypothyroidism: it has a long half-life, gives steady levels and is converted in the tissues to T3. **L-Thyronine (liothyronine, T3)** acts faster and more briefly and is reserved for myxoedema coma and similar situations. Only the **laevo (L)** isomers are active; the structure is two iodinated phenyl rings joined by an ether oxygen, with an alanine side chain.
- **Propylthiouracil** and **methimazole** are thioamides. They inhibit **thyroid peroxidase**, so iodide is not organified and the hormones are not made; propylthiouracil additionally blocks the peripheral conversion of T4 to T3, which is useful in thyroid storm. The effect appears only after existing stores are used up, so several weeks pass before the patient improves. The serious adverse effect to remember is **agranulocytosis** — a sore throat and fever during treatment must be investigated at once; propylthiouracil can also cause liver injury.
- Other measures in hyperthyroidism: iodide in large amounts briefly blocks hormone release (Wolff-Chaikoff effect) and firms the gland before surgery; radioactive iodine-131 destroys the gland tissue; beta blockers control the tremor, racing pulse and anxiety while the thioamides take effect.

## Remember

1. Steroid nucleus = cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene; C18 and C19 are the angular methyls; β is up (bold), α is down (dashed).
2. Estrane 18 C (oestrogens), androstane 19 C (androgens), pregnane 21 C (progestogens and corticosteroids).
3. Oestrogens alone have an **aromatic ring A with a phenolic 3-OH and no C19 methyl**; trans-diethylstilbestrol is active, the cis form is not.
4. A **17α-ethinyl or 17α-methyl group gives oral activity** by blocking first-pass metabolism; a 17β-ester gives a depot injection.
5. Testosterone → dihydrotestosterone by 5α-reductase; removing C19 (nandrolone) favours anabolic over androgenic action.
6. Sildenafil and tadalafil inhibit PDE5 so cGMP survives; never with nitrates.
7. Combined oral contraceptives work mainly by stopping the LH surge; mifepristone is a progesterone receptor antagonist, a regulated prescription drug.
8. Corticosteroid SAR: Δ4-3-ketone, 11β-OH and C21-OH essential; Δ1 gives prednisolone; 9α-F plus a 16-methyl gives dexamethasone (16α) and betamethasone (16β) with almost no salt retention.
9. Cortisone and prednisone are prodrugs reduced at C11 in the liver.
10. Thioamides block thyroid peroxidase and act slowly; agranulocytosis is the warning sign; levothyroxine (T4) is the drug of choice for hypothyroidism.

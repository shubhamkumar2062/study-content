# Unit 3: Antiarrhythmic, antihyperlipidaemic, coagulant and anticoagulant drugs, and drugs for heart failure

Four groups sit in this unit, all of them cardiovascular: drugs that correct the **rhythm** of the heart, drugs that lower **blood lipids**, drugs that help or hinder **clotting**, and drugs used in **congestive heart failure**. The starred drugs are **disopyramide phosphate** and **warfarin**.

## 3.1 The cardiac action potential and how antiarrhythmics work

A working cardiac cell has five phases. Phase 0 is the fast upstroke carried by **sodium** entering; phase 1 a brief repolarisation; phase 2 the plateau held up by **calcium** coming in; phase 3 repolarisation by **potassium** leaving; phase 4 the resting level, which in pacemaker cells drifts upward (the "funny" current) until threshold is reached.

```text
      1 ╭──╮ 2 (plateau, Ca2+ in)
        │   ╰─────────╮
   0 ▲  │              ╰╮ 3 (K+ out)
     │  │               ╰────────── 4 (rest / pacemaker drift)
  Na+ in
```

An arrhythmia arises either because an abnormal focus fires too fast or because an impulse circles round a ring of tissue (**re-entry**). Drugs act by slowing conduction, by lengthening the refractory period, or by suppressing abnormal automaticity. Note that every antiarrhythmic can itself provoke an arrhythmia — **pro-arrhythmia** is the great danger of this class.

### Vaughan Williams classification

| Class | Action | Drugs in the syllabus | Effect on the action potential |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IA | Moderate sodium block, also blocks potassium | Quinidine sulphate, procainamide hydrochloride, disopyramide phosphate* | Slows phase 0, lengthens the action potential and QT |
| IB | Weak sodium block, acts mainly on damaged tissue | Lidocaine hydrochloride, phenytoin sodium, tocainide hydrochloride, mexiletine hydrochloride | Shortens the action potential; little effect on normal cells |
| IC | Strong sodium block | Lorcainide hydrochloride (also flecainide, propafenone) | Markedly slows conduction, little change in duration |
| II | Beta blockers | Propranolol, esmolol, sotalol (also class III) | Slow the sinus node and AV conduction |
| III | Potassium channel block | Amiodarone, sotalol | Greatly lengthens the action potential and refractory period |
| IV | Calcium channel block | Verapamil, diltiazem | Slow the SA and AV nodes |

Points that examiners look for:

- **Quinidine**, a cinchona alkaloid and the dextro-isomer of quinine, also has antimuscarinic action, so it can speed AV conduction; it causes **cinchonism** (ringing in the ears, headache, blurred vision) and can lengthen the QT interval.
- **Procainamide** is the amide analogue of procaine: replacing the ester by an amide stops plasma esterases destroying it, so it is orally active and longer lasting. Long use produces a **lupus-like syndrome**, particularly in slow acetylators, because its metabolism depends on N-acetyltransferase.
- **Disopyramide** has the strongest antimuscarinic effect of the group (dry mouth, urinary retention) and the strongest negative effect on the force of contraction, so it is avoided in heart failure.
- **Lidocaine** is given only intravenously because of heavy first-pass metabolism; it is the traditional choice for ventricular arrhythmia after myocardial infarction.
- **Phenytoin** is useful mainly in arrhythmias caused by digitalis toxicity.
- **Amiodarone** is iodine-rich and very lipophilic, with a half-life of weeks; its toxicities are therefore organ-based — thyroid (both over- and underactivity), lung fibrosis, corneal deposits, skin discolouration and liver damage.
- **Sotalol** is a racemate: the *l*-isomer gives beta blockade (class II), while both isomers block potassium channels (class III).

### Synthesis of disopyramide phosphate

1. **Phenylacetonitrile** is treated with **sodium amide** and then with **2-chloropyridine** (or 2-bromopyridine). The carbanion displaces the halide and gives **α-phenyl-2-pyridylacetonitrile**.
2. A second deprotonation with sodium amide, followed by **2-(diisopropylamino)ethyl chloride**, alkylates the same carbon, giving 4-(diisopropylamino)-2-phenyl-2-(2-pyridyl)butanenitrile.
3. The nitrile is **partially hydrolysed with concentrated sulphuric acid** (stopping at the amide stage, not going on to the acid) to give **disopyramide**.
4. The base is neutralised with **phosphoric acid** to give the water-soluble **phosphate salt**.

## 3.2 Antihyperlipidaemic agents

Cholesterol and triglycerides travel in lipoproteins. **LDL** carries cholesterol to the tissues and is the harmful fraction; **HDL** carries it back to the liver and is protective. Lowering LDL slows atherosclerosis.

| Class | Drug | Mechanism | Points to note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fibrate | Clofibrate | Activates PPAR-α: more lipoprotein lipase, so triglycerides fall; HDL rises | Mainly for high triglycerides; causes gallstones and myopathy; largely replaced by newer fibrates |
| Statin | Lovastatin | Competitive inhibitor of **HMG-CoA reductase**, the rate-limiting enzyme of cholesterol synthesis; liver LDL receptors increase, so plasma LDL falls | A fungal product; it is a **lactone prodrug** opened in the body to the active hydroxy acid; myopathy and raised transaminases are the risks |
| Bile acid sequestrants | Cholestyramine, colestipol | Insoluble anion-exchange resins that bind bile acids in the gut and prevent their reabsorption, so the liver uses cholesterol to make more | Not absorbed, so systemically safe; cause constipation and bind fat-soluble vitamins and other drugs — give other drugs an hour before or several hours after |

The lactone ring of lovastatin opens to a **3,5-dihydroxy acid** whose arrangement imitates the natural substrate HMG-CoA; this mimicry is the standard example of substrate-analogue design in this subject. Best taken at night, since cholesterol synthesis peaks then.

## 3.3 Coagulants and anticoagulants

**Clotting in one picture.** Both the contact (intrinsic) and tissue-factor (extrinsic) pathways converge on factor X; activated factor X turns prothrombin into thrombin, and thrombin turns fibrinogen into fibrin. Factors II, VII, IX and X need **vitamin K** for a post-translational step in the liver.

```text
Vitamin K (reduced) ──► γ-carboxylation of factors II, VII, IX, X ──► vitamin K epoxide
          ▲                                                                │
          └────────── vitamin K epoxide reductase ◄───────────────────────┘
                              ✗ blocked by warfarin
```

### Coagulants (vitamin K compounds)

- **Menadione** (vitamin K3) is a synthetic naphthoquinone; it is water-insoluble but its sodium bisulphite adduct is soluble for injection. **Acetomenadione** (menadiol diacetate) is the diacetyl ester of the reduced form, used orally.
- Uses: bleeding from vitamin K deficiency, haemorrhagic disease of the newborn, obstructive jaundice and malabsorption, and reversal of excessive warfarin effect.
- Menadione can cause haemolysis and jaundice in newborns and in glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency, so phytomenadione (vitamin K1) is preferred in neonates.

### Oral anticoagulants

**Warfarin*** is a **4-hydroxycoumarin** with a 3-(1-phenyl-3-oxobutyl) side chain. It is the standard example of **bioisosterism with vitamin K** — the flat coumarin ring imitates the naphthoquinone, and so warfarin competitively inhibits **vitamin K epoxide reductase**. Because already-made clotting factors must be used up first, the effect begins only after 2–3 days, and warfarin is useless in an emergency. Features worth marks:

- Almost completely bound to albumin, so displacement (aspirin, sulphonamides, phenylbutazone) suddenly raises the free fraction.
- Metabolised by cytochrome P450; enzyme inducers (rifampicin, phenobarbitone, carbamazepine) weaken it, inhibitors (cimetidine, metronidazole, fluconazole) strengthen it. Eating large amounts of green leafy vegetables also weakens it.
- Monitoring is by **prothrombin time, reported as INR**. The antidote is **vitamin K**, with fresh frozen plasma or prothrombin complex when bleeding is severe.
- It crosses the placenta and is teratogenic, so **heparin is used in pregnancy** instead.
- **Anisindione** is an indanedione anticoagulant acting by the same mechanism, now little used because of hypersensitivity reactions.
- **Clopidogrel** is not an anticoagulant but an **antiplatelet** drug: it is a thienopyridine prodrug activated by cytochrome P450 to a metabolite that irreversibly blocks the platelet **P2Y12 ADP receptor** for the platelet's lifetime. It is used after stenting, in acute coronary syndrome and in stroke prevention.

### Synthesis of warfarin

1. **4-Hydroxycoumarin** is prepared from a salicylate derivative: methyl salicylate (or o-hydroxyacetophenone) is cyclised with ethyl acetate / sodium, giving the 4-hydroxycoumarin ring with its active methylene-like C3 position.
2. **Benzylideneacetone** (4-phenyl-3-buten-2-one) is made by an aldol condensation of **benzaldehyde with acetone** followed by loss of water.
3. The two are combined in a base-catalysed **Michael addition**: C3 of 4-hydroxycoumarin adds across the double bond of benzylideneacetone, giving **warfarin**, which is used as the racemate although the (S)-isomer is several times more potent.

## 3.4 Drugs used in congestive heart failure

In heart failure the ventricle cannot pump enough blood; the body compensates with sympathetic drive and with salt and water retention, which eventually make matters worse. Treatment either strengthens the beat or unloads the heart.

### Cardiac glycosides

**Digoxin** and **digitoxin** come from *Digitalis* species. Each has a **steroid nucleus with a cis-trans-cis ring fusion, an unsaturated five-membered lactone ring at C17 (a cardenolide) and a sugar chain at C3**.

SAR in three lines:

1. The **lactone ring at C17 is essential** — opening or saturating it destroys activity.
2. The **14β-hydroxyl and the cis ring junctions** give the molecule its characteristic bent shape; flattening it abolishes activity.
3. The **sugars** are not needed for action but control water solubility, absorption, protein binding and duration. Digitoxin has one fewer hydroxyl than digoxin, so it is more lipophilic, better absorbed, far more protein bound and much longer acting.

**Mechanism:** the glycoside inhibits the **Na+/K+-ATPase** of the cardiac cell. Intracellular sodium rises, so the sodium-calcium exchanger works less, intracellular calcium rises and contraction becomes stronger (**positive inotropic effect**). Vagal stimulation also slows the AV node, which is why digoxin is used to control the ventricular rate in atrial fibrillation.

**Toxicity** is common because the margin is narrow: anorexia, nausea and vomiting, yellow-green visual haloes, confusion, and arrhythmias of almost any kind. **Hypokalaemia** (often caused by a diuretic given at the same time) greatly increases toxicity, because potassium and the glycoside compete for the same site on the pump. Treatment is to stop the drug, correct potassium, give phenytoin or lidocaine for ventricular arrhythmia, and in severe cases digoxin-specific antibody fragments.

### Newer agents in the syllabus

- **Nesiritide** is recombinant human **B-type natriuretic peptide**. It raises cyclic GMP, dilates veins and arteries and promotes sodium loss; it is given by infusion in acutely decompensated heart failure. Being a peptide, it cannot be given orally.
- **Bosentan** is an orally active **endothelin receptor antagonist** (blocks ETA and ETB). Endothelin-1 is the body's strongest vasoconstrictor, so blocking it lowers pulmonary vascular resistance. Bosentan is used chiefly in **pulmonary arterial hypertension**; it is hepatotoxic and teratogenic, so liver tests and pregnancy prevention are mandatory.
- **Tezosentan** is a short-acting intravenous endothelin antagonist developed for acute heart failure.

Other classes used in heart failure, worth one line each: ACE inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers (reduce afterload and remodelling), beta blockers in low slowly increased doses (carvedilol, bisoprolol, metoprolol), diuretics for congestion, and spironolactone for its survival benefit.

## Remember

1. Vaughan Williams: I sodium (A lengthens, B shortens, C little change), II beta blockers, III potassium, IV calcium.
2. Quinidine → cinchonism; procainamide → lupus-like syndrome; disopyramide → antimuscarinic and negative inotropic; amiodarone → thyroid, lung, cornea, skin.
3. Procainamide is the amide of procaine; the amide resists esterases, so it is orally active.
4. Statins are HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors; lovastatin is a lactone prodrug; the open 3,5-dihydroxy acid mimics HMG-CoA.
5. Resins bind bile acids in the gut, are not absorbed, and interfere with the absorption of other drugs and of fat-soluble vitamins.
6. Warfarin is a 4-hydroxycoumarin bioisostere of vitamin K; it blocks vitamin K epoxide reductase, takes 2–3 days to act, is monitored by INR, is reversed by vitamin K, and is avoided in pregnancy.
7. Clopidogrel is a prodrug that irreversibly blocks the platelet P2Y12 receptor.
8. Cardiac glycoside SAR: C17 lactone essential, 14β-OH and cis ring fusion essential, sugars control pharmacokinetics only.
9. Digoxin inhibits Na+/K+-ATPase → calcium rises → stronger beat; hypokalaemia increases toxicity; yellow-green vision is the classic sign.
10. Syntheses to write without hesitation: disopyramide (phenylacetonitrile → 2-chloropyridine → aminoalkyl chloride → partial hydrolysis) and warfarin (4-hydroxycoumarin + benzylideneacetone, Michael addition).

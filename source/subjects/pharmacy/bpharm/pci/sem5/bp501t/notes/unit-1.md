# Unit 1: Antihistaminic agents, anti-ulcer drugs and antineoplastic agents

Three blocks sit in this unit. First the **H1 antihistamines**, second the **H2 blockers and proton pump inhibitors** that reduce gastric acid, third the **antineoplastic (anticancer) agents**. Starred drugs in the syllabus are diphenhydramine hydrochloride, triprolidine hydrochloride, promethazine hydrochloride, cimetidine, mechlorethamine, mercaptopurine and methotrexate. For the anticancer alkylating agents the examinable chemistry is the **alkylation mechanism**, not a laboratory preparation, and that is what these notes cover.

## 1.1 Histamine, its receptors and their distribution

Histamine is **2-(4-imidazolyl)ethylamine**: an imidazole ring carrying a two-carbon chain that ends in a primary amine. It is formed in the body by decarboxylation of the amino acid **histidine** (enzyme: L-histidine decarboxylase, cofactor pyridoxal phosphate) and is destroyed by **diamine oxidase (histaminase)** and by **histamine-N-methyltransferase**.

Storage sites: mast cells of skin, lung and gut; basophils; the enterochromaffin-like (ECL) cells of the stomach wall; and histaminergic neurons of the hypothalamus. Mast cell granules hold it bound to heparin. Release follows antigen bridging of IgE on the mast cell surface (type I hypersensitivity) or direct displacement by chemicals such as tubocurarine, morphine and radiocontrast media.

| Receptor | Main site | Effect when stimulated | Transduction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | Bronchial and gut smooth muscle, capillary endothelium, sensory nerves, brain | Bronchoconstriction, vasodilation with oedema and itch, wakefulness | Gq → IP3 / DAG, calcium rises |
| H2 | Gastric parietal cell, heart, uterus | Acid secretion, tachycardia | Gs → cAMP rises |
| H3 | Presynaptic in the CNS | Less histamine release (autoreceptor) | Gi |
| H4 | Eosinophils, mast cells, bone marrow | Chemotaxis in inflammation | Gi |

The **triple response of Lewis** (red spot → flare → wheal) after an intradermal injection is the classical demonstration of H1 vasodilation, axon-reflex flare and capillary leak.

## 1.2 H1 antihistamines: general structure and SAR

Almost every classical H1 blocker fits one skeleton:

```text
   Ar1 ╲
        X — CH2 — CH2 — N(R)2      X = O (ethanolamines)
   Ar2 ╱                            X = N (ethylenediamines)
                                    X = CH (propylamines / alkylamines)
                                    Ar1–Ar2 joined by S and N = phenothiazines
                                    Ar–CH–N in a ring = piperazines, tricyclics
```

SAR points examiners ask for:

1. **Two aryl rings** (phenyl, substituted phenyl, pyridyl, thienyl) are needed for good potency; one ring alone gives weak activity. The rings must not be coplanar — a slight twist fits the receptor best.
2. **The connecting atom X** sorts the drugs into chemical classes and decides the side effects: oxygen (ethanolamines) gives strong sedation and marked anticholinergic action; nitrogen (ethylenediamines) gives more gastrointestinal upset; carbon (alkylamines) gives the least sedation and the highest potency.
3. **A two-carbon chain** between X and the nitrogen is optimal; lengthening or branching reduces activity, except for a methyl on the alpha carbon, which can improve it.
4. **A tertiary amine** is essential. It is protonated at body pH, and the cation binds an anionic site on the receptor. Primary, secondary or quaternary nitrogen lowers activity; quaternisation also removes central effects.
5. **Chirality.** In the propylamines the (S)-isomer is far more active (dexchlorpheniramine is the active enantiomer of chlorpheniramine); in the ethanolamines the two isomers differ little.
6. **Halogen or small lipophilic substituent para on one ring** (chlorpheniramine, chlorcyclizine) raises potency and duration.
7. **Lipophilicity decides sedation.** Second-generation drugs are more polar or carry a carboxylic acid (cetirizine) or are P-glycoprotein substrates, so they stay out of the brain.

## 1.3 Classification of H1 antihistamines with uses

| Chemical class | Examples | Notes and uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ethanolamines | Diphenhydramine*, dimenhydrinate, doxylamine succinate, clemastine fumarate, diphenylpyraline | Strongly sedative and anticholinergic; motion sickness, allergy, insomnia, antiparkinsonian use of diphenhydramine |
| Ethylenediamines | Tripelennamine, antazoline | Moderately sedative, gastric upset common |
| Alkylamines (propylamines) | Chlorpheniramine maleate, triprolidine*, pheniramine | Least sedative of the first generation, highly potent, used by day |
| Piperazines | Chlorcyclizine, meclizine, buclizine, cetirizine, levocetirizine | Long acting; meclizine and buclizine for vertigo and motion sickness |
| Phenothiazines | Promethazine*, trimeprazine | Sedative, antiemetic, antipruritic; promethazine also used in anaphylaxis as an adjunct |
| Tricyclics / miscellaneous | Cyproheptadine, azatadine | Cyproheptadine also blocks 5-HT and stimulates appetite |
| Second generation | Astemizole, loratadine, cetirizine, levocetirizine, fexofenadine | Non-sedating, once daily; astemizole and terfenadine were withdrawn for QT prolongation |
| Mast cell stabiliser | Cromolyn sodium (sodium cromoglycate) | Not a receptor blocker; it prevents mast cell degranulation and is used prophylactically in asthma and allergic rhinitis |

**Common uses:** allergic rhinitis, urticaria and pruritus, drug and insect-bite reactions, motion sickness and vertigo, as antiemetics, in cough mixtures, and (first generation only) as night sedatives. They relieve the symptoms of anaphylaxis but never replace adrenaline.

### Synthesis of diphenhydramine hydrochloride

1. **Benzhydrol** (diphenylmethanol) is treated with hydrochloric acid or thionyl chloride to give **benzhydryl chloride**.
2. The halide is heated with **2-dimethylaminoethanol** with a base to take up the hydrogen chloride; a Williamson ether synthesis gives 2-(benzhydryloxy)-N,N-dimethylethylamine.
3. Treatment with hydrogen chloride in dry solvent gives the **hydrochloride salt**. (Combining this base with 8-chlorotheophylline gives **dimenhydrinate**.)

### Synthesis of triprolidine hydrochloride

1. **4-Methylacetophenone** is condensed with **2-pyridinecarbaldehyde**-derived intermediates in the usual route; practically, 1-(4-methylphenyl)-1-(2-pyridyl) ketone is first made by reacting 2-cyanopyridine with the Grignard reagent from 4-bromotoluene, then hydrolysing.
2. The ketone reacts with the Grignard reagent or the carbanion from **1-(2-chloroethyl)pyrrolidine**/pyrrolidine-containing fragment to give the tertiary alcohol.
3. **Dehydration** with acid gives the propenylamine; the desired geometry is the **(E)-isomer**, which is separated and converted to the hydrochloride.

### Synthesis of promethazine hydrochloride

1. **Phenothiazine** is treated with **sodium amide** (or sodium hydride) in an inert solvent to give the sodium salt at the ring nitrogen.
2. The salt is alkylated with **1-dimethylamino-2-chloropropane**, giving the branched three-carbon side chain that distinguishes promethazine from the antipsychotic phenothiazines.
3. The base is converted to the **hydrochloride**. Remember the rule: a **two-carbon branched chain (isopropyl type) gives an antihistamine**, a straight **three-carbon chain gives an antipsychotic**.

## 1.4 H2 antagonists

Blocking H2 receptors on the parietal cell stops histamine-driven acid secretion and reduces the response to gastrin and acetylcholine as well. They are used in peptic ulcer, gastro-oesophageal reflux and stress ulcer prophylaxis.

**SAR in brief.** Cimetidine came from histamine itself: an imidazole ring was kept, the side chain was lengthened to four atoms with a **thioether** in it, and the basic amino group was replaced by a **neutral, polar, planar guanidine-type group (cyanoguanidine)**, because a charged amine gave agonist activity. Later drugs replaced the imidazole by **furan (ranitidine)** or **thiazole (famotidine)** and used nitro-ethenediamine or sulphamoylamidine end groups; these are more potent and lack cimetidine's antiandrogenic effect and its inhibition of cytochrome P450.

| Drug | Ring | Polar end group | Point to remember |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cimetidine* | Imidazole | Cyanoguanidine | Inhibits CYP enzymes, gynaecomastia on long use |
| Ranitidine | Furan with dimethylaminomethyl | Nitroethenediamine | More potent, fewer interactions |
| Famotidine | Thiazole with guanidine | Sulphamoylamidine | Most potent of the three |

### Synthesis of cimetidine

1. **4-Methyl-5-(chloromethyl)imidazole hydrochloride** (obtained from the hydroxymethyl compound with thionyl chloride) is reacted with **cysteamine (2-mercaptoethylamine)**; the thiol displaces chloride to give 2-[(4-methylimidazol-5-yl)methylthio]ethylamine.
2. This amine is treated with **N-cyano-N',S-dimethylisothiourea** (made from dimethyl cyanodithioimidocarbonate and methylamine); methanethiol is lost and the cyanoguanidine is formed.
3. The product is crystallised to give **cimetidine**.

## 1.5 Gastric proton pump inhibitors

Omeprazole, lansoprazole, rabeprazole and pantoprazole are **substituted benzimidazoles linked by a sulphinyl (S=O) bridge to a pyridine ring**. They are prodrugs.

```text
Benzimidazole — S(=O) — CH2 — pyridine(substituted)
        │ accumulates in the acid space of the parietal cell (pH ~1)
        │ protonated, then rearranges
   active sulphenamide (cyclic, cationic)
        │ forms S—S bonds with cysteine residues
   H+/K+-ATPase irreversibly inhibited
```

Points for the exam: they are weak bases that concentrate only in the secretory canaliculus, so action is selective; they must be given as **enteric-coated** forms because they decompose in stomach acid; inhibition is **irreversible**, so acid output returns only when new pump protein is made (which is why a short half-life still gives a long effect); best taken before a meal, when pumps are active. Uses: peptic and duodenal ulcer, reflux disease, Zollinger-Ellison syndrome, and with antibiotics for *Helicobacter pylori*.

## 1.6 Antineoplastic agents

Cancer chemotherapy attacks dividing cells, so the main toxicities are shared: bone marrow depression, mucositis, hair loss, nausea and vomiting, infertility and immunosuppression.

### Classification

| Class | Members in the syllabus | Mechanism in one line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alkylating agents | Mechlorethamine*, cyclophosphamide, melphalan, chlorambucil, busulfan, thiotepa | Attach alkyl groups to DNA bases (mainly N7 of guanine), cross-link the strands |
| Antimetabolites | Mercaptopurine*, thioguanine, fluorouracil, floxuridine, cytarabine, methotrexate*, azathioprine | Fake building blocks that block nucleotide synthesis |
| Antibiotics | Dactinomycin, daunorubicin, doxorubicin, bleomycin | Intercalate DNA, inhibit topoisomerase II, make free radicals |
| Plant products | Etoposide, vinblastine sulphate, vincristine sulphate | Act on the mitotic spindle or topoisomerase II |
| Miscellaneous | Cisplatin, mitotane | Platinum cross-links DNA; mitotane is adrenolytic |

### Alkylating agents

The nitrogen mustards carry a **bis(2-chloroethyl)amino group**. In the body the nitrogen lone pair displaces one chloride internally to form a strained three-membered **aziridinium (ethyleneimonium) ion**, a powerful electrophile. This ion alkylates the **N7 of guanine**; the second arm then alkylates another guanine, giving an **interstrand cross-link** that stops replication. Alkylated guanine also mispairs with thymine and depurinates. Because they do not need the cell to be in a particular phase, alkylating agents are **cell cycle non-specific**.

Structural differences that matter:

- **Mechlorethamine** is the most reactive and must be given intravenously into a running drip; it is a vesicant.
- **Cyclophosphamide** is a cyclic phosphamide **prodrug**, inactive until hydroxylated by liver cytochrome P450 to 4-hydroxycyclophosphamide, which opens to phosphoramide mustard (active) and acrolein (bladder toxin). **Mesna** is given with it to trap acrolein and prevent haemorrhagic cystitis.
- **Melphalan** (phenylalanine mustard) and **chlorambucil** carry an aromatic ring, which lowers the nitrogen's basicity and makes them stable enough for oral use.
- **Busulfan** is an alkyl disulphonate (methanesulphonate ester); it acts mainly on myeloid cells and is used in chronic myeloid leukaemia; pulmonary fibrosis is its typical late toxicity.
- **Thiotepa** contains three aziridine rings already formed.

### Antimetabolites

| Drug | Resembles | Mechanism | Main use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mercaptopurine* | Hypoxanthine (6-SH replaces 6-OH) | Converted to thio-IMP, blocks purine synthesis; inactivated by xanthine oxidase, so allopurinol raises its levels | Acute leukaemia |
| Thioguanine | Guanine | Same family as above | Acute leukaemia |
| Azathioprine | Prodrug of mercaptopurine | Immunosuppression | Transplants, autoimmune disease |
| Fluorouracil | Uracil (5-F) | FdUMP blocks thymidylate synthase, so DNA lacks thymine ("thymineless death") | Solid tumours, topical in skin cancers |
| Floxuridine | Deoxyuridine analogue | Converted to the same active nucleotide | Infused in liver tumours |
| Cytarabine | Deoxycytidine with arabinose sugar | Ara-CTP inhibits DNA polymerase and is incorporated into DNA; S-phase specific | Acute myeloid leukaemia |
| Methotrexate* | Folic acid | Inhibits dihydrofolate reductase, so no tetrahydrofolate, no thymidylate or purines | Leukaemia, choriocarcinoma; also rheumatoid arthritis and psoriasis in small weekly doses |

**Folinic acid (leucovorin) rescue** restores reduced folate to normal cells after high-dose methotrexate. Methotrexate differs from folic acid in two ways only: an **amino group replaces the 4-hydroxy** of the pteridine, and the **N10 carries a methyl group**; these two changes raise its affinity for dihydrofolate reductase about a thousandfold and make the binding effectively irreversible.

### Synthesis of mercaptopurine

1. **4,5-Diaminopyrimidin-6-ol (4,5-diamino-6-hydroxypyrimidine)** is prepared from ethyl cyanoacetate and thiourea or urea by the standard pyrimidine route, followed by nitrosation at C5 and reduction to the amine.
2. Ring closure of the diamine with **formic acid** builds the imidazole ring, giving **hypoxanthine**.
3. Heating hypoxanthine with **phosphorus pentasulphide** in pyridine replaces the 6-oxo group with a thione, giving **6-mercaptopurine**, isolated as the monohydrate.

### Synthesis of methotrexate

1. **2,4,5,6-Tetraaminopyrimidine** (or its 2,4-diamino-6-hydroxy precursor) is the pteridine building block.
2. It is condensed with **2,3-dibromopropionaldehyde** (a three-carbon unit) together with **p-methylaminobenzoyl-L-glutamic acid**. The reaction builds the pyrazine ring of the pteridine and joins it through a methylene bridge to the N-methyl-p-aminobenzoyl glutamate fragment in one pot.
3. The crude product is purified by dissolving in alkali and reprecipitating at acid pH, giving **methotrexate** (4-amino-10-methylfolic acid).

### Antibiotics, plant products and miscellaneous agents

- **Dactinomycin (actinomycin D)** inserts between G-C pairs and blocks RNA polymerase; used in Wilms' tumour.
- **Daunorubicin and doxorubicin** are anthracyclines: they intercalate, poison topoisomerase II and generate free radicals; their special toxicity is **dose-related cardiomyopathy**.
- **Bleomycin** chelates iron, makes oxygen radicals and cuts DNA strands; marrow sparing, but causes **pulmonary fibrosis**.
- **Vincristine and vinblastine** bind tubulin and stop spindle formation (metaphase arrest); vincristine's limiting toxicity is **neuropathy**, vinblastine's is marrow depression.
- **Etoposide**, from podophyllotoxin, inhibits topoisomerase II and arrests cells in late S / G2.
- **Cisplatin** loses its chloride ligands inside the cell and forms intrastrand cross-links with adjacent guanines, acting much like an alkylating agent; it is highly emetic and **nephrotoxic**, so hydration and amifostine are used.
- **Mitotane**, related to the insecticide DDT, destroys adrenal cortical cells and is used in adrenal carcinoma.

## Remember

1. Histamine = imidazole + two-carbon chain + primary amine, made from histidine; H1 causes itch, wheal and bronchoconstriction, H2 causes acid secretion.
2. H1 blocker skeleton: two aryl rings → X (O, N or C) → two carbons → tertiary amine. X decides the class and the side effects; the amine must be tertiary.
3. Alkylamines are the least sedating first-generation drugs; ethanolamines the most sedating; second-generation drugs stay out of the brain.
4. Promethazine's branched three-carbon side chain makes it an antihistamine, not an antipsychotic.
5. Cimetidine kept histamine's imidazole but replaced the basic amine with a neutral cyanoguanidine; ranitidine uses furan, famotidine thiazole.
6. Proton pump inhibitors are acid-activated prodrugs that irreversibly block H+/K+-ATPase; give enteric coated, before food.
7. Nitrogen mustards act through an aziridinium ion that cross-links guanine N7; cyclophosphamide is a liver-activated prodrug and needs mesna.
8. Methotrexate = folic acid with 4-NH2 and N10-CH3; it blocks dihydrofolate reductase and is rescued by folinic acid.
9. Toxicity tags worth one mark each: doxorubicin → heart, bleomycin → lung, vincristine → nerves, cisplatin → kidney, busulfan → lung fibrosis, cyclophosphamide → bladder.

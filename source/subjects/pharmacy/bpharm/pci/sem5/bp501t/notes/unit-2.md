# Unit 2: Antianginal drugs, diuretics and antihypertensive agents

This unit covers three groups that all act on the circulation: drugs that relieve **angina** (organic nitrates and calcium channel blockers), **diuretics** classified by their site in the nephron, and **antihypertensive agents**. Starred drugs are nitroglycerin, isosorbide dinitrate, acetazolamide, chlorothiazide, furosemide and methyldopate hydrochloride. For the two organic nitrates the examinable chemistry is their **chemical nature, bioactivation and SAR**; nitrate esters are manufactured under strict industrial control and diluted before use, so no preparation procedure is given here.

## 2.1 Angina and the organic nitrates

Angina is chest pain from a mismatch between the oxygen the heart muscle needs and what the coronary flow supplies. Drugs help by lowering the work of the heart (preload and afterload), by widening coronary vessels, or by slowing the rate.

**Chemistry.** The organic nitrates are **esters of nitric acid with an alcohol (R—O—NO2)**. They are not nitro compounds (R—NO2) — a favourite one-mark question. The more nitrate ester groups the molecule has, the more lipophilic and the more potent it is, but the shorter the shelf life; they decompose with heat and shock, so pharmaceutical forms are **diluted on lactose or a similar carrier**, or formulated as sublingual tablets, sprays, ointments and transdermal patches.

**Mechanism of action.**

```text
R—O—NO2  ──(mitochondrial aldehyde dehydrogenase, thiols)──►  NO (nitric oxide)
                 │
      activates soluble guanylate cyclase in smooth muscle
                 │
           GTP ──► cyclic GMP rises
                 │
   protein kinase G: less intracellular Ca2+, myosin light chain dephosphorylated
                 │
             VASODILATION (veins >> arteries)
```

Venodilation lowers venous return, so the ventricle is less stretched and needs less oxygen. Large coronary arteries also dilate and collateral flow improves. **Tolerance** develops within a day of continuous exposure (depletion of tissue thiols and of the activating enzyme), so a **nitrate-free interval** of 8–12 hours each day is part of the treatment. Sublingual use avoids the heavy first-pass metabolism that destroys an oral dose. Nitrates must never be combined with sildenafil-type drugs, because both raise cGMP and the fall in pressure can be severe.

| Drug | Form used | Point to remember |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Amyl nitrite | Inhaled vapour, volatile ester | Very quick, very brief; also used in cyanide poisoning to form methaemoglobin |
| Nitroglycerin* (glyceryl trinitrate) | Sublingual tablet, spray, patch, infusion | The standard for an acute attack; tablets lose potency on storage |
| Isosorbide dinitrate* | Sublingual and oral | Active metabolite isosorbide 5-mononitrate gives a longer action |
| Pentaerythritol tetranitrate | Oral | Long acting, used for prophylaxis |
| Dipyridamole | Oral | Not a nitrate: inhibits adenosine uptake and phosphodiesterase; mainly an antiplatelet drug, can cause coronary steal |

**SAR of organic nitrates in brief.** Activity needs the **—O—NO2 ester group**; replacing it by —NO2 abolishes it. Polyol esters (glycerol, isosorbide, pentaerythritol) are used because a polyol gives several ester groups on a small framework. Increasing the number of nitrate groups raises lipophilicity, potency and the risk of decomposition; partial denitration in the body yields mono- and dinitrates that are still active but more water soluble and longer lasting.

**Manufacture, in general terms.** Both starred nitrates are nitrate esters made industrially by the controlled esterification of the parent alcohol (glycerol; isosorbide) with nitrating acid, then carefully washed, neutralised and immediately diluted or formulated. In an exam answer state the reaction type (**esterification of a polyol with nitric acid**), the product and the need for dilution and stabilisation — the plant procedure itself is not an exam requirement and is not reproduced here.

## 2.2 Calcium channel blockers

These block the **L-type voltage-gated calcium channel**, so less calcium enters cardiac and smooth muscle. The result is vasodilation, less force of cardiac contraction and, for some, slowing of the SA and AV nodes.

| Chemical class | Drugs | Where they act most | Uses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Phenylalkylamines | Verapamil, bepridil hydrochloride | Heart (rate and conduction) | Angina, supraventricular arrhythmia, hypertension |
| Benzothiazepines | Diltiazem hydrochloride | Heart and vessels equally | Angina, hypertension |
| Dihydropyridines | Nifedipine, amlodipine, felodipine, nicardipine, nimodipine | Vessels (little cardiac effect) | Hypertension, angina; nimodipine for cerebral vasospasm after subarachnoid haemorrhage |

SAR of the dihydropyridines: a **1,4-dihydropyridine ring** with ester groups at C3 and C5, a methyl at C2 and C6 and an **aryl group at C4** that must be roughly perpendicular to the ring. *Ortho* or *meta* substitution on that aryl ring (nitro in nifedipine) gives high activity; *para* substitution lowers it. Oxidation of the ring to a pyridine destroys activity, which is why nifedipine must be protected from light. Different esters change duration: amlodipine's basic side chain gives a very long half-life and once-daily dosing.

Dihydropyridines can cause reflex tachycardia, flushing, headache and ankle oedema; verapamil causes constipation and must not be combined with a beta blocker because both depress the AV node.

## 2.3 Diuretics

A diuretic increases urine volume by reducing sodium reabsorption; water follows the sodium. Classify them by their site in the nephron.

```text
 Glomerulus
    │
 Proximal tubule ──────────── carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (acetazolamide)
    │                         osmotic diuretics act here and in the loop (mannitol)
 Descending limb
 Thick ascending limb ─────── loop diuretics (furosemide, bumetanide, ethacrynic acid)
    │                         Na+/K+/2Cl- carrier
 Distal convoluted tubule ─── thiazides (chlorothiazide, hydrochlorothiazide)
    │                         Na+/Cl- carrier
 Collecting duct ──────────── potassium-sparing (spironolactone, triamterene, amiloride)
```

### Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors

Acetazolamide, methazolamide and dichlorphenamide are **sulphonamides with an unsubstituted —SO2NH2 group**, which is essential: it binds the zinc at the active site of carbonic anhydrase. Blocking the enzyme stops the supply of hydrogen ions for Na+/H+ exchange, so sodium bicarbonate and water are lost. The urine turns alkaline and the blood develops a **hyperchloraemic metabolic acidosis**, which is also why the drug becomes less effective after a few days (self-limiting).

SAR: a **heterocyclic ring carrying a free sulphamoyl group**; substitution on the sulphonamide nitrogen abolishes activity; an acyl group on the ring amino (acetazolamide) improves pharmacokinetics.

Uses: glaucoma (less aqueous humour), acute mountain sickness, certain epilepsies, and to alkalinise urine.

**Synthesis of acetazolamide.** 1. **Thiosemicarbazide** is heated with **ammonium thiocyanate** to build the 2-amino-5-mercapto-1,3,4-thiadiazole ring. 2. The amino group is acetylated with **acetic anhydride**. 3. The mercapto group is **chlorinated with chlorine in aqueous acid** to give the sulphonyl chloride. 4. Treatment with **ammonia** converts it to the sulphonamide, giving acetazolamide.

### Thiazides and related drugs

Chlorothiazide, hydrochlorothiazide, hydroflumethiazide and cyclothiazide share the **benzothiadiazine-1,1-dioxide** nucleus. They block the Na+/Cl- co-transporter in the distal tubule.

```text
            Cl (or CF3) at 6
              │
   H2NO2S ──[ benzothiadiazine 1,1-dioxide ]── position 3 substituent
   at position 7                                 2,3-dihydro = more potent
```

SAR points:

1. A **free sulphamoyl group at position 7** is essential for activity.
2. An **electron-withdrawing group at position 6** (chlorine, trifluoromethyl) is needed; moving it to position 7 destroys activity.
3. **Saturating the 3,4-double bond** (hydrochlorothiazide versus chlorothiazide) raises potency about ten times.
4. A **lipophilic substituent at position 3** (as in cyclothiazide) increases potency and duration.
5. Substitution on N2 with a small alkyl group increases lipophilicity and duration.

Effects and uses: moderate diuresis, lower blood pressure (initially by volume loss, later by reduced peripheral resistance), reduced urinary calcium (so useful in calcium stones) and a paradoxical benefit in nephrogenic diabetes insipidus. Unwanted effects: hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, hyperuricaemia, hyperglycaemia and raised lipids.

**Synthesis of chlorothiazide.** 1. **m-Chloroaniline** (3-chloroaniline) is treated with **chlorosulphonic acid**, which puts two chlorosulphonyl groups on the ring at positions 4 and 6. 2. Treatment with **ammonia** converts both to sulphonamides, giving 4-amino-6-chlorobenzene-1,3-disulphonamide. 3. Ring closure with **formic acid** (or formamide) joins the aromatic amine and one sulphonamide nitrogen through a one-carbon bridge, giving **chlorothiazide**. (Using formaldehyde instead of formic acid at step 3 gives the 3,4-dihydro compound, **hydrochlorothiazide**.)

### Loop (high-ceiling) diuretics

Furosemide, bumetanide and ethacrynic acid block the **Na+/K+/2Cl- carrier in the thick ascending limb**, where about a quarter of filtered sodium is reabsorbed — hence the large, rapid diuresis. They also lose calcium and magnesium in the urine and can cause hypokalaemic alkalosis, dehydration and, in high intravenous doses, **ototoxicity**.

Uses: acute pulmonary oedema and heart failure, oedema of renal or hepatic origin, hypercalcaemia, and forced diuresis in poisoning.

SAR: furosemide is an **anthranilic acid (5-sulphamoyl-2-aminobenzoic acid) derivative** — it needs a free carboxylic acid, a sulphamoyl group and a lipophilic substituent on the nitrogen (furfuryl in furosemide, phenoxy in bumetanide). Ethacrynic acid is chemically different: an **aryloxyacetic acid with an α,β-unsaturated ketone** that binds sulphydryl groups, so it works where sulphonamides fail.

**Synthesis of furosemide.** 1. **2,4-Dichlorobenzoic acid** is treated with **chlorosulphonic acid** to introduce a chlorosulphonyl group at position 5. 2. Reaction with **ammonia** gives the 5-sulphamoyl compound. 3. The chlorine at position 2 is displaced by **furfurylamine** (nucleophilic aromatic substitution, helped by the electron-withdrawing groups), giving **4-chloro-2-(furfurylamino)-5-sulphamoylbenzoic acid**, that is furosemide.

### Potassium-sparing diuretics and osmotic diuretics

- **Spironolactone** is a steroid that competes with aldosterone at its cytoplasmic receptor in the collecting duct; it spares potassium, is used in heart failure, resistant hypertension, ascites of cirrhosis and primary hyperaldosteronism, and causes gynaecomastia because of its steroid nucleus. Its metabolite canrenone is also active.
- **Triamterene and amiloride** are not steroids; they block the epithelial sodium channel (ENaC) directly, so they work whether or not aldosterone is present.
- All three carry a real risk of **hyperkalaemia**, especially with ACE inhibitors or potassium supplements.
- **Mannitol** is a sugar alcohol given intravenously; it is filtered, not reabsorbed, and holds water in the tubule by osmosis. Uses: raised intracranial pressure, acute glaucoma, and to maintain urine flow in impending renal failure. It must not be used in established anuria or pulmonary oedema, because it first expands the plasma volume.

## 2.4 Antihypertensive agents

Blood pressure is cardiac output multiplied by peripheral resistance, so a drug can act on the heart, the vessels, the kidney or the nervous control of all three.

| Group | Drugs in the syllabus | Mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beta blocker | Timolol | Blocks β1: less output, less renin; also used as eye drops in glaucoma |
| ACE inhibitors | Captopril, lisinopril, enalapril, benazepril hydrochloride, quinapril hydrochloride | Block the conversion of angiotensin I to II and slow the breakdown of bradykinin |
| Central α2 agonists | Clonidine hydrochloride, methyldopate hydrochloride*, guanabenz acetate | Stimulate α2 receptors in the brainstem, reducing sympathetic outflow |
| Adrenergic neuron blocker | Guanethidine monosulphate | Enters the nerve ending and prevents noradrenaline release |
| Vasodilators | Hydralazine hydrochloride, minoxidil, diazoxide, sodium nitroprusside | Relax arterioles directly; nitroprusside releases nitric oxide and dilates both sides |
| Rauwolfia alkaloid | Reserpine | Empties vesicular stores of amines |

Notes worth marks:

- **ACE inhibitors** all carry a zinc-binding group: a **sulphydryl in captopril**, a **carboxylate in enalapril and lisinopril**, a **phosphinate in fosinopril**. Captopril's thiol explains its rash and taste disturbance. Most of the others are **ester prodrugs** (enalapril → enalaprilat; quinapril → quinaprilat). The **dry cough** and the rare angio-oedema come from accumulated bradykinin. They are contraindicated in pregnancy and in bilateral renal artery stenosis.
- **Methyldopa** is itself a prodrug: it is decarboxylated and hydroxylated in the nerve ending to **α-methylnoradrenaline**, a false transmitter that is a strong α2 agonist, so central sympathetic outflow falls. It is still the preferred drug in **hypertension of pregnancy**. **Methyldopate hydrochloride** is its ethyl ester, made water soluble for injection. Side effects include sedation, a positive Coombs test and haemolytic anaemia, and drug fever.
- **Clonidine** is an imidazoline α2 agonist; sudden withdrawal causes rebound hypertension.
- **Sodium nitroprusside** is given only by infusion, protected from light, and its cyanide-containing structure limits the duration of use.
- **Minoxidil and diazoxide** open potassium channels; minoxidil causes hirsutism (used topically for baldness) and diazoxide raises blood sugar (used in hypoglycaemia from insulinoma).
- **Hydralazine** causes reflex tachycardia and, on long use, a lupus-like syndrome in slow acetylators.

**Synthesis of methyldopate hydrochloride.** 1. **Vanillin**-derived or **3,4-dimethoxyphenylacetone** routes are used industrially; the classical teaching route starts from **3,4-dimethoxyphenylacetone**, which is converted by the **Strecker reaction** (sodium cyanide and ammonium chloride/ammonia) to the amino nitrile. 2. **Hydrolysis** of the nitrile with acid gives racemic 3-(3,4-dimethoxyphenyl)-2-methylalanine; the methyl ethers are cleaved with hydrogen iodide or hydrobromic acid to free the catechol, giving racemic methyldopa, and the **(S)-isomer is resolved** (only the levo form is active). 3. **Esterification** with ethanol saturated with dry hydrogen chloride gives the ethyl ester as its **hydrochloride**, which is freely water soluble and suitable for intravenous use.

## Remember

1. Organic nitrates are nitric acid **esters** (R—O—NO2); they release nitric oxide, raise cGMP and dilate veins more than arteries.
2. Nitrate tolerance is real: keep a daily nitrate-free interval. Never give a nitrate with sildenafil.
3. Dihydropyridines act on vessels, verapamil on the heart, diltiazem on both. The C4 aryl group must be perpendicular and *ortho/meta* substituted.
4. Diuretic sites: acetazolamide proximal, mannitol osmotic, furosemide thick ascending limb, thiazides distal tubule, spironolactone/amiloride collecting duct.
5. A free —SO2NH2 is essential for acetazolamide, thiazides and furosemide; substituting its nitrogen kills activity.
6. Thiazide SAR: sulphamoyl at 7, electron-withdrawing group at 6, saturated 3,4-bond for ten times the potency.
7. Loop diuretics lose calcium; thiazides keep it. Loop diuretics are ototoxic in high intravenous doses.
8. ACE inhibitors: zinc-binding group (SH, COOH, phosphinate); cough from bradykinin; most are ester prodrugs; avoid in pregnancy.
9. Methyldopa works through the false transmitter α-methylnoradrenaline and is the drug of choice in pregnancy; methyldopate is its soluble ester for injection.
10. Syntheses to write without hesitation: acetazolamide (thiadiazole → acetylate → sulphonyl chloride → ammonia), chlorothiazide (chlorosulphonation → ammonia → formic acid ring closure), furosemide (chlorosulphonation → ammonia → furfurylamine), methyldopate (Strecker → hydrolyse and demethylate → resolve → esterify).

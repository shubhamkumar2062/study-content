# Unit 5: Pharmaceutical legislation history, code of ethics, MTP Act, RTI Act and IPR

This last unit is shorter in hours but full of short-answer questions: the **history of pharmaceutical legislation** and the committees behind it, the **Code of Pharmaceutical Ethics** and the pharmacist's oath, the **Medical Termination of Pregnancy Act**, the **Right to Information Act** and an **introduction to intellectual property rights**.

## 5.1 Pharmaceutical legislation in India — a brief review

### Why pharmaceutical laws are needed

- Medicines can **heal or harm**; the public cannot judge their quality by looking at them.
- Without control, **spurious and substandard** drugs, **misleading advertisements** and **unqualified sellers** flourish.
- Laws protect the public, set standards for education and practice, control **habit-forming drugs**, keep **prices** reasonable, and meet **international obligations**.

### Drugs Enquiry Committee (1930–31)

- Appointed by the Government of India in **1930** under the chairmanship of **Col. R. N. Chopra**, to enquire into the extent of sale of **substandard and adulterated drugs** and to suggest remedies. Report in **1931**.
- **Findings:** a large quantity of substandard, adulterated and spurious drugs on the market; no control on import, manufacture or sale; dispensing and sale by unqualified people; no pharmacy profession as such.
- **Main recommendations:**
  1. A **central law** to control the import, manufacture, distribution and sale of drugs.
  2. A **Central Drugs Laboratory** and provincial laboratories for testing.
  3. **Drugs control departments** at the Centre and in the provinces with trained inspectors.
  4. Regulation of the **profession of pharmacy**: education, registration, and dispensing only by qualified persons.
  5. A **pharmacopoeia** of India and standards for drugs.
- **Results:** the **Drugs Act, 1940**, the **Pharmacy Act, 1948**, and later the Indian Pharmacopoeia.

### Health Survey and Development Committee (1943–46)

- Chaired by **Sir Joseph Bhore**; appointed in **1943**, reported in **1946** (often called the **Bhore Committee**).
- Surveyed the health conditions and organisation of India and planned future development.
- **Recommendations relevant to pharmacy:** integrate preventive and curative services; set up **primary health centres**; improve **training of health personnel**, including pharmacists; production of essential drugs in the country; proper control of drugs and dispensing.

### Health Survey and Planning Committee — Mudaliar Committee (1959–61)

- Chaired by **Dr A. Lakshmanaswami Mudaliar**; reviewed progress since the Bhore report; reported in **1961**.
- **Recommendations:** strengthen primary health centres before expanding; improve the **quality of pharmacy education** and the status of pharmacists; strengthen **drug control** at Centre and States; expand **indigenous production** of drugs; regional drug-testing laboratories.

### Hathi Committee (1974–75)

- The **Committee on Drugs and Pharmaceutical Industry**, chaired by **Jaisukhlal Hathi**, appointed in **1974**, report in **1975**.
- **Main recommendations:**
  1. Prepare a **list of essential drugs** (it listed 117 essential drugs) and give them priority in production and supply.
  2. **Promote the Indian sector**, especially public sector units, to lead the industry; reduce the dominance of foreign companies.
  3. Use of **generic names**, with brand names to be abolished in a phased manner, starting with some common drugs.
  4. Set up a **National Drug Authority** to look after drug policy, quality and prices.
  5. Strengthen **quality control** and **price control** of drugs.
  6. Encourage **research** and production of bulk drugs in India.
- These ideas shaped the **Drug Policy of 1978** and later price control.

## 5.2 Code of Pharmaceutical Ethics

### Definition

**Ethics** is the set of **moral principles** that guide the conduct of a person. A **code of pharmaceutical ethics** is the set of standards of **professional conduct** framed by the **Pharmacy Council of India** to guide pharmacists in their relations with patients, the public, the trade, the medical profession and fellow pharmacists. Many of these duties are also part of the **Pharmacy Practice Regulations, 2015**.

### Pharmacist in relation to his job

1. **Scope of service:** a pharmacy should be open for a reasonable time and should stock and supply all **necessary medicines**; emergency supply should be arranged.
2. **Conduct of the pharmacy:** clean, orderly premises; work done only by or under a **registered pharmacist**.
3. **Handling of prescriptions:** check the prescription; **do not change** it without the prescriber's consent; if an error or overdose is suspected, **contact the prescriber**; never discuss the prescriber's choice with the patient in a way that lowers confidence in the doctor.
4. **Dispensing:** accurate compounding, proper labelling, correct directions; use only drugs of **standard quality**.
5. **Handling of drugs:** store as labelled; take special care with **poisons, narcotic and habit-forming drugs**; do not supply them for misuse.
6. **Apprentice and staff:** train apprentices properly; supervise staff.
7. **Confidentiality:** keep patient information **secret** except where the law requires disclosure.

### Pharmacist in relation to his trade

1. **Prices** should be fair, not excessive; follow notified prices (MRP, ceiling price).
2. **No unfair competition** — no price wars, no running down of other pharmacies.
3. **No misleading advertising**; no false claims for products.
4. **Fair dealing** with suppliers and customers; honour contracts.
5. **No sale of substandard, spurious or expired** goods; no sale of habit-forming drugs without proper prescription.
6. **Hawking** of drugs (selling from door to door or in streets) should not be practised.
7. The **pharmacy's name and display** should be dignified.

### Pharmacist in relation to the medical profession

1. **Respect and cooperate** with medical practitioners; act as the doctor's partner in patient care.
2. **No counter-prescribing** or diagnosing except in genuine emergencies (first aid) — refer patients to a doctor.
3. **No secret arrangements** with doctors for **commission** or for recommending a particular pharmacy.
4. Do not **recommend a particular doctor** to patients for gain.
5. Inform the prescriber tactfully of errors in a prescription.

### Pharmacist in relation to his profession

1. Uphold the **dignity and honour** of the profession.
2. Keep up to date by **continuing education**; contribute to professional knowledge.
3. Be a member of **professional associations** and support their work.
4. Do not do anything that **brings discredit** to the profession; report unethical conduct.
5. Help **fellow pharmacists**; do not take unfair advantage of them.
6. **Obey the laws** relating to drugs and pharmacy.

### Pharmacist's oath (in substance)

A pharmacist solemnly promises to:

- Devote himself to a lifetime of **service to others** through the profession of pharmacy;
- Consider the **welfare of humanity and relief of suffering** his primary concerns;
- Apply his **knowledge, experience and skills** to the best of his ability for the best outcomes for patients;
- **Respect and protect** all personal and health information entrusted to him;
- **Keep up to date** with developments in the profession;
- Hold himself to the highest principles of **moral, ethical and legal conduct**;
- Embrace and advocate changes that **improve patient care**;
- Use his knowledge to **prevent misuse** of medicines and to promote the public's health.

## 5.3 Medical Termination of Pregnancy Act, 1971 (amended 2021)

### Objectives

1. To provide for **termination of certain pregnancies by registered medical practitioners** under safe conditions.
2. To reduce **maternal deaths and illness** caused by unsafe abortion.
3. To lay down **when, where and by whom** a pregnancy may be terminated.

### Definitions

- **Registered medical practitioner (RMP):** a medical practitioner with a recognised medical qualification, registered under the medical registration laws, and having the **experience or training in gynaecology and obstetrics** laid down by the rules.
- **Guardian:** a person who has care of a minor or a mentally ill woman.
- **Termination of pregnancy:** a procedure to end a pregnancy by medical or surgical methods.

### When a pregnancy may be terminated (as amended in 2021, in force from 24 September 2021)

| Length of pregnancy | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Up to **20 weeks** | Opinion of **one RMP** |
| **20 to 24 weeks** | Opinion of **two RMPs**, only for **special categories of women** notified in the rules (for example, survivors of sexual assault or incest, minors, women whose marital status changes during pregnancy, women with disabilities) |
| Beyond **24 weeks** | Only for **substantial foetal abnormalities**, on the decision of a **State Medical Board** |
| Any time | To **save the life** of the woman, on the opinion of one RMP formed in good faith |

**Grounds** (the RMP's good-faith opinion): continuing the pregnancy would risk the **life** of the woman or cause grave injury to her **physical or mental health**; or there is a substantial risk that the child would have **serious physical or mental abnormality**. Pregnancy caused by **rape** is presumed to cause grave injury to mental health; pregnancy from **failure of a contraceptive** used by **any woman or her partner** may also be treated as such (the 2021 amendment removed the earlier "married woman" limit).

### Consent

- The **woman's written consent** is required.
- For a **minor** (below 18 years) or a **mentally ill** woman, the written consent of the **guardian**.

### Place

Termination may be done only in a **Government hospital** or a place **approved** by the Government (or a District Level Committee). In an emergency to save life, it may be done elsewhere by an RMP.

### Confidentiality and penalties

- **Section 5A** (2021): the name and particulars of a woman whose pregnancy has been terminated must not be revealed except to a person authorised by law; breaking this is punishable with imprisonment up to **one year**, or fine, or both.
- Termination by a person who is not an RMP, or at an unapproved place, is an offence punishable under the **criminal law** (Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, which replaced the Indian Penal Code from July 2024).
- **Pharmacist's role:** drugs used for medical termination (for example, mifepristone with misoprostol) are prescription drugs to be sold only on an RMP's prescription and kept with records; the pharmacist must not supply them over the counter.

## 5.4 Right to Information Act, 2005

### Objectives

1. To give citizens the **right to access information** under the control of **public authorities**.
2. To promote **transparency and accountability** in government.
3. To **contain corruption** and help citizens participate in governance.

### Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Information** | Any material in any form — records, documents, memos, e-mails, opinions, advice, press releases, circulars, orders, logbooks, contracts, reports, samples, models, electronic data |
| **Public authority** | Any body set up by the Constitution or by law of Parliament or a State, or by Government notification, and bodies **owned, controlled or substantially financed** by Government (including NGOs substantially financed) |
| **Right to information** | Right to inspect work, documents and records, take notes and certified copies, take **certified samples** of material, and get information in electronic form |
| **Public Information Officer (PIO)** | Officer designated in each public authority to deal with requests; **Assistant PIOs** receive requests at sub-divisional level |

### How to get information

```text
Citizen writes an application (English, Hindi or official language of the area)
   + application fee (₹10 for Central public authorities; nil for BPL card holders)
   -> PIO of the public authority (no need to give reasons)
        |
Reply within 30 days
   (48 hours if the information concerns the life or liberty of a person;
    5 more days if given to an Assistant PIO)
        |
No reply / refusal / unsatisfied
   -> First appeal to a senior officer in the same authority (within 30 days)
   -> Second appeal to the Central / State Information Commission (within 90 days)
```

### Exemptions (section 8) — main ones

Information need not be given if it would affect the **sovereignty, security** or economic interests of India; has been **forbidden by a court**; would cause **breach of privilege** of Parliament or a legislature; involves **commercial confidence, trade secrets or intellectual property** (unless larger public interest); is held in a **fiduciary** relationship; was received in confidence from a **foreign government**; would endanger **life or safety** of a person; would **impede investigation**; includes **Cabinet papers** (until decisions are taken); or is **personal information** with no relation to public activity. **Intelligence and security organisations** listed in the Second Schedule are outside the Act (section 24) except for information on corruption and human rights violations.

### Information Commissions

- **Central Information Commission:** a Chief Information Commissioner and up to ten Information Commissioners; **State Information Commissions** similarly.
- They hear complaints and appeals and can impose a **penalty on the PIO** of **₹250 per day** of delay, up to **₹25,000**, for refusing or delaying information without reasonable cause, and recommend disciplinary action.

### Proactive disclosure (section 4)

Every public authority must **publish** its organisation, functions, duties, powers of officers, rules, budget, names of PIOs and other details, and update them regularly.

### Relevance to pharmacy

Citizens and pharmacists can use RTI to get information from bodies such as **CDSCO, State Drug Controllers, NPPA and the Pharmacy Councils** (for example, licensing status, lists of banned drugs, inspection reports), subject to the exemptions. Trade secrets of a manufacturer are protected under section 8.

## 5.5 Introduction to Intellectual Property Rights (IPR)

### Meaning

**Intellectual property** is creation of the **mind** — inventions, literary and artistic works, designs, symbols, names and images used in commerce. **IPR** are legal rights that give the creator **exclusive rights** to use the creation for a **limited time**, in return for disclosure to society. Internationally they are governed by the **TRIPS Agreement** of the **World Trade Organization** (1995).

### Types of IPR in India

| Type | Indian law | What it protects | Term |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Patent** | Patents Act, 1970 | New **inventions** (products or processes) | **20 years** from the date of filing |
| **Trademark** | Trade Marks Act, 1999 | Brand names, logos, symbols that distinguish goods or services | 10 years, **renewable** indefinitely |
| **Copyright** | Copyright Act, 1957 | Literary, artistic, musical works, software | Generally the author's **lifetime + 60 years** |
| **Industrial design** | Designs Act, 2000 | Shape, pattern, ornamentation of an article (for example, a tablet shape or pack) | 10 years + 5 years extension |
| **Geographical indication** | Geographical Indications of Goods Act, 1999 | Goods identified with a place (for example, Darjeeling tea) | 10 years, renewable |
| **Trade secret** | No separate Act; protected by contracts and courts | Confidential know-how, formulae | As long as kept secret |

### Patents — key points for pharmacy

**Criteria for a patent:**

1. **Novelty** — not known or published before.
2. **Inventive step** — not obvious to a person skilled in the art; involves technical advance or economic significance.
3. **Industrial application** — can be made or used in industry.

**Not patentable (section 3) — examples:** discoveries of natural substances, methods of treatment of humans or animals, plants and animals as such (except micro-organisms), mere admixtures, and **section 3(d)** — a **new form of a known substance** (salt, ester, polymorph, isomer, etc.) is not an invention unless it shows **significantly enhanced efficacy**. The Supreme Court upheld this in the **Novartis (imatinib) case, 2013**.

**History in pharmacy:**

```text
Patents Act 1970   -> only PROCESS patents for drugs, food and chemicals
                      (term 5–7 years for these) -> growth of Indian generic industry
TRIPS (1995)       -> India given time to introduce product patents
Amendments 1999, 2002 -> mailbox applications, 20-year term
Patents (Amendment) Act 2005 -> PRODUCT patents for drugs restored; safeguards
                      like section 3(d), pre-grant opposition, compulsory licences
```

**Procedure (outline):** filing (provisional or complete specification) → publication (after 18 months) → request for examination → examination and objections → **pre-grant opposition** → grant → **post-grant opposition** (within one year) → renewal fees each year.

**Flexibilities:**

- **Compulsory licence (section 84):** after three years from grant, anyone may apply if the patented product is not available to the public at a **reasonably affordable price**, or reasonable requirements are not met, or it is not worked in India. India's first compulsory licence (2012) was for **sorafenib** (Natco vs Bayer).
- **Bolar exemption (section 107A):** using a patented invention to develop data for **regulatory approval** is not infringement, so generics can be ready when the patent expires.
- **Parallel import** of patented products under certain conditions.

### Importance of IPR for the pharmaceutical industry

- Rewards **research and development** of new drugs, which is costly and slow.
- Encourages **disclosure** of technology.
- **Brand protection** (trademarks) prevents confusion and look-alike drugs.
- Balanced with **access to medicines** through compulsory licences, 3(d) and price control.

- **Bhore 1943–46** (Health Survey and Development); **Mudaliar 1959–61** (Health Survey and Planning); **Hathi 1974–75** (Drugs and Pharmaceutical Industry; essential drugs, generic names, National Drug Authority).

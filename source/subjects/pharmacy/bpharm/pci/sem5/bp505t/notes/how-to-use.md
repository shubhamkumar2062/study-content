# How to use this guide

Pharmaceutical Jurisprudence is a **memory and structure** subject. There are no numericals except the DPCO ceiling-price calculation. Marks come from writing answers in a clear legal format: the **name and year of the Act**, its **objectives**, the **definition**, the **section number**, the **composition** of a body, and the **offence with its penalty**. Study one unit at a time: read the notes, write out the tables (schedules, licence forms, compositions, penalties) from memory, then answer the question bank. Each unit has very short (2-mark), short (5-mark) and long (10-mark) questions.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |

| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with tables and section numbers.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from common patterns and syllabus weight, not a promise of what will be asked.

**Laws change — what to check before the exam**

This subject is about **current law**, and several things in older textbooks are out of date:

1. **M&TP Act, 1955** was **repealed by section 174 of the CGST Act, 2017** (from 1 July 2017). Learn it, but say so in your answer.
2. **Pharmacy Act penalties** (sections 26A, 41, 42, 43) were changed by the **Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023**, in force from 31 December 2024, with a new **section 43A** for adjudication.
3. **Schedule Y** is replaced by the **New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019**; **Schedule M** was revised in December 2023; blood banks are now **blood centres**; medical devices have their own **Medical Devices Rules, 2017**.
4. **CPCSEA** was renamed **CCSEA** in 2023.
5. **NLEM 2022** (384 medicines) is the current First Schedule of DPCO 2013; a new list may come.
6. Drug bans under section 26A and licence form numbers change often.

Where a book and the official text differ, follow the official text on **cdsco.gov.in**, **indiacode.nic.in**, **pci.nic.in** and **nppaindia.nic.in**, and ask your teacher which version your university expects.

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. Begin each answer with the **full name of the Act, its year**, and a one-line **objective**. Examiners give marks for this framing.
2. Quote **section numbers** where you are sure of them (section 3 PCI, 10 Education Regulations, 19 State Council, 32 registration; D&C 5 DTAB, 6 CDL, 7 DCC, 17/17A/17B, 18, 26A, 27; NDPS 6, 7A, 8, 27, 64A). A wrong number costs more than no number.
3. Put **compositions** of DTAB, DCC, PCI, State Council and the NDPS Consultative Committee in a **table**.
4. For offences, use a three-column table: **offence – section – penalty**.
5. For schedules, write **content + label + example** for each.
6. In DPCO questions, show the **ceiling price formula** with a small worked example.
7. End long answers with a line on **recent amendments** — it shows you know the current law.
8. Very short answers (2 marks): definition + one example or one number.

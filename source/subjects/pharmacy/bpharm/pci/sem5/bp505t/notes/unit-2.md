# Unit 2: Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and Rules, 1945 – II

This unit studies the schedules named in the syllabus in detail (G, H, M, N, P, T, U, V, X, Y, Part XII B with Schedule F, and the schedule of diseases under the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act read with Schedule J), then the licences for **sale**, **labelling and packing**, and the **administration** of the Act: DTAB, CDL, DCC, government analysts, licensing and controlling authorities, and drugs inspectors.

> **Law changes.** Schedule M was replaced by a revised Schedule M in December 2023 (G.S.R. 922(E)); Schedule Y was replaced for new drugs and clinical trials by the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019; blood banks are now called blood centres. Older books describe the earlier versions. Check the current rules on cdsco.gov.in.

## 2.1 Schedules in detail

### Schedule G — drugs to be used under medical supervision

- Drugs in this schedule may be sold **without a prescription**, but must carry the warning:

```text
Caution: It is dangerous to take this preparation except under medical supervision.
```

- Examples (as listed): certain **antihistamines** and **oral antidiabetic** drugs, and some other drugs where unsupervised use is risky.
- The retailer should record the sale details as the rules require.

### Schedule H — prescription drugs

- Sold by retail **only on the prescription of a Registered Medical Practitioner (RMP)**.
- Label: the symbol **Rx** at the top left corner and the warning:

```text
Schedule H drug – Warning: To be sold by retail on the prescription of a
Registered Medical Practitioner only.
```

- If the drug is also in the NDPS Act lists, the label carries **NRx** in red with the same warning.
- The pharmacist must dispense only the quantity prescribed, keep the sale details (patient, prescriber, drug, quantity, batch) and stamp the prescription so it is not reused.
- Examples: most **antibiotics**, **antihypertensives**, **corticosteroids**, **anticancer drugs**, hormones and many others.

### Schedule H1 (added in 2013, in force from 2014)

- Third and fourth generation **antibiotics** (for example, cefepime, meropenem, moxifloxacin), **anti-tuberculosis** drugs (isoniazid, rifampicin, ethambutol, pyrazinamide) and some **habit-forming** drugs (for example, alprazolam, zolpidem, buprenorphine).
- Purpose: to slow **antimicrobial resistance** and curb misuse.
- Label: **Rx in red** and a warning in a box with a red border: it is dangerous to take the preparation except in accordance with medical advice, and it must not be sold without a prescription.
- The retailer keeps a **separate H1 register** (prescriber's name and address, patient's name, drug and quantity), kept for **three years**.

### Schedule X — narcotic and psychotropic drugs needing special care

- Drugs with high potential for misuse, for example **amphetamine**, **methylphenidate**, and barbiturates such as **amobarbital, pentobarbital and secobarbital** (the list is revised from time to time).
- Label: **XRx** in red with the Schedule H type warning.
- **Special licences** for manufacture, import, wholesale and retail.
- Retail sale only on a prescription **in duplicate**; the pharmacist keeps one copy for **two years**.
- Separate records of purchase and sale; drugs stored under **lock and key**.

### Schedule M — Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP)

Schedule M lays down GMP and the requirements of premises, plant and equipment for pharmaceutical products.

```text
Revised Schedule M (G.S.R. 922(E), Dec 2023)
Part I   General GMP: quality management system, quality risk management,
         product quality review, personnel, premises, equipment, sanitation,
         materials, documentation, production, QC, validation,
         complaints and recalls, self-inspection, contract work
Part I-A onward  Specific requirements for each dosage form
         (sterile products, oral solids, oral liquids, topicals, MDIs,
          active pharmaceutical ingredients, biologicals, etc.)
```

- The revised version brings Indian GMP close to **WHO GMP**, adding a **pharmaceutical quality system**, **quality risk management**, **product quality review**, **stability programmes**, **validation** and computerised systems.
- Large manufacturers had to comply first; small and medium firms (MSMEs) were given extended time (to 31 December 2025, on application). Check the current status.
- Schedule M-I covers homoeopathic medicines; M-II and M-III earlier covered cosmetics and devices (devices are now under the Medical Devices Rules, 2017).

### Schedule N — minimum equipment for a pharmacy

| Head | Requirement (main points) |
| --- | --- |
| Premises | Separated from living quarters; clean, well lit and ventilated; adequate area; dispensing section separate and closed to the public |
| Equipment | Balances and weights, measures, mortars and pestles, spatulas, funnels, beakers, water bath, refrigerator, ointment slab, suppository mould and similar items |
| Books | Current **Indian Pharmacopoeia**, **National Formulary of India**, and the Drugs and Cosmetics Act and Rules |
| Staff | A **registered pharmacist** in charge of compounding and dispensing |
| Materials | Drugs and containers as needed for the work done |

### Schedule P — life period of drugs

- Gives the **maximum life period** (shelf life from date of manufacture) and storage conditions for listed drugs, mostly antibiotics, vitamins and some biologicals.
- For drugs not listed, the shelf life is fixed by the manufacturer from **stability data** acceptable to the licensing authority.
- **Schedule P1** lists **pack sizes** for certain drugs.

### Schedule T — GMP for Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani medicines

- Requirements of premises (location, raw material store, manufacturing areas, packing area, quality control area), **equipment** for each dosage form (for example, for churna, gutika, bhasma, asava–arishta, taila, ghrita), **raw material identification**, **personnel** with qualifications in the system, and **documentation**.
- Compliance is a condition of an ASU manufacturing licence.

### Schedule U — manufacturing and analytical records

- **Manufacturing records:** batch number, quantities of raw materials, dates, in-process checks, yield, names of persons who supervised, packing details.
- **Analytical records:** tests on raw materials and finished products, reference to standards, results and signature of the analyst.
- Kept for the period laid down in the rules and shown to inspectors on demand. **Schedule U(1)** covers records for cosmetics.

### Schedule V — standards for patent or proprietary medicines

- Gives limits (minimum and maximum) for **vitamins and minerals** in patent or proprietary preparations sold for prophylaxis or treatment, so that products are neither too weak nor dangerously strong.

### Schedule Y and the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019

Schedule Y laid down how **clinical trials** were to be run and how permission was granted for **import or manufacture of new drugs**: phases of trials, ethics committee approval, informed consent, reporting of serious adverse events and compensation.

Since **2019** these matters are governed by the **New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019**, which replaced Part XA and Schedule Y for new drugs and clinical trials. Key features: **registered ethics committees**, approval timelines, **informed consent** (with audio-video recording in specified cases), **compensation** for trial-related injury or death, and conditions for academic trials and for waiving local trials.

```text
Clinical trial phases
Phase I   -> small number of healthy volunteers; safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics
Phase II  -> patients; dose finding and early efficacy
Phase III -> large patient groups; confirm efficacy and safety (basis of approval)
Phase IV  -> after marketing; post-marketing surveillance
```

### Part XII B and Schedule F — blood banks (blood centres)

- **Part XII B** of the Rules sets out the licensing and working of **blood banks**, now called **blood centres**, and blood component and plasma processing units; **Schedule F (Part XII B)** gives the detailed requirements.
- Covers: licence (granted by the State authority with central approval), **accommodation**, **personnel** (medical officer, technical supervisor, nurse, technicians), **equipment**, **donor selection criteria** (age, weight, haemoglobin, interval between donations), **testing of every unit** (blood group, HIV, hepatitis B and C, syphilis, malaria), **storage temperatures**, **labelling**, records and **discard** of unsuitable units.
- **Schedule F (Part XII C)** covers manufacture of blood products from bulk human plasma.

### Diseases that drugs may not claim to cure — Schedule J and the DMR (OA) Act

- **Schedule J** lists diseases and ailments that no drug may **purport to prevent or cure** (for example, cancer, diabetes, blindness, epilepsy, paralysis, sexual impotence, premature ageing). Such claims make import and sale illegal under the D&C Act.
- The **Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954** has its own **Schedule** of diseases for which **advertisements** are prohibited (Unit 4). Schedule J controls **claims on the drug and its label**, the DMR(OA) Act controls **advertisements**.

## 2.2 Sale of drugs

### Kinds of sale licence

| Licence | For |
| --- | --- |
| Form 20 | Retail sale of drugs other than Schedules C, C(1) and X |
| Form 21 | Retail sale of Schedule C and C(1) drugs (not X) |
| Form 20B | Wholesale of drugs other than C, C(1) and X |
| Form 21B | Wholesale of Schedule C and C(1) drugs |
| Form 20F / 20G | Retail / wholesale of Schedule X drugs |
| Form 20A / 21A | **Restricted licence** (no pharmacist) |

The **State Licensing Authority** grants sale licences. Like manufacturing licences, they now remain in force while the retention fee is paid.

### Conditions for a retail sale licence

1. **Premises:** at least **10 square metres** for retail sale (at least **15 square metres** if retail and wholesale are combined), clean and suitable, with proper storage.
2. **Refrigerator** where drugs need storage at low temperature.
3. Sale under the **personal supervision of a registered pharmacist**.
4. H, H1 and X drugs sold **only on prescription**; prescriptions stamped and records kept.
5. Licence and the pharmacist's name displayed.
6. Expired and damaged drugs kept apart and not sold.

### Conditions for a wholesale licence

1. Adequate premises (same minimum area) and storage.
2. Business under the charge of a **competent person**: a registered pharmacist, or a graduate with one year's experience in dealing with drugs, or a person who has passed the SSC (or equivalent) examination with four years' experience in drug dealing (as the rules list).
3. Sale only to licensed dealers, hospitals, institutions and registered medical practitioners, with proper invoices showing batch numbers and expiry dates.

### Restricted licence

- For dealers who do not employ a pharmacist, usually in **villages** where no pharmacy exists, and for itinerant vendors.
- Allows sale of only **certain drugs** — mainly household remedies — in **original sealed packs** from licensed manufacturers; no opening, mixing or dispensing.

### Offences and penalties for sale

Sale offences fall under **section 27** (the same scale as manufacture, Unit 1): spurious or adulterated drugs causing death or grievous hurt carry the heaviest penalty; sale without a licence carries 3–5 years; other breaches (for example, selling a Schedule H drug without prescription, not keeping records) fall under 27(d). The licence can also be **suspended or cancelled** by the licensing authority.

## 2.3 Labelling and packing of drugs

### General labelling requirements (main points of Rule 96)

The label on the innermost container and every covering must show:

1. **Name of the drug** — the proper (generic) name, shown more prominently than any trade name.
2. **Net content** (weight, volume, number of units).
3. **Content of active ingredients** per dose unit or per stated quantity.
4. **Name and address of the manufacturer** (and of the marketer if different).
5. **Distinctive batch number**.
6. **Manufacturing licence number**.
7. **Date of manufacture** and **date of expiry**.
8. **Maximum retail price** (under DPCO and packaging rules).
9. Special warnings: Schedule symbol (Rx, NRx, XRx), "For external use only", "Not for injection", "Shake well before use", storage conditions.
10. For injections: route of administration, and for multi-dose containers the preservative used.

**Special labels** apply to Schedules G, H, H1 and X (2.1), to ASU drugs ("Ayurvedic/Siddha/Unani medicine"), to drugs for **veterinary use** ("Not for human use; for veterinary use only"), to **physician samples** ("Physician's sample – not to be sold"), and to drugs supplied to the **Government** ("Government supply – not for sale").

### Specimen label (tablet)

```text
+--------------------------------------------------+
| Rx                    PARACETAMOL TABLETS IP 500 mg|
| Each uncoated tablet contains: Paracetamol IP 500 mg|
| 10 tablets                                        |
| Dosage: As directed by the physician              |
| Store in a cool, dry place, protected from light  |
| Mfg. Lic. No.: ......     B. No.: ......           |
| Mfg. date: MM/YYYY        Exp. date: MM/YYYY      |
| M.R.P. ₹ ....  (incl. of all taxes)               |
| Manufactured by: name and full address            |
+--------------------------------------------------+
(Rx and the Schedule H warning appear only if the drug is in Schedule H.)
```

### Labelling of cosmetics (main points)

Name of the cosmetic, name and address of the manufacturer, **list of ingredients** (in descending order of quantity for most products), net content, batch number, manufacturing licence number, date of manufacture and **date of expiry** (or "use before"), warnings and directions for safe use where needed.

### Permitted colours

Only the colours **permitted by the rules** may be used in drugs (a non-permitted colour makes a drug adulterated). The permitted list includes natural colours such as **caramel, beta-carotene, chlorophyll, curcumin, riboflavin, annatto, saffron, titanium dioxide and iron oxides**, and synthetic colours such as **tartrazine, sunset yellow FCF, erythrosine, ponceau 4R, carmoisine, brilliant blue FCF, indigo carmine and fast green FCF**, with their aluminium lakes. Confirm the current list in the rules.

### Packing

- Containers must be suitable and must not react with the contents.
- Pack sizes of some drugs are fixed (**Schedule P1**).

### Offences and penalties

Misbranding by wrong labels is an offence under sections 17 and 18, punishable under section 27 (usually 27(d)). Using a **Government Analyst's report** for advertising is an offence under **section 29**.

### Drugs Technical Advisory Board (DTAB) — section 5

- **Function:** advises the Central and State Governments on **technical matters** arising out of the administration of the Act (for example, banning drugs under section 26A, changes in schedules).
- **Composition** (three types of members):
  - **Ex officio:** the **Director General of Health Services (chairman)**, the **Drugs Controller General of India**, the Director of the **Central Drugs Laboratory**, Kolkata, the Director of the **Central Research Institute**, Kasauli, the Director of the **Indian Veterinary Research Institute**, Izatnagar, and the heads of certain other bodies named in the Act (such as the President of the Pharmacy Council of India).
  - **Nominated** by the Central Government, including persons in charge of drug control in the States and a person from the pharmaceutical industry.
  - **Elected** by professional bodies, including a **teacher of pharmacy** (elected by the Executive Committee of the PCI), a **teacher of medicine or therapeutics**, a **pharmacologist** (elected by the governing body of the ICMR), and representatives of the medical and pharmaceutical associations.
- Nominated and elected members hold office for **three years** and can be re-nominated or re-elected. Some bodies named in the Act (for example, the Medical Council of India) have since been replaced; check the current composition.

### Central Drugs Laboratory (CDL) — section 6

- Located at **Kolkata**; certain classes of drugs are tested at other notified central laboratories (for example, vaccines and sera at the Central Research Institute, Kasauli).
- **Functions:** analysis of samples sent by customs and drugs inspectors; **appellate (referee) testing** when a manufacturer disputes a Government Analyst's report — the CDL report is **final**; preparation of reference standards; advice on quality standards.

### Drugs Consultative Committee (DCC) — section 7

- **Composition:** **two representatives of the Central Government** and **one representative of each State Government**.
- **Function:** to secure **uniformity** in the administration of the Act throughout India; advises the Central Government, State Governments and the DTAB.
- Meets when called by the Central Government; regulates its own procedure.

### Government Analysts — section 20

- Appointed by the Central or State Government for notified areas and classes of drugs.
- Must have the qualifications laid down in the rules (degree in medicine, science or pharmacy with specified experience in drug testing); must have **no financial interest** in the import, manufacture or sale of drugs.
- **Duties:** analyse samples sent by inspectors or others and give a **report in the prescribed form** to the inspector; send reports to the Government.

### Licensing authorities

- **Central:** the **Drugs Controller General of India (DCGI)**, head of CDSCO — import licences, new drug permissions, clinical trials, Central Licence Approving Authority for Schedule C/C(1) items, vaccines and blood products.
- **State:** the **State Drugs Controller** (licensing authority) — manufacturing licences for most drugs and all sale licences.

### Controlling authority

The officer appointed by the State Government (usually the State Drugs Controller) to **control and supervise** the work of drugs inspectors, approve prosecutions and take action on reports.

### Drugs Inspectors — sections 21 to 23

**Qualifications:** a degree in pharmacy or pharmaceutical sciences or medicine (with clinical pharmacology or microbiology), as the rules require; for inspecting the manufacture of Schedule C items, additional experience is needed. An inspector must have **no financial interest** in the drug trade.

**Powers (section 22):**

1. **Inspect** premises where drugs or cosmetics are manufactured, stored, sold or distributed.
2. **Take samples** of any drug or cosmetic.
3. **Search** any person, place or vehicle believed to hold drugs in breach of the Act.
4. **Stop and search** vehicles, and **enter and search** premises.
5. **Seize** drugs, cosmetics, records and materials used in breach of the Act, and **examine records**.
6. Require production of documents.

**Procedure for taking a sample (section 23):**

```text
Inspector pays the fair price and gives a written receipt
      |
Divides the sample into 4 portions, seals and marks each
      |
 1 portion -> person from whom taken
 1 portion -> Government Analyst for test
 1 portion -> court (if prosecution)
 1 portion -> manufacturer (if named on the label)
```

**Duties:** inspect licensed premises regularly (generally at least once or twice a year as directed), check compliance with licence conditions, send samples for test, investigate complaints, institute prosecutions with sanction of the controlling authority, and maintain records of inspections.

## Remember

- **G** = caution label; **H** = Rx; **H1** = Rx in red + register (3 years); **X** = XRx, duplicate prescription (2 years), lock and key.
- **M** = GMP (revised Dec 2023); **N** = pharmacy minimum equipment; **P** = shelf life (P1 = pack sizes); **T** = ASU GMP; **U** = manufacturing and analytical records; **V** = vitamin/mineral standards for patent medicines; **Y** = clinical trials, now **NDCT Rules 2019**; **Part XII B / Sch F** = blood centres.
- Sale licences: 20/21 retail, 20B/21B wholesale, 20F/20G Schedule X, 20A/21A restricted; retail premises **10 m²**, combined **15 m²**.
- Labels: generic name, content, batch, licence no., Mfg and Exp dates, MRP, maker's address, schedule warnings.
- **DTAB** (s.5, chairman DGHS) = technical advice; **CDL** (s.6, Kolkata) = final appellate test; **DCC** (s.7, 2 Central + 1 per State) = uniformity; **Government Analyst** (s.20); **Inspector** (s.21–23) with powers to inspect, sample, search and seize.

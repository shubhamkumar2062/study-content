# Unit 3: Pharmacy Act, 1948; Medicinal and Toilet Preparations Act, 1955; NDPS Act, 1985

This unit has three Acts. The **Pharmacy Act, 1948** regulates the **profession** of pharmacy. The **Medicinal and Toilet Preparations (Excise Duties) Act, 1955** dealt with the **excise duty** on alcohol- and narcotic-containing preparations (it has been repealed — see 3.2). The **Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985** controls **narcotic and psychotropic** drugs. It is a large unit; learn the Pharmacy Act and NDPS Act in full, and the M&TP Act in outline.

## 3.1 Pharmacy Act, 1948

### Objectives

1. To **regulate the profession and practice of pharmacy** in India.
2. To set up the **Pharmacy Council of India (PCI)** and **State Pharmacy Councils**.
3. To lay down **minimum standards of pharmacy education** (Education Regulations) and approve institutions and courses.
4. To provide for **registration of pharmacists** and maintenance of registers.
5. To make sure that only **registered pharmacists** compound and dispense medicines (where the State has notified this).

### Important definitions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Central Council | The Pharmacy Council of India constituted under section 3 |
| State Council | A State Pharmacy Council constituted under section 19 |
| Joint State Council | A council formed by agreement between two or more States (section 20) |
| Registered pharmacist | A person whose name is entered in the register of pharmacists of the State in which he is residing or carrying on the business or profession of pharmacy |
| Register | The register of pharmacists maintained by a State Council |
| Medical practitioner | A person registered under the law relating to medical registration (now under the National Medical Commission system) |
| Education Regulations | Regulations made under section 10 |

### Pharmacy Council of India — constitution (section 3)

| Clause | Members |
| --- | --- |
| (a) | **Six** members elected by the **University Grants Commission** from among teachers of pharmacy, with at least one teacher each of **pharmaceutical chemistry, pharmacy, pharmacology and pharmacognosy** |
| (b) | **Six** members **nominated by the Central Government**, of whom at least **four** hold a degree or diploma in pharmacy or pharmaceutical chemistry, or are registered pharmacists |
| (c) | **One** member elected by the **Medical Council of India** from among its members (the MCI has since been replaced by the National Medical Commission) |
| (d) | The **Director General of Health Services**, ex officio |
| (dd) | The **Drugs Controller of India**, ex officio |
| (e) | The **Director of the Central Drugs Laboratory**, ex officio |
| (f) | **One representative each** of the **UGC** and the **AICTE** |
| (g) | **One registered pharmacist from each State**, elected by the State Pharmacy Council from among its members |
| (h) | **One member from each State**, nominated by the State Government, who is a registered pharmacist |

- The **President and Vice-President** are elected by the members from among themselves.
- Elected and nominated members normally hold office for **five years** and may be re-elected or re-nominated.
- An **Executive Committee** (President and Vice-President as ex officio members, with other members) carries out the Council's work between meetings.
- A **Secretary/Registrar** and staff are appointed by the Council.

### Functions of the PCI

1. **Education Regulations (section 10):** prescribe the **minimum standard of education** for registration as a pharmacist — nature and period of study and practical training, subjects of examination, equipment and facilities, and conditions of admission. They need the approval of the Central Government (examples: D.Pharm Education Regulations, 2020; B.Pharm Course Regulations, 2014; Pharm.D Regulations, 2008).
2. **Approval of courses and institutions (section 12):** an institution or examination that meets the Education Regulations is approved; only approved qualifications lead to registration.
3. **Withdrawal of approval (section 13)** if standards are not maintained, after inspection and a chance to explain.
4. **Inspection** of institutions and examinations through inspectors.
5. **Qualifications granted outside India (section 14):** recognition on a reciprocal basis.
6. **Central Register of Pharmacists (section 15A):** compiled from State registers.
7. Framing other regulations, for example the **Pharmacy Practice Regulations, 2015** (duties of pharmacists, code of ethics, record of prescriptions).
8. Advising the Government on pharmacy education and practice.

### State Pharmacy Councils (section 19)

| Clause | Members |
| --- | --- |
| (a) | **Six** members elected by **registered pharmacists** of the State from among themselves |
| (b) | **Five** members **nominated by the State Government**, of whom at least **three** hold a degree or diploma in pharmacy or pharmaceutical chemistry or are registered pharmacists |
| (c) | **One** member elected by the **State Medical Council** from among its members |
| (d) | The **chief administrative medical officer** of the State, ex officio |
| (dd) | The **officer in charge of drugs control** of the State, ex officio |
| (e) | The **Government Analyst** under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, ex officio (one chosen by the State if there are several) |

**Joint State Council (section 20):** two or more States (or a State and Union Territories) may agree to have one joint council; the agreement fixes the share of members and expenses.

**Functions of a State Council:**

1. Prepare and maintain the **register of pharmacists** of the State.
2. **Register** qualified persons, issue certificates and collect fees.
3. **Remove or restore** names in the register.
4. Appoint **Registrar** and **inspectors (section 26A)** to inspect premises where drugs are compounded or dispensed, check that a registered pharmacist is in charge, and investigate complaints.
5. Run continuing education and maintain discipline of the profession.

### Registration of pharmacists

```text
First register (section 29-31)
  -> prepared when the Act was first applied in a State; persons with
     the qualifications or experience listed in section 31 were entered

Subsequent registration (section 32)
  -> age 18 years or more
  -> holds an approved qualification (e.g. D.Pharm / B.Pharm under the
     Education Regulations)
  -> pays the prescribed fee
  -> name entered, certificate of registration issued

Renewal (section 34) -> fee as laid down; name removed if not renewed
Removal (section 36) -> misconduct, conviction for an offence, wrong entry,
                        after an inquiry and a chance to be heard
Restoration (section 37) -> on application and fee, if the Council agrees
```

### Offences and penalties

| Section | Offence |
| --- | --- |
| 26A | **Obstructing an inspector** or refusing information |
| 41 | **Falsely claiming to be registered**, or using the title "pharmacist" or letters suggesting registration when not registered |
| 42 | **Dispensing by an unregistered person**: after a date notified by the State Government, no one other than a registered pharmacist may compound, prepare, mix or dispense a medicine on the prescription of a medical practitioner (a doctor dispensing for his own patients is exempt) |
| 43 | **Failure to surrender the certificate of registration** after the name is removed |

**Penalties — check the current text.** In the original Act (the version in most textbooks), section 42 carried imprisonment up to **six months**, or fine up to **₹1,000**, or both; section 41 carried a fine up to **₹500** for the first offence and imprisonment or a higher fine for later offences; section 43 carried a small fine. The **Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023** amended these penalty sections (26A, 41, 42 and 43) — in force from **31 December 2024** — largely replacing short prison terms with much **larger monetary penalties**, and inserted **section 43A** for penalties to be imposed by an **adjudicating officer**. Learn the present figures from the latest bare Act or PCI notices before the exam.

## 3.2 Medicinal and Toilet Preparations (Excise Duties) Act, 1955

> **Repealed.** This Act was repealed by **section 174(1) of the Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017**, with effect from **1 July 2017**, when GST came in. Alcohol-based medicinal and toilet preparations are now taxed under GST. The PCI syllabus still lists the Act, so learn its outline and always add a line in your answer that it stands repealed. Check whether your university still asks it.

### Objectives (when in force)

1. To provide for the **levy and collection of excise duty** on medicinal and toilet preparations containing **alcohol, opium, Indian hemp or other narcotic drugs**.
2. To have **uniform rates** of duty throughout India (the Act was a Central law, but duties were collected by the **State excise departments**).
3. To prevent misuse of duty-free alcohol by **supervising manufacture**.

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Alcohol | Ethyl alcohol (C2H5OH) of any strength and purity |
| Dutiable goods | Medicinal and toilet preparations listed in the Schedule to the Act |
| Medicinal preparation | A drug that is a remedy or prescription prepared for internal or external use of human beings or animals, and includes Ayurvedic, homoeopathic and allopathic preparations |
| Toilet preparation | A preparation for use in the toilet of the human body or in perfumery (for example, hair oils, lotions, perfumes, mouthwashes) |
| Narcotic drug | Includes opium, cocaine, Indian hemp and their derivatives |
| Bonded manufactory | Premises where dutiable goods are made **under excise supervision** without prior payment of duty on the alcohol used |

### Licensing

- A **licence** from the State excise authority was needed to manufacture any dutiable goods, and the manufacturer also needed a drug manufacturing licence under the D&C Act.
- Licences covered manufacture in bond, manufacture outside bond, bonded warehouses, and wholesale of preparations containing narcotic drugs.

### Manufacture in bond and outside bond

| Point | In bond | Outside bond |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alcohol used | Supplied **without duty** | **Duty-paid** alcohol bought by the maker |
| Duty paid | When finished goods are **removed** from the manufactory | Already paid on the alcohol; extra duty as laid down |
| Supervision | Constant **excise officer** supervision; locks and stock registers | Periodic checks |
| Used for | Preparations **capable of being consumed as ordinary alcoholic beverages** ("restricted preparations") had to be made in bond | Preparations **not capable** of being drunk as a beverage ("unrestricted") could be made outside bond |

### Export of alcoholic preparations

Dutiable goods could be **exported without payment of duty** (under bond, with proof of export) or with **rebate** of the duty paid, so that Indian products remained competitive abroad.

### Ayurvedic, homoeopathic and patent or proprietary preparations

- **Ayurvedic** preparations such as **asavas and arishtas** contain **alcohol formed by their own fermentation**; the Act set conditions and duty for them, with special treatment where they were not capable of being consumed as ordinary alcoholic beverages.
- **Homoeopathic** preparations (mother tinctures and potencies) are made in alcohol; they were manufactured under licence and excise supervision.
- **Patent or proprietary** allopathic preparations containing alcohol (for example, tonics and cough syrups) carried duty according to their class.

### Offences and penalties

Manufacture without a licence, removal of goods without paying duty, use of duty-free alcohol for other purposes, and false accounts were offences punishable with **fine, imprisonment and confiscation** of the goods. Exact amounts are no longer relevant after the repeal.

## 3.3 Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985 and Rules

### Background and objectives

- Came into force in **November 1985**, replacing the **Opium Acts (1857, 1878)** and the **Dangerous Drugs Act, 1930**. Amended in **1989, 2001, 2014 and 2021**.
- Gives effect to India's obligations under the UN conventions on narcotic drugs (1961), psychotropic substances (1971) and illicit traffic (1988).

**Objectives**

1. To consolidate and amend the law on narcotic drugs.
2. To make **stringent provisions** for control and regulation of operations relating to narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances.
3. To provide for **forfeiture of property** derived from illicit traffic.
4. To implement international conventions.
5. To permit use **only for medical and scientific purposes**, and to support treatment and rehabilitation of addicts.

### Important definitions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Addict | A person dependent on any narcotic drug or psychotropic substance |
| Cannabis (hemp) | **Charas** (separated resin), **ganja** (flowering or fruiting tops, excluding seeds and leaves not accompanied by tops), and any mixture of them |
| Coca derivative | Crude cocaine, ecgonine, cocaine and their preparations |
| Opium | The coagulated juice of the opium poppy, and mixtures of it |
| Opium derivative | Medicinal opium, prepared opium, phenanthrene alkaloids (morphine, codeine, thebaine) and their salts, diacetylmorphine (heroin) |
| Poppy straw | All parts of the opium poppy after harvesting, except the seeds |
| Manufactured drug | Coca derivatives, medicinal hemp, opium derivatives, poppy straw concentrate, and other drugs declared by the Government |
| Narcotic drug | Coca leaf, cannabis, opium, poppy straw and all manufactured drugs |
| Psychotropic substance | Any substance, natural or synthetic, in the **list in the Schedule** to the Act (for example, diazepam, alprazolam, amphetamine, LSD) |
| Essential narcotic drug | A narcotic drug notified by the Central Government for medical and scientific use (added in **2014** to make morphine, fentanyl, methadone and similar drugs easier to get for **pain relief and palliative care** under uniform central rules) |
| Small / commercial quantity | Quantities **notified by the Central Government** for each drug; they decide the punishment |
| Controlled substance | A chemical (precursor) used to make narcotic drugs or psychotropic substances, regulated under section 9A |

### Authorities and officers

| Section | Authority | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | **Central Government** | Takes all measures for prevention and combating of abuse and illicit traffic; may constitute an authority — the **Narcotics Control Bureau (NCB)** was set up in 1986 |
| 5 | **Narcotics Commissioner** and other officers | Supervises **opium poppy cultivation and opium production**; heads the **Central Bureau of Narcotics** |
| 6 | **NDPS Consultative Committee** | Advises the Central Government |
| 7 | Officers of State Governments | Appointed by the States for their duties under the Act |
| 7A | **National Fund for Control of Drug Abuse** | See below |

### Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Consultative Committee (section 6)

- **Constitution:** set up by the Central Government by notification; a **Chairman** and other members **not exceeding twenty**, appointed by the Central Government.
- **Function:** to **advise the Central Government** on matters relating to the administration of the Act that are referred to it.
- It may form **sub-committees**, which may include persons who are not members.
- Meets when required and regulates its own procedure.

### National Fund for Controlling Drug Abuse (section 7A, inserted in 1989)

- Constituted by the Central Government; money comes from **sale proceeds of property forfeited** under the Act, **grants** from any person or institution, and income from investment of the fund.
- **Used for:**
  1. Combating illicit traffic in narcotic drugs.
  2. Controlling abuse of narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances.
  3. Identifying, treating and **rehabilitating addicts**.
  4. **Preventing drug abuse** and **educating the public**.
  5. Supplying drugs to addicts where this is medically necessary.

### Prohibition, control and regulation (sections 8 to 9A)

**Section 8** — no person shall:

1. **Cultivate** any coca plant, opium poppy or cannabis plant;
2. **Produce, manufacture, possess, sell, purchase, transport, warehouse, use, consume, import inter-State, export inter-State, import into India, export from India or tranship** any narcotic drug or psychotropic substance;

**except for medical or scientific purposes** and in the manner and to the extent allowed by the Act, rules and licences.

**Section 9 — powers of the Central Government** to permit, control and regulate: cultivation of the **opium poppy**, production of **opium** and **poppy straw**, manufacture of opium and manufactured drugs, sale of opium to manufacturers, **import and export**, and the use of psychotropic substances.

**Section 9A** — control of **controlled substances** (precursor chemicals such as acetic anhydride, ephedrine, pseudoephedrine).

**Section 10 — powers of State Governments** (by rules) to regulate: possession, transport, purchase and sale of poppy straw; possession, transport, use and sale of **manufactured drugs other than prepared opium** (except essential narcotic drugs, which are now centrally regulated); and **cultivation of cannabis** (except charas).

### Opium poppy cultivation and production of poppy straw

```text
Central Government notifies tracts (parts of Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan and
Uttar Pradesh) and the licensing policy each crop year
      |
Narcotics Commissioner grants LICENCES to cultivators
      |
Cultivator grows poppy in the licensed area, lances the capsules
      |
All opium collected is TENDERED to the Central Bureau of Narcotics
at the price fixed by Government (a minimum yield is required to keep the licence)
      |
Opium processed at Government Opium and Alkaloid Works (Ghazipur, Neemuch)
      |
Medicinal opium, morphine, codeine, thebaine -> licensed makers / export
```

- **Poppy straw** left after harvest is controlled; its possession, transport and sale are regulated by the States under licence. In recent years Government has also permitted production of **concentrate of poppy straw** under strict control.
- India is one of the countries recognised for **licit opium production** for medical use.

### Manufacture, sale and export of opium

- **Manufacture** of opium and of opium alkaloids from raw opium is done in **Government factories** (Government Opium and Alkaloid Works) under the Central Government.
- **Sale** of opium to licensed manufacturers of medicines is controlled by the Central Government.
- **Export** of opium is done **only by or on behalf of the Central Government**, under international control (import certificates of the importing country).
- Manufactured drugs and psychotropic substances are made and sold by private firms only under licences under the NDPS Rules and the D&C Act, with records and returns.

### Offences and penalties

**Offences by substance (sections 15 to 22):** poppy straw (15), coca plant and coca leaves (16), prepared opium (17), opium poppy and opium (18), embezzlement of opium by a licensed cultivator (19), cannabis (20), manufactured drugs (21), psychotropic substances (22), illegal import, export or transhipment (23), external dealings (24), allowing premises to be used (25), contraventions by licensees (26).

**Graded punishment by quantity (since 2001):**

| Quantity involved | Punishment (as in sections 15–22) |
| --- | --- |
| **Small quantity** | Rigorous imprisonment up to **1 year** (six months before the 2014 amendment), or fine up to **₹10,000**, or both |
| More than small but **less than commercial** | Rigorous imprisonment up to **10 years** and fine up to **₹1 lakh** |
| **Commercial quantity** | Rigorous imprisonment **10 to 20 years** and fine **₹1 lakh to ₹2 lakh** (the court may impose a higher fine, giving reasons) |

Examples of notified quantities (small / commercial): heroin 5 g / 250 g; opium 25 g / 2.5 kg; ganja 1 kg / 20 kg; charas 100 g / 1 kg; cocaine 2 g / 100 g. Check the current notification.

**Other important provisions:**

| Section | Provision |
| --- | --- |
| 27 | **Consumption**: for cocaine, morphine, diacetylmorphine or other notified drugs — imprisonment up to **1 year** or fine up to **₹20,000** or both; for other drugs — up to **6 months** or fine up to **₹10,000** or both |
| 27A | **Financing illicit traffic and harbouring offenders** — rigorous imprisonment **10 to 20 years** and fine **₹1 to 2 lakh** (a 2021 amendment corrected the cross-reference in this section) |
| 28, 29 | **Attempt, abetment and criminal conspiracy** — same punishment as the offence |
| 31 | **Repeat offence** — up to **one and a half times** the maximum punishment |
| 31A | **Death penalty** for certain repeat offences involving large quantities; since **2014** the court may instead give the punishment under section 31 |
| 32 | Offences with no specific penalty — imprisonment up to 6 months, or fine, or both |
| 35 | Court presumes a **culpable mental state**; the accused must prove otherwise |
| 36 | **Special Courts** |
| 37 | Serious offences are **cognizable** and bail is restricted |
| 64A | **Immunity from prosecution** for an addict charged with consumption or a small-quantity offence who **volunteers for treatment** at a recognised centre |
| 68A–68Y | **Forfeiture of property** derived from illicit traffic |

### NDPS Rules

- **NDPS Rules, 1985** (Central) cover cultivation, opium manufacture, import and export, manufactured drugs, psychotropic substances and **essential narcotic drugs**; **State NDPS Rules** cover possession, transport and sale within a State.
- Pharmacies stocking narcotic drugs need the required licence, keep **records** of receipt and sale in the prescribed registers, dispense only on prescription, and file returns.

## Remember

- **Pharmacy Act 1948:** PCI under **section 3** (6 UGC-elected teachers + 6 Central nominees + 1 MCI + DGHS + DCI + Director CDL + UGC and AICTE reps + 1 elected and 1 nominated per State); **section 10** Education Regulations; **section 19** State Council (6 elected + 5 nominated + 1 medical council + CMO + drugs controller + Government Analyst); **section 32** registration (18 years, approved qualification, fee).
- Offences: **41** false claim, **42** unregistered dispensing, **43** not surrendering certificate, **26A** obstructing inspector — penalties changed by **Jan Vishwas Act 2023** (from 31 Dec 2024), new **section 43A** adjudication.
- **M&TP Act 1955:** excise duty on alcohol/narcotic preparations; in bond (duty-free alcohol, restricted preparations) vs outside bond (duty-paid); **repealed by section 174 of the CGST Act, 2017, from 1 July 2017**.
- **NDPS 1985:** s.4 Central Govt (NCB), s.5 Narcotics Commissioner, **s.6 Consultative Committee (Chairman + up to 20)**, **s.7A National Fund (1989)**, s.8 prohibition, s.9 Central powers, s.10 State powers.
- Opium: licensed cultivation → tender to Government → Government Opium and Alkaloid Works (Ghazipur, Neemuch) → export only by Government.
- Punishment by quantity: small (up to 1 year), intermediate (up to 10 years), commercial (10–20 years); s.27 consumption; s.31A death penalty now discretionary; s.64A immunity for addicts seeking treatment.

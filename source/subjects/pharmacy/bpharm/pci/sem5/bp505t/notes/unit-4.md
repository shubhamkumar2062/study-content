# Unit 4: Drugs and Magic Remedies Act, Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, NPPA and DPCO 2013

This unit covers three different kinds of control: control of **advertisements** of drugs and remedies (DMR Act), control of **experiments on animals** (PCA Act and the CPCSEA/CCSEA system), and control of **drug prices** (NPPA, DPCO 2013 and the National List of Essential Medicines).

## 4.1 Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954 and Rules, 1955

### Objectives

1. To **control advertisements of drugs** in certain cases.
2. To **prohibit advertisements of remedies alleged to have magic qualities**.
3. To stop people from being misled into **self-medication** with harmful or useless remedies, and to protect them from **quackery** for serious diseases.

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Advertisement** | Any notice, circular, label, wrapper or other document, and any announcement made **orally or by any means** of producing or transmitting light, sound or smoke (today this covers print, radio, TV, internet and social media) |
| **Drug** | A medicine for internal or external use of human beings or animals; a substance meant for diagnosis, treatment, mitigation or prevention of disease; an article (other than food) meant to affect the organic functions of the body; any article meant as a component of these |
| **Magic remedy** | Includes a **talisman, mantra, kavacha, charm** or any other article which is claimed to have **miraculous powers** for curing, diagnosing, preventing or mitigating disease, or affecting the structure or function of the body |
| **Registered medical practitioner** | A person registered under the medical registration laws (including Indian systems of medicine) |
| **Taking part in the publication** | Includes printing, publishing, distributing or otherwise circulating an advertisement |

### Prohibition of certain advertisements

**Section 3** — no person shall take part in publishing an advertisement referring to a drug in terms that suggest or are meant to lead to its use for:

1. **Procuring miscarriage** in women or **preventing conception**;
2. **Maintenance or improvement of the capacity of human beings for sexual pleasure**;
3. **Correction of menstrual disorder** in women;
4. **Diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment or prevention** of any disease, disorder or condition **listed in the Schedule** to the Act.

The **Schedule** lists **54** diseases and conditions, for example: appendicitis, arteriosclerosis, blindness, cancer, cataract, deafness, diabetes, epilepsy, glaucoma, goitre, hernia, high or low blood pressure, leucoderma, paralysis, sexual impotence, premature ageing and venereal diseases.

**Section 4 — misleading advertisements.** No advertisement of a drug may contain matter that **directly or indirectly gives a false impression** about the true character of the drug, makes a **false claim** for it, or is otherwise **false or misleading** in any material particular.

**Section 5 — magic remedies.** No person shall take part in publishing any advertisement referring to a **magic remedy** that directly or indirectly claims it can cure, diagnose, prevent or mitigate any disease (or any Schedule condition).

**Section 6 — import and export** into or out of India of documents containing such advertisements is prohibited.

### Classes of exempted advertisements (section 14)

The Act does **not** apply to:

1. A **signboard or notice** displayed by a registered medical practitioner on his premises indicating that treatment for any disease is given there.
2. A **treatise or book** dealing with any of the matters in section 3 from a bona fide scientific or social standpoint.
3. Any advertisement relating to a drug **sent confidentially** to registered medical practitioners (and as the rules allow, to chemists, hospitals and laboratories), with the words "For the use only of registered medical practitioners or a hospital or laboratory".
4. Any advertisement **printed or published by the Government** or with its previous sanction.
5. Advertisements relating to family planning or other matters issued by Government or with its approval, and others **exempted by the Central Government** by notification.

```text
Is it an advertisement of a drug or remedy?
   |-- for a Schedule disease / sex / miscarriage / contraception / menses? --> PROHIBITED (s.3)
   |-- false or misleading?                                               --> PROHIBITED (s.4)
   |-- magic remedy claiming cure?                                        --> PROHIBITED (s.5)
   |-- exempt class under s.14 (RMP signboard, scientific book,
   |   confidential to doctors, Government)?                              --> ALLOWED
```

### Powers of entry, search and seizure (section 8)

A gazetted officer authorised by the State Government may enter and search premises, seize documents and articles believed to contravene the Act, and examine records.

### Offences and penalties (section 7)

| Offence | Punishment (as enacted) |
| --- | --- |
| **First conviction** | Imprisonment up to **six months**, or fine, or both |
| **Subsequent conviction** | Imprisonment up to **one year**, or fine, or both |

- Offences by a **company**: every person in charge of the business is liable unless he proves lack of knowledge and due diligence.
- The Ministry of Health published a **draft amendment bill in 2020** proposing much higher penalties and an updated Schedule. Check whether any amendment has been passed before quoting penalties.

### Relation with other laws

- **Schedule J** of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules prohibits **claims on the label** of a drug for listed diseases; the DMR Act prohibits **advertisements**.
- Advertising of ASU drugs is also controlled under the D&C Rules, and advertising standards are watched by the **Advertising Standards Council of India** (a self-regulatory body).

## 4.2 Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960

### Objectives

1. To **prevent the infliction of unnecessary pain or suffering** on animals.
2. To set up the **Animal Welfare Board of India**.
3. To **control and supervise experiments on animals** (Chapter IV) so that they are done only when necessary and with the least pain.

### Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Animal | Any living creature **other than a human being** |
| Captive animal | An animal (not domestic) kept in captivity or confinement, or maimed to prevent escape |
| Domestic animal | An animal tamed or being tamed for a purpose of use to man |
| Owner | Includes any person in possession or custody of the animal |
| Experiment | Use of animals for the advancement of knowledge, for saving or prolonging life, alleviating suffering, or combating disease, and for testing drugs, sera, vaccines and other biological products |

### Experiments on animals — Chapter IV (sections 14 to 20)

- **Section 14:** nothing in the Act makes it unlawful to perform experiments on animals for the advancement of knowledge (physiological, medical, veterinary) that will save or prolong life or alleviate suffering, or for combating disease.
- **Section 15:** the Central Government constitutes a **Committee for the purpose of Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals (CPCSEA)** — renamed the **Committee for Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals (CCSEA)** in 2023. Older books and the syllabus use "CPCSEA".
- **Section 17:** duties of the Committee and its power to make rules so that animals are not subjected to unnecessary pain before, during or after experiments.
- **Section 18:** power of the Committee to **inspect** establishments where experiments are done.
- **Section 19:** **power to prohibit** experiments, or to **suspend or revoke** the registration of an institution, if rules are not followed.
- **Section 20:** **penalties** for breaking the Committee's orders or conditions (a fine under the original Act).

The **Breeding of and Experiments on Animals (Control and Supervision) Rules, 1998** (amended later) give the details.

### Main provisions of the CPCSEA (CCSEA) system

**1. Registration of establishments**

Every institution that **breeds, trades in or uses** animals for experiments must be **registered** with the Committee; registration is renewed periodically.

**2. Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC)**

Every registered establishment must set up an **IAEC**, approved by the Committee. Its members include:

| Member | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Biological scientist | Chairs or guides scientific review |
| Scientists from different biological disciplines | Review scientific merit |
| Veterinarian | Responsible for care of the animals |
| Scientist in charge of the animal house facility | Reports on housing and husbandry |
| Scientist from outside the institute | Independent view |
| Socially aware, non-scientific member | Public interest |
| Main nominee and link nominee of the Committee | Ensure the guidelines are followed |

**Functions of the IAEC:** review and **approve protocols** for experiments on small animals; refer protocols involving **large animals** (and certain other categories) to the Committee; check that the number of animals is justified; ensure humane end points and anaesthesia or analgesia; inspect the animal house; keep records.

**3. Guidelines for breeding and stocking of animals**

- Animal house planned for the species: **separate areas** for breeding, quarantine, experimental animals, and surgery, with clean and dirty corridors.
- Controlled **temperature, humidity, light–dark cycle and ventilation**; noise control.
- Proper **caging, bedding, food and water**; space as per species and number.
- **Health monitoring**, quarantine of new arrivals, and **veterinary care**.
- **Trained staff** and personal hygiene; pest control.
- Animals should be obtained from **registered breeders**.

**4. Performance of experiments**

- Follow the **3 Rs**: **Replacement** (use non-animal methods where possible), **Reduction** (fewest animals that give valid results), **Refinement** (methods that reduce pain and distress); the Committee also stresses **Rehabilitation** of animals after experiments where possible.
- Experiments only by **qualified and trained** persons, after IAEC approval.
- Use of the **lowest species** on the phylogenetic scale that will serve the purpose.
- Proper **anaesthesia and analgesia** for painful procedures; **euthanasia** by approved humane methods when needed.
- Experiments must not be repeated unnecessarily; procedures already proven should not be repeated for demonstration where models or films will do.

**5. Transfer and acquisition of animals for experiment**

- Animals may be acquired only from **registered breeders** or other registered establishments.
- Transfer between institutions needs proper records and, where required, permission; transport in suitable conditions.

**6. Records**

Establishments keep records of **animals bred, acquired, transferred, used and disposed of**, protocols approved by the IAEC, experiments done, and veterinary care. Records are open to inspection and reports are sent to the Committee as required.

**7. Power to suspend or revoke registration**

If an establishment does not follow the rules or the conditions laid down, the Committee may, after giving a hearing, **suspend or revoke** its registration and **prohibit** it from doing experiments (section 19).

### Offences and penalties

- **Cruelty to animals (section 11):** a fine for the first offence and a higher fine or imprisonment for a repeat offence within three years. The amounts in the original Act are very small; a draft amendment proposing higher penalties has been discussed but check whether it has been passed.
- **Contravening an order under section 19** or a condition imposed by the Committee is punishable under **section 20** with a fine.
- Companies and their officers are liable as the Act provides.

## 4.3 National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA) and Drugs (Prices Control) Order, 2013

### NPPA

- Set up in **1997** under the **Department of Pharmaceuticals**, Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers.
- **Functions:** fix and revise **ceiling prices** of scheduled formulations; **monitor prices** of non-scheduled formulations; enforce the DPCO; recover **overcharged amounts** from companies; monitor **availability** and shortages of medicines; advise the Government on pricing policy; collect data on production, imports and sales.

### Drugs (Prices Control) Order, 2013

- Issued by the Central Government under **section 3 of the Essential Commodities Act, 1955**, based on the **National Pharmaceutical Pricing Policy, 2012**.
- Replaced **DPCO 1995**. The main change: prices are now fixed on the basis of **market prices** (market-based pricing) of **essential medicines**, instead of cost-based pricing of selected bulk drugs.

**Objectives**

1. To make **essential medicines available at reasonable prices**.
2. To keep enough room for **innovation and competition** so that the industry grows.
3. To ensure that price control does not cause **shortages**.

### Definitions (main ones)

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Scheduled formulation** | A formulation of a medicine in the **First Schedule** of the DPCO (the **NLEM**) in the same strength and dosage form |
| **Non-scheduled formulation** | A formulation not in the First Schedule |
| **Ceiling price** | The price fixed by the Government for a scheduled formulation; no one may sell above it |
| **Maximum retail price (MRP)** | The maximum price at which a drug can be sold to the consumer, inclusive of taxes |
| **Retail price** | The price fixed for a **new drug** (see below) |
| **Price to retailer (PTR)** | The price at which a drug is sold to the retailer, including taxes |
| **Moving annual turnover** | Sales value of a product over the preceding twelve months, used to decide market share |
| **New drug** (for DPCO) | A formulation launched by an existing manufacturer of a scheduled drug by combining it with another drug or by changing its strength or dosage form |
| **Bulk drug (API)** | Any pharmaceutical, chemical, biological or plant product used as an active ingredient in a formulation |
| **Wholesale price index (WPI)** | The index published by the Government used for annual revision of ceiling prices |

### Sale prices of bulk drugs

DPCO 2013 does **not** routinely fix prices of bulk drugs (DPCO 1995 did). The Government can, however, direct manufacturers of a bulk drug used in scheduled formulations to **maintain supply** to formulation makers, and may fix its price in special circumstances to ensure availability.

### Calculation of ceiling price of a scheduled formulation

```text
Step 1  Take all brands and generic versions of the formulation that have
        market share of 1% or more of the total market turnover of that medicine.
Step 2  Find each one's price to retailer (PTR).
Step 3  Take the SIMPLE AVERAGE of these PTRs.
Step 4  Add a RETAILER MARGIN of 16% to the average.
                Ceiling price = Average PTR x 1.16  (taxes extra as allowed)
```

**Worked example**

| Brand | Market share | PTR per 10 tablets (₹) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 30% | 20.00 |
| B | 20% | 24.00 |
| C | 10% | 16.00 |
| D | 0.5% | 40.00 (left out, share below 1%) |

Average PTR = (20 + 24 + 16) ÷ 3 = ₹20.00 → ceiling price = 20.00 × 1.16 = **₹23.20** per 10 tablets.

- Manufacturers selling **above** the ceiling price must **reduce** their price to the ceiling; those selling **below** must **not increase** it (except for the annual WPI increase).
- **Annual revision:** ceiling prices are revised every year on **1 April** in line with the **WPI** change for the previous calendar year (paragraph 16).

### Retail price of formulations and of a new drug

- For a **new drug** (as defined above) the manufacturer must apply, and the Government fixes its **retail price** using the market-based method on comparable formulations; the drug cannot be launched at a higher price.
- Every manufacturer must print the **MRP** on the label and issue a **price list** to dealers; retailers must display the price list and must not sell above the MRP or ceiling price.

### Non-scheduled formulations

- Prices are **monitored**; a manufacturer may not raise the MRP by more than **10% in a twelve-month period**. Excess must be reduced.

### Other important provisions

- **Paragraph 19** — the Government may, in **extraordinary circumstances** and in public interest, fix the ceiling or retail price of any drug for a stated period (used, for example, for cardiac stents and for trade-margin capping of certain cancer drugs).
- **Overcharging:** amounts charged above the notified price are **recovered with interest** under the Essential Commodities Act.
- **Discontinuation:** a manufacturer of a scheduled formulation intending to stop production must give **six months' notice**.
- **Exemptions:** certain patented drugs and drugs from new delivery systems or indigenous research may be exempted for a period as the Order allows.
- **Penalties:** contravention of the DPCO is punishable under the **Essential Commodities Act, 1955**.

### National List of Essential Medicines (NLEM)

- **Essential medicines** are those that satisfy the **priority health care needs** of the population, chosen for **efficacy, safety and cost-effectiveness**, and are meant to be available at all times in adequate amounts and affordable prices.
- India's lists: 1996, 2003, 2011, 2015 and **NLEM 2022** (released September 2022; **384 medicines** in **27 therapeutic categories**).
- NLEM 2022 forms the **First Schedule of the DPCO 2013**, so medicines in it come under **price control**.
- Medicines are listed by **generic name**, with dosage form and strength, and the level of health care (primary, secondary, tertiary).
- Criteria include: proven efficacy and safety, relevance to public health in India, cost-effectiveness, approval in India, and preference for single-ingredient medicines; fixed-dose combinations only where proven useful.

## Remember

- **DMR Act 1954:** s.3 bans adverts for miscarriage, contraception, sexual pleasure, menstrual disorders and **54 Schedule diseases**; s.4 misleading adverts; s.5 **magic remedies** (talisman, mantra, kavacha, charm); s.14 exemptions; s.7 penalty **6 months / 1 year** as enacted.
- **PCA Act 1960:** Chapter IV (s.14–20); **CPCSEA → CCSEA (2023)**; Rules 1998; IAEC; registration, 3 Rs, records, power to suspend or revoke registration (s.19), penalty (s.20).
- **NPPA (1997)** enforces **DPCO 2013** (under s.3 of the **Essential Commodities Act, 1955**; based on NPPP 2012).
- Ceiling price = **simple average PTR of brands with ≥1% share + 16% retailer margin**; revised yearly on **1 April** by **WPI**; non-scheduled drugs **≤10%** rise per year; para 19 for extraordinary circumstances.
- **NLEM 2022:** 384 medicines, 27 categories = First Schedule of DPCO 2013.

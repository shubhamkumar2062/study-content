# Unit 1: Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and Rules, 1945 – I

The Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 (the D&C Act) is the main law that controls the **import, manufacture, distribution and sale** of drugs and cosmetics in India. The Act lays down the broad rules; the **Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945** give the details (licence forms, fees, schedules, labels). This unit covers the objectives, definitions, schedules, import and manufacture. Unit 2 covers the remaining schedules, sale, labelling and the administration of the Act.

> **Law changes.** The Rules are amended many times a year by notifications (G.S.R.) of the Ministry of Health. Form numbers, fees, schedules and some penalties quoted in older textbooks have changed. Before an exam, compare your textbook with the current Act and Rules on the CDSCO website (cdsco.gov.in) or India Code (indiacode.nic.in).

## 1.1 Background and objectives

The Act came from the recommendations of the **Drugs Enquiry Committee (1930–31)**, chaired by Col. R. N. Chopra (Unit 5). It was first called the Drugs Act, 1940; amendments in the 1960s brought in cosmetics (the Act was renamed the Drugs and Cosmetics Act) and Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani drugs.

**Objectives**

1. To regulate the **import, manufacture, distribution and sale** of drugs and cosmetics through **licences** and **permits**.
2. To make sure that drugs and cosmetics sold in India are **safe, effective and of standard quality**.
3. To prevent sale of **substandard, misbranded, adulterated and spurious** drugs and cosmetics.
4. To regulate Ayurvedic, Siddha, Unani (ASU) and Homoeopathic drugs as well as allopathic drugs.
5. To set up advisory and testing bodies: the **Drugs Technical Advisory Board (DTAB)**, the **Drugs Consultative Committee (DCC)**, the **Central Drugs Laboratory (CDL)** and similar bodies for ASU drugs.
6. To provide for **inspectors and government analysts** and to lay down **offences and penalties**.

## 1.2 Important definitions

| Term | Meaning in simple words |
| --- | --- |
| **Drug** | All medicines for internal or external use of human beings or animals, and substances used in the diagnosis, treatment, mitigation or prevention of disease; substances (other than food) meant to affect the structure or function of the body; substances used against vermin or insects that cause disease; components of drugs such as empty capsules; and **devices notified** by the Central Government |
| **Cosmetic** | Any article meant to be rubbed, poured, sprinkled, sprayed or applied to the body for **cleansing, beautifying, promoting attractiveness or altering appearance**; includes components of cosmetics |
| **Ayurvedic, Siddha or Unani drug** | Medicines made exclusively according to the formulae in the authoritative books listed in the **First Schedule** of the Act |
| **Patent or proprietary medicine** | (Allopathic) a drug that is a remedy or prescription presented in a form ready for internal or external use and **not included in the current Indian Pharmacopoeia** or other recognised pharmacopoeia; (ASU) a drug in a ready form with ingredients from the authoritative books but not itself a classical formula |
| **Manufacture** | Any process or part of a process for making, altering, ornamenting, finishing, packing, labelling, breaking up or otherwise treating a drug or cosmetic with a view to sale or distribution. It does **not** include compounding or dispensing in the ordinary course of retail business |
| **Government Analyst** | A person appointed under section 20 to test samples |
| **Inspector** | A person appointed under section 21 to enforce the Act |
| **Import** | Bringing into India |

### Misbranded, adulterated and spurious drugs

| Category (section) | A drug is in this category if… |
| --- | --- |
| **Misbranded** (17) | It is coloured, coated, powdered or polished so that damage is concealed or it looks better than it is; it is **not labelled as prescribed**; or its label or container carries a **false or misleading** statement or claim |
| **Adulterated** (17A) | It consists of a **filthy, putrid or decomposed** substance; it was made or stored in **insanitary conditions**; its container is made of a **poisonous** substance; it has a colour other than a **permitted colour**; it contains a **harmful or toxic** substance; or a substance has been **mixed to reduce its quality or strength** |
| **Spurious** (17B) | It is sold under the **name of another drug** (imitation or substitute); it **pretends to be the product of a manufacturer** who did not make it; it bears the name of a **fictitious** manufacturer or company; it has been **substituted** wholly or partly by another drug; or it claims to be the product of a manufacturer of whom it is not truly a product |

Cosmetics have parallel definitions (misbranded, spurious and adulterated cosmetics — sections 17C, 17D and 17E).

```text
Not of standard quality  -> fails the standards in the Second Schedule (IP, etc.)
Misbranded               -> wrong or misleading label / presentation
Adulterated              -> bad material or bad conditions of making / storing
Spurious                 -> fake identity (imitation, false maker, substitution)
```

## 1.3 Schedules to the Act and to the Rules

The **Act** has two schedules:

| Schedule of the Act | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **First Schedule** | List of authoritative books of Ayurveda, Siddha and Unani systems |
| **Second Schedule** | **Standards** to be followed by imported and manufactured drugs (for example, Indian Pharmacopoeia standards for official drugs; label claim and formula for patent or proprietary medicines) |

The **Rules** have many schedules. Their **legal definitions** (what each schedule covers) are a common short question:

| Schedule (Rules) | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| A | Forms of applications, licences, certificates and reports |
| B | Fees for testing or analysis by the CDL and State laboratories |
| C and C(1) | Biological and special products (vaccines, sera, insulin, parenterals; digitalis and ergot preparations, ophthalmic preparations, etc.) needing special licences |
| D | Exemptions in respect of import of certain drugs |
| E(1) | Poisonous substances under ASU systems (warning labels) |
| F | Requirements for vaccines, sera and other biologicals; its Part XII B covers **blood banks (now called blood centres)** and Part XII C covers blood products from human plasma |
| F(1), F(2), F(3), F(F) | Standards for vaccines, sera, surgical dressings, umbilical tapes, ophthalmic preparations |
| G | Drugs to be used **under medical supervision** (caution label) |
| H and H1 | **Prescription drugs**; H1 = specified antibiotics, anti-TB and habit-forming drugs with a register |
| J | Diseases and ailments that **no drug may claim to prevent or cure** |
| K | Drugs and situations **exempted** from certain provisions |
| M, M-I to M-III | **Good Manufacturing Practices** (allopathic, homoeopathic, devices) |
| N | Minimum equipment for efficient running of a **pharmacy** |
| O | Standards for disinfectant fluids |
| P, P1 | **Life period** (shelf life) of drugs and pack sizes |
| Q | Permitted coal tar colours in cosmetics; cosmetic standards |
| R | Standards for mechanical contraceptives |
| S | Standards for cosmetics |
| T | GMP for **Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani** medicines |
| U, U(1) | Particulars of **manufacturing and analytical records** (drugs; cosmetics) |
| V | Standards for **patent or proprietary medicines** (vitamin and mineral content) |
| W | (Now omitted) generic names of drugs to be marketed under generic names |
| X | **Narcotic and psychotropic drugs** with high misuse potential, needing special licences |
| Y | Requirements for clinical trials and import or manufacture of **new drugs** — now replaced by the **New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019** |

Schedules G, H, M, N, P, T, U, V, X, Y and Part XII B with Schedule F are studied in detail in Unit 2.

## 1.4 Import of drugs and cosmetics

### Classes of drugs and cosmetics prohibited from import (section 10)

No one may import:

1. A drug or cosmetic that is **not of standard quality**.
2. A **misbranded**, **adulterated** or **spurious** drug, or a misbranded or spurious cosmetic.
3. A **patent or proprietary medicine** whose label does not show the true formula or list of active ingredients with quantities.
4. A drug that claims to **cure or mitigate a disease named in Schedule J** (or claims it falsely).
5. A **cosmetic** containing an ingredient that may make it unsafe or harmful under the directions for use.
6. Any drug or cosmetic whose import is **prohibited by the rules** (for example, drugs whose manufacture, sale or distribution is **prohibited in the country of origin**, except for certain approved purposes).
7. Any drug or cosmetic imported **without a licence** where one is needed.

Under **section 10A**, the Central Government can **prohibit import** of any drug or cosmetic that is likely to involve risk to people or animals, or that does not have the therapeutic value claimed. The Rules also prohibit import of **cosmetics tested on animals** (a 2014 amendment).

### Import under licence or permit

```text
Foreign manufacturer --(registration of site + product)--> Registration Certificate
            |
Indian importer --(applies to the Licensing Authority = DCGI, CDSCO)--> Import licence
            |
Customs ports with CDSCO port offices check every consignment
```

| Purpose of import | What is needed (older form numbers in brackets — check current rules) |
| --- | --- |
| Import of drugs for **sale** (other than Schedule X) | Registration certificate of the foreign maker and product, and an **import licence** (Form 10) |
| Import of **Schedule X** drugs | Separate licence (Form 10A) |
| Import for **examination, test or analysis** | Test licence (Form 11), small quantities, not for sale |
| Import for **personal use** of a patient | Small quantities for a patient's own use, with a prescription, are allowed under the conditions in the rules; larger quantities need a permit |
| Import of **new drugs** or for **clinical trials** | Permission under the **New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019** |
| Import of **medical devices** | Licence under the **Medical Devices Rules, 2017** |

**Conditions of an import licence** (main points): the importer must have premises with proper storage; must allow inspection; each consignment must carry the manufacturer's test report; drugs must have the prescribed **residual shelf life** at import; the importer must report any change and keep records of sales.

### Offences and penalties on import (section 13)

- Import of a **spurious or adulterated** drug (and of a drug or cosmetic that is not of standard quality in some cases) is punishable with **imprisonment and fine**, and the goods are **confiscated** (section 14).
- Import of any other drug or cosmetic in contravention of the Act attracts a smaller penalty, which can be **compounded** under section 32B (inserted in 2008).
- **Repeat offences** carry higher punishment.

The amounts for section 13 have been revised over the years; learn the pattern (spurious/adulterated = heavier; other breaches = lighter, compoundable) and check the current figures in the bare Act.

## 1.5 Manufacture of drugs

### Prohibition of manufacture and sale of certain drugs (section 18)

No person shall manufacture for sale, sell, stock, exhibit for sale or distribute:

1. A drug that is **not of standard quality**, or is **misbranded, adulterated or spurious**.
2. A cosmetic that is not of standard quality, or is misbranded, adulterated or spurious.
3. A **patent or proprietary medicine** that does not display the true formula on the label.
4. A drug that claims to cure or prevent a **Schedule J** disease.
5. A drug or cosmetic in contravention of the Act or Rules.
6. Any drug or cosmetic **without a licence**.

**Section 26A** allows the Central Government (after consulting the DTAB) to **regulate, restrict or prohibit** manufacture and sale of a drug that involves risk, lacks the claimed therapeutic value or has no therapeutic justification (irrational fixed-dose combinations have been banned this way). **Section 26B** (inserted in 2008) lets the Government regulate or restrict a drug needed in an **emergency** such as an epidemic or natural calamity.

### Types of manufacturing licence

| Licence (traditional form number) | For |
| --- | --- |
| Form 25 | Drugs other than Schedules C, C(1) and X |
| Form 28 | Drugs in Schedules C and C(1) (not X) |
| Form 25A / 28A | **Loan licence** (same groups as above) |
| Form 25B | **Repacking** licence |
| Form 29 | Manufacture for **examination, test or analysis** |
| Form 28D | Large volume parenterals, sera, vaccines, blood products and similar items (with central approval) |
| Separate licences | Schedule X drugs, cosmetics, homoeopathic medicines, ASU drugs |

The **State Licensing Authority** grants most licences. For Schedule C/C(1) products, large volume parenterals, vaccines, sera and blood products, the licence also needs the approval of the **Central Licence Approving Authority (the Drugs Controller General of India)**. Under amendments made in 2017–18, most licences no longer expire after five years; they stay valid while the **retention fee** is paid at the set interval. Confirm the current rule.

### Conditions for the grant of a manufacturing licence

1. **Premises, plant and equipment** that meet **Schedule M** (GMP).
2. **Technical staff:** manufacture must be under the **active direction and personal supervision** of a competent person with a degree in pharmacy, pharmaceutical chemistry or chemistry (or medicine, for certain products) with the experience the rules lay down.
3. **Testing staff and laboratory:** a separate quality control section headed by a qualified person, with its own testing facilities.
4. **Adequate storage** for raw materials, intermediates and finished products.
5. **Inspection** of the premises by drugs inspectors (jointly with central inspectors for C/C(1) items) before the licence is granted.

### Conditions of the licence (to be followed after grant)

1. Maintain records of manufacture and analysis as given in **Schedule U**, for the period laid down.
2. **Test each batch** of raw materials and finished products; release only after QC approval.
3. Keep **reference (retained) samples** of each batch.
4. Allow inspectors to **enter, inspect and take samples**, and supply samples on demand.
5. Report changes in technical staff, premises or products.
6. Recall batches found not of standard quality and inform the licensing authority.
7. Comply with **Schedule M** at all times; label as per the rules.
8. Pay the licence **retention fee** on time.

### Manufacture of drugs for test, examination and analysis

A licence (Form 29) lets an institution or manufacturer make **small quantities** of a drug for research, stability studies, development or clinical trial supply. Conditions: the drug **cannot be sold**; quantities are limited; records of production, use and disposal must be kept; the licence is for a stated period; inspectors may check the records.

### Manufacture of a new drug

A **new drug** is one not yet approved in India for the proposed claims, or an approved drug with a **new indication, new dosage form, new route, new strength** or **new fixed-dose combination**, or certain biologicals. A new drug can be made for sale only after **permission of the Central Licensing Authority (DCGI)** under the **New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019**, usually based on clinical trial data. Only then does the State licensing authority add the product to the manufacturing licence. (Before 2019, this was governed by Rule 122 and **Schedule Y**.)

### Loan licence

```text
Loan licensee (owns the brand, no factory)
        |
        |  uses premises, equipment and technical staff of
        v
Licensed manufacturer (has a Form 25/28 licence)
        |
        v
Product made under the loan licence (Form 25A / 28A); both share responsibility for quality
```

The loan licensee must show that the host factory has spare capacity and agrees to make the products. The loan licensee's own technical staff may be required to supervise. Both firms are answerable for quality.

### Repacking licence

**Repacking** is breaking up a drug from a bulk container into **small packs** for retail sale (for example, tablets, powders or ointments repacked from bulk). It needs a separate repacking licence (Form 25B). Conditions: suitable premises, supervision by a competent person (pharmacist or equivalent), proper labelling with the original manufacturer's details and batch number, and records. Certain categories (such as Schedule C/C(1) products) may not be repacked under it.

## 1.6 Offences and penalties for manufacture and sale (section 27, as amended in 2008)

| Offence | Punishment |
| --- | --- |
| **27(a)** Manufacture, sale or distribution of an **adulterated or spurious drug** which, when used, is likely to cause **death or grievous hurt** | Imprisonment **not less than 10 years**, which may extend to **life**; fine **not less than ₹10 lakh or three times the value of the drugs confiscated**, whichever is more. The fine is paid as **compensation** to the victim or, if the person died, to the relatives |
| **27(b)** **Adulterated** drug (not covered by (a)), or drug made or sold **without a valid licence** | Imprisonment **3 to 5 years**; fine **not less than ₹1 lakh or three times the value**, whichever is more |
| **27(c)** **Spurious** drug (not covered by (a)) | Imprisonment **not less than 7 years**, may extend to **life**; fine **not less than ₹3 lakh or three times the value**, whichever is more |
| **27(d)** Any other contravention (for example, not of standard quality, breach of licence conditions) | Imprisonment **1 to 2 years** and fine **not less than ₹20,000** (the court may give less for special reasons) |

Other features added in 2008:

- Offences under 27(a) and 27(c) are **cognizable and non-bailable**.
- **Special courts** can be designated for these offences; trials are by a **Court of Session**.
- **Prosecution** can be started by an inspector, an authorised gazetted officer, the aggrieved person or a recognised consumer association.
- Some minor offences can be **compounded** (section 32B). The **Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023** extended compounding to more offences (including section 27(d)) and changed some other penalty provisions of this Act; check the current text.
- **Repeat offences** (section 30) carry higher minimum terms.

## Remember

- Act = framework; **Rules, 1945** = details. First Schedule = ASU books; Second Schedule = standards.
- **Misbranded** = label fault; **adulterated** = material or condition fault; **spurious** = false identity.
- Import: **section 10** prohibited classes, **10A** Government power to ban import; import licence via **CDSCO**; test licence for analysis; NDCT Rules 2019 for new drugs; Medical Devices Rules 2017 for devices.
- Manufacture: **section 18** prohibitions; **26A** ban; licences 25/28 (general/C,C1), 25A/28A (loan), 25B (repack), 29 (test).
- Conditions of licence: Schedule M premises, qualified staff, own QC, Schedule U records, inspection, retained samples.
- Section 27 after 2008: (a) death/grievous hurt **10 years–life, ≥₹10 lakh**; (b) **3–5 years, ≥₹1 lakh**; (c) spurious **7 years–life, ≥₹3 lakh**; (d) others **1–2 years, ≥₹20,000**.
- Rules and forms keep changing — always check the current CDSCO text.

# Steam distillation

**Aim:** To purify a steam-volatile organic liquid (for example nitrobenzene or aniline) by steam distillation.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves. Nitrobenzene and aniline are toxic through the skin; handle them in a fume hood.

## Principle

When two immiscible liquids are heated together, each exerts its own vapour pressure. The mixture boils when the sum equals atmospheric pressure, so it boils **below 100 °C** and below the boiling point of the organic liquid. A high-boiling, water-insoluble compound therefore distils with steam at under 100 °C without decomposing. Non-volatile impurities stay behind.

Weight ratio in the distillate: w(organic) ÷ w(water) = (p(organic) × M(organic)) ÷ (p(water) × 18).

## Requirements

Steam generator (or boiling flask with water), round-bottom flask with the impure liquid, long condenser, receiver, separating funnel, anhydrous CaCl2, safety tube.

## Procedure

1. Put the impure liquid with a little water in the round-bottom flask, tilted, and connect it to the steam generator and condenser.
2. Pass steam. Heat the flask gently so water does not collect.
3. Collect the milky distillate until it runs clear.
4. Separate the organic layer in a separating funnel, dry it over anhydrous CaCl2 and weigh it.

## Precautions

- Keep a safety tube in the steam generator.
- Stop by disconnecting the steam line before turning off heat, or liquid sucks back.
- Use a long, efficient condenser.

## Viva questions

1. **Which compounds suit steam distillation?** Water-insoluble, steam-volatile, with a reasonable vapour pressure near 100 °C, and stable to steam.
2. **Why does the mixture boil below 100 °C?** The vapour pressures add up, so atmospheric pressure is reached at a lower temperature.
3. **Give pharmaceutical uses.** Volatile oils (clove, eucalyptus), purifying aniline and nitrobenzene, separating o-nitrophenol from p-nitrophenol.
4. **Why is o-nitrophenol steam-volatile but p-nitrophenol not?** The ortho isomer has intramolecular hydrogen bonding; the para isomer is held by intermolecular hydrogen bonds.

# Salicylic acid from methyl salicylate (hydrolysis)

**Aim:** To prepare salicylic acid by alkaline hydrolysis of methyl salicylate.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

Esters are hydrolysed by hot alkali to the alcohol and the salt of the acid (saponification). Acidifying the salt precipitates the free acid.

C6H4(OH)COOCH3 + 2 NaOH → C6H4(ONa)COONa + CH3OH + H2O
C6H4(ONa)COONa + 2 HCl → C6H4(OH)COOH + 2 NaCl

## Requirements

Methyl salicylate 2 ml, 10% NaOH 25 ml, conc. HCl, round-bottom flask with reflux condenser, ice, Büchner funnel.

## Procedure

1. Reflux 2 ml methyl salicylate with 25 ml 10% NaOH for about 30 minutes, until the oily drops disappear.
2. Cool, then add conc. HCl slowly with stirring until the solution is acidic to litmus. White salicylic acid separates.
3. Cool in ice, filter, wash with a little cold water, and dry.
4. Recrystallise from hot water.

## Result

Theoretical yield (2 ml methyl salicylate ≈ 2.36 g, M 152): (2.36 ÷ 152) × 138 ≈ 2.1 g.

## Viva questions

1. **Why does methyl salicylate need two moles of NaOH?** One for the ester group, one for the acidic phenolic –OH.
2. **How do you know hydrolysis is complete?** The oily layer and its wintergreen smell disappear.
3. **Test for salicylic acid?** Violet colour with neutral FeCl3 (phenolic –OH).
4. **Uses?** Keratolytic (corn and wart removers), and the starting material for aspirin.

# 1-Phenylazo-2-naphthol (diazotisation and coupling)

**Aim:** To prepare 1-phenylazo-2-naphthol, an orange-red azo dye, from aniline.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves. Keep the diazonium solution cold and use it at once; never let it dry.

## Principle

Aniline is diazotised with nitrous acid (NaNO2 + HCl) at 0–5 °C to benzenediazonium chloride. This weak electrophile couples with 2-naphthol in alkaline solution at C1, giving the azo dye.

C6H5NH2 + NaNO2 + 2 HCl → C6H5N2+Cl− + NaCl + 2 H2O
C6H5N2+Cl− + 2-naphthol (NaOH) → 1-phenylazo-2-naphthol + NaCl + H2O

## Requirements

Aniline 2 ml, conc. HCl 5 ml, sodium nitrite 1.6 g, 2-naphthol 3 g, 10% NaOH 25 ml, ice, starch–iodide paper, beakers, Büchner funnel.

## Procedure

1. Mix 2 ml aniline with 5 ml conc. HCl and 5 ml water; cool to 0–5 °C in ice.
2. Dissolve 1.6 g NaNO2 in 8 ml water and add it drop by drop, keeping below 5 °C, until starch–iodide paper turns blue (slight excess of nitrous acid).
3. Dissolve 3 g 2-naphthol in 25 ml 10% NaOH and cool in ice.
4. Pour the diazonium solution slowly into the naphthol solution with stirring. An orange-red solid forms.
5. Stir 15 minutes in ice, filter, wash with cold water, and dry. Recrystallise from ethanol if asked.

## Result

Practical yield: ___ g. Colour: orange-red. Melting point: ___ °C (reported: about 131 °C).

## Viva questions

1. **Why 0–5 °C?** Above this the diazonium salt loses N2 and forms phenol.
2. **What is the starch–iodide test for?** Free nitrous acid oxidises iodide to iodine, showing diazotisation is complete.
3. **Why couple in alkali?** 2-Naphthol becomes the naphthoxide ion, which is much more reactive.
4. **Where does coupling happen on 2-naphthol?** At C1 (the α-position next to –OH).
5. **Pharmaceutical relevance?** Prontosil (first sulfa drug) is an azo dye; the Bratton–Marshall assay of sulfonamides uses diazotisation and coupling.

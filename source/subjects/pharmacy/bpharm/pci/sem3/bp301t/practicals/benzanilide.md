# Benzanilide from aniline (benzoylation)

**Aim:** To prepare benzanilide from aniline by the Schotten–Baumann reaction.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves. Benzoyl chloride is a lachrymator and aniline is toxic; work in the fume hood.

## Principle

An amine reacts with an acid chloride in aqueous alkali (Schotten–Baumann). The alkali neutralises the HCl formed and destroys excess benzoyl chloride.

C6H5NH2 + C6H5COCl + NaOH → C6H5NHCOC6H5 + NaCl + H2O

## Requirements

Aniline 2 ml, benzoyl chloride 3 ml, 10% NaOH 25 ml, stoppered conical flask, Büchner funnel, ethanol for recrystallisation.

## Procedure

1. Put 2 ml aniline and 25 ml 10% NaOH in a stoppered conical flask.
2. Add 3 ml benzoyl chloride in small portions, stoppering and shaking hard after each addition.
3. Keep shaking for about 15 minutes, until the smell of benzoyl chloride is gone. The solution must stay alkaline.
4. Filter the solid, wash well with cold water, and dry.
5. Recrystallise from hot ethanol.

## Result

Theoretical yield (from 2 ml aniline, about 2.04 g, M 93): (2.04 ÷ 93) × 197 ≈ 4.3 g.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the Schotten–Baumann reaction?** Acylation of amines, alcohols or phenols with an acid chloride in aqueous alkali.
2. **Why NaOH?** It neutralises HCl and keeps the amine free to react; it also hydrolyses leftover benzoyl chloride.
3. **Why add benzoyl chloride slowly?** The reaction is exothermic.
4. **Pharmaceutical use of acylation?** Protecting –NH2 groups during synthesis, e.g. acetanilide in sulfonamide synthesis.

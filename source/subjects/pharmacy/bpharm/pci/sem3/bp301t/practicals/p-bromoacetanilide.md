# p-Bromoacetanilide from acetanilide (bromination)

**Aim:** To prepare p-bromoacetanilide from acetanilide.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves. Bromine is corrosive and toxic; use it only in the fume hood. Many labs make bromine in situ from KBr and KBrO3 instead.

## Principle

–NHCOCH3 is a moderately activating ortho/para director. Its bulk hinders the ortho position, so bromine enters mainly at para. Unlike the free –NH2 group of aniline, it does not give a tribromo product.

C6H5NHCOCH3 + Br2 → p-BrC6H4NHCOCH3 + HBr

## Requirements

Acetanilide 2 g, glacial acetic acid 10 ml, bromine in glacial acetic acid (about 1 ml Br2 in 5 ml), ice water, Büchner funnel, ethanol.

## Procedure

1. Dissolve 2 g acetanilide in 10 ml glacial acetic acid in a conical flask.
2. Add the bromine solution drop by drop with shaking until a faint yellow colour stays.
3. Leave 15 minutes, then pour into about 100 ml ice-cold water with stirring.
4. Filter, wash with cold water (add a little sodium bisulfite if it is still yellow), and dry.
5. Recrystallise from ethanol.

## Viva questions

1. **Why acetanilide and not aniline?** Aniline is so activating that it gives 2,4,6-tribromoaniline; the acetyl group tames it and the product is mono-brominated.
2. **Why is the para product major?** The bulky –NHCOCH3 group blocks the ortho positions.
3. **Why glacial acetic acid?** It dissolves both reactants and is not attacked by bromine.
4. **How do you get p-bromoaniline from the product?** Hydrolyse the amide with acid or alkali.

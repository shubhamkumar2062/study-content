# Iodine value of an oil (Wijs method)

**Aim:** To determine the iodine value of the given oil by the Wijs method.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves. Keep Wijs solution and chloroform in the fume hood.

## Principle

Iodine value is the number of grams of iodine absorbed by 100 g of oil. It measures unsaturation. Iodine monochloride (Wijs solution) adds across the C=C bonds. The unused ICl is turned into iodine by KI, and the iodine is titrated with standard sodium thiosulfate using starch. A blank gives the total ICl taken.

ICl + KI → KCl + I2;  I2 + 2 Na2S2O3 → 2 NaI + Na2S4O6

## Requirements

Oil, chloroform (or carbon tetrachloride / cyclohexane as your lab uses), Wijs solution, 10% KI, 0.1 N sodium thiosulfate, starch indicator, iodine flasks with stoppers, pipettes, burette.

## Procedure

1. Weigh 0.1–0.5 g oil (W) into an iodine flask; take less for highly unsaturated oils. Dissolve in 10 ml chloroform.
2. Pipette 25 ml Wijs solution in, stopper, swirl, and keep in the dark for 30 minutes. Set up a blank the same way without oil.
3. Add 15 ml 10% KI and 100 ml water to each.
4. Titrate with 0.1 N thiosulfate until pale yellow, add starch, and continue until the blue just disappears.
5. Note the readings: blank B ml, sample S ml.

## Calculation

Iodine value = (12.69 × N × (B − S)) ÷ W

where N is the normality of thiosulfate (12.69 = 126.9 × 100 ÷ 1000).

## Result

The iodine value of the given oil is ___ g iodine/100 g. Class: non-drying (< 100), semi-drying (100–130) or drying (> 130).

## Viva questions

1. **Why keep the flasks in the dark?** Light causes side reactions and loss of iodine.
2. **Why add starch only near the end?** At high iodine concentration the starch–iodine complex forms and does not release iodine easily, giving a late end point.
3. **Why use ICl rather than iodine alone?** Iodine adds slowly and incompletely; ICl is more reactive.
4. **Name a drying oil and a non-drying oil.** Linseed oil (drying), olive or castor oil (non-drying).

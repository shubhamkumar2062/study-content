# Recrystallisation and melting point

**Aim:** To purify crude benzoic acid by recrystallisation from water and find its melting point.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

A solid is far more soluble in a hot solvent than in the same solvent cold. Dissolve the crude solid in the least hot solvent, filter off insoluble dirt while hot, and let it cool. The pure compound crystallises; soluble impurities stay in the mother liquor. A pure solid melts sharply (within 1–2 °C); impurities lower the melting point and widen the range.

## Requirements

Crude benzoic acid (2 g), distilled water, activated charcoal, beakers, funnel, fluted filter paper, Büchner funnel and suction, capillary tubes, Thiele tube with liquid paraffin, thermometer.

## Procedure

1. Put 2 g crude benzoic acid in a beaker with about 40 ml water. Heat to boiling and stir; add hot water in small portions until it just dissolves.
2. If coloured, cool slightly, add a pinch of charcoal and boil 2–3 minutes.
3. Filter hot through a fluted paper in a warmed funnel.
4. Let the filtrate cool slowly to room temperature, then in ice.
5. Collect the crystals by suction, wash with a little ice-cold water, and dry.
6. Fill a capillary sealed at one end to about 3 mm with the dry powder. Tie it to the thermometer, heat the Thiele tube slowly (about 2 °C a minute near the melting point), and note the temperatures at which melting starts and ends.

## Result

Weight of pure benzoic acid: ___ g. Practical yield = (weight obtained ÷ weight taken) × 100 = ___ %.
Melting point: ___ °C (reported: 122 °C).

## Precautions

- Use the minimum hot solvent, or much product stays dissolved.
- Never add charcoal to a boiling solution; it froths over.
- Cool slowly for bigger, purer crystals.
- Heat slowly near the melting point.

## Viva questions

1. **What makes a good recrystallisation solvent?** Dissolves the compound well hot and poorly cold, does not react with it, dissolves impurities or not at all, and is easy to remove.
2. **Why use a fluted filter paper and a warm funnel?** Fast filtration, so crystals do not form in the funnel.
3. **What does charcoal do?** Adsorbs coloured impurities.
4. **What does a low, wide melting range show?** The sample is impure or wet.
5. **What is mixed melting point?** Mixing the sample with an authentic sample: no fall in melting point means they are the same compound.

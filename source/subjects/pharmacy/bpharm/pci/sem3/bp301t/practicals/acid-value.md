# Acid value of an oil

**Aim:** To determine the acid value of the given oil (for example castor oil).

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

Acid value is the number of milligrams of KOH needed to neutralise the **free** fatty acids in 1 g of oil. The oil is dissolved in neutral alcohol–ether and titrated with standard alkali using phenolphthalein. A high acid value shows hydrolytic rancidity or poor refining.

RCOOH + KOH → RCOOK + H2O

## Requirements

Oil, ethanol–ether (1:1) neutralised to phenolphthalein, 0.1 N KOH (or NaOH), phenolphthalein, conical flask, burette, balance.

## Procedure

1. Weigh about 5 g oil (W) into a conical flask.
2. Add 50 ml of the neutralised ethanol–ether and shake until the oil dissolves (warm gently if needed).
3. Add a few drops of phenolphthalein and titrate with 0.1 N KOH, shaking, until a faint pink lasts 30 seconds.
4. Note the volume (V ml). Repeat for concordant readings.

## Calculation

Acid value = (56.1 × N × V) ÷ W

where N is the normality of KOH and 56.1 the molecular weight of KOH.

## Result

The acid value of the given oil is ___ mg KOH/g.

## Viva questions

1. **Define acid value.** Mg of KOH to neutralise the free fatty acids in 1 g of fat or oil.
2. **Why neutralise the solvent first?** So only the acids from the oil use up the alkali.
3. **Why does acid value rise on storage?** Lipase or moisture hydrolyses the glycerides into free fatty acids (hydrolytic rancidity).
4. **How is acid value related to free fatty acid %?** FFA % (as oleic acid) is about acid value ÷ 2.

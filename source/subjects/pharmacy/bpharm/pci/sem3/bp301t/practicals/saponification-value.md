# Saponification value of an oil

**Aim:** To determine the saponification value of the given oil.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

Saponification value is the number of milligrams of KOH needed to saponify 1 g of oil, that is, to neutralise both free and combined (ester) fatty acids. The oil is refluxed with a known excess of alcoholic KOH, and the KOH left over is titrated with standard HCl. A blank without oil gives the total KOH taken.

Triglyceride + 3 KOH → glycerol + 3 RCOOK (soap)

A high value means short-chain fatty acids (low molecular weight), as in coconut oil.

## Requirements

Oil, 0.5 N alcoholic KOH, 0.5 N HCl, phenolphthalein, two round-bottom flasks with reflux condensers, water bath, burette, pipette.

## Procedure

1. Weigh about 2 g oil (W) into a round-bottom flask.
2. Pipette 25 ml of 0.5 N alcoholic KOH into it, and 25 ml into a second flask with no oil (blank).
3. Reflux both on a water bath for 30–60 minutes, until the oil drops disappear.
4. Cool, add phenolphthalein and titrate each with 0.5 N HCl until the pink just disappears.
5. Note the readings: blank B ml, sample S ml.

## Calculation

Saponification value = (56.1 × N × (B − S)) ÷ W

where N is the normality of HCl.

## Result

The saponification value of the given oil is ___ mg KOH/g.

## Viva questions

1. **Why run a blank?** Alcoholic KOH changes strength on standing; the blank gives the exact KOH taken.
2. **What does saponification value tell you?** The average chain length (molecular weight) of the fatty acids.
3. **What is ester value?** Saponification value minus acid value.
4. **Why alcoholic and not aqueous KOH?** Oil dissolves in alcohol, so the reaction is faster and complete.

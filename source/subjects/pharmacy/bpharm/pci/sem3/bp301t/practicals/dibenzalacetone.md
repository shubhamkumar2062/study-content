# Dibenzalacetone (Claisen–Schmidt condensation)

**Aim:** To prepare dibenzalacetone from benzaldehyde and acetone.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

In alkali, acetone forms an enolate that adds to benzaldehyde (which has no α-hydrogen, so it cannot condense with itself). Dehydration gives benzalacetone, and repeating on the other side of acetone gives dibenzalacetone (1,5-diphenylpenta-1,4-dien-3-one).

2 C6H5CHO + CH3COCH3 → C6H5CH=CHCOCH=CHC6H5 + 2 H2O

## Requirements

Benzaldehyde 2.5 ml, acetone 0.9 ml, ethanol 20 ml, 10% NaOH 25 ml, conical flask, Büchner funnel.

## Procedure

1. Mix 2.5 ml benzaldehyde, 0.9 ml acetone and 20 ml ethanol in a conical flask.
2. Add 25 ml 10% NaOH and shake or stir for 15–30 minutes. A yellow solid separates.
3. Cool in ice, filter, and wash well with cold water until the washings are neutral.
4. Dry, then recrystallise from ethanol.

Practical yield: ___ g. Colour: pale yellow. Melting point: ___ °C (reported: about 111 °C).

## Viva questions

1. **Why does benzaldehyde not self-condense?** It has no α-hydrogen to form an enolate.
2. **Why 2 moles of benzaldehyde per mole of acetone?** Acetone has α-hydrogens on both sides, so it condenses twice.
3. **What is the base for?** It removes an α-hydrogen from acetone to form the enolate.
4. **Name another condensation of benzaldehyde.** Perkin reaction (with acetic anhydride and sodium acetate) gives cinnamic acid.

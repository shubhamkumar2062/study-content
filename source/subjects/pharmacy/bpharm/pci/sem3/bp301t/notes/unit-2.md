# Unit 2: Phenols, aromatic amines and aromatic acids

One rule explains most of this unit: **anything that spreads out or pulls away a negative charge makes an acid stronger; anything that pulls away a lone pair makes a base weaker.** Electron-withdrawing groups (EWG, e.g. –NO2) therefore raise acidity and lower basicity; electron-donating groups (EDG, e.g. –CH3, –OCH3) do the opposite.

**Quick pKa scale (smaller pKa = stronger acid)**

| Compound | pKa | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Picric acid (2,4,6-trinitrophenol) | 0.4 | As strong as a mineral acid |
| Benzoic acid | 4.2 | Carboxylic acid |
| Acetic acid | 4.76 | For comparison |
| Carbonic acid (H2CO3) | 6.4 | Decides NaHCO3 test |
| p-Nitrophenol | 7.2 | EWG raises acidity |
| Phenol | 10.0 | Weak acid |
| p-Cresol | 10.2 | EDG lowers acidity |
| Water | 15.7 |  |
| Ethanol | \~16 | Alcohols weaker than phenol |

## 2.1 Phenols

**Phenol** = C6H5OH, an –OH directly on a benzene ring (also called **carbolic acid**).

**Why phenol is acidic (more than alcohols):**

1. Phenol loses H+ to give the **phenoxide ion** C6H5O−.
2. The negative charge on oxygen spreads into the ring by resonance onto the **two ortho carbons and the para carbon**.
3. Spreading charge = a more stable ion = a stronger acid. In ethoxide (from ethanol) the charge stays stuck on oxygen.

```text
Phenoxide resonance (draw 4 forms + hybrid):

  O−            O              O              O
  |             ||             ||             ||
 benzene  <→  ring with −  <→  ring with − <→  ring with −
               at ortho C      at para C       at other ortho C
```

**Why phenol is weaker than carboxylic acids:** in carboxylate, the charge is shared by **two equal oxygens**. In phenoxide, three of the forms put the charge on carbon, which is less electronegative and holds it less happily.

**Practical test of acidity:** phenol dissolves in **NaOH** but does **not** react with **NaHCO3** (no fizzing), because phenol (pKa 10) is weaker than carbonic acid (6.4). Benzoic acid does fizz with NaHCO3. This separates the two in the lab.

**Effect of substituents on acidity of phenol**

| Substituent | Effect | Acidity vs phenol | Example (pKa) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| –NO2 at o/p | –I and –R; charge spreads onto NO2 oxygens | Much stronger | p-nitrophenol 7.2; picric acid 0.4 |
| –NO2 at meta | Only –I (no resonance to meta) | Stronger, less so | m-nitrophenol 8.4 |
| –Cl | –I | Slightly stronger | p-chlorophenol 9.4 |
| –CH3 | +I, hyperconjugation | Weaker | p-cresol 10.2 |
| –OCH3 at para | +R | Weaker | p-methoxyphenol 10.2 |

Order to memorise: **picric acid > 2,4-dinitrophenol > p-nitrophenol ≈ o-nitrophenol > m-nitrophenol > p-chlorophenol > phenol > p-cresol**. o-Nitrophenol is slightly weaker than para because its –OH is held by an **intramolecular hydrogen bond** to the nitro group, which makes the H harder to lose. The same H-bond makes o-nitrophenol **steam volatile**, which is how the o- and p-isomers are separated.

**Qualitative tests for phenol**

| Test | How to do it | Positive result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ferric chloride | Add neutral 1% FeCl3 to aqueous phenol | Violet colour (complex) |
| Bromine water | Add bromine water | White precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol; colour of Br2 vanishes |
| Liebermann's nitroso test | Phenol + NaNO2 + conc. H2SO4, warm | Deep green/blue → red on dilution with water → blue again with NaOH |
| Phthalein test | Heat phenol + phthalic anhydride + conc. H2SO4; pour into NaOH | Pink-red (phenolphthalein forms) |
| Azo dye test | Add to cold benzenediazonium chloride in NaOH | Orange-red dye (p-hydroxyazobenzene) |

**Two named reactions often asked with phenol**

```text
Kolbe–Schmidt:  sodium phenoxide + CO2 (125 °C, 4–7 atm) → sodium salicylate —H+→ salicylic acid
                (salicylic acid → aspirin with acetic anhydride)

Reimer–Tiemann: phenol + CHCl3 + NaOH (60 °C) → salicylaldehyde (o-hydroxybenzaldehyde)
                (electrophile = dichlorocarbene :CCl2)
```

**Structure and uses of phenol and its relatives**

| Compound | Structure | Pharmaceutical uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phenol | C6H5OH | Antiseptic and disinfectant (Lister's carbolic acid); 0.5% preservative in injections; local anaesthetic in throat sprays; makes salicylic acid → aspirin; Bakelite |
| Cresols | Methylphenols (o-, m-, p-CH3C6H4OH) | Stronger disinfectants than phenol; Lysol (cresol + soap solution) for floors, instruments; m-cresol preservative in insulin |
| Resorcinol | 1,3-Dihydroxybenzene | Keratolytic (peels skin) in acne, eczema, dandruff lotions; antiseptic; makes fluorescein dye |
| α-Naphthol | 1-Hydroxynaphthalene | Molisch test for carbohydrates; azo dyes |
| β-Naphthol | 2-Hydroxynaphthalene | Azo dyes (coupling); antiseptic; once used against intestinal worms |

## 2.2 Aromatic amines

**Aniline** = C6H5NH2. Like ammonia, it is a base because the nitrogen has a **lone pair** that can grab H+.

**Why aniline is a weaker base than ammonia and aliphatic amines:**

1. The N lone pair is **delocalised into the ring** by resonance (shared with ortho and para carbons), so it is less available for H+.
2. The anilinium ion (C6H5NH3+) has no such resonance, so protonation loses this stabilisation.
3. The ring carbon is sp2 and pulls electrons (–I).

| Base | pKb (smaller = stronger) |
| --- | --- |
| Methylamine | 3.4 |
| Ammonia | 4.75 |
| Aniline | 9.4 |
| Diphenylamine | 13.1 |
| Triphenylamine | \~neutral |

Order: **aliphatic amines > NH3 > aniline > diphenylamine > triphenylamine**.

**Effect of substituents on basicity of aniline**

| Substituent | Effect | Basicity | Example (pKb) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| –CH3 (para) | +I, hyperconjugation pushes electrons to N | Stronger | p-toluidine 8.9 |
| –OCH3 (para) | +R | Stronger | p-anisidine 8.7 |
| –Cl (para) | –I | Weaker | p-chloroaniline 10.0 |
| –NO2 (meta) | –I | Weaker | m-nitroaniline 11.5 |
| –NO2 (para) | –I and –R (lone pair pulled to NO2) | Much weaker | p-nitroaniline 13.0 |

**Ortho effect:** nearly every ortho-substituted aniline (even o-toluidine) is a **weaker** base than aniline. The ortho group crowds the –NH3+ formed on protonation (steric inhibition of protonation and solvation).

## 2.3 Diazonium salts

**Diazotisation** — a primary aromatic amine reacts with nitrous acid at **0–5 °C**:

```text
C6H5NH2 + NaNO2 + 2 HCl  —(0–5 °C)→  C6H5N2+ Cl− (benzenediazonium chloride) + NaCl + 2 H2O

Mechanism outline: NaNO2 + HCl → HNO2 → NO+ (nitrosonium)
                   ArNH2 + NO+ → Ar–NH–N=O (N-nitrosamine) → tautomerise → Ar–N=N–OH
                   → protonate, lose H2O → Ar–N≡N+
```

**Why ice-cold and why aromatic only:** aryl diazonium ions are stabilised by resonance with the ring, but above about 5 °C they lose N2 to form phenol. Aliphatic diazonium ions have no ring to share charge and decompose instantly. Diazonium salts are **used in solution immediately**; dry salts can explode.

**Synthetic applications — the "diazonium wheel"**

```text
                          C6H5N2+ Cl−
   ┌──────────────────┬────────────────┼─────────────────┬────────────────┐
 CuCl/HCl         CuBr/HBr         CuCN/KCN          KI                HBF4, heat
 C6H5Cl           C6H5Br           C6H5CN            C6H5I             C6H5F
 (Sandmeyer)      (Sandmeyer)      (Sandmeyer)                         (Balz–Schiemann)

   ┌──────────────────┬────────────────┼─────────────────┬────────────────┐
 H2O, warm        H3PO2 or EtOH    Cu powder + HCl   phenol, NaOH      aniline, mild acid
 C6H5OH           C6H6             C6H5Cl            p-hydroxyazo-     p-aminoazobenzene
 (phenol)         (deamination)    (Gattermann)      benzene (orange)  (yellow)
                                                     └── coupling reactions (N2 kept) ──┘
```

- **Replacement reactions** — N2 leaves, another group takes its place (Sandmeyer, Gattermann, KI, Balz–Schiemann, hydrolysis, reduction to benzene).
- **Coupling reactions** — N2 stays; the diazonium ion acts as a weak electrophile and attacks the para position of an activated ring (phenol in alkali, pH 9–10; amine in mild acid, pH 4–5). The product has an –N=N– (azo) group and is coloured.
- **Reduction with SnCl2/HCl** gives phenylhydrazine (C6H5NHNH2), used to identify sugars (osazones).

*Pharmacy links:* **Prontosil**, the first sulfa drug, is an azo dye; **methyl orange** indicator is made by coupling; the **Bratton–Marshall** assay of sulfonamides uses diazotisation + coupling to give a coloured product read in a colorimeter.

## 2.4 Aromatic acids (benzoic acid)

**Benzoic acid** = C6H5COOH, white crystals, found in gum benzoin.

**Acidity:** pKa 4.2. It is stronger than phenol because the benzoate ion shares its negative charge **equally between two oxygens** (two identical resonance forms). It is slightly stronger than acetic acid (4.76) because the sp2 phenyl group pulls electrons (–I), while the methyl of acetic acid pushes electrons in.

```text
Benzoate resonance:
   C6H5–C(=O)–O−   <→   C6H5–C(–O−)=O        (charge shared by two equal O atoms)
```

**Effect of substituents on acidity of benzoic acid**

| Substituent | Position | pKa | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| –NO2 | ortho | 2.2 | Ortho effect + –I |
| –OH (salicylic acid) | ortho | 3.0 | Intramolecular H-bond stabilises the anion |
| –NO2 | para | 3.4 | –I, –R stabilise anion |
| –NO2 | meta | 3.5 | –I |
| –Cl | para | 4.0 | –I |
| None (benzoic acid) | — | 4.2 | Reference |
| –CH3 | para | 4.4 | +I destabilises anion |
| –OCH3 | para | 4.5 | +R destabilises anion |

**Ortho effect:** almost any group at the ortho position makes benzoic acid **stronger**, whether it donates or withdraws. The ortho group pushes –COOH out of the ring's plane, so the ring can no longer donate electrons to it (steric inhibition of resonance).

**Typical reactions of benzoic acid**

| Reaction | Reagent | Product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Salt formation | NaOH or NaHCO3 (fizzes, CO2) | Sodium benzoate |
| Esterification | Ethanol + conc. H2SO4 | Ethyl benzoate (fruity smell) |
| Acid chloride | SOCl2 or PCl5 | Benzoyl chloride |
| Amide | Benzoyl chloride + NH3 | Benzamide |
| Reduction | LiAlH4 | Benzyl alcohol |
| Decarboxylation | Heat with soda lime (NaOH + CaO) | Benzene |
| Ring nitration | conc. HNO3 + H2SO4 | m-Nitrobenzoic acid (–COOH is a meta director) |

**Test:** neutral FeCl3 gives a **buff (pale brown) precipitate** of ferric benzoate. Salicylic acid gives violet instead (it also has a phenol –OH).

**Uses:** benzoic acid and **sodium benzoate** are preservatives in syrups and foods (work best below pH 4.5); **Whitfield's ointment** (benzoic acid 6% + salicylic acid 3%) treats ringworm; benzoyl peroxide treats acne; salicylic acid makes aspirin and methyl salicylate.

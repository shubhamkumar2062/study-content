# How to use this guide

Study one unit a day: read the notes, practise drawing every diagram once from memory, then answer the question bank without looking. Each unit has Short Answer (5-mark) and Long Essay (10-mark) questions, rated by how often that kind of question appears in pharmacy university papers.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ or very short answers | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with diagrams.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

**Scoring tips**

1. Draw the structure or mechanism first, then explain. Examiners give marks for every correct step and arrow.
2. In a mechanism, always show: electrophile generation → attack on ring → carbocation (sigma complex) → loss of H+.
3. Write reagents and conditions on the arrow (e.g. conc. HNO3 + conc. H2SO4, 50 °C).
4. End every essay with 3–4 uses or examples; pharmacy examiners look for the drug link.
5. Underline key words: *resonance*, *electrophile*, *ortho/para director*, *sigma complex*.

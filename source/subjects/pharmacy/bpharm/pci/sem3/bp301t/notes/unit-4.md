# Unit 4: Polynuclear hydrocarbons

Polynuclear (polycyclic) aromatic hydrocarbons have two or more benzene rings. They come in two kinds: **fused** rings sharing a side (naphthalene, anthracene, phenanthrene) and **isolated** rings joined through a carbon (diphenylmethane, triphenylmethane). Their chief source is **coal tar**.

| Compound | Formula | Ring pattern | π electrons | Resonance energy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Benzene | C6H6 | 1 ring | 6 | 36 kcal/mol |
| Naphthalene | C10H8 | 2 fused | 10 | 61 kcal/mol |
| Anthracene | C14H10 | 3 fused in a line | 14 | 84 kcal/mol |
| Phenanthrene | C14H10 | 3 fused, angled (bent) | 14 | 92 kcal/mol |
| Diphenylmethane | C13H12 | 2 rings on a CH2 | 6 + 6 | — |
| Triphenylmethane | C19H16 | 3 rings on a CH | 6 + 6 + 6 | — |

Fused systems are aromatic but **less aromatic per ring** than benzene (61 / 2 rings = 30.5 per ring vs 36). So their middle rings react more easily, often by **addition**, especially at the 9,10-positions of anthracene and phenanthrene.

## 4.1 Naphthalene

**Structure and numbering**

```text
          8      1                Positions 1, 4, 5, 8 = α (next to the shared carbons)
       7 / \  /  \ 2              Positions 2, 3, 6, 7 = β
        |   ||    |               Shared carbons = 4a, 8a (no H)
       6 \ /  \  / 3              Draw as two hexagons sharing one side,
          5      4                with three Kekulé forms possible.
```

- C10H8, white crystals, smell of moth balls, sublimes. Planar; all 10 carbons sp2; 10 π electrons (4n + 2, n = 2) → aromatic.
- Three resonance structures. Bonds are **not all equal**: C1–C2 = 1.36 Å (more double-bond character), C2–C3 = 1.42 Å.

**Synthesis 1 — Haworth synthesis (most asked)**

```text
Benzene + succinic anhydride
   │  AlCl3  (Friedel–Crafts acylation)
   ▼
β-Benzoylpropionic acid   (C6H5–CO–CH2CH2–COOH)
   │  Zn-Hg / HCl  (Clemmensen: C=O → CH2)
   ▼
γ-Phenylbutyric acid      (C6H5–CH2CH2CH2–COOH)
   │  conc. H2SO4, heat  (ring closure = intramolecular acylation)
   ▼
α-Tetralone
   │  Zn-Hg / HCl  (Clemmensen)
   ▼
Tetralin (1,2,3,4-tetrahydronaphthalene)
   │  Se or Pd, heat  (dehydrogenation, –2 H2)
   ▼
Naphthalene
```

**Synthesis 2** — Industrially obtained from the middle oil fraction of **coal tar** (80–230 °C), crystallised and sublimed.

**Reactions of naphthalene**

*Electrophilic substitution prefers the α (1-) position.* Attack at C1 gives a σ-complex with **two** resonance forms that keep the other benzene ring fully intact; attack at C2 gives only **one** such form. More stable intermediate → faster reaction.

| Reaction | Reagent and condition | Main product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nitration | conc. HNO3 + H2SO4, 50 °C | 1-Nitronaphthalene |
| Bromination | Br2 in CCl4 (no catalyst needed) | 1-Bromonaphthalene |
| Sulfonation (low temp) | conc. H2SO4, 80 °C | Naphthalene-1-sulfonic acid (kinetic product) |
| Sulfonation (high temp) | conc. H2SO4, 160 °C | Naphthalene-2-sulfonic acid (thermodynamic product) |
| Friedel–Crafts acylation | CH3COCl, AlCl3 in CS2 | 1-Acetylnaphthalene |
| Friedel–Crafts acylation | CH3COCl, AlCl3 in nitrobenzene | 2-Acetylnaphthalene (bulky solvent complex) |
| Oxidation (strong) | O2/air, V2O5, 450 °C | Phthalic anhydride |
| Oxidation (mild) | CrO3 in acetic acid | 1,4-Naphthoquinone |
| Reduction | Na + ethanol | 1,4-Dihydronaphthalene |
| Reduction | Na + isoamyl alcohol (boiling) | Tetralin |
| Reduction | H2 / Ni, high temp + pressure | Decalin (decahydronaphthalene) |

**Why sulfonation changes with temperature:** at 80 °C the faster α-attack wins. The 1-sulfonic acid is crowded by the H at C8 (a **peri interaction**). At 160 °C sulfonation becomes reversible, so the less crowded, more stable 2-isomer builds up.

**Uses and medicinal relevance:** moth balls and insect repellent; phthalic anhydride (for phenolphthalein, plasticisers); naphthols and dyes. Drugs built on naphthalene: **naproxen** (NSAID), **propranolol** (β-blocker), **naphazoline** (nasal decongestant), **tolnaftate** (antifungal), **menadione** (vitamin K3 = 2-methyl-1,4-naphthoquinone, a clotting vitamin).

## 4.2 Anthracene

```text
          8     9     1
      7 /  \  /  \  /  \ 2        Three rings fused in a straight line.
       |    ||    ||    |         Positions 9 and 10 (middle ring) = "meso" positions,
      6 \  /  \  /  \  / 3        the most reactive sites.
          5    10     4
```

- C14H10, colourless crystals with violet fluorescence; 14 π electrons; 4 resonance structures.

**Synthesis — Haworth / phthalic anhydride route**

```text
Benzene + phthalic anhydride
   │  AlCl3  (Friedel–Crafts acylation)
   ▼
o-Benzoylbenzoic acid
   │  conc. H2SO4, heat  (ring closure, –H2O)
   ▼
9,10-Anthraquinone
   │  Zn dust, distillation  (reduction)
   ▼
Anthracene
```

Other routes: from the **anthracene oil** fraction of coal tar; **Elbs reaction** (heating an o-methyl diaryl ketone); Friedel–Crafts of benzene with tetrabromoethane.

**Reactions — almost all at C9 and C10**, because attacking there leaves **two intact benzene rings** (most stable intermediate).

| Reaction | Reagent | Product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oxidation | Na2Cr2O7 / H2SO4 (or CrO3) | 9,10-Anthraquinone |
| Reduction | Na + ethanol | 9,10-Dihydroanthracene |
| Bromination | Br2 in CCl4 | 9,10-Dibromo-9,10-dihydroanthracene (addition) → heat → 9-bromoanthracene |
| Nitration | HNO3 in acetic acid | 9-Nitroanthracene |
| Diels–Alder | Maleic anhydride, heat | Adduct across C9–C10 (anthracene acts as a diene) |

**Medicinal relevance:** **anthraquinone laxatives** — senna, cascara, aloe and rhubarb contain anthraquinone glycosides (sennosides, emodin); **dithranol** (anthralin) for psoriasis; **anthracycline** anticancer drugs (doxorubicin, daunorubicin); alizarin dyes.

## 4.3 Phenanthrene

```text
             9 ── 10
           /         \            Three rings fused at an angle (bent like a "kinked" anthracene).
     8 ─ ring A    ring C ─ 1     The 9,10 bond in the middle ring behaves almost like
           \         /            an isolated C=C double bond.
            ── ring B ──
```

- C14H10, isomer of anthracene; 14 π electrons; **five** resonance structures, so it is **more stable** than anthracene (92 vs 84 kcal/mol).

**Synthesis 1 — Haworth synthesis**

```text
Naphthalene + succinic anhydride
   │  AlCl3, in nitrobenzene  (acylation at C2)
   ▼
β-(2-Naphthoyl)propionic acid
   │  Zn-Hg / HCl  (Clemmensen)
   ▼
γ-(2-Naphthyl)butyric acid
   │  conc. H2SO4  (ring closure)
   ▼
Cyclic ketone (tetrahydrophenanthrenone)
   │  Zn-Hg / HCl, then Se / heat  (reduce, then dehydrogenate)
   ▼
Phenanthrene
```

**Synthesis 2 — Pschorr synthesis:** o-amino-α-phenylcinnamic acid is diazotised (NaNO2/HCl, 0–5 °C); copper powder closes the ring → phenanthrene-9-carboxylic acid → heat (decarboxylation) → phenanthrene.

**Reactions — at the 9,10 bond:**

| Reaction | Reagent | Product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oxidation | CrO3 / acetic acid | 9,10-Phenanthrenequinone |
| Further oxidation | H2O2 or KMnO4 | Diphenic acid (biphenyl-2,2'-dicarboxylic acid) |
| Reduction | H2 / Pd (or Na + alcohol) | 9,10-Dihydrophenanthrene |
| Bromination | Br2 in CCl4 | 9,10-Dibromo-9,10-dihydrophenanthrene (addition) → heat → 9-bromophenanthrene |
| Nitration | HNO3 | Mixture, mainly 9-nitrophenanthrene |

**Medicinal relevance (very important):** the **cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene** (steroid) nucleus — phenanthrene, fully hydrogenated, with a five-membered ring attached — is the skeleton of **cholesterol, bile acids, sex hormones (oestrogen, testosterone, progesterone), corticosteroids (cortisone) and cardiac glycosides (digoxin)**. **Morphine and codeine** also contain a partly reduced phenanthrene skeleton.

## 4.4 Diphenylmethane

**Structure:** (C6H5)2CH2 — two benzene rings on one CH2. The CH2 is **benzylic** (next to two rings), so its hydrogens are reactive.

**Synthesis**

```text
1. C6H6 + C6H5CH2Cl (benzyl chloride)  —(AlCl3)→  (C6H5)2CH2 + HCl      [Friedel–Crafts]
2. 2 C6H6 + CH2Cl2  —(AlCl3)→  (C6H5)2CH2 + 2 HCl
3. 2 C6H6 + HCHO  —(conc. H2SO4)→  (C6H5)2CH2 + H2O
4. Benzophenone (C6H5)2C=O  —(Zn-Hg/HCl, Clemmensen)→  (C6H5)2CH2
```

**Reactions**

- **Oxidation** (CrO3 or K2Cr2O7/H2SO4) → **benzophenone**.
- **Side-chain bromination** (Br2, light) → diphenylbromomethane (C6H5)2CHBr.
- **Weakly acidic CH2** — a strong base (NaNH2) removes H+ to give the diphenylmethyl carbanion, stabilised over both rings.
- **Ring substitution** — each CH2-linked ring is activated (like toluene); nitration gives mainly **4,4'-dinitrodiphenylmethane**.

**Uses:** fragrance (orange-geranium odour) in soaps; the **diphenylmethyl (benzhydryl) group** appears in antihistamines **diphenhydramine, cyclizine, cetirizine, meclizine**, and in benztropine (antiparkinsonian).

## 4.5 Triphenylmethane

**Structure:** (C6H5)3CH — three benzene rings on one carbon. The three rings sit like a propeller and stabilise any charge or radical on the central carbon (trityl).

**Synthesis**

```text
1. 3 C6H6 + CHCl3  —(AlCl3)→  (C6H5)3CH + 3 HCl        [Friedel–Crafts]
2. C6H5MgBr + (C6H5)2C=O → (C6H5)3C–OMgBr —H3O+→ (C6H5)3C–OH (triphenylcarbinol)
                       —(HI or Zn / acetic acid, reduce)→  (C6H5)3CH   [Grignard route]
3. 3 C6H6 + CCl4 —(AlCl3)→ (C6H5)3CCl (trityl chloride) —reduce→ (C6H5)3CH
```

**Reactions — all at the central C–H**

| Reaction | Reagent | Product | Why it happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oxidation | CrO3 / acetic acid | Triphenylcarbinol (C6H5)3COH | Weak tertiary benzylic C–H |
| Acidity | NaNH2 or Na | Trityl anion (C6H5)3C− Na+, blood-red | Charge spread over 3 rings |
| Chlorination | Cl2 | Trityl chloride (C6H5)3CCl |  |
| Trityl cation | Triphenylcarbinol + conc. H2SO4 | (C6H5)3C+, yellow solution | Very stable carbocation |
| Gomberg free radical | Trityl chloride + Zn or Ag | (C6H5)3C•, first stable free radical (Gomberg, 1900) | Radical spread over 3 rings |

**Uses:** parent of the **triphenylmethane dyes** — **crystal violet / gentian violet** (antiseptic, antifungal, Gram stain), **malachite green**, **brilliant green** (antiseptic), **basic fuchsin** (rosaniline, for Schiff's reagent), and the indicator **phenolphthalein** (once a laxative). The trityl group is in **clotrimazole** (antifungal) and is used to protect –OH groups in synthesis.

*Safety link:* bigger fused hydrocarbons such as **benzo\[a\]pyrene** (in cigarette smoke, burnt food, vehicle exhaust) are **carcinogenic**; the body oxidises them to epoxides that bind DNA.

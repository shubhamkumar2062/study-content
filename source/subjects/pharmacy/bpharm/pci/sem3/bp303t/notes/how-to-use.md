# How to use this guide

Pharmaceutical Microbiology rewards two things: **exact numbers** (121 °C, 15 psi, 15 min; 160 °C for 2 h; 0.22 µm; 25 kGy) and **clear, labelled diagrams** (bacterial cell, growth curve, autoclave, laminar flow cabinet, cup-plate assay). Read one unit a day, then redraw every diagram from memory before you open the question bank.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ or very short answers | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

Sessional (internal) marks of 25 are added separately in most colleges, and the practical BP307P is examined on its own with a separate paper, experiment, spotting and viva.

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; learn the full answer with its diagram and numbers.
- **Medium** — asked every few years, or as one part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare in the written paper, but useful for Section A and for viva.

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. **Start with the definition.** Begin every answer with one clean sentence defining the term (sterilisation, disinfection, MIC, water activity). It is almost always worth a mark.
2. **Give the conditions.** Never write "autoclaving" alone — write 121 °C, 15 psi, 15 minutes. Marks follow numbers.
3. **Use a table for anything with two sides** — Gram-positive against Gram-negative, moist heat against dry heat, MIC against MBC, primary against continuous cell line. Tables are quicker to write and easier to mark.
4. **Draw before you write.** Label the bacterial cell, the growth curve with its four phases, the autoclave, the laminar flow cabinet and the cup-plate design. A labelled diagram earns marks even when the words run short.
5. **Name the organism.** Examiners look for *Geobacillus stearothermophilus* for autoclave indicators, *Bacillus atrophaeus* for dry heat and ethylene oxide, *Bacillus pumilus* for radiation, *Brevundimonas diminuta* for filters, *Salmonella typhi* for the phenol coefficient, *Lactobacillus leichmannii* for vitamin B12. Write species names in full the first time.
6. **End with uses.** Two or three pharmaceutical applications close an answer well, because this is an applied subject: which products are sterilised that way, which preservative goes into eye drops, which cell line makes a vaccine.
7. **Keep the words separate.** Sterilisation kills everything including spores; disinfection does not; antisepsis is on living tissue; preservation stops growth during use. Mixing them up loses easy marks.
8. **For mechanism questions, write the steps in order** — the stain steps, the stages of virus replication, the phases of the growth curve — and number them.

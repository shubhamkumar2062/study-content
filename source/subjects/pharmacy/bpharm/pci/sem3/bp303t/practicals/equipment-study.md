# Study of laboratory equipment and processes

**Aim:** To study the construction, working principle and uses of the equipment used in a microbiology laboratory: BOD incubator, laminar air flow unit, aseptic hood, autoclave, hot air oven, colony counter and compound microscope.

> Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where details differ. Wear a lab coat, tie back long hair and never work at an autoclave or oven that is still under pressure or hot.

## Principle

Microbiological work needs three things: a way to kill organisms on apparatus and media (autoclave, hot air oven), a clean space to work in without adding new organisms (laminar flow unit, aseptic hood), and a controlled place for wanted organisms to grow (incubator). Counting and observation equipment complete the set.

## Requirements

BOD incubator, laminar air flow unit, autoclave (vertical or horizontal), hot air oven, colony counter, compound microscope with oil immersion objective, refrigerator, hot plate, weighing balance.

## Procedure

1. **BOD (biochemical oxygen demand) incubator.** Note the double wall with insulation, the heating element, the cooling coil, the thermostat and the perforated shelves. It can both heat and cool, so it holds a set temperature between about 5 °C and 60 °C. Set it to 37 °C for bacteria and to 25 °C for fungi, and record how long it takes to steady.
2. **Laminar air flow unit.** Note the pre-filter, blower, HEPA filter, work surface and ultraviolet lamp. Switch on the ultraviolet lamp for 15–20 minutes with the sash closed, switch it off, run the blower for 15 minutes, swab the surface with 70% alcohol, and then work at least 15 cm inside the front edge. Record the air velocity shown on the gauge (about 0.45 m/s).
3. **Aseptic hood (inoculation chamber).** A small enclosed cabinet disinfected with alcohol or formaldehyde and fitted with an ultraviolet lamp; used for transfers where a full laminar flow unit is not available.
4. **Autoclave.** Identify the chamber, lid with gasket and clamps, water level mark, heating element, pressure gauge, safety valve, air-escape (steam) valve and drain. Load, expel the air, hold at 121 °C and 15 psi for 15 minutes, and allow the pressure to return to zero before opening.
5. **Hot air oven.** Identify the double wall, heating coils, thermostat, fan and perforated shelves. Sterilise dry glassware at 160 °C for 2 hours and cool inside the oven before removing.
6. **Colony counter.** Note the illuminated base, magnifying lens, grid plate and counting pen or digital counter. Place a plate on the grid and count colonies square by square.
7. **Compound microscope.** Identify the eyepiece, objectives (10x, 45x, 100x oil immersion), condenser with iris diaphragm, stage, coarse and fine adjustments and light source. Focus a prepared slide under low power first, then add a drop of immersion oil for the 100x objective.

## Observations

## Result

The construction, working and applications of the equipment listed above were studied and recorded.

## Precautions

1. Never open the autoclave until the pressure gauge reads zero.
2. Do not look at a lit ultraviolet lamp; switch it off before working.
3. Let glassware cool inside the hot air oven to prevent cracking.
4. Do not overload any sterilising equipment; air and steam must circulate.
5. Wipe the oil immersion lens with lens paper after use.

## Viva questions

1. **Why must air be removed from an autoclave?** An air–steam mixture is cooler than pure steam at the same pressure, and air pockets stop steam reaching the load.
2. **What does a HEPA filter retain?** At least 99.97% of particles of 0.3 µm diameter.
3. **Why is dry heat used at a higher temperature than moist heat?** Dry air carries less heat and penetrates poorly; it kills by oxidation, which is slower than coagulation of proteins by moist heat.
4. **What is the use of the iris diaphragm?** It controls the amount of light reaching the specimen, which adjusts contrast.
5. **Why is the BOD incubator preferred for fungi?** It can cool as well as heat, so it holds 25 °C even in a warm laboratory.

# Acid-fast (Ziehl-Neelsen) staining

**Aim:** To stain a given smear by the Ziehl-Neelsen method and to detect acid-fast bacilli.

> Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where details differ. In teaching laboratories a safe demonstration organism or a prepared slide is used, not a pathogen; handle the acid solution with care and never heat the stain to boiling.

## Principle

The wall of *Mycobacterium* contains a large amount of waxy **mycolic acid**, so ordinary dyes do not enter. Heating with phenol-containing **carbol fuchsin** softens the wax and drives the dye in. Once inside, the dye is held so firmly that even a strong acid cannot remove it — the cell is **acid-fast** and stays red. All other cells lose the red dye in acid and take the blue counterstain.

## Requirements

Smear (sputum smear for demonstration or a prepared slide), strong carbol fuchsin, 20% sulphuric acid (or 3% acid-alcohol), methylene blue, staining rack, burner or spirit lamp with a swab on a holder, forceps, wash bottle, blotting paper, immersion oil, microscope.

## Procedure

1. Prepare a thin smear, air dry it and heat fix it.
2. Flood the slide with **strong carbol fuchsin**.
3. **Heat the underside of the slide gently** with a flame until steam just rises. Keep it steaming for about **5 minutes**, adding more stain so the smear never dries. Do not boil.
4. Let the slide cool, then wash with water.
5. **Decolourise** with **20% sulphuric acid** for 2–3 minutes, or until the smear is pale pink. Wash with water. Repeat if colour still comes away.
6. Counterstain with **methylene blue** for about 1 minute. Wash with water.
7. Blot dry and examine under the oil immersion objective, scanning several fields.

## Observations

| Structure | Colour | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acid-fast bacilli | Bright red or pink slender rods, often slightly curved, singly or in small groups | Acid-fast organism present |
| Other cells, pus cells, background | Blue | Non-acid-fast |

## Result

The given smear showed ______ acid-fast bacilli, so the organism is (acid-fast / non-acid-fast).

## Precautions

1. Keep the stain steaming but never boiling, and never let the slide dry during heating.
2. Add more carbol fuchsin during heating as it evaporates.
3. Cool the slide before washing so the glass does not crack.
4. Decolourise fully; the background must be free of red, or blue cells may be mistaken for acid-fast bacilli.
5. Use a fresh slide for each smear and dispose of slides in disinfectant.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is the stain heated?** Heat and phenol help the dye pass through the waxy mycolic acid layer of the wall.
2. **What does "acid-fast" mean?** The cell holds the primary stain even when treated with a strong acid, so it does not decolourise.
3. **Which component of the wall is responsible?** Mycolic acid, with other long-chain lipids.
4. **Why is a weaker acid used for *Mycobacterium leprae*?** It is less strongly acid-fast, so 5% sulphuric acid is used to avoid losing the stain.
5. **Name a modification that avoids heating.** The cold method using a stain with a wetting agent, and fluorescent staining with auramine, which is examined under a fluorescence microscope.

# Evaluation of a disinfectant (phenol coefficient)

**Aim:** To determine the phenol coefficient of a given disinfectant by the Rideal-Walker method.

> Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where details differ. Phenol is corrosive; wear gloves and avoid skin contact.

## Principle

The **phenol coefficient** compares the killing power of a disinfectant with that of phenol under fixed conditions. Dilutions of phenol and of the test disinfectant are each mixed with a standard culture of *Salmonella typhi*, and sub-cultures are taken after 2.5, 5, 7.5 and 10 minutes. The coefficient is the ratio of the highest dilution of the disinfectant that kills in 7.5 minutes but not in 5 minutes, to the dilution of phenol doing the same. A value greater than 1 means the agent is more active than phenol at those dilutions.

## Requirements

Test disinfectant, phenol (as the standard), 24-hour broth culture of *Salmonella typhi* (a safe demonstration organism is substituted in teaching laboratories), sterile nutrient broth tubes, sterile pipettes, standard loop, water bath at 20 °C (or the stated temperature), stopwatch, incubator at 37 °C.

## Procedure

1. Prepare a series of dilutions of phenol, for example 1:90, 1:100, 1:110 and 1:120, and a series of dilutions of the test disinfectant over a suitable range.
2. Place 5 ml of each dilution in a test tube and leave the tubes in the water bath until all are at the same temperature.
3. At zero time add **0.5 ml of the standard culture** to the first tube, mix, and start the stopwatch. Add the culture to the remaining tubes at measured intervals so that each can be sub-cultured exactly on time.
4. At **2.5, 5, 7.5 and 10 minutes**, transfer one loopful from each tube into a separate tube of sterile nutrient broth.
5. Incubate all sub-culture tubes at 37 °C for 48 hours.
6. Record growth (+) or no growth (−) in each tube.
7. Find, for phenol and for the test disinfectant, the highest dilution that gives **no growth at 7.5 and 10 minutes but growth at 5 minutes**.

## Observations and calculation

| Dilution | 2.5 min | 5 min | 7.5 min | 10 min |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Phenol 1:90 | | | | |
| Phenol 1:100 | | | | |
| Phenol 1:110 | | | | |
| Disinfectant 1:200 | | | | |
| Disinfectant 1:250 | | | | |
| Disinfectant 1:300 | | | | |

(+ = growth, − = no growth)

Phenol coefficient = highest dilution of disinfectant killing in 7.5 but not 5 minutes ÷ highest dilution of phenol killing in 7.5 but not 5 minutes

*Worked example:* if the disinfectant satisfies the condition at 1:300 and phenol at 1:100, then the phenol coefficient = 300 ÷ 100 = **3**, so the disinfectant is about three times as active as phenol under these conditions.

## Precautions

1. Keep the temperature constant throughout; activity changes sharply with temperature.
2. Use a culture of standard age and density, since a heavier load needs longer contact.
3. Time the sub-cultures exactly with a stopwatch; use a fresh sterile loop each time.
4. Transfer the same volume at each sub-culture.
5. Include a control tube of culture with no disinfectant, which must show growth.

## Viva questions

1. **Which organism is used and why?** *Salmonella typhi*, chosen as a standard test organism of known and reproducible sensitivity.
2. **What is the main limitation of the Rideal-Walker test?** No organic matter is present, only one organism and one temperature are used, and the method suits phenol-like agents rather than all classes of disinfectant.
3. **How does the Chick-Martin test differ?** Dried yeast or faecal suspension is added to imitate organic matter, so the value obtained is lower and closer to practical use.
4. **What does a phenol coefficient of less than 1 mean?** The disinfectant is weaker than phenol at the dilutions tested.
5. **Name two other ways of evaluating a disinfectant.** The capacity (Kelsey-Sykes) test and the in-use test, along with determination of the minimum inhibitory concentration.

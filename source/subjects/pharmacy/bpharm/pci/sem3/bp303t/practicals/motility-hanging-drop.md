# Motility by the hanging drop method

**Aim:** To find out whether a given bacterial culture is motile, using a hanging drop preparation.

> Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where details differ. The preparation contains live organisms; seal and discard it in disinfectant.

## Principle

Motile bacteria swim by means of flagella. In a **hanging drop** a drop of young broth culture hangs from a coverslip over the hollow of a cavity slide, so the organisms are seen alive and unstained in a thin layer of fluid. True motility is a **directional, purposeful movement** of individual cells from one field to another; it must be told apart from **Brownian movement**, which is a trembling in one place caused by the bombardment of water molecules, and from **drift**, in which all the cells move together in one direction with the current.

## Requirements

Young (6–18 hour) broth culture of a motile organism (for example *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Proteus* or *Bacillus subtilis*) and of a non-motile organism (*Staphylococcus aureus* or *Klebsiella*), cavity (depression) slide, coverslip, petroleum jelly or wax, inoculating loop, burner, microscope.

## Procedure

1. Clean the cavity slide and the coverslip and dry them.
2. Place a small ring of petroleum jelly round the edge of the coverslip, using a matchstick.
3. Place **one loopful** of the young broth culture in the centre of the coverslip. Keep the drop small, or it will touch the slide.
4. Invert the cavity slide over the coverslip so that the hollow lies exactly over the drop and the jelly touches the slide.
5. Press gently and turn the slide the right way up quickly. The drop now hangs from the coverslip inside the sealed cavity.
6. Examine first under low power with the **iris diaphragm nearly closed** to get contrast, focusing on the edge of the drop.
7. Switch to the high-power (45x) objective and watch several fields for 2–3 minutes.

## Observations

| Culture | Movement seen | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organism A | Cells darting across the field, changing direction | Motile |
| Organism B | Cells trembling in one place only | Non-motile (Brownian movement) |

## Result

The given organism (is / is not) motile by the hanging drop method.

## Precautions

1. Use a young actively growing broth culture; motility is lost in old cultures.
2. Keep the drop small so it hangs free and does not spread.
3. Seal the cavity with jelly, or the drop dries and currents form.
4. Reduce the light with the iris diaphragm; a bright field makes unstained cells invisible.
5. Do not mistake drift (all cells moving the same way) or Brownian movement (trembling in place) for true motility.

## Viva questions

1. **What is Brownian movement?** A trembling of particles caused by collisions with water molecules; the cells do not change position.
2. **Name two motile and two non-motile bacteria.** Motile: *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Proteus vulgaris*. Non-motile: *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Staphylococcus aureus*.
3. **What other method shows motility?** Stabbing a semi-solid motility medium: motile organisms grow out from the stab line and turn the medium turbid; a motility indicator medium with tetrazolium shows the spread as colour.
4. **Why is the light reduced?** Unstained living cells have little contrast, which is improved by closing the iris diaphragm.
5. **What structure gives motility?** The flagellum, a protein filament of flagellin anchored in the cell envelope.

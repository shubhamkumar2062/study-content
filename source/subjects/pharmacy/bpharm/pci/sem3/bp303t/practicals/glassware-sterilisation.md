# Sterilisation of glassware and preparation of media

**Aim:** To clean and sterilise laboratory glassware and to sterilise prepared culture media, and to confirm that the sterilisation was effective.

> Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where details differ. Use heat-resistant gloves for hot glassware.

## Principle

Glassware is sterilised by **dry heat** in a hot air oven (160 °C for 2 hours), which kills organisms by oxidation. Culture media are aqueous and heat-stable, so they are sterilised by **moist heat** in an autoclave (121 °C, 15 psi, 15 minutes), which kills by coagulating proteins. Sugar-containing and gelatin media that cannot take 121 °C are sterilised by Tyndallisation at 100 °C on three successive days.

## Requirements

Test tubes, Petri dishes, pipettes, conical flasks, cotton, brown paper or aluminium foil, pipette cans, detergent, chromic acid or other cleaning solution, hot air oven, autoclave, prepared nutrient broth and agar, indicator tape or Browne's tubes.

## Procedure

**A. Cleaning and packing glassware**

1. Soak used glassware in disinfectant, then wash with detergent and a brush, rinse with tap water and finally with distilled water.
2. Dry the glassware completely; dry heat will not sterilise wet articles reliably.
3. Plug test tubes and flasks with non-absorbent cotton plugs, wrap Petri dishes in paper or foil, and place pipettes in metal cans with a plug of cotton at the mouthpiece end.
4. Attach a piece of indicator tape to a few packs.

**B. Dry heat sterilisation**

5. Arrange the packs on the oven shelves with space between them for air to move.
6. Heat at **160 °C for 2 hours**, counting the time only after the oven reaches 160 °C.
7. Switch off and let the oven cool before removing the glassware.

**C. Moist heat sterilisation of media**

8. Dispense the prepared broth or molten agar into tubes or flasks, plug them and cover the plugs with paper.
9. Fill the autoclave to the water mark, load the containers loosely and close the lid with the air valve open.
10. Allow steam to escape steadily to drive out the air, close the valve, and let the pressure rise to **15 psi (121 °C)**. Hold for **15 minutes**.
11. Switch off, allow the pressure to fall to zero on its own, then open the lid and remove the containers. Slope the agar tubes for slants, or cool them to about 45 °C before pouring plates.

**D. Checking the sterilisation**

12. Note the colour change of the indicator tape or the Browne's tube.
13. Incubate two uninoculated tubes of the sterilised medium at 37 °C for 24–48 hours. No growth means the medium is sterile.

## Observations

| Article | Method | Conditions | Indicator result | Sterility check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Petri dishes, pipettes, test tubes | Dry heat | 160 °C, 2 h | | |
| Nutrient broth, nutrient agar | Moist heat | 121 °C, 15 psi, 15 min | | |

## Result

The glassware was sterilised by dry heat at 160 °C for 2 hours and the media by autoclaving at 121 °C and 15 psi for 15 minutes; the uninoculated control tubes showed no growth, so the sterilisation was effective.

## Precautions

1. Glassware must be completely dry before dry heat sterilisation.
2. Do not pack the oven or the autoclave tightly.
3. Close caps only loosely in the autoclave, or the containers may burst.
4. Never open the autoclave while it is under pressure.
5. Keep sterilised articles wrapped until the moment of use.

## Viva questions

1. **Why are media autoclaved and not baked?** They are aqueous; steam heats them quickly and evenly at a temperature low enough not to spoil the nutrients, while dry heat would evaporate and char them.
2. **Why is cotton used as a plug?** It lets air and steam pass but filters out organisms as the tube cools and draws in air.
3. **Which media cannot be autoclaved at 121 °C?** Those with sugars, gelatin, serum or vitamins; they are Tyndallised, filtered or autoclaved at a lower temperature.
4. **What does indicator tape prove?** Only that the pack was exposed to the process; it does not prove the organisms were killed.
5. **At what temperature is molten agar poured into plates?** About 45 °C, cool enough not to kill the inoculum and warm enough to stay liquid.

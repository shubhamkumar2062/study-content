# Isolation of a pure culture by streak plate

**Aim:** To isolate a pure culture from a mixed culture by the quadrant streak plate method and to describe the colonies obtained.

> Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where details differ.

## Principle

A **pure culture** contains only one kind of organism. In the **streak plate** method a loopful of a mixed culture is dragged over the agar in a series of linked strokes, each starting from the previous one. Cells are left behind at every stroke, so by the last sector single cells are separated far enough apart that each grows into a separate **colony** — a visible mass of cells descended from one cell. Picking one well-separated colony and transferring it to fresh medium gives a pure culture.

## Requirements

Mixed broth culture, sterile nutrient agar plates, inoculating loop, burner or laminar air flow unit, incubator, marker pen, colony counter or hand lens, discard jar.

## Procedure

1. Label the base of the plate with the organism, date and your name, and mark it into four sectors.
2. Flame the loop, cool it, and take one loopful of the mixed culture.
3. **Sector 1:** lift the lid slightly and streak the loopful back and forth close together over about a quarter of the plate.
4. **Flame the loop and cool it.** Turn the plate about 90°, and draw the loop three or four times through the end of sector 1, then streak out into sector 2 without re-entering sector 1 afterwards.
5. **Flame and cool the loop again.** Repeat from the end of sector 2 into sector 3, and once more into sector 4, so the inoculum becomes thinner at each step.
6. Flame the loop before putting it down.
7. Invert the plate and incubate at 37 °C for 24–48 hours.
8. Examine the plate: the first sector shows confluent growth, the last sector well-separated colonies.
9. Pick a single, well-separated colony with a sterile loop and sub-culture it onto a slant. This is the pure culture; confirm it by a Gram-stained smear showing one type of cell.

```text
        /  2  \         Each sector starts from the previous one,
       | ----- |        with the loop flamed in between, so the
       | 1   3 |        number of cells carried over falls sharply.
        \  4  /         Sector 4 should give single colonies.
```

## Precautions

1. Cool the loop after flaming, or the heat will kill the inoculum and spatter the culture.
2. Do not dig into or tear the agar.
3. Open the lid only as much as needed and for as short a time as possible.
4. Do not return to an earlier sector after flaming, or the dilution effect is lost.
5. Invert the plates for incubation.

## Viva questions

1. **Define a colony.** A visible group of cells on solid medium that has grown from a single cell or a single clump, so it is one colony-forming unit.
2. **Name other methods of obtaining a pure culture.** Pour plate, spread plate, serial dilution in broth, use of selective and enrichment media, and single-cell picking with a micromanipulator.
3. **Why is the loop flamed between sectors?** To reduce the number of cells carried into the next sector, which is what separates the colonies.
4. **In the pour plate method, why is the agar cooled to about 45 °C?** Hotter agar would kill the organisms; cooler agar would set before mixing.
5. **How would you confirm a culture is pure?** All colonies look alike, a Gram-stained smear shows one type of cell, and biochemical tests give one consistent pattern.

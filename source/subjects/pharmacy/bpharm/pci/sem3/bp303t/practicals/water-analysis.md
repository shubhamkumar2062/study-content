# Bacteriological analysis of water

**Aim:** To test a given water sample for bacteriological quality by the multiple tube (most probable number) method and the membrane filtration method, and to perform the methylene blue reduction test.

> Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where details differ. Collect the sample in a sterile bottle and test it as soon as possible.

## Principle

Water is tested not for every organism but for **indicator organisms** of faecal pollution, chiefly **coliforms** and *Escherichia coli*, which are easy to detect and outlive most pathogens. In the **multiple tube method**, measured volumes of the sample are added to tubes of lactose broth with an inverted Durham tube; acid and gas within 48 hours is a presumptive positive, and the pattern of positive tubes is read against a **most probable number (MPN)** table to give the likely number of coliforms per 100 ml. In the **membrane filtration method**, a known volume is filtered through a **0.45 µm** membrane that retains the bacteria; the membrane is placed on a selective medium and the typical colonies are counted directly. The **methylene blue reduction test** judges the general bacterial load by how fast the dye is decolourised by bacterial metabolism.

## Requirements

Water sample in a sterile bottle, MacConkey or lactose broth (single and double strength) with Durham tubes, brilliant green bile broth, membrane filtration assembly with 0.45 µm membranes, selective agar for coliforms, sterile pipettes, methylene blue solution, incubators at 37 °C and 44 °C, colony counter.

## Procedure

**A. Multiple tube (MPN) method — presumptive coliform count**

1. Set up tubes: 1 tube with 50 ml of double-strength broth, 5 tubes with 10 ml of double-strength broth, and 5 tubes with 5 ml of single-strength broth, each with an inverted Durham tube and no air bubble.
2. Add 50 ml of sample to the large tube, 10 ml to each of the five 10 ml tubes, and 1 ml to each of the five remaining tubes.
3. Incubate at 37 °C for 24 hours; if there is no acid and gas, incubate up to 48 hours.
4. Record every tube showing **acid (colour change) and gas (a bubble in the Durham tube)** as presumptive positive.
5. Read the combination of positives against the MPN table to obtain the **presumptive coliform count per 100 ml**.

**B. Confirmed and completed tests**

6. Sub-culture each positive tube into brilliant green bile broth and incubate at 37 °C; gas confirms coliforms.
7. Incubate a parallel set at **44 °C**; gas and a positive indole test at this temperature indicate **faecal coliforms (*E. coli*)**.

**C. Membrane filtration method**

8. Filter 100 ml of the sample through a sterile 0.45 µm membrane under suction.
9. Place the membrane, grid side up, on a pad soaked in selective medium or on selective agar.
10. Incubate at 37 °C for 18–24 hours and count the typical coliform colonies. Report as colony-forming units per 100 ml.

**D. Methylene blue reduction test**

11. To 10 ml of sample add 1 ml of methylene blue solution, mix, cover and incubate at 37 °C.
12. Note the time taken for the blue colour to disappear. A short time means a heavy bacterial load.

## Precautions

1. Collect the sample aseptically in a sterile bottle; if the supply is chlorinated, add sodium thiosulphate to the bottle to neutralise residual chlorine.
2. Test within a few hours, or store at about 4 °C.
3. Fill Durham tubes completely, with no air bubble before incubation.
4. Flame the mouth of bottles and tubes at every transfer.
5. Include an uninoculated tube of each medium as a sterility control.

## Viva questions

1. **Why are coliforms used as indicator organisms?** They are always present in faeces, are easy to detect, survive longer in water than most pathogens, and do not usually multiply in clean water.
2. **What is the MPN?** A statistical estimate of the number of organisms per 100 ml, obtained from the pattern of positive and negative tubes, not a direct count.
3. **Why is a parallel set incubated at 44 °C?** Growth with gas and indole production at that temperature distinguishes faecal *E. coli* from other coliforms of soil or plant origin.
4. **What pore size is used for the membrane and why?** 0.45 µm, which retains bacteria while allowing a reasonable filtration rate.
5. **What does rapid decolourisation of methylene blue mean?** A heavy bacterial load, because active metabolism quickly lowers the oxidation-reduction potential and reduces the dye to its colourless form.

# Preparation of culture media for bacteria

**Aim:** To prepare nutrient broth, nutrient agar slants and nutrient agar plates, sterilise them and check them for sterility.

> Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where details differ.

## Principle

A culture medium supplies water, a nitrogen and carbon source, minerals and growth factors at a suitable pH. **Nutrient broth** is a simple liquid basal medium; adding 1.5–2% **agar**, which melts near 100 °C and sets near 40 °C and is not digested by most bacteria, makes a solid medium that can be poured as plates or set as slants.

## Requirements

Peptone, beef extract, sodium chloride, agar, distilled water, 0.1 N sodium hydroxide and hydrochloric acid, pH paper or pH meter, conical flasks, test tubes, Petri dishes, balance, hot plate, autoclave, laminar air flow unit.

## Formula (for 1000 ml)

| Ingredient | Nutrient broth | Nutrient agar | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Peptone | 5 g | 5 g | Nitrogen, amino acids |
| Beef extract | 3 g | 3 g | Vitamins, minerals, amino acids |
| Sodium chloride | 5 g | 5 g | Osmotic balance |
| Agar | — | 15–20 g | Solidifying agent |
| Distilled water | to 1000 ml | to 1000 ml | Solvent |
| Final pH | 7.2–7.4 | 7.2–7.4 | Suits most bacteria |

## Procedure

1. Weigh the ingredients accurately and dissolve peptone, beef extract and sodium chloride in about 800 ml of distilled water with gentle warming and stirring.
2. Adjust the pH to **7.2–7.4** with 0.1 N sodium hydroxide or hydrochloric acid; check with pH paper or a meter.
3. Make up to 1000 ml with distilled water.
4. For agar media, add the agar and heat with constant stirring until it dissolves completely and the liquid is clear. Do not let it boil over.
5. Dispense: about 5–10 ml of broth per tube; about 5 ml of molten agar per tube for slants; the rest in flasks for pouring plates.
6. Plug the tubes and flasks with cotton and cover with paper.
7. Autoclave at **121 °C and 15 psi for 15 minutes**.
8. **Slants:** lay the hot agar tubes on the bench at a slope so the agar sets with a long surface, leaving a short butt.
9. **Plates:** cool the flask to about 45 °C, and in the laminar air flow unit pour 15–20 ml into each sterile Petri dish; replace the lid at once and let it set on a level surface.
10. Incubate two tubes of each medium and two poured plates at 37 °C for 24 hours as a sterility check.

## Observations

| Medium | Appearance before sterilisation | Appearance after sterilisation | Sterility check (growth / no growth) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nutrient broth | Clear light amber liquid | | |
| Nutrient agar slant | Molten amber liquid | Firm sloped surface | |

## Result

Nutrient broth, nutrient agar slants and nutrient agar plates were prepared at pH 7.2–7.4, sterilised at 121 °C and 15 psi for 15 minutes, and found sterile after incubation at 37 °C for 24 hours.

## Precautions

1. Dissolve the agar completely before autoclaving, or the medium will set unevenly.
2. Adjust the pH before adding the agar and before sterilising.
3. Cool the agar to about 45 °C before pouring; hotter agar produces condensation, cooler agar sets in the flask.
4. Pour plates inside the laminar air flow unit and open the lid only slightly.
5. Let the plates set undisturbed on a level surface and store them inverted to stop condensation dropping onto the agar.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is agar preferred to gelatin?** It melts near 100 °C and sets near 40 °C, stays solid at 37 °C and is not digested by most bacteria, while gelatin melts at body temperature and is liquefied by many organisms.
2. **What is the purpose of peptone?** It supplies nitrogen as amino acids and peptides.
3. **Why is the pH set at 7.2–7.4?** Most medically important bacteria grow best near neutral pH.
4. **Why are plates stored inverted?** So that condensed water on the lid does not fall onto the agar surface and spread colonies.
5. **What would you add to make the medium selective for fungi?** Lower the pH to about 5.6 and use a high-glucose peptone medium, or add an antibacterial such as chloramphenicol.

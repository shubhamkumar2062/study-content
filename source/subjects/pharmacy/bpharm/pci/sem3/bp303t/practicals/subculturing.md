# Sub-culturing of bacteria and fungi: stabs and slants

**Aim:** To transfer a given bacterial culture aseptically to fresh nutrient broth, a nutrient agar slant, a nutrient agar stab and a plate, to subculture a fungus on Sabouraud dextrose agar, and to observe the growth.

> Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where details differ. Treat every culture as infectious; disinfect the bench before and after work.

## Principle

Bacteria die when nutrients run out and waste collects, so stock cultures are kept alive by **sub-culturing**: transferring a small inoculum to fresh medium at intervals with a sterile loop or needle, using **aseptic technique** so that no other organism gets in and nothing escapes into the room.

## Requirements

Stock culture of a named organism (for example *Escherichia coli* or *Bacillus subtilis*), sterile nutrient broth tubes, nutrient agar slants, nutrient agar plates, nichrome inoculating loop and straight wire, Bunsen burner or spirit lamp, laminar air flow unit, incubator, marker pen, discard jar with disinfectant.

## Procedure

1. Disinfect the work surface with 70% alcohol and light the burner (or work in the laminar air flow unit).
2. Label each fresh tube or plate with the organism, your name, the date and the medium.
3. **Flame the loop** until it is red hot along its whole length, then let it cool for a few seconds near the flame without touching anything.
4. Hold the stock culture tube in the left hand. Remove the plug with the little finger of the right hand (the hand holding the loop), **flame the mouth** of the tube, and take a loopful of growth.
5. Flame the mouth again, replace the plug, and pick up the fresh tube.
6. **Broth:** remove the plug, flame the mouth, dip the loop in the broth and shake gently to release the inoculum, flame the mouth and replace the plug.
7. **Slant:** remove the plug, flame the mouth, and draw a single straight or zigzag line up the slope of the agar from the butt to the top without digging into the agar. Flame and replug.
8. **Plate:** lift the lid only enough to admit the loop and streak the surface lightly in a zigzag over one quadrant and then spread out.
9. **Flame the loop** again before putting it down, so nothing live is left on it.
10. **Stab culture.** Charge a sterile **straight wire** with growth and push it vertically into the centre of a tube of nutrient agar butt, almost to the bottom, withdrawing it along the same track. Stabs are used for motility, for anaerobic growth and for stock cultures.
11. **Fungal subculture.** Transfer a small piece of the mould or a loopful of yeast onto a **Sabouraud dextrose agar** slant with a straight wire, and incubate at 25 °C for 3–7 days; fungi grow slowly and prefer the lower temperature and the acidic medium.
12. Incubate at 37 °C for 24 hours: tubes upright, plates inverted.
13. Keep one uninoculated tube and one uninoculated plate as sterility controls.

## Observations

| Medium | Expected appearance of growth | Observed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nutrient broth | Uniform turbidity, or a surface pellicle or a sediment depending on the organism | |
| Nutrient agar slant | A continuous line of growth along the streak, with its own colour and texture | |
| Nutrient agar plate | Discrete or confluent colonies, described by size, shape, margin, elevation, colour and opacity | |
| Nutrient agar stab | Growth along the stab line, spreading outwards if the organism is motile | |
| Sabouraud slant (fungus) | Cottony, powdery or creamy growth after 3–7 days at 25 °C | |
| Uninoculated control | No growth | |

## Result

The given culture was sub-cultured aseptically into broth, onto a slant and onto a plate; growth appeared on all three after incubation at 37 °C for 24 hours, while the uninoculated controls remained clear, showing that the technique was aseptic.

## Precautions

1. Cool the flamed loop before touching the culture, or the heat will kill the cells and spatter them.
2. Never put the plug down on the bench; hold it in the hand.
3. Flame the mouth of every tube when opening and closing it.
4. Do not dig into the agar; the loop should glide over the surface.
5. Flame the loop before and after every transfer, and discard everything into disinfectant.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is the mouth of the tube flamed?** The hot air rising from the mouth stops airborne organisms from settling inside.
2. **Why is the loop made of nichrome or platinum?** These metals heat and cool quickly and do not corrode.
3. **What is the difference between a loop and a straight wire?** A loop carries a measured drop for surface streaking; a straight wire is used for stab cultures and for picking single colonies.
4. **Why are plates incubated upside down?** So that condensation on the lid does not drip onto the growth.
5. **Why are fungi subcultured at 25 °C on Sabouraud agar?** Most fungi grow best at 25–30 °C, and the high sugar content with an acidic pH of about 5.6 favours them while holding back bacteria.
6. **How often should a stock culture be sub-cultured?** Usually every few weeks when stored at 4 °C; long-term storage uses paraffin overlay, freezing or freeze-drying.

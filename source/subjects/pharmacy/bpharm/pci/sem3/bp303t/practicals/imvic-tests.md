# Biochemical identification by IMViC tests

**Aim:** To perform the indole, methyl red, Voges-Proskauer and citrate tests on given cultures and to distinguish *Escherichia coli* from *Enterobacter aerogenes*.

> Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where details differ. Kovac's and Barritt's reagents are corrosive; add them carefully.

## Principle

Different enteric bacteria break down the same nutrients by different routes, and the products can be detected with simple reagents.

- **Indole:** the enzyme tryptophanase splits tryptophan into indole, pyruvate and ammonia. Indole reacts with the aldehyde in Kovac's reagent to give a red compound that collects in the alcohol layer on top.
- **Methyl red:** organisms using the mixed-acid pathway produce so much acid that the pH falls to about 4.4 or lower, where methyl red is red.
- **Voges-Proskauer:** organisms using the butanediol pathway form acetoin, which is oxidised in air and alkali to diacetyl; this reacts with a component of peptone in the presence of alpha-naphthol to give a pink colour.
- **Citrate:** an organism able to use citrate as its only carbon source grows on the medium and releases alkaline products, turning the bromothymol blue indicator from green to blue.

## Requirements

24-hour cultures of *E. coli* and *Enterobacter aerogenes*, tryptone (peptone) water, glucose phosphate (MR-VP) broth, Simmons citrate agar slants, Kovac's reagent, methyl red solution, Barritt's reagents (5% alpha-naphthol in alcohol and 40% potassium hydroxide), sterile loop and straight wire, burner, incubator at 37 °C.

## Procedure

**Indole test**

1. Inoculate tryptone water with the test organism and incubate at 37 °C for 24–48 hours.
2. Add about 0.5 ml of Kovac's reagent down the side of the tube and shake gently. Read within a minute.

**Methyl red test**

3. Inoculate MR-VP broth and incubate at 37 °C for 48 hours (a shorter time can give a false negative).
4. Add 5 drops of methyl red solution to about 5 ml of the culture and read at once.

**Voges-Proskauer test**

5. Inoculate a second tube of MR-VP broth and incubate for 48 hours.
6. Add 0.6 ml of alpha-naphthol solution and then 0.2 ml of 40% potassium hydroxide, shake well and leave the tube slanted for 15–30 minutes, shaking occasionally to let air in.

**Citrate test**

7. Streak the surface of a Simmons citrate slant with a straight wire carrying a light inoculum (do not carry over nutrients from the previous medium).
8. Incubate at 37 °C for 24–48 hours with the cap loose, since the test needs air.

**Controls:** include an uninoculated tube of each medium.

## Observations

| Test | Positive result | Negative result | *E. coli* | *E. aerogenes* |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Indole | Red ring in the alcohol layer | Yellow layer | | |
| Methyl red | Red | Yellow | | |
| Voges-Proskauer | Pink to red in 15–30 min | No colour change | | |
| Citrate | Blue, with growth on the slant | Green, no growth | | |

## Precautions

1. Use a pure, fresh culture and label every tube.
2. Incubate the MR and VP tubes for a full 48 hours before adding reagents.
3. Add alpha-naphthol before potassium hydroxide in the Voges-Proskauer test, and shake to admit air.
4. Use a very light inoculum for citrate and keep the cap loose; carried-over nutrients give a false positive.
5. Read the indole test soon after adding Kovac's reagent, as the colour fades.

## Viva questions

1. **What does IMViC stand for?** Indole, Methyl red, Voges-Proskauer, Citrate; the small "i" is only for pronunciation.
2. **Why are two separate tubes used for MR and VP?** The tests are read at different times and each needs its own reagents; they are both performed on the same medium but cannot be done on one tube.
3. **What is the active ingredient of Kovac's reagent?** p-Dimethylaminobenzaldehyde in amyl alcohol with hydrochloric acid.
4. **Why does the citrate test need an alkaline shift?** Using citrate as the sole carbon source releases ammonia and carbonates, raising the pH so bromothymol blue turns blue.
5. **What is the importance of IMViC in pharmacy?** It helps identify coliforms in water and in microbial limit testing, where *E. coli* is an objectionable organism in oral products.

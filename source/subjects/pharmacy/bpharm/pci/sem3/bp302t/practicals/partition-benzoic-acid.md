# Partition coefficient of benzoic acid between benzene and water

**Aim:** To determine the partition coefficient of benzoic acid between benzene and water and to show that benzoic acid exists as a dimer in benzene.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Benzene is toxic and flammable: work in a fume hood, away from flames, with gloves and goggles, and collect the organic layer for proper disposal.

## Principle

By the distribution law, a solute shared between two immiscible liquids at constant temperature has a constant ratio of concentrations, provided it has the same molecular form in both. Benzoic acid forms **hydrogen-bonded dimers** in benzene, while in water it exists mainly as single molecules. Therefore the simple ratio Cbenzene/Cwater is not constant, but

**K = Cwater / √Cbenzene** is constant.

Both layers are titrated with standard NaOH to find the concentrations.

## Requirements

Benzoic acid, benzene, distilled water, 0.02 N and 0.1 N NaOH (standardised), phenolphthalein, separating funnels or stoppered bottles (250 ml), pipettes, burettes, conical flasks.

## Procedure

1. Set up three stoppered bottles. In each put 50 ml water and 50 ml benzene.
2. Add different amounts of benzoic acid: about 1.0 g, 1.5 g and 2.0 g.
3. Stopper and shake each bottle for about 30 minutes; let the layers separate completely at room temperature.
4. With a pipette, take 10 ml of the upper benzene layer into a flask containing about 20 ml of neutral water (or ethanol); titrate with 0.1 N NaOH using phenolphthalein, shaking well.
5. Take 10 ml of the lower aqueous layer, passing the pipette through the benzene layer while blowing gently to prevent contamination; titrate with 0.02 N NaOH.
6. Repeat each titration for concordant readings.

**Worked example.** Bottle 1: Cb = 0.25 M, Cw = 0.0175 M. Cb/Cw = 14.3; Cw/√Cb = 0.0175/0.50 = 0.035. Bottle 2: Cb = 0.49 M, Cw = 0.0245 M. Cb/Cw = 20.0; Cw/√Cb = 0.0245/0.70 = 0.035. The second ratio is constant, confirming dimerisation in benzene.

A plot of log Cw against log Cb gives a slope of about 0.5 (= 1/n, where n = 2).

## Result

The ratio Cw/√Cb is constant at ___, showing that benzoic acid is associated as a dimer in benzene. The simple ratio Cb/Cw is not constant.

## Precautions

1. Keep the temperature constant; shake for the same time.
2. Do not contaminate the aqueous sample with benzene.
3. Use separate pipettes for the two layers.
4. Allow complete separation of layers.

## Viva questions

1. **State the distribution law.** At constant temperature, the ratio of concentrations of a solute in two immiscible liquids is constant if the solute has the same molecular form in both.
2. **Why is Cw/√Cb constant here?** Benzoic acid dimerises in benzene through hydrogen bonds.
3. **Why use a weaker NaOH for the water layer?** The aqueous concentration is much lower.
4. **Give two applications of partition coefficient.** Solvent extraction and predicting drug absorption.
5. **What would happen if the acid ionised in water?** The un-ionised fraction alone would partition; a correction (1 − α) is needed.
6. **What is log P?** The log of the octanol–water partition coefficient, used as a measure of lipophilicity.

# Surface tension by the drop count and drop weight methods

**Aim:** To determine the surface tension of the given liquids (for example ethanol and a soap solution) by the drop count and drop weight methods using a stalagmometer.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles and a lab coat.

## Principle

A drop grows at the tip of a capillary until its weight just exceeds the surface tension holding it along the circumference of the tip (Tate's law). For the same stalagmometer, the weight of one drop is proportional to the surface tension of the liquid. Water is used as the reference.

**Drop weight method:** γ1 / γw = m1 / mw (m = mass of one drop)

**Drop count method:** for the same volume, the number of drops is inversely proportional to the drop size:

γ1 / γw = (nw × ρ1) / (n1 × ρw)

ρ = density, measured with a specific gravity bottle.

## Requirements

Stalagmometer (a pipette with a capillary tip and marks above and below the bulb), clean dry beakers, weighing bottle, analytical balance, specific gravity bottle, distilled water, test liquids, rubber bulb, thermometer.

## Procedure

**Cleaning:** clean the stalagmometer with chromic acid or detergent, rinse with plenty of distilled water, and dry; grease on the tip changes drop size.

**Drop count**

1. Fill the stalagmometer with water above the upper mark using the bulb; clamp it vertically.
2. Let it drain and start counting drops when the meniscus passes the upper mark; stop when it passes the lower mark.
3. Repeat three times and take the mean (nw).
4. Dry the stalagmometer (rinse with the test liquid) and repeat with each test liquid (n1).
5. Find the density of each liquid with a specific gravity bottle.

**Drop weight**

6. Collect 20 drops of water in a weighed weighing bottle; weigh and find the mass of one drop.
7. Repeat with each test liquid.

**Worked example (drop count).** Water 20 drops; liquid 35 drops; density 0.79 g/ml. γ = 72 × (20 × 0.79)/(35 × 1.0) = **32.5 dyne/cm**.

**Worked example (drop weight).** Mass of 20 drops: water 1.000 g, liquid 0.450 g. γ = 72 × (0.450/1.000) = **32.4 dyne/cm**.

## Precautions

1. The stalagmometer must be clean, grease-free and vertical.
2. Drops should form slowly (about 15–20 per minute); fast flow gives errors.
3. Avoid vibration and air bubbles.
4. Use the same temperature for all liquids.

## Viva questions

1. **Define surface tension.** The force per unit length acting parallel to the surface at right angles to a line on it; unit dyne/cm or mN/m.
2. **Why is water used as reference?** Its surface tension is accurately known at each temperature.
3. **Why does soap solution give more drops?** Lower surface tension makes smaller drops.
4. **Effect of temperature on surface tension?** It decreases as temperature rises.
5. **Name other methods.** Capillary rise, du Noüy ring, Wilhelmy plate.
6. **Can this apparatus measure interfacial tension?** Yes, by forming drops of one liquid under another.

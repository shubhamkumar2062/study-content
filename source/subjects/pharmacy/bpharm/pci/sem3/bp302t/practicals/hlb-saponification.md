# HLB number of a surfactant by the saponification method

**Aim:** To determine the HLB number of a fatty acid ester surfactant (for example glyceryl monostearate or a sorbitan ester) by the saponification method.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles and a lab coat; alcoholic KOH is corrosive and flammable.

## Principle

For esters of polyhydric alcohols with fatty acids, Griffin's equation gives:

**HLB = 20 (1 − S / A)**

S = **saponification value** of the ester (mg KOH needed to saponify 1 g of the ester); A = **acid value** of the fatty acid obtained from it (mg KOH to neutralise 1 g of the acid).

S is found by refluxing the ester with excess alcoholic KOH and back-titrating the unused KOH with HCl. A is found by direct titration of the fatty acid (or taken from the literature value of the pure acid, e.g. stearic acid about 198).

## Requirements

Surfactant (ester), fatty acid (e.g. stearic acid), 0.5 N alcoholic KOH, 0.5 N HCl, 0.1 N KOH (or NaOH), neutralised ethanol, phenolphthalein, round-bottom flasks with reflux condensers, water bath, burettes, pipettes, balance.

## Procedure

**Saponification value of the ester**

1. Weigh about 2 g of the ester (W) into a round-bottom flask.
2. Add exactly 25 ml of 0.5 N alcoholic KOH. Fit a reflux condenser and heat on a water bath for about 1 hour, swirling occasionally.
3. Set up a **blank** with 25 ml of alcoholic KOH only, refluxed in the same way.
4. Cool, add phenolphthalein and titrate both with 0.5 N HCl until the pink colour disappears.
   Sample titre = a ml; blank titre = b ml.

**Acid value of the fatty acid**

5. Weigh about 1 g of fatty acid (w), dissolve in 25 ml of hot neutralised ethanol, add phenolphthalein and titrate with 0.1 N KOH to a faint pink (v ml).

## Observations and calculation

S = 28.05 × (b − a) / W (28.05 = mg of KOH in 1 ml of 0.5 N KOH)

A = 5.61 × v / w (5.61 = mg of KOH in 1 ml of 0.1 N KOH)

HLB = 20 (1 − S/A)

**Worked example.** W = 2.0 g; b = 25.0 ml; a = 13.5 ml. S = 28.05 × 11.5 / 2.0 = 161. Stearic acid A = 198. HLB = 20 (1 − 161/198) = **3.7**, typical of a w/o emulsifier.

## Result

1. Saponification value of the ester = ___ mg KOH/g.
2. Acid value of the fatty acid = ___ mg KOH/g.
3. HLB of the surfactant = ___, so it is suitable as ______ (see HLB table).

## Precautions

1. Reflux long enough for complete saponification.
2. Run the blank at the same time and under the same conditions.
3. Avoid loss of alcohol during reflux; use a condenser with water flowing.
4. Titrate while warm if soap gels form, and shake well.

## Viva questions

1. **What does HLB stand for?** Hydrophile–lipophile balance.
2. **What HLB range suits o/w emulsifiers?** About 8–18; w/o emulsifiers 3–6.
3. **Why is a blank needed?** To know the total KOH added, so the KOH used by the sample can be found by difference.
4. **Define saponification value.** Mg of KOH needed to saponify 1 g of fat or ester.
5. **How is HLB calculated when only ethylene oxide is hydrophilic?** HLB = E/5 (E = weight % oxyethylene).
6. **How is HLB of a blend found?** Weighted average of the HLB of the components.

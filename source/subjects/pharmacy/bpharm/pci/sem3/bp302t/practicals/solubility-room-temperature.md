# Solubility of a drug at room temperature

**Aim:** To determine the solubility of the given drug (for example salicylic acid or benzoic acid) in water at room temperature.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

Solubility is the concentration of solute in a **saturated solution** at a stated temperature, where dissolved solute is in equilibrium with undissolved solid. An excess of drug is shaken with water until saturation is reached, the undissolved solid is filtered off, and the drug in a known volume of the clear filtrate is assayed. For an acidic drug the assay is a simple titration with standard sodium hydroxide:

C6H4(OH)COOH + NaOH → C6H4(OH)COONa + H2O

## Requirements

Salicylic acid (or benzoic acid), distilled water, 0.05 N NaOH (standardised), phenolphthalein, stoppered conical flasks, thermometer, mechanical shaker (or hand shaking), filter paper or a dry funnel with cotton, pipettes, burette.

## Procedure

1. Note the room temperature.
2. Put an excess of the drug (about 1 g salicylic acid) in a stoppered flask with 100 ml of distilled water. Some solid must remain undissolved throughout.
3. Shake vigorously for 30–60 minutes (or on a shaker) at room temperature to reach saturation. Allow to stand for a few minutes.
4. Filter through a dry filter paper into a dry flask, rejecting the first few ml of filtrate (filter paper may adsorb some drug).
5. Pipette 10 ml of the clear filtrate into a conical flask, add 2 drops of phenolphthalein and titrate with 0.05 N NaOH to a faint permanent pink.
6. Repeat until two readings agree (concordant).
7. If time allows, shake for a further 30 minutes and assay again; equal results confirm that equilibrium was reached.

## Observations and calculation

| Titration | Filtrate (ml) | Burette initial | Burette final | NaOH used (ml) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 10 |  |  |  |
| 2 | 10 |  |  |  |
| Concordant |  |  |  | V |

Concentration of drug (mol/L): N1V1 = N2V2 → Cdrug = (0.05 × V) / 10

Solubility (g/100 ml) = Cdrug × M × 100 / 1000, where M = molecular weight (salicylic acid 138, benzoic acid 122).

**Worked example.** 10 ml filtrate needs 3.2 ml of 0.05 N NaOH. C = 0.05 × 3.2 / 10 = 0.016 M. Solubility = 0.016 × 138 / 10 = **0.22 g/100 ml** of salicylic acid at room temperature.

## Precautions

1. Keep excess solid present and the temperature constant.
2. Use dry filtering apparatus so the filtrate is not diluted.
3. Reject the first portion of filtrate.
4. Do not let undissolved particles pass into the pipette.

## Viva questions

1. **Define solubility.** Concentration of solute in a saturated solution at a given temperature and pressure.
2. **Why is excess solid kept?** To make sure the solution is saturated and at equilibrium.
3. **How does temperature affect solubility of salicylic acid?** It increases on warming because dissolving is endothermic.
4. **What is "sparingly soluble" in the pharmacopoeia?** 30–100 parts of solvent needed for 1 part of solute.
5. **How would you raise the water solubility of salicylic acid?** Raise the pH (salt formation), add a cosolvent such as ethanol, or use complexation or surfactants.
6. **What is intrinsic solubility?** Solubility of the un-ionised form of a weak acid or base.

# Stability constant of the PABA–caffeine complex by the solubility method

**Aim:** To determine the stability constant and the donor–acceptor ratio of the complex formed between p-aminobenzoic acid (PABA) and caffeine by the solubility method.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles and a lab coat.

## Principle

When a poorly soluble substance (substrate, PABA) is shaken in excess with solutions containing increasing amounts of a ligand (caffeine) that forms a soluble complex, the total amount of substrate in solution increases. A plot of total PABA dissolved against caffeine concentration is a **phase solubility diagram** (Higuchi–Connors).

If the plot is a straight line (type AL), a 1:1 complex is formed, and:

**K(1:1) = slope / [S0 (1 − slope)]**

S0 = solubility of PABA with no caffeine (intercept). The **donor–acceptor ratio** is the stoichiometry of the complex, given by the diagram type (linear AL → 1:1) or, for a BS diagram, from the plateau region.

PABA in solution is assayed by titration with standard NaOH; caffeine is practically neutral and does not interfere.

## Requirements

PABA, caffeine, distilled water, 0.05 N NaOH (standardised), phenolphthalein, stoppered bottles, shaker, filter paper, pipettes, burette, volumetric flasks.

## Procedure

1. Prepare caffeine solutions of 0, 0.01, 0.02, 0.03, 0.04 and 0.05 M (50 ml each).
2. Add an excess of PABA (about 0.5 g) to each bottle so that solid remains after shaking.
3. Stopper and shake for about 1 hour (or as directed) at room temperature until equilibrium is reached.
4. Filter, rejecting the first few ml.
5. Titrate 10 ml of each filtrate with 0.05 N NaOH using phenolphthalein.
6. Calculate total PABA concentration and plot against caffeine concentration.

From the straight line: intercept = S0, slope = m.

K = m / [S0 (1 − m)]

**Worked example.** S0 = 0.035 M; slope m = 0.25. K = 0.25 / (0.035 × 0.75) = 0.25 / 0.02625 = **9.5 M⁻¹**. A straight line shows a 1:1 (PABA : caffeine) complex. (Use your own data; these figures only show the method.)

## Precautions

1. Keep excess PABA in all bottles.
2. Shake for the same time at constant temperature.
3. Filter without loss of solvent; reject first filtrate.
4. Use carbonate-free NaOH.

## Viva questions

1. **What type of complex is PABA–caffeine?** An organic molecular complex (caffeine type), held by dipole and hydrogen-bond forces.
2. **What does an AL diagram show?** A soluble complex that is first order in ligand (1:1).
3. **What does a BS diagram show?** A complex of limited solubility; the plateau and fall give stoichiometry.
4. **Why does caffeine not interfere in the titration?** It is a very weak base and is practically neutral to phenolphthalein.
5. **Give pharmaceutical uses of caffeine complexes.** Caffeine–sodium benzoate injection (solubility), benzocaine–caffeine (stability).
6. **What is a stability constant?** The equilibrium constant for formation of the complex; larger value means a more stable complex.

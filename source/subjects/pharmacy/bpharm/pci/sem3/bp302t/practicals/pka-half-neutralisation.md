# pKa by half-neutralisation and the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation

**Aim:** To determine the pKa of a weak acid (for example acetic acid) by the half-neutralisation method and to confirm it using the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

For a weak acid HA ⇌ H+ + A−, the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation is:

pH = pKa + log [A−] / [HA]

When exactly half of the acid has been neutralised by NaOH, [A−] = [HA], so log 1 = 0 and **pH = pKa**. The acid is titrated with NaOH while the pH is followed with a pH meter. The equivalence point is found from the titration curve, and the pH at half that volume of alkali gives the pKa. pKa can also be calculated from the pH at other points using the measured ratio of salt to acid.

## Requirements

0.1 M acetic acid, 0.1 M NaOH (standardised), pH meter with glass electrode, standard buffers (pH 4.0 and 7.0 or 9.2), beaker, magnetic stirrer, burette, pipette.

## Procedure

1. Calibrate the pH meter with the standard buffers.
2. Pipette 25 ml of 0.1 M acetic acid into a beaker, dip the electrode and stir gently.
3. Record the initial pH.
4. Add 0.1 M NaOH from the burette, 1 ml at a time, recording the pH after each addition; add 0.2–0.5 ml portions near the equivalence point where pH changes fast.
5. Continue a few ml beyond the equivalence point.
6. Plot pH (y-axis) against volume of NaOH (x-axis). Find the equivalence volume (Ve) at the steepest point (or from a ΔpH/ΔV plot).
7. Read the pH at Ve/2 — this is the pKa.
8. Alternatively, prepare a half-neutralised solution directly: mix 25 ml acid with exactly half the volume of NaOH needed for neutralisation and read its pH.

**Using Henderson–Hasselbalch at any point before equivalence.** After adding v ml of NaOH (concentration Cb) to Va ml of acid (concentration Ca):

[A−] ∝ v × Cb; [HA] ∝ (Va × Ca − v × Cb); pKa = pH − log [A−]/[HA]

**Worked example.** Ve = 25 ml. At 12.5 ml NaOH the pH is 4.75, so pKa = **4.75**. Check at 5 ml NaOH (pH 4.15): [A−] : [HA] = 5 : 20 = 0.25; pKa = 4.15 − log 0.25 = 4.15 + 0.60 = 4.75.

## Result

The pKa of the given weak acid is ___ (literature value for acetic acid about 4.76 at 25 °C).

## Precautions

1. Calibrate the pH meter at the working temperature.
2. Stir gently and wait for a steady reading after each addition.
3. Rinse the electrode with distilled water between solutions; never let the glass bulb dry.
4. Use carbonate-free NaOH.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is pH = pKa at half-neutralisation?** Because the concentrations of salt and acid are equal, so the log term becomes zero.
2. **Define pKa.** The negative logarithm of the acid dissociation constant.
3. **Why is pKa important in pharmacy?** It decides ionisation, solubility, absorption and choice of buffers.
4. **Name other methods of pKa determination.** UV spectrophotometry, conductometry, solubility method.
5. **What is buffer capacity at pH = pKa?** Maximum (0.576 × total concentration).
6. **Why use a glass electrode?** It responds selectively to H+ activity and is not affected by oxidising or reducing agents.

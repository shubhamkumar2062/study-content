# Freundlich and Langmuir constants using activated charcoal

**Aim:** To study the adsorption of acetic acid on activated charcoal and to determine the Freundlich and Langmuir constants.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles and a lab coat.

## Principle

Activated charcoal adsorbs acetic acid from solution. After equilibrium, the acid left in solution is titrated; the amount adsorbed (x) per gram of charcoal (m) is found by difference.

**Freundlich isotherm:** x/m = k c^(1/n) → **log (x/m) = log k + (1/n) log c**

Plot log (x/m) against log c: slope = 1/n, intercept = log k.

**Langmuir isotherm:** **c / (x/m) = 1/(b ym) + c/ym**

Plot c/(x/m) against c: slope = 1/ym, intercept = 1/(b ym).

c = equilibrium concentration of acid in solution; ym = amount adsorbed for a monolayer; b = constant related to affinity.

## Requirements

Activated charcoal, acetic acid solutions (0.5 M stock), 0.1 N NaOH (standardised), phenolphthalein, stoppered bottles (250 ml), pipettes, burette, filter paper, funnels, balance.

## Procedure

1. Prepare 50 ml each of acetic acid solutions of 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4 and 0.5 M by diluting the stock.
2. Titrate 10 ml of each solution (before adding charcoal) with 0.1 N NaOH to find the exact initial concentration (C0).
3. Put 1.0 g of activated charcoal into each of six stoppered bottles and add 50 ml of each solution.
4. Shake the bottles for 30–60 minutes and allow to stand to reach equilibrium.
5. Filter each, rejecting the first few ml.
6. Titrate 10 ml of each filtrate with 0.1 N NaOH to find the equilibrium concentration (c).

**Worked example (one point).** C0 = 0.200 M, c = 0.170 M, m = 1 g, 50 ml. x = 0.030 × 0.05 = 0.0015 mol; x/m = 1.5 × 10⁻³ mol/g; c/(x/m) = 0.170 / 0.0015 = 113 g/L.

From the Freundlich plot: if slope = 0.45 and intercept = −2.4, then 1/n = 0.45 (n = 2.2) and k = 10^(−2.4) = 4.0 × 10⁻³.

From the Langmuir plot: if slope = 250 g/mol and intercept = 70 g/L, then ym = 1/250 = 4.0 × 10⁻³ mol/g and b = 1/(ym × intercept) = 1/(0.004 × 70) = 3.6 L/mol.

## Precautions

1. Use the same mass and grade of charcoal in each bottle.
2. Keep the bottles stoppered and the temperature constant.
3. Shake each bottle for the same time.
4. Reject the first portion of filtrate (filter paper adsorbs some acid).

## Viva questions

1. **Define adsorption.** Accumulation of a substance at a surface in a higher concentration than in the bulk.
2. **Is this physical or chemical adsorption?** Mainly physical (van der Waals), reversible.
3. **What does 1/n indicate?** The intensity of adsorption; values between 0 and 1 are favourable.
4. **Assumptions of Langmuir isotherm?** Monolayer adsorption, uniform sites, no interaction between adsorbed molecules.
5. **Give pharmaceutical uses of activated charcoal.** Antidote in poisoning, decolourising solutions, removing pyrogens.
6. **Effect of temperature on physical adsorption?** It decreases as temperature rises.

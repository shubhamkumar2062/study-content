# Uniformity index of a mix in a double cone blender

**Aim:** To mix a coloured tracer with a powder in a double cone blender and to calculate the uniformity (mixing) index of the mix at different mixing times.

> Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Stop the blender completely before opening it to take samples.

## Principle

A mix is judged by taking several samples from different places and measuring the amount of one component (the **tracer**) in each. In a perfect mix every sample would have the same content. The spread of results is measured by the **standard deviation (s)** of the tracer content of the samples.

- s0 = standard deviation of the completely **unmixed** (segregated) system = √[p(1 − p)], where p is the fraction of tracer.
- sR = standard deviation expected for a **completely random** mix = √[p(1 − p)/n], where n is the number of particles in a sample.
- s = standard deviation actually found.

**Lacey's mixing index:** M = (s0² − s²) ÷ (s0² − sR²). M is 0 for an unmixed system and approaches 1 for a random mix.

A simpler **uniformity index** used in many labs is based on the relative standard deviation (RSD) of the samples: RSD (%) = s ÷ mean × 100. A low RSD means good uniformity.

## Requirements

Laboratory double cone blender, lactose (or starch) as the bulk powder, a coloured or easily assayed tracer (e.g. a water-soluble dye, or sodium chloride/salicylic acid that can be titrated), sample thief or spatula, balance, volumetric flasks, colorimeter (for dye) or titration apparatus, stopwatch.

## Procedure

1. Weigh the bulk powder (e.g. 490 g lactose) and tracer (e.g. 10 g, so p = 0.02).
2. Load the bulk powder and place the tracer on top in one spot (unmixed start).
3. Run the blender for 2 minutes; stop and take samples (e.g. 1 g each) from at least 6–10 places (top, middle, bottom, both cones).
4. Analyse each sample for tracer (dissolve and read absorbance, or titrate).
5. Replace samples, then repeat sampling after 5, 10, 15 and 20 minutes of total mixing.
6. Calculate mean, s, RSD and Lacey's index at each time.

## Precautions

1. Stop the blender fully before sampling.
2. Take samples of equal size from fixed, well-spread positions each time.
3. Do not overfill the blender (about 50–60% of volume).
4. Use a tracer of similar particle size and density to the bulk to avoid segregation.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the main mechanism of mixing in a double cone blender?** Diffusive mixing by tumbling.
2. **What does a mixing index of 1 mean?** The mix is as uniform as a random mix can be.
3. **Why can over-mixing reduce uniformity?** Long tumbling can cause segregation of particles differing in size or density.
4. **Why take samples from many positions?** To detect dead spots and segregation anywhere in the blender.
5. **What is geometric dilution?** Mixing a small amount of a potent drug with an equal amount of diluent, then doubling stepwise, to obtain a uniform mix.

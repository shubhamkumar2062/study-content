# Laws of size reduction and critical speed of a ball mill

**Aim:** To verify the laws of size reduction using a ball mill, to determine Kick's, Rittinger's and Bond's coefficients and the power requirement, and to calculate the critical speed of the ball mill.

> Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Keep the mill guard closed while it runs.

## Principle

The energy used in size reduction depends on the feed size (L1) and product size (L2):

- **Rittinger's law:** E = KR (1/L2 − 1/L1)
- **Kick's law:** E = KK ln (L1/L2)
- **Bond's law:** E = KB (1/√L2 − 1/√L1)

If the energy used (from power × time) and the average sizes before and after grinding are known, each constant can be calculated. The law that gives the most constant value over several runs fits the material and size range best.

**Critical speed:** the speed at which the balls are carried round with the shell and stop grinding:

Nc = (1/2π) √( g / (R − r) ) revolutions per second

where R is the radius of the mill and r the radius of a ball. Optimum speed is about 0.5–0.75 of Nc.

## Requirements

Laboratory ball mill with motor (power rating known, or a wattmeter), balls, coarse material (e.g. crystals of sodium chloride, calcium carbonate lumps or crushed brick), set of sieves and shaker, balance, stopwatch, tachometer (or count of revolutions), vernier calipers.

## Procedure

1. Measure the inner diameter of the mill (2R) and the diameter of a ball (2r).
2. Find the average size of the feed (L1) by sieve analysis.
3. Load a weighed amount of feed (e.g. 200 g) and balls (about 30–50% of volume).
4. Run the mill for a fixed time (e.g. 10 minutes); note the power (P, watts) and the speed (rpm).
5. Remove the product, separate the balls and find its average size (L2) by sieve analysis.
6. Repeat for different times (e.g. 20 and 30 minutes) with fresh feed.
7. Note the speed at which balls begin to stick to the wall (if the mill has a speed control and window), for comparison.

Energy per unit mass can be used (E ÷ mass of feed). Then:

- KR = E ÷ (1/L2 − 1/L1)
- KK = E ÷ ln (L1/L2)
- KB = E ÷ (1/√L2 − 1/√L1)

**Critical speed:** with R and r in metres, Nc (rpm) = 60 × (1/2π) × √(9.81/(R − r)) ≈ 29.9/√(R − r).

**Worked example (illustration only):** mill diameter 0.30 m (R = 0.15 m), ball diameter 0.02 m (r = 0.01 m). R − r = 0.14 m. Nc = 29.9/√0.14 ≈ 29.9/0.374 ≈ 80 rpm. Optimum about 40–60 rpm.

Energy example: P = 100 W for 600 s → E = 60,000 J for 0.2 kg → 300,000 J/kg. If L1 = 2000 µm and L2 = 500 µm: KK = 300,000/ln 4 ≈ 216,000 J/kg; KR = 300,000/(1/500 − 1/2000) = 300,000/0.0015 = 2.0 × 10⁸ J·µm/kg.

## Precautions

1. Use the same weight of feed and balls in each run.
2. Run at a steady speed below critical.
3. Sieve feed and product in the same way.
4. Power measured includes losses in the motor and mill; note this as a source of error.

## Viva questions

1. **Which law applies to fine grinding?** Rittinger's law.
2. **Which law applies to coarse crushing?** Kick's law.
3. **What happens at critical speed?** Balls are held against the wall by centrifugal force and no grinding occurs.
4. **Why are mixed ball sizes used?** Large balls break coarse feed; small balls fill gaps and do fine grinding.
5. **Why is only a small fraction of energy used for size reduction?** Most is lost as heat, noise, friction and elastic deformation.

# Moisture content and loss on drying

**Aim:** To determine the moisture content and the loss on drying (LOD) of a given sample (for example wet granules, starch or a crude drug powder).

> Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Use tongs and a desiccator for hot bottles.

## Principle

When a moist sample is heated to constant weight, the loss in weight is taken as the water (and any volatile matter) it contained. The result is expressed in two ways:

- **Loss on drying (LOD), wet basis:** LOD (%) = (weight of water ÷ weight of wet sample) × 100
- **Moisture content (MC), dry basis:** MC (%) = (weight of water ÷ weight of dry sample) × 100

LOD can never exceed 100%; MC can exceed 100% for very wet materials. Pharmacopoeias usually specify LOD (for example, drying at 105 °C to constant weight).

Relation: MC = LOD ÷ (100 − LOD) × 100.

## Requirements

Sample, flat glass weighing bottles with stoppers (or Petri dishes), hot air oven at 105 °C (or the temperature given in the monograph), desiccator with silica gel, analytical balance, spatula. A moisture balance (infrared/halogen) can be used for a quick check.

## Procedure

1. Dry an empty weighing bottle with its stopper at 105 °C for 30 minutes, cool in a desiccator and weigh (W1).
2. Put about 1–2 g of the sample in the bottle, spread evenly, and weigh (W2).
3. Place the bottle in the oven with the stopper removed (kept beside it). Dry at 105 °C for the specified time (e.g. 1–2 hours).
4. Close the bottle, cool in the desiccator and weigh.
5. Repeat drying for 30-minute periods until two successive weighings do not differ by more than 0.5 mg (constant weight, W3).
6. Do the test in duplicate.

## Observations and calculations

| Reading | Weight (g) |
| --- | --- |
| Empty bottle (W1) | |
| Bottle + wet sample (W2) | |
| Bottle + dried sample (W3) | |

- Weight of wet sample = W2 − W1
- Weight of water = W2 − W3
- Weight of dry sample = W3 − W1
- LOD (%) = (W2 − W3) ÷ (W2 − W1) × 100
- MC (%) = (W2 − W3) ÷ (W3 − W1) × 100

**Worked example (illustration only):** wet sample 2.000 g, dry sample 1.900 g, water 0.100 g. LOD = 0.100/2.000 × 100 = 5.0%. MC = 0.100/1.900 × 100 ≈ 5.26%.

## Precautions

1. Bottles must be dried and cooled before the first weighing.
2. Cool in a desiccator before every weighing; never weigh hot.
3. Keep the sample layer thin (not more than about 5 mm).
4. Weigh quickly to prevent moisture pick-up.
5. Do not exceed the specified temperature, or the sample may decompose and give a falsely high result.

## Viva questions

1. **Differentiate LOD and moisture content.** LOD is water per unit wet weight; MC is water per unit dry weight.
2. **Why dry to constant weight?** To make sure all removable moisture has gone.
3. **Why is LOD important for granules?** Too much moisture makes tablets stick and degrade; too little causes capping and poor binding.
4. **What other method measures water specifically?** Karl Fischer titration, which measures water only and not other volatile matter.
5. **What is the purpose of the desiccator?** To let the sample cool without absorbing moisture from air.

# Size analysis of tablet granules by sieving

**Aim:** To determine the particle size distribution of given tablet granules by sieve analysis, and to construct size–frequency curves, including cumulative and logarithmic-probability plots.

> Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ.

## Principle

A weighed sample is shaken through a nest of standard sieves arranged with the largest opening at the top. Each sieve retains particles larger than its opening. The weight retained on each sieve gives the distribution of particle sizes. The size of the particles retained between two sieves is taken as the arithmetic mean of the two openings.

From the data:

- **Frequency (histogram) curve:** % retained against mean size.
- **Cumulative curves:** cumulative % undersize (or oversize) against size.
- **Log-probability plot:** cumulative % undersize on a probability scale against log size; a straight line means a **log-normal distribution**. The size at 50% is the **geometric mean diameter (dg)**, and the geometric standard deviation σg = d84.13 / d50.

Average size by weight: d(avg) = Σ (% retained × mean size) ÷ 100.

## Requirements

Granules (about 100 g), set of standard sieves (for example sieve numbers 10, 22, 44, 60, 85 and a receiver pan), lid, mechanical sieve shaker, balance, brush, arithmetic and log-probability graph paper.

## Procedure

1. Clean and weigh each sieve and the pan.
2. Arrange the sieves with the coarsest at the top and the pan at the bottom.
3. Weigh 100 g of granules and place on the top sieve; cover with the lid.
4. Shake on the sieve shaker for 10–20 minutes at a fixed amplitude.
5. Weigh each sieve with its retained granules; find the weight retained.
6. Check that the total equals the sample weight (loss not more than about 1–2%).

## Precautions

1. Sieves must be clean, dry and undamaged.
2. Do not overload the top sieve.
3. Shake for a fixed time; too long breaks granules and gives more fines.
4. Brush particles from the underside of each sieve into the next before weighing.

## Viva questions

1. **What does the sieve number mean?** Roughly the number of meshes per 25.4 mm; a higher number means a smaller opening.
2. **Why is granule size distribution important?** It affects flow, die filling, weight variation, hardness and dissolution of tablets.
3. **What does a straight line on log-probability paper indicate?** The sizes follow a log-normal distribution.
4. **Define geometric mean diameter.** The size at 50% on the cumulative log-probability plot.
5. **What is a limitation of sieve analysis?** It is unreliable below about 40 µm and depends on particle shape; long shaking causes attrition.

# Demonstration of colloid mill, planetary mixer, fluidised bed dryer and freeze dryer

**Aim:** To observe the demonstration of major pharmaceutical equipment (colloid mill, planetary mixer, fluidised bed dryer, freeze dryer and similar equipment) and to record their principle, construction, working and uses.

> This is a demonstration. Machines are operated by the teacher or technician only. Stand behind guards and keep clear of moving parts.

## Principle

Each machine performs a unit operation: the colloid mill reduces droplet or particle size by shear, the planetary mixer mixes and kneads, the fluidised bed dryer dries granules by fluidisation, and the freeze dryer dries by sublimation.

## Requirements

The equipment (or models), suitable demonstration material (an oil-in-water emulsion premix, wet granulation mass, wet granules, a frozen solution in vials), record book.

## Procedure

1. Watch the demonstration of each machine and note the steps.
2. Identify and label all parts in a sketch.
3. Record principle, construction, working, uses, merits and demerits.

## Observations

### 1. Colloid mill

**Principle:** **shear**. Material passes through a very narrow gap between a high-speed **rotor** and a stationary **stator**; intense shear breaks droplets or particles to a very small size (often a few micrometres).

**Construction:** a conical rotor turning at high speed inside a matching conical stator; the gap is adjustable (fine clearance, fractions of a millimetre); feed hopper, outlet, cooling jacket.

```text
     feed ↓
   ┌──────────┐
   │ ╲ stator ╱│
   │  ╲rotor ╱ │ ← narrow adjustable gap
   │   ╲    ╱  │
   └────╲__╱───┘ → product
```

**Working:** the premix is fed into the hopper; it is drawn through the gap, sheared and thrown out to the outlet; it may be recirculated.

**Uses:** emulsions, creams, ointments, suspensions, pastes; reducing particle size of insoluble drugs in liquid.

**Merits:** very fine and uniform product; continuous; adjustable gap. **Demerits:** produces heat (cooling needed); not for dry powders; wear of rotor.

### 2. Planetary mixer

**Principle:** the blade turns on its own axis and also travels round the bowl (planetary motion), so the whole bowl is covered.

**Construction:** removable stainless steel bowl; vertical shaft with interchangeable blades (flat beater, whisk, dough hook) driven through planetary gears; speed control; bowl lift.

**Working:** material is placed in the bowl, the blade lowered and the mixer run at the selected speed; granulating fluid is added slowly for wet granulation.

**Uses:** wet granulation, creams, ointments, pastes, small batches.

**Merits:** no dead spots; variable speed; easy cleaning. **Demerits:** batch only; heat on long mixing; limited capacity.

### 3. Fluidised bed dryer

**Principle:** hot air passing up through a bed of wet granules lifts and suspends them, giving fast, uniform heat and mass transfer.

**Construction:** product bowl with perforated base on a trolley; air inlet with filter, fan and heater; expansion chamber with bag filters and shaker; exhaust.

**Working:** wet granules are loaded, the bowl placed in position, hot air started; the bed fluidises; drying is checked by LOD; bags are shaken to return fines; the bowl is removed and emptied.

**Uses:** drying tablet granules; granulation and coating in fluid bed processors.

**Merits:** fast (about 20–40 minutes), uniform, gentle temperature. **Demerits:** attrition and fines; static charge (must be earthed); not for sticky materials.

### 4. Freeze dryer (lyophiliser)

**Principle:** frozen material is dried by sublimation of ice under high vacuum, below the triple point of water.

**Construction:** drying chamber with temperature-controlled shelves; very cold condenser; vacuum pump; refrigeration unit; control panel; stoppering mechanism for vials.

**Working:** freezing on the shelves → vacuum applied → primary drying (gentle shelf heat, ice sublimes and collects on the condenser) → secondary drying (higher shelf temperature removes bound water) → vials stoppered.

**Uses:** antibiotics, vaccines, sera, plasma, enzymes, proteins, injectable powders.

**Merits:** best for very heat-sensitive products; porous, quickly reconstituted cake; long shelf life. **Demerits:** costly; long cycle; hygroscopic product.

## Result

The principle, construction, working and uses of the colloid mill, planetary mixer, fluidised bed dryer and freeze dryer were observed and recorded.

## Precautions

1. Follow the standard operating procedure for each machine.
2. Ensure FBD is earthed and filters are in place before starting.
3. Do not open the freeze dryer chamber under vacuum.
4. Set the colloid mill gap before starting the rotor.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the principle of a colloid mill?** Shear in a narrow gap between a high-speed rotor and a stator.
2. **What is planetary motion in a mixer?** The blade spins about its own axis while revolving around the bowl.
3. **What is fluidisation?** The state in which particles are suspended in an upward gas stream and behave like a boiling liquid.
4. **Name the three stages of freeze drying.** Freezing, primary drying (sublimation), secondary drying (desorption).
5. **Why is the condenser of a freeze dryer kept very cold?** To trap the water vapour as ice so the vacuum is maintained and sublimation continues.

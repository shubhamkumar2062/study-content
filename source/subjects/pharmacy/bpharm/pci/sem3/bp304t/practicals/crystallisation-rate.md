# Effect of time on the rate of crystallisation

**Aim:** To study the effect of time on the rate of crystallisation of a given substance (for example potassium nitrate, copper sulfate or potash alum) from its supersaturated solution.

> Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Handle hot solutions carefully.

## Principle

**Crystallisation** is the formation of solid crystals from a solution. A hot, nearly saturated solution becomes **supersaturated** on cooling, and the excess solute separates in two steps:

1. **Nucleation:** formation of tiny crystal nuclei.
2. **Crystal growth:** solute deposits on the nuclei.

Supersaturation is the driving force. It is high at first, so crystallisation is fast; as solute separates, the solution approaches saturation and the rate falls. Therefore the amount of crystals formed rises quickly at first and then levels off with time. Slow cooling gives fewer, larger crystals; fast cooling gives many small ones.

## Requirements

Potassium nitrate (or copper sulfate or potash alum), distilled water, beakers, measuring cylinder, water bath or hot plate, thermometer, glass rod, filter paper and funnel (or Buchner funnel), watch glasses, balance, stopwatch, ice bath (optional).

## Procedure

1. Dissolve a weighed amount of solute in water by warming on a water bath (e.g. 40 g potassium nitrate in 50 ml water at about 60 °C), so that the solution will become supersaturated on cooling; filter while hot if needed.
2. Divide equal volumes (e.g. 20 ml) into five identical beakers.
3. Allow all to cool at room temperature without disturbance. Start the stopwatch.
4. At fixed intervals (e.g. 10, 20, 30, 45 and 60 minutes), filter the crystals from one beaker.
5. Dry the crystals between filter papers (or in a warm oven) and weigh.
6. Note the shape and approximate size of the crystals.

## Result

The weight of crystals increased with time, rapidly at first and then slowly, levelling off as the solution approached saturation. The rate of crystallisation was highest in the first ___ minutes.

## Precautions

1. Use equal volumes of the same solution in identical beakers.
2. Do not disturb or shake the beakers during cooling.
3. Dry all crystal crops in the same way before weighing.
4. Keep the room temperature steady.

## Viva questions

1. **What is supersaturation?** A state in which a solution contains more solute than its equilibrium solubility at that temperature.
2. **Name the two steps of crystallisation.** Nucleation and crystal growth.
3. **Why does the rate fall with time?** The supersaturation (driving force) decreases as solute separates.
4. **How do you get large crystals?** Slow cooling and low supersaturation, so fewer nuclei form and each grows larger.
5. **What is seeding?** Adding small crystals to a supersaturated solution to start crystallisation at a controlled point.

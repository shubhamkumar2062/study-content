# Factors affecting the rate of filtration and evaporation

**Aim:** To study the effect of (a) surface area, (b) concentration and (c) thickness/viscosity on the rate of filtration and the rate of evaporation.

> Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Handle hot dishes with tongs.

## Principle

**Filtration:** by Darcy's equation, V/t = K A ΔP / (η L). The rate of filtration increases with **filter area (A)** and pressure difference, and decreases with **viscosity (η)** of the liquid and **thickness of the cake (L)**. A more concentrated slurry builds a thicker cake faster, so the rate falls quickly.

**Evaporation:** the rate of evaporation increases with **surface area** exposed and temperature, and decreases as the **concentration** (and so the boiling point and viscosity) of the solution rises.

## Requirements

Funnels of different diameters, filter paper (same grade), measuring cylinders, stopwatch, calcium carbonate (for slurries), glycerin or sucrose (to change viscosity), water; evaporating dishes of different diameters, water bath, balance, sodium chloride or sucrose solutions of different concentrations.

## Procedure

**A. Filtration**

1. *Surface area:* filter equal volumes (e.g. 50 ml) of the same calcium carbonate slurry (e.g. 5% w/v) through funnels of two or three different sizes (filter paper folded in the same way). Note the time to collect a fixed volume of filtrate (e.g. 30 ml).
2. *Concentration:* filter 50 ml of slurries of 2%, 5% and 10% calcium carbonate through the same size funnel. Note times.
3. *Viscosity:* filter 50 ml of water, and of 20% and 40% glycerin (or sucrose) solutions, each containing the same amount of calcium carbonate, through the same funnel. Note times.

**B. Evaporation**

1. *Surface area:* put equal volumes (e.g. 25 ml) of water in dishes of different diameters on the same water bath. Weigh at the start and after 30 minutes.
2. *Concentration:* put 25 ml each of water and of 10%, 20% and 30% sucrose (or sodium chloride) solution in identical dishes on the bath. Weigh at the start and after 30 minutes.

**Worked example (illustration only):** 30 ml collected in 120 s through the small funnel → 0.25 ml/s; in 60 s through the large funnel → 0.5 ml/s. A dish of 8 cm diameter (area ≈ 50 cm²) lost 6 g in 30 min → 0.2 g/min.

Plot rate against each variable.

## Result

1. The rate of filtration increased with filter area and decreased with increasing slurry concentration and viscosity.
2. The rate of evaporation increased with surface area and decreased with increasing concentration of solute.

## Precautions

1. Use the same grade of filter paper folded in the same way.
2. Stir slurries well before pouring; pour at the same rate.
3. Keep the water bath temperature constant and dishes at the same level.
4. Weigh dishes after wiping the outside dry.

## Viva questions

1. **State Darcy's equation.** V/t = KAΔP/ηL.
2. **Why does a concentrated slurry filter slowly?** It builds a thicker cake quickly, increasing resistance.
3. **How can viscous liquids be filtered faster?** Warm them to lower viscosity, use pressure or vacuum, use a larger area, use filter aids.
4. **Why does a concentrated solution evaporate more slowly?** Its vapour pressure is lower (boiling point higher) and viscosity higher.
5. **Which evaporator increases surface area by forming a thin film?** Film evaporators, such as the climbing film evaporator.

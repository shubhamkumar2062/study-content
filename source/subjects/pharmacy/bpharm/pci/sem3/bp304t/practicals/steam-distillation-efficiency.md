# Efficiency of steam distillation

**Aim:** To carry out steam distillation of a water-immiscible liquid (for example turpentine oil or a volatile oil) and to calculate the efficiency of the process.

> Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Steam and hot glassware cause burns; keep a safety tube in the steam generator.

## Principle

When two immiscible liquids are heated together, each exerts its own full vapour pressure. The mixture boils when the **sum of the two vapour pressures equals atmospheric pressure**, so it boils below 100 °C and below the boiling point of the oil.

The theoretical ratio of the two liquids in the distillate is:

mo / mw = (po × Mo) / (pw × Mw)

where m is mass, p vapour pressure at the boiling temperature and M molecular weight (o = oil, w = water).

In practice the steam does not become fully saturated with oil vapour, so less oil distils than theory predicts. The **efficiency** is:

Efficiency (%) = (actual mass of oil per gram of water ÷ theoretical mass of oil per gram of water) × 100

## Requirements

Steam generator with safety tube, round-bottom distillation flask, Liebig condenser, receiver (measuring cylinder or separating funnel), thermometer, heating mantle or burner, turpentine oil (or clove oil), water, barometer reading, table of vapour pressure of water.

## Procedure

1. Put a known volume of oil (e.g. 20 ml) with a little water in the distillation flask and fit the steam inlet tube to reach near the bottom.
2. Connect the flask to the condenser and receiver. Warm the flask gently so that steam does not condense too much in it.
3. Pass steam from the generator through the mixture.
4. Note the temperature of the distilling vapour (it stays constant below 100 °C).
5. Collect the distillate in a graduated cylinder or separating funnel. Note the volumes of the oil layer and water layer collected over a set time.
6. Convert volumes to masses using densities.
7. Note the atmospheric pressure.

## Observations and calculations

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Boiling temperature of the mixture | ___ °C |
| Atmospheric pressure (P) | ___ mm Hg |
| Vapour pressure of water at that temperature (pw, from tables) | ___ mm Hg |
| Vapour pressure of oil, po = P − pw | ___ mm Hg |
| Volume / mass of oil collected | ___ |
| Volume / mass of water collected | ___ |

1. Theoretical ratio = (po × Mo) / (pw × 18).
2. Actual ratio = mass of oil ÷ mass of water collected.
3. Efficiency = actual ratio ÷ theoretical ratio × 100.

**Worked example (illustration only):** boiling at 95.5 °C, P = 760, pw = 646, po = 114 mm Hg; turpentine (taking M ≈ 136). Theoretical ratio = (114 × 136)/(646 × 18) ≈ 1.33 g oil per g water. If 10 g oil and 9.0 g water were collected, actual ratio = 1.11, efficiency ≈ 83%.

## Precautions

1. Keep the steam generator safety tube in place to avoid pressure build-up.
2. Warm the flask slightly so steam does not flood it.
3. Keep the condenser water flowing so the oil does not escape as vapour.
4. Separate layers carefully and allow time for them to settle.

## Viva questions

1. **Why does the mixture boil below 100 °C?** The total vapour pressure is the sum of both liquids' vapour pressures and reaches atmospheric pressure at a lower temperature.
2. **Why is the efficiency less than 100%?** Steam passes through the liquid too quickly to become fully saturated with oil vapour; some heat and vapour are lost.
3. **Give pharmaceutical uses.** Extraction of volatile oils (clove, eucalyptus, peppermint) and preparation of aromatic waters.
4. **What is a Florentine receiver?** A vessel that separates an oil from water by density, with outlets for each layer.
5. **Can steam distillation be used for water-miscible liquids?** No, the method depends on the liquids being immiscible.

# Humidity of air: wet and dry bulb, and dew point

**Aim:** To determine the humidity of air (i) from wet and dry bulb temperatures and (ii) by the dew point method.

> Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Ether (if used in the dew point hygrometer) is highly flammable; keep flames away.

## Principle

**Humidity** is the amount of water vapour in air.

- **Absolute humidity:** mass of water vapour per unit mass of dry air (kg/kg).
- **Relative humidity (RH):** ratio of the partial pressure of water vapour in the air to the saturated vapour pressure at the same temperature, × 100.
- **Dew point:** temperature at which air becomes saturated when cooled at constant pressure, so moisture starts to condense.

**Wet and dry bulb method:** a dry thermometer reads the air temperature (dry bulb). A thermometer whose bulb is covered with wet muslin reads lower (wet bulb), because evaporation of water cools it. The drier the air, the faster the evaporation and the larger the difference (wet bulb depression). From the two readings, RH is read from a **psychrometric chart** or humidity tables.

**Dew point method:** a polished surface is cooled slowly (by evaporating ether or adding ice to water) until dew just appears. The temperature then is the dew point. RH = (saturated vapour pressure at the dew point ÷ saturated vapour pressure at air temperature) × 100.

## Requirements

Wet and dry bulb hygrometer (or sling psychrometer), muslin wick, distilled water, psychrometric chart or humidity tables, dew point hygrometer (Regnault's or a polished metal can), ether or ice, thermometers, aspirator or bellows, table of saturated vapour pressure of water.

## Procedure

**(i) Wet and dry bulb**

1. Wet the muslin around the wet bulb with distilled water.
2. Ventilate the bulbs (whirl the sling psychrometer or use a fan) for 1–2 minutes until the wet bulb reading is steady.
3. Note the dry bulb (Td) and wet bulb (Tw) temperatures. Repeat three times.
4. Read RH and absolute humidity from the chart or tables.

**(ii) Dew point**

1. Place ether (or water) in the polished container with a thermometer.
2. Cool slowly by bubbling air through the ether (or by adding ice bit by bit), stirring.
3. Note the temperature (T1) at which a film of dew first appears on the polished surface.
4. Stop cooling and note the temperature (T2) at which the dew disappears.
5. Dew point = (T1 + T2)/2. Note the room (dry bulb) temperature.

## Precautions

1. Use distilled water and a clean wick; ventilate well before reading.
2. Do not touch or breathe on the bulbs or the polished surface.
3. Cool slowly near the dew point so it is not overshot.
4. Keep ether away from flames.

## Viva questions

1. **Why does the wet bulb read lower?** Evaporation of water from the wick takes latent heat from the bulb.
2. **When are wet and dry bulb readings equal?** When the air is saturated (RH 100%), so no evaporation takes place.
3. **Why is humidity control important in pharmacy?** Hygroscopic and effervescent products, gelatin capsules and tablet compression need controlled RH.
4. **What is a psychrometric chart?** A chart relating dry bulb, wet bulb, dew point, humidity and enthalpy of air–water mixtures.
5. **How is air dehumidified?** By cooling below the dew point (refrigeration) or by adsorbents such as silica gel (desiccant wheel).

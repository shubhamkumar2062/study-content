# How to use this guide

Pharmaceutical Engineering is the most "machine-heavy" subject of semester 3. Almost every long question asks you to describe one or two pieces of equipment, and almost every short question is either a machine, a law or a definition. Study one unit a day: read the notes, draw every sketch once from memory, then answer the question bank without looking.

**PCI end-semester paper as used by HPTU (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective questions (MCQs, fill in the blanks or one-line answers) | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays: attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers: attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

**Where marks usually come from**

| Unit | Typical weight | Favourite questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Heavy | Reynolds number, Bernoulli, venturi/orifice/rotameter, laws of size reduction, ball mill (critical speed), hammer mill, fluid energy mill, cyclone, official powder grades |
| 2 | Heavy | Fourier's law, heat exchangers, climbing film and multiple effect evaporators, economy, steam and molecular distillation, fractional distillation |
| 3 | Heavy | Rate of drying curve, EMC, spray dryer, FBD, freeze dryer, mechanisms of mixing, double cone, ribbon, sigma blade, Silverson |
| 4 | Medium–heavy | Theories and factors of filtration, filter aids, plate and frame, rotary drum, membrane filters, super centrifuge, perforated basket |
| 5 | Medium | Factors for material selection, stainless steel, theories and types of corrosion, prevention, material handling |

**Probability ratings**

- **High:** asked in most years in some form; prepare the full answer with a labelled sketch.
- **Medium:** asked every few years, or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low:** rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice in Section C.

The ratings are estimates from the syllabus weight and the usual pattern of PCI pharmacy papers. They are not a promise of what will be asked.

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. **Use the six standard headings for every machine:** Principle → Construction (with a labelled diagram) → Working → Uses → Merits → Demerits. Examiners mark each heading.
2. **Draw the sketch first.** A neat labelled block or line drawing takes two minutes and earns a large share of the marks. Label inlet, outlet, heating medium and moving parts.
3. **Write the equation with every symbol explained:** Re = Duρ/µ, Fourier's law, Darcy's equation, F = mω²r, the three laws of size reduction, economy of evaporation.
4. **Give numbers you are sure of:** laminar Re below about 2000, ball mill optimum at about 0.5–0.75 of critical speed, 0.22 µm membrane for sterilisation, 18/8 stainless steel.
5. **Compare in a table** when a question says "differentiate" (evaporation vs drying, solid vs liquid mixing, orifice vs venturi, perforated vs non-perforated basket).
6. **End with pharmaceutical uses:** name real products (granules, antibiotics, vaccines, syrups, volatile oils). This links engineering to pharmacy and fetches the last marks.
7. **For curves** (rate of drying, sorption isotherm) label both axes and every stage.

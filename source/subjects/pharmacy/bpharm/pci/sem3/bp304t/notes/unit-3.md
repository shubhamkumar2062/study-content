# Unit 3: Drying and mixing

Drying and mixing appear in almost every solid dosage form. Wet granules must be dried before compression; powders must be mixed so that every tablet carries the right dose. This unit covers the theory of drying, six dryers, the theory of mixing, and the common mixers for powders, liquids and semisolids.

## 3.1 Drying

**Drying** is the removal of a liquid (usually water) from a solid, semisolid or liquid by evaporation, to give a dry solid. It differs from evaporation in that the product is a **solid** and the amount of liquid removed is relatively small.

### Objectives and applications

1. **Preservation and stability:** moisture favours hydrolysis and microbial growth (e.g. aspirin, vitamins, crude drugs).
2. **Tablet manufacture:** wet granules are dried to the right moisture content before compression.
3. **Easier handling:** dry powders flow, mix and pack better; less weight to transport.
4. **Size reduction:** dry, brittle material grinds more easily.
5. **Preparing dry extracts, dried blood products, vaccines and antibiotics.**
6. **Improving properties:** spray-dried lactose flows and compresses well.

### Moisture terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Moisture content (dry basis) | Mass of water ÷ mass of dry solid × 100 |
| Loss on drying (wet basis) | Mass of water ÷ mass of wet sample × 100 |
| Bound water | Water held in capillaries or adsorbed, with vapour pressure lower than pure water |
| Unbound water | Water in excess of bound water; behaves like free water with normal vapour pressure |
| **Equilibrium moisture content (EMC)** | Moisture content of a solid when its vapour pressure equals the partial pressure of water in the surrounding air at a given temperature and humidity; no further drying or gain takes place |
| **Free moisture content** | Total moisture − EMC; the only part that can be removed under the given conditions |
| Critical moisture content | Moisture content at which the constant rate period ends |

### Equilibrium moisture content: measurement and applications

**Measurement:** weighed samples are placed in closed desiccators or chambers each held at a known relative humidity (using saturated salt solutions or sulfuric acid solutions of known strength) at constant temperature. They are weighed until constant weight. The final moisture content at each RH is plotted against RH to give a **moisture sorption isotherm**. Gaining moisture gives the *sorption* curve; losing it gives the *desorption* curve; the gap between them is **hysteresis**.

```text
 Moisture

   │                       ╱╱ desorption
   │                    ╱╱
   │               ╱‾‾╱  sorption
   │         ___╱‾‾
   │ ____‾‾‾‾
   └──────────────────────────────── Relative humidity (%)
     0                           100
```

**Applications:**

1. Shows the lowest moisture a product can reach in given air, so the drying end point is not set lower than possible.
2. Decides storage conditions and packaging (hygroscopic drugs such as effervescent salts need low-RH rooms and tight packs).
3. Predicts whether a dried product will pick up moisture during storage or compression.
4. Helps choose the conditions of drying air.

### Mechanism of drying

Drying involves **two processes at the same time**:

1. **Heat transfer** to the solid to supply the latent heat of vaporisation (by conduction, convection or radiation).
2. **Mass transfer**: water moves from the inside of the solid to the surface (by liquid diffusion or capillary flow), evaporates, and the vapour is carried away by the air.

When the surface is fully wet, the rate is controlled by how fast heat reaches the surface and vapour leaves it. Once the surface dries out, the rate is controlled by how fast water moves from inside.

### Rate of drying curve

If a wet solid is dried under constant conditions and weighed at intervals, the rate of drying (kg water per m² per hour) can be plotted against free moisture content.

```text
 Rate of
 drying
   │              C ___________________ B
   │              ╱  constant rate       ╲
   │          D ╱                          ╲ A
   │          ╱  first falling              (initial adjustment)
   │       ╱  second falling
   │ E __╱
   └──────────────────────────────────────────►  Free moisture content
     EMC        critical MC (C)          initial MC

   Drying starts at A (right) and moves left towards E as water is removed.
```

| Section | Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A–B | Initial adjustment | Solid warms up to the drying temperature; rate rises |
| B–C | **Constant rate period** | Surface is fully wet; water evaporates as from an open surface; rate depends on air temperature, humidity and velocity |
| C | **Critical moisture content** | Water can no longer reach the surface as fast as it evaporates; dry patches appear |
| C–D | First falling rate period | Surface unsaturated; wetted area shrinks; rate falls |
| D–E | Second falling rate period | Evaporation happens inside the solid; rate controlled by diffusion of water through the solid |
| E | EMC | Rate becomes zero |

**Uses of the curve:** predicts drying time, shows which period controls the process, helps choose the dryer and conditions, and helps scale up.

### Classification of dryers

| Basis | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Static bed (solid does not move) | Tray dryer, vacuum dryer, freeze dryer |
| Moving bed | Drum dryer |
| Fluidised bed | Fluidised bed dryer |
| Pneumatic / dilute phase | Spray dryer, flash dryer |
| Specialised | Freeze dryer, microwave, infrared |

### Tray dryer (shelf or compartment dryer)

**Principle:** hot air passes over (and sometimes through) the wet material spread on trays. Heat is transferred by **convection** and water vapour is carried off by the air.

**Construction:** an insulated rectangular cabinet with a door, holding a **trolley or racks of shallow trays** (about 16–40 trays). A **fan** circulates air over **heaters** (steam coils or electric) and then across the trays. Part of the air is let out and replaced with fresh air to remove moisture. **Baffles** spread the air evenly.

```text
 ┌───────────────────────────────────┐
 │ fresh air in →        → humid air out
 │  ┌──┐   ═══════════ tray          │
 │  │F │→  ═══════════ tray   ← air  │
 │  │A │→  ═══════════ tray     flow │
 │  │N │→  ═══════════ tray          │
 │  └──┘   heaters                    │
 └───────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Working:** wet material is spread thinly (1–2 cm) on trays, loaded and the door closed. Hot air circulates over the trays until the material is dry (often several hours). The fan speed and temperature are set as required.

**Uses:** drying wet granules, crude drugs, chemicals, powders and sticky materials in small batches.

**Merits:** batch type, so good for small quantities and varied products; any material can be dried; little product loss; easy to control.

**Demerits:** long drying time; labour for loading and unloading; drying may be uneven (top and bottom trays differ); low heat efficiency.

### Drum dryer (roller dryer)

**Principle:** a thin film of liquid or slurry on the surface of a **heated rotating drum** dries by **conduction** within less than one revolution and is scraped off.

**Construction:** a hollow horizontal metal drum (polished cast iron or stainless steel), heated inside by steam. The drum dips into a **feed trough** (or the feed is applied by a spreader roller). A **doctor knife** at the other side scrapes off the dry product into a conveyor. Double drum dryers have two drums turning towards each other, with feed between them.

```text
          ┌──────┐
         ╱ steam  ╲   ← drum rotates
        │  inside  │
         ╲        ╱╲ ← doctor knife
          └──────┘   ╲→ dry flakes
     ═════════════
     feed trough
```

**Working:** as the drum turns, it picks up a thin film; the film dries during the turn; the knife scrapes off a dry, flaky sheet.

**Uses:** milk products, starch, malt extract, glucose, slurries of insoluble drugs (e.g. calcium phosphate), plant extracts.

**Merits:** fast, so heat exposure is short; compact; continuous; heat used efficiently (conduction).

**Demerits:** product is flaky and may need milling; not for salt solutions that scale or for very heat-sensitive material; skilled operation; drum surface wears.

### Spray dryer

**Principle:** a solution or suspension is **atomised** into fine droplets that meet a stream of hot air. The enormous surface area dries each droplet in a few seconds. Evaporation keeps the droplet cool, so even heat-sensitive materials survive.

**Construction:** a large **drying chamber** (stainless steel) with a conical bottom; an **atomiser** at the top (a rotary disc spinning at high speed, or a pressure or two-fluid nozzle); an air heater and fan; a **cyclone separator** (and bag filter) to collect the powder.

```text
           feed ↓
    hot air → ◎ ← atomiser (disc/nozzle)
   ┌──────────╨──────────┐
   │    · . · : · . ·    │  drying chamber
   │   droplets → dry    │
   │      particles      │
    ╲                   ╱
     ╲_________________╱ → air + powder → cyclone → product
```

**Working:** feed is pumped to the atomiser and sprayed into hot air; water evaporates within seconds; dry particles fall to the cone or are carried with the air to the cyclone, where they are separated.

**Uses:** spray-dried lactose, acacia, gelatin, milk, coffee and plant extracts; antibiotics and enzymes; microencapsulation of flavours.

**Merits:** very rapid drying (seconds), so suitable for thermolabile materials; continuous; product is fine, spherical, often hollow, free-flowing and quickly soluble; liquid goes straight to powder (no separate size reduction).

**Demerits:** very bulky and expensive; low thermal efficiency (much hot air leaves with heat); not economical for small batches; fine dust needs collection.

### Fluidised bed dryer (FBD)

**Principle:** hot air is blown **upward through a bed of wet granules** at a velocity high enough to lift and suspend them. Each particle is surrounded by hot air (**fluidisation**), so heat and mass transfer are very fast and uniform.

**Construction:** a stainless steel **product container (bowl)** with a perforated or mesh bottom, mounted on a trolley. Below it is the air inlet with a **fan, heater and filter**. Above it is an expansion chamber with **bag filters** (finger bags) to stop fine particles escaping, and a shaker to clean them.

```text
        exhaust ↑
     ┌─────┴─────┐
     │ bag filter│
     │ ░░░░░░░░░ │
     │  · · · ·  │ ← fluidised granules
     │ · · · · · │
     └─┬───────┬─┘  ← perforated bowl base
       ↑ hot air ↑ (fan + heater + filter)
```

**Working:** granules are loaded into the bowl; hot air is blown up through them; at the right velocity the bed boils like a liquid; drying takes about 20–40 minutes; the bowl is removed and emptied.

**Uses:** drying granules for tablets; also granulation and coating in the same machine (fluid bed processor).

**Merits:** fast drying; uniform temperature in the bed; short time, so mild on drugs; large capacity; free-flowing granules; can granulate and coat as well.

**Demerits:** turbulent motion breaks fragile granules and makes fines; static charge can build up and cause dust explosions (must be earthed); sticky or very fine materials do not fluidise well; high air flow cost.

### Vacuum dryer (vacuum oven)

**Principle:** under **reduced pressure** water boils at a lower temperature, so material dries at low temperature. Heat is supplied by **conduction** from heated shelves.

**Construction:** a jacketed cast-iron or steel **chamber** with a tight door, containing **hollow shelves** through which steam or hot water flows. Trays of material rest on the shelves. A **condenser** and **vacuum pump** are connected to the chamber.

**Working:** material is loaded on trays, the door sealed and vacuum applied; shelves are heated; vapour is drawn off to the condenser.

**Uses:** thermolabile and easily oxidised drugs; hygroscopic materials; recovery of solvents; drying of toxic substances (closed system); dry extracts.

**Merits:** low temperature; no oxygen, so no oxidation; solvent can be recovered; closed system; uniform product.

**Demerits:** batch process with low capacity; expensive; slow because heat transfer by conduction is poor under vacuum; danger of overheating in contact with hot shelves.

### Freeze dryer (lyophilisation)

**Principle:** the material is **frozen**, then water is removed by **sublimation** (ice → vapour directly) under high vacuum. This is possible below the **triple point of water** (about 0.01 °C and 4.6 mm Hg). The product is never heated much, so even very sensitive materials survive.

**Construction:** a **drying chamber** with temperature-controlled **shelves** (which can cool and heat); a **condenser** held at a very low temperature to trap water vapour as ice; a **vacuum pump**; refrigeration system; controls.

**Stages of working:**

1. **Freezing:** the solution (often in vials) is frozen below its eutectic (or collapse) temperature so all of it is solid.
2. **Primary drying:** vacuum is applied and the shelves are warmed gently to supply the latent heat of sublimation; ice sublimes and condenses on the cold condenser. This removes most of the water.
3. **Secondary drying:** shelf temperature is raised further to remove bound (adsorbed) water by desorption, down to a low residual moisture.
4. Vials are stoppered under vacuum or nitrogen.

```text
 [chamber + shelves] ──vapour──► [condenser −ve temp] ──► vacuum pump
   frozen product         ice collects here
```

**Uses:** antibiotics (penicillin), vaccines, sera and blood plasma, enzymes and proteins, hormones, injectable powders, some plant extracts and foods.

**Merits:** suitable for very heat-sensitive materials; product is porous and dissolves quickly (easy reconstitution); low moisture gives long shelf life; can be done aseptically; little loss of volatile components.

**Demerits:** very expensive equipment and running cost; long cycle (often a day or more); product is hygroscopic and needs airtight packing; skilled operation.

## 3.2 Mixing

**Mixing** is a unit operation in which two or more components are put together so that each particle lies as close as possible to a particle of every other component. A **perfect mix** is one in which every sample has the same composition; in practice we aim for a **random mix**.

### Objectives and applications

1. Uniform content of drug in tablets, capsules and powders (very important for low-dose potent drugs).
2. Mixing drug with excipients before granulation; mixing lubricants with granules.
3. Preparing solutions, syrups, suspensions and emulsions.
4. Preparing ointments, creams and pastes.
5. Speeding up heat transfer and reactions in vessels.

### Factors affecting mixing

| Factor | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Particle size | Large differences cause segregation; fine particles may stick |
| Particle shape | Irregular particles interlock and mix poorly; spheres flow but segregate easily |
| Density | Dense particles sink to the bottom |
| Proportion of ingredients | Small amounts are hard to spread; use geometric (stepwise) dilution |
| Electrostatic charge | Charged particles cling to walls or each other |
| Moisture and stickiness | Causes lumps |
| Volume filled in the mixer | Overfilling leaves no room for movement (often about half to two-thirds full) |
| Mixing time | Too short gives poor mixing; too long may cause segregation or break granules |
| Speed and type of mixer | Must suit the material |

### Difference between solid and liquid mixing

| Solid (powder) mixing | Liquid mixing |
| --- | --- |
| No natural diffusion; mixing only by mechanical movement | Molecular diffusion helps |
| Mixed powders can segregate again | True solutions stay mixed once mixed |
| Mechanisms: convective, shear, diffusive | Mechanisms: bulk transport, turbulent, laminar, molecular diffusion |
| Equipment: tumblers, ribbon blender | Equipment: propellers, turbines, paddles |
| Result judged by statistical sampling | Usually uniform by eye or simple test |

### Mechanism of solid mixing

1. **Convective mixing:** groups of particles move from one place to another (by blades, ribbons or tumbling). Mixes on a large scale quickly.
2. **Shear mixing:** layers of particles slip over each other along slip planes, mixing at the boundaries.
3. **Diffusive mixing:** individual particles move randomly over freshly exposed surfaces as the bed tumbles. Mixes on the fine scale; slow.

**Segregation (demixing)** is the opposite: particles separate by **size** (fine particles fall through gaps between coarse ones), **density** and **shape**. It is reduced by keeping particle sizes similar, adding a little moisture or binder, and not over-mixing.

### Mechanism of liquid mixing

1. **Bulk transport:** large portions of liquid are moved around by the impeller.
2. **Turbulent mixing:** eddies of many sizes break up the liquid; the main mechanism in low-viscosity liquids.
3. **Laminar mixing:** in viscous liquids, layers are stretched and folded (shear) without turbulence.
4. **Molecular diffusion:** final mixing at the molecular level.

### Mechanism of semisolid mixing

Ointments and pastes are too stiff to flow. They are mixed by **shearing, kneading, folding and rolling**: the mass is cut, compressed, spread and folded again (sigma blade mixer, planetary mixer). Final smoothness is obtained by a roller mill.

### Double cone blender

**Principle:** **tumbling** (diffusive mixing). Powder falls and slides over itself as the vessel rotates.

**Construction:** two cones joined at their wide ends, with a short cylinder between, made of stainless steel. It is mounted on a horizontal shaft through the middle and rotated by a motor. Openings at the end for loading and unloading. Fill about 50–60% of volume.

```text
          ╱╲
         ╱  ╲
  ═══●═══    ═══●═══   ← rotates about horizontal axis
         ╲  ╱
          ╲╱
```

**Working:** the blender rotates at a low speed; powder tumbles from one cone to the other, creating fresh surfaces; mixing takes several minutes.

**Uses:** mixing of dry free-flowing powders and granules; adding lubricants to granules.

**Merits:** gentle (does not break granules); easy to load, empty and clean; no dead spots; closed, little dust.

**Demerits:** not for sticky or cohesive powders; poor for powders of very different size or density (segregation); large headroom for rotation.

### Twin shell blender (V-blender)

**Principle:** tumbling. The powder splits into two at each turn and joins again, giving repeated division and recombination.

**Construction:** two cylinders joined in a **V shape**, rotating about a horizontal axis. Some models have an **intensifier bar** (a high-speed bar with blades) inside to break lumps or add liquid.

**Working:** as the V rotates, the powder moves into the two arms and then back together into the apex.

**Uses:** dry powders and granules; lubricant blending; with intensifier bar, can mix cohesive powders and add small amounts of liquid.

**Merits:** efficient, gentle, easy to clean; large capacities. **Demerits:** segregation with very different particle sizes; high headroom; not for sticky material without the bar.

### Ribbon blender

**Principle:** **convective** mixing. Ribbons move powder in opposite directions along the trough.

**Construction:** a horizontal U-shaped **trough** with a central shaft carrying two **helical ribbons**: the outer ribbon moves material one way, the inner ribbon the other way. Discharge valve at the bottom.

```text
  ┌──────────────────────────────┐
  │  ∿∿∿∿∿∿∿∿∿∿∿∿∿∿∿∿∿∿∿∿∿∿∿∿∿   │ ← outer ribbon (→)
  │════════ shaft ════════════════│
  │  ∽∽∽∽∽∽∽∽∽∽∽∽∽∽∽∽∽∽∽∽∽∽∽∽∽   │ ← inner ribbon (←)
   ╲____________________________╱  U-trough
```

**Uses:** dry powders, granules, and mixing small amounts of liquid into powders; preparing premixes.

**Merits:** large capacity; little headroom; works with cohesive powders better than tumblers.

**Demerits:** can damage fragile granules; dead spots at ends; harder to clean; poor at fine-scale mixing.

### Sigma blade mixer

**Principle:** **shearing and kneading** by two heavy blades.

**Construction:** a double trough with two **Σ-shaped (sigma) blades** that rotate towards each other at **different speeds** (e.g. one about twice the other). The trough can be tilted for emptying; may be jacketed.

**Working:** the blades pull, fold and compress the wet mass against each other and the trough wall.

**Uses:** **wet granulation** (mixing powder with granulating fluid), kneading of pastes, ointments, heavy doughy masses.

**Merits:** strong shearing; small dead space; good for very viscous materials. **Demerits:** high power; fixed speed in simple models; produces heat.

### Planetary mixer

**Principle:** the blade moves in a **planetary motion**: it spins about its own axis and also travels around the bowl, so it reaches every part of the bowl.

**Construction:** a removable stainless steel bowl; a vertical shaft carrying a blade (beater, whisk or hook) moved by gears in planetary fashion; variable speed.

**Uses:** wet granulation, creams and ointments, pastes, mixing small batches.

**Merits:** no dead spots; speed variable; bowl removable for easy cleaning. **Demerits:** batch, small capacity; mechanical heat; high power for stiff masses.

### Propellers

**Principle:** an impeller with angled blades (like a ship's propeller) pushes liquid **axially** (parallel to the shaft), creating strong circulation and turbulence.

**Construction:** usually three blades on a shaft, rotating at high speed; mounted at an angle or off-centre in the vessel to avoid a vortex; baffles may be fitted.

**Uses:** low-viscosity liquids; solutions and dispersing suspended solids.

**Merits:** high flow, cheap, portable; good for large tanks. **Demerits:** not for viscous liquids; a central vortex draws air if mounted centrally without baffles.

### Turbines

**Principle:** flat or curved blades on a disc throw liquid outward **radially**; strong shear at the blade tips.

**Construction:** a disc with several blades (flat, curved or pitched), on a central shaft, rotating at moderate speed; baffles prevent swirling. Diameter about one-third of the vessel.

**Uses:** liquids of low to medium viscosity, dispersing gases, emulsions, dissolving solids.

**Merits:** high shear and turbulence; good for a wide viscosity range. **Demerits:** more power; can form a vortex without baffles; costly.

### Paddles

**Principle:** wide flat blades rotate slowly and push liquid **tangentially (round and round)**, with little vertical movement.

**Construction:** two or four flat blades on a vertical shaft; diameter large (half to most of the vessel width); low speed. **Anchor** paddles fit closely to the wall.

**Uses:** viscous liquids and semisolids, preventing deposits on heated walls, gentle mixing (e.g. preparing syrups, ointment bases in jacketed kettles).

**Merits:** simple, cheap, gentle. **Demerits:** poor vertical mixing; liquid tends to swirl as a mass; slow.

### Silverson emulsifier (high-shear rotor–stator mixer)

**Principle:** a high-speed **rotor** turns inside a close-fitting **stator** (a perforated or slotted screen). Liquid is sucked into the head, thrown out at high speed through the stator holes, and torn apart by intense shear.

**Construction:** a motor-driven shaft with a four-bladed rotor at its tip, surrounded by a stationary workhead with holes or slots; can be immersed in a vessel or mounted in-line.

**Working:** the rotor sucks material into the workhead; it is subjected to mechanical shear in the gap between rotor and stator, then forced out at high speed through the stator holes into the vessel, while fresh material is drawn in.

**Uses:** emulsions, creams and lotions, fine suspensions, dissolving gums, homogenising.

**Merits:** very fine droplet size and stable emulsions; fast; versatile workheads. **Demerits:** produces heat; not for very viscous masses; wear of rotor and stator.

## Remember

- EMC = moisture when the solid's vapour pressure equals that of the air; free moisture = total − EMC.
- Drying curve: initial → constant rate → critical MC → first falling → second falling → EMC.
- Spray dryer and drum dryer = rapid, thermolabile; freeze dryer = most sensitive (sublimation below the triple point).
- FBD: fast, uniform, but earth it against static and dust explosion.
- Solid mixing: convective, shear, diffusive; segregation by size, density, shape.
- Tumblers (double cone, V-blender) = gentle, dry powders; ribbon = convective; sigma blade and planetary = wet masses.
- Propeller = axial flow, thin liquids; turbine = radial, high shear; paddle = tangential, viscous; Silverson = rotor–stator, emulsions.

# Angle of repose and the influence of a lubricant

**Aim:** To determine the angle of repose of a given powder or granules and to study the effect of different concentrations of a lubricant (magnesium stearate or talc) on it.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ.

## Principle

When a powder is allowed to flow freely onto a flat surface, it forms a cone. The **angle of repose (θ)** is the maximum angle between the sloping surface of the cone and the horizontal. It reflects inter-particle friction and cohesion: the more cohesive the powder, the steeper the cone.

**tan θ = h / r**, so θ = tan⁻¹(h/r)

where h = height of the cone and r = radius of its base.

Small amounts of a lubricant or glidant coat the particles, reduce friction between them and improve flow, so θ falls. Beyond an optimum concentration, excess fine lubricant may make the powder cohesive again or fill spaces between particles, so θ may stop falling or rise slightly.

**Interpretation (USP scale):** 25–30° excellent, 31–35° good, 36–40° fair, 41–45° passable, 46–55° poor, higher values very poor.

## Requirements

Powder or granules (for example lactose, a granulated blend or microcrystalline cellulose), magnesium stearate (or talc), funnel fixed on a stand, graph paper or a flat board, ruler or scale, balance, mortar and pestle or polybag for blending, spatula.

## Procedure (fixed funnel method)

1. Fix a funnel on a stand so its tip is at a fixed height (for example 2–3 cm) above a sheet of graph paper on a level surface.
2. Pour the plain powder gently through the funnel until the apex of the cone just touches the funnel tip. Stop pouring.
3. Mark the circumference of the base at several points; measure the diameter in at least three directions and take the mean radius r. The height h is the distance from the paper to the funnel tip.
4. Calculate θ = tan⁻¹(h/r). Repeat three times.
5. Prepare blends of the powder with 0.5%, 1% and 2% w/w magnesium stearate by gentle mixing for a fixed time (over-mixing coats particles excessively).
6. Determine the angle of repose of each blend in the same way.
7. Plot angle of repose against lubricant concentration and identify the optimum.

## Observations and calculations

Base radius kept for comparison (example): r = 4.0 cm.

| Magnesium stearate (% w/w) | h (cm) | r (cm) | tan θ = h/r | θ (degrees) | Flow |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 2.6 | 4.0 | 0.650 | 33.0 | Good |
| 0.5 | 2.2 | 4.0 | 0.550 | 28.8 | Excellent |
| 1.0 | 2.0 | 4.0 | 0.500 | 26.6 | Excellent |
| 2.0 | 2.1 | 4.0 | 0.525 | 27.7 | Excellent |

**Worked calculation.** For the plain powder: tan θ = 2.6/4.0 = 0.650; θ = tan⁻¹ 0.650 = **33.0°**. With 1% magnesium stearate: tan θ = 0.500, θ = **26.6°**.

In this example the angle fell from 33.0° to 26.6° as lubricant rose to 1%, then rose slightly at 2%: the optimum is about 1%.

## Result

1. Angle of repose of the plain powder = ___°, indicating ___ flow.
2. The angle of repose decreased with lubricant up to ___% (θ = ___°), beyond which it ___. The optimum concentration of lubricant is ___%.

## Precautions

1. Keep the funnel height and pouring rate constant for all samples.
2. Pour gently; do not tap or disturb the cone.
3. The surface must be level and free from vibration.
4. Blend the lubricant for the same short time each time; prolonged mixing changes its effect.
5. Measure the base diameter in several directions.

## Viva questions

1. **Define angle of repose.** The maximum angle between the surface of a pile of powder and the horizontal plane.
2. **Write the formula.** tan θ = h/r.
3. **What angle indicates excellent flow?** About 25–30°.
4. **Why does a lubricant lower the angle of repose?** It coats the particles and reduces friction between them.
5. **Why may too much lubricant worsen flow?** Excess fine, cohesive lubricant particles stick together and fill voids; magnesium stearate in excess also softens tablets and slows dissolution.
6. **Difference between glidant and lubricant?** A glidant (colloidal silica, talc) improves powder flow; a lubricant (magnesium stearate) reduces friction between the tablet and the die wall; magnesium stearate shows both effects at low levels.
7. **Name two other methods of assessing flow.** Carr's index and Hausner ratio; flow rate through an orifice.

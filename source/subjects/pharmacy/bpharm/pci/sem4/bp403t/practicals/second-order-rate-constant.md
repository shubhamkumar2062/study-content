# Rate constant of a second-order reaction (saponification of ethyl acetate)

**Aim:** To determine the rate constant of the saponification of ethyl acetate by sodium hydroxide (equal initial concentrations) and to show that the reaction is second order.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. The readings below are example values to show the calculation.

## Principle

Ethyl acetate reacts with sodium hydroxide:

CH3COOC2H5 + NaOH → CH3COONa + C2H5OH

The rate depends on the concentrations of both ester and hydroxide, so the reaction is **second order**. When both start at the same concentration a:

**k = (1/at) × x/(a − x)**, or 1/(a − x) − 1/a = kt

where x = amount reacted at time t. A plot of 1/(a − x) against t is a straight line with slope k.

**Following the reaction by back titration.** A sample is run into a known excess of standard HCl, which neutralises the remaining NaOH and stops the reaction. The excess HCl is titrated with standard NaOH. As the reaction proceeds, less NaOH remains, more HCl is left over, so the back-titre Vt rises. In terms of titres:

- V0 = back-titre at zero time; V∞ = back-titre at completion
- (V∞ − V0) ∝ a; (Vt − V0) ∝ x; (V∞ − Vt) ∝ (a − x)

So **k = (1/at) × (Vt − V0)/(V∞ − Vt)**, with a in mol/L.

## Requirements

0.04 M ethyl acetate solution (freshly prepared), 0.04 N NaOH (carbonate free), 0.02 N HCl, 0.02 N NaOH for back titration (standardised), phenolphthalein, two 250 ml stoppered flasks, 10 ml and 20 ml pipettes, burette, conical flasks, stopwatch, water bath (25 °C).

## Procedure

1. Place 50 ml of 0.04 M ethyl acetate and 50 ml of 0.04 N NaOH in separate stoppered flasks in the water bath at 25 °C for 10 minutes.
2. Mix them quickly and start the stopwatch. The concentration of each reactant in the mixture is now a = 0.02 M.
3. Pipette 20 ml of 0.02 N HCl into each of several conical flasks in advance.
4. At 5, 10, 15, 20 and 30 minutes, withdraw 10 ml of the reaction mixture and run it into one of the HCl flasks (note the exact time of half-delivery).
5. Titrate the excess HCl with 0.02 N NaOH using phenolphthalein (Vt).
6. **V0:** titrate a blank prepared by adding 10 ml of a mixture of 5 ml 0.04 N NaOH and 5 ml water (same NaOH content as the reaction mixture at zero time) to 20 ml HCl; or calculate it. In this design V0 = 10.0 ml.
7. **V∞:** warm a portion of the reaction mixture in a stoppered flask at about 50–60 °C for 30 minutes (or leave overnight) so the reaction is complete; cool and titrate 10 ml in the same way. In this design V∞ = 20.0 ml (no NaOH left, so all the HCl is in excess).
8. Calculate k at each time and plot 1/(a − x) against t.

## Observations and calculations

a = 0.02 M; V0 = 10.0 ml; V∞ = 20.0 ml. (a − x) = a × (V∞ − Vt)/(V∞ − V0).

| Time t (min) | Vt (ml) | Vt − V0 (∝ x) | V∞ − Vt (∝ a − x) | k = (1/at)(Vt − V0)/(V∞ − Vt) (M⁻¹ min⁻¹) | 1/(a − x) (M⁻¹) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 10.0 | 0 | 10.0 | | 50.0 |
| 5 | 13.3 | 3.3 | 6.7 | 4.93 | 74.6 |
| 10 | 15.0 | 5.0 | 5.0 | 5.00 | 100.0 |
| 15 | 16.0 | 6.0 | 4.0 | 5.00 | 125.0 |
| 20 | 16.7 | 6.7 | 3.3 | 5.08 | 151.5 |
| 30 | 17.5 | 7.5 | 2.5 | 5.00 | 200.0 |

**Worked calculation (t = 10 min).** k = [1/(0.02 × 10)] × 5.0/5.0 = 5 × 1 = **5.0 M⁻¹ min⁻¹**.

The constant k (mean ≈ **5.0 M⁻¹ min⁻¹**) and the straight line of 1/(a − x) against t (slope ≈ 5) confirm second-order kinetics. Half-life = 1/(ka) = 1/(5.0 × 0.02) = **10 min**; at t = 10 min exactly half has reacted (Vt − V0 = V∞ − Vt), as expected.

## Result

The saponification of ethyl acetate by NaOH is second order. The rate constant at ___ °C is ___ M⁻¹ min⁻¹ and the half-life (a = ___ M) is ___ min.

## Precautions

1. Use carbonate-free NaOH and freshly prepared ethyl acetate solution.
2. Bring both solutions to the bath temperature before mixing; keep the temperature constant.
3. Note the time accurately at half-delivery of each sample into the acid.
4. Use the same indicator and end point in every titration.
5. Keep flasks stoppered to prevent loss of ester and absorption of CO2.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is this a second-order reaction?** The rate depends on the concentrations of both ethyl acetate and hydroxide ions.
2. **Units of a second-order rate constant?** Concentration⁻¹ time⁻¹ (M⁻¹ min⁻¹).
3. **How does the half-life depend on concentration?** t½ = 1/(ka); it is inversely proportional to the initial concentration.
4. **Why is the sample run into excess HCl?** The acid neutralises the remaining NaOH and stops the reaction instantly; the excess acid is then measured.
5. **Which plot is linear for a second-order reaction?** 1/(a − x) against t.
6. **What would happen if the concentrations were unequal?** The equation k = [2.303/(t(a − b))] log[b(a − x)/(a(b − x))] is used.
7. **Why use carbonate-free NaOH?** Carbonate reacts differently with the acid and indicator and causes error.

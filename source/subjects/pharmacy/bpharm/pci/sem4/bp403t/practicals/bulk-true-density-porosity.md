# Bulk density, true density and porosity

**Aim:** To determine the bulk density, tapped density and true density of a given powder and to calculate its porosity, bulkiness, Carr's index and Hausner ratio.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Organic liquids such as xylene are flammable and harmful: use them in a fume hood with gloves.

## Principle

- **Bulk density (ρb)** = mass / bulk volume, where bulk volume includes the solid, its pores and the voids between particles. It is found by gently pouring the powder into a graduated cylinder.
- **Tapped density (ρtap)** = mass / volume after tapping to constant volume.
- **True density (ρt)** = mass / volume of the solid alone. It is found by liquid displacement in a specific gravity bottle (pycnometer) using a liquid in which the powder is insoluble and which wets it:

  Mass of liquid displaced = W1 + W − W2, so volume of powder = (W1 + W − W2)/ρL

  **ρt = W × ρL / (W1 + W − W2)**

  where W = mass of powder, W1 = bottle filled with liquid, W2 = bottle with powder and filled with liquid, ρL = density of the liquid.

- **Porosity ε = 1 − ρb/ρt** (× 100 for %); **bulkiness = 1/ρb**.
- **Carr's index = (ρtap − ρb)/ρtap × 100**; **Hausner ratio = ρtap/ρb**.

## Requirements

Powder (for example lactose, calcium carbonate, talc), 100 ml graduated cylinder, tapped density apparatus (or tapping by hand on a pad from a fixed height), specific gravity bottle (25 or 50 ml), a non-solvent liquid of known density (for example xylene, liquid paraffin or kerosene, depending on the powder), balance, funnel, thermometer.

## Procedure

**A. Bulk and tapped density**

1. Weigh accurately about 25 g of the powder (passed through a coarse sieve to break lumps).
2. Pour it gently through a funnel into a dry 100 ml graduated cylinder without compacting. Level the surface without pressing and read the bulk volume.
3. Tap the cylinder from a fixed height (or on the tapping apparatus, for example 500 taps, then another 750) until the volume no longer changes. Read the tapped volume.
4. Repeat three times and take the mean.

**B. True density (liquid displacement)**

5. Clean, dry and weigh the specific gravity bottle (W0).
6. Fill with the liquid, insert the stopper, wipe dry and weigh (W1). Note the temperature; obtain ρL (or determine it from W1 − W0 and the bottle volume).
7. Empty and dry the bottle. Add about 2 g of powder accurately weighed (W).
8. Add the liquid to cover the powder; remove trapped air by gentle swirling (or under vacuum). Fill to the brim, insert the stopper, wipe and weigh (W2).
9. Calculate true density and then porosity and the other indices.

## Precautions

1. Pour the powder gently and do not press or tap while measuring bulk volume.
2. Tap from the same height and count taps for reproducible tapped volume.
3. The liquid for true density must neither dissolve nor swell the powder, and must wet it well.
4. Remove all air bubbles from the powder in the bottle.
5. Keep the temperature constant; liquid density changes with temperature.

## Viva questions

1. **Define porosity.** Fraction of the bulk volume of a powder bed that is void space: ε = 1 − ρb/ρt.
2. **Which density is always the highest?** True density, because it excludes all voids and pores.
3. **Why is helium used in modern pycnometers?** Its small atoms penetrate the finest pores, giving the true solid volume.
4. **What is bulkiness?** The reciprocal of bulk density; it rises as particle size falls.
5. **What does a Carr's index of 10% or less mean?** Excellent flow.
6. **What is granule density and how is it measured?** Density including intra-granular pores but excluding inter-granular voids; mercury displacement.
7. **Why does tapping reduce volume?** Particles rearrange into a closer packing and inter-particle voids decrease.

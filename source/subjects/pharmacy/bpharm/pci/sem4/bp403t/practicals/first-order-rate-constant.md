# Rate constant of a first-order reaction (acid hydrolysis of methyl acetate)

**Aim:** To determine the rate constant of the acid-catalysed hydrolysis of methyl acetate and to show that the reaction follows first-order (pseudo-first-order) kinetics.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. The readings below are example values to show the calculation. Methyl acetate is volatile and flammable: keep flasks stoppered and away from flames.

## Principle

Methyl acetate is hydrolysed by water in the presence of hydrochloric acid, which acts as a catalyst:

CH3COOCH3 + H2O → CH3COOH + CH3OH (H⁺ catalyst)

Water is present in large excess and the HCl concentration does not change, so the rate depends only on the ester concentration: **pseudo-first-order** kinetics.

The progress is followed by titrating samples of the mixture with standard NaOH. The titre includes the constant HCl plus the acetic acid formed:

- V0 = titre at zero time (HCl only)
- Vt = titre at time t
- V∞ = titre at completion (HCl + acetic acid from all the ester)

Then (V∞ − V0) is proportional to the initial ester concentration a, and (V∞ − Vt) to the ester left (a − x), so:

**k = (2.303/t) log [(V∞ − V0)/(V∞ − Vt)]**

A plot of log (V∞ − Vt) against t is a straight line with slope −k/2.303.

## Requirements

Methyl acetate, 0.5 N HCl, 0.2 N NaOH (standardised), phenolphthalein, ice-cold distilled water, 250 ml stoppered conical flask, 5 ml pipettes, burette, conical flasks, stopwatch, thermostatic water bath (25 °C).

## Procedure

1. Place 100 ml of 0.5 N HCl in a stoppered flask and keep it in the water bath at 25 °C for 10 minutes. Keep methyl acetate at the same temperature.
2. Add 4 ml of methyl acetate to the acid, shake well and start the stopwatch (when half the ester has been added).
3. Immediately withdraw 5 ml of the mixture and run it into about 20–25 ml of ice-cold water in a conical flask (cooling and dilution practically stop the reaction). Titrate with 0.2 N NaOH using phenolphthalein. This is V0.
4. Withdraw further 5 ml samples at 10, 20, 30, 45, 60 and 90 minutes; quench and titrate each in the same way (Vt).
5. For V∞, heat about 25 ml of the reaction mixture in a stoppered flask in a water bath at about 60 °C for about an hour (or leave it for 24 hours) to complete the hydrolysis; cool to room temperature and titrate 5 ml.
6. Calculate k for each time and plot log (V∞ − Vt) against t.

## Observations and calculations

V0 = 12.0 ml; V∞ = 24.0 ml; V∞ − V0 = 12.0 ml.

| Time t (min) | Vt (ml) | V∞ − Vt (ml) | (V∞ − V0)/(V∞ − Vt) | log of ratio | k = (2.303/t) × log (min⁻¹) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 12.0 | 12.0 | 1.000 | 0 | |
| 10 | 12.6 | 11.4 | 1.053 | 0.0223 | 5.13 × 10⁻³ |
| 20 | 13.1 | 10.9 | 1.101 | 0.0418 | 4.81 × 10⁻³ |
| 30 | 13.7 | 10.3 | 1.165 | 0.0663 | 5.09 × 10⁻³ |
| 45 | 14.4 | 9.6 | 1.250 | 0.0969 | 4.96 × 10⁻³ |
| 60 | 15.1 | 8.9 | 1.348 | 0.1298 | 4.98 × 10⁻³ |
| 90 | 16.3 | 7.7 | 1.558 | 0.1927 | 4.93 × 10⁻³ |

**Worked calculation (t = 45 min).** k = (2.303/45) × log (12.0/9.6) = 0.05118 × 0.0969 = **4.96 × 10⁻³ min⁻¹**.

The values of k are nearly constant (mean **≈ 5.0 × 10⁻³ min⁻¹**), confirming first-order kinetics. Half-life = 0.693/k = 0.693/0.0050 ≈ **139 min**.

## Precautions

1. Keep the temperature constant throughout; k changes with temperature.
2. Note times exactly; quench each sample immediately in ice-cold water.
3. Titrate quickly and to the same faint pink end point.
4. Keep the reaction flask stoppered to prevent loss of volatile ester.
5. Make sure the reaction is truly complete for V∞.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is this called pseudo-first order?** Two reactants (ester and water) take part, but water is in great excess, so its concentration is constant and the rate depends only on the ester.
2. **What is the role of HCl?** It is a catalyst (specific acid catalysis); it is not consumed.
3. **Why is V0 not zero?** The titre at zero time is due to the HCl catalyst.
4. **Why are samples added to ice-cold water?** Cooling and dilution practically stop the reaction so the titre corresponds to the sampling time.
5. **What are the units of a first-order rate constant?** Time⁻¹ (min⁻¹ here).
6. **Does the half-life depend on initial concentration?** No; t½ = 0.693/k for a first-order reaction.
7. **How is V∞ obtained?** By heating a portion of the mixture to complete the hydrolysis (or leaving it long enough) and titrating.

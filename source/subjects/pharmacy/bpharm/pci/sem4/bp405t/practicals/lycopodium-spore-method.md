# Determination of number of starch grains by the lycopodium spore method

**Aim:** To determine the number of starch grains per mg of the given powdered drug (for example powdered ginger) and its purity by the lycopodium spore method.

> Follow your lab manual for the amounts and the suspending liquid. Work out the purity only if a standard (genuine) sample has been counted the same way.

## Principle

Lycopodium spores (from *Lycopodium clavatum*) are very uniform in size and shape, and **1 mg contains about 94,000 spores**. A known weight of lycopodium is mixed with a known weight of the powdered drug, and the spores serve as an internal standard. By counting, in the same fields, the spores and a characteristic countable element of the drug (starch grains, oil cells, pollen grains), the number of such elements in 1 mg of the drug is found:

**Number of starch grains per mg = (N × W × 94,000) / (S × M)**

where:
- N = starch grains counted,
- S = lycopodium spores counted in the same fields,
- W = weight of lycopodium (mg),
- M = weight of drug (mg).

**% purity = (starch grains per mg of sample / starch grains per mg of genuine drug) × 100**

## Requirements

Powdered drug, lycopodium spores (dried), mucilage of tragacanth or a suitable suspending liquid, glycerin, water, mortar and pestle, analytical balance, slides, cover slips (large), microscope.

## Procedure

1. Accurately weigh about 100 mg of the powdered drug (M) and about 50 mg of lycopodium (W).
2. Mix them thoroughly in a mortar with a little mucilage, then dilute with water or glycerin–water to a known volume, so the suspension is uniform.
3. Place one drop of the well-shaken suspension on a slide, cover with a large cover slip, and examine under the microscope.
4. In each of at least 25 fields, count the lycopodium spores (S) and the starch grains (N) of the drug.
5. Total the counts over all fields and calculate.

**Worked example.** M = 100 mg drug, W = 50 mg lycopodium. Over 25 fields, S = 400 spores and N = 1,000 starch grains.

Starch grains per mg = (1,000 × 50 × 94,000) / (400 × 100) = 4,700,000,000 / 40,000 = **117,500 per mg**.

If the genuine drug gives 125,000 per mg, purity = 117,500/125,000 × 100 = **94%**.

## Precautions

1. Weigh both the drug and lycopodium accurately.
2. Mix thoroughly so that the suspension is uniform; shake before taking each drop.
3. Count enough fields (at least 25) to reduce error.
4. Use a fixed rule for objects on the edge of the field.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is lycopodium used as the standard?** Its spores are very uniform in size and shape and of known number per mg.
2. **How many spores are there in 1 mg of lycopodium?** About 94,000.
3. **What shape are lycopodium spores?** Tetrahedral with a reticulate (net-like) surface, about 25 µm across.
4. **For what kind of drugs is this method used?** Powdered drugs where a characteristic element can be counted, such as starch grains in ginger, oil cells or pollen grains.
5. **What else can the method measure?** The proportion of an adulterant, if the adulterant has a countable feature (e.g., foreign starch).
6. **Why not simply weigh the starch?** Starch cannot be separated quantitatively from the powder; counting against a standard is easier and accurate.

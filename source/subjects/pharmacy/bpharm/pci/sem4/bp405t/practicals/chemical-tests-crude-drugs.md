# Analysis of crude drugs by chemical tests: tragacanth, acacia, agar, gelatin, starch, honey and castor oil

**Aim:** To identify tragacanth, acacia, agar, gelatin, starch, honey and castor oil by their characteristic chemical tests.

> Follow your lab manual and your teacher where details differ. Benzidine is a carcinogen: many laboratories now skip the benzidine test or use it only as a demonstration, wearing gloves.

## Principle

Unorganised drugs have no cells to examine under the microscope, so they are identified by physical behaviour (swelling, gel formation, solubility) and by chemical reactions of their main constituents:
- polysaccharides (gums, agar, starch) react with iodine, lead salts and dyes such as ruthenium red;
- proteins (gelatin) are precipitated by tannic acid and picric acid and give ammonia on heating with soda lime;
- reducing sugars (honey) reduce Fehling's solution, and fructose gives Seliwanoff's test;
- castor oil is identified by its unusual solubility in alcohol.

## Requirements

**Drugs:** samples of tragacanth, acacia, agar, gelatin, starch (maize or potato), honey and castor oil.

**Reagents:**
- iodine solution (0.1 N) and lead subacetate solution;
- ruthenium red solution and 20% sodium hydroxide solution;
- tannic acid solution (10%) and saturated picric acid solution;
- soda lime, Fehling's solutions A and B, and Seliwanoff's reagent (resorcinol in HCl);
- ethanol (95%) and light petroleum.

**Apparatus:** test tubes, water bath, beakers, glass rods, slides and microscope.

## Procedure and observations

| Drug | Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Tragacanth** | Add 1 g powder to 20 ml water and allow to stand | Swells to a stiff, translucent gel | Bassorin swells |
|  | Mount powder in iodine solution and examine | A few small blue starch grains | Traces of starch |
|  | Boil a little powder with 20% NaOH | Canary-yellow colour | Tragacanth |
|  | Mount powder in ruthenium red | Particles stain pink | Mucilage |
| **Acacia** | Dissolve 1 g in 2 ml water | Dissolves slowly to a viscous, clear solution | Water-soluble gum |
|  | Add lead subacetate to the solution | White precipitate | Arabin (polysaccharide) |
|  | Hydrolyse with dilute acid, neutralise, warm with Fehling's | Brick-red precipitate | Reducing sugars after hydrolysis |
|  | (Demonstration) solution + alcoholic benzidine + H2O2 | Blue colour | Oxidase enzyme |
| **Agar** | Boil 0.5 g in 30 ml water, cool | Clear solution sets to a firm gel | Agarose |
|  | Warm a little powder with iodine solution | Crimson to violet colour | Agar |
|  | Mount in ruthenium red | Pink | Mucilage |
|  | Add tannic acid to a warm 0.5% solution | No precipitate | Distinguishes agar from gelatin |
| **Gelatin** | Dissolve 1 g in 50 ml hot water; cool a portion | Sets to a gel | Gelatin |
|  | Add tannic acid to the warm solution | Buff/white precipitate | Protein |
|  | Add picric acid | Yellow precipitate | Protein |
|  | Heat solid gelatin with soda lime | Ammonia smell; moist red litmus turns blue | Nitrogen (protein) |
| **Starch** | Boil 1 g with 15 ml water and cool | Translucent viscous mucilage | Starch |
|  | Add iodine solution to the cooled mucilage | Deep blue; disappears on warming, returns on cooling | Starch–iodine complex |
|  | Mount powder in water and examine | Grains of typical shape and hilum | Identity of the starch source |
| **Honey** | Dilute 1 ml with 5 ml water, add Fehling's A + B, boil | Brick-red precipitate | Reducing sugars (invert sugar) |
|  | Warm with Seliwanoff's reagent | Red colour | Fructose |
|  | Fiehe's test on an ether extract with resorcinol–HCl | No cherry-red colour | Absence of artificial invert sugar |
| **Castor oil** | Shake 1 ml with 1 ml ethanol (95%) | Clear mixture | Miscible with alcohol (hydroxy fatty acid) |
|  | Shake 1 ml with 3 ml light petroleum | Turbid; does not mix completely | Only slightly soluble |
|  | Note viscosity | Very viscous | Ricinoleic acid glycerides |

## Result

The given samples were identified as tragacanth, acacia, agar, gelatin, starch, honey and castor oil by the tests above.

## Precautions

1. Use clean, dry test tubes and fresh reagents.
2. Add reagents drop by drop and note colours immediately.
3. Heat test tubes on a water bath, pointing away from yourself and others.
4. Handle benzidine and NaOH with gloves.

## Viva questions

1. **What makes tragacanth swell but not dissolve completely?** Bassorin, the water-insoluble fraction, swells to a gel; only tragacanthin dissolves.
2. **Why is acacia solution heated before use with some drugs?** To inactivate its oxidase enzyme, which can destroy easily oxidised drugs.
3. **How do you tell agar from gelatin?** Gelatin gives a precipitate with tannic acid; agar does not. Gelatin gives ammonia with soda lime; agar does not.
4. **Why does the blue starch–iodine colour vanish on warming?** Heat releases iodine from the helical amylose–iodine complex; it reforms on cooling.
5. **What does Fiehe's test detect?** Artificial invert sugar, through the hydroxymethylfurfural formed during its manufacture.
6. **Why is castor oil miscible with alcohol?** Its ricinoleic acid carries a hydroxyl group, making the oil more polar than other fixed oils.
7. **Name the two fractions of agar.** Agarose (gelling) and agaropectin (sulphated, non-gelling).

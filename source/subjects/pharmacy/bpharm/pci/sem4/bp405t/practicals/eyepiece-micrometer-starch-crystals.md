# Determination of size of starch grains and calcium oxalate crystals by eyepiece micrometer

**Aim:** To calibrate an eyepiece micrometer and to measure the size of starch grains (for example potato, maize or rice starch) and calcium oxalate crystals (for example in senna or rhubarb powder).

> Follow your lab manual for the samples used. Record the objective used for each measurement, because the calibration factor differs for each objective.

## Principle

An **eyepiece micrometer** is a glass disc with an arbitrary scale (often 100 divisions) placed in the eyepiece. Its divisions have no fixed value; their real length depends on the objective in use. It is therefore calibrated against a **stage micrometer**, a slide on which 1 mm is divided into 100 parts, each equal to 0.01 mm = **10 µm**.

When lines of both scales coincide at two points:

**Value of one eyepiece division (µm) = (number of stage divisions × 10) / (number of eyepiece divisions)**

The size of an object = number of eyepiece divisions it covers × value of one eyepiece division.

## Requirements

Compound microscope, eyepiece micrometer, stage micrometer, slides, cover slips, starch powders, drug powder containing calcium oxalate (senna, rhubarb), water, glycerin, chloral hydrate solution.

## Procedure

**A. Calibration**

1. Unscrew the eye lens, place the eyepiece micrometer on the diaphragm (scale up) and replace the lens.
2. Place the stage micrometer on the stage and focus with the low-power objective, then with the high-power objective.
3. Rotate the eyepiece so both scales are parallel. Make the zero lines coincide.
4. Find another point further along where a line of the eyepiece scale exactly coincides with a line of the stage scale.
5. Count the divisions of each scale between the two coinciding points and calculate the value of one eyepiece division.

**B. Measurement**

1. Remove the stage micrometer.
2. Mount starch in water (or dilute glycerin). Mount calcium oxalate powder cleared in chloral hydrate.
3. Measure the length (and breadth) of at least 25 starch grains or crystals in eyepiece divisions. For starch, measure the longest axis.
4. Convert to micrometres and find the range and the average.

**Worked example.** 40 eyepiece divisions coincide with 10 stage divisions.
Value of one eyepiece division = (10 × 10) / 40 = **2.5 µm**.
A starch grain covering 18 eyepiece divisions measures 18 × 2.5 = **45 µm**.

## Precautions

1. Calibrate separately for each objective and do not change the objective or the eyepiece after calibration.
2. Choose coinciding lines as far apart as possible for accuracy.
3. Measure grains lying flat, and enough of them to give a fair range.
4. Clean the stage micrometer gently; its scale is easily scratched.

## Viva questions

1. **Why must the eyepiece micrometer be calibrated?** Its divisions have no fixed value; their real size depends on the objective magnification.
2. **What is the value of one stage micrometer division?** 0.01 mm, that is 10 µm.
3. **How do you identify potato starch?** Large, oval to egg-shaped simple grains with an eccentric hilum and clear concentric striations.
4. **How do you identify maize starch?** Polygonal simple grains with a central star-shaped (stellate) hilum.
5. **How do you confirm calcium oxalate?** It is insoluble in acetic acid and dissolves in HCl without effervescence.
6. **Name the shapes of calcium oxalate crystals.** Prisms, rosettes (clusters), acicular raphides and microsphenoidal (sandy) crystals.

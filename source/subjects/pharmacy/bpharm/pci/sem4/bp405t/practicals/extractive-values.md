# Determination of extractive values of crude drugs

**Aim:** To determine the alcohol-soluble and water-soluble extractive values of the given powdered crude drug.

> Follow your lab manual or the pharmacopoeia monograph for the solvent and strength of alcohol specified for the drug. Ethanol is flammable: evaporate on a water bath, never over a naked flame.

## Principle

The **extractive value** is the amount of material extracted from a known weight of drug by a particular solvent under fixed conditions, expressed as % w/w of the air-dried drug. It gives an idea of the amount of active or characteristic constituents, especially when no chemical assay exists.
- An exhausted or adulterated drug gives a lower value.
- Water extracts sugars, gums, tannins, glycosides and salts.
- Alcohol extracts resins, alkaloids, glycosides, tannins and some fats.
- Ether (volatile and non-volatile) extracts fats, volatile oils and resins.

## Requirements

Powdered drug (coarse powder), ethanol (90% or as specified), chloroform water (water saturated with chloroform, to prevent microbial growth during maceration), 250 ml stoppered conical flasks, measuring cylinders, filter paper, funnel, pipette (25 ml), tared flat-bottomed evaporating dish, water bath, hot-air oven at 105 °C, desiccator, analytical balance.

## Procedure

**A. Alcohol-soluble extractive**

1. Accurately weigh about 5 g of coarsely powdered drug into a stoppered flask.
2. Add 100 ml of ethanol of the specified strength.
3. Macerate for 24 hours: shake frequently during the first 6 hours, then allow to stand for 18 hours.
4. Filter rapidly, taking care to avoid loss of solvent.
5. Pipette 25 ml of the filtrate into a tared flat-bottomed dish and evaporate to dryness on a water bath.
6. Dry the residue at 105 °C to constant weight, cool in a desiccator, and weigh.

**B. Water-soluble extractive**

Proceed in the same way, using 100 ml of chloroform water instead of ethanol.

## Observations and calculation

| Item | Alcohol | Water |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weight of drug (g) |  |  |
| Empty dish (g) |  |  |
| Dish + residue from 25 ml (g) |  |  |
| Weight of residue (g) |  |  |

Since 25 ml is one-quarter of the 100 ml used:

**% extractive = (weight of residue × 4 × 100) / weight of drug**

**Worked example.** 5.000 g drug macerated with 100 ml ethanol; 25 ml of filtrate gave 0.150 g residue.

% alcohol-soluble extractive = 0.150 × 4 × 100 / 5 = **12.0% w/w**

## Precautions

1. Use the correct particle size and the exact solvent strength stated for the drug.
2. Keep the flask stoppered during maceration to prevent solvent loss.
3. Filter rapidly and cover the funnel to reduce evaporation.
4. Dry the residue to constant weight and cool in a desiccator before weighing.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the significance of extractive value?** It estimates the soluble constituents of a drug and helps detect exhausted or adulterated samples.
2. **Why is chloroform water used for the water-soluble extractive?** Chloroform acts as a preservative and prevents microbial growth during the 24-hour maceration.
3. **Why shake for 6 hours and then let stand for 18 hours?** To bring the solvent and drug to equilibrium in a standard, reproducible way.
4. **What is the difference between volatile and non-volatile ether-soluble extractive?** Volatile extractive is mainly volatile oil, lost on drying; non-volatile extractive includes fixed oils and resins.
5. **Why is only 25 ml of filtrate evaporated?** It is an aliquot; the value is multiplied by 4 for the full 100 ml.
6. **Why dry at 105 °C?** To remove the solvent and moisture completely without decomposing most constituents.

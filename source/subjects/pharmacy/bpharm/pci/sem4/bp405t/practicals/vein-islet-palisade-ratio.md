# Determination of vein-islet number, vein-termination number and palisade ratio

**Aim:** To determine the vein-islet number, vein-termination number and palisade ratio of the given leaf (for example senna, datura or digitalis).

> Follow your lab manual for the leaf and clearing method. Compare results with the values listed in your manual for that drug.

## Principle

- **Vein-islet** — the smallest area of photosynthetic tissue completely surrounded by veinlets.
- **Vein-islet number** — the average number of vein-islets per square millimetre of leaf surface, counted midway between the midrib and the margin.
- **Vein-termination number** — the average number of free veinlet endings per square millimetre.
- **Palisade ratio** — the average number of palisade cells beneath one upper epidermal cell.

These values are constant for a species and do not depend on the size of the leaf. They help identify whole and broken leaves and detect substitution with related species (for example *Cassia angustifolia* and *C. acutifolia*).

## Requirements

Leaves, chloral hydrate solution, glycerin, phloroglucinol and conc. HCl (optional, to stain veins red), slides, cover slips, compound microscope, camera lucida, stage micrometer, drawing paper.

## Procedure

**A. Vein-islet and vein-termination number**

1. Cut square pieces of leaf (about 2–3 mm) from the lamina midway between midrib and margin.
2. Clear by boiling in chloral hydrate solution until transparent; stain with phloroglucinol–HCl if needed so that veins show red.
3. Mount in glycerin.
4. With the camera lucida and stage micrometer, draw a square of **2 mm × 2 mm (4 mm²)** on the paper to scale.
5. Replace with the specimen and trace the veins inside the square.
6. Count the vein-islets in the square. Count islets cut by the top and left sides; ignore those cut by the bottom and right sides.
7. Count the vein terminations inside the square.
8. Divide by 4 to get the values per mm². Repeat for several pieces and average.

**B. Palisade ratio**

1. Clear small pieces of leaf in chloral hydrate and mount with the upper surface towards the objective.
2. Using the camera lucida, trace a group of **four** upper epidermal cells.
3. Focus down to the palisade layer and trace the palisade cells lying beneath these four cells. Count a palisade cell if more than half of it lies under the group.
4. Palisade ratio = number of palisade cells ÷ 4.
5. Repeat for at least 10 groups and take the average (also note the range).

**Worked example.** In 4 mm², 96 vein-islets and 104 terminations were counted.
- Vein-islet number = 96/4 = **24 per mm²**.
- Vein-termination number = 104/4 = **26 per mm²**.

Under four epidermal cells, 30 palisade cells were counted, so palisade ratio = 30/4 = **7.5**.

## Precautions

1. Take pieces only from midway between midrib and margin.
2. Clear completely so all veinlets are visible.
3. Do not change the camera lucida setting after calibration.
4. Follow a fixed rule for counting islets on the boundary.

## Viva questions

1. **Define vein-islet.** The smallest area of green tissue surrounded on all sides by veinlets.
2. **Who introduced vein-islet number?** Levin (1929).
3. **Why are these values useful?** They are constant for a species, independent of leaf size, and work even on broken leaf pieces.
4. **Why is the square drawn as 4 mm² rather than 1 mm²?** A larger area gives a more reliable average with fewer boundary errors.
5. **Where is the palisade ratio counted?** Under the upper epidermis, in groups of four epidermal cells.
6. **Why is phloroglucinol–HCl used?** It stains the lignified xylem of veins red, making them easy to trace.

# Determination of swelling index and foaming index

**Aim:**
1. To determine the swelling index of a mucilage-containing drug (for example isapgol/ispaghula seed or husk, linseed).
2. To determine the foaming index of a saponin-containing drug (for example liquorice root, quillaia bark or ritha).

> Follow your lab manual for the drug and quantities used. Use the same type of graduated cylinder and test tubes throughout, since the shape changes the readings.

## Principle

**Swelling index** is the volume in ml occupied by 1 g of a drug, including any adhering mucilage, after it has swollen in an aqueous liquid for a fixed time.
- Mucilage-containing drugs absorb water and swell; this is the basis of their action as bulk laxatives.
- A low swelling index suggests exhausted or adulterated drug.

**Foaming index** expresses the foaming ability of a saponin-containing drug.
- Saponins lower surface tension and form a lasting froth when their aqueous decoction is shaken.
- The index is the dilution of the decoction that gives a froth 1 cm high under set conditions.

**Foaming index = 1000 / a**

where a = volume (ml) of the decoction used in the tube that gave 1 cm of foam.

## Requirements

**Swelling index:** isapgol seed or husk, 25 ml glass-stoppered graduated measuring cylinders, water, balance.

**Foaming index:**
- powdered saponin drug and water;
- 500 ml conical flask, 100 ml volumetric flask and filter paper;
- 10 stoppered test tubes of the same size (about 16 mm × 16 cm) and pipettes;
- burner or heating mantle and a ruler.

## Procedure

**A. Swelling index**

1. Accurately weigh 1 g of the drug into a 25 ml glass-stoppered measuring cylinder. Note the volume it occupies.
2. Add 25 ml of water and shake thoroughly.
3. Shake every 10 minutes for 1 hour.
4. Allow to stand for 3 hours at room temperature.
5. Read the volume (ml) occupied by the swollen drug, including the adhering mucilage.
6. Do the test in triplicate and take the mean.

**B. Foaming index**

1. Weigh 1 g of the coarsely powdered drug into a conical flask containing 100 ml of boiling water.
2. Keep at moderate boiling for 30 minutes.
3. Cool, filter into a 100 ml volumetric flask, and make up to volume with water through the filter.
4. Pour 1, 2, 3 … 10 ml of the decoction into 10 test tubes in order.
5. Make the volume in each tube up to 10 ml with water.
6. Stopper the tubes and shake each lengthwise for 15 seconds at about two shakes per second.
7. Allow to stand for 15 minutes and measure the height of the foam in each tube.
8. Find the tube in which the foam height is 1 cm. The volume of decoction (a) in that tube gives the index.

**Reading the result**
- If every tube has foam below 1 cm, the foaming index is less than 100.
- If the first tube already exceeds 1 cm, dilute the decoction further and repeat.

## Precautions

1. Use a dry cylinder and weigh the drug accurately.
2. Keep the shaking schedule exactly as stated.
3. Read the volume of swollen drug at eye level without disturbing the cylinder.
4. Use test tubes of identical size and shake all tubes in the same way.
5. Measure foam height exactly 15 minutes after shaking.

## Viva questions

1. **Why does isapgol swell?** Its seed coat contains mucilage, a polysaccharide that absorbs water and forms a gel.
2. **How does swelling explain its laxative action?** The swollen mass increases stool bulk, softens it and stimulates peristalsis.
3. **What gives a decoction its foaming property?** Saponins, which are surface-active glycosides.
4. **Give the formula for foaming index.** 1000/a, where a is ml of decoction in the tube giving 1 cm foam.
5. **What other test detects saponins?** Haemolysis of red blood cells.
6. **Why must the tubes be identical?** Foam height depends on the diameter of the tube.

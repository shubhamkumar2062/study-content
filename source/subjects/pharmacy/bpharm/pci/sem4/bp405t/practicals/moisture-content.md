# Determination of moisture content of crude drugs (loss on drying)

**Aim:** To determine the moisture content of the given powdered crude drug by the loss on drying method.

> Follow your lab manual for the weight of sample and drying temperature. For drugs rich in volatile oil, the toluene distillation method is used instead, because oven drying would also drive off the oil.

## Principle

Crude drugs always contain some water. Too much water:
- allows moulds and bacteria to grow,
- lets enzymes break down constituents (for example glycosides),
- adds useless weight.

The **loss on drying** is the loss in weight, expressed as % w/w, when the drug is dried at a fixed temperature (usually 105 °C) to constant weight. It measures water plus any other matter that is volatile at that temperature.

For drugs containing volatile oils (clove, fennel), moisture is found by **azeotropic (toluene) distillation**:
- the drug is distilled with water-immiscible toluene;
- water distils with the toluene and collects in a graduated receiver (Dean–Stark type) under the toluene layer, where its volume is read.

## Requirements

Powdered drug, tared flat glass-stoppered weighing bottle (or Petri dish), hot-air oven at 105 °C, desiccator with fresh desiccant, analytical balance, tongs.

## Procedure

1. Dry an empty weighing bottle with its stopper at 105 °C for 30 minutes, cool in a desiccator, and weigh (W1).
2. Place about 2–5 g of the powdered drug in the bottle, spread evenly, and weigh accurately (W2).
3. Dry in the oven at 105 °C with the stopper removed and kept beside the bottle.
4. After the set time (for example 2 hours), close the stopper, cool in a desiccator, and weigh.
5. Repeat drying for 30-minute periods until two successive weighings agree within 0.5 mg (constant weight) (W3).

## Observations and calculation

| Item | Weight (g) |
| --- | --- |
| Empty bottle (W1) |  |
| Bottle + drug before drying (W2) |  |
| Bottle + drug after drying to constant weight (W3) |  |

**% loss on drying = (W2 − W3) × 100 / (W2 − W1)**

**Worked example.** W1 = 25.300 g, W2 = 28.300 g (3.000 g drug), W3 = 28.060 g.
Loss = 0.240 g; % loss on drying = 0.240 × 100/3.000 = **8.0% w/w**.

## Result

The moisture content (loss on drying) of the given drug is ___% w/w.

## Precautions

1. Keep the layer of powder thin so drying is even.
2. Always cool in a desiccator with the stopper closed before weighing, since dried drug picks up moisture quickly.
3. Dry to constant weight.
4. Do not use this method for drugs rich in volatile oil.
5. Handle hot bottles with tongs; do not touch them with fingers, which adds moisture and grease.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is moisture content important?** Excess moisture favours microbial growth and enzyme action, lowers potency and adds weight.
2. **Why 105 °C?** It is just above the boiling point of water, removing water without decomposing most constituents.
3. **What does loss on drying measure besides water?** Any other matter volatile at the drying temperature.
4. **Which method is used for drugs containing volatile oil?** Toluene (azeotropic) distillation in a Dean–Stark apparatus.
5. **Name a chemical method for water content.** Karl Fischer titration, based on the reaction of iodine and sulphur dioxide with water.
6. **What is meant by constant weight?** Two successive weighings after further drying differ by not more than 0.5 mg.

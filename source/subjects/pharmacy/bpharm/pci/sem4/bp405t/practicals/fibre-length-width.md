# Determination of fibre length and width

**Aim:** To determine the length and width of fibres in the given drug or fibre sample (for example cotton, jute, or fibres from a bark such as cinchona or cinnamon).

> Follow your lab manual for the maceration method. Schultze's reagent is a strong oxidant: use it in a fume hood, away from organic matter, with gloves and goggles.

## Principle

Fibres are long, narrow, thick-walled cells. Their length and width are fairly constant for a given drug and help identify it and detect adulteration. In a tissue the fibres are held together, so they are first separated by **maceration**:
- with Schultze's reagent (potassium chlorate in nitric acid), which dissolves the middle lamella, or
- for soft tissues, by boiling with dilute alkali.

The separated fibres are measured with a calibrated eyepiece micrometer:
- length usually under low power (or by projecting with a camera lucida);
- width under high power.

## Requirements

Fibre or drug sample, potassium chlorate, conc. nitric acid (or 50% nitric acid), water, glycerin, test tubes, water bath, needles, slides, cover slips, microscope, eyepiece and stage micrometers, camera lucida.

## Procedure

1. Calibrate the eyepiece micrometer for the low-power and high-power objectives (see the eyepiece micrometer practical).
2. Cut the material into small pieces. For cotton, simply tease out a few fibres; maceration is not needed.
3. Macerate: warm the pieces with a few crystals of potassium chlorate and 50% nitric acid until the tissue softens and separates (Schultze's maceration).
4. Pour off the acid, wash the pieces several times with water, and tease them apart with needles.
5. Mount the separated fibres in dilute glycerin.
6. Measure the length of at least 25 complete (unbroken) fibres under low power. For long fibres, trace them with the camera lucida and measure the tracing against a traced stage micrometer scale.
7. Measure the width of the same fibres at the middle under high power.
8. Note the shape of the fibre ends, lumen and wall thickness, and lignification (stain with phloroglucinol–HCl).

**Worked example.** Low power: one eyepiece division = 15 µm. A fibre covers 42 divisions, so its length = 42 × 15 = **630 µm**. High power: one division = 2.5 µm; width = 10 divisions = **25 µm**.

## Precautions

1. Measure only complete fibres; broken ones give low values.
2. Do not over-macerate, or the fibres will break.
3. Wash off all the acid before mounting.
4. Calibrate for each objective used.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the purpose of maceration?** To dissolve the middle lamella so that individual fibres separate.
2. **Composition of Schultze's maceration fluid?** Potassium chlorate with nitric acid.
3. **How does cotton differ from jute under the microscope?** Cotton is a single flattened, twisted, unlignified cell; jute fibres are lignified bast cells with an irregular lumen.
4. **How do you test for lignin?** Phloroglucinol with conc. HCl stains lignified walls pink to red.
5. **Which fibre is used for absorbent cotton?** The seed hairs of *Gossypium* species.
6. **Why measure many fibres?** Fibre size varies, so a range and an average from many fibres are reliable.

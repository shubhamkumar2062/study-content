# Determination of stomatal number and stomatal index

**Aim:** To determine the stomatal number and stomatal index of the given leaf (for example senna or datura) on the upper and lower surfaces.

> Quantities and the leaf used follow your lab manual. Compare your values with those given in your manual for the drug, not with values from a different leaf.

## Principle

**Stomatal number** is the average number of stomata per square millimetre of epidermis. It changes with the age of the leaf, its position on the plant and growing conditions, so it is less reliable.

**Stomatal index (SI)** is the percentage of stomata out of the total number of epidermal cells and stomata in the same area:

**SI = S × 100 / (E + S)**

where S = number of stomata and E = number of ordinary epidermal cells in the same unit area. Since leaf area changes both E and S together, SI stays fairly constant for a species. It is used to identify a leaf drug and to separate closely related species (for example Indian and Alexandrian senna).

## Requirements

Fresh or dried leaves, chloral hydrate solution, glycerin, slides, cover slips, compound microscope, camera lucida, stage micrometer, drawing paper, sharp pencil, forceps, water bath.

## Procedure

1. Take small pieces of leaf from the middle part of the lamina, between midrib and margin.
2. Clear them by boiling with chloral hydrate solution on a water bath until transparent (or peel off the epidermis).
3. Mount the upper and lower epidermis separately in glycerin, with the surface uppermost.
4. Set up the camera lucida. Using the stage micrometer, draw a square of 1 mm × 1 mm to scale on the paper (or calibrate the field area).
5. Replace the stage micrometer with the specimen. Trace the epidermal cells and stomata falling inside the square.
6. Count the stomata (S) and the epidermal cells (E). Count a cell or stoma cut by the boundary only if more than half of it lies inside the square (or count those on two sides only).
7. Repeat for at least 10 fields on each surface and take the average.

Stomatal number = average S per mm².

**Worked example.** In one field (1 mm²) of the lower surface, E = 152 and S = 38.
SI = 38 × 100 / (152 + 38) = 3800/190 = **20.0**. The stomatal number for that field = 38 per mm².

## Precautions

1. Clear the leaf fully so cell outlines are sharp.
2. Use leaves from the middle of the lamina, avoiding the midrib and margins.
3. Keep the camera lucida setting unchanged once calibrated.
4. Count enough fields and take the average.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is stomatal index more reliable than stomatal number?** Leaf expansion changes the number per area, but stomata and epidermal cells increase together, so their ratio stays constant.
2. **Who introduced the stomatal index?** Salisbury (1927).
3. **Name the four common stomatal types with one example each.** Anomocytic (digitalis), anisocytic (belladonna), diacytic (vasaka), paracytic (senna).
4. **Why is chloral hydrate used?** It clears the tissue by dissolving starch, chlorophyll and other contents, making cell walls visible.
5. **Which surface usually has more stomata?** The lower surface in most dicot leaves.
6. **What is the use of these values?** Identification of leaf drugs, and detecting substitution with related species.

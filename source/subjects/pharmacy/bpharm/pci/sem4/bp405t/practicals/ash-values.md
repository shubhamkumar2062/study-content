# Determination of ash values of a crude drug

**Aim:** To determine the total ash, acid-insoluble ash and water-soluble ash of the given powdered crude drug.

> Follow the method and limits in your lab manual or the pharmacopoeia monograph for the drug used. Handle hot crucibles with tongs and let them cool in a desiccator before weighing.

## Principle

When a crude drug is incinerated, the organic matter burns away and an inorganic residue (**ash**) is left.

| Value | What it measures | Significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Total ash** | All inorganic matter: natural salts of the plant (physiological ash) plus adhering earth and sand (non-physiological ash) | High value suggests contamination or adulteration with earthy matter |
| **Acid-insoluble ash** | Residue after boiling the total ash with dilute HCl: mainly silica | Measures sand and soil contamination; more useful than total ash for drugs naturally rich in calcium oxalate |
| **Water-soluble ash** | Part of total ash dissolved by water | Very low value suggests an exhausted drug (soluble salts removed by previous extraction) |

## Requirements

Powdered drug (passed through a sieve), silica or platinum crucible with lid, muffle furnace or burner, analytical balance, desiccator, ashless filter paper, dilute hydrochloric acid (about 2 M), distilled water, tongs, water bath, funnel.

## Procedure

**A. Total ash**

1. Heat an empty crucible to redness, cool in a desiccator, and weigh. Repeat until the weight is constant.
2. Accurately weigh about 2–3 g of the powdered drug into the crucible.
3. Spread it evenly. Heat gently at first, then raise the temperature gradually, not above about 450 °C, until the ash is free from carbon (white or grey).
4. If carbon persists, cool, moisten the residue with hot water, dry on a water bath, and ignite again.
5. Cool in a desiccator and weigh. Repeat heating, cooling and weighing to constant weight.

**B. Acid-insoluble ash**

1. Boil the total ash with 25 ml of dilute HCl for 5 minutes.
2. Collect the insoluble matter on an ashless filter paper and wash it with hot water until free from acid.
3. Transfer the paper with residue to the tared crucible. Dry, ignite to constant weight, cool and weigh.

**C. Water-soluble ash**

1. Boil the total ash with 25 ml of water for 5 minutes.
2. Collect the insoluble matter on an ashless filter paper and wash with hot water.
3. Ignite the paper and residue in the tared crucible for 15 minutes at a temperature not above 450 °C, cool and weigh.
4. Subtract this weight from the weight of total ash. The difference is the water-soluble ash.

## Observations and calculation

| Item | Weight (g) |
| --- | --- |
| Empty crucible (W1) |  |
| Crucible + drug (W2) |  |
| Crucible + total ash (W3) |  |
| Crucible + acid-insoluble ash (W4) |  |
| Crucible + water-insoluble ash (W5) |  |

Weight of drug = W2 − W1

- % total ash = (W3 − W1) × 100 / (W2 − W1)
- % acid-insoluble ash = (W4 − W1) × 100 / (W2 − W1)
- % water-soluble ash = [(W3 − W1) − (W5 − W1)] × 100 / (W2 − W1)

**Worked example.** Drug taken = 2.000 g. Total ash = 0.140 g, acid-insoluble ash = 0.020 g, water-insoluble ash = 0.090 g.

- Total ash = 0.140 × 100/2 = **7.0% w/w**
- Acid-insoluble ash = 0.020 × 100/2 = **1.0% w/w**
- Water-soluble ash = (0.140 − 0.090) × 100/2 = **2.5% w/w**

## Precautions

1. Heat gently at first so the powder does not puff out of the crucible.
2. Do not exceed about 450 °C, since some salts (chlorides, carbonates) volatilise or decompose at higher temperatures.
3. Always cool in a desiccator before weighing, because hot ash takes up moisture.
4. Use ashless filter paper only.
5. Heat to constant weight.

## Viva questions

1. **What is physiological ash?** Ash from the plant's own inorganic constituents.
2. **What is non-physiological ash?** Ash from adhering soil and sand.
3. **Why is acid-insoluble ash important?** It measures silica (sand and earth) contamination, since natural plant salts dissolve in dilute HCl.
4. **What does a very low water-soluble ash suggest?** That the drug has been exhausted (extracted) before sale.
5. **What is sulphated ash?** Ash obtained after treating the drug with sulphuric acid before ignition; it converts salts to sulphates for a more constant value.
6. **Why does a drug like rhubarb have high total ash?** It contains much calcium oxalate, which leaves calcium oxide or carbonate.
7. **Why use ashless filter paper?** It leaves no measurable residue on burning.

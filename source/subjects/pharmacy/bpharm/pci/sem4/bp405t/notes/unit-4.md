# Unit 4: Pharmacognosy in systems of medicine and introduction to secondary metabolites

Natural drugs are used in every system of medicine practised in India: modern (allopathic) medicine, and the traditional systems recognised under the Ministry of AYUSH. The first half of this unit shows the role of pharmacognosy in each system. The second half introduces secondary metabolites — the plant compounds responsible for most drug actions — with their definition, classification, properties and identification tests.

## 4.1 Role of pharmacognosy in allopathy (modern medicine)

Allopathy uses mostly pure, single compounds, but a large share of its medicines come from, or were modelled on, natural products.

| Role | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Pure active principles used as such | Morphine and codeine (opium), quinine (cinchona), digoxin (*Digitalis lanata*), atropine and hyoscine (solanaceous plants), reserpine (rauwolfia), vincristine and vinblastine (*Catharanthus roseus*), paclitaxel (*Taxus*), artemisinin (*Artemisia annua*), ephedrine (ephedra), colchicine (colchicum) |
| Starting materials for semi-synthesis | Diosgenin (dioscorea) → steroid hormones; thebaine → semi-synthetic opioids; artemisinin → artesunate, artemether |
| Lead molecules for synthetic drugs | Cocaine → local anaesthetics (procaine); salicin (willow) → aspirin; quinine → chloroquine |
| Antibiotics from microbes | Penicillin, streptomycin, tetracyclines |
| Pharmaceutical aids | Acacia, tragacanth, agar, starch, gelatin, beeswax, wool fat, castor oil, volatile oils as flavours |
| Crude drug preparations still in use | Senna laxatives, ispaghula, tinctures and extracts |

Pharmacognosy makes sure these materials are correctly identified, free from adulteration and of standard strength.

## 4.2 Ayurveda

- **Origin:** India; rooted in the Atharvaveda. The name means "science of life" (*ayu* = life, *veda* = knowledge).
- **Classical texts:** Charaka Samhita (internal medicine), Sushruta Samhita (surgery), Ashtanga Hridaya of Vagbhata.
- **Basic principles:**
  - The body and universe are made of five elements (**panchamahabhuta**: earth, water, fire, air, space).
  - Health depends on the balance of three **doshas** — *vata*, *pitta* and *kapha* — together with seven body tissues (*dhatus*) and waste products (*malas*).
  - Disease is an imbalance; treatment restores balance through diet, lifestyle, cleansing (*panchakarma*) and medicines.
- **Drugs:** mainly plants, with some minerals, metals (as specially processed *bhasmas*) and animal products. Each drug is described by its taste (*rasa*), potency (*virya*), post-digestive effect (*vipaka*) and special action (*prabhava*).
- **Dosage forms:**
  - churna (powder), kwatha (decoction), asava and arishta (self-fermented liquids containing alcohol),
  - avaleha (semi-solid confection), gutika/vati (pills), ghrita (medicated ghee), taila (medicated oil),
  - bhasma (calcined metal/mineral).
- **Common drugs:** ashwagandha, brahmi, guduchi, tulsi, triphala (amla, haritaki, bibhitaki), shatavari, guggul, turmeric, neem.
- **Role of pharmacognosy:** authentication of raw herbs, which is often difficult because one Sanskrit name may refer to different plants; standardisation of formulations; detecting heavy metals and pesticides; validating traditional claims.

## 4.3 Unani

- **Origin:** Greece (Hippocrates, Galen), developed by Arab and Persian physicians. Avicenna's (Ibn Sina) *Canon of Medicine* is its chief text. It came to India in medieval times and is still widely practised here.
- **Basic principles:** health depends on the balance of four humours (*akhlat*): **dam** (blood), **balgham** (phlegm), **safra** (yellow bile) and **sauda** (black bile). Each person has a temperament (*mizaj*: hot, cold, moist, dry). Disease is an imbalance of humours; treatment uses diet, regimen (*ilaj-bil-tadbeer*), drugs and surgery.
- **Drugs:** mainly plants, also animal and mineral products; drugs are graded by temperament and potency.
- **Dosage forms:** majoon (semi-solid confection), khamira (fermented confection), sharbat (syrup), arq (distillate), safoof (powder), habb (pill), roghan (oil), itrifal.
- **Common drugs:** unnab (jujube), gulbanafsha (violet), khatmi (marshmallow), asgandh (ashwagandha), sana (senna), zafran (saffron).

## 4.4 Siddha

- **Origin:** Tamil Nadu, South India; traced to the 18 **Siddhars** (Agastya is regarded as the first). Texts are in Tamil.
- **Basic principles:**
  - Like Ayurveda, it accepts five elements and three humours: *vatham*, *pitham* and *kabam*.
  - It describes the body through 96 basic principles (*thathuvas*).
  - Diagnosis uses eight methods (*envagai thervu*), especially pulse (*naadi*).
- **Drugs:** plants (*mooligai*), and a far greater use of **metals and minerals** (*thathu*) and animal products (*jeevam*) than other systems. These are purified and processed before use.
- **Dosage forms:** chooranam (powder), kudineer (decoction), chendooram (red calcined powder), parpam (white calcined powder), mezhugu (waxy preparation), lehyam (confection), thailam (oil), pathangam (sublimate).
- **Common drugs:** nilavembu (*Andrographis paniculata*; nilavembu kudineer was widely used for fevers), adathodai (vasaka), thoothuvalai, karisalai.

## 4.5 Homeopathy

- **Origin:** Germany, founded by **Samuel Hahnemann** at the end of the 18th century.
- **Basic principles:**
  - **Similia similibus curentur** — "like cures like": a substance that causes certain symptoms in a healthy person can cure similar symptoms in a sick person. Drug effects are learnt by "provings" in healthy volunteers.
  - **Single remedy** and **minimum dose**.
  - **Potentisation:** the drug is diluted step by step (decimal X or centesimal C scale) with vigorous shaking (succussion) or trituration. Homeopaths hold that potency increases with dilution.
- **Sources of drugs:** plants, which are about two-thirds of remedies, and animals and minerals.
  - Plant examples: Arnica montana, Belladonna, Nux vomica, Pulsatilla, Aconite, Bryonia, Calendula.
  - Animal examples: Apis (honey bee), Lachesis (snake venom).
  - Mineral examples: Natrum muriaticum, Sulphur.
- **Preparations:** mother tincture (Ø, the strongest liquid preparation made from fresh plant), dilutions, triturations, globules and pills medicated with dilutions, ointments.
- **Role of pharmacognosy:** correct identification of fresh plant material and quality of mother tinctures, as specified in the Homoeopathic Pharmacopoeia of India.

## 4.6 Chinese system of medicine (Traditional Chinese Medicine)

- **Origin:** China, more than 2,000 years old; the herbal of Shen Nung and later the *Bencao Gangmu* of Li Shizhen.
- **Basic principles:**
  - Balance of **yin** (cold, passive) and **yang** (hot, active).
  - Flow of vital energy **qi** through channels called meridians.
  - Five phases or elements: wood, fire, earth, metal, water.
  - Diagnosis by pulse and tongue.
- **Therapies:** herbal medicine, acupuncture, moxibustion, cupping, diet and exercise (tai chi, qigong). Herbs are usually given as mixtures of several drugs, taken mainly as decoctions.
- **Drugs that reached modern medicine:**
  - *Ephedra sinica* (ma huang) → ephedrine;
  - *Artemisia annua* (qinghao) → artemisinin, the antimalarial;
  - ginseng (*Panax ginseng*).
  - Others: liquorice, ginkgo, *Rheum* (rhubarb), dong quai (*Angelica sinensis*).

**Comparison in one table**

| System | Origin | Theory | Notable feature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ayurveda | India | Five elements, three doshas | Bhasmas, asava/arishta |
| Unani | Greece–Arabia | Four humours | Majoon, khamira |
| Siddha | Tamil Nadu | Five elements, three humours, 96 principles | Heavy use of metals and minerals |
| Homeopathy | Germany | Like cures like, potentisation | Mother tincture, dilutions |
| Chinese | China | Yin–yang, qi, five phases | Acupuncture, multi-herb decoctions |

## 4.7 Secondary metabolites: definition and classification

**Primary metabolites** are compounds needed by every living cell for growth and life: carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, nucleic acids. **Secondary metabolites** are compounds that are not essential for the basic growth of the plant. They are usually made from primary metabolites, are often restricted to particular plant groups, and help the plant survive by defence against animals, insects and microbes, by attracting pollinators, or by protection from UV light. Most plant drugs owe their action to secondary metabolites.

**Main biosynthetic groups:**
- **Alkaloids** — from amino acids.
- **Phenolic compounds** (flavonoids, tannins, coumarins, lignans) — from the shikimic acid and acetate pathways.
- **Terpenoids** (volatile oils, resins, saponins, cardiac glycoside aglycones) — from the mevalonate and methylerythritol phosphate pathways.
- **Glycosides** — any of these aglycones joined to a sugar.

## 4.8 Alkaloids

**Definition.** Alkaloids are basic, nitrogen-containing organic compounds of plant origin, usually with the nitrogen in a heterocyclic ring, having marked physiological action. They are commonly bitter.

**Classification**
- **True alkaloids:** heterocyclic N, derived from amino acids (morphine, atropine, quinine).
- **Protoalkaloids:** N not in a ring, from amino acids (ephedrine, mescaline, colchicine).
- **Pseudoalkaloids:** not from amino acids (caffeine — purine; solanidine and conessine — steroidal; aconitine — terpenoid).

**Chemical classification by ring system:**

| Ring system | Examples (source) |
| --- | --- |
| Pyridine–piperidine | Lobeline (lobelia), arecoline (areca), nicotine (tobacco), piperine (black pepper) |
| Tropane | Atropine, hyoscyamine, hyoscine (belladonna, datura), cocaine (coca) |
| Quinoline | Quinine, quinidine (cinchona) |
| Isoquinoline | Morphine, codeine, papaverine (opium), emetine (ipecac), berberine |
| Indole | Reserpine (rauwolfia), vincristine (vinca), strychnine (nux vomica), ergometrine (ergot) |
| Imidazole | Pilocarpine (jaborandi) |
| Purine | Caffeine, theophylline (tea, coffee) |
| Steroidal | Solanidine, conessine (kurchi) |
| Amino alkaloids | Ephedrine (ephedra), colchicine |

**Properties**
- Mostly colourless, crystalline solids; a few are liquids (nicotine, coniine, arecoline); berberine is yellow.
- Bitter taste; most are optically active (usually laevorotatory).
- **Free bases** are soluble in organic solvents (chloroform, ether) and insoluble in water. **Salts** are soluble in water and insoluble in organic solvents. This difference is the basis of acid–base extraction.
- Precipitated by heavy metal salts, tannic acid and picric acid.

**General tests** (on an acidified extract):

| Reagent | Composition | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mayer's | Potassium mercuric iodide | Cream/white precipitate |
| Dragendorff's | Potassium bismuth iodide | Orange to reddish-brown precipitate |
| Wagner's | Iodine in potassium iodide | Reddish-brown precipitate |
| Hager's | Saturated picric acid | Yellow precipitate |
| Tannic acid | Tannic acid solution | Buff precipitate |

**Special tests:**
- **Murexide test** (purines such as caffeine): evaporate with HCl and KClO3; the residue turns purple with ammonia.
- **Vitali–Morin test** (tropane alkaloids): evaporate with fuming nitric acid; the residue gives violet with alcoholic KOH.
- **Thalleioquin test** (quinine): bromine water followed by ammonia gives emerald green.

## 4.9 Glycosides

**Definition.** Glycosides are compounds in which a sugar (glycone) is joined through a glycosidic bond to a non-sugar part (aglycone or genin). They are hydrolysed by acids or by specific enzymes into sugar and aglycone. The aglycone usually carries the activity; the sugar affects solubility and absorption.

**Classification**
- **By linkage:**
  - O-glycosides (sennosides, digitoxin);
  - C-glycosides (barbaloin of aloe);
  - S-glycosides (sinigrin of black mustard);
  - N-glycosides (nucleosides such as adenosine).
- **By sugar:** glucosides, rhamnosides, digitoxosides (deoxy sugars of cardiac glycosides).
- **By aglycone (most useful):**

| Class | Examples (drug) |
| --- | --- |
| Cardiac (steroidal) | Digitoxin, digoxin (digitalis), thevetin (thevetia) |
| Anthraquinone | Sennosides (senna), barbaloin (aloe), rhubarb, cascara |
| Saponin | Glycyrrhizin (liquorice), dioscin (dioscorea) |
| Cyanogenetic | Amygdalin (bitter almond), prunasin (wild cherry) |
| Isothiocyanate | Sinigrin (black mustard) |
| Flavonol | Rutin (buckwheat) |
| Coumarin / furanocoumarin | Psoralen (psoralea, ammi) |
| Aldehyde | Glucovanillin (vanilla) |
| Phenolic | Arbutin (bearberry) |
| Bitter (iridoid) | Gentiopicroside (gentian) |

**Properties**
- Usually colourless, crystalline or amorphous solids, with a bitter taste.
- Soluble in water and alcohol, but the aglycones are less water-soluble.
- Hydrolysed by acids and by enzymes present in the same plant, often in separate cells: emulsin in almond, myrosin in mustard. Crushing a moist drug brings enzyme and glycoside together.

**Tests:**

| Test | Detects | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keller–Killiani | 2-deoxy sugars (cardiac glycosides) | Extract in glacial acetic acid with a trace of FeCl3, underlaid with conc. H2SO4 → reddish-brown ring at the junction; upper layer turns bluish-green |
| Legal | Unsaturated lactone ring of cardenolides | Pyridine + sodium nitroprusside + NaOH → pink to red |
| Baljet | Cardenolide lactone ring | Alkaline sodium picrate → orange |
| Borntrager's | Free anthraquinones | Extract with benzene or chloroform, shake with dilute ammonia → pink to red in the ammonia layer |
| Modified Borntrager's | C-glycosidic anthraquinones (aloe) | Hydrolyse first with FeCl3 and HCl, then as above |
| Froth test | Saponins | Persistent honeycomb froth on shaking |
| Haemolysis | Saponins | Lysis of red blood cells |
| Sodium picrate paper | Cyanogenetic glycosides (HCN) | Yellow paper turns brick red |

## 4.10 Flavonoids

**Definition.** Flavonoids are polyphenolic compounds with a C6–C3–C6 skeleton: two benzene rings (A and B) joined by a three-carbon bridge, usually closed into a pyran ring, giving the 2-phenylchromone (flavone) nucleus. Many are yellow pigments (Latin *flavus* = yellow). They occur free or as glycosides.

**Classification**

| Class | Examples (source) |
| --- | --- |
| Flavones | Apigenin (chamomile), luteolin |
| Flavonols | Quercetin, kaempferol; rutin (buckwheat) |
| Flavanones | Hesperidin, naringin (citrus peel) |
| Isoflavones | Genistein, daidzein (soya) |
| Flavan-3-ols (catechins) | Catechin, epicatechin (tea, catechu) |
| Anthocyanidins | Cyanidin, delphinidin (coloured flowers and fruits) |
| Chalcones and aurones | Butein, isoliquiritigenin |

**Properties:**
- Crystalline, usually yellow or colourless (anthocyanins are red, purple or blue depending on pH).
- Glycosides are water-soluble; aglycones are soluble in organic solvents.
- Antioxidant, anti-inflammatory and capillary-protective actions.

**Tests:**
- **Shinoda test:** alcoholic extract + magnesium turnings + conc. HCl → pink, red or magenta.
- **Alkaline reagent test:** NaOH gives intense yellow, which becomes colourless on adding dilute acid.
- **Lead acetate test:** yellow precipitate.
- **Ferric chloride:** green to blackish colour (phenolic groups).

## 4.11 Tannins

**Definition.** Tannins are complex, high-molecular-weight polyphenolic compounds that precipitate proteins, convert animal hide into leather, and have an astringent taste.

**Classification**

| Type | Nature | Ferric chloride | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Hydrolysable tannins** (pyrogallol type) | Esters of gallic acid (gallitannins) or ellagic acid (ellagitannins) with glucose; hydrolysed by acids and enzymes | Blue-black | Nutgall, chebulic myrobalan (haritaki), bearberry |
| **Condensed (non-hydrolysable) tannins** (catechol type) | Polymers of flavan-3-ols; acids convert them to red insoluble phlobaphenes | Brownish-green | Pale and black catechu, cinnamon, cinchona, krameria |
| **Pseudotannins** | Small phenolics that do not tan hide | Variable; no precipitate with gelatin | Gallic acid, chlorogenic acid (coffee), catechin |

**Properties:**
- Amorphous, non-crystalline, soluble in water forming colloidal solutions, acidic to litmus.
- Astringent.
- Precipitate proteins, alkaloids and heavy metals.

**Uses:**
- Astringents in diarrhoea, haemorrhoids and burns.
- Antidote in alkaloid and heavy metal poisoning.
- Tanning of leather.

**Tests:**
- **Ferric chloride:** blue-black (hydrolysable) or green-black (condensed).
- **Gelatin test:** 1% gelatin containing NaCl gives a white precipitate.
- **Goldbeater's skin test:**
  - The skin (ox intestine membrane) is soaked in 2% HCl, washed, and placed in the tannin solution for a few minutes.
  - It is washed again and dipped in ferric sulphate or ferric chloride.
  - A brown or black colour shows true tannin.
- **Phenazone test:** a bulky coloured precipitate.
- **Bromine water:** buff precipitate with condensed tannins.
- **Matchstick test (catechins):** a matchstick dipped in extract, dried and warmed after moistening with conc. HCl turns pink.

## 4.12 Volatile oils

**Definition.** Volatile (essential) oils are odorous, oily liquids that evaporate at ordinary temperature, are obtained from plants mainly by steam distillation, and give the plant its characteristic odour.

**Occurrence:** in special structures — glandular hairs (Lamiaceae), oil cells (Zingiberaceae), oil ducts or vittae (Apiaceae), schizolysigenous cavities (citrus, clove).

**Chemical classification (by main functional group):**

| Group | Example constituent | Drug |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hydrocarbons | Pinene, limonene | Turpentine, lemon |
| Alcohols | Menthol, linalool | Peppermint, coriander |
| Aldehydes | Citral, cinnamaldehyde | Lemongrass, cinnamon |
| Ketones | Camphor, carvone | Camphor, caraway, dill |
| Phenols | Eugenol, thymol | Clove, ajowan |
| Phenolic ethers | Anethole | Fennel, anise |
| Oxides | Cineole (eucalyptol) | Eucalyptus |
| Esters | Linalyl acetate | Lavender, bergamot |

Chemically most are **terpenoids** (mainly monoterpenes and sesquiterpenes) or **phenylpropanoids** (eugenol, anethole, cinnamaldehyde).

**Properties:**
- Colourless when fresh; darken and thicken (resinify) on exposure to air and light.
- Characteristic odour; high refractive index; most optically active.
- Immiscible with water but give it their odour; soluble in alcohol and ether.
- Unlike fixed oils, a spot on filter paper evaporates and **leaves no permanent grease mark**.
- Stored in well-filled, airtight, amber containers in a cool place.

**Tests:**
- Filter-paper spot evaporates completely.
- Sudan III stains oil globules red in sections.
- Tincture of alkanna gives a red colour.
- Solubility in 90% alcohol.

**Uses:** flavouring, perfumery, carminatives, antiseptics (thymol, eugenol), counter-irritants (turpentine, camphor), expectorants (eucalyptus).

## 4.13 Resins

**Definition.** Resins are amorphous, solid or semi-solid, brittle products of plants, formed in ducts or cavities (often after injury). They are insoluble in water, soluble in alcohol and other organic solvents, and soften or melt on heating. They burn with a smoky flame.

**Chemistry:** complex mixtures of:
- resin acids (abietic acid in colophony),
- resin alcohols (resinols, resinotannols),
- resin esters,
- inert hydrocarbons called resenes.

**Classification by associated constituents:**

| Type | Composition | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resins (hard) | Mainly resin | Colophony, copal, mastic |
| Oleoresins | Resin + volatile oil | Turpentine, copaiba, ginger and capsicum oleoresins |
| Gum resins | Resin + gum | Gamboge |
| Oleo-gum-resins | Resin + volatile oil + gum | Asafoetida, myrrh, guggul |
| Balsams | Resins with benzoic and/or cinnamic acid | Benzoin, Tolu balsam, Peru balsam, storax |
| Glycoresins | Resin combined with sugar | Jalap, podophyllum |

**Tests:**
- An alcoholic solution turns milky when poured into water, because the resin precipitates.
- Physical constants: acid value, saponification value, sulphated ash, alcohol-soluble extractive.
- **Balsams:** warming with potassium permanganate gives the odour of benzaldehyde if cinnamic acid is present.
- **Asafoetida:** warmed with HCl and then made alkaline with ammonia, it shows blue fluorescence (umbelliferone).

**Uses:**
- Tincture of benzoin as an inhalant and antiseptic.
- Colophony in plasters and adhesives.
- Podophyllum resin for warts.
- Asafoetida as a carminative.
- Myrrh in mouthwashes.

## Remember

- Allopathy uses pure natural principles (morphine, quinine, digoxin, vincristine, artemisinin), semi-synthetics and pharmaceutical aids.
- Ayurveda: three doshas; Unani: four humours (dam, balgham, safra, sauda); Siddha: vatham, pitham, kabam with metals; Homeopathy: Hahnemann, like cures like; Chinese: yin–yang, qi, ephedra and artemisinin.
- Secondary metabolites are not needed for basic growth; they defend the plant and give drugs their activity.
- Alkaloid reagents: Mayer's (cream), Dragendorff's (orange), Wagner's (reddish-brown), Hager's (yellow).
- Keller–Killiani for deoxy sugars, Legal and Baljet for the cardenolide ring, Borntrager's for anthraquinones, froth test for saponins.
- Shinoda (Mg + HCl → pink/red) for flavonoids.
- Tannins: FeCl3 blue-black (hydrolysable) or green-black (condensed); gelatin precipitate; goldbeater's skin test.
- Volatile oils leave no grease spot; resins dissolve in alcohol, not water; balsams contain benzoic/cinnamic acid.

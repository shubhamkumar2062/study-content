# Unit 5: Plant products, primary metabolites and marine drugs

This unit studies individual drugs. For each one, learn the biological source (plant or animal with family), the chemical nature, how it is prepared, how it is tested, and its uses. The unit starts with plant fibres and with harmful plant products (hallucinogens, teratogens, allergens), then covers primary metabolites used as medicines or pharmaceutical aids — carbohydrates, proteins and enzymes, and lipids — and ends with drugs from the sea.

## 5.1 Plant fibres

Fibres are long, thick-walled cells (or groups of cells). Pharmaceutically they are used as surgical dressings, filtering and straining media, and packaging.

| Fibre | Biological source | Part | Chemistry | Uses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cotton** | Epidermal hairs of the seeds of *Gossypium herbaceum*, *G. hirsutum*, *G. barbadense* and other species, family **Malvaceae** | Seed hairs (trichomes) | Almost pure cellulose (about 90% when raw; more after purification); not lignified | Absorbent cotton wool and gauze for dressings, filtering, swabs; raw material for collodion and nitrocellulose |
| **Jute** | Phloem (bast) fibres from the stems of *Corchorus capsularis* and *C. olitorius*, family Tiliaceae (now placed in Malvaceae) | Stem bast | Lignocellulose (cellulose with lignin) | Sacking, gunny bags, packaging, ropes, coarse cloth, tow for padding |
| **Hemp** | Pericyclic (bast) fibres from the stems of *Cannabis sativa*, family **Cannabaceae** | Stem bast | Mainly cellulose, slightly lignified | Ropes, twine, sacking, canvas, caulking; oakum |

**Preparation of absorbent cotton.** Raw cotton is:
1. freed from seeds (ginning),
2. carded to remove short fibres and dirt,
3. boiled with dilute alkali (NaOH) under pressure to remove the wax and fat that make it water-repellent,
4. washed, bleached (hypochlorite), washed again, dried and carded into sheets.

**Identification of fibres**

| Test | Cotton | Jute | Hemp |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Microscopy | Flattened, twisted, ribbon-like single cell with a lumen and convolutions | Short cells in bundles with irregular lumen and joints | Long cells with cross-markings and forked ends |
| Phloroglucinol + HCl (lignin) | No colour | Red | Faint colour (slight lignin) |
| Iodine + 80% H2SO4 | Blue (cellulose) | Yellow (lignin) | Blue to greenish-blue |

Cotton swells and dissolves in cuprammonium solution (cuoxam); lignified fibres such as jute only swell.

Absorbent cotton should sink in water and absorb quickly. It is tested for absorbency, sinking time, fibre length, water-soluble substances, acidity/alkalinity and foreign fibres.

## 5.2 Hallucinogens, teratogens and natural allergens

### Hallucinogens

**Hallucinogens** are substances that change perception, thought and mood, producing hallucinations (seeing or hearing things that are not present). Many are plant or fungal products; most are controlled drugs in India.

| Source | Active principle | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Cannabis sativa* (Cannabaceae) — flowering tops (ganja), resin (charas), leaves (bhang) | Δ9-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) | Euphoria, altered perception; controlled under the NDPS Act |
| Peyote cactus (*Lophophora williamsii*) | Mescaline (a protoalkaloid) | Visual hallucinations |
| *Psilocybe* mushrooms | Psilocybin, psilocin | Indole hallucinogens |
| Ergot (*Claviceps purpurea*) | Lysergic acid → LSD (semi-synthetic) | LSD is extremely potent |
| Morning glory seeds (*Ipomoea* spp.) | Lysergic acid amide (ergine) | Weaker LSD-like action |
| *Datura*, belladonna | Hyoscine, hyoscyamine | Delirium and hallucinations in poisoning |
| Fly agaric (*Amanita muscaria*) | Ibotenic acid, muscimol | Intoxication |
| Nutmeg (*Myristica fragrans*) in large doses | Myristicin | Hallucinations and toxicity |
| *Salvia divinorum* | Salvinorin A | Short, intense effects |

### Teratogens

**Teratogens** are agents that cause malformations in the developing embryo or foetus when exposure occurs during pregnancy. Several plant teratogens were found from birth defects in grazing animals.

| Plant | Teratogenic principle | Effect reported |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Veratrum californicum* (corn lily) | Cyclopamine, jervine (steroidal alkaloids) | Cyclopia (single eye) in lambs |
| Lupins (*Lupinus* spp.) | Anagyrine (quinolizidine alkaloid) | Crooked calf disease (skeletal deformities) |
| Poison hemlock (*Conium maculatum*) | Coniine | Skeletal malformations in livestock |
| Tobacco (*Nicotiana* spp.) | Anabasine, nicotine | Limb deformities in piglets; nicotine harms the human foetus |
| Locoweeds (*Astragalus*, *Oxytropis* spp.) | Swainsonine | Abortion and foetal defects in livestock |

Other natural products to avoid in pregnancy because of harm to the foetus or uterine action include podophyllum resin, high-dose vitamin A, and ergot alkaloids (uterine contraction).

### Natural allergens

**Allergens** are substances, usually proteins or small reactive molecules, that cause an exaggerated immune (allergic) reaction in sensitive people.

- **Type I (immediate, IgE-mediated):**
  - causes hay fever, asthma, urticaria and anaphylaxis;
  - pollens such as grasses, ragweed (*Ambrosia*) and *Parthenium hysterophorus* (congress grass);
  - mould spores, house-dust mites, animal dander, insect venoms (bee, wasp);
  - foods such as peanut;
  - natural rubber latex proteins (*Hevea brasiliensis*).
- **Type IV (delayed, contact dermatitis):**
  - urushiol from poison ivy and related *Toxicodendron* species, and similar phenols from the cashew-nut shell and marking nut (*Semecarpus anacardium*);
  - primin from *Primula obconica*;
  - sesquiterpene lactones from Asteraceae (parthenin in parthenium; also chrysanthemum and arnica);
  - wool fat (lanolin) in some users.

Pharmacists should know these because herbal products, cosmetics and excipients from natural sources can provoke allergy, and allergen extracts are used for diagnosis (skin tests) and immunotherapy.

## 5.3 Primary metabolites — general introduction

Primary metabolites are carbohydrates, proteins and lipids that are essential for the life of the organism. Many are used in pharmacy either as **medicines** (honey as a demulcent, enzymes as digestants or thrombolytics, castor oil as a purgative) or as **pharmaceutical aids** (gums as suspending and emulsifying agents, gelatin for capsules, beeswax and wool fat in ointment bases). For each, learn the source, preparation, chemistry, evaluation, storage and uses.

## 5.4 Carbohydrates

### Acacia (gum acacia, gum arabic)

- **Biological source:** dried gummy exudation from the stems and branches of *Acacia senegal* (Linn.) Willd. and other African species of *Acacia*, family **Leguminosae** (sub-family Mimosoideae).
- **Geographical source:** Sudan (Kordofan) is the main source, also other parts of Africa; India produces Indian gum from *Acacia arabica* (babul).
- **Collection:** transverse incisions are made in the bark in the dry season and strips of bark peeled off. Gum exudes as tears, which harden in a few weeks and are hand-picked, sorted, sun-bleached and graded.
- **Description:** round or oval tears, white to pale yellow, opaque because of fine cracks; brittle; glassy fracture; odourless, bland mucilaginous taste. Slowly but almost completely soluble in twice its weight of water.
- **Chemistry:**
  - **Arabin**, a mixture of the calcium, magnesium and potassium salts of **arabic acid**, a branched polysaccharide;
  - on hydrolysis arabic acid gives L-arabinose, D-galactose, L-rhamnose and D-glucuronic acid;
  - also contains **oxidase and peroxidase** enzymes.
- **Tests:**
  - an aqueous solution gives a white precipitate with lead subacetate;
  - blue colour with benzidine in alcohol and hydrogen peroxide, because of the oxidase enzyme;
  - after hydrolysis it reduces Fehling's solution.
- **Incompatibility:** the oxidase can destroy easily oxidised drugs (some alkaloids, phenols, vitamins), so an acacia solution is heated to about 100 °C for a short time to inactivate the enzyme before use with such drugs. It is precipitated by alcohol and by ferric salts and borax.
- **Uses:**
  - emulsifying agent, especially for oils;
  - suspending agent, binder in tablets and lozenges;
  - demulcent;
  - in adhesives and confectionery.
- **Storage:** well-closed containers, protected from moisture.

### Agar (agar-agar)

- **Biological source:** dried gelatinous substance obtained by extraction from red algae (Rhodophyceae) such as *Gelidium amansii* and other species of *Gelidium*, and *Gracilaria* species.
- **Geographical source:** Japan; also India, Spain, USA, New Zealand and other coasts.
- **Preparation:**
  1. Algae are collected, washed and bleached.
  2. They are boiled with water (made slightly acid) for several hours.
  3. The hot extract is filtered and allowed to set into a gel.
  4. The gel is cut, frozen and thawed; this squeezes out water with soluble impurities, so it acts as purification.
  5. The gel is then dried and cut into strips or powdered.
- **Description:** thin translucent strips, flakes or powder; colourless to pale yellow; tough when damp, brittle when dry; insoluble in cold water; dissolves in boiling water; a 1.5% solution sets to a firm gel on cooling.
- **Chemistry:**
  - **Agarose** — the gelling fraction; a neutral polysaccharide of D-galactose and 3,6-anhydro-L-galactose units;
  - **Agaropectin** — the non-gelling, sulphated fraction.
- **Tests:**
  - with iodine solution it gives a crimson to violet colour;
  - mucilage stains pink with ruthenium red;
  - a hot solution sets to a gel on cooling;
  - no precipitate with tannic acid (distinction from gelatin).
- **Uses:**
  - culture media for bacteria and fungi, because few microbes digest it;
  - bulk laxative;
  - suspending, emulsifying and gelling agent;
  - in food (jellies, desserts, ice cream).

### Tragacanth

- **Biological source:** dried gummy exudation from *Astragalus gummifer* Labill. and other Asiatic species of *Astragalus*, family **Leguminosae** (Papilionaceae).
- **Geographical source:** Iran (Persian tragacanth) and Turkey.
- **Collection and formation:** the gum is formed by transformation (gummosis) of the cell walls of the pith and medullary rays. Incisions are made in the stems; the gum exudes and dries as ribbons (best quality, flat, curved, with ridges) or flakes.
- **Description:** white to pale yellow, horny, translucent ribbons or flakes; tough; odourless; mucilaginous taste. It swells in water to form a stiff gel.
- **Chemistry:**
  - **Tragacanthin** — water-soluble, about 30–40%;
  - **Bassorin** — water-insoluble, swelling, about 60–70%;
  - small amounts of starch and cellulose.
- **Tests:**
  - swells with water to form a gel;
  - small starch grains give blue specks with iodine;
  - boiling with strong NaOH solution gives a canary-yellow colour;
  - mucilage stains with ruthenium red.
- **Adulterant:** karaya gum (sterculia, *Sterculia urens*), called Indian tragacanth.
- **Uses:**
  - suspending agent for insoluble powders;
  - emulsifying and thickening agent in lotions and creams;
  - tablet binder, demulcent;
  - in toothpastes and as a bulk laxative.

### Honey

- **Biological source:** viscid, sugary secretion deposited in the honeycomb by the hive bee *Apis mellifera* and other species of *Apis*, family **Apidae** (order Hymenoptera).
- **Formation:** bees suck nectar from flowers into the honey sac. There the enzyme **invertase** converts sucrose to glucose and fructose (invert sugar). The bees deposit it in wax cells, where water evaporates and it ripens.
- **Collection:**
  1. The wax caps are cut off the combs.
  2. Honey is removed by centrifugal extractors (or by pressing or draining).
  3. It is warmed gently, strained and allowed to settle.
- **Description:** thick, syrupy, translucent liquid; pale yellow to reddish-brown; characteristic odour; sweet taste. It may crystallise on keeping because glucose separates.
- **Chemistry:**
  - invert sugar about 70–80% (fructose slightly more than glucose);
  - water about 17–20%; sucrose only a small amount;
  - dextrins, enzymes (invertase, diastase, glucose oxidase), formic acid and other organic acids;
  - minerals, vitamins, pollen and colouring and aromatic substances.
- **Evaluation and adulteration:**
  - Common adulterants: cane sugar syrup, artificial invert sugar, glucose syrup, jaggery.
  - **Fiehe's test** for artificial invert sugar: an ether extract with resorcinol in HCl gives a cherry-red colour. The colour is due to hydroxymethylfurfural formed in artificially inverted or overheated sugar.
  - Natural honey is laevorotatory; a dextrorotatory sample suggests added glucose or dextrins.
  - Other checks: moisture, ash, reducing sugars and sucrose content, and microscopy for pollen.
  - Honey reduces Fehling's solution strongly and gives Seliwanoff's test (red, fructose).
- **Uses:**
  - demulcent and sweetening agent;
  - vehicle in linctuses and cough preparations;
  - nutrient;
  - mild antiseptic dressing for wounds and burns;
  - vehicle for Ayurvedic medicines (anupana).
- **Storage:** well-closed containers.

## 5.5 Proteins and enzymes

### Gelatin

- **Source:** a protein obtained by partial hydrolysis of **collagen** from the skin, white connective tissue and bones of animals (cattle, pigs).
- **Preparation:**
  - **Type A** gelatin — from acid-treated raw material (mainly pig skin); isoelectric point about pH 7–9.
  - **Type B** gelatin — from alkali (lime)-treated material (bones and hides); isoelectric point about pH 4.7–5.2.
  - After pre-treatment the material is washed and extracted with hot water in stages. The extract is filtered, concentrated, chilled to a gel, cut and dried, then ground.
- **Description:** colourless or pale yellow, translucent sheets, shreds or powder; almost odourless. It swells in cold water, absorbing 5–10 times its weight, and dissolves in hot water. The solution sets to a gel on cooling.
- **Chemistry:**
  - rich in glycine, proline and hydroxyproline;
  - contains very little tyrosine and lacks tryptophan, so it is an incomplete protein;
  - gel strength is measured as **Bloom strength**.
- **Tests:**
  - tannic acid gives a buff precipitate; picric acid a yellow precipitate;
  - heating with soda lime gives ammonia (nitrogen);
  - biuret test violet;
  - a 1% hot solution sets to a gel on cooling.
- **Uses:**
  - hard and soft capsules; suppository and pessary bases (glycero-gelatin);
  - tablet binder and coating; microencapsulation;
  - absorbable gelatin sponge as a haemostat;
  - gelatin-based plasma expanders;
  - culture media; food and photography.

### Casein

- **Source:** the main protein of milk (about 80% of cow's milk protein).
- **Preparation:** skimmed milk is acidified to about pH 4.6 (the isoelectric point of casein), and casein precipitates as curd. The curd is washed, dried and ground. Precipitation by the enzyme rennet gives rennet casein (paracasein).
- **Chemistry:**
  - a **phosphoprotein** (phosphate bound to serine);
  - present in milk as calcium caseinate micelles;
  - contains all essential amino acids.
- **Tests:**
  - biuret test positive;
  - Millon's test positive (tyrosine);
  - phosphorus detected after ashing.
- **Uses:**
  - nutritional supplements for protein deficiency;
  - sodium caseinate as an emulsifier;
  - in tablets, adhesives, paints and casein plastics;
  - substrate for assaying proteolytic enzymes.

### Proteolytic enzymes

Enzymes are protein catalysts. Proteolytic enzymes (proteases) break proteins into peptides and amino acids.

| Enzyme | Source | Nature | Uses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Papain** | Dried, purified latex of the unripe fruits of *Carica papaya*, family **Caricaceae**. Shallow incisions on green fruits; latex collected and dried (sun, oven or spray) | Cysteine (thiol) protease, activated by cysteine and other reducing agents; works over a wide pH range around neutral. Latex also contains chymopapain | Digestive aid; meat tenderiser; clarifying beer; debridement of dead tissue in wounds; chymopapain was used for dissolving prolapsed disc material |
| **Bromelain** | Pineapple, *Ananas comosus*, family **Bromeliaceae**; mainly from the stem (stem bromelain), also the fruit | Mixture of cysteine proteases | Reducing inflammation and oedema after injury or surgery; debridement of burns; digestive aid; meat tenderising |
| **Serratiopeptidase** | Produced by fermentation using the non-pathogenic enterobacterium *Serratia* sp. E-15, originally isolated from the gut of the silkworm | Zinc-containing metalloprotease; given as enteric-coated tablets | Used as an anti-inflammatory and anti-oedema agent, and to thin secretions, in ENT, dental and surgical swelling |
| **Urokinase** | Human urine or cultures of human kidney cells | Serine protease that directly converts plasminogen to plasmin; not antigenic | Thrombolytic: pulmonary embolism, deep vein thrombosis, clearing blocked catheters |
| **Streptokinase** | Culture filtrate of β-haemolytic streptococci (Lancefield group C) | Protein, not itself an enzyme: it binds plasminogen, and the complex converts other plasminogen to plasmin. Antigenic, so it may cause allergy and should not be repeated soon after a previous course | Thrombolytic: acute myocardial infarction, deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism |
| **Pepsin** | Glandular layer of fresh stomach of the pig, sheep or cattle | Aspartic protease, active in strongly acid medium (around pH 1.5–2) | Digestive aid in pepsin deficiency (often with dilute HCl); laboratory digestion of proteins |

**Evaluation of enzymes:** enzymes are assayed by their activity, not their weight.
- Papain and pepsin are tested by the amount of a protein substrate they digest under set conditions. Substrates include casein, coagulated egg albumin and haemoglobin.
- Thrombolytics are assayed in units by their plasminogen-activating or clot-lysing action.

**Storage:** cool and dry, in airtight containers; many are refrigerated, since heat, moisture and extremes of pH destroy activity.

## 5.6 Lipids (fixed oils, fats and waxes)

Fixed oils and fats are esters of glycerol with fatty acids (triglycerides). Oils are liquid at room temperature and fats are solid. Waxes are esters of fatty acids with long-chain monohydric alcohols (or sterols), not glycerol. Lipids are evaluated by acid value, saponification value, iodine value, ester value, acetyl value, unsaponifiable matter, refractive index and specific gravity.

### Castor oil

- **Biological source:** fixed oil obtained by cold expression of the seeds of *Ricinus communis*, family **Euphorbiaceae**.
- **Geographical source:** India is the largest producer; also Brazil and China.
- **Preparation:**
  1. Seeds (about 45–55% oil) are decorticated and cold-pressed in hydraulic presses.
  2. The oil is heated with water or steamed to coagulate albuminous matter and inactivate the enzyme lipase.
  3. It is filtered and bleached.
  - The toxic protein **ricin** stays in the press cake because it is insoluble in oil; the cake is poisonous unless heat-treated.
- **Description:** pale yellow, almost colourless, viscous liquid; faint odour; bland then slightly acrid taste.
- **Chemistry:**
  - triglycerides of **ricinoleic acid** (about 85–90%), a 12-hydroxy unsaturated fatty acid;
  - small amounts of oleic, linoleic, stearic and dihydroxystearic acids.
- **Special properties (from the hydroxyl group):**
  - unusually high viscosity and specific gravity;
  - high acetyl value;
  - miscible with ethanol, unlike most fixed oils;
  - only slightly soluble in light petroleum.
- **Action and uses:**
  - **Purgative:** lipase in the small intestine releases ricinoleic acid, which irritates the gut and alters water and electrolyte transport.
  - Emollient in skin and hair products; plasticiser in flexible collodion.
  - Raw material for hydrogenated castor oil, sulphonated castor oil (Turkey red oil) and polyoxyl castor oils (solubilisers).
  - Lubricant.
- **Storage:** well-filled, airtight containers, protected from light.

### Chaulmoogra oil (hydnocarpus oil)

- **Biological source:** fixed oil expressed from the seeds of *Hydnocarpus wightiana*, *H. kurzii* (*Taraktogenos kurzii*), *H. anthelmintica* and related species, family Flacourtiaceae (now Achariaceae).
- **Description:** yellowish to brownish oil, which partly solidifies to a soft fat in cold weather; characteristic odour; acrid taste.
- **Chemistry:**
  - glycerides of unusual **cyclopentenyl fatty acids** — hydnocarpic, chaulmoogric and gorlic acids;
  - these make the oil **dextrorotatory**, unusual for a fixed oil and used as an identity check.
- **Uses:**
  - formerly the main treatment for leprosy (the oil, or its ethyl esters by injection), and used in other chronic skin diseases;
  - now replaced by modern multidrug therapy (dapsone, rifampicin, clofazimine).

### Wool fat (anhydrous lanolin)

- **Biological source:** purified fat-like substance obtained from the wool of the sheep *Ovis aries*, family **Bovidae**.
- **Preparation:**
  1. Raw wool is scoured (washed) with soap and soda or solvents.
  2. The greasy wool wax in the washings is recovered (by centrifuging or acid cracking).
  3. It is purified, bleached and deodorised.
  - Hydrous wool fat (lanolin) is wool fat mixed with water.
- **Description:** pale yellow, tenacious, unctuous mass; faint characteristic odour; melts a little above body temperature.
- **Chemistry:**
  - not a true fat (no glycerides) but a wax;
  - esters of sterols (cholesterol, lanosterol) and aliphatic alcohols with fatty acids, plus free alcohols.
- **Important property:** it absorbs about twice its weight of water to form a water-in-oil emulsion, which makes it valuable in ointment and cream bases.
- **Tests:**
  - **Liebermann–Burchard test:** a chloroform solution with acetic anhydride and conc. H2SO4 gives a red colour changing to blue-green (cholesterol).
  - Acid value, iodine value, and limits for chloride and water.
- **Uses:**
  - water-absorbing ointment bases;
  - emollient in creams and cosmetics;
  - ingredient of hydrophilic bases.
- **Caution:** it can cause contact allergy in some people.

### Beeswax

- **Biological source:** purified wax obtained from the honeycomb of the hive bee *Apis mellifera* and other species of *Apis*, family **Apidae**.
- **Formation:** worker bees secrete the wax from glands on the underside of the abdomen to build the comb.
- **Preparation:**
  - **Yellow beeswax:** combs (after removing honey) are melted in hot water, and the wax is strained and allowed to solidify in moulds.
  - **White beeswax:** yellow wax is bleached by exposure of thin ribbons to sunlight, air and moisture, or by chemical oxidising agents.
- **Description:** yellow to brownish-yellow (white wax nearly white) solid; honey-like odour; breaks with a granular fracture; plastic when warm; melts at about 61–65 °C.
- **Chemistry:**
  - mainly **myricyl palmitate** (ester of myricyl alcohol and palmitic acid);
  - free acids, chiefly **cerotic acid**;
  - hydrocarbons; colouring matter (cerolein) in yellow wax.
- **Evaluation:**
  - melting point;
  - acid value, ester value and the **ratio number** (ester value ÷ acid value), which is characteristic;
  - absence of adulterants such as paraffin wax, ceresin, stearic acid, tallow, Japan wax and resin.
  - Paraffin and ceresin do not saponify, so they cause turbidity when the wax is saponified and diluted.
- **Uses:**
  - stiffening agent in ointments and creams;
  - with borax it forms cold cream (the borax and free acids form a soap emulsifier);
  - cosmetics such as lip balms; polishing and coating of tablets;
  - dental wax, candles, polishes.

## 5.7 Marine drugs: novel medicinal agents from marine sources

The sea holds a huge variety of organisms. Sponges, soft corals, tunicates (sea squirts), molluscs, bryozoans, algae and marine microbes produce unusual compounds — many with halogens, peptides or complex polyether rings — evolved for defence. Several have become medicines or leads.

| Activity | Compound | Marine source | Status / use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Anticancer | **Cytarabine** (ara-C) | Synthetic analogue based on spongothymidine and spongouridine nucleosides from the sponge *Cryptotethya crypta* (*Tectitethya crypta*) | Approved; acute leukaemias |
| Anticancer | **Trabectedin** (ecteinascidin 743) | Tunicate *Ecteinascidia turbinata* | Approved; soft-tissue sarcoma, ovarian cancer |
| Anticancer | **Eribulin** | Synthetic analogue of halichondrin B from the sponge *Halichondria okadai* | Approved; metastatic breast cancer |
| Anticancer | **Monomethyl auristatin E** (in antibody–drug conjugates such as brentuximab vedotin) | Derived from dolastatin 10 of the sea hare *Dolabella auricularia* | Approved as part of conjugates |
| Anticancer | Bryostatin 1 | Bryozoan *Bugula neritina* | Investigational |
| Antiviral | **Vidarabine** (ara-A) | Nucleoside modelled on sponge nucleosides | Formerly for herpes infections |
| Analgesic | **Ziconotide** (ω-conotoxin MVIIA) | Venom peptide of the cone snail *Conus magus* | Approved; severe chronic pain, given intrathecally |
| Antibacterial | **Cephalosporins** | Fungus *Cephalosporium acremonium* (*Acremonium*), first isolated from sea water near a sewage outlet in Sardinia | Major class of antibiotics |
| Lipid lowering / cardiovascular | **Omega-3 fatty acids** (EPA, DHA) | Fish oils | Approved for high triglycerides |
| Heparin antagonist | **Protamine sulphate** | Sperm of fish such as salmon | Reverses heparin |
| Nutrients | Cod liver and shark liver oils | Fish livers | Vitamins A and D |
| Research tools / toxins | Tetrodotoxin (puffer fish), saxitoxin (dinoflagellates), okadaic acid | Various | Sodium channel and phosphatase studies |
| Anti-inflammatory | Pseudopterosins | Soft coral *Pseudopterogorgia elisabethae* | Used in cosmetic products |
| Pharmaceutical aids | Agar, alginic acid and alginates, carrageenan, chitosan | Red and brown algae; crustacean shells | Gelling, suspending, disintegrating and film-forming agents |

**Challenges in developing marine drugs:**
- **Supply** — compounds are present in tiny amounts, and collecting large quantities harms reefs.
- **Difficult total synthesis.**
- **Identifying the true producer**, which is often a symbiotic microbe rather than the animal.

**Solutions:**
- aquaculture,
- semi-synthesis (trabectedin is made from a bacterial product),
- synthetic analogues (eribulin),
- culture of the producing microbes.

## Remember

- Cotton = seed hairs of *Gossypium* (Malvaceae), pure cellulose, no lignin; jute = bast of *Corchorus*, lignified; hemp = bast of *Cannabis sativa*.
- Hallucinogens: THC, mescaline, psilocybin, LSD; teratogens: cyclopamine (*Veratrum*), anagyrine (lupins), coniine; allergens: parthenium, urushiol, primin, latex.
- Acacia: *Acacia senegal*, Leguminosae; arabin (Ca, Mg, K arabate); contains oxidase — heat before use with oxidisable drugs.
- Agar: *Gelidium*, *Gracilaria* (red algae); agarose + agaropectin; culture media, bulk laxative.
- Tragacanth: *Astragalus gummifer*; tragacanthin (soluble) + bassorin (swells); canary-yellow with hot NaOH.
- Honey: *Apis mellifera*; invert sugar 70–80%; Fiehe's test detects artificial invert sugar.
- Gelatin from collagen (Type A acid, Type B alkali); casein from milk at pH 4.6.
- Papain (papaya), bromelain (pineapple), serratiopeptidase (*Serratia*), urokinase (urine/kidney cells), streptokinase (group C streptococci), pepsin (stomach).
- Castor oil: ricinoleic acid, miscible with alcohol, purgative; chaulmoogra oil: cyclopentenyl acids, dextrorotatory, leprosy; wool fat: sterol esters, absorbs water; beeswax: myricyl palmitate + cerotic acid.
- Marine: cytarabine (sponge), trabectedin (tunicate), eribulin (sponge), ziconotide (cone snail), cephalosporins (marine fungus).

# How to use this guide

Pharmacognosy and Phytochemistry I is a descriptive subject: marks come from clear definitions, neat classifications, correct biological sources and well-labelled tables and diagrams. Study one unit a day. Read the notes, then close them and write out every classification table and every drug monograph (source, family, chemistry, tests, uses) from memory. Finish with the question bank and the MCQs for that unit.

The five units follow the five units of the PCI syllabus exactly. Unit 5 is the longest because it covers many individual drugs; spread it over two days.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ questions | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

The question bank has long essay (10-mark), short answer (5-mark) and very short (2-mark) questions. Very short questions help with Section A and with quick revision.

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with tables, examples and a diagram where one fits.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from syllabus weight and common patterns in PCI-syllabus pharmacy papers, not a promise of what will be asked.

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. Every drug monograph has the same skeleton: **biological source with family → geographical source → collection/preparation → description → chemical constituents → tests → uses → adulterants or storage.** Write each heading even if you remember only one line under it.
2. Write the biological name in full the first time, genus with a capital letter and species in small letters (underline it in handwriting), followed by the family: *Acacia senegal*, Leguminosae.
3. For classification questions use a table with one example per class. Tables earn marks fast and are easy to check.
4. For identification tests give **reagent → observation → what it shows** (for example, Shinoda: Mg + HCl → pink/red → flavonoids).
5. Draw simple labelled diagrams: steps of tissue culture, the growth curve of a suspension culture, the lycopodium spore method, camera lucida set-up, auxin–cytokinin balance.
6. Learn the few formulas in this subject cold: stomatal index = S × 100/(E + S); lycopodium count = N × W × 94,000/(S × M); foaming index = 1000/a.
7. In 10-mark essays on evaluation, tissue culture or secondary metabolites, end with a short list of pharmaceutical applications — examiners look for the link to medicines.
8. Do not confuse similar pairs: organised vs unorganised drugs; hydrolysable vs condensed tannins; tragacanthin vs bassorin; agarose vs agaropectin; Type A vs Type B gelatin; urokinase vs streptokinase; stomatal number vs stomatal index.

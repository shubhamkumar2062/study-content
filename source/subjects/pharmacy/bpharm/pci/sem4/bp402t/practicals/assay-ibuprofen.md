# Assay of ibuprofen

**Aim:** To determine the percentage purity of ibuprofen by direct acid–base titration in alcohol.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your own lab manual, the current Indian Pharmacopoeia monograph and your teacher where they differ.

## Principle

Ibuprofen, 2-(4-isobutylphenyl)propanoic acid, is a carboxylic acid that is practically insoluble in water but dissolves freely in alcohol. It is therefore dissolved in **neutralised ethanol** and titrated directly with standard **sodium hydroxide** using phenolphthalein, the alcohol serving only as the solvent.

```text
R-COOH  +  NaOH   →   R-COONa  +  H2O
```

**Each ml of 0.1 M sodium hydroxide is equivalent to 20.63 mg of ibuprofen** (molecular weight 206.28, one carboxyl group).

## Requirements

Ibuprofen sample, ethanol (95%), 0.1 M sodium hydroxide (standardised against potassium hydrogen phthalate), phenolphthalein solution, conical flask 250 ml, burette, analytical balance.

## Procedure

1. Take about 50 ml of ethanol in a conical flask, add a few drops of phenolphthalein and **neutralise it** by adding 0.1 M sodium hydroxide drop by drop until a faint permanent pink appears. This removes any acidity of the solvent.
2. Weigh accurately about 0.5 g of ibuprofen and dissolve it in the neutralised alcohol; the pink colour disappears.
3. Titrate with 0.1 M sodium hydroxide, swirling constantly, until a pink colour persists for about 30 seconds.
4. Repeat until two concordant readings are obtained. A potentiometric end point may be used instead of the indicator.

Percentage purity = (V × molarity factor × 20.63 × 100) / (weight of sample in mg), where V is the volume of 0.1 M sodium hydroxide used and the molarity factor is (actual molarity / 0.1).

**Worked example.** Sample 0.5025 g (502.5 mg); titre 24.3 ml of 0.1 M sodium hydroxide. Ibuprofen found = 24.3 × 20.63 = 501.3 mg. Percentage purity = (501.3/502.5) × 100 = **99.8%**.

## Result

The sample of ibuprofen contains ___ % w/w of C13H18O2 (the pharmacopoeial limit is given in the current monograph, normally not less than 98.5% and not more than 101.0% on the dried basis).

## Precautions

1. Neutralise the alcohol before dissolving the drug; alcohol often carries traces of acid, which would give a high result.
2. Dissolve the sample completely before titrating, since undissolved particles react slowly and the end point creeps.
3. Add the titrant slowly near the end point and accept the first permanent pink.
4. Keep the flask stoppered between readings to limit loss of alcohol and entry of carbon dioxide.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is alcohol used as the solvent?** Ibuprofen is practically insoluble in water but freely soluble in alcohol, which takes no part in the reaction.
2. **Why must the alcohol be neutralised first?** Any free acid in the solvent would consume sodium hydroxide and be reported as drug.
3. **Why is phenolphthalein suitable?** The titration is of a weak acid with a strong base, so the equivalence point is on the alkaline side, where phenolphthalein changes.
4. **Which enantiomer of ibuprofen is active?** The S enantiomer inhibits cyclo-oxygenase; the R form is largely converted to S in the body, and the marketed drug is the racemate.
5. **To which chemical class does ibuprofen belong?** The arylpropionic acids, which also include naproxen and ketoprofen.
6. **Name another method of assay for ibuprofen.** Ultraviolet spectrophotometry and high performance liquid chromatography, which are also used for its formulations.

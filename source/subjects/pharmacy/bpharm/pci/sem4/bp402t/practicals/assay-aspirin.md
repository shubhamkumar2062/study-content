# Assay of aspirin

**Aim:** To determine the percentage purity of aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) by back titration after alkaline hydrolysis.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your own lab manual, the current Indian Pharmacopoeia monograph and your teacher where they differ.

## Principle

Aspirin is both an acid and an ester. If it were titrated directly, only the free carboxyl group would be measured. In the official method an **excess of standard sodium hydroxide** is added and the mixture is boiled: one molecule of alkali neutralises the carboxylic acid and a second hydrolyses the acetyl ester, giving salicylate and acetate. The unused alkali is then **back titrated** with standard acid.

```text
Step 1  CH3COO-C6H4-COOH  +  NaOH        →  CH3COO-C6H4-COONa  +  H2O
Step 2  CH3COO-C6H4-COONa +  NaOH  (heat) →  HO-C6H4-COONa     +  CH3COONa
```

Two moles of alkali are therefore used per mole of aspirin, so **each ml of 0.5 M sodium hydroxide is equivalent to 45.04 mg of aspirin** (molecular weight 180.16, half of it per mole of alkali).

## Requirements

Aspirin sample, 0.5 M sodium hydroxide (standardised), 0.5 M hydrochloric acid (standardised), phenolphthalein solution, conical flask 250 ml with a reflux or air condenser, water bath, burette, analytical balance.

## Procedure

1. Weigh accurately about 1.5 g of aspirin into a 250 ml conical flask.
2. Add exactly 50.0 ml of 0.5 M sodium hydroxide from a pipette or burette.
3. Fit an air condenser and boil the mixture gently on a water bath for about 10 minutes to complete the hydrolysis; then cool to room temperature.
4. Add 2 to 3 drops of phenolphthalein; the solution is pink because alkali remains.
5. Titrate the excess alkali with 0.5 M hydrochloric acid until the pink colour just disappears.
6. Carry out a **blank**: repeat steps 2 to 5 without the sample, so that any carbonate in the alkali is allowed for.
7. Repeat for concordant readings.

## Observations and calculation

| Reading | Weight of sample (g) | 0.5 M NaOH added (ml) | 0.5 M HCl used (ml) | Volume of NaOH consumed (ml) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | | 50.0 | | |
| 2 | | 50.0 | | |
| Blank | — | 50.0 | | — |

Volume of alkali consumed by the drug, V = (blank titre − sample titre) in ml of 0.5 M hydrochloric acid, which is numerically equal to the ml of 0.5 M sodium hydroxide used.

Percentage purity = (V × molarity factor × 45.04 × 100) / (weight of sample in mg).

**Worked example.** Sample 1.5020 g (1502.0 mg); blank titre 50.0 ml; sample titre 16.6 ml; both solutions exactly 0.5 M. V = 50.0 − 16.6 = 33.4 ml. Aspirin found = 33.4 × 45.04 = 1504.3 mg. Percentage purity = (1504.3/1502.0) × 100 = **100.2%**.

## Result

The sample of aspirin contains ___ % w/w of C9H8O4 (the pharmacopoeial limit is given in the current monograph, normally not less than 99.5% and not more than 101.0%).

## Precautions

1. Add the alkali accurately; the whole assay depends on knowing exactly how much was taken.
2. Boil long enough for complete hydrolysis, but keep the condenser fitted so that nothing is lost by evaporation.
3. Cool before titrating; a hot solution gives a drifting end point with phenolphthalein.
4. Always run a blank with the same alkali, since carbonate in the sodium hydroxide affects the result.
5. Store aspirin in a dry place; it hydrolyses on standing in moist air, which shows as a smell of acetic acid and a positive ferric chloride test.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is a back titration used instead of a direct one?** A direct titration would measure only the free carboxyl group; the back titration measures the ester as well, which is what the monograph requires.
2. **How many moles of alkali react with one mole of aspirin?** Two: one neutralises the acid and one hydrolyses the ester.
3. **Why is phenolphthalein chosen?** The final titration is of a strong base with a strong acid in the presence of weak acid salts, and the colour change on the alkaline side suits this system.
4. **How can free salicylic acid in the sample be detected?** By the violet colour given with neutral ferric chloride solution; the monograph sets a limit for it.
5. **Why does the antiplatelet action of aspirin last about a week?** It acetylates cyclo-oxygenase irreversibly, and the platelet has no nucleus to make new enzyme.
6. **What is the purpose of the blank determination?** To correct for carbonate and for any acidity or alkalinity of the reagents and the glassware.

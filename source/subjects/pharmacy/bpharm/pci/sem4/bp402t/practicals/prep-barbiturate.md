# Preparation of a barbiturate (barbituric acid)

**Aim:** To prepare barbituric acid by condensing diethyl malonate with urea in the presence of sodium ethoxide, and to report the yield and melting point.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Sodium metal reacts violently with water: cut it under kerosene, handle with dry forceps and never let it touch water or wet glassware.

## Principle

A barbiturate is a cyclic ureide formed from a **malonic ester and urea**. Sodium ethoxide, made from sodium and absolute alcohol, removes a proton and makes urea nucleophilic; each nitrogen then attacks one ester group, two molecules of ethanol are eliminated and the six-membered ring closes. The product separates first as its sodium salt and is set free by acid.

```text
CH2(COOC2H5)2  +  H2N-CO-NH2   --NaOC2H5-->   barbituric acid  +  2 C2H5OH
```

The hypnotic barbiturates are made by the same route after both hydrogens at C5 have been replaced: diethyl **diethyl**malonate with urea gives barbital, and diethyl ethylphenylmalonate gives phenobarbital. Barbituric acid itself is **not** a hypnotic, because C5 carries no substituents.

## Requirements

Sodium metal 1.5 g, absolute ethanol 40 ml, diethyl malonate 6 ml, dry urea 4 g, concentrated hydrochloric acid, round-bottomed flask 250 ml with a calcium chloride guard tube, water bath, Buchner funnel, melting point apparatus.

## Procedure

1. Dissolve 1.5 g of clean, dry sodium metal in 40 ml of absolute ethanol in a dry 250 ml round-bottomed flask fitted with a condenser and a calcium chloride guard tube. Allow the sodium to react completely.
2. To the warm sodium ethoxide solution add 6 ml of diethyl malonate and swirl.
3. Add 4 g of dry urea dissolved in the least quantity of warm absolute alcohol.
4. Reflux the mixture on a water bath for about two hours, protecting it from moisture. A white solid (the sodium salt) separates.
5. Cool the mixture, add about 50 ml of warm water to dissolve the sodium salt.
6. Acidify the clear solution with concentrated hydrochloric acid until just acidic to litmus and cool in ice. Barbituric acid crystallises out.
7. Filter under suction, wash with a little cold water, dry and recrystallise from hot water.
8. Record the weight and melting point and calculate the percentage yield.

**Worked example.** 6 ml of diethyl malonate (density about 1.055 g/ml) is 6.33 g, that is 0.0395 mol (molecular weight 160.17). One mole gives one mole of barbituric acid (molecular weight 128.09), so the theoretical yield is 0.0395 × 128.09 = 5.06 g. If 3.0 g is obtained, the yield is (3.0/5.06) × 100 = **59%**.

## Precautions

1. Everything must be dry: absolute alcohol, dry urea and dry glassware, since water destroys sodium ethoxide.
2. Add sodium in small pieces and let each dissolve before adding the next; keep the flask protected from moisture with a guard tube.
3. Do not let unreacted sodium remain at the end of the experiment; destroy any residue carefully with alcohol, never with water.
4. Acidify only to the point where the solution is just acidic, and cool well before filtering.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is sodium ethoxide used?** It is a strong enough base to generate the nucleophile and to keep the reaction free of water; it also drives off ethanol as the ring closes.
2. **Why is barbituric acid itself inactive as a hypnotic?** Both hydrogens at C5 are free; activity requires a 5,5-disubstituted compound with about six to ten carbon atoms in the two substituents.
3. **How would you prepare barbital by this route?** Alkylate diethyl malonate twice with ethyl bromide and sodium ethoxide, then condense the diethyl diethylmalonate with urea.
4. **Why are barbiturates acidic?** The imide hydrogen between the two carbonyl groups is weakly acidic, and the resulting anion is stabilised by resonance; the sodium salts are therefore water soluble.
5. **What happens to the duration of action if the C2 oxygen is replaced by sulphur?** The compound becomes far more lipid soluble and ultra-short acting, as with thiopental.
6. **Which simple test identifies a barbiturate?** It gives a violet colour with cobalt nitrate in an alkaline medium, and it is soluble in sodium hydroxide, being reprecipitated by acid.

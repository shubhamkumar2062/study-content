# Assay of chlorpromazine hydrochloride

**Aim:** To determine the percentage purity of chlorpromazine hydrochloride by non-aqueous titration.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your own lab manual, the current Indian Pharmacopoeia monograph and your teacher where they differ. Perchloric acid, glacial acetic acid and acetic anhydride are corrosive, and mercuric acetate is highly toxic: handle in a fume cupboard and collect mercury waste separately.

## Principle

Chlorpromazine hydrochloride is the salt of a weak base (the aliphatic tertiary amine of the side chain; the ring nitrogen is not appreciably basic) with a strong acid. In water this base is too weak to be titrated sharply. In **glacial acetic acid**, which is a weakly basic solvent for this purpose, the base is strengthened and can be titrated with **perchloric acid**, the strongest acid in that medium.

The chloride of the salt would otherwise behave as a strong acid and prevent titration, so **mercuric acetate** is added; it ties up the chloride as undissociated mercuric chloride and liberates an equivalent amount of acetate, which is titratable.

```text
Drug.HCl + Hg(CH3COO)2  →  Drug.CH3COOH  +  HgCl2 (undissociated)
Drug (base) + HClO4     →  Drug.HClO4
```

**Each ml of 0.1 M perchloric acid is equivalent to 35.53 mg of chlorpromazine hydrochloride** (molecular weight 355.33, one basic centre titrated).

## Requirements

Chlorpromazine hydrochloride sample, glacial acetic acid, 5% w/v mercuric acetate in glacial acetic acid, 0.1 M perchloric acid (standardised against potassium hydrogen phthalate), crystal violet indicator (0.5% in glacial acetic acid) or a potentiometric assembly, conical flask with stopper, burette, analytical balance.

## Procedure

1. Weigh accurately about 0.5 g of the sample and transfer it to a dry conical flask.
2. Dissolve it in about 50 ml of glacial acetic acid, warming gently if needed, and cool.
3. Add 10 ml of mercuric acetate solution and 2 to 3 drops of crystal violet indicator.
4. Titrate with 0.1 M perchloric acid until the violet colour changes to blue-green (or determine the end point potentiometrically).
5. Carry out a **blank titration** with the same volumes of all reagents but no sample, and subtract the blank reading.
6. Repeat until two concordant readings are obtained.

Corrected volume V = (titre − blank), in ml.

Percentage purity = (V × molarity factor × 35.53 × 100) / (weight of sample in mg), where the molarity factor is (actual molarity of perchloric acid / 0.1).

**Worked example.** Sample 0.5120 g (512.0 mg); titre 14.6 ml; blank 0.2 ml; perchloric acid exactly 0.1 M. Corrected volume = 14.4 ml. Chlorpromazine hydrochloride found = 14.4 × 35.53 = 511.6 mg. Percentage purity = (511.6/512.0) × 100 = **99.9%**.

## Result

The sample of chlorpromazine hydrochloride contains ___ % w/w of C17H19ClN2S,HCl (the pharmacopoeial limit is given in the current monograph, normally not less than 98.5% and not more than 101.0% on the dried basis).

## Precautions

1. All glassware and reagents must be dry; water in the system blunts the end point because it is a stronger base than the drug.
2. Standardise the perchloric acid on the day of use and apply a temperature correction if the laboratory temperature differs much from that of standardisation, since glacial acetic acid expands markedly.
3. Always run a blank; mercuric acetate solution itself consumes a little titrant.
4. Dispose of mercury-containing waste in the labelled container, never down the sink.
5. Protect the drug from light; phenothiazines oxidise to pink or violet products in light and air.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is the titration carried out in a non-aqueous medium?** The drug is a weak base whose salt gives no sharp end point in water; glacial acetic acid enhances its basic strength so a sharp titration is possible.
2. **What is the role of mercuric acetate?** It removes the chloride ion as undissociated mercuric chloride, replacing a strong acid by titratable acetate.
3. **Why is crystal violet suitable?** It changes colour in the acidic non-aqueous range, giving a violet to blue-green transition at the end point.
4. **Why is a blank required?** The reagents, especially mercuric acetate and the solvent, consume a small volume of titrant that must be subtracted.
5. **Name another assay method for chlorpromazine.** Ultraviolet spectrophotometry and cerimetric titration have been used; high performance liquid chromatography is used for related substances.
6. **Why must the sample be protected from light?** The phenothiazine ring oxidises to sulphoxide and coloured products, which would make the assay and the colour of the solution unreliable.

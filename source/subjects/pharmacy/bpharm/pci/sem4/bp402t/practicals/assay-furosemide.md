# Assay of furosemide

**Aim:** To determine the percentage purity of furosemide by titration in dimethylformamide with sodium hydroxide.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your own lab manual, the current Indian Pharmacopoeia monograph and your teacher where they differ. Dimethylformamide is harmful by inhalation and through the skin; work in a fume cupboard with gloves.

## Principle

Furosemide, 4-chloro-2-(furan-2-ylmethylamino)-5-sulphamoylbenzoic acid, carries a **carboxylic acid** group and a weakly acidic sulphonamide group. It is practically insoluble in water, so the assay is carried out in **dimethylformamide**, a basic solvent in which the acid is strengthened and dissolves completely. It is then titrated with standard **sodium hydroxide**, the end point being taken with bromothymol blue or, better, potentiometrically.

**Each ml of 0.1 M sodium hydroxide is equivalent to 33.07 mg of furosemide** (molecular weight 330.74, the carboxyl group being titrated).

## Requirements

Furosemide sample, dimethylformamide, 0.1 M sodium hydroxide (standardised, carbonate free), bromothymol blue indicator or a potentiometric assembly with a glass and calomel electrode, nitrogen supply, conical flask, burette with guard tube, analytical balance.

## Procedure

1. Weigh accurately about 0.25 g of furosemide and dissolve it in about 50 ml of dimethylformamide in a dry conical flask.
2. Pass a gentle stream of nitrogen over the solution to keep out carbon dioxide.
3. Add a few drops of bromothymol blue, or place the electrodes for a potentiometric titration.
4. Titrate with 0.1 M sodium hydroxide until the yellow colour changes to blue, or to the inflexion of the potentiometric curve.
5. Carry out a blank titration on the same volume of dimethylformamide and subtract it.
6. Repeat for concordant readings.

Corrected volume V = (titre − blank), in ml.

Percentage purity = (V × molarity factor × 33.07 × 100) / (weight of sample in mg).

**Worked example.** Sample 0.2510 g (251.0 mg); titre 7.8 ml; blank 0.2 ml; titrant exactly 0.1 M. Corrected volume = 7.6 ml. Furosemide found = 7.6 × 33.07 = 251.3 mg. Percentage purity = (251.3/251.0) × 100 = **100.1%**.

## Result

The sample of furosemide contains ___ % w/w of C12H11ClN2O5S (the pharmacopoeial limit is given in the current monograph, normally not less than 98.5% and not more than 101.0% on the dried basis).

## Precautions

1. Use dry, neutral dimethylformamide and run a blank, since the solvent itself is slightly acidic on storage.
2. Keep carbon dioxide away from the carbonate-free alkali; use a guard tube on the burette.
3. Dissolve the sample completely before titrating.
4. Protect furosemide from light, as it is light sensitive and discolours.

## Viva questions

1. **Which group of the molecule is titrated?** The carboxylic acid group; the sulphonamide group is much weaker and is not titrated under these conditions.
2. **Why is dimethylformamide used?** It dissolves the drug and, being a basic solvent, strengthens the weak acid so that a sharp end point is obtained.
3. **To which class of diuretics does furosemide belong and where does it act?** It is a high-ceiling (loop) diuretic acting on the sodium-potassium-two-chloride carrier in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle.
4. **What is the chemical relationship between furosemide and the sulphonamides?** It is an anthranilic acid derivative carrying a sulphamoyl group, a development from the sulphonamide diuretics.
5. **Why is a blank determination necessary?** To correct for acidic impurities in the solvent and for dissolved carbon dioxide.
6. **Name an important adverse effect of this drug.** Hypokalaemia, along with dehydration, hyponatraemia, hyperuricaemia and, with rapid high intravenous doses, ototoxicity.

# Preparation of 1,3-oxazole (2,4,5-triphenyloxazole)

**Aim:** To prepare 2,4,5-triphenyloxazole by the fusion of benzoin with benzamide, and to report the yield and melting point.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where they differ. The fusion is carried out at a high temperature, so use a sand bath and a clamp stand, never a naked flame on a glass flask held by hand.

## Principle

An oxazole is a five-membered ring containing one oxygen and one nitrogen at positions 1 and 3. In this **Robinson–Gabriel type synthesis** an alpha-hydroxy ketone (benzoin) reacts with an amide (benzamide). The amide nitrogen attacks the ketone carbonyl and the amide oxygen attacks the carbon bearing the hydroxyl; two molecules of water are then lost and the aromatic ring closes.

```text
C6H5-CO-CH(OH)-C6H5  +  C6H5-CO-NH2
        (benzoin)          (benzamide)
                 │ heat, −2 H2O
      2,4,5-triphenyloxazole   (phenyl groups at C2, C4 and C5)
```

## Requirements

Benzoin 5 g, benzamide 4 g, dry round-bottomed flask 100 ml with air condenser, sand bath, thermometer, ethanol for recrystallisation, Buchner funnel, melting point apparatus.

## Procedure

1. Mix 5 g of benzoin and 4 g of benzamide thoroughly in a dry 100 ml round-bottomed flask and fit an air condenser.
2. Heat the mixture on a sand bath so that it melts and is kept at about 160–180 °C for one and a half to two hours. Swirl occasionally.
3. Allow the melt to cool a little, then pour it while still warm into about 50 ml of cold water with stirring; a solid mass separates.
4. Break up the solid, filter under suction and wash it with warm water to remove unchanged benzamide, which is water soluble.
5. Wash the residue with a little cold alcohol to remove unchanged benzoin.
6. Recrystallise the product from hot ethanol and dry it in a desiccator.
7. Record the weight and the melting point and calculate the percentage yield.

**Worked example.** 5 g of benzoin (molecular weight 212.25) is 0.0236 mol. One mole gives one mole of 2,4,5-triphenyloxazole (molecular weight 297.35), so the theoretical yield is 0.0236 × 297.35 = 7.01 g. If 4.2 g of dry product is obtained, the yield is (4.2/7.01) × 100 = **60%**.

## Precautions

1. Both reactants must be dry; moisture reduces the yield because the reaction itself eliminates water.
2. Keep the temperature in the stated range. Below it the reaction is incomplete; well above it the product chars.
3. Pour the hot melt into water carefully and away from the face.
4. Wash with warm water first to remove benzamide; washing with alcohol first dissolves some product.

## Viva questions

1. **Which atoms occupy positions 1 and 3 of the ring?** Oxygen at position 1 and nitrogen at position 3; this is why the ring is called 1,3-oxazole.
2. **What is the role of benzamide?** It supplies the nitrogen and the C2 carbon of the ring and becomes the 2-phenyl substituent.
3. **Why is the product washed with warm water?** Unreacted benzamide dissolves in warm water, while the oxazole does not.
4. **Name a drug containing an oxazole or isoxazole ring.** Cloxacillin and sulphamethoxazole contain an isoxazole ring; oxazole and oxazolidinedione rings appear in trimethadione and in linezolid's oxazolidinone.
5. **How does an oxazole differ from an isoxazole and an imidazole?** In isoxazole the oxygen and nitrogen are adjacent (1,2); in imidazole both heteroatoms are nitrogen at positions 1 and 3.
6. **Is the ring basic?** Only weakly: the nitrogen lone pair is in the plane of the ring and is available, but the electronegative oxygen reduces the basicity well below that of imidazole.

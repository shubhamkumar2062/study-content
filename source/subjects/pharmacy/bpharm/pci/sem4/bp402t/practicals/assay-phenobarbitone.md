# Assay of phenobarbitone

**Aim:** To determine the percentage purity of phenobarbitone by non-aqueous titration with sodium methoxide.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your own lab manual, the current Indian Pharmacopoeia monograph and your teacher where they differ. Dimethylformamide and methanol are harmful by inhalation; sodium methoxide is corrosive. Work in a fume cupboard.

## Principle

Phenobarbitone is a very weak acid: the hydrogen of the imide group (-CO-NH-CO-) between the two carbonyl groups can be removed because the resulting anion is stabilised by resonance. The acid is far too weak for a sharp titration in water, so a **basic solvent, dimethylformamide**, is used. It accepts the proton readily and so strengthens the acid, allowing titration with the strong base **sodium methoxide** in a methanol–toluene mixture.

```text
Phenobarbitone (imide H)  +  CH3ONa   →   sodium phenobarbitone  +  CH3OH
```

**Each ml of 0.1 M sodium methoxide is equivalent to 23.22 mg of phenobarbitone** (molecular weight 232.24, one acidic hydrogen titrated).

## Requirements

Phenobarbitone sample, dimethylformamide (neutralised), 0.1 M sodium methoxide (standardised against benzoic acid), thymol blue indicator (0.3% in methanol) or a potentiometric assembly, nitrogen cylinder with delivery tube, conical flask with a two-holed stopper, burette protected with a guard tube, analytical balance.

## Procedure

1. Weigh accurately about 0.2 g of phenobarbitone and dissolve it in about 25 ml of dimethylformamide in a dry conical flask.
2. Pass a slow stream of **nitrogen** through the flask throughout the titration, to keep out carbon dioxide and moisture.
3. Add 2 to 3 drops of thymol blue indicator; the solution is yellow.
4. Titrate with 0.1 M sodium methoxide from a burette fitted with a guard tube until the colour changes to **blue**.
5. Perform a blank titration on the same volume of dimethylformamide and subtract it.
6. Repeat for concordant readings.

Corrected volume V = (titre − blank), in ml.

Percentage purity = (V × molarity factor × 23.22 × 100) / (weight of sample in mg).

**Worked example.** Sample 0.2015 g (201.5 mg); titre 9.0 ml; blank 0.3 ml; titrant exactly 0.1 M. Corrected volume = 8.7 ml. Phenobarbitone found = 8.7 × 23.22 = 202.0 mg. Percentage purity = (202.0/201.5) × 100 = **100.2%**.

## Result

The sample of phenobarbitone contains ___ % w/w of C12H12N2O3 (the pharmacopoeial limit is given in the current monograph, normally not less than 99.0% and not more than 101.0% on the dried basis).

## Precautions

1. Keep carbon dioxide out: it is an acid in this medium and would be titrated along with the drug, so the nitrogen stream and the guard tube are essential.
2. Use neutralised, water-free dimethylformamide and standardise the titrant on the day of use, as sodium methoxide absorbs carbon dioxide on storage.
3. Always subtract the blank.
4. Add the indicator after dissolving the sample, and judge the first permanent blue tint as the end point.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is phenobarbitone acidic?** The imide hydrogen between the two carbonyl groups is removed easily because the resulting anion is resonance stabilised.
2. **Why is dimethylformamide used?** It is a basic (protophilic) solvent that accepts the proton and so levels up the apparent strength of a very weak acid, giving a sharp end point.
3. **Why pass nitrogen through the flask?** To exclude atmospheric carbon dioxide and moisture, both of which would consume titrant and blur the end point.
4. **What indicator is used and what is the colour change?** Thymol blue, which changes from yellow to blue.
5. **Name an alternative assay for a barbiturate.** Argentimetric titration with silver nitrate in an alkaline medium, or an ultraviolet spectrophotometric method in alkaline borate buffer.
6. **Why is the sodium salt of phenobarbitone used for injection?** The free acid is practically insoluble in water, while the sodium salt dissolves readily, though the solution is alkaline and hydrolyses on storage.

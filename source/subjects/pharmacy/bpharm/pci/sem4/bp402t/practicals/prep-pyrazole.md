# Preparation of 1,3-pyrazole (3,5-dimethylpyrazole)

**Aim:** To prepare 3,5-dimethylpyrazole from acetylacetone and hydrazine hydrate, and to report the yield and melting point.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Hydrazine hydrate is toxic and corrosive: use gloves, goggles and a fume cupboard.

## Principle

A pyrazole is a five-membered ring with two adjacent nitrogen atoms. It is made by a **Knorr pyrazole synthesis**: a 1,3-diketone reacts with hydrazine, each nitrogen attacking one carbonyl group. Two molecules of water are lost and the aromatic ring closes.

```text
CH3-CO-CH2-CO-CH3  +  H2N-NH2   →   3,5-dimethylpyrazole  +  2 H2O
   (acetylacetone)    (hydrazine)        (ring with N-N)
```

The ring is aromatic, weakly basic at N2 and weakly acidic at N1-H, so the product dissolves in both dilute acid and strong alkali.

## Requirements

Acetylacetone (pentane-2,4-dione) 5 ml, hydrazine hydrate (99%) about 2.5 ml, ethanol 15 ml, round-bottomed flask 100 ml, water condenser, water bath, ice bath, Buchner funnel, melting point apparatus.

## Procedure

1. Dissolve 5 ml of acetylacetone in 10 ml of ethanol in a 100 ml round-bottomed flask.
2. Cool the flask in an ice bath and **add the hydrazine hydrate drop by drop** with shaking. The reaction is exothermic.
3. Fit a water condenser and reflux the mixture on a water bath for about 30 minutes.
4. Cool the flask, then pour the contents into about 50 ml of crushed ice and water with stirring.
5. Allow the solid to separate, filter under suction on a Buchner funnel and wash with a little cold water.
6. Recrystallise the crude product from hot water (or aqueous ethanol), dry it between filter papers and then in a desiccator.
7. Record the weight and the melting point, and report the percentage yield.

Percentage yield = (practical yield / theoretical yield) × 100.

**Worked example.** 5 ml of acetylacetone (density about 0.97 g/ml) is 4.85 g, that is 0.0485 mol. One mole of diketone gives one mole of 3,5-dimethylpyrazole (molecular weight 96.13), so the theoretical yield is 0.0485 × 96.13 = 4.66 g. If 3.4 g of dry product is obtained, the yield is (3.4/4.66) × 100 = **73%**.

## Precautions

1. Add hydrazine hydrate slowly and with cooling, since the reaction gives out heat.
2. Keep hydrazine off the skin and avoid breathing the vapour; work in a fume cupboard.
3. Reflux gently; vigorous boiling loses acetylacetone, which is volatile.
4. Dry the crystals completely before taking the melting point, since moisture lowers it.

## Viva questions

1. **What type of reaction is this?** A cyclocondensation (Knorr pyrazole synthesis) between a 1,3-diketone and a hydrazine, with loss of two water molecules.
2. **Why is the product aromatic?** The five-membered ring has six delocalised electrons, counting the lone pair of the N1 nitrogen, so it satisfies Huckel's rule.
3. **Why does the product dissolve in both acid and alkali?** N2 is basic and takes a proton, while the N1-H hydrogen is weakly acidic, so pyrazoles are amphoteric.
4. **Name two drugs containing a pyrazole or pyrazolone ring.** Phenylbutazone and antipyrine (also celecoxib and metamizole).
5. **Why is ethanol used as the solvent?** It dissolves both reactants, boils low enough for a water bath, and the product crystallises when the mixture is poured into water.
6. **What would an unsymmetrical diketone give?** A mixture of two isomeric pyrazoles, since either nitrogen can attack either carbonyl group.

# Preparation of benzimidazole

**Aim:** To prepare benzimidazole from o-phenylenediamine and formic acid, and to report the yield and melting point.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where they differ. o-Phenylenediamine is harmful and stains the skin; formic acid is corrosive.

## Principle

Benzimidazole is a benzene ring fused to an imidazole ring. It is prepared by the **Phillips synthesis**: an aromatic ortho-diamine is condensed with a carboxylic acid. One amino group forms the amide, the second attacks the same carbon, and loss of two water molecules closes the five-membered ring. Formic acid, having one carbon, gives benzimidazole itself; acetic acid would give 2-methylbenzimidazole.

```text
o-C6H4(NH2)2  +  HCOOH   →   benzimidazole  +  2 H2O
```

## Requirements

o-Phenylenediamine 5 g, formic acid (90%) 3 ml, round-bottomed flask 100 ml, water condenser, water bath, 10% ammonia solution, activated charcoal, Buchner funnel, melting point apparatus.

## Procedure

1. Place 5 g of o-phenylenediamine in a 100 ml round-bottomed flask and add 3 ml of 90% formic acid.
2. Fit a water condenser and heat the mixture on a water bath at about 100 °C for two hours.
3. Cool the flask to room temperature.
4. Add 10% ammonia solution slowly with stirring until the mixture is just alkaline to litmus; the crude benzimidazole separates as a solid.
5. Filter under suction, wash the solid with cold water until the washings are neutral, and press it dry.
6. Dissolve the crude solid in about 100 ml of boiling water, add a little activated charcoal, boil for a few minutes and filter hot.
7. Cool the filtrate in ice; colourless needles of benzimidazole separate. Filter, dry and record the weight and melting point.

**Worked example.** 5 g of o-phenylenediamine (molecular weight 108.14) is 0.0462 mol. One mole gives one mole of benzimidazole (molecular weight 118.14), so the theoretical yield is 0.0462 × 118.14 = 5.46 g. If 3.8 g is obtained, the yield is (3.8/5.46) × 100 = **70%**.

## Precautions

1. Use a slight excess of formic acid, but not a large excess, or the product remains in solution as the formate salt.
2. Neutralise with ammonia slowly and with stirring; the diamine itself dissolves in acid, so precipitation is complete only when the mixture is alkaline.
3. Use charcoal sparingly; too much adsorbs the product and lowers the yield.
4. Dry the crystals fully before the melting point is taken.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the role of ammonia at the end?** It neutralises the excess formic acid and frees benzimidazole from its salt, so the solid separates.
2. **Why is charcoal used?** o-Phenylenediamine oxidises easily and gives coloured impurities; charcoal adsorbs them and gives colourless crystals.
3. **Which drugs contain a benzimidazole ring?** Omeprazole and the other proton pump inhibitors, albendazole and mebendazole, and candesartan and telmisartan.
4. **What product would acetic acid give?** 2-Methylbenzimidazole, since the 2-position carries whatever group the acid supplies.
5. **Is benzimidazole acidic or basic?** Both weakly: the pyridine-type nitrogen is basic and the N-H is weakly acidic, so it is amphoteric and shows tautomerism between the two nitrogens.
6. **Why is water a good recrystallising solvent here?** Benzimidazole is much more soluble in hot water than in cold, so clean needles separate on cooling.

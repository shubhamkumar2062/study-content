# Preparation of 2,3-diphenylquinoxaline

**Aim:** To prepare 2,3-diphenylquinoxaline from benzil and o-phenylenediamine, and to report the yield and melting point.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where they differ.

## Principle

A quinoxaline is a benzene ring fused to a pyrazine ring. It is formed when an aromatic **ortho-diamine** condenses with a **1,2-diketone**: each amino group attacks one carbonyl carbon, two molecules of water are eliminated and the six-membered ring with two nitrogens closes. The reaction is fast, clean and almost quantitative, which is why it is also used as a test for 1,2-diketones.

```text
C6H5-CO-CO-C6H5  +  o-C6H4(NH2)2   →   2,3-diphenylquinoxaline  +  2 H2O
     (benzil)        (o-phenylenediamine)
```

## Requirements

Benzil 4 g, o-phenylenediamine 2.2 g, ethanol 40 ml, round-bottomed flask 100 ml, water condenser, water bath, Buchner funnel, melting point apparatus.

## Procedure

1. Dissolve 4 g of benzil in about 25 ml of warm ethanol in a 100 ml round-bottomed flask.
2. Dissolve 2.2 g of o-phenylenediamine in 15 ml of ethanol and add it to the flask.
3. Fit a water condenser and reflux the mixture on a water bath for about 30 minutes. The solution darkens as the reaction proceeds.
4. Cool the flask in ice; pale yellow crystals separate. If they do not, add a little water drop by drop to start crystallisation.
5. Filter under suction and wash with a little cold ethanol.
6. Recrystallise from hot ethanol, dry the crystals and record the weight and melting point.

**Worked example.** 4 g of benzil (molecular weight 210.23) is 0.0190 mol; 2.2 g of o-phenylenediamine (molecular weight 108.14) is 0.0203 mol, so benzil is the limiting reactant. One mole gives one mole of 2,3-diphenylquinoxaline (molecular weight 282.34), so the theoretical yield is 0.0190 × 282.34 = 5.37 g. If 4.3 g is obtained, the yield is (4.3/5.37) × 100 = **80%**.

## Precautions

1. Use a very slight excess of the diamine only; a large excess colours the product.
2. Reflux gently and keep the condenser running, since ethanol is volatile and inflammable. Never heat it over a flame.
3. Cool well before filtering so that crystallisation is complete.
4. Dry the crystals before taking the melting point.

## Viva questions

1. **What type of reaction is this?** A condensation (cyclocondensation) between a 1,2-diketone and an ortho-diamine, with loss of two water molecules.
2. **Why is the reaction used as a test for 1,2-diketones?** Only a diketone with the two carbonyls adjacent can form the six-membered ring, and the product is a well-defined crystalline solid.
3. **Name the ring system formed.** Quinoxaline, that is benzopyrazine: a benzene ring fused to a pyrazine ring with nitrogen at positions 1 and 4.
4. **How is benzil obtained?** By oxidising benzoin with concentrated nitric acid or with copper(II) sulphate in pyridine.
5. **Mention a medicinal use of the quinoxaline ring.** Quinoxaline 1,4-dioxides have antibacterial activity, and the ring appears in the antiviral and anticancer field as well as in dyes.
6. **Why is ethanol chosen as the solvent?** Both reactants dissolve in it when warm, and the product is much less soluble when cold, so it crystallises directly.

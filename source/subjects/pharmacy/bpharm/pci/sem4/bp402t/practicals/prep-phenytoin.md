# Preparation of phenytoin

**Aim:** To prepare phenytoin (5,5-diphenylhydantoin) from benzil and urea, and to report the yield and melting point.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Sodium hydroxide solution is corrosive; wear goggles.

## Principle

Benzil is a 1,2-diketone. In hot alcoholic alkali, one nitrogen of **urea** attacks a carbonyl carbon; the alkoxide then drives a **benzilic acid type rearrangement**, in which a phenyl group migrates so that both phenyl groups end up on the same carbon. The second nitrogen closes the five-membered hydantoin ring. The product first forms as the water-soluble sodium salt, and the free drug is precipitated by acid.

```text
C6H5-CO-CO-C6H5  +  H2N-CO-NH2   --NaOH/ethanol-->   sodium phenytoin
                                      --HCl-->       phenytoin (5,5-diphenylhydantoin)
```

If benzil is not available it is prepared first from benzoin by oxidation with concentrated nitric acid, benzoin itself being made from benzaldehyde by the benzoin condensation.

## Requirements

Benzil 4 g, urea 1.5 g, 30% sodium hydroxide solution 10 ml, ethanol (95%) 30 ml, dilute hydrochloric acid, activated charcoal, round-bottomed flask 250 ml, water condenser, water bath, Buchner funnel, melting point apparatus.

## Procedure

1. Place 4 g of benzil, 1.5 g of urea and 30 ml of ethanol in a 250 ml round-bottomed flask.
2. Add 10 ml of 30% sodium hydroxide solution, fit a water condenser and reflux the mixture on a water bath for about two hours.
3. Pour the hot mixture into about 100 ml of water, stir well and let it stand for 15 minutes. A small amount of insoluble by-product may appear.
4. Filter off the insoluble material; the filtrate contains the sodium salt of phenytoin.
5. Treat the filtrate with a little activated charcoal, boil and filter hot.
6. Cool the clear filtrate and acidify it carefully with dilute hydrochloric acid until just acidic to litmus. Phenytoin separates as a white solid.
7. Filter under suction, wash with cold water until the washings are neutral, dry, and recrystallise from ethanol.
8. Record the weight and the melting point and calculate the percentage yield.

**Worked example.** 4 g of benzil (molecular weight 210.23) is 0.0190 mol. One mole gives one mole of phenytoin (molecular weight 252.27), so the theoretical yield is 0.0190 × 252.27 = 4.79 g. If 3.1 g is obtained, the yield is (3.1/4.79) × 100 = **65%**.

## Precautions

1. Reflux long enough; a short reaction leaves unchanged benzil, which shows as yellow colour in the product.
2. Filter off the insoluble by-product before acidifying, or it contaminates the phenytoin.
3. Acidify slowly and only to the point where the solution is just acidic; a large excess of acid is wasteful and can make the product sticky.
4. Use a high-range melting point apparatus, since phenytoin melts near 300 °C.

## Viva questions

1. **Which rearrangement occurs in this preparation?** A benzilic acid type rearrangement, in which a phenyl group migrates from one carbonyl carbon to the next, so that both phenyls sit on C5 of the hydantoin ring.
2. **Why does the product first stay in solution?** The imide hydrogen is weakly acidic, so in alkali the drug exists as its soluble sodium salt.
3. **What is the role of urea?** It supplies the two nitrogen atoms and the C2 carbonyl of the hydantoin ring.
4. **How is benzil prepared?** By oxidation of benzoin with concentrated nitric acid, or with copper(II) sulphate in pyridine.
5. **What is the therapeutic use of phenytoin?** It is an anticonvulsant for generalised tonic-clonic and partial seizures and for status epilepticus; it also has antiarrhythmic use.
6. **Why is the injection given as the sodium salt?** The free acid is practically insoluble in water; the sodium salt dissolves, but the solution is strongly alkaline and must be given slowly.

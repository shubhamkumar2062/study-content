# How to use this guide

Medicinal Chemistry I is a memory subject with a logic behind it. Almost every mark comes from four things: **classification**, **structure–activity relationship (SAR)**, **mechanism of action with uses**, and the **synthesis of the starred drugs**. Work unit by unit: read the notes, write the SAR points as a labelled skeleton from memory, then write each starred synthesis as a numbered scheme without looking. Finish the unit with the question bank and the MCQs.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ or very short answers | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; learn the full answer with the structure skeleton, the SAR points and one synthesis.
- **Medium** — asked every few years, or as a part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for the objective section and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from syllabus weight and common patterns in pharmacy papers, not a promise of what will be asked.

**Where the marks usually come from**

| Unit | Topics that keep coming back |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Introduction | Physicochemical properties (ionization, partition coefficient, protein binding, chelation), bioisosterism with examples, phase I and phase II metabolism with reactions, factors affecting metabolism |
| 2 Adrenergic | Biosynthesis and catabolism of catecholamines, SAR of sympathomimetics, SAR of beta blockers, classification of adrenergic antagonists, synthesis of phenylephrine, salbutamol, tolazoline, propranolol |
| 3 Cholinergic | Biosynthesis of acetylcholine, SAR of parasympathomimetics, mechanism of anticholinesterases and pralidoxime, SAR and classification of anticholinergics, synthesis of carbachol, neostigmine, ipratropium, dicyclomine, procyclidine |
| 4 CNS I | SAR of benzodiazepines and barbiturates, SAR of phenothiazines with classification of antipsychotics, SAR and mechanism of anticonvulsants, synthesis of diazepam, barbital, chlorpromazine, phenytoin, ethosuximide, carbamazepine |
| 5 CNS II | Classification of general anaesthetics, SAR of morphine analogues, classification of NSAIDs, synthesis of halothane, methohexital, ketamine, fentanyl, methadone, mefenamic acid, ibuprofen |

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. **Draw first, write second.** Start a SAR answer with the labelled skeleton (phenylethylamine, aryloxypropanolamine, benzodiazepine, phenothiazine, barbituric acid, morphine "T"), then number the SAR points against the positions you have marked.
2. **Numbered schemes for synthesis.** Write each step as "reagent → product" with the name of the reaction where it has one (Friedel–Crafts, Mannich, Grignard, Knoevenagel, Ullmann, benzoin condensation, Pinner). Even if you cannot draw the structures neatly, a correct numbered sequence carries most of the marks.
3. **Classification answers should be a table or tree**, with one named example and one use per class. Examiners count the classes, not the adjectives.
4. **Link chemistry to the clinic in every answer.** Say *why* a catechol is inactive orally, *why* a quaternary nitrogen keeps a drug out of the brain, *why* thiopental wears off in minutes.
5. **Keep a drug list per unit** with class, mechanism and one use. Most objective questions are "which class does this drug belong to" or "which drug is the antidote".
6. **Learn the starred drugs cold.** Twenty-two drugs carry an asterisk in this syllabus and they are the most likely 10-mark questions: phenylephrine, salbutamol, tolazoline, propranolol, carbachol, neostigmine, ipratropium bromide, dicyclomine, procyclidine, diazepam, barbital, chlorpromazine, phenytoin, ethosuximide, carbamazepine, halothane, methohexital sodium, ketamine, fentanyl, methadone, mefenamic acid, ibuprofen.
7. **Do not mix up the two SARs that look alike.** Sympathomimetics use a phenylethylamine skeleton (nitrogen substituent decides alpha or beta); anticholinergics need two bulky rings and a cationic nitrogen. Writing one for the other loses the whole question.
8. **For practicals**, remember the reaction type, the melting point range, the recrystallising solvent and the titrant with its equivalence factor. Viva questions come from exactly these four.

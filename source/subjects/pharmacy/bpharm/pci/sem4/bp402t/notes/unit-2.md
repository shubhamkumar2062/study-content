# Unit 2: Adrenergic drugs

The sympathetic (adrenergic) nerves use **noradrenaline**, and the adrenal medulla releases **adrenaline**, into the blood. Drugs that copy these transmitters are **sympathomimetics**; drugs that block their receptors are **adrenergic antagonists**. The syllabus asks for the biosynthesis and breakdown of catecholamines, the receptors, the SAR of sympathomimetic amines and of beta blockers, and the synthesis of the starred drugs: **phenylephrine, salbutamol, tolazoline and propranolol**.

## 2.1 Biosynthesis of catecholamines

All catecholamines come from the amino acid **tyrosine**.

```text
Phenylalanine
   │ phenylalanine hydroxylase
Tyrosine
   │ tyrosine hydroxylase  ← RATE-LIMITING step (needs tetrahydrobiopterin)
DOPA (3,4-dihydroxyphenylalanine)
   │ aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (DOPA decarboxylase, pyridoxal phosphate)
Dopamine
   │ dopamine beta-hydroxylase (inside the storage vesicle, needs Cu and vitamin C)
Noradrenaline
   │ phenylethanolamine-N-methyltransferase (PNMT, adrenal medulla, needs SAM)
Adrenaline
```

Points examiners look for:

- **Tyrosine hydroxylase** is rate-limiting and is inhibited by metyrosine; it is also controlled by end-product (feedback) inhibition.
- **Carbidopa and benserazide** block DOPA decarboxylase outside the brain; this is why they are given with levodopa.
- Noradrenaline is stored in vesicles with ATP and chromogranin; release is by exocytosis when calcium enters the nerve ending.
- PNMT is found chiefly in the adrenal medulla, so adrenaline is mainly a hormone, noradrenaline mainly a transmitter.

## 2.2 Catabolism (termination of action)

1. **Reuptake 1** into the nerve ending (uptake-1) is the main way the signal stops; cocaine and tricyclic antidepressants block it. **Uptake-2** takes the amine into surrounding tissue.
2. **Monoamine oxidase (MAO)**, on the outer mitochondrial membrane inside the nerve, deaminates the amine to an aldehyde, then to **DOPEG / DOMA** and finally to **vanillylmandelic acid (VMA)**.
3. **Catechol-O-methyltransferase (COMT)**, outside the nerve, methylates the 3-OH group to give **normetanephrine and metanephrine**.

The combined product, **VMA**, is measured in urine to diagnose phaeochromocytoma. Dopamine's main urinary product is homovanillic acid. Because catechols are destroyed quickly by COMT, catecholamines are **not active orally and act only for minutes**; removing or shifting a hydroxyl group (ephedrine, phenylephrine, salbutamol) produces oral, longer-acting drugs.

## 2.3 Adrenergic receptors and their distribution

| Receptor | Main locations | Response | Second messenger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| α1 | Vascular smooth muscle, radial muscle of iris, bladder neck, prostate | Vasoconstriction, mydriasis, urinary retention | Gq → IP3/DAG, Ca²⁺ rise |
| α2 | Presynaptic nerve ending, CNS (vasomotor centre), platelets, pancreas | Less noradrenaline release, central fall in sympathetic outflow, platelet aggregation, less insulin | Gi → cAMP falls |
| β1 | Heart, juxtaglomerular cells | Rate and force rise, renin release | Gs → cAMP rises |
| β2 | Bronchi, uterus, skeletal-muscle vessels, liver | Bronchodilation, uterine relaxation, vasodilation, glycogenolysis | Gs → cAMP rises |
| β3 | Fat cells, bladder detrusor | Lipolysis, bladder relaxation | Gs → cAMP rises |
| D1 | Renal, mesenteric and coronary vessels | Vasodilation | Gs → cAMP rises |

Potency order: at alpha receptors adrenaline ≥ noradrenaline >> isoprenaline; at beta receptors isoprenaline > adrenaline > noradrenaline. Noradrenaline has very little β2 action, which is why it raises blood pressure without bronchodilation.

## 2.4 SAR of sympathomimetic agents

Take **β-phenylethylamine** as the parent: a benzene ring, a two-carbon chain and a terminal amino group.

```text
        OH (beta)
         |
 Ar —— C — C —— NH-R      Ar carries 3-OH and 4-OH in catecholamines
   (3,4-OH) |
          R' (alpha substituent)
```

**1. Aromatic ring.** A 3,4-dihydroxy (catechol) pattern gives maximum activity at both α and β receptors, but such drugs are destroyed by COMT and are inactive orally (adrenaline, noradrenaline, dopamine, isoprenaline).

- Removing the 4-OH (phenylephrine, metaraminol) keeps α activity, gives oral activity and a longer action.
- Replacing the 3-OH with a group COMT cannot methylate keeps β2 activity and allows oral use: **hydroxymethyl in salbutamol**, a 3,5-dihydroxy (resorcinol) pattern in terbutaline.
- Removing both OH groups (ephedrine, amphetamine) abolishes direct action but allows entry into the CNS and indirect release of noradrenaline.
- Imidazoline rings with a lipophilic aromatic group (naphazoline, oxymetazoline, xylometazoline) are α agonists used as nasal decongestants.

**2. Alcoholic –OH on the beta carbon.** Needed for good direct activity; the (R) configuration (laevorotatory) is far more active (Easson–Stedman three-point attachment). Loss of this OH (dopamine, amphetamine) reduces direct action and favours CNS and indirect effects.

**3. Substituent on the alpha carbon.** A methyl group (ephedrine, metaraminol, amphetamine) blocks MAO, prolonging action and giving oral activity; it also increases indirect action. The (S) form at this centre is more active for CNS stimulation.

**4. Substituent on nitrogen.** This is the main switch between alpha and beta selectivity.

| N-substituent | Effect | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H (primary amine) | α activity favoured | Noradrenaline |
| Methyl | α and β | Adrenaline |
| Isopropyl | β selective | Isoproterenol |
| tert-Butyl or larger branched | β2 selective | Terbutaline, salbutamol |
| Very bulky arylalkyl | β2 selective and long acting | Bitolterol, salmeterol |

Nitrogen must be basic enough to be protonated at body pH; a secondary amine is ideal. Quaternisation destroys activity.

## 2.5 Classification of sympathomimetics with uses

**Direct acting** (act on the receptor itself): noradrenaline (shock, vasopressor), adrenaline (anaphylaxis, cardiac arrest, with local anaesthetics), **phenylephrine*** (nasal decongestant, mydriatic, hypotension), dopamine (shock, in low dose renal vasodilation), methyldopa (converted to α-methylnoradrenaline, a central α2 agonist for hypertension in pregnancy), clonidine (central α2 agonist), dobutamine (β1, cardiogenic shock), isoproterenol (β1 and β2), terbutaline and **salbutamol*** (β2, asthma and premature labour), bitolterol (a prodrug ester), naphazoline, oxymetazoline and xylometazoline (imidazoline decongestants).

**Indirect acting** (release stored noradrenaline): hydroxyamphetamine, pseudoephedrine, propylhexedrine — decongestants; they show **tachyphylaxis** as stores run down.

**Mixed action**: ephedrine (asthma, decongestion, hypotension) and metaraminol (vasopressor) act both directly and by releasing noradrenaline.

### Synthesis of phenylephrine

1. Start from **3-hydroxyacetophenone**. Brominate the side-chain methyl group with bromine in a solvent such as acetic acid to give **ω-bromo-3-hydroxyacetophenone**.
2. Treat this with **N-benzylmethylamine**; the amine displaces bromide and gives the amino ketone, 3-hydroxy-ω-(N-benzyl-N-methylamino)acetophenone.
3. **Hydrogenate over palladium on charcoal.** This both reduces the ketone to a secondary alcohol and removes the benzyl protecting group, giving racemic phenylephrine.
4. **Resolve** the racemate with (+)-tartaric acid and liberate the base; the (−)-(R)-enantiomer is the active drug, marketed as the hydrochloride.

### Synthesis of salbutamol

1. **Methyl salicylate** is acetylated in the ring (Friedel–Crafts, acetyl chloride with aluminium chloride) to **methyl 5-acetylsalicylate**.
2. The side-chain methyl is brominated with bromine to give the **ω-bromoacetyl** compound.
3. Reaction with **N-benzyl-tert-butylamine** replaces bromide and gives the amino ketone.
4. **Reduction with lithium aluminium hydride** converts both the ketone to the secondary alcohol and the aromatic ester to a **hydroxymethyl (–CH2OH)** group — the change that protects the drug from COMT.
5. **Hydrogenolysis over palladium** removes the benzyl group, giving salbutamol, isolated as the sulphate (racemic; the (R) form carries the activity).

## 2.6 Adrenergic antagonists

### Alpha blockers

| Class | Drugs | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Imidazolines (competitive, short) | **Tolazoline***, phentolamine | Non-selective α1 and α2; vasodilators, phentolamine in phaeochromocytoma |
| Haloalkylamines (irreversible) | Phenoxybenzamine | Forms a covalent bond through an ethyleniminium ion; action lasts days |
| Quinazolines (α1 selective) | Prazosin, terazosin, doxazosin | Hypertension and benign prostatic hyperplasia; first-dose hypotension |
| Ergot alkaloids | Dihydroergotamine, methysergide | Partial agonist and 5-HT actions; migraine |

Non-selective blockers also block presynaptic α2, so more noradrenaline is released and reflex tachycardia is marked — a drawback avoided by prazosin.

### Synthesis of tolazoline

1. **Benzyl cyanide (phenylacetonitrile)** is treated with dry hydrogen chloride in ethanol to give the **imino ether hydrochloride** (Pinner reaction).
2. The imino ether is condensed with **ethylenediamine**; ammonia and ethanol are lost and the five-membered ring closes.
3. The product is **2-benzyl-2-imidazoline (tolazoline)**, isolated as the hydrochloride.

### Beta blockers

Early beta blockers were **aryl ethanolamines** (dichloroisoprenaline, sotalol); nearly all modern ones are **aryloxypropanolamines**, where an –OCH2– group is inserted between the ring and the side chain.

```text
Ar — O — CH2 — CH(OH) — CH2 — NH — CH(CH3)2
         aryloxypropanolamine (propranolol type)
```

**SAR of beta blockers**

1. The **aryloxypropanolamine** side chain gives 10–100 times the potency of the matching ethanolamine.
2. The **secondary amine with an isopropyl or tert-butyl group** is essential; primary, tertiary and quaternary amines are much weaker.
3. The **β-hydroxyl** must be present; the **(S)** configuration carries nearly all the blocking activity.
4. **Aromatic ring**: a bicyclic ring (naphthalene in propranolol) gives potent non-selective blockade and high lipid solubility, so CNS effects and first-pass metabolism are marked. A **para substituent on a benzene ring** gives β1 (cardio) selectivity — atenolol, metoprolol, betaxolol, bisoprolol. Substituting at the ortho or meta position reduces selectivity.
5. **Partial agonist activity (ISA)** comes from certain ring systems (pindolol, acebutolol) and causes less bradycardia.
6. Extra groups add other actions: labetalol and carvedilol also block α1; esmolol carries an ester that plasma esterases hydrolyse quickly, giving a half-life of minutes.
7. Lipophilic blockers (propranolol, metoprolol) are metabolised by the liver and enter the brain; hydrophilic ones (atenolol, nadolol) are excreted unchanged by the kidney.

**Drugs**: propranolol (non-selective — hypertension, angina, arrhythmia, migraine prophylaxis, thyrotoxicosis, anxiety tremor), metibranolol, atenolol, betaxolol, bisoprolol, esmolol (short acting, intravenous), metoprolol, labetalol (used in pregnancy-related hypertension), carvedilol (heart failure).

Beta blockers are avoided in asthma (β2 blockade) and used carefully in diabetes, because they hide the warning signs of hypoglycaemia.

### Synthesis of propranolol

1. **1-Naphthol** is treated with **epichlorohydrin** in the presence of a base such as sodium hydroxide. The phenolate attacks the epoxide, chloride is lost and the ring closes again, giving **1-(1-naphthyloxy)-2,3-epoxypropane**.
2. The epoxide is heated with **isopropylamine**. The amine opens the epoxide at the less hindered carbon, giving the aryloxypropanolamine.
3. The product, **1-(isopropylamino)-3-(1-naphthyloxy)propan-2-ol**, is converted to the hydrochloride. The drug is used as the racemate, although the (S) enantiomer does the blocking.

## Remember

1. Tyrosine → DOPA → dopamine → noradrenaline → adrenaline; tyrosine hydroxylase is rate-limiting, PNMT is adrenal.
2. Uptake-1 stops the signal; MAO and COMT destroy the amine; VMA is the final urinary product.
3. Catechol group = potent but short acting and inactive orally; change or remove an OH to get an oral drug.
4. Nitrogen substituent decides selectivity: H → α, methyl → α+β, isopropyl → β, tert-butyl → β2.
5. β-OH must be (R) in agonists and (S) in aryloxypropanolamine blockers.
6. Para substitution on a single benzene ring gives β1 selectivity; a naphthalene ring gives non-selective, lipophilic propranolol.
7. Syntheses to write without hesitation: phenylephrine (bromoketone + amine, then reduce), salbutamol (LiAlH4 gives the hydroxymethyl group), tolazoline (benzyl cyanide + ethylenediamine), propranolol (naphthol + epichlorohydrin + isopropylamine).

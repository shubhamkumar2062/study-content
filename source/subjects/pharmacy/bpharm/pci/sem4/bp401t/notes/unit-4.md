# Unit 4: Azoles, pyridine and fused rings, pyrimidine, purine and azepines

This unit continues heterocyclic chemistry with the rings that appear in the largest number of drugs: the five-membered rings with two hetero atoms (azoles), pyridine and the benzo-fused rings built on it, indole, and the six- and seven-membered rings with nitrogen. For each compound learn **one or two named syntheses, the key reactions with the position of attack, and two or three drugs**.

## 4.1 Two facts that explain most of this unit

**1. Pyridine-type nitrogen vs pyrrole-type nitrogen.**

| | Pyrrole-type N (–NH– in a five-membered ring) | Pyridine-type N (–N= in the ring) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lone pair | Part of the aromatic sextet | In an sp2 orbital in the ring plane, **not** part of the sextet |
| Basicity | Almost none | Basic (can take a proton without losing aromaticity) |
| Effect on ring | Pushes electrons in (π-excessive) | Pulls electrons out (π-deficient) |

The azoles contain **one of each**: imidazole, pyrazole, oxazole and thiazole all have a pyridine-type N.

**2. Electron-rich vs electron-poor rings.** Five-membered rings react with electrophiles easily. Six-membered rings with nitrogen (pyridine, pyrimidine) resist electrophiles but are attacked by **nucleophiles** at the positions ortho and para to nitrogen (C-2, C-4, C-6).

**Approximate basicity (pKa of the conjugate acid)**

| Compound | pKa (approx.) | Comment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Piperidine (saturated) | 11.1 | sp3 N, strong base |
| Imidazole | 7.0 | Cation stabilised by resonance over both N |
| Isoquinoline | 5.4 | |
| Pyridine | 5.2 | sp2 N |
| Quinoline | 4.9 | |
| Aniline (for comparison) | 4.6 | Lone pair delocalised into the benzene ring |
| Thiazole | 2.5 | |
| Pyrazole | 2.5 | Neighbouring N withdraws electrons |
| Pyrimidine | 1.3 | Second N withdraws electrons |
| Oxazole | 0.8 | O withdraws electrons |
| Pyrrole | about −3.8 | Lone pair in the sextet |

## 4.2 Pyrazole (1,2-diazole)

Five-membered ring with two adjacent nitrogens: N-1 (pyrrole-type, NH) and N-2 (pyridine-type). Colourless solid, weak base and weak acid. Like imidazole it shows **tautomerism**: the NH hydrogen moves between N-1 and N-2, so 3- and 5-substituted pyrazoles interconvert.

### Synthesis

1. **From 1,3-dicarbonyl compounds and hydrazine (Knorr-type)** — the most general method.

```text
 CH3–CO–CH2–CO–CH3  +  H2N–NH2   ──►   3,5-dimethylpyrazole  +  2 H2O
   acetylacetone        hydrazine        (both C=O groups condense with the two NH2 ends)
```

   With phenylhydrazine, N-phenylpyrazoles are obtained. Ethyl acetoacetate + phenylhydrazine gives a **pyrazolone**, the starting point for antipyrine (phenazone).

2. **From α,β-unsaturated carbonyl compounds and hydrazine** — gives a pyrazoline (dihydropyrazole), which is then oxidised to the pyrazole.
3. **From diazomethane and acetylene** — 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition gives pyrazole directly.

### Reactions

- **Electrophilic substitution at C-4** (the most electron-rich carbon): nitration (HNO3/H2SO4) → 4-nitropyrazole; sulphonation → pyrazole-4-sulphonic acid; bromination → 4-bromopyrazole.
- **N-alkylation and N-acylation** at the NH nitrogen.
- **Salt formation** with strong acids at N-2.
- **Stable to oxidation**; side chains can be oxidised to carboxylic acids. Reduction gives pyrazoline and then pyrazolidine.

### Medicinal uses

| Drug | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Antipyrine (phenazone), aminopyrine, metamizole (dipyrone) | Analgesic–antipyretics (pyrazolones) |
| Phenylbutazone, oxyphenbutazone | Anti-inflammatory (pyrazolidinediones) |
| Celecoxib | Selective COX-2 inhibitor |
| Sildenafil | PDE-5 inhibitor (pyrazolopyrimidinone) |
| Allopurinol | Gout (pyrazolo[3,4-d]pyrimidine, a purine isomer) |

## 4.3 Imidazole (1,3-diazole)

Nitrogens at 1 (NH) and 3 (=N–). Colourless solid with a high boiling point (about 256 °C) because molecules link by strong intermolecular N–H···N hydrogen bonds. It is **amphoteric**: a moderately strong base (pKa about 7) and a weak acid (NH pKa about 14.5). Its basicity near physiological pH is why the imidazole ring of histidine acts as a proton donor and acceptor in many enzymes.

Tautomerism makes positions 4 and 5 equivalent in the unsubstituted ring; a 4-substituted imidazole is often written 4(5)-substituted.

### Synthesis

1. **Debus–Radziszewski synthesis** — a 1,2-dicarbonyl compound, an aldehyde and ammonia condense together.

```text
 glyoxal (OHC–CHO) + formaldehyde (HCHO) + 2 NH3  ──►  imidazole + 3 H2O
 benzil + benzaldehyde + NH3 (as ammonium acetate)  ──►  2,4,5-triphenylimidazole
```

2. **From α-haloketones and formamide (Bredereck)** — heating an α-haloketone (or α-hydroxyketone) with formamide gives 4-substituted imidazoles.
3. **Marckwald synthesis** — an α-aminoketone (or α-aminoaldehyde acetal) reacts with potassium thiocyanate to give a 2-mercaptoimidazole; the –SH is then removed by oxidation (dilute HNO3) to give the imidazole.
4. **From o-phenylenediamine** — with formic acid or an aldehyde, it gives **benzimidazole**, the parent of a large drug family.

### Reactions

- **Basic reactions** — forms stable salts (imidazolium) with acids; reacts with alkyl halides at N-3 to give N-alkylimidazoles and then imidazolium salts.
- **N-Acylation** — gives N-acylimidazoles, which are reactive acylating agents; carbonyldiimidazole (CDI) is used to couple acids and amines in peptide and drug synthesis.
- **Electrophilic substitution at C-4/C-5**, needing stronger conditions than pyrrole: nitration (HNO3/H2SO4) → 4(5)-nitroimidazole (the core of metronidazole); bromination → 2,4,5-tribromoimidazole (substitution goes all the way).
- **Diazo coupling at C-2** with diazonium salts in alkaline solution.
- Resistant to oxidation and reduction.

### Medicinal uses

| Drug or compound | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Histidine, histamine | Amino acid; mediator of allergy and gastric acid secretion |
| Metronidazole, tinidazole, ornidazole | Nitroimidazoles: amoebiasis, giardiasis, trichomoniasis, anaerobic infections |
| Clotrimazole, miconazole, ketoconazole | Azole antifungals |
| Cimetidine | H2 receptor antagonist |
| Albendazole, mebendazole | Benzimidazole anthelmintics |
| Omeprazole, pantoprazole | Benzimidazole proton pump inhibitors |
| Pilocarpine | Glaucoma (cholinergic) |
| Losartan | Angiotensin II receptor blocker |

## 4.4 Oxazole (1,3-oxazole)

Oxygen at 1 and pyridine-type nitrogen at 3. It is a weak base (pKa about 0.8) and is the least aromatic of the azoles because oxygen holds its lone pair tightly. Its isomer **isoxazole** (1,2-oxazole) has O and N next to each other.

### Synthesis

1. **Robinson–Gabriel synthesis** — an α-acylaminoketone (2-acylamino ketone) is cyclised by dehydrating agents such as conc. H2SO4, POCl3 or PCl5.

```text
 R–CO–CH2–NH–CO–R'   ──(conc. H2SO4 or POCl3, −H2O)──►   2,5-disubstituted oxazole
 α-acylaminoketone
```

2. **Fischer oxazole synthesis** — an aldehyde cyanohydrin and an aromatic aldehyde react in dry ether with dry HCl to give a 2,5-diaryloxazole.
3. **From α-haloketones and amides (Bredereck)** — an α-haloketone heated with formamide (or another amide) gives an oxazole.
4. **van Leusen synthesis** — an aldehyde and tosylmethyl isocyanide (TosMIC) with a base (K2CO3) give a 5-substituted oxazole.

### Reactions

- **Electrophilic substitution** is difficult (the ring is electron-poor); with activating groups it occurs at **C-5**, then C-4.
- **Deprotonation at C-2** with strong bases (butyllithium); the C-2 hydrogen is the most acidic.
- **Nucleophilic substitution at C-2** when a leaving group (e.g. Cl) is present.
- **Diels–Alder reaction as a diene** — oxazoles react with dienophiles to give pyridine derivatives. A 5-ethoxyoxazole with a suitable dienophile is the basis of an industrial route to **pyridoxine (vitamin B6)**.
- Ring opening by acids or bases.

### Medicinal uses

| Drug | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Oxaprozin | NSAID (oxazole) |
| Sulfamethoxazole | Sulphonamide antibacterial (isoxazole ring), with trimethoprim as co-trimoxazole |
| Cloxacillin, dicloxacillin, oxacillin | Penicillinase-resistant penicillins (isoxazolyl side chain) |
| Leflunomide | Rheumatoid arthritis (isoxazole) |
| Linezolid | Antibacterial (oxazolidinone, the saturated relative) |

## 4.5 Thiazole (1,3-thiazole)

Sulphur at 1 and pyridine-type nitrogen at 3. Weak base (pKa about 2.5), more aromatic than oxazole.

### Synthesis

1. **Hantzsch thiazole synthesis** — the most important. An α-haloketone condenses with a **thioamide**; with **thiourea** the product is a 2-aminothiazole.

```text
 CH3–CO–CH2Cl   +   CH3–CS–NH2     ──►   2,4-dimethylthiazole  + HCl + H2O
 chloroacetone      thioacetamide
 CH3–CO–CH2Cl   +   H2N–CS–NH2     ──►   2-amino-4-methylthiazole
                    thiourea
```

2. **Gabriel synthesis** — an α-acylaminoketone heated with phosphorus pentasulphide (P2S5) gives a thiazole (the sulphur analogue of the Robinson–Gabriel oxazole synthesis).
3. **Cook–Heilbron synthesis** — an α-aminonitrile reacts with carbon disulphide (or dithioacids, isothiocyanates) to give 5-aminothiazoles.

### Reactions

- **Electrophilic substitution at C-5** (the preferred site), easier when activated: 2-aminothiazole brominates and nitrates at C-5.
- **N-Alkylation** at N-3 gives **thiazolium salts**. The C-2 hydrogen of a thiazolium salt is acidic; removing it gives a reactive species that catalyses decarboxylation of α-keto acids. This is how **thiamine pyrophosphate** (vitamin B1 coenzyme) works in the body.
- **Deprotonation at C-2** with butyllithium; **nucleophilic substitution** of 2-halothiazoles.
- **Desulphurisation with Raney nickel** opens the ring.
- Thiazole is resistant to oxidation; reduction gives thiazoline and thiazolidine (the thiazolidine ring is fused to the β-lactam in penicillins).

### Medicinal uses

| Drug or compound | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Thiamine (vitamin B1) | Thiazolium + pyrimidine; deficiency causes beriberi |
| Penicillins | Thiazolidine ring fused with the β-lactam |
| Sulfathiazole | Sulphonamide antibacterial |
| Famotidine, nizatidine | H2 receptor antagonists |
| Meloxicam | NSAID (thiazolyl amide) |
| Ritonavir | HIV protease inhibitor |
| Thiabendazole | Anthelmintic (thiazolyl benzimidazole) |
| Pioglitazone | Antidiabetic (thiazolidinedione) |

## 4.6 Pyridine

Pyridine (C5H5N) is a colourless liquid (bp about 115 °C) with an unpleasant smell, obtained from coal tar (light oil) and bone oil. It is miscible with water and most organic solvents and is widely used as a basic solvent and catalyst (acylations).

**Structure.** Like benzene, with one CH replaced by N. Six π electrons (one from each ring atom), planar, aromatic. The nitrogen lone pair lies in an sp2 orbital in the plane of the ring, outside the π system. Nitrogen is more electronegative than carbon, so it draws electron density from the ring (inductive and resonance effects); C-2, C-4 and C-6 become electron-poor.

### Basicity of pyridine

- Pyridine is a **moderately weak base (pKa about 5.2)**. It forms salts such as pyridinium chloride, and its lone pair is freely available because it is not part of the sextet.
- It is **much weaker than aliphatic amines** (piperidine about 11.1, triethylamine about 10.7). The lone pair is in an **sp2** orbital (33% s character) — held closer to the nucleus than an sp3 lone pair (25% s), so it is less available to a proton.
- It is **slightly stronger than aniline** (4.6), whose lone pair is delocalised into the benzene ring.
- It is **much stronger than pyrrole**, whose lone pair is part of the aromatic sextet.
- **Effect of substituents**: electron-donating groups (methyl, amino at C-2/C-4) increase basicity (4-aminopyridine and 4-dimethylaminopyridine are much stronger bases); electron-withdrawing groups (nitro, halogen, cyano) decrease it.

### Synthesis

1. **Hantzsch pyridine synthesis** — two molecules of a β-ketoester (ethyl acetoacetate), one aldehyde and ammonia condense to give a **1,4-dihydropyridine**, which is oxidised (HNO3, or other oxidants) to the pyridine.

```text
 2 CH3COCH2COOEt + RCHO + NH3 ──► 1,4-dihydropyridine-3,5-dicarboxylate ──[O]──► pyridine
 (The 1,4-dihydropyridine stage is itself the core of nifedipine, amlodipine and
  felodipine — calcium channel blockers made by this very route.)
```

2. **Chichibabin pyridine synthesis (industrial)** — aldehydes (or ketones) heated with ammonia over a catalyst: acetaldehyde + formaldehyde + ammonia give pyridine and methylpyridines (picolines).
3. **From acetylene and hydrogen cyanide** — passed through a red-hot tube.
4. **From pentamethylenediamine** — heating its hydrochloride gives piperidine, which is dehydrogenated (e.g. with heat and a catalyst, or conc. H2SO4) to pyridine.

### Reactions of pyridine

**(a) Reactions at nitrogen (as a base and nucleophile)**

- With acids → pyridinium salts.
- With alkyl halides → N-alkylpyridinium salts (CH3I → N-methylpyridinium iodide).
- With acyl chlorides → N-acylpyridinium ions, which is why pyridine catalyses acylation.
- With SO3 → pyridine–SO3 complex (a mild sulphonating agent).
- With peracids (H2O2 in acetic acid, or perbenzoic acid) → **pyridine N-oxide**.

**(b) Electrophilic substitution — difficult, at C-3**

Pyridine reacts roughly a million times more slowly than benzene. The ring is electron-poor and, under the acidic conditions of nitration or sulphonation, the nitrogen is protonated to the even more deactivated pyridinium ion. Attack at C-3 is preferred because the intermediate from C-2 or C-4 attack would place a positive charge on the electronegative nitrogen.

| Reaction | Conditions | Product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nitration | KNO3 + fuming H2SO4, about 300 °C | 3-Nitropyridine (low yield) |
| Sulphonation | Oleum with HgSO4 catalyst, about 230–250 °C | Pyridine-3-sulphonic acid |
| Bromination | Br2 in oleum, about 130 °C | 3-Bromopyridine |
| Friedel–Crafts | Does not occur (N complexes the Lewis acid) | — |

**Pyridine N-oxide** is more reactive: the oxygen gives electrons back into the ring, so it nitrates at **C-4**. The oxygen can then be removed (e.g. with PCl3), giving 4-substituted pyridines.

**(c) Nucleophilic substitution — easy, at C-2 and C-4**

| Reaction | Reagent | Product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Chichibabin reaction** | Sodium amide (NaNH2) in toluene or liquid NH3, heat | 2-Aminopyridine (+ H2 lost as hydride) |
| With organolithium | Phenyllithium or butyllithium | 2-Phenyl- or 2-butylpyridine |
| With KOH | Fusion at about 300 °C | 2-Pyridone (2-hydroxypyridine) |
| Halide displacement | 2- or 4-chloropyridine + amines, alkoxides | Substituted pyridines |

Attack at C-2/C-4 is preferred because the negative charge in the intermediate can sit on the electronegative nitrogen.

**(d) Reduction** — sodium in ethanol, or H2 with Pt, gives **piperidine**.

**(e) Oxidation** — the ring resists oxidation, but alkyl side chains are oxidised to –COOH:

```text
 3-methylpyridine (β-picoline) ──KMnO4 / HNO3──► nicotinic acid (niacin, vitamin B3)
 4-methylpyridine (γ-picoline) ──[O]──► isonicotinic acid ──(ester + hydrazine)──► isoniazid
```

### Medicinal uses

| Drug | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Nicotinic acid, nicotinamide | Vitamin B3 (pellagra); nicotinic acid also lowers lipids |
| Isoniazid | Antitubercular |
| Pyridoxine | Vitamin B6 |
| Nifedipine, amlodipine | Calcium channel blockers (1,4-dihydropyridines) |
| Chlorpheniramine, pheniramine | Antihistamines |
| Pyridostigmine | Myasthenia gravis (cholinesterase inhibitor) |
| Nikethamide | Respiratory stimulant |
| Omeprazole | Proton pump inhibitor (pyridine + benzimidazole) |
| Piroxicam | NSAID (2-pyridyl amide) |

## 4.7 Quinoline (benzo[b]pyridine)

A benzene ring fused to the 2,3-bond of pyridine; nitrogen at position 1. Colourless oily liquid (bp about 238 °C), found in coal tar; first obtained by distilling quinine with alkali (hence the name). Weak base, pKa about 4.9.

```text
          5     4
       6 /\ ___/\ 3
         ||    ||          benzene ring (5–8) fused to pyridine ring (N-1 to C-4)
       7 \/‾‾‾‾\/ 2
          8     N 1
```

### Synthesis

1. **Skraup synthesis** — the classical method. Aniline is heated with glycerol, conc. H2SO4 and an oxidising agent (nitrobenzene; arsenic acid was also used); ferrous sulphate is added to moderate the otherwise violent reaction.

```text
 Step 1  glycerol ──conc. H2SO4 (−2 H2O)──► acrolein (CH2=CH–CHO)
 Step 2  aniline adds to the C=C of acrolein (Michael / 1,4-addition)
          → 3-anilinopropanal
 Step 3  acid-catalysed ring closure onto the benzene ring (ortho position), then loss of water
          → 1,2-dihydroquinoline
 Step 4  oxidation by nitrobenzene → quinoline
          (nitrobenzene is reduced to aniline, which can also react)
```

   Substituted anilines give substituted quinolines (p-toluidine → 6-methylquinoline).

2. **Doebner–Miller synthesis** — like Skraup, but an α,β-unsaturated aldehyde (or two aldehydes that form one by aldol) is used with HCl: aniline + crotonaldehyde → 2-methylquinoline (quinaldine).
3. **Friedländer synthesis** — o-aminobenzaldehyde (or o-aminoacetophenone) condenses with an aldehyde or ketone having an α-CH2 group in the presence of a base: o-aminobenzaldehyde + acetaldehyde → quinoline.
4. **Combes synthesis** — aniline + a 1,3-diketone (acetylacetone), then conc. H2SO4 → 2,4-dimethylquinoline.
5. **Conrad–Limpach synthesis** — aniline + a β-ketoester; the enamine formed is cyclised at high temperature to a 4-quinolone (4-hydroxyquinoline). Related routes give the 4-quinolone-3-carboxylic acid core of the fluoroquinolone antibiotics.

### Reactions

- **Electrophilic substitution in the benzene ring, at C-5 and C-8** (the pyridine ring is deactivated): nitration with HNO3/H2SO4 → mixture of 5- and 8-nitroquinoline; sulphonation with oleum → quinoline-8-sulphonic acid (and 5-isomer at different temperatures); bromination in the presence of acid → 5- and 8-bromo.
- **Nucleophilic substitution in the pyridine ring, at C-2 (and C-4)**: NaNH2 → 2-aminoquinoline; KOH fusion → 2-quinolone (carbostyril); organolithiums → 2-alkyl/aryl quinolines.
- **Oxidation** with alkaline KMnO4 destroys the benzene ring and gives **quinolinic acid** (pyridine-2,3-dicarboxylic acid) — showing that the pyridine ring is the more stable one.
- **Reduction**: tin and HCl, or mild catalytic hydrogenation, reduce the pyridine ring → 1,2,3,4-tetrahydroquinoline; vigorous hydrogenation → decahydroquinoline.
- **Basic reactions**: salts with acids, quaternary salts with alkyl halides, N-oxide with peracids.

### Medicinal uses

| Drug | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Quinine | Antimalarial (from cinchona bark) |
| Quinidine | Antiarrhythmic (stereoisomer of quinine) |
| Chloroquine, amodiaquine (4-aminoquinolines), mefloquine | Antimalarials |
| Primaquine (8-aminoquinoline) | Antimalarial (radical cure of vivax malaria) |
| Ciprofloxacin, norfloxacin, ofloxacin | Fluoroquinolone antibacterials (4-quinolone ring) |
| 8-Hydroxyquinoline (oxine), clioquinol | Chelating agent; antiseptic / amoebicide |
| Montelukast | Asthma (leukotriene receptor antagonist) |

## 4.8 Isoquinoline (benzo[c]pyridine)

Benzene fused to the 3,4-bond of pyridine; nitrogen at position 2. Colourless solid (mp about 26 °C), found in coal tar. Slightly stronger base than quinoline (pKa about 5.4).

### Synthesis

1. **Bischler–Napieralski synthesis** — an N-acyl derivative of β-phenylethylamine is cyclised with a dehydrating agent (POCl3, P2O5) to a **3,4-dihydroisoquinoline**, which is dehydrogenated (Pd, heat) to the isoquinoline.

```text
 C6H5–CH2–CH2–NH–CO–R  ──POCl3 / P2O5 (−H2O)──►  1-R-3,4-dihydroisoquinoline
 N-acyl-β-phenylethylamine                                │ Pd, heat (−H2)
                                                          ▼
                                                 1-R-isoquinoline
```

   Electron-donating groups (–OCH3) on the benzene ring help the ring closure; this route was used to make **papaverine**.

2. **Pictet–Spengler synthesis** — β-phenylethylamine condenses with an aldehyde (formaldehyde) in acid; the iminium ion cyclises to a **1,2,3,4-tetrahydroisoquinoline**. The same reaction makes many isoquinoline alkaloids in plants.
3. **Pomeranz–Fritsch synthesis** — benzaldehyde + aminoacetaldehyde diethyl acetal → Schiff base (benzalaminoacetal), which is cyclised by conc. H2SO4 → isoquinoline.

### Reactions

- **Electrophilic substitution at C-5** (some at C-8): nitration → mainly 5-nitroisoquinoline; sulphonation → 5-sulphonic acid.
- **Nucleophilic substitution at C-1** (the most reactive position): NaNH2 → 1-aminoisoquinoline; KOH → 1-isoquinolone.
- **Oxidation** with alkaline KMnO4 gives a mixture of **phthalic acid** and **cinchomeronic acid** (pyridine-3,4-dicarboxylic acid) — both rings can be attacked.
- **Reduction** (Sn/HCl or catalytic) → 1,2,3,4-tetrahydroisoquinoline.
- Salts, quaternary salts and N-oxides as for pyridine.

### Medicinal uses

| Drug | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Papaverine | Smooth muscle relaxant (benzylisoquinoline alkaloid of opium) |
| Noscapine | Antitussive |
| Emetine | Amoebicide |
| Tubocurarine, atracurium | Neuromuscular blockers |
| Berberine | Antimicrobial alkaloid |
| Quinapril | ACE inhibitor (tetrahydroisoquinoline) |
| Praziquantel | Anthelmintic (pyrazinoisoquinoline) |

## 4.9 Acridine (dibenzo[b,e]pyridine)

Three fused six-membered rings — a pyridine ring in the middle of two benzene rings (the aza analogue of anthracene); nitrogen at position 10, the reactive carbon opposite it at **C-9**. Pale yellow crystalline solid from coal tar; weak base (pKa about 5.6); its dilute solutions fluoresce blue.

### Synthesis

1. **Bernthsen synthesis** — diphenylamine is heated with a carboxylic acid (or formic acid) and anhydrous zinc chloride at about 200–270 °C. Formic acid gives acridine; other acids give 9-substituted acridines (acetic acid → 9-methylacridine).
2. **From N-phenylanthranilic acid (via acridone)** — o-chlorobenzoic acid and aniline are coupled with copper (Ullmann reaction) to N-phenylanthranilic acid. Heating with conc. H2SO4 closes the ring to **acridone**; POCl3 converts it to **9-chloroacridine**, a key intermediate (9-chloroacridine + NH3 or amines → 9-aminoacridines).

### Reactions

- **Nucleophilic attack at C-9** — the most reactive site. Sodamide gives 9-aminoacridine; 9-chloroacridine exchanges Cl for amines, alkoxides and thiols easily.
- **Electrophilic substitution** occurs in the outer benzene rings, mainly at C-2 and C-7 (nitration, sulphonation).
- **Oxidation** with dichromate in acetic acid → acridone (C-9 becomes C=O).
- **Reduction** (Zn/HCl) → 9,10-dihydroacridine (acridan).
- Forms salts, quaternary salts and N-oxides.

### Medicinal uses

| Drug | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Proflavine (3,6-diaminoacridine), acriflavine | Topical antiseptics (wound dressings) |
| Ethacridine (Rivanol) | Antiseptic |
| Aminacrine (9-aminoacridine) | Antiseptic |
| Mepacrine (quinacrine) | Antimalarial (historic); giardiasis |
| Amsacrine | Anticancer (DNA intercalator) |
| Tacrine | Alzheimer's disease (tetrahydroacridine) |

The flat acridine ring slides between DNA base pairs (**intercalation**), which explains both the antibacterial and the anticancer actions.

## 4.10 Indole (benzo[b]pyrrole)

Benzene fused to the 2,3-bond of pyrrole; NH at position 1. Colourless solid (mp about 52 °C) with a faecal smell when concentrated (but floral when very dilute; used in perfumes). Found in coal tar and jasmine oil; part of the amino acid tryptophan.

```text
          4      3
       5 /\ ___/\         benzene ring fused to pyrrole ring;
         ||    ||  2      most reactive carbon = C-3
       6 \/‾‾‾‾\/
          7      N 1
                 H
```

### Synthesis

1. **Fischer indole synthesis** — the most important method. A phenylhydrazone of an aldehyde or ketone is heated with an acid catalyst (ZnCl2, polyphosphoric acid, BF3 or H2SO4).

```text
 C6H5–NH–NH2 + CH3COCH3 ──► acetone phenylhydrazone
          │ ZnCl2, heat
          ▼
 tautomerises to the ene-hydrazine
 → [3,3]-sigmatropic rearrangement (new C–C bond to the ortho carbon of the ring)
 → cyclisation and loss of NH3
 → 2-methylindole
```

   The carbonyl compound must have a CH2 (or CH3) group α to the C=O. Acetaldehyde does not work well, so indole itself is made from **pyruvic acid** phenylhydrazone → indole-2-carboxylic acid → heated (decarboxylation) → indole.

2. **Madelung synthesis** — an N-acyl-o-toluidine is heated strongly with a strong base (sodium amide or potassium tert-butoxide); intramolecular condensation gives a 2-substituted indole. N-formyl-o-toluidine gives indole itself.
3. **Reissert synthesis** — o-nitrotoluene condenses with diethyl oxalate (potassium ethoxide) to give ethyl o-nitrophenylpyruvate; reduction (Zn/acetic acid or Fe) turns –NO2 into –NH2, which cyclises onto the keto group → indole-2-carboxylic acid ester; hydrolysis and decarboxylation give indole.
4. **Bischler synthesis** — an α-haloketone (phenacyl bromide) heated with excess aniline gives a 2-arylindole.

### Reactions

- **Electrophilic substitution at C-3** (not C-2 as in pyrrole). Attack at C-3 gives an intermediate in which the positive charge is stabilised by nitrogen **without** disturbing the benzene ring's aromaticity; attack at C-2 would need to involve the benzene ring.

| Reaction | Reagent | Product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formylation | DMF + POCl3 (Vilsmeier–Haack) | Indole-3-carbaldehyde |
| Mannich | HCHO + dimethylamine + acetic acid | Gramine (3-dimethylaminomethylindole), used to make tryptophan |
| Nitration | Benzoyl nitrate (non-acidic) | 3-Nitroindole |
| Halogenation | Mild reagents (e.g. N-bromosuccinimide) | 3-Haloindoles |
| Acylation | Acetic anhydride with heat | 3-Acetylindole (with some N-acetyl) |
| Michael addition | α,β-Unsaturated carbonyls | 3-Substituted indoles |

- If C-3 is occupied, substitution goes to C-2.
- **N–H acidity** — like pyrrole (pKa about 17); forms N-sodium or N-magnesium salts that can be alkylated.
- **Very weak base**; protonates on C-3 in strong acid and polymerises.
- **Reduction** with zinc and HCl, or catalytically, → indoline (2,3-dihydroindole).
- **Oxidation** — easily oxidised in air; controlled oxidation gives indoxyl, oxindole or isatin; ozone cleaves the 2,3-bond.

## 4.11 Pyrimidine (1,3-diazine)

Six-membered ring with nitrogens at 1 and 3. Colourless solid, very weak base (pKa about 1.3) because each nitrogen withdraws electrons from the other. Strongly π-deficient. It is the parent of three nucleic acid bases: **cytosine, thymine and uracil**.

```text
          4
       5 /\ N 3          N at 1 and 3; C-2, C-4 and C-6 lie between or next to
         ||  |           nitrogens (electron-poor, attacked by nucleophiles);
       6 \/\ 2           C-5 is the only position open to electrophiles
          N
          1
```

### Synthesis

The general **"principal synthesis"** joins a three-carbon unit (a 1,3-dicarbonyl compound — malonic ester, β-ketoester or β-diketone) with an N–C–N unit (urea, thiourea, guanidine or an amidine).

| Three-carbon unit | N–C–N unit | Product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diethyl malonate (sodium ethoxide) | Urea | **Barbituric acid** (pyrimidine-2,4,6-trione) |
| Diethyl phenylethylmalonate | Urea | Phenobarbital |
| Ethyl acetoacetate | Thiourea | 6-Methyl-2-thiouracil (antithyroid) |
| β-Diketone | Guanidine | 2-Aminopyrimidines |
| β-Diketone | Amidine | 2-Substituted pyrimidines (Pinner synthesis) |

```text
 CH2(COOEt)2  +  H2N–CO–NH2  ──NaOEt, heat──►  barbituric acid  +  2 EtOH
 diethyl malonate     urea
```

**Parent pyrimidine** — barbituric acid + POCl3 → 2,4,6-trichloropyrimidine; the chlorines are then removed by reduction (zinc dust in water, or catalytic H2/Pd) → pyrimidine.

### Reactions

- **Nucleophilic substitution** at C-2, C-4 and C-6 is easy when there is a leaving group: chloropyrimidines react with ammonia, amines, alkoxides and thiols (C-4 reacts before C-2). Chichibabin-type amination is also possible.
- **Electrophilic substitution** is very difficult in the parent ring; with two or more activating groups (–OH, –NH2) it occurs at **C-5**. Uracil brominates at C-5; this position is also where fluorine sits in 5-fluorouracil.
- **N-Alkylation** with alkyl halides (quaternisation at one nitrogen).
- **Tautomerism** — "hydroxypyrimidines" such as uracil and barbituric acid exist mainly in the keto (lactam) form.
- **Reduction** gives di- and tetrahydropyrimidines.

### Medicinal uses

| Drug | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Cytosine, thymine, uracil | Nucleic acid bases |
| Thiamine (vitamin B1) | Contains a pyrimidine ring |
| Phenobarbital, thiopental | Barbiturates: antiepileptic; IV anaesthetic induction |
| 5-Fluorouracil | Anticancer |
| Zidovudine | Anti-HIV (thymidine analogue) |
| Trimethoprim, pyrimethamine | Antibacterial; antimalarial (dihydrofolate reductase inhibitors) |
| Sulfadiazine | Sulphonamide antibacterial |
| Propylthiouracil, methylthiouracil | Antithyroid |
| Flucytosine | Antifungal |
| Minoxidil | Antihypertensive; hair growth |

## 4.12 Purine (imidazo[4,5-d]pyrimidine)

A pyrimidine ring fused with an imidazole ring. Purine itself is a colourless solid, amphoteric (weak base, pKa about 2.4; weak acid, pKa about 8.9). The two nucleic acid bases **adenine** (6-aminopurine) and **guanine** (2-amino-6-oxopurine) are purines, as are uric acid, xanthine and caffeine. Purine uses a special numbering:

```text
          6
     1 N ╱ ╲ 5 ── N 7
       ‖     ‖      ╲
     2 ╲     ‖       8         pyrimidine ring: 1–6
       3 N ╲ ╱ 4 ── N 9        imidazole ring: 4, 5, 7, 8, 9
                     H
```

### Synthesis

1. **Traube purine synthesis** — the standard method. A **4,5-diaminopyrimidine** is heated with formic acid (or formamide, or ethyl orthoformate). The extra carbon closes the imidazole ring at C-8.

```text
 4,5-diaminopyrimidine  +  HCOOH  ──heat (−2 H2O)──►  purine
 2,4,5-triamino-6-hydroxypyrimidine + HCOOH ──►  guanine
 4,5-diamino-1,3-dimethyluracil  + HCOOH ──►  theophylline
```

   The diaminopyrimidine is made by the principal synthesis, nitrosation at C-5 and reduction of the nitroso group.

2. **Fischer's route from uric acid** — uric acid + POCl3 → 2,6,8-trichloropurine; the chlorines are replaced or removed stepwise (reduction with HI gives purine).

### Reactions

- **Nucleophilic substitution of chloropurines** — 6-chloropurine with thiourea or NaSH → **6-mercaptopurine**; with ammonia → adenine.
- **N-Alkylation** — methylation of xanthines: theophylline (1,3-dimethylxanthine) or theobromine (3,7-dimethylxanthine) + a methylating agent → **caffeine** (1,3,7-trimethylxanthine).
- **Electrophilic substitution at C-8** in activated purines (bromination of caffeine → 8-bromocaffeine).
- **Oxidation** — hypoxanthine → xanthine → uric acid (in the body by the enzyme xanthine oxidase; allopurinol blocks it).
- **Murexide test** — uric acid and caffeine evaporated with HNO3 (or H2O2/HCl) and then exposed to ammonia give a purple colour; used to identify purines.

### Medicinal uses

| Drug or compound | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Adenine, guanine; ATP, NAD, coenzyme A | Nucleic acids and coenzymes |
| Caffeine | CNS stimulant |
| Theophylline, aminophylline | Bronchodilator in asthma |
| Theobromine | Mild diuretic, smooth muscle relaxant |
| 6-Mercaptopurine, 6-thioguanine | Anticancer (leukaemia) |
| Azathioprine | Immunosuppressant (pro-drug of 6-mercaptopurine) |
| Acyclovir, ganciclovir | Antivirals (guanine analogues) |
| Allopurinol (purine isomer) | Gout (xanthine oxidase inhibitor) |

## 4.13 Azepines and their derivatives

**Azepine** is a seven-membered ring with one nitrogen. The fully unsaturated 1H-azepine would have 8 π electrons (anti-aromatic if flat), so it is **non-planar (boat-shaped)**, non-aromatic and unstable; it behaves like a polyene. N-substituted azepines (N-ethoxycarbonyl) are more stable. Pharmaceutically important compounds are its **saturated or benzo-fused** derivatives, and the closely related **diazepines** (seven-membered rings with two nitrogens).

| Ring system | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Azepane (hexahydroazepine, fully saturated) | ε-Caprolactam (azepan-2-one) |
| Benzazepine | Benazepril, fenoldopam |
| Dibenz[b,f]azepine | Carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine |
| 10,11-Dihydrodibenz[b,f]azepine (iminodibenzyl) | Imipramine, desipramine, clomipramine |
| 1,4-Benzodiazepine | Diazepam, chlordiazepoxide, lorazepam, alprazolam (triazolo-fused) |
| Dibenzodiazepine / thienobenzodiazepine | Clozapine, olanzapine |

### Synthesis

1. **1H-Azepines by ring expansion of benzene** — ethyl azidoformate (N3COOEt), on heating or irradiation, loses N2 to give a nitrene, which adds to benzene to form an azanorcaradiene; this opens (valence tautomerism) to **N-ethoxycarbonyl-1H-azepine**.
2. **ε-Caprolactam by Beckmann rearrangement** — cyclohexanone oxime + conc. H2SO4 → caprolactam (Unit 5). Caprolactam polymerises to nylon-6.
3. **Imipramine** — 2,2'-diaminobibenzyl is heated with acid; ring closure with loss of NH3 gives iminodibenzyl (10,11-dihydro-5H-dibenz[b,f]azepine). Its N–H is deprotonated with sodamide and alkylated with 3-chloro-N,N-dimethylpropylamine → **imipramine**.
4. **Carbamazepine** — iminostilbene (5H-dibenz[b,f]azepine) reacts with phosgene to give the N-carbonyl chloride, which with ammonia gives the carboxamide, **carbamazepine**.
5. **Diazepam (1,4-benzodiazepine)** — 2-amino-5-chlorobenzophenone is acylated with bromoacetyl bromide (or condensed with glycine ethyl ester); ammonia then closes the seven-membered ring to nordazepam, and N-methylation gives **diazepam**.

### Medicinal uses

| Drug | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Imipramine, clomipramine, desipramine | Tricyclic antidepressants |
| Carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine | Antiepileptics; trigeminal neuralgia |
| Diazepam, lorazepam, chlordiazepoxide | Anxiolytic, sedative, anticonvulsant (benzodiazepines) |
| Clozapine, olanzapine | Atypical antipsychotics |
| Benazepril | ACE inhibitor (benzazepinone) |

## Remember

- Azoles have one pyrrole-type and one pyridine-type N. Basicity: imidazole (7) > pyridine (5.2) > pyrazole ≈ thiazole (2.5) > oxazole (0.8) >> pyrrole.
- Pyrazole: 1,3-diketone + hydrazine; substitution at C-4. Imidazole: Debus–Radziszewski (glyoxal + HCHO + NH3); substitution at C-4/5. Oxazole: Robinson–Gabriel; Diels–Alder route to vitamin B6. Thiazole: Hantzsch (α-haloketone + thioamide/thiourea); substitution at C-5; thiamine is a thiazolium.
- Pyridine: Hantzsch (2 β-ketoester + aldehyde + NH3 → 1,4-dihydropyridine → oxidise). Electrophiles at C-3 (hard); nucleophiles at C-2/C-4 (Chichibabin: NaNH2 → 2-aminopyridine). N-oxide nitrates at C-4. pKa 5.2: weaker than piperidine (sp2 lone pair), stronger than aniline and pyrrole.
- Quinoline: Skraup (aniline + glycerol + H2SO4 + nitrobenzene; acrolein intermediate); E+ at C-5/C-8, Nu at C-2; KMnO4 → quinolinic acid.
- Isoquinoline: Bischler–Napieralski (N-acyl-β-phenylethylamine + POCl3), Pictet–Spengler, Pomeranz–Fritsch; E+ at C-5, Nu at C-1.
- Acridine: Bernthsen (diphenylamine + RCOOH + ZnCl2); C-9 most reactive; proflavine, mepacrine, amsacrine.
- Indole: Fischer (phenylhydrazone + acid, [3,3] shift, −NH3); E+ at **C-3**; tryptophan, serotonin, indomethacin, sumatriptan.
- Pyrimidine: 1,3-dicarbonyl + urea/thiourea/guanidine (barbituric acid from malonic ester + urea); Nu at 2/4/6, E+ only at 5.
- Purine: Traube (4,5-diaminopyrimidine + HCOOH); caffeine, theophylline, 6-mercaptopurine, acyclovir; murexide test.
- Azepines: non-planar 7-ring; drugs are dibenzazepines (imipramine, carbamazepine) and benzodiazepines (diazepam).

# Effect of drugs on rabbit eye

**Aim:** To study the effects of mydriatics, miotics and local anaesthetics on the rabbit eye.

> This experiment is shown by simulation or video as per the PCI note. A live study needs IAEC approval under CPCSEA rules, gentle handling and the fewest animals (3Rs). Use the drug strengths in your lab manual.

## Principle

The size of the pupil depends on two muscles of the iris:

- **Sphincter pupillae** (circular, muscarinic M3, parasympathetic) – contraction makes the pupil small (**miosis**).
- **Dilator pupillae** (radial, α1-adrenergic, sympathetic) – contraction makes the pupil large (**mydriasis**).

| Drug | Type | Pupil | Light reflex | Corneal reflex |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Atropine | Antimuscarinic | Dilated (mydriasis) | Absent | Present |
| Phenylephrine | α1 agonist | Dilated (mydriasis) | Present | Present |
| Pilocarpine | Muscarinic agonist | Constricted (miosis) | Present (sluggish) | Present |
| Physostigmine | Anticholinesterase | Constricted (miosis) | Present | Present |
| Lignocaine | Local anaesthetic | Little change | Present | Absent |

Atropine paralyses the sphincter, so the dilated pupil does not react to light. Phenylephrine contracts the dilator while the sphincter still works, so the light reflex remains. A local anaesthetic blocks the sensory nerves of the cornea, so touching it gives no blink.

Some rabbits have an enzyme (atropinesterase) that breaks down atropine, so the effect of atropine may be shorter in them.

## Requirements

Albino rabbits or simulation software, rabbit holder, torch, millimetre scale or pupillometer, cotton wisp or soft hair, droppers; eye drops of atropine, pilocarpine (or physostigmine), phenylephrine and lignocaine (commercial ophthalmic preparations).

## Procedure

1. Place the rabbit in the holder in a room with steady light.
2. Before giving any drug, record in both eyes: pupil size (mm), light reflex (shine the torch and watch for constriction), corneal reflex (touch the cornea with a cotton wisp from the side and watch for a blink), and congestion of the conjunctiva.
3. Pull down the lower eyelid of one eye to form a pouch and put in 2–3 drops of the test drug. Hold the lid closed briefly and press at the inner corner of the eye to reduce drainage into the nose. The other eye is the control.
4. Record the same signs at fixed intervals (for example 5, 10, 15 and 30 min).
5. Use a different rabbit (or a different session after full recovery) for each drug, as in your manual.

## Observations

**Specimen readings at 15 min (illustrative only, not real data):**

| Drug | Pupil before (mm) | Pupil after (mm) | Light reflex | Corneal reflex | Conjunctiva | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Control eye | 6 | 6 | Present | Present | Normal | No change |
| Atropine | 6 | 10 | Absent | Present | Normal | Mydriasis with cycloplegic action |
| Phenylephrine | 6 | 9 | Present | Present | Blanched | Mydriasis (α1 agonist) |
| Pilocarpine | 6 | 3 | Present, sluggish | Present | Congested | Miosis |
| Lignocaine | 6 | 6 | Present | Absent | Normal | Corneal (surface) anaesthesia |

## Result

Atropine and phenylephrine caused mydriasis (light reflex lost with atropine, kept with phenylephrine); pilocarpine caused miosis; lignocaine abolished the corneal reflex (specimen values).

## Precautions

1. Keep the room light constant while measuring the pupil.
2. Touch the cornea from the side so the rabbit does not blink at the approaching object.
3. Do not touch the cornea with the dropper.
4. Press the inner corner of the eye after instillation to limit systemic absorption.
5. Always compare with the untreated eye.

## Viva questions

1. **What is mydriasis?** Dilatation of the pupil.
2. **Why is the light reflex lost with atropine but not with phenylephrine?** Atropine paralyses the sphincter pupillae; phenylephrine acts on the dilator and leaves the sphincter working.
3. **Name two miotics.** Pilocarpine and physostigmine.
4. **What is the corneal reflex?** A blink when the cornea is touched.
5. **Why is the corneal reflex lost with lignocaine?** It blocks the sensory nerve endings of the cornea.
6. **What is cycloplegia?** Paralysis of the ciliary muscle so the eye cannot focus for near vision; caused by atropine.
7. **Why are miotics used in glaucoma?** They open the drainage angle and increase outflow of aqueous humour.
8. **Why might atropine act briefly in some rabbits?** Some rabbits have atropinesterase, which destroys atropine.

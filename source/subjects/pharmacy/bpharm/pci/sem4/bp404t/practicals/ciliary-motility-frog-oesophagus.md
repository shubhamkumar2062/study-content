# Effect of drugs on ciliary motility of frog oesophagus

**Aim:** To study the effect of drugs on the ciliary movement of the frog oesophagus.

> Dissection of frogs for teaching is now discouraged and this experiment is shown by simulation or video, as the PCI syllabus notes. Any live use needs IAEC approval under CPCSEA rules and the 3Rs apply.

## Principle

The inner lining of the frog's mouth and oesophagus carries cilia that beat in one direction, towards the stomach, and carry mucus and small particles with them. The speed of a light particle placed on the lining is a measure of ciliary activity.

Ciliary movement is increased by cholinergic drugs (acetylcholine, pilocarpine, physostigmine) and this effect is blocked by atropine. Local anaesthetics depress ciliary movement.

**Velocity of the particle = Distance travelled ÷ Time taken**

## Requirements

Frog (or simulation software / video), pithing needle, dissection set, frog board or cork board, pins, frog Ringer solution, droppers, a light marker particle (poppy seed, small piece of cork or a grain of charcoal), ruler, stopwatch; drug solutions such as acetylcholine, atropine and a local anaesthetic (lignocaine).

## Procedure

1. Pith the frog (destroy brain and spinal cord) so it feels no pain.
2. Pin it on its back on the board. Cut through the lower jaw in the midline and open the mouth and oesophagus down to the stomach.
3. Pin the cut edges so the lining of the mouth and oesophagus lies flat and faces up. Keep it moist with frog Ringer throughout.
4. Mark two points a fixed distance apart (for example 1 cm) along the oesophagus.
5. Place the marker particle at the upper mark and note the time it takes to reach the lower mark. Repeat three times for a steady control value.
6. Put a few drops of acetylcholine on the lining, wait a short time, and repeat the timing.
7. Wash with Ringer, allow recovery, then apply atropine and repeat; then atropine followed by acetylcholine.
8. Wash again and test the local anaesthetic in the same way.

## Observations and calculation

**Specimen readings (illustrative only, not real data):** distance between marks = 1 cm.

| Treatment | Time 1 (s) | Time 2 (s) | Time 3 (s) | Mean time (s) | Velocity (cm/s) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Control (Ringer) | 20 | 21 | 19 | 20.0 | 0.050 |
| Acetylcholine | 12 | 13 | 12 | 12.3 | 0.081 |
| Atropine, then acetylcholine | 21 | 22 | 20 | 21.0 | 0.048 |
| Lignocaine | 35 | 38 | 37 | 36.7 | 0.027 |

## Result

Acetylcholine increased the speed of ciliary transport, atropine blocked this effect, and the local anaesthetic decreased ciliary movement (specimen values).

## Precautions

1. Pith the frog completely before dissection.
2. Keep the tissue moist with Ringer at all times; drying stops the cilia.
3. Use a particle of the same size each time and place it at the same point.
4. Wash well and allow recovery between drugs.
5. Do not touch the lining with instruments more than needed.

## Viva questions

1. **In which direction do the cilia beat?** Towards the stomach.
2. **What is the effect of acetylcholine on ciliary motility?** It increases it.
3. **How does atropine act here?** It blocks muscarinic receptors and prevents the increase caused by acetylcholine.
4. **Effect of local anaesthetics on cilia?** They depress ciliary movement.
5. **Why is frog Ringer used?** It keeps the tissue alive by matching the salts of frog body fluid.
6. **What is pithing?** Destroying the brain and spinal cord of the frog with a needle so it feels no pain.
7. **Name a clinical link of ciliary function.** Mucociliary clearance in the airways, which is reduced by smoking and some drugs.

# Introduction to experimental pharmacology

**Aim:** To study the scope, methods and ethical framework of experimental pharmacology.

> PCI notes that all laboratory techniques and animal experiments in BP408P are demonstrated by simulation software and videos. Your college may run every experiment in this course as a simulation. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ from this guide.

## Principle

Experimental pharmacology studies the action of drugs on living systems under controlled conditions. Its purpose is to find out **what a drug does, how it does it, how much is needed, and how safe it is** before it is given to people.

Experiments are of four broad kinds:

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In vivo | In the whole living animal | Rota-rod, actophotometer, MES test |
| In vitro | On isolated tissues, cells or enzymes outside the body | Isolated guinea pig ileum, receptor binding |
| Ex vivo | Tissue taken from a treated animal and then tested | Liver enzymes from rats given an inducer |
| In silico | Computer models and simulations | Simulated experiments, docking |

Main uses:

1. **Screening** of new compounds for a desired activity (for example anticonvulsant or anxiolytic).
2. **Bioassay**: measuring the potency of a drug by comparing its effect with a standard.
3. **Mechanism of action** studies using agonists and antagonists.
4. **Toxicity testing**: acute, sub-acute and chronic toxicity, LD50 and safety margins.
5. **Pharmacokinetic studies**: absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion.

### Ethics: the 3Rs and CPCSEA

Animal use is controlled by law and by ethics.

- **The 3Rs** (Russell and Burch, 1959):
  - **Replacement** – use non-animal methods wherever possible (cell lines, isolated tissues, simulations, videos).
  - **Reduction** – use the fewest animals that still give a reliable answer (good design, proper statistics).
  - **Refinement** – reduce pain and distress (anaesthesia, analgesia, humane end points, gentle handling).
- **CPCSEA** (Committee for the Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals) is a statutory body formed under the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960. It registers institutions and breeders and lays down rules for housing, care and experiments.
- Every institution using animals must have an **Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC)** that approves each protocol before any animal is used.
- For teaching, CPCSEA encourages alternatives. This is why the PCI syllabus asks for these experiments to be demonstrated by simulations and videos.

## Procedure

2. Read the 3Rs and give one example of each from this course.
3. Read the role of CPCSEA and IAEC; note what a protocol submitted to the IAEC must contain (aim, species, number of animals with justification, procedures, anaesthesia/analgesia, end points, method of euthanasia).
4. Open the simulation software and go through its menu: animal selection, drug selection, dose entry, recording screen and result screen.
5. Run one simple simulated experiment (for example drug effect on locomotor activity) and note how the screen shows observations.
6. Write a short note comparing a simulated experiment with a live animal experiment.

## Precautions

1. Never use a live animal in a class experiment without IAEC approval.
2. Treat simulation results as a learning aid; real experiments show biological variation.
3. Enter doses and units in the software carefully (mg/kg, not mg).
4. Keep a proper written record, as you would for a real experiment.

## Viva questions

1. **Define experimental pharmacology.** The study of drug action on living systems (whole animals, tissues or cells) under controlled conditions.
2. **What are the 3Rs?** Replacement, Reduction and Refinement of animal use.
3. **What is CPCSEA?** The statutory committee under the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960 that controls and supervises experiments on animals in India.
4. **What is the IAEC?** The Institutional Animal Ethics Committee, which must approve every animal protocol in an institution.
5. **Difference between in vivo and in vitro?** In vivo is in the whole living animal; in vitro is on tissues or cells outside the body.
6. **Give two alternatives to animal experiments.** Computer simulations and cell or tissue cultures.
7. **What is screening?** Testing many compounds by simple tests to pick out those with a desired activity.
8. **Why are simulations preferred for teaching?** They replace animals, are repeatable and cheap, and show the expected result clearly.

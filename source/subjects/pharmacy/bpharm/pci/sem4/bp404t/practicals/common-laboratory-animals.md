# Study of common laboratory animals

**Aim:** To study the common laboratory animals, their features and their uses in pharmacological experiments.

> This study is done from charts, videos and simulation software. Any live animal work needs IAEC approval under CPCSEA rules; the 3Rs apply (prefer alternatives, use the fewest animals, avoid pain).

## Principle

Different species respond differently to drugs. A species is chosen because its body, size or sensitivity suits the test. Knowing these features explains why, for example, the guinea pig is used for histamine studies and the rabbit for eye and pyrogen studies.

## Requirements

Charts or videos of laboratory animals, simulation software, notebook.

## Procedure

1. Study each animal: scientific name, common strains, approximate adult weight used in experiments, special features and uses.
2. Note how each animal is held and identified (ear punch, ear tag, picric acid marks on fur, cage cards).
3. Fill in the table below from your study.

### Main animals

| Animal | Scientific name | Common strains | Special features | Main uses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mouse | *Mus musculus* | Swiss albino | Small, cheap, breeds fast, needs little drug | Acute toxicity (LD50), screening of CNS drugs, analgesics, anticonvulsants |
| Rat | *Rattus norvegicus* | Wistar, Sprague-Dawley | No gall bladder; cannot vomit; good size for blood sampling and surgery | Toxicity, anti-inflammatory and anti-ulcer tests, behaviour, isolated tissues |
| Guinea pig | *Cavia porcellus* | Dunkin-Hartley | Cannot make its own vitamin C; very sensitive to histamine | Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, isolated ileum, local anaesthetic (intradermal wheal) |
| Rabbit | *Oryctolagus cuniculus* | New Zealand white | Large ear veins; large eyes; fever response to pyrogens | Pyrogen test, eye experiments, local anaesthetics on cornea, isolated intestine |
| Frog | Indian bull frog (now *Hoplobatrachus tigerinus*) | – | Cold-blooded; tissues survive at room temperature | Isolated heart, rectus abdominis, ciliary motility (mostly replaced by simulations) |
| Hamster | *Mesocricetus auratus* | Syrian | Cheek pouches | Some infection and cancer studies |

Larger animals such as dogs, cats and monkeys are used only in special studies with strict permissions, and not for undergraduate teaching.

## Result

The common laboratory animals, their special features and their uses in experimental pharmacology were studied.

## Precautions

1. Hold each animal by the method suited to it (a mouse by the tail base and scruff, a rabbit by the scruff with its hind quarters supported – never by the ears).
2. Use animals of the same strain, sex range, age and weight range in one experiment.
3. Allow animals to settle in the lab before testing.
4. Follow CPCSEA rules for every live animal.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is the mouse used for LD50 studies?** It is small, cheap, breeds fast and needs very little drug.
2. **Which laboratory animal has no gall bladder?** The rat.
3. **Why is the guinea pig used for anaphylaxis studies?** It is very sensitive to histamine and develops strong bronchospasm.
4. **Which animal is used for the pyrogen test?** The rabbit.
5. **Which vitamin must be added to the guinea pig's diet?** Vitamin C, because it cannot make it.
6. **Name two rat strains.** Wistar and Sprague-Dawley.
7. **Why is the rabbit used for eye experiments?** Its eye is large and the pupil of the albino rabbit is easy to watch.
8. **Why can't rats be used to test anti-emetics?** Rats cannot vomit.

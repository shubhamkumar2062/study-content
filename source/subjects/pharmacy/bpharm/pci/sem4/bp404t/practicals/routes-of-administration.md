# Study of different routes of drug administration in mice and rats

**Aim:** To study the common routes of drug administration in mice and rats and to calculate the volume of drug solution to be given.

> These techniques are demonstrated by simulation and videos as per the PCI note. On live animals they need IAEC approval, trained handling and CPCSEA rules. Volumes below are general guides; follow your lab manual.

## Principle

The route decides how fast and how much of a drug reaches the blood. Intravenous gives the fastest effect and complete availability; intraperitoneal is quick and easy in rodents; oral is slowest and subject to first-pass metabolism; subcutaneous and intramuscular give slower, steadier absorption.

Dose volume is calculated from the animal's weight:

**Volume to inject (ml) = Dose (mg/kg) × Body weight (kg) ÷ Concentration (mg/ml)**

## Requirements

Mice (about 20–30 g) and rats (about 150–250 g) or simulation software, animal balance, 1 ml tuberculin/insulin syringes, fine needles (higher gauge for mice), oral feeding (gavage) needle, rodent restrainer, spirit swabs, normal saline.

## Procedure

1. Weigh each animal and calculate the volume using the formula above.
2. Hold the animal firmly but gently (mouse: tail base and loose skin of the neck; rat: around the shoulders).
3. Give the dose by the chosen route as below.

| Route | Site and method | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oral (gavage) | Ball-tipped feeding needle passed gently over the tongue into the oesophagus with the head and body in a straight line | Never force; resistance means the trachea may be entered |
| Intraperitoneal (i.p.) | Lower part of the abdomen to one side of the midline, animal held head-down so the gut moves forward | Avoid the bladder and gut; most used route in rodent practicals |
| Subcutaneous (s.c.) | Loose skin at the back of the neck or flank, lifted into a tent | Slow absorption |
| Intramuscular (i.m.) | Thigh muscles of the hind limb | Small volumes only in mice |
| Intravenous (i.v.) | Lateral tail vein, warmed to dilate it, animal in a restrainer | Fastest action; inject slowly; small volumes |
| Others | Intradermal, intracerebroventricular, topical, rectal, inhalation | Special purposes |

4. After injection, return the animal to its cage and watch for distress or leakage at the site.

A common upper guide for oral and intraperitoneal dosing in mice is about 10 ml/kg; smaller volumes are used for i.m. and i.v.

## Observations and calculation

**Worked example (illustrative values only):** a mouse weighs 25 g (0.025 kg). The dose is 10 mg/kg and the stock solution is 1 mg/ml.
Amount of drug = 10 × 0.025 = 0.25 mg. Volume = 0.25 ÷ 1 = **0.25 ml**.

**Specimen record (illustrative only, not real data):**

| Animal | Weight (g) | Dose (mg/kg) | Concentration (mg/ml) | Volume given (ml) | Route | Onset of observed effect (min) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mouse 1 | 25 | 10 | 1 | 0.25 | i.v. | 1 |
| Mouse 2 | 24 | 10 | 1 | 0.24 | i.p. | 5 |
| Mouse 3 | 26 | 10 | 1 | 0.26 | s.c. | 12 |
| Mouse 4 | 25 | 10 | 1 | 0.25 | Oral | 20 |

The order of onset in the specimen record (i.v. < i.p. < s.c. < oral) is the expected order.

## Result

The routes of administration in mice and rats were studied. The expected order of onset of action is intravenous < intraperitoneal < subcutaneous < oral.

## Precautions

1. Weigh each animal on the day of the experiment.
2. Use a fresh, sharp needle of the right size; remove air bubbles before i.v. injection.
3. Pull back the plunger slightly before i.p. injection; if blood, urine or gut contents appear, withdraw.
4. Do not exceed recommended volumes.
5. Handle gently; stress changes results.

## Viva questions

1. **Which route gives the fastest action?** Intravenous.
2. **Which vein is used for i.v. injection in mice?** The lateral tail vein.
3. **Why is the animal held head-down for i.p. injection?** So the intestines move towards the chest and are not punctured.
4. **What is an oral feeding needle?** A blunt, ball-tipped needle for giving liquids directly into the stomach.
5. **How do you calculate injection volume?** Dose (mg/kg) × weight (kg) ÷ concentration (mg/ml).
6. **Why is oral availability lower?** Because of incomplete absorption and first-pass metabolism in the gut wall and liver.
7. **Why is the tail warmed before i.v. injection?** To dilate the vein so it is easier to see and enter.

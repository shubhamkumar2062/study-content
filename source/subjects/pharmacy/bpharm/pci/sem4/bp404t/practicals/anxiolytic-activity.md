# Study of anxiolytic activity of drugs using rats or mice

**Aim:** To study the anxiolytic (anti-anxiety) activity of a drug in rats or mice using the elevated plus maze.

> Shown by simulation or video as per the PCI note. Live use needs IAEC approval under CPCSEA rules; each animal is tested only once on the maze, so plan group sizes carefully (3Rs). Use the drug and dose from your lab manual.

## Principle

Rodents naturally avoid open, raised spaces and prefer enclosed spaces. The **elevated plus maze** has two open arms and two closed (walled) arms in a plus shape, raised above the floor. A normal animal spends most of its time in the closed arms. An **anxiolytic** such as **diazepam** reduces this fear, so the animal **enters the open arms more often and spends more time in them**.

```text
                  closed arm (walls)
                        ||
     open arm  ======== [ ] ========  open arm
                        ||
                  closed arm (walls)
            (whole maze raised above the floor)
```

**% open-arm entries = Open-arm entries ÷ (Open + Closed entries) × 100**
**% time in open arms = Time in open arms ÷ (Time in open + closed arms) × 100**

Other anxiety models: light–dark box, open field, hole-board, social interaction test.

## Requirements

Elevated plus maze (dimensions as in your manual), rats or mice, diazepam, vehicle, syringes, balance, stopwatch or video tracking, dilute alcohol for cleaning; or simulation software.

## Procedure

1. Divide animals into a control (vehicle) group and a test (diazepam) group.
2. Give the vehicle or drug (usually intraperitoneally) and wait for the time in your manual (for example 30 min).
3. Place each animal in the centre of the maze facing an open arm.
4. For a fixed period (commonly 5 min) record:
   - number of entries into open arms and closed arms (an entry = all four paws inside the arm);
   - time spent in open and closed arms.
5. Remove the animal, clean the maze, and test the next one.
6. Calculate % open-arm entries and % time in open arms.

## Observations and calculation

**Specimen readings for a 5-min test (illustrative only, not real data):**

| Group | Animal | Open-arm entries | Closed-arm entries | % open entries | Time open (s) | Time closed (s) | % time open |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Control | 1 | 2 | 10 | 16.7 | 25 | 240 | 9.4 |
| | 2 | 3 | 9 | 25.0 | 35 | 225 | 13.5 |
| Diazepam | 1 | 7 | 8 | 46.7 | 110 | 150 | 42.3 |
| | 2 | 8 | 7 | 53.3 | 125 | 140 | 47.2 |

Worked example (Control animal 1): % open entries = 2 ÷ (2 + 10) × 100 = 16.7 %.

## Result

Diazepam increased the percentage of open-arm entries and time spent in the open arms, showing anxiolytic activity (specimen values).

## Precautions

1. Test each animal only once on the maze; repeat exposure changes behaviour.
2. Clean the maze between animals to remove smell cues.
3. Keep light, noise and time of day the same for all animals.
4. The observer should sit still and away from the maze, or use video.
5. Note any fall from an open arm and exclude that animal as per the manual.

## Viva questions

1. **Principle of the elevated plus maze?** Natural fear of open, raised spaces; anxiolytics increase exploration of the open arms.
2. **Standard anxiolytic used?** Diazepam.
3. **Mechanism of diazepam?** It increases the frequency of GABA-A chloride channel opening in response to GABA.
4. **What counts as an arm entry?** All four paws inside the arm.
5. **Name two other anxiety models.** Light–dark box and open field test.
6. **Why is each animal tested only once?** Prior exposure reduces open-arm exploration and makes the test insensitive.
7. **What would an anxiogenic drug do?** Reduce open-arm entries and time.

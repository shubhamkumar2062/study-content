# Effect of drugs on locomotor activity using the actophotometer

**Aim:** To study the effect of CNS depressant and CNS stimulant drugs on locomotor activity in mice using the actophotometer.

> Shown by simulation or video as per the PCI note. Live use needs IAEC approval under CPCSEA rules; compare each animal with itself to keep numbers low (3Rs). Use the drugs and doses in your lab manual.

## Principle

Locomotor activity is a measure of alertness. The actophotometer has light beams falling on photocells across the floor of an arena. Each time the animal moves across a beam, the beam is cut and a digital counter adds one count. The arena is usually square for rats and circular for mice.

- **CNS depressants** (diazepam, chlorpromazine, phenobarbitone) **lower** the count.
- **CNS stimulants** (caffeine, amphetamine) **raise** the count.

**% change in activity = (After − Before) ÷ Before × 100**  (negative = decrease)

## Requirements

Actophotometer, mice, a CNS depressant (for example diazepam or chlorpromazine) and, if required, a stimulant (for example caffeine), vehicle, syringes, balance, stopwatch; or simulation software.

## Procedure

1. Switch on the actophotometer and check that all beams fall on the photocells (counter steady when the arena is empty).
2. Place a mouse in the arena and record the count for a fixed time (for example 10 min). This is the basal activity.
3. Give the drug (usually intraperitoneally) and return the mouse to its cage.
4. After the waiting time in your manual (for example 30 min), place it in the arena again and record the count for the same time.
5. Repeat for each mouse and calculate the % change.

## Observations and calculation

**Specimen readings for a 10-min count (illustrative only, not real data):**

| Group | Mouse | Basal count | Count after drug | % change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Diazepam | 1 | 260 | 95 | −63.5 |
| | 2 | 240 | 88 | −63.3 |
| | 3 | 275 | 110 | −60.0 |
| Caffeine | 1 | 250 | 390 | +56.0 |
| | 2 | 230 | 360 | +56.5 |
| | 3 | 265 | 400 | +50.9 |

## Result

The CNS depressant decreased and the CNS stimulant increased locomotor activity in mice (specimen: about −62 % and +54 %).

## Precautions

1. Allow each mouse to settle in the test room before the basal count.
2. Keep the room quiet, with constant light and temperature.
3. Use the same counting time before and after the drug.
4. Clean the arena between animals.
5. Test at the same time of day; activity varies with the light–dark cycle.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the principle of the actophotometer?** The moving animal cuts light beams falling on photocells; each cut is counted.
2. **Effect of diazepam on locomotor activity?** It decreases it.
3. **Effect of caffeine?** It increases it.
4. **Why is the basal count taken?** Each animal serves as its own control.
5. **Shape of the arena for mice and rats?** Circular for mice, square for rats.
6. **Why should the room be quiet?** Noise and disturbance change the animal's activity.
7. **Mechanism of caffeine's stimulant action?** Blockade of adenosine receptors in the brain.

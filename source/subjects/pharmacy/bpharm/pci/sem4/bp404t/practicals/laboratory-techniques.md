# Common laboratory techniques: blood withdrawal, serum and plasma separation, anaesthesia and euthanasia

**Aim:** To study the methods of blood withdrawal from laboratory animals, the separation of serum and plasma, and the anaesthetics and euthanasia methods used in animal studies.

> PCI asks for these techniques to be demonstrated by simulations and videos. On live animals they are done only by trained persons, under an IAEC-approved protocol and CPCSEA rules. Follow your manual for drug choices and doses.

## Principle

Blood samples are needed to measure drug levels, enzymes, glucose, lipids and blood cells. The method chosen depends on the species, the volume needed and whether the animal must survive. Animals must not feel pain or distress, so suitable anaesthesia is used, and at the end of a study they are killed humanely (euthanasia) by approved methods.

**Serum** is the clear fluid left after blood clots; it has no clotting factors such as fibrinogen. **Plasma** is the fluid part of blood kept from clotting by an anticoagulant; it still contains fibrinogen.

## Requirements

Capillary tubes (heparinised and plain), sample tubes (plain, EDTA, heparin, citrate), centrifuge, micropipettes, needles and syringes, restrainer, cotton, spirit, xylene or warm water for dilating tail veins, anaesthetic agents, simulation software or videos.

## Procedure

### A. Blood withdrawal

| Site | Species | Key points | Survival? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Retro-orbital plexus | Rat, mouse | Under light anaesthesia, a fine capillary is passed at the inner corner of the eye into the venous plexus; blood flows by capillary action | Yes, if done correctly; refined methods are now preferred |
| Lateral tail vein / tail snip | Rat, mouse | Warm the tail to dilate the vein; small volumes | Yes |
| Saphenous vein | Rat, mouse | Shave the leg, prick the vein, collect drops | Yes |
| Marginal ear vein | Rabbit | Dilate the vein by warming or gentle rubbing; use a fine needle | Yes |
| Cardiac puncture | Rat, mouse, rabbit | Under deep anaesthesia; large volume | No (terminal only) |

General rules: take the smallest volume that is enough; as a rough rule, no more than about 10 % of the animal's blood volume at one time (blood volume is roughly 6–8 % of body weight); allow recovery time between samples; press the site to stop bleeding.

### B. Separation of serum
1. Collect blood in a plain tube (no anticoagulant).
2. Let it stand at room temperature until it clots (about 20–30 min).
3. Centrifuge (for example about 3000 rpm for 10–15 min).
4. Pipette the clear, straw-coloured upper layer (serum) into a labelled tube.

### C. Separation of plasma
1. Collect blood in a tube containing an anticoagulant (EDTA, heparin or sodium citrate) and mix gently by inversion.
2. Centrifuge at once.
3. Pipette the upper layer (plasma) without disturbing the buffy coat and red cells.
4. Store serum and plasma cold (refrigerated or frozen) until analysis.

### D. Anaesthetics used in animal studies

| Agent | Route | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Isoflurane | Inhalation | Fast onset and recovery; preferred where available |
| Ketamine with xylazine | Injection (i.p./i.m.) | Common combination for surgery in rodents |
| Thiopentone / pentobarbitone | Injection (i.p./i.v.) | Barbiturates; also used in overdose for euthanasia |
| Urethane | Injection | Long anaesthesia for terminal experiments only; carcinogenic, handle with care |
| Diethyl ether | Inhalation | Old method; irritant and highly flammable, now discouraged |

Check depth of anaesthesia by loss of the pedal (toe-pinch) reflex and the righting reflex; watch breathing and keep the animal warm.

### E. Euthanasia
Acceptable methods (as per CPCSEA, chosen for species and purpose) include an overdose of a barbiturate, carbon dioxide in a properly controlled chamber, and physical methods such as cervical dislocation of small rodents done only by trained persons. Confirm death by absence of heartbeat, breathing and corneal reflex. Dispose of carcasses by incineration. Methods causing distress, such as drowning, are never acceptable.

## Precautions

1. Use sharp, fine needles and the correct site to avoid injury and haemolysis.
2. Do not shake blood vigorously; it causes haemolysis.
3. Label every tube with animal number, group, time and date.
4. Check depth of anaesthesia before any painful step.
5. Use only CPCSEA-approved euthanasia methods and confirm death.

## Viva questions

1. **Difference between serum and plasma?** Serum is the fluid after clotting and has no fibrinogen; plasma is from anticoagulated blood and contains fibrinogen.
2. **Name three anticoagulants.** EDTA, heparin and sodium citrate.
3. **Which blood collection method is terminal?** Cardiac puncture.
4. **Where is blood taken in rabbits?** From the marginal ear vein.
5. **Why is ether discouraged now?** It irritates the airways and is highly flammable.
6. **Why is urethane used only for terminal experiments?** It gives long anaesthesia and is carcinogenic, so the animal is not allowed to recover.
7. **How is depth of anaesthesia checked?** By loss of the pedal (toe-pinch) and righting reflexes.
8. **Name two acceptable euthanasia methods.** Barbiturate overdose and controlled carbon dioxide exposure.

# Effect of skeletal muscle relaxants using the rota-rod apparatus

**Aim:** To study the effect of a centrally acting skeletal muscle relaxant on motor coordination in mice using the rota-rod apparatus.

> Shown by simulation or video as per the PCI note. Live use needs IAEC approval under CPCSEA rules; screen and train animals so fewer are needed (3Rs). Use the drug and dose from your lab manual.

## Principle

The rota-rod is a horizontal rod turning at a fixed speed. A normal, trained mouse can keep walking on it for a long time. Drugs that relax skeletal muscle or impair motor coordination (for example diazepam and other benzodiazepines, or centrally acting relaxants) make the mouse lose grip and **fall off earlier**. The fall-off time before and after the drug is compared.

```text
        rotating rod (fixed speed)
   ====================================
     |  mouse  |  mouse  |  mouse  |      partitions keep
     |   lane  |   lane  |   lane  |      animals apart
   ------------------------------------
        fall-off area / trip platforms
```

**% decrease in fall-off time = (Before − After) ÷ Before × 100**

## Requirements

Rota-rod apparatus, mice, diazepam (or another centrally acting muscle relaxant), vehicle (normal saline or as suited to the drug), syringes, balance, stopwatch; or simulation software.

## Procedure

1. Switch on the rota-rod and set the speed fixed by your manual.
2. **Screening:** place each mouse on the rod. Select only mice that stay on for a set cut-off time (for example 3–5 min) in repeated trials; reject the others.
3. Record the basal fall-off time of each selected mouse.
4. Give the test drug to the test group and vehicle to the control group (usually intraperitoneally).
5. After the waiting time in your manual (for example 30 min), place each mouse on the rod again and note the fall-off time. Stop at the cut-off time if the mouse has not fallen.
6. Calculate the % decrease in fall-off time for each mouse and the group mean.

## Result

Diazepam reduced the fall-off time on the rota-rod, showing loss of muscle tone and motor coordination (specimen: about ___ % decrease).

## Precautions

1. Use only screened, trained mice.
2. Keep the rod speed and cut-off time the same for all animals.
3. Test at the same time after dosing for every mouse.
4. Keep the rod clean and dry; a soiled rod changes grip.
5. Place a soft pad below the rod so falling mice are not hurt.

## Viva questions

1. **What does the rota-rod test measure?** Motor coordination and muscle tone.
2. **Why are mice screened first?** To use only animals that can stay on the rod, so a fall after the drug is due to the drug.
3. **Name a standard drug for this test.** Diazepam.
4. **Mechanism of diazepam's muscle relaxation?** It enhances GABA-A receptor action in the spinal cord and brain, reducing reflex muscle tone.
5. **Name a centrally acting muscle relaxant other than benzodiazepines.** Baclofen (also tizanidine, chlorzoxazone).
6. **Name a peripherally acting muscle relaxant.** d-Tubocurarine or succinylcholine (neuromuscular blockers).
7. **What else besides muscle relaxation can make a mouse fall?** Sedation or ataxia from any CNS depressant, such as alcohol.

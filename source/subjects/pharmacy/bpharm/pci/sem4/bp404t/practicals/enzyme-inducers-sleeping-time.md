# Effect of hepatic microsomal enzyme inducers on phenobarbitone sleeping time in mice

**Aim:** To study the effect of pretreatment with a hepatic microsomal enzyme inducer on barbiturate-induced sleeping time in mice.

> Per the PCI note, this experiment is shown by simulation software or video. Any live version needs IAEC approval under CPCSEA rules; keep the number of animals to the minimum (3Rs). Use the drugs and doses in your lab manual.

## Principle

Barbiturates are broken down mainly by cytochrome P450 enzymes in the smooth endoplasmic reticulum (microsomes) of liver cells. **Enzyme inducers** such as phenobarbitone, phenytoin, carbamazepine and rifampicin increase the amount of these enzymes when given for a few days. A barbiturate given afterwards is metabolised faster, so its blood level falls sooner and **sleeping time becomes shorter**.

**Enzyme inhibitors** (for example cimetidine or ketoconazole) do the opposite and prolong sleeping time; some manuals add an inhibitor group for comparison.

Sleep is judged by the **righting reflex**: a mouse placed on its back normally turns over at once.

- **Onset of sleep** = time from injection to loss of righting reflex.
- **Sleeping time (duration)** = time from loss to return of righting reflex.

The syllabus names phenobarbitone as the hypnotic. Phenobarbitone is slow-acting, so many manuals use a shorter-acting barbiturate such as pentobarbitone or thiopentone on the test day to measure sleeping time; the principle is the same.

## Requirements

Mice of the same sex and similar weight, divided into groups; enzyme inducer (for example phenobarbitone), barbiturate for the test day, normal saline, syringes, balance, stopwatch, cages; or simulation software.

## Procedure

1. Divide the mice into two groups (an inhibitor group may be added).
   - **Group I (control):** normal saline daily for the pretreatment period.
   - **Group II (induced):** the enzyme inducer daily for the pretreatment period (usually several days, as in your manual).
2. On the test day, weigh every mouse and give the barbiturate intraperitoneally at the same dose to all groups.
3. Start the stopwatch at injection. Turn each mouse on its back at short intervals; note the time when it can no longer turn over (loss of righting reflex = onset).
4. Keep the mice warm and watch them; note the time when the righting reflex returns.
5. Calculate onset and duration of sleep for each mouse and the group mean.

## Observations and calculation

**Specimen readings (illustrative only, not real data):**

% decrease in sleeping time = (Control mean − Test mean) ÷ Control mean × 100
= (91.7 − 45.7) ÷ 91.7 × 100 ≈ **50 %** (specimen value).

In a real experiment, compare group means with an unpaired t-test (two groups) or one-way ANOVA (more groups).

## Result

Pretreatment with the hepatic microsomal enzyme inducer shortened the barbiturate sleeping time (specimen: by about ___ %), showing faster metabolism of the barbiturate after enzyme induction.

## Precautions

1. Use mice of the same sex, age and weight range, and fast them equally if the manual asks.
2. Give the same barbiturate dose (mg/kg) to all groups.
3. Keep sleeping mice warm; low body temperature prolongs sleep and can kill.
4. Test the righting reflex the same way for every mouse.
5. Note times exactly with a stopwatch.

## Viva questions

1. **What is enzyme induction?** An increase in the amount of drug-metabolising enzymes caused by repeated exposure to certain drugs.
2. **Name three enzyme inducers.** Phenobarbitone, rifampicin and phenytoin (also carbamazepine).
3. **Why does an inducer shorten sleeping time?** The barbiturate is metabolised faster, so its level falls below the sleep-producing level sooner.
4. **What is the righting reflex?** The reflex by which an animal placed on its back turns back onto its feet.
5. **Name an enzyme inhibitor and its effect here.** Cimetidine or ketoconazole; it prolongs sleeping time.
6. **Where are microsomal enzymes found?** In the smooth endoplasmic reticulum of liver cells.
7. **Why is pretreatment given for several days?** Induction needs new enzyme to be made, which takes days.
8. **Clinical importance of enzyme induction?** It lowers the effect of other drugs, for example rifampicin reducing the effect of oral contraceptives.

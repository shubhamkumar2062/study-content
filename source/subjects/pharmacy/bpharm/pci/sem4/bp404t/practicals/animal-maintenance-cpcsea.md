# Maintenance of laboratory animals as per CPCSEA guidelines

**Aim:** To study the housing, feeding, care and record keeping of laboratory animals as laid down in the CPCSEA guidelines.

> This topic is studied from the CPCSEA guidelines, videos and, where possible, a supervised visit to the registered animal house. Always check the current CPCSEA document; values below are general guides.

## Principle

Healthy, unstressed animals give reliable results. Poor housing, noise, wrong temperature or rough handling change body functions and spoil experiments. CPCSEA, set up under the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960, lays down standards so that animals are kept humanely and experiments are valid. These standards put the **Refinement** part of the 3Rs into practice.

## Requirements

Copy of the current CPCSEA guidelines, animal house plan, sample record registers, cage and bedding samples (or pictures), notebook.

## Procedure

Study and note each heading below.

### 1. Registration and committees
1. The institution and every breeder supplying animals must be **registered with CPCSEA**.
2. An **IAEC** (Institutional Animal Ethics Committee) includes scientists, a veterinarian, a person from outside the institution, a socially aware non-scientist and CPCSEA nominees. It approves protocols and inspects the facility.
3. Animals are bought only from registered breeders.

### 2. Building and environment
| Factor | General practice for rodents |
| --- | --- |
| Temperature | Kept steady, about 22 ± 3 °C |
| Relative humidity | About 30–70 % |
| Light | 12 h light : 12 h dark cycle |
| Ventilation | Fresh, filtered air with frequent air changes; no recirculation of dirty air |
| Noise | Kept low; no loud machines near rooms |
| Separation | Separate rooms for species, quarantine, breeding and experiments; clean and dirty corridors kept apart |

### 3. Cages and bedding
- Polypropylene or stainless-steel cages with stainless-steel grill tops; easy to clean and autoclave.
- Clean, dry, dust-free bedding (for example paddy husk or corn cob), sterilised and changed regularly.
- Not too many animals in one cage; space as per CPCSEA tables.
- Cage card on every cage: species, strain, sex, number, date of arrival, project and investigator.

### 4. Food and water
- Standard pelleted diet and clean drinking water, normally available at all times (ad libitum) unless a protocol needs fasting.
- Guinea pigs need extra vitamin C.
- Feed stored in a clean, dry, vermin-proof store.

### 5. Health care
- **Quarantine** new animals and allow an **acclimatisation** period (often about a week) before use.
- A veterinarian checks animal health; sick animals are separated and treated or humanely killed.
- Pain is controlled with anaesthetics and analgesics; humane end points are set in every protocol.

### 6. Hygiene and waste
- Regular cleaning and disinfection of rooms and cages.
- Dead animals and carcasses are disposed of by incineration or as per biomedical waste rules.

### 7. Records
Animal purchase register, breeding register, health and treatment record, experiment record, and death/disposal register.

## Result

The CPCSEA requirements for registration, housing, feeding, health care, hygiene and records of laboratory animals were studied.

## Precautions

1. Wear apron, gloves, mask and cap in the animal house.
2. Do not move animals between rooms without permission.
3. Never overcrowd cages.
4. Keep records up to date; they are inspected.

## Viva questions

1. **Under which Act was CPCSEA set up?** The Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960.
2. **What is quarantine?** Keeping newly arrived animals apart and under watch so that diseases are not brought into the colony.
3. **What is acclimatisation?** A period that lets animals settle to the new environment before use.
4. **What light cycle is used?** 12 h light and 12 h dark.
5. **What is ad libitum?** Free access to food and water at all times.
6. **What material is used for cages?** Polypropylene or stainless steel, with steel grill tops.
7. **How are carcasses disposed of?** By incineration or as per biomedical waste rules.
8. **Which part of the 3Rs does good housing support?** Refinement.

# Anticonvulsant effect of drugs by the MES and PTZ methods

**Aim:** To study the anticonvulsant effect of drugs against maximal electroshock (MES) seizures and pentylenetetrazole (PTZ)-induced seizures in rats or mice.

> Shown by simulation or video as per the PCI note. Seizure models cause distress, so live use needs strict IAEC approval under CPCSEA rules, the fewest animals and humane end points (3Rs). Use drugs and doses from your lab manual.

## Principle

### A. Maximal electroshock (MES) – model of generalised tonic-clonic seizures
An electric current is passed through corneal or ear electrodes from an electroconvulsiometer. A commonly used setting is 150 mA for 0.2 s in rats and 50 mA for 0.2 s in mice. The seizure has these phases:

```text
 tonic flexion → tonic extensor (hind limbs stretched back) → clonus → stupor → recovery (or death)
```

**Abolition of the tonic hind-limb extensor phase** (or a clear cut in its duration) is taken as protection. **Phenytoin** is the standard drug; phenobarbitone, carbamazepine and valproate also protect.

### B. Pentylenetetrazole (PTZ) – model of absence and myoclonic seizures
PTZ is a GABA-A receptor antagonist. Given by injection, it produces clonic jerks and convulsions. Delay in onset of clonic seizures, fewer or no seizures, and survival show protection. **Diazepam** (also ethosuximide, valproate, phenobarbitone) protects. **Phenytoin does not protect against PTZ seizures**, which matches its clinical lack of effect in absence seizures.

## Requirements

Electroconvulsiometer with corneal or ear-clip electrodes, saline for wetting electrodes, PTZ solution, phenytoin, diazepam, vehicle, syringes, balance, stopwatch, observation cages; or simulation software.

## Procedure

### A. MES method
1. Weigh the animals and divide them into control and test groups.
2. Give vehicle to the control group and phenytoin to the test group (usually intraperitoneally); wait for the time in your manual (for example 30 min).
3. Wet the electrodes with saline and apply them to the corneas (or ears).
4. Pass the current at the setting fixed in the manual.
5. Record the duration (s) of each phase: flexion, extensor, clonus, stupor; note recovery or death.

### B. PTZ method
1. Give vehicle to the control group and diazepam to the test group; wait for the time in your manual.
2. Inject PTZ to all animals as in the manual.
3. Watch each animal in a separate cage for a fixed period (for example 30 min).
4. Record onset (latency) of the first clonic jerk, number of clonic convulsions, occurrence of tonic seizure, and death or survival.

## Result

Phenytoin abolished the tonic hind-limb extensor phase in MES seizures and diazepam delayed or prevented PTZ seizures (specimen values), showing anticonvulsant activity.

## Precautions

1. Wet the electrodes with saline for good contact; keep everything else dry.
2. Use the same current and duration for all animals in a group.
3. Do not apply electrodes while the machine is switched on.
4. Observe each PTZ-treated animal in its own cage.
5. Set humane end points in advance and keep suffering to a minimum.

## Viva questions

1. **Which seizure type does MES represent?** Generalised tonic-clonic (grand mal) seizures.
2. **Which seizure type does PTZ represent?** Absence (petit mal) and myoclonic seizures.
3. **What is the end point of protection in MES?** Abolition of the tonic hind-limb extensor phase.
4. **Standard drug for MES?** Phenytoin.
5. **Standard drug for PTZ?** Diazepam (also ethosuximide or valproate).
6. **How does PTZ cause seizures?** By blocking GABA-A receptors and so reducing inhibition.
7. **Why is phenytoin ineffective in PTZ seizures?** It blocks sodium channels and prevents seizure spread, which does not stop PTZ-type (absence-like) seizures.
8. **List the phases of an MES seizure.** Flexion, extensor, clonus, stupor and recovery or death.

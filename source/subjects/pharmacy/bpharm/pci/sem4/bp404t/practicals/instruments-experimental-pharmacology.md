# Commonly used instruments in experimental pharmacology

**Aim:** To study the construction, working and uses of instruments commonly used in experimental pharmacology.

> These instruments may be shown as real equipment, as pictures or inside simulation software. Animal use with any of them needs IAEC approval under CPCSEA rules; follow the 3Rs.

## Principle

Each instrument either keeps a tissue alive, records a response, or measures a change in behaviour. Knowing what each measures helps you choose the right test for a drug.

## Requirements

The instruments listed below (or their images / simulated versions), lab manual, notebook.

## Procedure

1. Study each instrument: its parts, how it works, the response it records and the drugs it is used for.
2. Draw a labelled sketch of the organ bath and kymograph.
3. Fill in the observation table.

### A. Isolated tissue set-up

```text
        aerator (air / O2)
             |
   +---------+---------+        writing lever
   |  outer water bath  |  thread   _______/
   |  (thermostat)      |---------o
   |  +-------------+   |          \  writing point on
   |  | inner organ |   |             smoked drum
   |  | tube: tissue|   |
   |  | in PSS      |   |
   |  +------+------+   |
   +---------|----------+
         drain tap
```

- **Student organ bath:** an inner tube holding the tissue in a physiological salt solution (PSS), kept at a set temperature by an outer water bath and bubbled with air or oxygen. Used for isolated ileum, rectus abdominis, uterus and similar tissues.
- **Thermostat and heater:** keep the bath at the required temperature (for example about 37 °C for mammalian tissue; frog tissue is kept at room temperature).
- **Aerator:** supplies air or oxygen to the PSS.
- **Sherrington rotating drum (kymograph):** a drum covered with smoked or glazed paper that turns at a chosen speed; the lever writes the tissue's response on it.
- **Levers:** frontal writing lever (isotonic contractions of smooth muscle), Starling heart lever (frog heart). Modern labs use a force transducer and a physiograph or computer recording system instead.

### B. Behavioural and in vivo instruments

| Instrument | What it measures | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actophotometer | Locomotor activity (beam breaks by the moving animal) | CNS stimulants and depressants |
| Rota-rod | Motor coordination (time an animal stays on a turning rod) | Muscle relaxants, sedatives |
| Electroconvulsiometer | Delivers a measured electric shock | Maximal electroshock (MES) seizures |
| Eddy's hot plate / tail-flick analgesiometer | Reaction time to heat | Analgesics |
| Plethysmometer | Paw volume by fluid displacement | Anti-inflammatory drugs (paw oedema) |
| Elevated plus maze | Time and entries in open vs closed arms | Anxiolytics |
| Pole-climbing apparatus | Conditioned avoidance response | Antipsychotics |
| Metabolic cage | Separate collection of urine and faeces | Diuretics, excretion studies |

### C. Handling and dosing aids

Animal weighing balance, rodent restrainer, oral feeding (gavage) needle, insulin and tuberculin syringes, fine needles, rabbit holder, frog board and dissection set, stopwatch, centrifuge for blood samples.

## Result

## Precautions

1. Keep the organ bath temperature and aeration steady; changes alter tissue responses.
2. Clean behavioural apparatus between animals so smell cues do not affect the next animal.
3. Check that electrodes and electrical instruments are earthed and dry.
4. Handle animals gently; stress changes behavioural results.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the use of a kymograph?** To record tissue responses on a moving drum against time.
2. **Why is the PSS aerated?** To supply oxygen and keep the tissue alive and responsive.
3. **What does an actophotometer measure?** Locomotor activity, as the number of light-beam interruptions.
4. **What does the rota-rod test?** Motor coordination; muscle relaxants make the animal fall earlier.
5. **Which instrument is used for MES seizures?** Electroconvulsiometer.
6. **What is a plethysmometer used for?** Measuring paw volume in paw-oedema models of inflammation.
7. **Name a modern replacement for the kymograph.** A force transducer with a physiograph or computer data-acquisition system.

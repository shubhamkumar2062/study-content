# Study of stereotypy and anti-catatonic activity of drugs in rats or mice

**Aim:** To study drug-induced stereotyped behaviour and its blockade by antipsychotics, and drug-induced catatonia (catalepsy) and its reversal by anti-catatonic drugs, in rats or mice.

> Shown by simulation or video as per the PCI note. Live use needs IAEC approval under CPCSEA rules; set a cut-off time so animals are not held in abnormal postures for long (3Rs, refinement). Use drugs and doses from your lab manual.

## Principle

### A. Stereotypy
Strong stimulation of dopamine receptors in the striatum by **amphetamine** (releases dopamine) or **apomorphine** (direct dopamine agonist) produces repeated, purposeless movements: sniffing, head bobbing, licking, gnawing and biting. These are scored on a rating scale. **Antipsychotics** (haloperidol, chlorpromazine) block D2 receptors and **reduce** the stereotypy score. This is a screening test for antipsychotic activity.

### B. Catatonia (catalepsy)
Blocking D2 receptors in the nigrostriatal pathway with **haloperidol** (or chlorpromazine) makes the animal keep an abnormal posture for a long time – **catalepsy**, a model of drug-induced parkinsonism (extrapyramidal side effects). Drugs that reduce this – **anticholinergics** such as scopolamine, benztropine or trihexyphenidyl, and dopaminergic drugs – show **anti-catatonic activity**.

In the **bar test**, the forepaws of the animal are placed on a horizontal bar raised above the floor; the time it keeps this posture is noted (with a fixed cut-off).

## Requirements

Rats or mice, amphetamine or apomorphine, haloperidol, an anticholinergic (for example scopolamine), vehicle, syringes, balance, observation cages, horizontal bar (catalepsy bar) set at the height in your manual, stopwatch; or simulation software.

## Procedure

### A. Stereotypy
1. Divide animals into a control group (vehicle, then amphetamine/apomorphine) and a test group (antipsychotic, then amphetamine/apomorphine).
2. Give the antipsychotic or vehicle first; after the waiting time, give amphetamine or apomorphine.
3. Place each animal in a separate cage and score stereotyped behaviour at fixed intervals (for example every 10 min for 1 h) using the scale below.

**Example rating scale (follow the scale in your manual):**

| Score | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Normal, asleep or still |
| 1 | Moving about, occasional sniffing |
| 2 | Continuous sniffing and head movements |
| 3 | Continuous sniffing with licking or rearing |
| 4 | Gnawing or biting of the cage bars |

### B. Catatonia and anti-catatonic activity
1. Give haloperidol to two groups. Group I then receives vehicle; Group II receives the anticholinergic (as timed in your manual).
2. At fixed intervals (for example 30, 60, 90 and 120 min after haloperidol), place the forepaws gently on the bar with the hind paws on the floor.
3. Note the time until the animal removes both forepaws (catalepsy time). Stop at the cut-off time (for example 180 s).

## Observations

**Specimen readings – stereotypy score at 30 min (illustrative only, not real data):**

| Group | Animal 1 | Animal 2 | Animal 3 | Mean score |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vehicle + amphetamine | 4 | 3 | 4 | 3.7 |
| Haloperidol + amphetamine | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0.7 |

**Specimen readings – catalepsy (bar) time at 60 min, cut-off 180 s (illustrative only, not real data):**

| Group | Animal 1 (s) | Animal 2 (s) | Animal 3 (s) | Mean (s) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Haloperidol + vehicle | 180 | 165 | 180 | 175 |
| Haloperidol + scopolamine | 35 | 20 | 40 | 31.7 |

## Result

The antipsychotic reduced amphetamine-induced stereotypy, and the anticholinergic reduced haloperidol-induced catatonia, showing anti-catatonic activity (specimen values).

## Precautions

1. Score behaviour at the same times for all animals, preferably without knowing the group (blind scoring).
2. Keep one animal per cage while scoring.
3. Place the forepaws on the bar gently; do not force the animal.
4. Use a fixed cut-off time in the bar test.
5. Keep the room quiet and evenly lit.

## Viva questions

1. **What is stereotypy?** Repeated, purposeless movements such as sniffing, licking and gnawing caused by strong dopamine stimulation.
2. **Name two drugs that cause stereotypy.** Amphetamine and apomorphine.
3. **Why do antipsychotics reduce stereotypy?** They block D2 dopamine receptors.
4. **What is catalepsy?** A state in which the animal keeps an imposed abnormal posture for a long time.
5. **Which drug is used to produce catalepsy?** Haloperidol.
6. **Clinical meaning of catalepsy in animals?** It predicts extrapyramidal (parkinsonism-like) side effects of antipsychotics.
7. **Name an anti-catatonic drug.** Scopolamine, benztropine or trihexyphenidyl (anticholinergics).
8. **Why do anticholinergics reduce catatonia?** D2 blockade leaves striatal cholinergic activity unopposed; anticholinergics restore the balance.

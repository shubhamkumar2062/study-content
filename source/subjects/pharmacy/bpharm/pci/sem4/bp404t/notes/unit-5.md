# Unit 5: Drugs acting on the central nervous system (part 2)

This unit covers drugs that change mood, thought and behaviour (antipsychotics, antidepressants, anti-anxiety drugs, anti-manic drugs and hallucinogens), drugs for Parkinson's and Alzheimer's disease, CNS stimulants and nootropics, opioid analgesics and their antagonists, and finally drug addiction, abuse, tolerance and dependence.

## 5.1 Psychopharmacological agents: overview

**Psychopharmacology** studies drugs used to treat mental illness.

| Disorder | Main feature | Drug group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Psychosis (schizophrenia, mania with psychotic features) | Loss of contact with reality: delusions, hallucinations, disordered thinking | Antipsychotics |
| Depression | Persistent low mood, loss of interest, sleep and appetite change, suicidal thoughts | Antidepressants |
| Anxiety disorders | Excessive worry, fear, panic | Anxiolytics |
| Bipolar disorder | Episodes of mania and depression | Mood stabilisers (anti-manic drugs) |

## 5.2 Antipsychotics (neuroleptics)

**Dopamine hypothesis of schizophrenia:** overactivity of the mesolimbic dopamine pathway causes positive symptoms (hallucinations, delusions); underactivity of the mesocortical pathway may cause negative symptoms (apathy, social withdrawal). Most antipsychotics block **D2 receptors**; atypical ones also block **5-HT2A**.

**Classification**

| Group | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| Typical (first generation) – phenothiazines | Chlorpromazine, thioridazine, trifluoperazine, fluphenazine |
| Typical – butyrophenones | Haloperidol, droperidol |
| Typical – thioxanthenes | Flupenthixol, zuclopenthixol |
| Typical – others | Pimozide, loxapine |
| Atypical (second generation) | Clozapine, olanzapine, risperidone, quetiapine, ziprasidone, aripiprazole (partial D2 agonist), amisulpride, paliperidone |
| Depot (long-acting injections) | Fluphenazine decanoate, haloperidol decanoate, flupenthixol decanoate, paliperidone palmitate |

**Actions of chlorpromazine (prototype):** neuroleptic syndrome (calm, emotional quietening, reduced psychotic symptoms without much sleep), antiemetic (D2 block in chemoreceptor trigger zone), hypothermia, α-blockade (postural hypotension), anticholinergic effects, antihistaminic sedation, increased prolactin (D2 block in pituitary).

**Uses:** schizophrenia, acute mania and bipolar disorder, psychotic depression, delirium and agitation, nausea and vomiting (prochlorperazine), intractable hiccups (chlorpromazine), Tourette's syndrome (haloperidol), neuroleptanalgesia (droperidol with fentanyl).

**Adverse effects**

| Effect | Cause | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extrapyramidal symptoms: acute dystonia, parkinsonism, akathisia | Nigrostriatal D2 block | Commoner with haloperidol; dystonia and parkinsonism treated with anticholinergics (trihexyphenidyl, promethazine) |
| Tardive dyskinesia | Long-term use; receptor supersensitivity | Repetitive mouth and tongue movements; may be irreversible; anticholinergics worsen it |
| Neuroleptic malignant syndrome | Rare, serious | Rigidity, fever, autonomic instability, raised CPK; stop drug, give dantrolene or bromocriptine |
| Hyperprolactinaemia | Tuberoinfundibular D2 block | Galactorrhoea, amenorrhoea, gynaecomastia |
| Postural hypotension | α-blockade | |
| Anticholinergic effects | Muscarinic block | Dry mouth, constipation, urinary retention |
| Weight gain, diabetes, dyslipidaemia | Atypicals (olanzapine, clozapine) | Metabolic syndrome |
| Agranulocytosis | Clozapine | Regular blood counts needed |
| QT prolongation | Thioridazine, pimozide, ziprasidone | |

**Typical vs atypical:** atypicals cause fewer extrapyramidal symptoms, help negative symptoms better and are used in resistant cases (clozapine), but cause more metabolic side effects.

## 5.3 Antidepressants

**Monoamine hypothesis:** depression is linked with a functional deficit of noradrenaline and/or serotonin in the brain. Antidepressants raise their levels quickly, but the mood benefit takes **2–4 weeks**, suggesting that adaptive receptor changes are responsible.

**Classification**

| Group | Drugs | Mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs) | Imipramine, amitriptyline, clomipramine, nortriptyline, desipramine, doxepin | Block reuptake of noradrenaline and serotonin; also block muscarinic, H1 and α1 receptors |
| Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) | Fluoxetine, sertraline, paroxetine, citalopram, escitalopram, fluvoxamine | Selective block of serotonin transporter |
| Serotonin–noradrenaline reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs) | Venlafaxine, duloxetine, desvenlafaxine | Block both transporters, without the receptor-blocking actions of TCAs |
| Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) | Phenelzine, tranylcypromine (non-selective, irreversible); moclobemide (reversible MAO-A) | Prevent breakdown of monoamines |
| Atypical antidepressants | Bupropion (noradrenaline–dopamine reuptake), mirtazapine (α2 block), trazodone, tianeptine, agomelatine | Various |

**TCAs — adverse effects:** anticholinergic (dry mouth, constipation, urinary retention, blurred vision), sedation (amitriptyline), postural hypotension, weight gain, cardiac arrhythmias. **Overdose is dangerous**: arrhythmias, convulsions, coma, anticholinergic signs. Other uses: neuropathic pain, migraine prophylaxis, nocturnal enuresis (imipramine), obsessive–compulsive disorder (clomipramine).

**SSRIs — advantages:** safer in overdose, little anticholinergic or cardiac effect, once daily; now first-line. **Adverse effects:** nausea, insomnia or drowsiness, anxiety at the start, sexual dysfunction, weight change, bleeding risk with NSAIDs, discontinuation symptoms, risk of suicidal thoughts in young people early in treatment. **Serotonin syndrome** (agitation, hyperthermia, sweating, tremor, myoclonus) can occur when SSRIs are combined with MAOIs, tramadol, linezolid or triptans. Uses: depression, anxiety and panic disorder, OCD, PTSD, bulimia, premature ejaculation (dapoxetine).

**MAOIs:** the **cheese reaction** — tyramine in foods (aged cheese, wine, pickled fish, yeast extracts) is not broken down in the gut and liver, releases noradrenaline, and causes a hypertensive crisis. Many drug interactions. Moclobemide (reversible) has a much smaller risk. Selegiline (MAO-B) is used in Parkinson's disease.

| Group | Drugs | Notes |

| Others | Hydroxyzine (antihistamine), pregabalin | |

## 5.5 Anti-manic drugs (mood stabilisers)

**Mania:** elevated or irritable mood, overactivity, reduced need for sleep, pressured speech, grandiosity. Mood stabilisers treat mania and prevent recurrent episodes in bipolar disorder.

**Lithium carbonate**

- Mechanism not fully known: inhibits inositol monophosphatase (depletes inositol and reduces phosphatidylinositol signalling) and glycogen synthase kinase-3.
- Uses: acute mania, prophylaxis of bipolar disorder, augmentation in resistant depression; reduces suicide risk.
- **Narrow therapeutic index: serum lithium levels must be monitored.**
- Adverse effects: tremor, thirst, polyuria (nephrogenic diabetes insipidus), hypothyroidism, weight gain, teratogenic (Ebstein's cardiac anomaly), toxicity with vomiting, diarrhoea, coarse tremor, ataxia, confusion and convulsions.
- Interactions: thiazide diuretics, NSAIDs and ACE inhibitors reduce lithium excretion and cause toxicity; sodium depletion also raises lithium levels.

**Other mood stabilisers:** valproate, carbamazepine, lamotrigine (prevents depressive episodes), atypical antipsychotics (olanzapine, quetiapine, aripiprazole), and benzodiazepines for acute agitation.

## 5.6 Hallucinogens (psychotomimetics, psychedelics)

These drugs alter perception, thought and mood and can produce a psychosis-like state with hallucinations, without much effect on memory or consciousness. They have no accepted therapeutic use in India and are abused.

| Drug | Source / notes |
| --- | --- |
| LSD (lysergic acid diethylamide) | Semisynthetic from ergot; 5-HT2A agonist; extremely potent; vivid visual hallucinations, "bad trips", flashbacks |
| Mescaline | Peyote cactus; similar to LSD |
| Psilocybin | "Magic mushrooms" |
| Cannabis (bhang, ganja, charas/hashish) | THC acts on cannabinoid CB1 receptors; euphoria, altered time sense, red eyes, tachycardia, impaired memory; heavy use may precipitate psychosis |
| Phencyclidine (PCP), ketamine | NMDA blockers; dissociative state |
| MDMA ("ecstasy") | Amphetamine derivative; releases serotonin; hyperthermia, dehydration |

## 5.7 Drugs used in Parkinson's disease

**Parkinson's disease (paralysis agitans)** results from degeneration of dopaminergic neurons of the substantia nigra, so the striatum has too little dopamine and relatively too much acetylcholine. Features: **tremor at rest, rigidity, bradykinesia (slow movement), postural instability**, mask-like face, shuffling gait.

```text
Normal:      Dopamine (inhibitory) ⇄ Acetylcholine (excitatory)   balanced
Parkinson's: Dopamine ↓                Acetylcholine relatively ↑   → symptoms
Treatment:  raise dopamine action  and/or  lower acetylcholine action
```

**Classification**

| Group | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| Dopamine precursor | Levodopa |
| Peripheral decarboxylase inhibitors (given with levodopa) | Carbidopa, benserazide |
| Dopamine agonists | Bromocriptine, ropinirole, pramipexole, rotigotine (patch), apomorphine (injection) |
| MAO-B inhibitors | Selegiline, rasagiline, safinamide |
| COMT inhibitors | Entacapone, tolcapone |
| Dopamine releaser / NMDA blocker | Amantadine |
| Central anticholinergics | Trihexyphenidyl (benzhexol), benztropine, procyclidine, biperiden |

**Levodopa**

- Dopamine does not cross the BBB; its precursor levodopa does (by an amino acid carrier), and is converted to dopamine in the brain by dopa decarboxylase.
- Given alone, most levodopa is decarboxylated in the periphery, causing nausea, vomiting and hypotension. **Carbidopa** (does not cross the BBB) blocks this peripheral conversion, so less levodopa is needed and side effects fall.
- Best on bradykinesia and rigidity; less on tremor.
- **Adverse effects:** nausea, vomiting, postural hypotension, cardiac arrhythmias, involuntary movements (dyskinesias), behavioural changes and hallucinations, "wearing-off" and "on–off" fluctuations after years of use.
- **Interactions:** pyridoxine (vitamin B6) increases peripheral decarboxylation when levodopa is given alone; non-selective MAOIs → hypertensive crisis; antipsychotics block its effect; high-protein meals reduce absorption.

**Other drugs**

- Dopamine agonists (ropinirole, pramipexole): used early in younger patients to delay levodopa, or as add-on; cause sleepiness, hallucinations and impulse-control problems. Bromocriptine also lowers prolactin.
- Selegiline: selective MAO-B inhibitor, slows dopamine breakdown in the brain; add-on.
- Entacapone: inhibits peripheral COMT, prolongs each levodopa dose ("wearing-off").
- Amantadine: mild effect, reduces levodopa-induced dyskinesia; also an antiviral.
- Anticholinergics: help tremor; also treat **drug-induced parkinsonism** from antipsychotics (where levodopa is not useful). Side effects: dry mouth, confusion in the elderly.

## 5.8 Drugs used in Alzheimer's disease

**Alzheimer's disease** is the commonest cause of dementia: progressive loss of memory, thinking and daily function. Brain changes include amyloid-β plaques, neurofibrillary tangles (tau protein), loss of cholinergic neurons (especially in the basal forebrain) and glutamate excitotoxicity.

| Group | Drugs | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Central cholinesterase inhibitors | Donepezil, rivastigmine (also patch), galantamine (also nicotinic modulator); tacrine (withdrawn, hepatotoxic) | Raise brain ACh; mild to moderate disease; side effects nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, bradycardia |
| NMDA receptor antagonist | Memantine | Blocks excess glutamate activity; moderate to severe disease; can be combined with donepezil |
| Anti-amyloid monoclonal antibodies | Lecanemab, donanemab (approved abroad in recent years) | Remove amyloid plaques; early disease; risk of brain swelling and bleeding |

These drugs improve symptoms modestly but do not cure the disease.

## 5.9 CNS stimulants and nootropics

**CNS stimulants** increase alertness, activity and wakefulness.

| Group | Drugs | Notes and uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Convulsants (no therapeutic use) | Strychnine (glycine antagonist), picrotoxin, pentylenetetrazole (GABA-A blockers) | Used experimentally; pentylenetetrazole for screening anticonvulsants |
| Analeptics (respiratory stimulants) | Doxapram, nikethamide | Limited use; mechanical ventilation preferred |
| Psychomotor stimulants | Amphetamine, dexamphetamine, methylphenidate, modafinil, cocaine | Methylphenidate and amphetamines for ADHD; modafinil and amphetamine for narcolepsy; abuse potential |
| Methylxanthines | Caffeine, theophylline, theobromine | Adenosine receptor antagonists and phosphodiesterase inhibitors; caffeine for apnoea of prematurity and in analgesic and migraine mixtures; theophylline for asthma |

**Amphetamine:** releases noradrenaline and dopamine, blocks their reuptake. Effects: wakefulness, euphoria, reduced fatigue and appetite, raised BP. Tolerance and psychological dependence; high doses cause a paranoid psychosis.

**Caffeine:** in tea and coffee; reduces drowsiness, mild diuretic, increases gastric acid; tolerance and withdrawal headache.

**Nootropics (cognition enhancers)** are drugs claimed to improve memory, learning and attention.

- **Piracetam** and related racetams: said to improve neuronal metabolism and membrane function; evidence of benefit is limited.
- Others used with this aim: cholinesterase inhibitors and memantine (in dementia), modafinil, citicoline, ginkgo extract.
- Uses claimed: dementia, after stroke or head injury, learning disorders. Their clinical value outside dementia drugs is not well established.

## 5.10 Opioid analgesics and antagonists

**Opioids** are natural, semisynthetic or synthetic drugs that act on opioid receptors and relieve pain without loss of consciousness. Opium is the dried latex of the unripe capsules of *Papaver somniferum*; it contains morphine, codeine, thebaine (phenanthrenes) and papaverine, noscapine (benzylisoquinolines).

**Opioid receptors (all Gi/Go coupled)**

| Receptor | Effects of activation |
| --- | --- |
| μ (mu) | Supraspinal and spinal analgesia, respiratory depression, euphoria, miosis, constipation, physical dependence |
| κ (kappa) | Spinal analgesia, sedation, dysphoria, diuresis, miosis |
| δ (delta) | Analgesia, mood effects |

Activation closes Ca2+ channels (less transmitter release) and opens K+ channels (hyperpolarisation), reducing pain transmission.

**Classification**

| Group | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| Natural opium alkaloids | Morphine, codeine |
| Semisynthetic | Diacetylmorphine (heroin), hydromorphone, oxycodone, buprenorphine |
| Synthetic | Pethidine (meperidine), fentanyl, methadone, tramadol, tapentadol, dextropropoxyphene |
| Mixed agonist–antagonists / partial agonists | Pentazocine (κ agonist, weak μ antagonist), butorphanol, nalbuphine, buprenorphine (μ partial agonist) |
| Pure antagonists | Naloxone, naltrexone, nalmefene |
| Peripherally acting (for gut) | Loperamide (antidiarrhoeal) |

**Actions of morphine**

- **CNS:** strong analgesia (especially dull, continuous pain), euphoria (or dysphoria), sedation, **respiratory depression** (reduced sensitivity of the respiratory centre to CO2 — the usual cause of death in poisoning), cough suppression, **miosis** (pin-point pupils, via Edinger–Westphal nucleus), nausea and vomiting (stimulates chemoreceptor trigger zone), raised ADH.
- **CVS:** vasodilation, postural hypotension (histamine release).
- **GIT:** constipation (reduced peristalsis, increased tone), spasm of the sphincter of Oddi (raises biliary pressure).
- **Urinary:** retention (increased sphincter tone).
- **Others:** histamine release (itching, bronchospasm), raised intracranial pressure secondary to CO2 retention.

**Uses of opioids:** severe pain (cancer, myocardial infarction, trauma, postoperative), pre-anaesthetic medication and balanced anaesthesia (fentanyl), acute left ventricular failure (morphine reduces preload and anxiety), cough (codeine, dextromethorphan — a non-analgesic), diarrhoea (loperamide, diphenoxylate), opioid dependence (methadone, buprenorphine maintenance).

**Contraindications/cautions:** head injury, bronchial asthma, hypothyroidism, liver disease, elderly and infants, biliary colic (spasm), with other CNS depressants.

**Notes on other opioids**

- **Codeine:** prodrug converted partly to morphine (CYP2D6); weaker analgesic; good antitussive.
- **Pethidine:** shorter acting, less constipation and miosis; toxic metabolite norpethidine (convulsions); dangerous with MAOIs.
- **Fentanyl:** very potent, short acting, used IV in anaesthesia and as a transdermal patch.
- **Methadone:** long acting, orally effective; maintenance therapy in opioid dependence.
- **Tramadol:** weak μ agonist plus noradrenaline and serotonin reuptake inhibition; can cause seizures and serotonin syndrome.
- **Buprenorphine:** partial μ agonist, long acting, ceiling on respiratory depression; used sublingually for opioid dependence; respiratory depression is hard to reverse with naloxone.

**Morphine (opioid) poisoning**

- Triad: **coma, pin-point pupils, respiratory depression**; also cyanosis, hypotension.
- Treatment: maintain airway and ventilation; **naloxone** IV, repeated as needed because it is short acting compared with most opioids.

**Opioid antagonists**

- **Naloxone:** pure competitive antagonist at all opioid receptors; given IV/IM/intranasal; rapid onset, short action; reverses opioid overdose and neonatal respiratory depression from opioids given in labour; can precipitate withdrawal in dependent persons.
- **Naltrexone:** orally active, long acting; used to prevent relapse in opioid and alcohol dependence.

## 5.11 Drug addiction, drug abuse, tolerance and dependence

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Drug abuse | Use of a drug in a way not approved medically or socially, usually self-administered for non-medical reasons |
| Drug misuse | Using a drug for a wrong indication or in a wrong dose |
| Drug addiction | Chronic relapsing disorder of compulsive drug-seeking and use despite harmful consequences, with loss of control |
| Tolerance | Need for a higher dose to get the same effect (see Unit 1); types: pharmacokinetic (enzyme induction — barbiturates, alcohol), pharmacodynamic (cellular adaptation — opioids), learned (behavioural) |
| Cross-tolerance | Tolerance to one drug extending to related drugs (alcohol, barbiturates, BZDs) |
| Psychological dependence | Compulsive desire to repeat use for pleasure or to avoid discomfort; craving |
| Physical dependence | Body adapts; abrupt stopping produces a withdrawal (abstinence) syndrome, usually the opposite of the drug's effects |
| Withdrawal syndrome | Opioids: yawning, lacrimation, rhinorrhoea, sweating, dilated pupils, gooseflesh, diarrhoea, muscle aches, craving (uncomfortable but rarely fatal). Alcohol, barbiturates and BZDs: anxiety, tremor, seizures, delirium (can be fatal) |

**Common drugs of abuse**

| Class | Examples | Psychological dependence | Physical dependence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opioids | Heroin (smack, brown sugar), morphine, codeine cough syrups, pentazocine | ++++ | ++++ |
| CNS depressants | Alcohol, barbiturates, benzodiazepines | +++ | +++ |
| Stimulants | Cocaine, amphetamine, methamphetamine | ++++ | Mild or absent |
| Cannabis | Ganja, bhang, charas | ++ | Mild |
| Hallucinogens | LSD, mescaline | + | Absent |
| Nicotine | Tobacco (cigarettes, bidi, gutkha) | ++++ | ++ |
| Volatile solvents | Glue, petrol, thinner | ++ | Mild |

**Mechanism of addiction:** most drugs of abuse increase dopamine release in the **mesolimbic reward pathway** (ventral tegmental area → nucleus accumbens), which reinforces drug-taking behaviour.

**Management of dependence**

1. **Detoxification** with supervised withdrawal: opioids — methadone or buprenorphine taper, clonidine for autonomic symptoms; alcohol — long-acting benzodiazepines plus thiamine.
2. **Maintenance or substitution therapy:** methadone, buprenorphine (with naloxone), nicotine replacement (gum, patch), varenicline (nicotinic partial agonist), bupropion for smoking.
3. **Relapse prevention:** naltrexone (opioids, alcohol), disulfiram and acamprosate (alcohol).
4. **Psychosocial support:** counselling, behavioural therapy, self-help groups, rehabilitation.

**Legal control in India:** narcotic and psychotropic substances are controlled under the **Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act, 1985**.

## Remember

- Antipsychotics block D2 (atypicals also 5-HT2A); EPS and hyperprolactinaemia with typicals; clozapine → agranulocytosis.
- TCAs: anticholinergic, cardiotoxic in overdose; SSRIs safer, first-line; MAOIs → cheese reaction; antidepressant effect takes 2–4 weeks.
- Buspirone: non-sedating anxiolytic, 5-HT1A partial agonist, no dependence.
- Lithium: narrow TI, monitor levels; toxicity raised by thiazides, NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors.
- Parkinson's: levodopa + carbidopa; anticholinergics for drug-induced parkinsonism; avoid pyridoxine with levodopa alone.
- Alzheimer's: donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine (ChE inhibitors); memantine (NMDA).
- Morphine: analgesia, respiratory depression, miosis, constipation; poisoning triad = coma + pin-point pupils + respiratory depression → naloxone.
- Dependence: psychological (craving) vs physical (withdrawal); reward pathway = mesolimbic dopamine; NDPS Act, 1985.

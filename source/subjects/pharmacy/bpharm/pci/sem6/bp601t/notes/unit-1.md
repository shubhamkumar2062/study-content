# Unit 1: β-Lactam antibiotics, aminoglycosides and tetracyclines

Medicinal Chemistry III opens with the antibiotics. For every class in this unit the syllabus asks the same six things: **historical background, nomenclature, stereochemistry, structure–activity relationship (SAR), chemical degradation, and classification with the important products**. Learn each class against that checklist. No drug in this unit is starred, so no synthesis is set here; the marks are in the nucleus, its numbering, the SAR points and the degradation products.

## 1.1 Antibiotics: definitions and history

An **antibiotic** is a substance produced by a micro-organism (or made semi-synthetically or synthetically on the same pattern) that, in low concentration, inhibits the growth of or kills other micro-organisms. A **chemotherapeutic agent** is the wider term for any chemical used against infection or cancer, natural or synthetic.

| Year | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 1928 | Alexander Fleming notices that a *Penicillium notatum* mould clears staphylococci on a culture plate |
| 1940–41 | Florey and Chain purify penicillin and show it cures infections in animals and man |
| 1944 | Waksman and Schatz isolate streptomycin from *Streptomyces griseus*; Waksman coins the word "antibiotic" |
| 1945 | Dorothy Hodgkin establishes the β-lactam structure of penicillin by X-ray crystallography |
| 1948 | Brotzu isolates *Cephalosporium acremonium*; Duggar reports chlortetracycline from *Streptomyces aureofaciens* |
| 1959 | 6-Aminopenicillanic acid (6-APA) isolated, opening the way to semi-synthetic penicillins |

**Ways of classifying antibiotics**

1. **By chemical structure:** β-lactams, aminoglycosides, tetracyclines, macrolides, polypeptides, chloramphenicol, lincosamides, rifamycins, polyenes.
2. **By mechanism:** cell wall synthesis inhibitors (β-lactams, vancomycin, cycloserine); protein synthesis inhibitors on the 30S subunit (aminoglycosides, tetracyclines) or the 50S subunit (macrolides, chloramphenicol, clindamycin); nucleic acid inhibitors (rifampicin, quinolones); membrane-active agents (polymyxins, polyenes); antimetabolites (sulphonamides, trimethoprim).
3. **By spectrum:** narrow (penicillin G), broad (tetracyclines, chloramphenicol).
4. **By effect:** bactericidal (β-lactams, aminoglycosides) or bacteriostatic (tetracyclines, macrolides, chloramphenicol).

## 1.2 Penicillins

### Nucleus, nomenclature and numbering

Penicillins are N-acyl derivatives of **6-aminopenicillanic acid (6-APA)**. The nucleus is a four-membered **β-lactam ring fused to a five-membered thiazolidine ring**. The fused bicyclic skeleton is called **penam**; with the gem-dimethyl and carboxyl groups it is **penicillanic acid**.

```text
                 H   H
     R–CO–NH ─ C6 ─ C5        S1
                │    │  ╲   ╱  ╲
                C7 ─ N4    C2(CH3)2
               ╱       ╲   │
             O          C3 ─ COOH
     β-lactam ring        thiazolidine ring
     (penam numbering: S1, C2, C3, N4, C5, C6, C7)
```

- Systematic name of the nucleus: **4-thia-1-azabicyclo[3.2.0]heptane** (Chemical Abstracts numbers N as 1 and S as 4); benzylpenicillin is then a 3,3-dimethyl-7-oxo-6-(2-phenylacetamido)-4-thia-1-azabicyclo[3.2.0]heptane-2-carboxylic acid.
- In everyday medicinal chemistry the **penam numbering** above is used: sulphur 1, the carboxyl carbon 3, nitrogen 4, side chain on C6, lactam carbonyl C7.

### Stereochemistry

The nucleus has **three chiral carbons: C3, C5 and C6** (penam numbering), with the natural, active configuration **3S, 5R, 6R** (written 2S, 5R, 6R in the CA name). The hydrogens on C5 and C6 are **cis**. The two rings are not flat: they fold along the C5–N4 bond like a half-open book, so the lactam nitrogen cannot share its lone pair with the carbonyl. This lack of amide resonance, plus ring strain, is why the β-lactam is so reactive — towards the bacterial enzyme and towards water.

Biosynthetically the nucleus comes from **L-cysteine and D-valine** (via the tripeptide δ-(L-α-aminoadipyl)-L-cysteinyl-D-valine).

### Mechanism of action

Bacterial cell walls are made of **peptidoglycan**: chains of N-acetylglucosamine and N-acetylmuramic acid cross-linked by short peptides. The last cross-linking step is catalysed by **transpeptidases**, called **penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs)**. The enzyme normally reacts with the **D-alanyl-D-alanine** end of the peptide. The β-lactam ring mimics this D-Ala-D-Ala, so the active-site **serine attacks the β-lactam carbonyl** and forms a stable **penicilloyl–enzyme** ester. The enzyme is inactivated, cross-links are not made, and the growing bacterium lyses (aided by its own autolysins). Penicillins are therefore **bactericidal** to dividing cells, and act mainly on Gram-positive organisms, whose thick peptidoglycan is exposed.

### SAR of penicillins

1. **The β-lactam ring is essential**; opening it (penicilloic acid) abolishes activity.
2. **The fused thiazolidine ring** strains the lactam and raises its reactivity; the bicyclic system is required.
3. **The free 3-carboxyl group** is needed for binding to the PBP; esters are inactive in vitro but can serve as **oral prodrugs** (pivampicillin, bacampicillin, talampicillin are esters of ampicillin hydrolysed in the body).
4. **The cis-stereochemistry 3S, 5R, 6R** is required.
5. **The 6-acylamino side chain** can be varied freely, and this is where the useful semi-synthetic changes are made:
   - **Electron-withdrawing group on the α-carbon** of the side chain (phenoxy in penicillin V, NH3+ in ampicillin) lowers the nucleophilicity of the side-chain carbonyl, so it cannot attack the β-lactam in acid → **acid-stable, oral** penicillins.
   - **Bulky groups next to the amide** (2,6-dimethoxyphenyl in methicillin, 3-aryl-5-methylisoxazolyl in oxacillin, 2-ethoxynaphthyl in nafcillin) shield the β-lactam from **penicillinase** → β-lactamase-resistant penicillins.
   - **A polar group (NH2, COOH, ureido) on the α-carbon** helps passage through the porins of Gram-negative bacteria → **extended spectrum**. Amino (ampicillin, amoxicillin) adds *E. coli*, *H. influenzae*; carboxyl (carbenicillin, ticarcillin) and ureido (piperacillin, azlocillin) add *Pseudomonas*.
   - In ampicillin the side-chain α-carbon must be **D-(−), i.e. R**; the L-isomer is much weaker.
6. **Halogen on the isoxazole aryl ring** (cloxacillin Cl, dicloxacillin 2 Cl, flucloxacillin Cl + F) improves oral absorption and raises protein binding.
7. **Replacing the 6-acylamino by an amidino group** (mecillinam) gives Gram-negative activity through a different PBP.

### Chemical degradation of penicillins

Penicillins are unstable because of the strained β-lactam. Know these products — they explain storage rules, the need for acid-stable forms, assays and allergy.

```text
                       Penicillin
          ┌───────────────┼────────────────────┐
   dilute acid (pH ~2)   alkali / β-lactamase   amines, proteins
          │               │                     │
   penicillenic acid    PENICILLOIC ACID      penicilloyl amides
          │  (rearranges)  │  acid, warm          (penicilloyl–protein
   penillic acid           │ → penilloic acid + CO2   = main allergen)
          │  HgCl2         │  HgCl2
   penicillamine +         penicillamine + penilloaldehyde + CO2
   penaldic acid
```

- **Acid:** the side-chain amide oxygen attacks the β-lactam carbonyl (neighbouring-group participation), giving **penicillenic acid**, which rearranges to **penillic acid**. This is why penicillin G is destroyed in the stomach.
- **Alkali or β-lactamase:** water or hydroxide opens the β-lactam to **penicilloic acid** (inactive). This reaction is used in the **iodometric assay**, because penicilloic acid takes up iodine while intact penicillin does not.
- **Penicilloic acid** on warming in acid loses CO2 to **penilloic acid**; with mercuric chloride it splits into **penicillamine** (D-β,β-dimethylcysteine) and **penilloaldehyde**.
- **Amines and protein amino groups** open the lactam to **penicilloyl amides**. The penicilloyl–protein conjugate acts as a hapten and is the major determinant of **penicillin allergy**.

### Classification and important products

| Group | Examples | Key point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Natural | Benzylpenicillin (penicillin G), phenoxymethylpenicillin (penicillin V), procaine and benzathine penicillin (depot salts) | G is acid-labile and injected; V is acid-stable and oral |
| β-Lactamase-resistant | Methicillin, nafcillin, oxacillin, cloxacillin, dicloxacillin, flucloxacillin | Bulky side chain; used for penicillinase-producing staphylococci |
| Aminopenicillins | Ampicillin, amoxicillin; prodrugs pivampicillin, bacampicillin, talampicillin | Extended spectrum, oral; amoxicillin is better absorbed (4-hydroxy group) |
| Carboxypenicillins | Carbenicillin, ticarcillin | Antipseudomonal |
| Ureidopenicillins | Azlocillin, mezlocillin, piperacillin | Antipseudomonal, broader still |
| Amidinopenicillin | Mecillinam (pivmecillinam is its oral ester) | Gram-negative, PBP-2 selective |

**Semi-synthetic manufacture.** Benzylpenicillin from fermentation is split by **penicillin acylase (amidase)** to 6-APA; 6-APA is then acylated with the acid chloride (or anhydride) of the desired side-chain acid, in the cold and in a buffered medium so that the β-lactam survives.

**Uses:** streptococcal and pneumococcal infections, syphilis (benzathine penicillin), rheumatic fever prophylaxis, meningitis, respiratory and urinary infections (aminopenicillins), *H. pylori* regimens (amoxicillin), serious *Pseudomonas* infections (piperacillin with tazobactam). Main adverse effect: **hypersensitivity**, up to anaphylaxis.

## 1.3 Cephalosporins

### Discovery and nucleus

Brotzu found *Cephalosporium acremonium* (now *Acremonium chrysogenum*) in a Sardinian sewage outfall in 1948; cephalosporin C was isolated from it in Oxford. Its hydrolysis gives **7-aminocephalosporanic acid (7-ACA)**, the starting material for the semi-synthetic cephalosporins, much as 6-APA is for penicillins.

```text
                H   H
    R1–CO–NH ─ C7 ─ C6      S1
               │    │  ╲   ╱  ╲
               C8 ─ N5    C2
              ╱       ╲   ║  (C2 is CH2)
             O         C4 ═ C3 ─ CH2–R2
                       │
                       COOH
    β-lactam fused to a six-membered DIHYDROTHIAZINE ring = cephem
```

- Nucleus name: **cephem** (5-thia-1-azabicyclo[4.2.0]oct-2-ene in CA terms). Cephalosporanic acid carries the 4-carboxyl and the 3-acetoxymethyl group.
- **Stereochemistry:** two chiral carbons, **C6 and C7, both R (6R, 7R)**, hydrogens cis.
- Two places to vary: the **7-acylamino side chain (R1)** and the **3-substituent (R2)**. This gives more scope than in penicillins.
- Compared with penicillins, the six-membered ring makes the lactam a little less strained, but the **Δ3 double bond conjugated with the lactam nitrogen** and the 3-acetoxy **leaving group** keep it reactive: when the lactam opens, the acetoxy group is expelled, which helps acylation of the PBP.

### SAR of cephalosporins

1. **The β-lactam, the Δ3 double bond and the 4-carboxyl group are essential.** Isomerisation of the double bond to Δ2 gives inactive compounds.
2. **7-Acylamino side chain (R1)** controls spectrum and β-lactamase stability:
   - An **α-amino group** on a phenylglycine side chain (cephalexin, cefaclor, cefadroxil, cephradine) gives **oral activity**.
   - A **syn-methoxyimino (oxime) group** on the side chain (cefuroxime, cefotaxime, ceftriaxone) gives strong **β-lactamase stability**.
   - A **2-aminothiazol-4-yl ring** (cefotaxime, ceftriaxone, ceftazidime, cefepime) increases affinity for Gram-negative PBPs; the aminothiazole-oxime pair defines the third generation.
3. **7α-Methoxy group** (the **cephamycins**: cefoxitin, cefotetan, cefmetazole) blocks β-lactamases and adds anaerobic (*Bacteroides*) cover.
4. **3-Substituent (R2)** controls pharmacokinetics:
   - **Acetoxymethyl** (cephalothin, cefotaxime) is removed by esterases to a less active **3-hydroxymethyl (desacetyl)** metabolite, which can cyclise with the 4-carboxyl to an inactive **lactone**.
   - **Methyl** (cephalexin, cefadroxil) gives a stable, orally absorbed drug.
   - **Chlorine** (cefaclor) also suits oral use.
   - **Heterocyclic thiomethyl** groups resist esterases and lengthen action; the **N-methyltetrazolethiol (NMTT)** group (cefamandole, cefoperazone, cefotetan) causes **hypoprothrombinaemia and a disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol**.
   - A **pyridinium or quaternary ammonium** group (ceftazidime, cefepime) gives a zwitterion that crosses the Gram-negative outer membrane fast — antipseudomonal activity.
5. **Oxygen in place of sulphur** (oxacephem: latamoxef/moxalactam) raises potency.
6. **Esters of the 4-carboxyl** (cefuroxime axetil, cefpodoxime proxetil) are oral prodrugs.

### Degradation

The β-lactam hydrolyses in alkali or by cephalosporinase to inactive open-ring products; the 3-acetoxymethyl compounds lose acetate first (**desacetyl**), then lactonise. The free amino side chain of cephalexin can attack its own β-lactam intramolecularly in solution, forming a diketopiperazine.

### Generations and products

| Generation | Examples | Spectrum / feature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | Cephalothin, cefazolin (parenteral); cephalexin, cefadroxil, cephradine (oral) | Mainly Gram-positive, some *E. coli*, *Klebsiella*; surgical prophylaxis |
| Second | Cefuroxime (and its axetil ester), cefaclor, cefamandole; cephamycins cefoxitin, cefotetan | Better Gram-negative; cephamycins cover anaerobes |
| Third | Cefotaxime, ceftriaxone, ceftazidime, cefoperazone, ceftizoxime; oral cefixime, cefpodoxime proxetil | Strong Gram-negative; ceftriaxone for meningitis, gonorrhoea, typhoid; ceftazidime and cefoperazone antipseudomonal |
| Fourth | Cefepime, cefpirome | Zwitterions, broad, β-lactamase stable |
| Fifth | Ceftaroline fosamil, ceftobiprole | Active against MRSA |

Cross-allergy with penicillins is possible but uncommon.

## 1.4 β-Lactamase inhibitors

**β-Lactamases** are bacterial serine (or zinc) enzymes that open the β-lactam ring — the commonest cause of resistance. Inhibitors are themselves β-lactams with weak antibacterial action. They **acylate the β-lactamase serine**, then rearrange into a form that cannot be released, so the enzyme is destroyed: they are **mechanism-based ("suicide") inhibitors**. They are always given **with** a penicillin or cephalosporin.

| Inhibitor | Structure | Partner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clavulanic acid | A **clavam**: β-lactam fused to an **oxazolidine** ring (O in place of S), with no 6-acylamino group and a 2-(2-hydroxyethylidene) side chain; from *Streptomyces clavuligerus* | Amoxicillin (co-amoxiclav), ticarcillin |
| Sulbactam | **Penicillanic acid sulphone** (sulphur oxidised to SO2, no side chain) | Ampicillin, cefoperazone |
| Tazobactam | Penicillanic acid sulphone with a **triazolylmethyl** group on C2 | Piperacillin |

Avibactam is a newer **non-β-lactam** (diazabicyclooctane) inhibitor used with ceftazidime. Related β-lactams worth one line: the **carbapenems** (imipenem, meropenem) have CH2 in place of S and a C2–C3 double bond; imipenem is broken down by renal dehydropeptidase-I, so it is given with **cilastatin**.

## 1.5 Monobactams

Monobactams are **monocyclic β-lactams**: the lactam ring stands alone. The natural leads (e.g. sulfazecin) came from soil bacteria such as *Chromobacterium violaceum*; the clinical drug **aztreonam** is fully synthetic.

Structure of aztreonam: a β-lactam whose nitrogen carries a **sulphonic acid (N–SO3H)** group, which activates the ring in place of the fused second ring; a **4-methyl** group that improves β-lactamase stability; and the **aminothiazole–oxime** side chain of the third-generation cephalosporins (here carrying a 2-carboxyprop-2-oxy group).

Features: active only against **aerobic Gram-negative bacilli** including *Pseudomonas* (it binds PBP-3 of these organisms); no Gram-positive or anaerobic cover; **very little cross-allergy** with penicillins, so it can be used in penicillin-allergic patients; given by injection.

## 1.6 Aminoglycosides

### Chemistry and general features

Aminoglycosides are **amino sugars joined by glycosidic links to an aminocyclitol** (a cyclohexane carrying amino and hydroxy groups). Two cyclitols occur:

- **Streptidine** (1,3-diguanidino-inositol) — in streptomycin.
- **2-Deoxystreptamine** — in neomycin, kanamycin, gentamicin, tobramycin and amikacin.

They are **polybasic, highly water-soluble cations**, used as **sulphates**. Consequences: not absorbed orally (oral neomycin acts only in the gut), poor entry into the CSF and cells, excretion unchanged by the kidney.

**Mechanism.** They enter Gram-negative bacteria through porins and an **oxygen-dependent** transport system (so anaerobes are naturally resistant), bind the **30S ribosomal subunit**, cause **misreading of mRNA** with faulty proteins that damage the membrane, and block initiation. Killing is **bactericidal and concentration dependent**, with a **post-antibiotic effect** — the basis of once-daily dosing.

**Toxicity:** **ototoxicity** (vestibular and cochlear, irreversible), **nephrotoxicity**, and **neuromuscular blockade** (worse with curare-like drugs or in myasthenia).

**Resistance and SAR.** Bacteria inactivate aminoglycosides with **acetyltransferases (on amino groups), phosphotransferases and adenylyltransferases (on hydroxy groups)**. SAR follows from this:

1. The amino groups, especially those on the **6' and 2' positions** of ring I, are needed for activity.
2. Removing the **3'-hydroxy** (tobramycin) or the **3',4'-hydroxyls** (dibekacin) prevents phosphorylation at that site.
3. Acylating the **1-amino group of deoxystreptamine** with **L-4-amino-2-hydroxybutyric acid** turns kanamycin A into **amikacin**, which resists most inactivating enzymes; it is the reserve aminoglycoside.

### Streptomycin

- Isolated in 1944 from ***Streptomyces griseus*** (Waksman, Schatz). First drug effective against tuberculosis.
- Structure: **streptidine** linked to **streptobiosamine**, which is **L-streptose** (a branched sugar carrying an aldehyde) joined to **N-methyl-L-glucosamine**.
- **Degradation:** mild acid hydrolysis splits it into streptidine and streptobiosamine. In **alkali the streptose part rearranges to maltol** (3-hydroxy-2-methyl-4-pyrone), which gives a violet colour with ferric ion — a classical test and assay. The aldehyde can be reduced to give **dihydrostreptomycin** (more ototoxic, no longer used).
- Uses: tuberculosis (with other drugs), plague, tularaemia, with penicillin in enterococcal endocarditis.

### Neomycin

From ***Streptomyces fradiae*** (1949). A mixture of **neomycin B and C**, each made of **neamine** (deoxystreptamine + neosamine) joined through ribose to another neosamine. Too toxic for injection; used **topically** (skin, eye, ear, often with bacitracin and polymyxin) and **orally** for bowel preparation and to reduce ammonia-forming gut bacteria in hepatic coma.

### Kanamycin

From ***Streptomyces kanamyceticus*** (1957). Mixture of kanamycins **A (major), B and C**: deoxystreptamine carrying two glucose-derived amino sugars. Used in resistant Gram-negative and, formerly, in multidrug-resistant tuberculosis; parent of amikacin. Other important products: **gentamicin** (from *Micromonospora*, hence "-micin"), **tobramycin**, **amikacin**, **netilmicin**.

## 1.7 Tetracyclines

### History and products

| Drug | Source / origin | Year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chlortetracycline | *Streptomyces aureofaciens* (Duggar) | 1948 |
| Oxytetracycline | *Streptomyces rimosus* | 1950 |
| Tetracycline | Catalytic hydrogenolysis of the 7-Cl of chlortetracycline; also by fermentation | 1953 |
| Demeclocycline | Mutant strain of *S. aureofaciens* (lacks the 6-methyl) | 1957 |
| Methacycline, doxycycline | Semi-synthetic from oxytetracycline | 1960s |
| Minocycline | Semi-synthetic | 1967 |
| Tigecycline | Semi-synthetic glycylcycline | 2005 |

### Nucleus, numbering and stereochemistry

The skeleton is a **partially hydrogenated naphthacene**: four linearly fused six-membered rings **A, B, C, D**, with ring D aromatic. Numbering runs from C1 in ring A round to C12, with the ring-fusion carbons 4a, 5a, 6a, 10a, 11a and 12a.

```text
        D        C        B        A
     ┌─────┐  ┌─────┐  ┌─────┐  ┌─────┐
  7  │arom.│6 │     │5 │     │4 │ N(CH3)2 (4α)
     │     │  │ 6-OH│  │     │  │     │ 3 C=O / enol
     │     │  │6-CH3│  │     │  │     │ 2 CONH2
  9  └─────┘  └─────┘  └─────┘  └─────┘ 1 C=O
     10-OH (phenol)  11 C=O   12-OH(enol)  12a-OH
     ← upper "variable" side: 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 can be changed
       lower "essential" side: 10, 11, 12, 1, 2, 3, 4, 12a must stay
```

- Chiral centres: **C4, C4a, C5a, C6 and C12a** (plus **C5** in oxytetracycline and doxycycline). The natural configuration is required.
- The molecule carries **three acidic/basic groups**: the C1–C3 tricarbonyl (enolised) system, the 4-dimethylamino group, and the C10–C12 phenol–diketone system. Tetracyclines are therefore **amphoteric**, with three pKa values (about 3.3, 7.7 and 9.7 for tetracycline), and are used as **hydrochlorides**.

### Chemical degradation

1. **Epimerisation at C4** (pH about 2–6): the dimethylamino group inverts to give **4-epitetracycline**, which is far less active. It is reversible.
2. **Acid dehydration:** the 6-OH and the 5a-H are lost, making ring C aromatic: **anhydrotetracycline**. Combined with epimerisation this gives **4-epianhydrotetracycline**, the product of outdated tetracycline that caused renal tubular damage (**Fanconi-type syndrome**). Hence outdated stock must be destroyed.
3. **Base cleavage:** in alkali the 6-OH attacks the 11-ketone and ring C opens to a lactone, **isotetracycline**, which is inactive.
4. **Chelation:** the 11,12 keto-enol and the 1,3 system bind **Ca2+, Mg2+, Al3+, Fe2+**. Consequences: antacids, milk and iron salts cut absorption; tetracycline is deposited in growing **teeth (yellow-brown staining)** and **bones**, so it is avoided in pregnancy and in children under about 8 years.

Doxycycline and minocycline lack the 6-OH, so they **cannot form anhydro or iso products** — they are more stable.

### SAR of tetracyclines

1. **The four rings in linear fusion, with ring D aromatic, are needed**; tricyclic analogues are inactive.
2. **Ring A is the pharmacophore:** the **2-carboxamide** must be a primary amide (substitution lowers activity, except the Mannich bases of rolitetracycline, which are water-soluble prodrugs); the **1,3-dicarbonyl (keto–enol) system** and the **4-dimethylamino group in the natural α configuration** are required; 4-epimers are weak.
3. **The C10 phenol, C11 ketone, C12 enol and 12a-OH** (the lower, chelating face) must be kept intact.
4. **Positions 5, 5a, 6, 7, 8 and 9 (upper face) can be changed** to tune properties:
   - **5-OH** (oxytetracycline) slightly lowers lipophilicity.
   - **Removal of the 6-OH** (doxycycline, minocycline) or conversion to **6-methylene** (methacycline) gives acid and base stability, better absorption and a longer half-life.
   - **Removal of the 6-methyl** (demeclocycline) slows renal clearance.
   - **7-Chloro** (chlortetracycline, demeclocycline) raises potency; **7-dimethylamino with 6-deoxy-6-demethyl** gives **minocycline**, the most lipophilic, with CNS and vestibular penetration.
   - **9-Glycylamido group** (tigecycline) overcomes efflux and ribosomal protection resistance.
5. Lipophilicity order (affects absorption and duration): minocycline > doxycycline > tetracycline > oxytetracycline.

### Mechanism, uses and adverse effects

Tetracyclines are carried into bacteria by an energy-dependent system, bind reversibly to the **30S subunit** and **block the binding of aminoacyl-tRNA to the A-site** of the ribosome, so the peptide cannot lengthen. They are **bacteriostatic** and **broad spectrum**.

Uses: rickettsial infections (typhus, Rocky Mountain spotted fever), chlamydial infections (trachoma, lymphogranuloma, non-gonococcal urethritis), *Mycoplasma* pneumonia, cholera, brucellosis (with an aminoglycoside), Lyme disease, acne, and doxycycline for malaria prophylaxis and leptospirosis; demeclocycline for inappropriate ADH secretion. Resistance is common, mostly by efflux pumps and ribosomal protection proteins.

Adverse effects: gastrointestinal upset and superinfection (candidiasis, pseudomembranous colitis), **teeth and bone deposition**, **phototoxicity** (most with demeclocycline), liver damage in pregnancy, **vestibular toxicity with minocycline**, and the degraded-drug renal syndrome.

## Remember

- Penicillin = β-lactam + thiazolidine (penam), 3S,5R,6R; cephalosporin = β-lactam + dihydrothiazine (cephem), 6R,7R.
- β-Lactams acylate the serine of transpeptidases (PBPs) by mimicking D-Ala-D-Ala.
- Acid → penicillenic/penillic acid; alkali or β-lactamase → penicilloic acid; amines → penicilloyl amides (allergy).
- α-Electron-withdrawing group = acid stable; bulky group = penicillinase resistant; polar α-group = extended spectrum.
- Cephalosporin 7-side chain controls spectrum; 3-substituent controls pharmacokinetics; 7α-methoxy = cephamycin; NMTT = bleeding + disulfiram reaction.
- Clavulanic acid (clavam), sulbactam and tazobactam (sulphones) are suicide inhibitors; aztreonam is a monobactam with N-SO3H, Gram-negative only.
- Streptomycin = streptidine + streptose + N-methylglucosamine; alkali gives maltol. Others use 2-deoxystreptamine; amikacin = kanamycin A + HABA on N1.
- Tetracyclines: lower face (1–4, 10–12, 12a) essential, upper face (5–9) modifiable; no 6-OH = stable (doxy, mino); 4-epi and anhydro products; chelation with metals.

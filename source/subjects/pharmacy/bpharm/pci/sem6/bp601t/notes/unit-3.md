# Unit 3: Antitubercular agents, urinary tract anti-infectives and antiviral agents

Three groups sit in this unit: the drugs used against **tuberculosis**, the **quinolones and other urinary tract anti-infectives**, and the **antiviral agents**. Starred drugs are isoniazid, para-aminosalicylic acid, ciprofloxacin, nitrofurantoin and acyclovir; their preparations are set from your prescribed textbook, while the chemistry below — classification, mechanism, SAR and uses — is what carries most of the marks in a written paper.

## 3.1 Tuberculosis and the basis of its treatment

Tuberculosis is caused by ***Mycobacterium tuberculosis***, a slow-growing, aerobic, acid-fast bacillus whose cell wall is rich in **mycolic acids** (long branched fatty acids) and arabinogalactan. The waxy wall keeps most ordinary antibiotics out, makes the organism acid-fast, and explains why treatment is long.

Four facts shape the chemotherapy:

1. The bacillus **divides slowly** (about once in 18–24 h) and some bacilli are dormant, so treatment lasts months.
2. **Resistant mutants arise spontaneously**, so drugs are always given in **combinations** — never alone.
3. Bacilli live **inside macrophages and in acidic caseous material**, so a drug must penetrate cells and work at low pH (pyrazinamide does this best).
4. Treatment is given as **directly observed short-course therapy** in an intensive phase followed by a continuation phase, as in India's national tuberculosis programme.

**Classification of antitubercular drugs**

| Group | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| First-line (synthetic) | **Isoniazid***, **pyrazinamide**, **ethambutol**, **para-aminosalicylic acid*** (now a second-line reserve) |
| First-line (antibiotics) | **Rifampicin**, rifabutin, **streptomycin** |
| Second-line synthetic | **Ethionamide**, cycloserine, thiacetazone, fluoroquinolones (levofloxacin, moxifloxacin) |
| Second-line antibiotics | **Capreomycin sulphate**, kanamycin, amikacin |
| Newer agents | Bedaquiline, delamanid (for multidrug-resistant disease) |

## 3.2 Synthetic antitubercular agents

### Isoniazid (isonicotinic acid hydrazide, INH)*

```text
        ┌───┐
        │ N │  pyridine ring
        └─┬─┘
          4-position
          C(=O)–NH–NH2    acid hydrazide
```

- **Chemistry:** the hydrazide of isonicotinic acid (pyridine-4-carboxylic acid). A white crystalline powder, freely soluble in water, and a **reducing agent** — it reduces ammoniacal silver nitrate and Fehling's solution, and is assayed by **bromatometric titration** (see the practical guide).
- **Mechanism:** isoniazid is a **prodrug**. The mycobacterial catalase-peroxidase enzyme **KatG** oxidises it to an isonicotinoyl radical, which couples with NAD to form an adduct that blocks **InhA (enoyl-ACP reductase)**, the enzyme of **mycolic acid synthesis**. Without mycolic acid the cell wall cannot be built, so the drug is **bactericidal to dividing bacilli** and selective for mycobacteria. Resistance usually follows a mutation in *katG*.
- **SAR:** activity needs the **hydrazide group on the 4-position of pyridine**; the 2- and 3-isomers are much weaker. Substituting the terminal nitrogen gives derivatives that must be converted back to isoniazid in the body (for example iproniazid, which was dropped because it inhibits monoamine oxidase).
- **Metabolism:** acetylated by N-acetyltransferase 2. People are **fast or slow acetylators** — a classical example of pharmacogenetic variation; slow acetylators are more liable to peripheral neuritis.
- **Adverse effects:** **peripheral neuropathy** (it is a pyridoxine antagonist, so pyridoxine is given with it), hepatitis, and rarely a lupus-like syndrome.

### Ethionamide

The 2-ethyl analogue of **thioisonicotinamide** — pyridine-4-carbothioamide with an ethyl group at position 2. Like isoniazid it is activated (by the mycobacterial enzyme EthA) and blocks InhA, so there is partial cross-resistance. It is a second-line drug; gastric irritation, a metallic taste, hepatitis and hypothyroidism limit its use.

### Pyrazinamide

**Pyrazine-2-carboxamide**, the amide analogue of nicotinamide with a second ring nitrogen. It is a **prodrug** converted by the mycobacterial enzyme **pyrazinamidase** to **pyrazinoic acid**, which acts only in an **acidic medium** — exactly the environment inside macrophages and caseous lesions. This is why it shortens treatment so effectively and is used in the first two months. Adverse effects: hepatotoxicity, and **hyperuricaemia** (pyrazinoic acid blocks urate excretion), which may bring on gout.

### Ethambutol

**(S,S)-(+)-2,2'-(ethylenediimino)di-1-butanol**, a simple symmetrical diamino-diol.

SAR points worth remembering: the molecule must be the **dextrorotatory (S,S) isomer** — it is many times more active than the meso and (R,R) forms; the **two basic nitrogens separated by an ethylene bridge** and the **two primary alcohol groups** are both needed; lengthening the chain or replacing the hydroxyls lowers activity.

Mechanism: inhibits **arabinosyl transferase**, so **arabinogalactan** of the cell wall cannot be assembled; bacteriostatic. Its characteristic toxicity is **optic neuritis with loss of red–green colour vision**, which is reversible if the drug is stopped early; patients are warned to report any visual change.

### Para-aminosalicylic acid (PAS)*

**4-Aminosalicylic acid** = 4-amino-2-hydroxybenzoic acid. It is a **structural analogue of p-aminobenzoic acid (PABA)** and acts, like the sulphonamides, by competing with PABA for **dihydropteroate synthase**, blocking folate synthesis in the bacillus. It is bacteriostatic and weak, so it was used to protect isoniazid and streptomycin against resistance; today it is a reserve drug for multidrug-resistant tuberculosis, usually as the sodium salt or as granules.

**Stability point for the exam:** PAS **decarboxylates on warming or in solution** to m-aminophenol, which darkens on oxidation; a discoloured preparation must not be used. Large doses cause marked gastric irritation; it is a starred drug, so also revise its preparation from your textbook.

## 3.3 Antitubercular antibiotics

| Drug | Chemistry | Mechanism and points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rifampicin** | Semi-synthetic **rifamycin**: a macrocyclic "ansa" ring bridging an aromatic naphthoquinone core; a **methylpiperazinyl-iminomethyl** group is added to rifamycin SV | Inhibits **DNA-dependent RNA polymerase** (β subunit, *rpoB*); bactericidal, kills intracellular and dormant bacilli; a strong **inducer of CYP enzymes** (lowers the effect of oral contraceptives, warfarin, antiretrovirals); stains urine, tears and sweat **orange-red**; can cause hepatitis and a flu-like syndrome with intermittent use |
| **Rifabutin** | A spiro-piperidyl rifamycin | Same mechanism, a weaker enzyme inducer, so preferred in patients on antiretroviral drugs; used in *M. avium* complex |
| **Cycloserine** | **D-4-amino-3-isoxazolidinone**, a rigid cyclic analogue of **D-alanine** | Blocks **alanine racemase and D-Ala-D-Ala ligase**, so the peptidoglycan pentapeptide cannot be made; second-line; causes psychosis, depression and convulsions, so pyridoxine is given with it |
| **Streptomycin** | Aminoglycoside (Unit 1) | 30S inhibitor, acts only on extracellular, actively dividing bacilli; given by injection; ototoxic and nephrotoxic |
| **Capreomycin sulphate** | **Cyclic polypeptide** antibiotic from *Streptomyces capreolus* (capreomycins IA and IB) | Inhibits protein synthesis; injectable second-line drug for resistant tuberculosis; ototoxic, nephrotoxic, causes electrolyte loss |

## 3.4 Urinary tract anti-infective agents: the quinolones

### Development

**Nalidixic acid** (1962) was isolated as a by-product of chloroquine manufacture. It is a **1,8-naphthyridine**, active only against Gram-negative organisms and reaching useful concentrations only in urine. Adding a **fluorine at C6** and a **piperazine at C7** produced the **fluoroquinolones** — norfloxacin, then ciprofloxacin and the rest — with far greater potency and a systemic spectrum.

### Nucleus and numbering

```text
        O     O
        ║     ║
   5 ┌──C4────C3–OH   ← 3-COOH and 4-oxo: ESSENTIAL pair
 6 F │            │
   7 │  quinolone │
  R7─┤            │
   8 └────N1──────┘
          │
          R1 (cyclopropyl, ethyl, fluorophenyl)
```

The core is **4-oxo-1,4-dihydroquinoline-3-carboxylic acid**. In nalidixic acid position 8 is a nitrogen (naphthyridine); in ofloxacin, N1 and C8 are bridged by an oxazine ring, which creates a chiral centre — **levofloxacin is the active (S)-isomer** of ofloxacin.

### SAR of quinolones (an almost certain question)

1. **The 3-carboxylic acid and the 4-oxo group are essential** — they chelate the magnesium ion that anchors the drug to the DNA–enzyme complex. Esterify or remove either and activity is lost.
2. **N1 substituent** controls potency and spectrum: **cyclopropyl** (ciprofloxacin, moxifloxacin) is the best, ethyl (norfloxacin, enoxacin) is weaker, a **2,4-difluorophenyl** group (sparfloxacin) gives a long half-life.
3. **Fluorine at C6** increases potency against both the enzyme (about 10-fold) and cell penetration (about 100-fold) — the single change that created the class.
4. **C7 substituent** controls spectrum and pharmacokinetics: a **piperazine** improves Gram-negative activity (norfloxacin, ciprofloxacin, ofloxacin, lomefloxacin), a **methylpiperazine** or **pyrrolidine** (gatifloxacin, moxifloxacin) adds Gram-positive and anaerobic cover and lengthens action.
5. **C8 substituent**: a **methoxy group** (gatifloxacin, moxifloxacin) improves anaerobic activity and reduces resistance; a **halogen at C8** (lomefloxacin, sparfloxacin) raises **phototoxicity**.
6. **C5 amino group** (sparfloxacin) increases potency.
7. A **bulky group at C2 or loss of the free acid abolishes activity**; the ring positions 2 and 3 must stay flat.
8. The free acid and the basic amine on the C7 ring make most fluoroquinolones **zwitterionic**; they **chelate metal ions**, so antacids, iron, calcium and milk reduce absorption — a classical counselling point.

### Mechanism, products and adverse effects

Quinolones inhibit **DNA gyrase (topoisomerase II)** in Gram-negative bacteria and **topoisomerase IV** in Gram-positive bacteria. The enzyme cuts DNA to relieve supercoiling; the drug traps the enzyme–DNA complex, so the broken strands are never resealed and the cell dies. They are **bactericidal**.

| Drug | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Nalidixic acid** | Urinary antiseptic only; Gram-negative; resistance develops fast |
| **Norfloxacin** | Urinary and gut infections; poor tissue levels |
| **Enoxacin** | Similar; marked theophylline interaction |
| **Ciprofloxacin*** | The classical broad fluoroquinolone: Gram-negative including *Pseudomonas*, typhoid, bone and soft-tissue infection, anthrax prophylaxis |
| **Ofloxacin / levofloxacin** | Better oral availability; levofloxacin is the active (S)-enantiomer; good against pneumococci and used in tuberculosis regimens |
| **Lomefloxacin, sparfloxacin** | Long acting; phototoxic (C8 halogen); sparfloxacin can prolong the QT interval |
| **Gatifloxacin, moxifloxacin** | "Respiratory" quinolones; good against pneumococci and anaerobes; moxifloxacin is used in tuberculosis; gatifloxacin was withdrawn from systemic use in India because of dysglycaemia, and remains in eye drops |

Adverse effects of the class: gastrointestinal upset, headache and dizziness, **tendinitis and tendon rupture** (worse in the elderly and with corticosteroids), **cartilage damage in young animals**, so they are avoided in growing children and pregnancy unless there is no alternative; **QT prolongation**; photosensitivity; and raised theophylline levels.

## 3.5 Other urinary tract anti-infectives

### Nitrofurantoin*

A **5-nitrofuran** joined through a hydrazone link to **hydantoin** (1-[(5-nitrofurfurylidene)amino]hydantoin). Bacterial **nitroreductases** reduce the nitro group to reactive intermediates that damage bacterial DNA and ribosomal proteins; mammalian cells reduce it far more slowly, which gives selectivity. Because it is excreted and concentrated in urine and acts best at acidic pH, it works **only in the lower urinary tract** — it is never used for systemic infection or pyelonephritis. It stains urine **brown**. Adverse effects: nausea, **acute and chronic pulmonary reactions**, peripheral neuropathy, haemolysis in G6PD deficiency. Related compound: **furazolidone**, also a nitrofuran, formerly used in bacterial diarrhoea and giardiasis.

### Methenamine (hexamine)

**Hexamethylenetetramine**, the cage-like condensation product of **formaldehyde and ammonia**. It is a classic **prodrug of formaldehyde**: it is inert at blood pH but, in acidic urine (pH below about 5.5), it **hydrolyses back to formaldehyde**, which is a non-specific antibacterial. It is therefore given with an acidifier such as mandelic acid or hippuric acid (as methenamine mandelate or hippurate), and must not be combined with urinary alkalisers or sulphonamides. It is used for long-term prophylaxis of recurrent urinary infection, not for acute infection. Its preparation is one of the practical exercises in BP607P.

## 3.6 Antiviral agents

### Why antiviral chemotherapy is difficult

Viruses use the host cell's own machinery, so there are few targets unique to the virus. Useful drugs act at one of these steps: **attachment and entry**, **uncoating**, **nucleic acid synthesis** (the biggest group), **protein processing (proteases)**, and **release (neuraminidase)**.

### Classification with important products

| Group | Drugs | Mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Uncoating / M2 ion channel inhibitors | **Amantadine hydrochloride**, **rimantadine hydrochloride** | Block the M2 channel of influenza A, preventing uncoating; amantadine is also antiparkinsonian; most circulating strains are now resistant |
| Anti-herpes nucleoside analogues | **Idoxuridine**, trifluridine, **acyclovir***, **ganciclovir**, valacyclovir, famciclovir | Phosphorylated to the triphosphate, then inhibit viral DNA polymerase and terminate the chain |
| Nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs) | **Zidovudine**, **didanosine**, **zalcitabine**, **lamivudine**, stavudine, abacavir | Lack the 3'-OH, so DNA synthesis stops after they are added |
| Non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NNRTIs) | **Delavirdine**, nevirapine, efavirenz, **loviride** | Bind an allosteric pocket on HIV-1 reverse transcriptase |
| Protease inhibitors | **Saquinavir**, **indinavir**, **ritonavir**, lopinavir, atazanavir | Peptidomimetics that block HIV protease, so immature, non-infectious particles are formed |
| Broad-spectrum / other | **Ribavirin**, interferon alfa, oseltamivir and zanamivir (neuraminidase inhibitors) | Several mechanisms; ribavirin is a guanosine analogue used in hepatitis C and respiratory syncytial virus |

### Acyclovir* — the model of selective toxicity

**9-[(2-Hydroxyethoxy)methyl]guanine**: guanine with an **acyclic side chain** that mimics only part of the sugar — it has the 5'-hydroxyl needed for phosphorylation but **no 3'-OH**.

```text
   guanine ─ N9 – CH2 – O – CH2CH2 – OH
                     (acyclic "sugar", no 3'-OH)
```

Three steps give its remarkable selectivity:

1. **Viral thymidine kinase** (herpes simplex and varicella zoster) phosphorylates acyclovir to the monophosphate. Human kinases do this very poorly, so the drug is activated **only in infected cells**.
2. **Host kinases** add two more phosphates to give acyclovir triphosphate.
3. The triphosphate is a far better substrate for the **viral DNA polymerase** than for the human enzyme; it is incorporated and, having no 3'-OH, acts as a **chain terminator** and also inactivates the polymerase.

Uses: herpes simplex (genital, labial, encephalitis), varicella and herpes zoster; it is given orally, intravenously and topically. Its oral availability is poor (about 15–20%), which is why **valacyclovir**, its L-valyl ester prodrug, was developed. **Ganciclovir** has an extra hydroxymethyl group on the side chain and is used in cytomegalovirus disease; it is far more myelosuppressive. **Idoxuridine** and **trifluridine** are 5-substituted deoxyuridines, too toxic for systemic use and given only in eye infections.

### Zidovudine and the other NRTIs

**Zidovudine (AZT)** is **3'-azido-3'-deoxythymidine**: the 3'-OH of thymidine is replaced by an azide group. It is phosphorylated by host kinases, and the triphosphate competes with thymidine triphosphate at **HIV reverse transcriptase**; once incorporated it stops the chain. Main toxicity is **bone marrow suppression**. **Didanosine** (a dideoxyinosine) and **zalcitabine** (dideoxycytidine) cause **peripheral neuropathy and pancreatitis**; **lamivudine**, an L-cytosine analogue with a sulphur in the sugar ring, is the best tolerated and is used in hepatitis B as well. All are given in **combinations** (highly active antiretroviral therapy) because HIV mutates rapidly.

### Protease inhibitors

These are **peptidomimetics**: they imitate the phenylalanine–proline sequence that HIV protease cleaves, but the scissile bond is replaced by a **hydroxyethylene** or similar group that cannot be cut, so the enzyme is blocked. **Ritonavir** is now used mainly in small doses as a **pharmacokinetic booster**, because it strongly inhibits CYP3A4 and so raises the levels of the partner protease inhibitor. Common class effects: gastrointestinal upset, **lipodystrophy**, hyperlipidaemia and insulin resistance; indinavir can form crystals in the urine.

### Amantadine and ribavirin

**Amantadine** is **1-adamantanamine** — a rigid, symmetrical, cage-shaped tricyclic amine. Besides influenza A it is used in Parkinson's disease, where it releases dopamine. **Rimantadine** is its α-methyl derivative, better tolerated, with fewer central effects. **Ribavirin** is a **triazole carboxamide nucleoside** that resembles guanosine; it has several actions (inhibition of inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase, interference with mRNA capping, error catastrophe in the viral genome) and is used in chronic hepatitis C with interferon and by inhalation in severe respiratory syncytial virus infection; it causes **haemolytic anaemia** and is teratogenic.

## Remember

- Isoniazid is a prodrug activated by KatG; it blocks mycolic acid synthesis; neuropathy is prevented by pyridoxine; acetylator status matters.
- Pyrazinamide works only in acid; ethambutol must be the (S,S) isomer and causes optic neuritis; PAS is a PABA analogue that decarboxylates on storage.
- Rifampicin inhibits DNA-dependent RNA polymerase, induces CYP enzymes and turns secretions orange-red; cycloserine is a D-alanine analogue; capreomycin is a cyclic polypeptide.
- Quinolone SAR: 3-COOH and 4-oxo essential, 6-F for potency, N1-cyclopropyl best, C7-piperazine for spectrum, C8-OMe for anaerobes, C8-halogen for phototoxicity.
- Quinolones inhibit DNA gyrase (Gram-negative) and topoisomerase IV (Gram-positive); tendon rupture and metal chelation are the counselling points.
- Nitrofurantoin acts only in urine and needs bacterial nitroreductase; methenamine is a prodrug that releases formaldehyde in acidic urine.
- Acyclovir needs viral thymidine kinase for the first phosphate and has no 3'-OH, so it terminates the chain — selectivity in two steps.
- Zidovudine is 3'-azidothymidine; protease inhibitors are peptidomimetics with an uncleavable bond; amantadine blocks the influenza A M2 channel.

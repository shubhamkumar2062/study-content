# Preparation of chlorobutanol

**Aim:** To prepare chlorobutanol (1,1,1-trichloro-2-methylpropan-2-ol) from acetone and chloroform, and to report the yield and melting point.

> Carried out only in the college laboratory under the teacher's supervision, with the quantities given in your own lab manual. Chloroform is volatile, narcotic and harmful; potassium hydroxide is corrosive; the reaction is exothermic. Work in a fume cupboard with goggles, a lab coat and gloves, away from any flame.

## Principle

Chlorobutanol is a **preservative and mild antiseptic** used in injections, eye drops and some topical preparations. It is made by the base-catalysed addition of chloroform to acetone, a classical example of a **nucleophilic addition to a carbonyl group**.

```text
CHCl3 + KOH → CCl3−  +  H2O  +  K+            (the trichloromethyl carbanion)

         CH3                     CH3
          │                       │
CCl3− +   C=O      →     Cl3C ─ C ─ O−  --(H+)-->  Cl3C–C(CH3)2–OH
          │                       │
         CH3                     CH3
      acetone                 CHLOROBUTANOL
```

The strong base removes a proton from chloroform, whose three chlorine atoms stabilise the resulting **trichloromethyl carbanion**. This carbanion adds to the carbonyl carbon of acetone, and the alkoxide formed is protonated on working up, giving the tertiary alcohol. The product crystallises as the **hemihydrate**, which is the official form in the pharmacopoeia.

## Requirements

Acetone (dry), chloroform, powdered potassium hydroxide, crushed ice, water, round-bottom flask with a reflux condenser and a guard tube, ice bath, Buchner funnel and flask, desiccator, melting point apparatus.

## Procedure (outline, as directed by the teacher)

1. Place dry acetone and chloroform in the flask and cool the mixture well in an ice bath.
2. Add powdered potassium hydroxide in small portions with constant shaking or stirring, keeping the mixture cold; the reaction is vigorous and exothermic.
3. When the addition is complete, allow the mixture to stand as directed, with occasional shaking, so that the reaction goes to completion.
4. Pour the mixture into a large volume of cold water with stirring; chlorobutanol separates as a solid.
5. Filter the crude product at the pump and wash it with cold water to remove potassium salts.
6. Recrystallise from a small volume of warm water, or from dilute alcohol, allowing the solution to cool slowly.
7. Filter, dry the crystals in a desiccator at room temperature, weigh and determine the melting point.

## Observations and calculations

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Volume (and weight) of acetone taken | ______ |
| Theoretical yield | ______ g |
| Weight of dry product | ______ g |
| Percentage yield | ______ % |
| Melting point observed | ______ °C |
| Reported melting point | about 95 to 99 °C (hemihydrate) |
| Appearance | Colourless crystals with a camphor-like odour |

```text
Theoretical yield (g) = weight of acetone (g) × (177.5 ÷ 58.1)
                        (molecular weight of anhydrous chlorobutanol ÷ that of acetone)
Percentage yield = (practical yield ÷ theoretical yield) × 100
```

**Worked example.** From 5.8 g of acetone the theoretical yield is 5.8 × 177.5 ÷ 58.1 = 17.7 g. A dry product of 9.5 g gives (9.5 ÷ 17.7) × 100 = **53.7 %**.

**Identification tests**

- The crystals have a characteristic **camphor-like odour** and sublime readily on warming.
- Heating with alcoholic potassium hydroxide, acidifying with nitric acid and adding silver nitrate gives a **white precipitate of silver chloride**, showing organically bound chlorine.
- The melting point agrees with the reported value for the hemihydrate.

## Precautions

1. Keep the mixture cold while the potassium hydroxide is added; the reaction is strongly exothermic and may become violent if the alkali is added too fast.
2. Use dry acetone and dry alkali; water lowers the yield.
3. Work in a fume cupboard, away from flames, because chloroform vapour is harmful and volatile.
4. Dry the product at room temperature only; chlorobutanol is volatile and sublimes, so oven drying causes losses and changes the water content.
5. Store the product in a tightly closed container, since it sublimes slowly even at room temperature.
6. Use the minimum volume of warm solvent for recrystallisation and allow slow cooling.

## Viva questions

1. **What type of reaction is involved?** Base-catalysed nucleophilic addition of the trichloromethyl carbanion to the carbonyl group of acetone.
2. **Why is the trichloromethyl carbanion stable enough to form?** The three electronegative chlorine atoms withdraw electron density and disperse the negative charge.
3. **Why is potassium hydroxide preferred to sodium hydroxide here?** It is more soluble in the organic medium and gives a smoother reaction, although either strong base can generate the carbanion.
4. **In what form is chlorobutanol official?** As the hemihydrate, which is the crystalline form obtained on recrystallisation from water.
5. **Give its pharmaceutical uses.** As an antimicrobial preservative in injections and eye drops, and as a mild local anaesthetic and antiseptic in some topical and dental preparations.
6. **Why must it not be dried at high temperature?** It is volatile and sublimes, so the product is lost and the water of crystallisation is disturbed.
7. **How would you test for the chlorine in the molecule?** Decompose the compound with alcoholic alkali, acidify with nitric acid and test with silver nitrate for a white precipitate.

# Preparation of 2,4,5-triphenylimidazole (lophine)

**Aim:** To prepare 2,4,5-triphenylimidazole from benzil, benzaldehyde and ammonium acetate, and to report the yield and melting point.

> Carried out only in the college laboratory under the teacher's supervision, with the quantities given in your own lab manual. Glacial acetic acid is corrosive and the mixture is heated; use a fume cupboard, goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

This is the **Radziszewski synthesis** of an imidazole ring: a 1,2-diketone, an aldehyde and two molecules of ammonia (supplied by ammonium acetate) condense in one pot.

```text
Benzil (PhCO–COPh) + benzaldehyde (PhCHO) + 2 NH3
        --(glacial acetic acid, reflux)-->  2,4,5-triphenylimidazole + 3 H2O
```

Mechanism in outline: ammonia adds to the aldehyde to give an **imine**; a second molecule of ammonia condenses with the diketone; cyclisation and loss of water then aromatise the five-membered ring. The product is historically called **lophine** and is **chemiluminescent** — in alkaline solution with an oxidising agent it emits a pale yellow light, which makes the experiment memorable.

The imidazole ring itself is important in medicinal chemistry: it occurs in histamine and cimetidine, in metronidazole and in the azole antifungals, so this preparation illustrates a nucleus met throughout the course.

```text
        Ph
         │
    N ───C═N          2-position carries the phenyl group from benzaldehyde
    │       │         4- and 5-positions carry the phenyl groups from benzil
   Ph-C ═ C-Ph
```

## Requirements

Benzil, benzaldehyde (freshly distilled or free from benzoic acid), ammonium acetate, glacial acetic acid, round-bottom flask with reflux condenser, heating mantle or water bath, beaker, crushed ice, dilute ammonia solution, ethanol, Buchner funnel and flask, melting point apparatus.

## Procedure (outline, as directed by the teacher)

1. Place benzil, benzaldehyde and a large excess of ammonium acetate in the flask with glacial acetic acid as the solvent.
2. Fit the reflux condenser and heat the mixture on a water bath or mantle for the period stated in the manual, until the reaction is complete.
3. Cool the mixture and pour it into cold water, or on to crushed ice, with stirring; the product separates as a solid.
4. Make the mixture just alkaline with dilute ammonia solution to neutralise the acetic acid, and stir well.
5. Filter the crude product at the pump and wash it with cold water until free from acid.
6. Recrystallise from hot ethanol, allowing slow cooling.
7. Filter, dry, weigh and determine the melting point.

## Observations and calculations

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Weight of benzil taken | ______ g |

| Weight of dry product | ______ g |
| Percentage yield | ______ % |
| Melting point observed | ______ °C |
| Reported melting point | about 275 °C |
| Appearance | Colourless to pale yellow crystals |

```text
Theoretical yield (g) = weight of benzil (g) × (296.4 ÷ 210.2)
                        (molecular weight of product ÷ that of benzil)
Percentage yield = (practical yield ÷ theoretical yield) × 100
```

**Worked example.** From 2.1 g of benzil, theoretical yield = 2.1 × 296.4 ÷ 210.2 = 2.96 g. A dry product of 2.1 g gives (2.1 ÷ 2.96) × 100 = **70.9 %**.

**Identification tests**

- The melting point agrees with the reported value and a mixed melting point is not depressed.
- The compound dissolves in dilute mineral acid (the basic ring nitrogen) and is reprecipitated by alkali.
- A classical confirmatory observation is the faint **chemiluminescence** seen when an alkaline alcoholic solution is treated with an oxidising agent, as demonstrated by the teacher.

## Precautions

1. Use a large excess of ammonium acetate, since it is the source of both nitrogen atoms and some ammonia is lost on heating.
2. Benzaldehyde that has oxidised to benzoic acid gives a poor yield, so use a fresh or freshly distilled sample.
3. Reflux gently and steadily; vigorous boiling drives off ammonia.
4. Pour the hot mixture into water slowly and with stirring to obtain a filterable solid rather than a sticky mass.
5. Wash the product free from acetic acid before recrystallisation.
6. Keep ethanol away from naked flames during recrystallisation.

## Viva questions

1. **Name the reaction.** The Radziszewski imidazole synthesis, from a 1,2-diketone, an aldehyde and ammonia.
2. **What is the role of ammonium acetate?** It supplies ammonia in situ, which provides both nitrogen atoms of the ring, and acetate buffers the medium.
3. **Which carbon of the ring comes from benzaldehyde?** The 2-position, between the two nitrogen atoms.
4. **Why is glacial acetic acid used as the solvent?** It dissolves all the reactants, provides mildly acidic conditions that catalyse the condensation and boils at a convenient temperature.
5. **Give the common name of the product and the property it is known for.** Lophine, which is chemiluminescent in alkaline solution with an oxidising agent.
6. **Name three drugs that contain an imidazole ring.** Metronidazole, cimetidine and clotrimazole or miconazole.
7. **Why does the product dissolve in dilute acid?** The pyridine-type nitrogen of the imidazole ring is basic and forms a salt.

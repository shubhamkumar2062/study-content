# Preparation of hexamine (methenamine)

**Aim:** To prepare hexamine from formaldehyde solution and ammonia, and to report the yield, melting or sublimation behaviour and identity of the product.

> Carried out only in the college laboratory under the teacher's supervision, with the quantities given in your own lab manual. Formaldehyde solution and strong ammonia are both irritant and have pungent vapours; work in a fume cupboard with goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

Hexamine, also called methenamine or hexamethylenetetramine, is formed when **formaldehyde and ammonia condense in the ratio of six to four** with loss of water. The product has a rigid, symmetrical **cage (adamantane-like) structure** with four nitrogen atoms at the corners of a tetrahedron joined by six methylene bridges.

```text
6 HCHO + 4 NH3 → (CH2)6N4 + 6 H2O
```

Its pharmaceutical interest lies in its behaviour as a **prodrug of formaldehyde**. Hexamine is stable at the pH of blood, but in **acidic urine (below about pH 5.5)** the reaction reverses and formaldehyde is liberated, which acts as a non-specific antibacterial in the urinary tract. For this reason it is given as the mandelate or hippurate salt, which also acidify the urine, and it is used for long-term prophylaxis of recurrent urinary infection rather than for an acute attack.

## Requirements

Formaldehyde solution (about 37 to 40 per cent), strong ammonia solution, porcelain dish or beaker, water bath, glass rod, ethanol, Buchner funnel and flask, desiccator, melting point apparatus, litmus paper.

## Procedure (outline, as directed by the teacher)

1. Measure the formaldehyde solution into a dish or beaker and cool it in an ice bath.
2. Add strong ammonia solution slowly with constant stirring until the mixture is **just alkaline to litmus**; the reaction is exothermic, so keep it cool.
3. Allow the mixture to stand for the time directed so that the condensation is complete.
4. Evaporate the solution to dryness on a water bath, taking care not to overheat, when a white crystalline residue of hexamine is obtained.
5. Dissolve the residue in the minimum quantity of hot ethanol, filter hot if necessary, and allow it to cool slowly for crystallisation.
6. Filter the crystals at the pump, wash with a little cold ethanol, dry in a desiccator, weigh and record the yield.
7. Examine the behaviour of the crystals on heating: hexamine does not melt sharply but **sublimes and decomposes at about 260 °C**.

```text
Theoretical yield (g) = weight of formaldehyde (g) × (140.2 ÷ 180.1)
   because 6 × 30.03 g of formaldehyde (180.1 g) gives 140.2 g of hexamine
Percentage yield = (practical yield ÷ theoretical yield) × 100
```

**Worked example.** 25 ml of 37 per cent w/v formaldehyde solution contains 9.25 g of formaldehyde. Theoretical yield = 9.25 × 140.2 ÷ 180.1 = 7.20 g. A dry product of 5.4 g gives (5.4 ÷ 7.20) × 100 = **75.0 %**.

**Identification tests**

- An aqueous solution is neutral or slightly alkaline to litmus and has no odour.
- **Hydrolysis test:** warming the solution with dilute sulphuric acid liberates formaldehyde, detected by its pungent odour, by the violet colour with chromotropic acid, or by Schiff's reagent, while ammonia can be detected in the alkaline layer.
- **Silver mirror test:** the formaldehyde released from the acid-hydrolysed solution, when made alkaline, reduces Tollens' reagent to metallic silver.

## Precautions

1. Add the ammonia slowly and keep the mixture cool, since the condensation is exothermic.
2. Do not add a large excess of ammonia; stop as soon as the mixture is just alkaline.
3. Evaporate gently on a water bath; strong heating decomposes hexamine and darkens the product.
4. Carry out all operations in a fume cupboard, as formaldehyde and ammonia vapours are irritant.
5. Dry the product in a desiccator rather than an oven, because it sublimes on heating.
6. Store in a well-closed container, protected from acids and moisture.

## Viva questions

1. **Give the chemical and official names of the product.** Hexamethylenetetramine; the official names are hexamine and methenamine.
2. **In what ratio do formaldehyde and ammonia react?** Six molecules of formaldehyde with four of ammonia, losing six molecules of water.
3. **How does hexamine act as a urinary antiseptic?** It is a prodrug; in acidic urine it hydrolyses back to formaldehyde, which is a non-specific antibacterial.
4. **Why is it given as the mandelate or hippurate?** These acids keep the urine acidic, which is necessary for the release of formaldehyde, and they contribute a mild antibacterial effect of their own.
5. **Why must it not be given with urinary alkalisers or sulphonamides?** Alkaline urine prevents the liberation of formaldehyde, and formaldehyde can form an insoluble precipitate with some sulphonamides.
6. **Why is hexamine useless in an acute severe urinary infection?** The amount of formaldehyde released is small and acts slowly, so it is suitable only for long-term prophylaxis.
7. **How would you confirm the presence of formaldehyde after hydrolysis?** By the violet colour with chromotropic acid in sulphuric acid, by the restoration of colour to Schiff's reagent, or by the silver mirror test with Tollens' reagent.

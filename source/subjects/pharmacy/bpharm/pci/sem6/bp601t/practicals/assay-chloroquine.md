# Assay of chloroquine phosphate by non-aqueous titration

**Aim:** To determine the percentage purity of a given sample of chloroquine phosphate by non-aqueous titration with perchloric acid.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves; perchloric acid and acetic anhydride are corrosive.

## Principle

Chloroquine is a 4-aminoquinoline with a basic quinoline nitrogen and a basic tertiary amine in the side chain, so it is a **weak base**. Weak bases cannot be titrated sharply in water, because water is itself basic enough to level the reaction. In **glacial acetic acid**, which is a weakly acidic (protogenic) solvent, the basic strength of the drug is greatly enhanced and a sharp end point is obtained with **perchloric acid**, the strongest acid in that medium.

```text
Drug (base) + HClO4 → Drug·H+ ClO4−        (in glacial acetic acid)
```

Chloroquine phosphate is the bis(dihydrogen phosphate). The phosphate ion is itself too weak a base to interfere, so each molecule of the salt consumes **two molecules of perchloric acid** at its two basic nitrogen atoms. Crystal violet is used as the indicator; the colour changes from violet through blue to **blue-green** at the end point.

**Equivalent factor:** 1 ml of 0.1 M perchloric acid ≡ **0.02580 g of chloroquine phosphate** (molecular weight 515.86, two basic centres, so the equivalent weight is 257.93).

## Requirements

Chloroquine phosphate sample, glacial acetic acid, 0.1 M perchloric acid in glacial acetic acid (standardised with potassium hydrogen phthalate), crystal violet indicator (0.5 % w/v in glacial acetic acid), burette, conical flask, analytical balance, dry glassware.

## Procedure

1. Dry all glassware; even a trace of water blunts the end point.
2. Weigh accurately about 0.2 g of the chloroquine phosphate sample into a dry conical flask.
3. Dissolve it in about 40 ml of glacial acetic acid, warming gently if necessary, and cool to room temperature.
4. Add 2 to 3 drops of crystal violet indicator; the solution is violet.
5. Titrate with 0.1 M perchloric acid from a dry burette until the colour changes from violet through blue to **blue-green**, which persists on swirling.
6. Repeat until two concordant readings are obtained.
7. Carry out a blank titration on 40 ml of glacial acetic acid with the indicator and subtract the blank.

```text
Chloroquine phosphate (g) = corrected volume (ml) × 0.02580 × molarity factor
% purity = (amount found × 100) / weight of sample taken
```

**Worked example.** Sample weight 0.2000 g; corrected titre 7.75 ml of 0.1 M perchloric acid.

- Amount found = 7.75 × 0.02580 = 0.1999 g
- Percentage purity = (0.1999 ÷ 0.2000) × 100 = **100.0 % w/w**

## Precautions

1. All glassware and the burette must be dry; water is a competing base and flattens the end point.
2. Keep the titration flask stoppered or covered as far as possible, because glacial acetic acid is hygroscopic and volatile.
3. Perchloric acid in acetic acid expands markedly with temperature, so the solution should be used near the temperature at which it was standardised, or a temperature correction applied.
4. Never allow perchloric acid to come into contact with organic matter in the dry state; wipe spills at once with plenty of water.
5. Add the indicator in small amount; crystal violet itself is a weak base and consumes titrant, which the blank corrects for.
6. Warm gently only if needed, and cool before titrating.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is water unsuitable as the solvent here?** Water is sufficiently basic to level weak bases, so the pH change at the end point is too small to see.
2. **What is the role of glacial acetic acid?** It is a protogenic solvent that increases the apparent basicity of the drug by accepting protons from perchloric acid to form the acetonium ion.
3. **Why does chloroquine phosphate consume two moles of perchloric acid?** It has two basic nitrogen atoms, the quinoline nitrogen and the side-chain tertiary amine; the phosphate is too weak a base to react.
4. **Name the indicators used in non-aqueous titration of bases.** Crystal violet, methyl rose, oracet blue B and quinaldine red; potentiometric end-point detection is also used.
5. **How is 0.1 M perchloric acid standardised?** Against potassium hydrogen phthalate dissolved in glacial acetic acid, using crystal violet.
6. **Why is acetic anhydride sometimes added to the perchloric acid solution?** To remove the water introduced with commercial perchloric acid, since the anhydride reacts with water to form acetic acid.
7. **Which other drugs are assayed by this method?** Many basic drugs, including chlorpheniramine maleate, metronidazole, atropine sulphate, ephedrine and most alkaloidal salts.

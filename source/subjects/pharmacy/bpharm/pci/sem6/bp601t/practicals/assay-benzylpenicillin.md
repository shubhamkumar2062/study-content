# Assay of benzylpenicillin by the iodometric method

**Aim:** To determine the potency of a given sample of benzylpenicillin (penicillin G) sodium or potassium by iodometric titration.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves. Students with a known penicillin allergy must tell the teacher before handling the powder.

## Principle

Intact penicillin does not react with iodine. When the **beta-lactam ring is opened by alkali** (inactivation), the product, **penicilloic acid**, is readily oxidised and **consumes iodine** — classically taken as about eight equivalents per molecule under the conditions of the assay.

```text
Penicillin  --(NaOH)-->  penicilloic acid  --(excess I2, pH 4.5)--> oxidised products
Excess iodine is then titrated with standard sodium thiosulphate:
I2 + 2 Na2S2O3 → 2 NaI + Na2S4O6
```

The assay is therefore a **difference method** with two titrations run on the same sample solution:

1. **Blank (inactivated blank is not used first):** an untreated aliquot is mixed with iodine; intact penicillin does not react, so the iodine recovered gives the blank value B.
2. **Test:** another aliquot is first hydrolysed with sodium hydroxide, neutralised with acid, buffered, then treated with the same amount of iodine; the iodine left over is titrated to give T.

The difference (B − T) corresponds to the iodine consumed by the penicilloic acid and so to the penicillin content.

**Equivalent factor:** 1 ml of 0.01 N iodine (0.005 M), measured as 1 ml of 0.01 M sodium thiosulphate, ≡ **0.0004455 g of benzylpenicillin sodium** (8 equivalents of iodine per molecule; molecular weight of the sodium salt 356.4; 356.4 ÷ 8 × 0.01 = 0.4455 mg). The figure of 8 equivalents is approximate, so pharmacopoeial iodometric methods check the result against a penicillin reference standard run the same way; follow your lab manual if it gives a different factor.

## Requirements

Benzylpenicillin sodium (or potassium) sample, 1 M sodium hydroxide, 1 M hydrochloric acid, pH 4.5 acetate buffer, 0.005 M (0.01 N) iodine, 0.01 M sodium thiosulphate (standardised), starch mucilage, volumetric flask, pipettes, iodine flasks with stoppers, burette, analytical balance.

## Procedure

**Preparation of the sample solution**

1. Weigh accurately about 0.1 g of the sample, dissolve it in water and make up to 100 ml in a volumetric flask. Use the solution at once; penicillin solutions decompose on standing.

**Blank titration (intact drug)**

2. Pipette 10 ml of the solution into an iodine flask.
3. Add 5 ml of pH 4.5 acetate buffer and exactly 25 ml of 0.005 M (0.01 N) iodine. Stopper and keep in the dark for 15 minutes.
4. Titrate the unreacted iodine with 0.01 M sodium thiosulphate, adding starch mucilage near the end point, until the blue colour just disappears. Note the volume as **B**.

**Test titration (after inactivation)**

5. Pipette another 10 ml of the sample solution into a second iodine flask.
6. Add 1 ml of 1 M sodium hydroxide, stopper and allow to stand for 15 minutes so that the beta-lactam ring is opened.
7. Add 1 ml of 1 M hydrochloric acid to neutralise, then 5 ml of pH 4.5 buffer and exactly 25 ml of 0.005 M (0.01 N) iodine. Stopper and keep in the dark for 15 minutes.
8. Titrate the excess iodine with 0.01 M sodium thiosulphate using starch as before. Note the volume as **T**.

```text
Iodine consumed by penicilloic acid (as ml of 0.01 M thiosulphate) = B − T
Benzylpenicillin sodium (g) in 10 ml aliquot = (B − T) × 0.0004455 × molarity factor
Amount in 100 ml = that value × 10
% purity = (amount found × 100) / weight of sample taken
```

**Worked example.** Sample weight 0.1000 g made up to 100 ml. B = 24.6 ml, T = 2.2 ml of 0.01 M thiosulphate (example readings).

- Iodine consumed = 24.6 − 2.2 = 22.4 ml
- Benzylpenicillin sodium in the aliquot = 22.4 × 0.0004455 = 0.00998 g
- Amount in 100 ml = 0.0998 g
- Percentage purity = (0.0998 ÷ 0.1000) × 100 = **99.8 % w/w**

Potency may also be expressed in units: 1 mg of benzylpenicillin sodium corresponds to about **1670 units**, so the calculated weight can be converted to units for a label claim check.

## Precautions

1. Prepare the sample solution immediately before use; penicillin hydrolyses in water, which would raise the blank-corrected result.
2. Keep the flasks stoppered and in the dark during the 15-minute periods, because iodine is volatile and light-sensitive.
3. Allow exactly the stated contact time for alkaline hydrolysis in both the test and, if required, the blank, so that the comparison is fair.
4. Add starch only near the end point; iodine adsorbed on starch is released slowly and gives a premature end point.
5. Standardise the thiosulphate solution on the day of use.
6. Avoid skin contact with the powder and wash hands afterwards, since penicillin can sensitise.

## Viva questions

1. **Why does intact penicillin not react with iodine?** The beta-lactam ring is a closed, strained amide that offers no group readily oxidised by iodine under these conditions.
2. **What is penicilloic acid?** The inactive product formed when the beta-lactam ring of penicillin is opened by alkali, acid or beta-lactamase.
3. **Why is the titration carried out at pH 4.5?** At this pH the oxidation of penicilloic acid by iodine is reproducible and side reactions are minimised.
4. **What is the role of starch?** It forms a deep blue complex with iodine and acts as the indicator for the thiosulphate titration.
5. **Why are iodine flasks used?** Their ground-glass stoppers and sealing cups prevent loss of volatile iodine during the standing period.
6. **Name another method for assaying penicillins.** Microbiological assay by the cylinder-plate or turbidimetric method, mercurimetric titration, and high-performance liquid chromatography.
7. **Why must penicillin injections be prepared immediately before use?** Because the beta-lactam ring hydrolyses in solution, so potency falls and the degradation products may increase the risk of sensitisation.

# Preparation of 7-hydroxy-4-methylcoumarin (Pechmann reaction)

**Aim:** To prepare 7-hydroxy-4-methylcoumarin from resorcinol and ethyl acetoacetate by the Pechmann condensation, and to report the yield and melting point.

> Carried out only in the college laboratory under the teacher's supervision, with the quantities given in your own lab manual. Concentrated sulphuric acid is strongly corrosive and the reaction is exothermic; use goggles, a lab coat, gloves and a fume cupboard.

## Principle

The **Pechmann condensation** builds a coumarin ring from a phenol and a beta-keto ester in the presence of a strong acid catalyst, usually concentrated sulphuric acid.

```text
Resorcinol + ethyl acetoacetate --(conc. H2SO4, cold)--> 7-hydroxy-4-methylcoumarin + EtOH + H2O
```

The sequence is: the acid protonates the keto group, the activated ring of resorcinol attacks it in a **Friedel-Crafts type hydroxyalkylation** para to one hydroxyl, then **transesterification and dehydration** close the lactone ring, giving the coumarin. The product is also known as **4-methylumbelliferone** and is important because coumarins are the parent structures of the anticoagulant warfarin and of several natural products; the compound itself is strongly **fluorescent in alkali**, which is the basis of its use as a fluorimetric reagent.

```text
          HO ─┐  7
              │ ┌────┐
              │ │    │ 4-CH3
          HO ─┘ └─O──C=O      lactone (2-oxo-2H-chromene)
```

## Requirements

Resorcinol, ethyl acetoacetate, concentrated sulphuric acid, crushed ice, dilute sodium hydroxide, dilute hydrochloric acid, ethanol, beaker, glass rod, ice bath, Buchner funnel and flask, melting point apparatus.

## Procedure (outline, as directed by the teacher)

1. Dissolve the resorcinol in ethyl acetoacetate in a dry beaker.
2. Cool the mixture well in an ice-salt bath.
3. Add concentrated sulphuric acid **drop by drop with constant stirring**, keeping the temperature low as directed; the mixture thickens and may turn red.
4. Allow the mixture to stand in the cold for the period stated in the manual, so that the condensation and ring closure are complete.
5. Pour the mixture slowly, with stirring, on to crushed ice; a solid separates.
6. Filter the crude product at the pump and wash it thoroughly with cold water until the washings are no longer acidic to litmus.
7. **Purify by dissolving the solid in dilute sodium hydroxide, filtering, and reprecipitating by the careful addition of dilute hydrochloric acid**, then filter, wash with water and recrystallise from hot ethanol.
8. Dry the crystals, weigh them and determine the melting point.

```text
Theoretical yield (g) = weight of resorcinol (g) × (176.2 ÷ 110.1)
                        (molecular weight of product ÷ that of resorcinol)
Percentage yield = (practical yield ÷ theoretical yield) × 100
```

**Worked example.** From 5.5 g of resorcinol, theoretical yield = 5.5 × 176.2 ÷ 110.1 = 8.80 g. A dry product of 6.2 g gives (6.2 ÷ 8.80) × 100 = **70.5 %**.

**Identification tests**

- A solution of the product in dilute sodium hydroxide shows an intense **blue fluorescence** in daylight or under ultraviolet light.
- It dissolves in alkali (phenolic hydroxyl and lactone ring opening) and is reprecipitated by acid.
- The melting point agrees with the reported value and a mixed melting point is not depressed.

## Precautions

1. Add the sulphuric acid slowly and with continuous cooling; the reaction is strongly exothermic and a rise in temperature causes charring and a dark product.
2. All glassware must be dry.
3. Pour the reaction mixture on to ice, never the reverse.
4. Wash the crude product free from acid before recrystallising, or the melting point will be low and indefinite.
5. Use the minimum volume of hot ethanol for recrystallisation and allow slow cooling for good crystals.
6. Handle the hot solvent away from any flame.

## Viva questions

1. **Name the reaction used.** The Pechmann condensation between a phenol and a beta-keto ester in the presence of a strong acid.
2. **Why is resorcinol used rather than phenol?** Its two hydroxyl groups activate the ring strongly and are placed so that cyclisation to the coumarin is easy; phenol reacts far less readily.
3. **What is the role of sulphuric acid?** It is the catalyst and the dehydrating agent; it activates the keto group and drives the lactonisation.
4. **Why does the product fluoresce in alkali?** The phenolate form of the coumarin is a rigid conjugated system that emits strongly in the blue region, which is why it is used as a fluorimetric reagent.
5. **Give the other name of the compound.** 4-Methylumbelliferone.
6. **Why is the crude product purified through its sodium salt?** The phenolic product dissolves in alkali while neutral impurities do not, so filtering the alkaline solution and reprecipitating with acid removes them.
7. **Name a drug containing the coumarin nucleus.** Warfarin, the oral anticoagulant, which is a 4-hydroxycoumarin derivative.

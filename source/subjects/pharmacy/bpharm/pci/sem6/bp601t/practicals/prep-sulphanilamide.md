# Preparation of sulphanilamide

**Aim:** To prepare and purify sulphanilamide from acetanilide, and to report the yield, melting point and identity of the product.

> This is a demonstration-type exercise carried out only in the college laboratory, under the direct supervision of the teacher, with the quantities given in your own lab manual. Chlorosulphonic acid reacts violently with water and gives off hydrogen chloride; the whole first stage is done in a fume cupboard, behind a safety screen, with goggles, a lab coat and gloves. Never attempt any part of this outside the laboratory.

## Principle

Sulphanilamide is 4-aminobenzenesulphonamide, the parent of all sulphonamide antibacterials. It is made from **acetanilide** in three stages, and the reason for the first stage is the heart of the experiment: the amino group of aniline must be **protected as its acetyl derivative**, otherwise it would be attacked by the sulphonating reagent and would also activate the ring so strongly that the reaction could not be controlled.

```text
Stage 1  Acetanilide  --(chlorosulphonic acid)-->  p-acetamidobenzenesulphonyl chloride
         (electrophilic substitution, para to the acetamido group)

Stage 2  Sulphonyl chloride  --(ammonia solution)-->  p-acetamidobenzenesulphonamide

Stage 3  p-Acetamidobenzenesulphonamide  --(dilute HCl, heat)-->  SULPHANILAMIDE
         (selective hydrolysis of the amide; the sulphonamide group survives)
```

Three chemical points to understand:

1. The **acetamido group is an ortho-para director**, and because the ortho positions are hindered, substitution occurs almost entirely **para**.
2. The sulphonyl chloride is **reactive towards nucleophiles**, so ammonia converts it to the sulphonamide; the reaction is exothermic.
3. In stage 3 the **carboxamide is hydrolysed much faster than the sulphonamide**, so the protecting group can be removed selectively; the product is isolated by neutralising with sodium bicarbonate to the isoelectric point, where sulphanilamide, being amphoteric, is least soluble.

## Requirements

Acetanilide, chlorosulphonic acid, strong ammonia solution, dilute hydrochloric acid, sodium bicarbonate, crushed ice, round-bottom flask with a guard tube, fume cupboard, water bath, Buchner funnel and flask, filter paper, beakers, melting point apparatus.

## Procedure (outline, as directed by the teacher)

1. **Protection already done:** acetanilide is used as the starting material, so the amino group is protected from the outset.
2. **Stage 1.** The dry acetanilide is added in portions to chlorosulphonic acid, cooling as directed, and the mixture is then warmed on a water bath until the evolution of hydrogen chloride ceases. The product is poured very cautiously on to crushed ice, when the sulphonyl chloride separates as a solid; it is filtered and washed with cold water but **not dried**, because it hydrolyses.
3. **Stage 2.** The moist sulphonyl chloride is stirred with strong ammonia solution; the mixture is warmed gently on a water bath to complete the reaction and then cooled, and the crude p-acetamidobenzenesulphonamide is filtered and washed with cold water.
4. **Stage 3.** The solid is boiled under reflux with dilute hydrochloric acid until a clear solution is obtained, which shows that hydrolysis of the acetyl group is complete.
5. The solution is cooled and neutralised cautiously with solid sodium bicarbonate until effervescence ceases and the mixture is just alkaline to litmus; sulphanilamide separates.
6. The crude product is filtered, washed with a little cold water and **recrystallised from hot water** (or from dilute alcohol), filtered, dried and weighed.

## Observations and calculations

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Weight of acetanilide taken | ______ g |
| Theoretical yield of sulphanilamide | ______ g |
| Weight of dry product obtained | ______ g |
| Percentage yield | ______ % |
| Melting point observed | ______ °C |
| Reported melting point | about 165 °C |
| Appearance | White crystalline powder |

```text
Theoretical yield (g) = weight of acetanilide (g) × (172.2 ÷ 135.2)
                        (molecular weight of sulphanilamide ÷ that of acetanilide)
Percentage yield = (practical yield ÷ theoretical yield) × 100
```

**Worked example.** From 5.0 g of acetanilide the theoretical yield is 5.0 × 172.2 ÷ 135.2 = 6.37 g. If 3.6 g of dry product is obtained, the percentage yield is (3.6 ÷ 6.37) × 100 = **56.5 %**.

**Identification tests**

- Melting point agrees with the reported value and a mixed melting point with authentic sulphanilamide is not depressed.
- It dissolves both in dilute hydrochloric acid and in sodium hydroxide solution, showing its **amphoteric** character.
- It gives a positive test for a **primary aromatic amine**: with sodium nitrite and hydrochloric acid at ice temperature followed by alkaline beta-naphthol, an orange-red azo dye is produced.

## Precautions

1. Chlorosulphonic acid must never be added to water or ice; the reaction mixture is poured **on to** ice, slowly and in a fume cupboard behind a screen.
2. All apparatus for the first stage must be perfectly dry, and a guard tube is fitted to exclude moisture.
3. Do not dry the intermediate sulphonyl chloride; it hydrolyses readily and the yield falls.
4. Add ammonia solution with cooling, as the reaction is exothermic.
5. Neutralise slowly with solid sodium bicarbonate; rapid addition causes frothing and loss of product.
6. Recrystallise from the minimum volume of hot solvent, and dry the product at a low temperature.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is acetanilide used rather than aniline?** The acetyl group protects the amino group from attack by the sulphonating reagent and moderates the activation of the ring, so the reaction can be controlled and gives the para product.
2. **Why is substitution para and not ortho?** The acetamido group directs ortho and para, but the ortho positions are sterically hindered by the bulky side chain, so the para isomer predominates.
3. **Why does the acetyl group hydrolyse but not the sulphonamide?** A carboxamide is hydrolysed far more easily than a sulphonamide, so controlled acid hydrolysis removes only the protecting group.
4. **Why is the product precipitated with sodium bicarbonate rather than with sodium hydroxide?** Sulphanilamide is amphoteric and dissolves in excess alkali; bicarbonate neutralises the acid gently and stops near the isoelectric point, where solubility is lowest.
5. **How would you confirm that the product is a primary aromatic amine?** By the diazotisation and coupling test, which gives a coloured azo dye.
6. **What is the pharmacological importance of sulphanilamide?** It is the active metabolite of Prontosil and the parent of the sulphonamide antibacterials, which act by antagonising p-aminobenzoic acid.
7. **Which recrystallising solvent is used and why?** Hot water, because sulphanilamide is much more soluble hot than cold, so it crystallises well on cooling.

# Assay of chlorpheniramine maleate by non-aqueous titration

**Aim:** To determine the percentage purity of a given sample of chlorpheniramine maleate by non-aqueous titration with perchloric acid.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves; perchloric acid, acetic anhydride and mercuric acetate are hazardous — handle with care and dispose of mercury waste as your institution directs.

## Principle

Chlorpheniramine maleate is the maleate salt of an alkylamine antihistamine that contains a **pyridine nitrogen and a tertiary aliphatic amine**. In glacial acetic acid both the tertiary amine and the maleate anion can be titrated, so the assay is designed to measure the **two basic centres of the drug**, while the maleic acid released remains too weak an acid in this solvent to interfere.

```text
Drug·maleate + 2 HClO4 → drug·2H+ (ClO4−)2 + maleic acid     (glacial acetic acid)
```

Crystal violet is the indicator, changing from violet through blue to **blue-green**. The Indian Pharmacopoeia method dissolves the sample in glacial acetic acid with acetic anhydride, which removes traces of water and sharpens the end point.

**Equivalent factor:** 1 ml of 0.1 M perchloric acid ≡ **0.01954 g of chlorpheniramine maleate** (molecular weight 390.86, two basic centres titrated, equivalent weight 195.43).

## Requirements

Chlorpheniramine maleate sample, glacial acetic acid, acetic anhydride, 0.1 M perchloric acid in glacial acetic acid, crystal violet indicator, dry burette and conical flask, analytical balance.

## Procedure

1. Dry all the glassware carefully.
2. Weigh accurately about 0.3 g of the sample into a dry conical flask.
3. Dissolve it in a mixture of about 25 ml of glacial acetic acid and 10 ml of acetic anhydride, warming gently if necessary, then cool.
4. Add 2 to 3 drops of crystal violet indicator.
5. Titrate with 0.1 M perchloric acid to a blue-green end point.
6. Repeat for two concordant readings and perform a blank titration on the solvent mixture, subtracting the blank.

```text
Chlorpheniramine maleate (g) = corrected volume (ml) × 0.01954 × molarity factor
% purity = (amount found × 100) / weight of sample taken
```

**Worked example.** Sample weight 0.3000 g; corrected titre 15.3 ml of 0.1 M perchloric acid.

- Amount found = 15.3 × 0.01954 = 0.2990 g
- Percentage purity = (0.2990 ÷ 0.3000) × 100 = **99.7 % w/w**

## Precautions

1. Everything must be dry; even a little water spoils the end point.
2. Acetic anhydride must be handled in a fume cupboard; it is lachrymatory and corrosive.
3. If a halide salt such as a hydrochloride is being titrated instead, mercuric acetate is added to remove the halide ion, which is otherwise a competing base; collect mercury-containing waste separately.
4. Keep the flask covered as far as possible, since glacial acetic acid is hygroscopic.
5. Use a white background to judge the blue-green colour.
6. Standardise the perchloric acid against potassium hydrogen phthalate on the day of use.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is the maleate salt used in the formulation?** It is a stable, crystalline, water-soluble salt that is convenient to handle and to formulate as tablets and syrups.
2. **Why does mercuric acetate have to be added for hydrochloride salts?** The chloride ion behaves as a base in glacial acetic acid; mercuric acetate removes it as the un-ionised mercuric chloride so that only the drug is titrated.
3. **Which chemical class does chlorpheniramine belong to?** The alkylamines or propylamines among the H1 antihistamines, which are potent and only mildly sedative.
4. **Which enantiomer of chlorpheniramine is more active?** The dextrorotatory (S)-isomer, which is marketed separately as dexchlorpheniramine.
5. **Name the indicator and its colour change.** Crystal violet, violet to blue-green.
6. **Why does the assay consume two moles of perchloric acid per mole of drug?** Both the pyridine nitrogen and the aliphatic tertiary amine are sufficiently basic in this solvent to be titrated.
7. **Give one therapeutic use of chlorpheniramine maleate.** Relief of allergic rhinitis, urticaria and other hypersensitivity reactions.

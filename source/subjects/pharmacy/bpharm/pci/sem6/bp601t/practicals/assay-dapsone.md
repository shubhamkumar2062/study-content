# Assay of dapsone by diazotisation (nitrite) titration

**Aim:** To determine the percentage purity of a given sample of dapsone by titration with standard sodium nitrite solution.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves; work at ice temperature as directed.

## Principle

Dapsone is 4,4'-diaminodiphenylsulfone and carries **two primary aromatic amino groups**. A primary aromatic amine reacts quantitatively with nitrous acid, formed in situ from sodium nitrite and hydrochloric acid, to give a **diazonium salt**:

```text
NaNO2 + HCl → HNO2 + NaCl
Ar–NH2 + HNO2 + HCl → Ar–N2+Cl− + 2 H2O        (0 to 5 °C)
```

Because both amino groups react, **one molecule of dapsone consumes two molecules of sodium nitrite**. The end point is detected either with **starch iodide paper** as an external indicator — the first drop of excess nitrous acid liberates iodine from potassium iodide and turns the paper blue — or electrometrically by a dead-stop (biamperometric) end point.

**Equivalent factor:** 1 ml of 0.1 M sodium nitrite ≡ **0.01242 g of dapsone** (molecular weight 248.30, two amino groups, equivalent weight 124.15).

## Requirements

Dapsone sample, 0.1 M sodium nitrite (standardised against sulphanilamide), concentrated hydrochloric acid, potassium bromide, crushed ice, starch iodide paper, beaker, glass rod, burette, analytical balance, thermometer.

## Procedure

1. Weigh accurately about 0.25 g of the dapsone sample and transfer it to a beaker.
2. Add 20 ml of concentrated hydrochloric acid and about 50 ml of water and stir until dissolved; add about 3 g of potassium bromide.
3. Cool the solution in an ice bath to below 5 °C and keep it there throughout the titration.
4. Titrate slowly with 0.1 M sodium nitrite from the burette, with the tip of the burette dipping below the surface of the liquid, stirring constantly.
5. After each addition near the expected end point, wait about one minute, then withdraw a drop on a glass rod and touch it to starch iodide paper.
6. The end point is an **immediate blue colour** on the paper that is still obtained after a further minute of stirring.
7. Repeat for two concordant readings.

```text
Dapsone (g) = volume of 0.1 M NaNO2 (ml) × 0.01242 × molarity factor
% purity = (amount found × 100) / weight of sample taken
```

**Worked example.** Sample weight 0.2500 g; titre 20.1 ml of 0.1 M sodium nitrite.

- Amount found = 20.1 × 0.01242 = 0.2496 g
- Percentage purity = (0.2496 ÷ 0.2500) × 100 = **99.8 % w/w**

## Precautions

1. Keep the temperature below 5 °C; diazonium salts decompose above this and the result is low.
2. Add the titrant slowly with constant stirring, because diazotisation is not instantaneous.
3. Dip the burette tip below the surface so that nitrous acid is not lost as oxides of nitrogen.
4. Potassium bromide acts as a catalyst and speeds the reaction; do not omit it.
5. Confirm the end point after waiting a minute, because a premature blue colour may fade as the reaction continues.
6. Use freshly prepared starch iodide paper; old paper may give a false colour.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is a low temperature necessary?** The diazonium salt is thermally unstable and decomposes to a phenol with liberation of nitrogen if the solution warms.
2. **What is the role of potassium bromide?** It catalyses the diazotisation by forming nitrosyl bromide, which reacts faster than nitrous acid itself.
3. **Why does dapsone need two moles of nitrite?** It contains two primary aromatic amino groups, each of which is diazotised.
4. **How is the end point detected electrometrically?** By the dead-stop method, in which a small potential is applied across two platinum electrodes and a sudden current flows when free nitrous acid appears.
5. **Name other drugs assayed by diazotisation.** Sulphonamides such as sulphacetamide and sulphamethoxazole, procaine, benzocaine and primaquine after hydrolysis to an aromatic amine.
6. **How is sodium nitrite solution standardised?** Against pure sulphanilamide, which contains one primary aromatic amino group.
7. **Give the therapeutic use of dapsone.** It is a key component of multidrug therapy for leprosy, and is also used in dermatitis herpetiformis.

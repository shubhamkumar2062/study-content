# Assay of metronidazole by non-aqueous titration

**Aim:** To determine the percentage purity of a given sample of metronidazole by non-aqueous titration with perchloric acid.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your own lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves; perchloric acid is corrosive.

## Principle

Metronidazole is 2-methyl-5-nitroimidazole-1-ethanol. The **N3 nitrogen of the imidazole ring is weakly basic**, too weak to be titrated in water, but strong enough in **glacial acetic acid**, where the solvent enhances basicity and perchloric acid behaves as the strongest available acid.

```text
Metronidazole (base) + HClO4 → metronidazole·H+ ClO4−     (glacial acetic acid)
```

One molecule of metronidazole reacts with **one molecule of perchloric acid**. The indicator is crystal violet, which changes from violet through blue to **blue-green**. The nitro group lowers the basicity of the ring, so the end point must be observed carefully; a potentiometric titration is used where available.

**Equivalent factor:** 1 ml of 0.1 M perchloric acid ≡ **0.01712 g of metronidazole** (molecular weight 171.15).

## Requirements

Metronidazole sample, glacial acetic acid, 0.1 M perchloric acid in glacial acetic acid, crystal violet indicator, dry burette, conical flask, analytical balance.

## Procedure

1. Dry all glassware thoroughly.
2. Weigh accurately about 0.15 g of metronidazole into a dry conical flask.
3. Dissolve it in about 50 ml of glacial acetic acid, warming gently if necessary and cooling to room temperature.
4. Add 2 to 3 drops of crystal violet indicator.
5. Titrate with 0.1 M perchloric acid until the violet colour changes to blue-green.
6. Repeat for two concordant readings and carry out a blank titration, subtracting the blank from each reading.

```text
Metronidazole (g) = corrected volume (ml) × 0.01712 × molarity factor
% purity = (amount found × 100) / weight of sample taken
```

**Worked example.** Sample weight 0.1500 g; corrected titre 8.70 ml of 0.1 M perchloric acid.

- Amount found = 8.70 × 0.01712 = 0.1489 g
- Percentage purity = (0.1489 ÷ 0.1500) × 100 = **99.3 % w/w**

## Precautions

1. Keep everything dry; water reduces the sharpness of the end point.
2. Dissolve the sample completely before titrating; warm gently and cool again if needed.
3. Observe the end point against a white tile, as the blue-green colour is easier to judge in good light.
4. Correct for temperature if the perchloric acid solution is used well away from its standardisation temperature.
5. Always subtract a blank titration.

## Viva questions

1. **Which part of metronidazole is basic?** The N3 nitrogen of the imidazole ring; the hydroxyethyl group and the nitro group are not basic.
2. **Why is the nitro group important for the drug's action?** Anaerobic organisms reduce it to reactive species that break DNA strands, which is why the drug is selective for anaerobes.
3. **Why is the end point less sharp than with an alkaloidal salt?** The electron-withdrawing nitro group lowers the basicity of the imidazole nitrogen.
4. **What alternative end-point detection may be used?** Potentiometric titration with a glass and calomel or a glass and silver-silver chloride electrode system.
5. **Name another assay method for metronidazole.** Ultraviolet spectrophotometry, since the compound absorbs strongly near 277 nm in 0.1 M hydrochloric acid, and high-performance liquid chromatography.
6. **Give two therapeutic uses of metronidazole.** Amoebiasis and giardiasis; trichomoniasis and anaerobic bacterial infections.
7. **What must a patient taking metronidazole avoid?** Alcohol, because the drug produces a disulfiram-like reaction.

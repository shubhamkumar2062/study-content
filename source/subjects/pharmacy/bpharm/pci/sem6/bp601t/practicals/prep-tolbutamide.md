# Preparation of tolbutamide

**Aim:** To prepare tolbutamide from p-toluenesulphonamide and n-butyl isocyanate (or by the carbamate route used in your laboratory), and to report the yield and melting point.

> Carried out only in the college laboratory under the teacher's supervision, with the quantities given in your own lab manual. Isocyanates are irritant and moisture-sensitive; work in a fume cupboard with goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

Tolbutamide is a **first-generation sulphonylurea** oral antidiabetic drug. Its structure is a p-substituted aryl ring, the sulphonylurea bridge and an n-butyl group:

```text
   CH3 ─[ benzene ]─ SO2 ─ NH ─ CO ─ NH ─ C4H9
                     sulphonyl   urea     n-butyl
```

The sulphonylurea link is made by adding the weakly acidic **sulphonamide nitrogen** of p-toluenesulphonamide across the carbon-nitrogen double bond of an **isocyanate**:

```text
Ar–SO2NH2 + O=C=N–C4H9  --(base)-->  Ar–SO2–NH–CO–NH–C4H9
```

The base (for example anhydrous potassium carbonate or a tertiary amine) removes the proton from the sulphonamide so that the resulting anion can attack the electrophilic carbon of the isocyanate. The product is acidic enough to dissolve in dilute alkali and is therefore purified by dissolving it in alkali and reprecipitating it with acid.

**Why the SAR matters here.** A p-methyl group gives tolbutamide; oxidation of this methyl group in the liver to a carboxyl group ends its action, which is why tolbutamide is short acting, whereas chlorpropamide, with a p-chloro group, cannot be metabolised in that way and acts much longer.

## Requirements

p-Toluenesulphonamide, n-butyl isocyanate, anhydrous potassium carbonate (or a tertiary amine as directed), dry acetone or toluene, dilute sodium hydroxide, dilute hydrochloric acid, ethanol, round-bottom flask with reflux condenser and guard tube, Buchner funnel and flask, melting point apparatus.

## Procedure (outline, as directed by the teacher)

1. Place p-toluenesulphonamide, anhydrous potassium carbonate and dry solvent in the flask and stir to form the salt of the sulphonamide.
2. Add n-butyl isocyanate slowly to the stirred mixture and fit a reflux condenser with a guard tube.
3. Heat gently under reflux for the period directed, with stirring, to complete the addition.
4. Cool the mixture and pour it into water; any unchanged starting material and inorganic salts dissolve.
5. Make the aqueous mixture alkaline with dilute sodium hydroxide so that the acidic product passes into solution, and filter off any insoluble impurity.
6. Acidify the filtrate cautiously with dilute hydrochloric acid; tolbutamide separates as a white solid.
7. Filter at the pump, wash with cold water, recrystallise from aqueous ethanol, dry, weigh and determine the melting point.

## Observations and calculations

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Weight of p-toluenesulphonamide taken | ______ g |
| Theoretical yield | ______ g |
| Weight of dry product | ______ g |
| Percentage yield | ______ % |
| Melting point observed | ______ °C |
| Reported melting point | about 128 to 130 °C |
| Appearance | White crystalline powder, practically insoluble in water |

```text
Theoretical yield (g) = weight of p-toluenesulphonamide (g) × (270.3 ÷ 171.2)
                        (molecular weight of tolbutamide ÷ that of the sulphonamide)
Percentage yield = (practical yield ÷ theoretical yield) × 100
```

**Worked example.** From 3.4 g of p-toluenesulphonamide the theoretical yield is 3.4 × 270.3 ÷ 171.2 = 5.37 g. A dry product of 3.8 g gives (3.8 ÷ 5.37) × 100 = **70.8 %**.

**Identification tests**

- The product is practically insoluble in water but dissolves readily in dilute sodium hydroxide, showing the acidic sulphonylurea hydrogen, and is reprecipitated on acidification.
- The melting point agrees with the reported value and a mixed melting point with an authentic sample is not depressed.
- An infrared spectrum, where available, shows absorption for the urea carbonyl and the sulphonyl group.

## Precautions

1. All apparatus and reagents must be dry, because isocyanates react readily with water to give amines and carbon dioxide, lowering the yield.
2. Add the isocyanate slowly and with stirring; the reaction is exothermic.
3. Isocyanate vapour is irritant to the eyes and respiratory tract, so work in a fume cupboard.
4. Acidify the alkaline solution slowly and with stirring to obtain a filterable precipitate.
5. Wash the product free from salts before recrystallisation; residual acid or alkali lowers the melting point.
6. Dry at a moderate temperature and store the product in a closed container.

## Viva questions

1. **To which class of drugs does tolbutamide belong?** The first-generation sulphonylurea oral hypoglycaemic agents.
2. **How does it lower blood glucose?** It blocks the ATP-sensitive potassium channel of the pancreatic beta cell, which depolarises the cell, opens calcium channels and causes release of stored insulin.
3. **Why is tolbutamide short acting compared with chlorpropamide?** Its p-methyl group is oxidised in the liver to an inactive carboxyl derivative, whereas the p-chloro group of chlorpropamide is not metabolised in that way.
4. **Which part of the molecule is acidic?** The hydrogen on the nitrogen between the sulphonyl and the carbonyl groups, which is why the drug dissolves in alkali.
5. **State the SAR requirements of a sulphonylurea.** A para-substituted aryl ring, the intact sulphonylurea bridge, and an alkyl or alicyclic group of about three to six carbon atoms on the terminal nitrogen.
6. **Why is the product purified through its sodium salt?** The acidic drug dissolves in alkali while neutral impurities do not, so they can be filtered off before reprecipitation.
7. **Name a second-generation sulphonylurea.** Glibenclamide, glipizide or gliclazide.

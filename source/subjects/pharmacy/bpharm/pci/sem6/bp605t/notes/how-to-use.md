# How to use this guide

Pharmaceutical Biotechnology is a Semester 6 subject carrying 4 theory credits. It bridges the molecular biology you studied in earlier years with the practical reality of how modern protein drugs are made, tested and regulated. The subject is unusually broad — it touches virology, microbiology, immunology, process engineering, analytical chemistry and pharmacoeconomics — but the exam rewards students who can explain a few key processes clearly and completely, with labelled diagrams.

**Prerequisites.** A solid grasp of Microbiology and Immunology (earlier semesters) is assumed throughout. If the terms "antibody", "antigen", "gram-positive", "plasmid", "promoter" and "restriction enzyme" are unfamiliar, revisit those notes first.

**How the units here match the syllabus.** The PCI syllabus has five units, and these notes keep one file per unit in the same order. Unit 1 covers the molecular tools; Unit 2 covers how those tools are used to make specific proteins; Unit 3 covers antibodies, vaccines and diagnostics; Unit 4 covers fermentation; Unit 5 covers purification and regulation.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, as universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ or very short answers | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; learn the full answer, include a labelled diagram where relevant.
- **Medium** — asked every few years, or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for the objective section and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from syllabus weight and common patterns in pharmacy papers, not a promise of what will be asked.

**High-probability topics (most likely to appear in Section B or as a 5-mark question):**

1. Steps in gene cloning with a flow diagram
2. PCR — components, cycle and applications
3. Hybridoma technology — step by step
4. Recombinant insulin — history and production
5. Recombinant EPO — why mammalian cells, uses and misuse
6. ELISA — sandwich ELISA mechanism and applications
7. Penicillin fermentation — organism, process, kinetics
8. Downstream processing of a monoclonal antibody
9. Biosimilar vs biologic originator
10. Viral clearance strategies in biopharmaceutical manufacture

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. **Draw labelled diagrams.** Examiners in this subject award marks for diagrams that most other pharmacy subjects do not test: draw the bioreactor (impeller, sparger, baffles, pH/DO probes, jacket), the hybridoma technology flowchart, and the gene cloning scheme. Even a rough diagram with correct labels scores marks.
2. **For MCQs, know the enzyme names and their actions.** Restriction enzyme names (EcoRI, HindIII), Taq polymerase (heat-stable, 72°C), T4 DNA ligase (seals nicks), Protein A (binds Fc of IgG) and Limulus amebocyte lysate (endotoxin test) appear repeatedly.
3. **Classify and name before explaining.** When asked about expression systems, give the name (*E. coli*, yeast, CHO), state the key advantage and limitation, then explain the detail. Examiners count correct examples.
4. **Distinguish the antibody naming suffixes.** -omab = murine; -ximab = chimeric; -zumab = humanised; -umab = fully human. Learn at least one example for each.
5. **Link the product to the host.** For any recombinant protein, state clearly which host is used and why. EPO → CHO (needs glycosylation); insulin → *E. coli* or yeast (small, no glycan needed); mAbs → CHO (Fc glycosylation needed for effector function).
6. **For regulatory questions, name all three agencies.** CDSCO (India), FDA (USA), EMA (Europe). Know the BLA pathway for the exam; Indian students are also asked about CDSCO.

# Unit 5: Herbal drug industry and GMP for Indian systems of medicine (Schedule T)

This short unit looks at herbal medicine as an industry: how big its scope is, where it is heading, which kinds of plant-based industries and institutions exist in India, and the Good Manufacturing Practice rules — Schedule T of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules — that every Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani manufacturer must follow.

## 5.1 Herbal drug industry — present scope

India is one of the richest countries in medicinal plants and has living traditions of Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani, Sowa-Rigpa, folk medicine and Homoeopathy. Thousands of plant species are used in these systems, and a large number of licensed ASU manufacturing units, from small cottage units to large companies, make classical and proprietary medicines.

**Segments of the herbal industry**

| Segment | Products |
| --- | --- |
| Classical ASU medicines | Churnas, asavas, arishtas, vatis, lehyas, bhasmas, tailas, ghritas |
| Proprietary herbal medicines | Branded tablets, syrups, capsules (liver tonics, cough syrups, digestives) |
| Herbal extracts and phytochemicals | Standardised extracts for domestic use and export; isolated compounds (sennosides, diosgenin, menthol, papain, caffeine, colchicine) |
| Essential oils and aroma chemicals | Mint, lemongrass, citronella, palmarosa, vetiver, eucalyptus oils; menthol |
| Nutraceuticals and health foods | Ashwagandha, amla, spirulina, moringa, turmeric products |
| Herbal cosmetics and toiletries | Creams, shampoos, hair oils, toothpastes, soaps |
| Raw herbs (crude drugs) | Senna, isabgol, ginger, turmeric, spices — for domestic and export trade |

**Strengths of India**

- Huge biodiversity and many agro-climatic zones.
- Large body of documented traditional knowledge.
- Cheap skilled labour and established manufacturing.
- World leader in some crops — for example, **isabgol (psyllium)** and **senna**, and a major producer of **menthol mint**.
- Government support through the **Ministry of AYUSH**, the **National Medicinal Plants Board** and the National AYUSH Mission.

**Problems the industry faces**

- Raw material: dependence on wild collection, over-harvesting, adulteration, poor supply chain, no traceability.
- Quality: lack of standardisation, contamination with heavy metals, pesticides and microbes; batch-to-batch variation.
- Evidence: limited clinical data for many products.
- Regulation and export: different rules in importing countries; strict limits on residues.
- Research and investment: limited R&D spending in small units; poor protection of intellectual property.

## 5.2 Future prospects

1. **Global demand** for herbal medicines, nutraceuticals and natural cosmetics continues to rise.
2. **Standardised, evidence-based products** — phytopharmaceuticals, marker-standardised extracts and clinical studies will open regulated markets.
3. **Cultivation and GACP** — contract farming, organic and certified cultivation, quality planting material and tissue culture will secure raw material.
4. **Modern technology** — supercritical fluid extraction, HPTLC/HPLC fingerprinting, DNA barcoding, novel delivery systems (phytosomes, nanoparticles) and automation.
5. **New drug discovery** from plants guided by traditional knowledge (reverse pharmacology).
6. **Integration** of AYUSH in public health, AYUSH hospitals and wellness tourism.
7. **Intellectual property** — TKDL, benefit sharing, geographical indications and patents on genuine innovations.
8. **Careers** for pharmacists in quality control, regulatory affairs, formulation, research, cultivation and export.

## 5.5 Schedule T — Good Manufacturing Practices for Indian systems of medicine

**Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP)** is the part of quality assurance that makes sure products are **consistently produced and controlled to the quality standards** suited to their intended use. For ASU medicines the GMP requirements are given in **Schedule T** of the **Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945**. Compliance with Schedule T is a condition of the licence to manufacture ASU drugs, and a manufacturer who complies can obtain a **GMP certificate** from the licensing authority.

### Objectives of Schedule T

1. Ensure that **raw materials are authentic** and free from contamination.
2. Ensure that manufacture is carried out in a **clean, hygienic environment** with suitable premises and equipment.
3. Ensure that the **process is followed exactly** as per the authoritative texts or approved formula, through written procedures.
4. Ensure **adequate quality control** of raw materials, in-process material and finished products.
5. Keep **complete records** so every batch can be traced.
6. Protect the consumer by producing medicines of **assured quality, safety and efficacy**, and build confidence for domestic and export markets.

### Components of GMP under Schedule T

Schedule T is in two parts: **Part I** sets out the GMP requirements; **Part II** lists the recommended machinery and equipment and the minimum manufacturing area for each category of ASU dosage form (churna, gutika, asava–arishta, avaleha, bhasma, taila–ghrita and so on). The main headings of Part I are summarised below.

#### 1. Factory premises — location, surroundings and buildings

- Located in a **sanitary place**, away from open sewage, drains, refuse, dirty ponds, public lavatories and factories that give off smoke, dust or fumes.
- Buildings built so that they **protect against weather, dust, insects, rodents and birds**; walls and floors smooth, washable and free from cracks; adequate **lighting and ventilation**; drainage that does not cause contamination.
- Separate sections for different operations, with a logical flow so that raw materials, in-process material and finished goods do not mix.

#### 2. Water supply

- Water used in manufacture must be **pure and of potable quality**; storage tanks cleaned periodically.

#### 3. Disposal of waste

- Waste water and residues disposed of so that they do not contaminate the premises; compliance with pollution-control requirements.

#### 4. Container cleaning

- Facilities for washing, cleaning and drying containers and closures before filling.

#### 5. Stores (storage areas)

| Store | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| **Raw materials** | Separate areas for plant, animal and mineral drugs and for poisonous drugs (kept under lock); raw materials in labelled containers; **quarantine area** for materials awaiting testing; "under test", "approved" and "rejected" labelling; protection from moisture, heat, insects and rodents; first-in, first-out use |
| **Packing materials** | Separate, clean store; printed labels kept securely to avoid mix-ups |
| **Finished goods** | Separate store, quarantined until released by quality control, then stored under proper conditions |

#### 6. Working space

- Adequate space for each manufacturing operation to allow **orderly placement of equipment and material**, avoid crowding, and prevent **mix-ups and cross-contamination** between products.
- Separate areas for operations that create dust or heat (powdering, *bhasma* incineration) and for those that need cleanliness (packing, filling of liquids).

#### 7. Machinery and equipment

- Suitable machinery for each process (pulverisers, sieves, mixers, granulators, pill and tablet machines, boiling vessels, fermentation vats, filters, filling machines, furnaces for bhasma).
- **Easy to clean**, made of materials that do not react with the product; regular **cleaning, maintenance and calibration** with records.
- The recommended list for each dosage form is in Part II of the Schedule.

#### 8. Standard operating procedures (SOPs)

- **Written, approved procedures** for every important activity: receipt and testing of raw material, cleaning of premises and equipment, each manufacturing step, in-process checks, packing, labelling, storage, quality control and handling of complaints.
- The **master formula** (manufacturing formula) for each product, based on the authoritative text or approved composition, gives ingredients, quantities, processing steps and in-process controls.

#### 9. Health, clothing and sanitation of workers (health and hygiene)

- Workers free from contagious diseases; **medical examination** on joining and periodically.
- **Clean uniforms**, caps or head covers, masks and gloves where needed.
- Washing and toilet facilities separate from manufacturing areas; no eating, smoking or spitting in work areas.
- **Training** of workers in hygiene and their jobs.
- **Medical services**: first-aid facilities and arrangements for medical care.

#### 10. Documentation and records

| Record | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Raw-material records | Source, quantity received, date, test results, approval or rejection |
| **Batch manufacturing record** | Batch number, master formula reference, quantities actually used, each processing step with date and signature, in-process checks, yield, packing and labelling details |
| Quality control records | Tests on raw material, in-process and finished product, reference to methods and standards |
| Distribution records | Where each batch was sent, so that it can be **recalled** if needed |
| Market complaints and adverse reactions | Complaints received, investigation and action taken |
| Equipment and cleaning records | Cleaning, maintenance and calibration logs |

Records are kept for a period specified in the Schedule (generally related to the shelf life of the product).

#### 11. Quality control

- Every manufacturer must have a **quality control section** of its own, or use an **approved drug-testing laboratory**.
- Raw materials tested for **identity, purity and quality** as per the ASU pharmacopoeias; finished products tested as per their standards; **retained samples** of each batch kept.
- Quality control staff should be qualified (for example in Ayurveda, pharmacy, botany or chemistry) and independent of production.

### GMP flow in an ASU factory

```text
Receipt of raw herbs → Quarantine → Identity and quality tests (QC)
        │ approved                         │ rejected → returned/destroyed
        ▼
Approved raw-material store (FIFO, poisonous drugs locked)
        │
        ▼
Dispensing as per master formula → Processing (cleaning, shodhana,
powdering, decoction, fermentation, pill making…) with SOPs and
in-process checks → Batch manufacturing record
        │
        ▼
Finished product quarantine → QC testing and release
        │
        ▼
Packing and labelling → Finished-goods store → Distribution (records for recall)
```

### Benefits of following Schedule T

- Consistent, safe and effective medicines.
- Fewer complaints, recalls and legal actions.
- Eligibility for GMP certification and for higher standards (such as WHO-GMP certificates of pharmaceutical products) needed for export.
- Better public trust in Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani medicines.

## Remember

- The herbal industry covers classical ASU medicines, proprietary products, extracts and phytochemicals, essential oils, nutraceuticals, cosmetics and crude drugs.
- Problems: wild collection, adulteration, lack of standardisation and clinical evidence, contamination. Future: standardisation, GACP, modern extraction, novel delivery, reverse pharmacology, export.
- Key institutions: Ministry of AYUSH, NMPB, PCIM&H (Ghaziabad), CCRAS, CCRUM, CCRS, CCRH, CSIR-CIMAP, NBRI and CDRI (Lucknow), CSIR-IIIM (Jammu), CSIR-IHBT (Palampur), ICAR-DMAPR (Anand), TBGRI, FRLHT, BSI, TKDL.
- Schedule T = GMP for ASU drugs under the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945; Part I requirements, Part II machinery and minimum area.
- Components: location and buildings, water, waste disposal, container cleaning, stores (raw material with quarantine, packing, finished goods), working space, machinery and equipment, SOPs and master formula, health and hygiene of workers, medical services, batch records and documentation, distribution and complaint records, quality control.
- Objective: authentic raw materials, hygienic manufacture, standard procedures, quality control and traceable records → safe, effective medicine.

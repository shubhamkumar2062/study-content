# Unit 2: Nutraceuticals and herb–drug, herb–food interactions

Many people now take herbs not as medicines for a disease but as foods and supplements to stay healthy. This unit explains what nutraceuticals are, what kinds of products are on the market and how they help in common diseases. It then covers ten herbs used as health foods, and finally the interactions of herbs with modern drugs and foods — a topic that matters to every pharmacist because patients rarely tell their doctor that they take herbal supplements.

## 2.1 Nutraceuticals — general aspects

The word **nutraceutical** joins "nutrition" and "pharmaceutical". It was coined by Stephen DeFelice in 1989. A nutraceutical is **a food, or part of a food, that provides medical or health benefits, including the prevention or treatment of disease**, beyond basic nutrition.

Related terms:

| Term | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dietary supplement** | Product taken by mouth that adds a dietary ingredient (vitamin, mineral, herb, amino acid) to the diet; sold as tablets, capsules, powders | Multivitamin tablet, ginseng capsule |
| **Functional food** | An ordinary-looking food that has been fortified or is naturally rich in a health-promoting component | Probiotic yoghurt, omega-3 eggs, iodised salt |
| **Medical food** | Food made for the dietary management of a disease under medical supervision | Formulas for phenylketonuria |
| **Phytonutrient** | Health-promoting non-nutrient compound from plants | Lycopene, resveratrol, sulforaphane |

In India, nutraceuticals and health supplements are regulated as **foods** by the **Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI)** under the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 and its regulations for health supplements and nutraceuticals — not as drugs under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act. They may not claim to cure a disease.

### Classification of nutraceuticals

1. **By source**: plant (lycopene, curcumin, flavonoids), animal (fish oil omega-3, chitosan, glucosamine, collagen), microbial (probiotics such as *Lactobacillus*, spirulina, yeast products), minerals.
2. **By chemical nature**: polyphenols (flavonoids, anthocyanins, catechins), carotenoids (beta-carotene, lycopene, lutein), dietary fibre (inulin, psyllium, beta-glucan), fatty acids (omega-3: EPA, DHA, alpha-linolenic acid), phytosterols, glucosinolates, peptides and amino acids, vitamins and minerals.
3. **By product type**:
   - **Dietary supplements** — tablets, capsules, powders, liquids.
   - **Functional foods** — fortified or enriched foods, probiotic foods, prebiotic foods.
   - **Herbal/botanical products** — ginseng, ashwagandha, garlic, ginkgo.
   - **Sports and energy products**, **weight-management products**, **infant and elderly nutrition**.

### Probiotics, prebiotics and synbiotics

- **Probiotics**: live microorganisms that, when taken in adequate amounts, give a health benefit (*Lactobacillus*, *Bifidobacterium*, *Saccharomyces boulardii*).
- **Prebiotics**: non-digestible food components that selectively feed beneficial gut bacteria (inulin from chicory, fructo-oligosaccharides, galacto-oligosaccharides).
- **Synbiotics**: a probiotic and a prebiotic combined in one product.

## 2.2 Market, growth and scope

The nutraceutical market in India and worldwide has grown fast and continues to grow. Reasons for growth:

- Rising awareness of health, diet and preventive care.
- Increase in lifestyle diseases — diabetes, obesity, heart disease, hypertension.
- Ageing population and higher spending on wellness.
- Belief that natural products are safe; growing interest in Ayurveda and traditional foods.
- Easy access through pharmacies, supermarkets and online sales; sports and fitness culture.
- India's strengths: rich biodiversity, a long tradition of herbs in food (turmeric, amla, ginger), and a strong manufacturing base.

Challenges: unproven health claims, poor quality control, adulteration, lack of clinical evidence for many products, unclear regulation in the past, and interactions with medicines.

Scope for the pharmacist: formulation and stability of nutraceuticals, quality control and standardisation, regulatory documentation, research on evidence, and counselling patients on safe use.

## 2.3 Health benefits and role in diseases

| Condition | Nutraceuticals used | How they are thought to act |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Diabetes** | Fenugreek seed, bitter gourd, cinnamon, gymnema, dietary fibre (psyllium, guar gum), chromium, alpha-lipoic acid | Slow carbohydrate absorption (fibre), improve insulin sensitivity, increase insulin secretion, reduce oxidative stress |
| **Cardiovascular disease** | Omega-3 fatty acids, garlic, phytosterols, soluble fibre (oat beta-glucan, psyllium), soy protein, flavonoids (cocoa, tea), coenzyme Q10, red yeast rice | Lower LDL cholesterol and triglycerides, lower blood pressure, reduce platelet aggregation, antioxidant protection of LDL |
| **Cancer** (prevention, supportive) | Lycopene (tomato), curcumin, green tea catechins (EGCG), sulforaphane (broccoli), resveratrol, selenium, dietary fibre | Antioxidant action, induce detoxifying enzymes, block cell proliferation, induce apoptosis, anti-inflammatory |
| **Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)** | Probiotics, soluble fibre (psyllium), peppermint oil, ginger | Restore gut flora, normalise bowel movement, relax smooth muscle (peppermint), reduce gas and pain |
| **Other gastrointestinal diseases** — constipation, diarrhoea, ulcers, inflammatory bowel disease | Fibre (isabgol), probiotics, prebiotics (inulin), liquorice (deglycyrrhizinised), aloe, zinc | Bulk and softening of stool, protection of mucosa, competition with harmful bacteria, short-chain fatty acids for colon cells |

Answers in this topic should name the nutraceutical, its key constituent and its mechanism, and add that nutraceuticals **support** but do not replace prescribed treatment.

## 2.4 Herbs as health foods

For each herb learn: biological source with family, important constituents, health-food uses and any caution.

### Alfalfa

- **Source**: leaves and herb of *Medicago sativa*, family Fabaceae (Leguminosae).
- **Constituents**: proteins, vitamins (A, C, E, K and B group), minerals (calcium, iron, magnesium), chlorophyll, saponins, isoflavones and coumestans (coumestrol, a phytoestrogen), fibre.
- **Uses**: nutritive tonic, cholesterol lowering (saponins bind cholesterol), source of vitamin K, used in sprouts and tablets.
- **Caution**: seeds and sprouts contain **L-canavanine**, linked to lupus-like reactions; vitamin K content can reduce the effect of warfarin.

### Chicory

- **Source**: roots and leaves of *Cichorium intybus*, family Asteraceae.
- **Constituents**: **inulin** (a fructan, a major part of the dried root), sesquiterpene lactones (bitter: lactucin, lactucopicrin), coumarins, phenolic acids (chicoric acid).
- **Uses**: roasted root as a **coffee substitute or additive**; inulin as a **prebiotic** and dietary fibre; mild laxative, appetiser, liver tonic; source of inulin for food industry.
- **Caution**: allergy in people sensitive to the Asteraceae family.

### Ginger

- **Source**: rhizome of *Zingiber officinale*, family Zingiberaceae.
- **Constituents**: volatile oil (zingiberene, bisabolene), pungent principles **gingerols** (fresh) and **shogaols** (formed on drying and heating), zingerone, starch.
- **Uses**: anti-nausea (motion sickness, morning sickness), carminative, digestive, anti-inflammatory, used in foods, beverages and confectionery.
- **Caution**: may add to the anti-platelet effect of anticoagulants in large amounts.

### Fenugreek

- **Source**: seeds of *Trigonella foenum-graecum*, family Fabaceae.
- **Constituents**: mucilage (**galactomannan**), steroidal saponins (diosgenin as the sapogenin), alkaloid **trigonelline**, amino acid **4-hydroxyisoleucine**, proteins, fixed oil.
- **Uses**: lowers blood glucose (fibre slows absorption; 4-hydroxyisoleucine increases insulin release), lowers cholesterol, galactagogue, spice, source of diosgenin.
- **Caution**: may increase the effect of antidiabetic drugs; maple-syrup odour of sweat and urine.

### Garlic

- **Source**: bulbs of *Allium sativum*, family Liliaceae (now often placed in Amaryllidaceae).
- **Constituents**: the odourless sulphur amino acid **alliin**; when the bulb is crushed, the enzyme **alliinase** converts it to **allicin**, which gives the smell and much of the activity; allicin breaks down to diallyl disulphide, ajoenes and other sulphides.
- **Uses**: lowers cholesterol and blood pressure modestly, reduces platelet aggregation, antimicrobial, antioxidant, food flavour.
- **Caution**: bleeding risk with anticoagulants; reduces blood levels of saquinavir (see 2.6).

### Honey

- **Source**: sugary substance made by honey bees, *Apis mellifera* and other *Apis* species (family Apidae), from the nectar of flowers.
- **Constituents**: invert sugar (fructose and glucose), a little sucrose, water, enzymes (invertase, diastase, glucose oxidase), organic acids, minerals, flavonoids and phenolic acids.
- **Uses**: nutritive sweetener, demulcent in coughs, wound and burn dressing (glucose oxidase forms hydrogen peroxide; high sugar and low pH stop microbes), vehicle (*anupana*) in Ayurveda.
- **Caution**: **not to be given to infants** under one year (risk of infant botulism); adds sugar load in diabetics; adulteration with sugar syrups is common.

### Amla (Indian gooseberry)

- **Source**: fruits of *Emblica officinalis* (*Phyllanthus emblica*), family Euphorbiaceae (now Phyllanthaceae).
- **Constituents**: one of the richest natural sources of **vitamin C** (ascorbic acid), hydrolysable tannins (emblicanins, gallic acid, ellagic acid), flavonoids, pectin.
- **Uses**: antioxidant, immunity booster, rasayana; ingredient of **Chyawanprash** and **Triphala**; hair tonic; lowers lipids and blood sugar in some studies; made into juices, candies and murabba.

### Ginseng

- **Source**: roots of *Panax ginseng* (Asian/Korean ginseng) and *Panax quinquefolius* (American ginseng), family Araliaceae.
- **Constituents**: triterpenoid saponins called **ginsenosides** (panaxosides), polysaccharides, volatile oil.
- **Uses**: **adaptogen** (increases resistance to stress), tonic for fatigue, improves mental and physical performance, mild blood-glucose lowering, immune modulation.
- **Caution**: insomnia, nervousness and high blood pressure with long use ("ginseng abuse syndrome"); interactions with warfarin, MAO inhibitors and antidiabetic drugs.

### Ashwagandha

- **Source**: roots of *Withania somnifera*, family Solanaceae.
- **Constituents**: steroidal lactones called **withanolides** (withaferin A, withanolide D), alkaloids (withanine, somniferine), sitoindosides, iron.
- **Uses**: rasayana and **adaptogen**, reduces stress and anxiety, improves sleep and strength, used for general debility; popular in powders, capsules and health drinks.
- **Caution**: may add to the effect of sedatives and thyroid medicines; avoided in pregnancy.

### Spirulina

- **Source**: dried biomass of the blue-green alga (cyanobacterium) *Spirulina platensis* and *S. maxima* (now *Arthrospira*), grown in alkaline water ponds.
- **Constituents**: very high **protein** content (more than half the dry weight, with all essential amino acids), the blue pigment **phycocyanin**, chlorophyll, carotenoids (beta-carotene), **gamma-linolenic acid**, B vitamins, iron.
- **Uses**: protein and micronutrient supplement in malnutrition, antioxidant and immune support, food colour (phycocyanin), feed for fish and poultry.
- **Caution**: products from contaminated ponds may carry **microcystins** (toxins from other algae) and heavy metals; its vitamin B12 is largely an inactive analogue, so it is not a reliable B12 source.

## 2.5 Herb–drug and herb–food interactions — introduction

An **interaction** occurs when the effect of one substance is changed by another taken at the same time. With herbs this is a real risk because: herbs contain many active compounds; patients take them without telling their doctor; products are not standardised; and many herbs affect drug-metabolising enzymes.

### Classification

| Type | Mechanism | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pharmacokinetic** — changes how much drug reaches its site | | |
| Absorption | Binding, changed gut transit, changed P-glycoprotein | Fibre (isabgol) reduces absorption of drugs taken at the same time |
| Distribution | Displacement from plasma proteins | — |
| **Metabolism** (most important) | **Induction** or **inhibition** of cytochrome P450 (especially CYP3A4) and conjugating enzymes | St John's wort induces CYP3A4; piperine inhibits it |
| Excretion | Changed renal elimination or P-gp efflux | Liquorice changes potassium and fluid balance |
| **Pharmacodynamic** — changes the effect at the site | | |
| Additive / synergistic | Two agents with the same effect | Garlic + warfarin → bleeding; kava + benzodiazepines → sedation |
| Antagonistic | Opposing effects | Ginseng may reduce warfarin effect; ephedra opposes antihypertensives |

**Herb–food interactions** include foods that change drug or herb handling — the classic one being **grapefruit juice**, whose furanocoumarins inhibit intestinal CYP3A4 and raise levels of drugs such as some calcium-channel blockers and statins; and **tyramine-rich foods** (aged cheese, fermented foods) with MAO inhibitors. Herbs taken with food may also change food absorption — fibres slow sugar absorption, tannins reduce iron absorption.

```text
            Herb taken with drug
                    │
     ┌──────────────┴───────────────┐
 Pharmacokinetic                 Pharmacodynamic
 (drug level changes)           (drug effect changes)
     │                                │
 Induction of CYP/P-gp → ↓ level   Same effect → ↑ effect (additive)
 Inhibition of CYP/P-gp → ↑ level  Opposite effect → ↓ effect (antagonism)
```

## 2.6 Study of individual herbs: side effects and interactions

### Hypericum (St John's wort)

- **Source**: flowering tops of *Hypericum perforatum*, family Hypericaceae (Clusiaceae).
- **Constituents**: naphthodianthrones (**hypericin**, pseudohypericin), phloroglucinols (**hyperforin**), flavonoids.
- **Use**: mild to moderate depression.
- **Side effects**: **photosensitivity** (hypericin), gastrointestinal upset, restlessness.
- **Interactions**: hyperforin strongly **induces CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein**, which lowers the blood levels and effect of cyclosporine (risk of transplant rejection), digoxin, warfarin, **oral contraceptives** (breakthrough bleeding, pregnancy), HIV protease inhibitors (indinavir), theophylline and some anticancer drugs. With **SSRIs** and other serotonergic drugs it can cause **serotonin syndrome** (pharmacodynamic).

### Kava-kava

- **Source**: roots/rhizome of *Piper methysticum*, family Piperaceae (Pacific islands).
- **Constituents**: **kavalactones** (kavain, methysticin, yangonin).
- **Use**: anxiety, sleeplessness, ceremonial drink.
- **Side effects**: **liver damage** (hepatotoxicity — led to restrictions in several countries), skin scaling (kava dermopathy) with heavy use, drowsiness.
- **Interactions**: additive **CNS depression** with alcohol, benzodiazepines (alprazolam), barbiturates; may worsen parkinsonism and oppose levodopa; added risk of liver injury with other hepatotoxic drugs.

### Ginkgo biloba

- **Source**: leaves of *Ginkgo biloba*, family Ginkgoaceae; standardised extracts are used.
- **Constituents**: flavonol glycosides, terpene lactones (**ginkgolides A, B, C** and **bilobalide**).
- **Use**: memory and cognitive decline, peripheral circulation, tinnitus.
- **Side effects**: headache, gastrointestinal upset, allergic skin reactions; seeds contain a neurotoxin (ginkgotoxin) that can cause seizures.
- **Interactions**: ginkgolide B antagonises **platelet-activating factor**, so with **warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel or NSAIDs** there is a risk of bleeding; avoid before surgery; may lower the seizure threshold with drugs such as anticonvulsants.

### Ginseng

- **Side effects**: insomnia, nervousness, headache, raised blood pressure, oestrogen-like effects (breast tenderness), hypoglycaemia.
- **Interactions**: may **reduce the effect of warfarin** (lowered INR reported); with **MAO inhibitors** (phenelzine) — headache, tremor, mania; with **insulin and oral antidiabetics** — added fall in blood glucose; with stimulants — overstimulation.

### Garlic

- **Side effects**: bad breath and body odour, heartburn, flatulence, contact dermatitis.
- **Interactions**: antiplatelet effect → **bleeding** with warfarin, aspirin and other anticoagulants (stop before surgery); **reduces blood levels of saquinavir** (an HIV protease inhibitor), probably by inducing intestinal CYP3A4/P-gp; additive fall in blood glucose with antidiabetic drugs.

### Pepper

- **Source**: dried unripe fruits of *Piper nigrum* (black pepper) and *Piper longum* (long pepper), family Piperaceae.
- **Constituent**: the alkaloid **piperine**.
- **Side effects**: gastric irritation in large amounts.
- **Interactions**: piperine **inhibits CYP3A4, P-glycoprotein and glucuronidation**, and increases blood flow in the gut, so it **raises the bioavailability** of many drugs — phenytoin, propranolol, theophylline, rifampicin, nevirapine. This "**bioenhancer**" effect is used in the Ayurvedic combination **Trikatu** (black pepper, long pepper, ginger) and in some modern formulations (piperine with curcumin), but it can also push narrow-therapeutic-index drugs into toxic levels.

### Ephedra (Ma huang)

- **Source**: stems of *Ephedra sinica* and other *Ephedra* species, family Ephedraceae.
- **Constituents**: **ephedrine** and pseudoephedrine (sympathomimetic amines).
- **Use**: asthma, nasal congestion; widely misused in weight-loss and sports products.
- **Side effects**: hypertension, palpitations, arrhythmias, stroke, seizures, insomnia, anxiety; deaths have been reported, and the US FDA banned dietary supplements containing ephedra alkaloids.
- **Interactions**: with **MAO inhibitors** — hypertensive crisis; with **caffeine** and other stimulants — added cardiovascular toxicity; with **cardiac glycosides** and halogenated anaesthetics (halothane) — arrhythmias; opposes antihypertensive drugs; with ergot alkaloids or oxytocin — severe hypertension.

### Quick interaction table

| Herb | Key constituent | Main interaction | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| St John's wort | Hyperforin | CYP3A4 and P-gp induction | ↓ cyclosporine, digoxin, OCs, warfarin; serotonin syndrome with SSRIs |
| Kava-kava | Kavalactones | Additive CNS depression; liver toxicity | Sedation with benzodiazepines, alcohol |
| Ginkgo | Ginkgolide B | PAF antagonism | Bleeding with anticoagulants |
| Ginseng | Ginsenosides | Pharmacodynamic | ↓ warfarin effect; mania with MAOIs; hypoglycaemia |
| Garlic | Allicin, ajoene | Antiplatelet; CYP3A4 induction | Bleeding; ↓ saquinavir |
| Pepper | Piperine | CYP3A4, P-gp inhibition | ↑ phenytoin, theophylline, propranolol |
| Ephedra | Ephedrine | Sympathomimetic | Hypertensive crisis with MAOIs; arrhythmia |

**Role of the pharmacist**: always ask about herbal and supplement use when dispensing, especially with narrow-therapeutic-index drugs (warfarin, digoxin, cyclosporine, phenytoin, theophylline), before surgery, in pregnancy and in the elderly; report suspected interactions through pharmacovigilance.

## Remember

- Nutraceutical = food or food part with medical benefit (DeFelice, 1989); in India regulated by FSSAI as food.
- Types: dietary supplements, functional foods, herbal products, probiotics/prebiotics/synbiotics.
- Diabetes: fenugreek, fibre, cinnamon; CVS: omega-3, garlic, phytosterols, fibre; cancer: lycopene, curcumin, EGCG, sulforaphane; IBS/GI: probiotics, psyllium, peppermint oil.
- Alfalfa — *Medicago sativa* (L-canavanine); chicory — inulin; ginger — gingerols/shogaols; fenugreek — galactomannan, 4-hydroxyisoleucine, diosgenin; garlic — alliin → allicin by alliinase; honey — invert sugar, not for infants; amla — vitamin C, emblicanins; ginseng — ginsenosides, adaptogen; ashwagandha — withanolides; spirulina — protein, phycocyanin, GLA.
- Interactions: pharmacokinetic (CYP3A4/P-gp induction or inhibition) and pharmacodynamic (additive or antagonistic).
- Hypericum induces CYP3A4 (↓ drug levels); piperine inhibits it (↑ drug levels, Trikatu bioenhancer).
- Ginkgo and garlic → bleeding with anticoagulants; kava → liver injury and sedation; ephedra → hypertensive crisis with MAOIs.
- Grapefruit juice inhibits CYP3A4 — the classic food–drug interaction.

# Evaluation of excipients of natural origin

**Aim:** To evaluate given natural excipients (for example starch, acacia, tragacanth, guar gum, isabgol husk or sodium alginate) for identity, purity and functional properties.

> Follow your lab manual for exact quantities and the excipient monographs in the Indian Pharmacopoeia for limits.

## Principle

Natural excipients vary with source, season and processing, and can carry moisture, ash and microbes. Before use in a formulation they are tested for **identity** (chemical tests, microscopy), **purity** (loss on drying, ash, pH, foreign matter) and **functional properties** that decide how they behave in a dosage form — swelling, viscosity, flow, bulk density and, for disintegrants, swelling capacity and water uptake.

## Requirements

**Samples:** maize or potato starch, acacia, tragacanth, guar gum, isabgol husk, sodium alginate (as available).

**Chemicals:** iodine solution (0.005 M), lead acetate, ruthenium red, ethanol (95%), dilute HCl, purified water, buffer tablets for pH meter.

**Apparatus:** hot-air oven, silica crucibles, muffle furnace, analytical balance, 25 ml stoppered measuring cylinders, 100 ml measuring cylinder, Brookfield or Ostwald viscometer, pH meter, funnel and stand for angle of repose, tapping device, microscope.

## Procedure

1. **Organoleptic and microscopic identity**
   - Note colour, odour, taste and texture.
   - Starch: mount in water and iodine; observe grain shape and hilum (maize — polygonal with central hilum; potato — large ovoid with eccentric hilum and striations); blue-black colour with iodine.
   - Gums: ruthenium red stains mucilage pink; acacia gives a white precipitate with lead subacetate (basic lead acetate); tragacanth swells to a gel and gives a yellow colour on warming with sodium hydroxide solution.
2. **Loss on drying**: dry 1–2 g at 105 °C to constant weight.
3. **Total ash and acid-insoluble ash**: ignite 2 g in a tared crucible at not more than 450 °C until carbon-free; boil the ash with 25 ml dilute HCl, collect the insoluble part on ashless filter paper, ignite and weigh.
4. **pH**: of a 1% (or monograph-specified) dispersion in water.
5. **Swelling index**: place 1 g in a 25 ml stoppered cylinder, add water to 25 ml, shake every 10 minutes for 1 hour and allow to stand (24 h for isabgol); read the volume occupied by the swollen material.
6. **Viscosity**: prepare a 1% w/v dispersion (or as specified), allow to hydrate, and measure viscosity with the viscometer at a fixed temperature and spindle speed.
7. **Micromeritic properties** (powders such as starch):
   - Bulk density = mass ÷ untapped volume; tapped density = mass ÷ tapped volume.
   - Carr's index = (tapped − bulk density) × 100 ÷ tapped density; Hausner ratio = tapped ÷ bulk density.
   - Angle of repose: pour powder through a fixed funnel; tan θ = h ÷ r.
8. **Microbial limit** (if facilities exist): total aerobic count and absence of *E. coli* and *Salmonella* as per the monograph.

## Observations and calculations

| Test | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Loss on drying (%) | (initial weight − dried weight) × 100 ÷ initial weight |
| Total ash (%) | weight of ash × 100 ÷ weight of sample |
| Swelling index (ml/g) | volume of swollen material ÷ weight taken |
| Carr's index (%) | (ρtapped − ρbulk) × 100 ÷ ρtapped |
| Angle of repose | θ = tan⁻¹ (h ÷ r) |

**Worked example (starch).** 20 g starch: untapped volume 40 ml, tapped volume 32 ml.
ρbulk = 20/40 = 0.50 g/ml; ρtapped = 20/32 = 0.625 g/ml.
Carr's index = (0.625 − 0.50) × 100 ÷ 0.625 = **20%** (fair flow); Hausner ratio = 0.625/0.50 = **1.25**.
Heap height 2.5 cm, radius 4.0 cm: tan θ = 0.625, θ ≈ **32°** (passable flow).

**Worked example (isabgol husk).** 1 g swelled to 40 ml → swelling index = **40 ml/g**.

## Precautions

1. Disperse gums slowly with stirring to avoid lumps; allow full hydration before measuring viscosity.
2. Measure viscosity at a fixed temperature, since it falls as temperature rises.
3. Ignite ash at a controlled temperature and cool in a desiccator before weighing.
4. Use the same tapping method and number of taps for all samples.

## Viva questions

1. **Why must natural excipients be standardised?** — Their properties vary with source and processing; microbial load and moisture also vary.
2. **What does a high acid-insoluble ash indicate?** — Contamination with sand or earthy matter (silica).
3. **What does Carr's index tell you?** — Flowability and compressibility; lower values mean better flow.
4. **Why is swelling index important for isabgol?** — Its laxative action depends on swelling of mucilage.
5. **Name a natural disintegrant and a natural binder.** — Starch (disintegrant); acacia or starch paste (binder).
6. **How is maize starch distinguished from potato starch under the microscope?** — Maize grains are small and polygonal with a central hilum; potato grains are large, ovoid, with eccentric hilum and clear striations.

# Determination of phenol content of a volatile oil

**Aim:** To determine the phenol content of a given volatile oil — for example eugenol in clove oil — by the cassia flask (absorption) method, and to estimate total phenolics in a herbal extract by the Folin–Ciocalteu method.

> Follow your lab manual and the oil's monograph for exact quantities. Potassium hydroxide is corrosive; wear gloves and goggles.

## Principle

**Method A — cassia flask method (volumetric).** Phenols are weak acids. They react with aqueous **potassium hydroxide** to form water-soluble potassium phenoxides (for example potassium eugenolate), while non-phenolic constituents (such as caryophyllene and eugenyl acetate) stay as an oily layer. When a known volume of oil is shaken with KOH solution in a cassia flask, the phenols dissolve and the volume of unabsorbed oil read in the graduated neck gives, by difference, the volume of phenols.

**Method B — Folin–Ciocalteu method (spectrophotometric, total phenolics).** Phenolic compounds reduce the Folin–Ciocalteu reagent (phosphomolybdic–phosphotungstic acid) in alkaline medium to a blue complex measured at about 760 nm. The result is expressed as gallic acid equivalents (GAE) from a standard curve.

## Requirements

**Method A:** clove oil, 1 M (about 5–6% w/v) potassium hydroxide solution, cassia flask (graduated neck in 0.1 ml), 10 ml pipette, water bath.

**Method B:** herbal extract, Folin–Ciocalteu reagent, 20% sodium carbonate solution, gallic acid standard, volumetric flasks, UV-visible spectrophotometer.

## Procedure

**Method A (clove oil)**
1. Pipette **10 ml** of clove oil into the cassia flask.
2. Add about 75 ml of potassium hydroxide solution, stopper and shake for 5 minutes.
3. Warm on a water bath for about 10 minutes, shaking occasionally, to complete the reaction.
4. Add more KOH solution to bring the oily layer into the graduated neck.
5. Allow to stand (until the layers separate clearly, often several hours or overnight).
6. Read the volume of unabsorbed oil in the neck.

**Method B (total phenolics)**
1. Prepare gallic acid standards (e.g. 10–100 µg/ml).
2. To 1 ml of each standard or diluted extract add Folin–Ciocalteu reagent (diluted as directed) and, after a few minutes, sodium carbonate solution; make up to volume.
3. Keep in the dark for the time stated; measure absorbance at about 760 nm against a reagent blank.
4. Plot the standard curve and read the concentration of the sample.

## Observations and calculations

**Method A.**
% phenols (v/v) = (10 − volume of unabsorbed oil) × 100 ÷ 10

**Worked example.** Unabsorbed oil = 1.5 ml → (10 − 1.5) × 10 = **85% v/v** phenols (as eugenol).

**Method B.**
Total phenolics (mg GAE/g extract) = C × V × D ÷ W
where C = concentration from curve (mg/ml), V = volume of extract solution (ml), D = dilution factor, W = weight of extract (g).

**Worked example.** 50 mg extract dissolved in 50 ml (1 mg/ml), diluted 1 in 2; curve gives 0.040 mg/ml → C × V × D ÷ W = 0.040 × 50 × 2 ÷ 0.050 = **80 mg GAE/g**.

## Precautions

1. Shake well and warm so that all phenols react with the alkali.
2. Let the layers separate completely before reading; read at the bottom of the meniscus.
3. Keep the flask upright and at room temperature while reading.
4. In Method B, keep the reaction time and light conditions the same for standards and samples.

## Viva questions

1. **Why do phenols dissolve in KOH?** — They are weakly acidic and form water-soluble potassium phenoxides.
2. **What is the main phenol of clove oil?** — Eugenol.
3. **What remains undissolved in the cassia flask?** — Non-phenolic constituents such as caryophyllene and eugenyl acetate.
4. **Name another oil whose phenols can be estimated this way.** — Thyme or ajowan oil (thymol).
5. **In what units are total phenolics expressed?** — Gallic acid equivalents (mg GAE per g).
6. **Why should the Folin–Ciocalteu reaction be kept in the dark?** — The blue complex and reagent are light-sensitive; light changes the absorbance.

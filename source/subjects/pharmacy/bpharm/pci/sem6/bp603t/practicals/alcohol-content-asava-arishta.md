# Determination of the alcohol content of Asava and Arishta

**Aim:** To determine the percentage of ethanol (self-produced alcohol) in a given Asava or Arishta by distillation and specific gravity.

> Follow your lab manual and the monograph of the product for exact volumes and limits. Keep flames away from the distillate.

## Principle

Asava and Arishta contain alcohol produced by fermentation of sugar. Because the liquid also contains sugars, extractives and acids, its specific gravity cannot be used directly. A measured volume is therefore **distilled**: ethanol (with water) passes into the distillate, leaving the dissolved solids behind. The distillate is made up to a known volume with water, its **specific gravity** is measured with a pycnometer (specific gravity bottle) at a fixed temperature, and the corresponding ethanol concentration is read from the **alcoholometric table** of the pharmacopoeia. The result is corrected for any dilution.

```text
25 ml sample + 25 ml water ──distil──→ distillate collected (≈ 45–48 ml)
                                        │ make up to 50 ml with water at 20 °C
                                        ▼
                         specific gravity by pycnometer
                                        │ alcoholometric table
                                        ▼
                     % v/v ethanol in distillate × (50 / 25) = % v/v in sample
```

## Requirements

**Sample:** a marketed Asava or Arishta (for example Drakshasava, Dashamularishta, Ashokarishta).

**Apparatus:** distillation flask (about 200–250 ml) with condenser and adapter, 50 ml volumetric flask (receiver), 25 ml pipette, pycnometer (specific gravity bottle), analytical balance, thermometer, water bath kept at 20 °C (or the temperature of the table), heating mantle, anti-bumping chips (pumice).

**Chemicals:** purified water; a little antifoam (liquid paraffin or a trace of silicone) if the sample foams; sodium hydroxide or dilute acid to neutralise volatile acids or bases if the monograph asks.

## Procedure

1. Mix the sample well and bring it to 20 °C.
2. Pipette **25 ml** of the sample into the distillation flask and add about **25 ml water** and a few pieces of pumice. (Add antifoam if the sample foams.)
3. Fit the condenser and collect the distillate in a 50 ml volumetric flask kept in cold water, with the end of the adapter dipping just below a little water in the receiver.
4. Distil steadily until about 45–48 ml of distillate has been collected (not less than nine-tenths of the final volume).
5. Bring the distillate to 20 °C and make up to **50 ml** with water; mix well.
6. Weigh the clean, dry pycnometer empty (W1), filled with water at 20 °C (W2), and filled with distillate at 20 °C (W3).
7. Calculate the specific gravity and read the corresponding ethanol content (% v/v) from the alcoholometric table.
8. Multiply by the dilution factor (50/25 = 2) to get the alcohol content of the sample.

## Observations and calculations

| Item | Weight (g) |
| --- | --- |
| Empty pycnometer (W1) |  |
| Pycnometer + water (W2) |  |
| Pycnometer + distillate (W3) |  |

Specific gravity of distillate = (W3 − W1) ÷ (W2 − W1)

% v/v ethanol in sample = (% v/v from table for the distillate) × (volume of distillate ÷ volume of sample taken)

**Worked example.** W1 = 20.000 g; W2 = 45.000 g; W3 = 44.875 g.
Specific gravity = 24.875 ÷ 25.000 = **0.9950**.
Suppose the table gives about **3.5% v/v** ethanol for this specific gravity at 20 °C. Sample alcohol = 3.5 × 50/25 = **7.0% v/v**.
(Use the table in your pharmacopoeia; the value above only illustrates the arithmetic.)

## Precautions

1. Measure the sample and the distillate at the same temperature as the alcoholometric table.
2. Keep the receiver cold and the joints tight to avoid loss of ethanol vapour.
3. Distil slowly to prevent frothing and carry-over of the sample.
4. The pycnometer must be clean and dry; wipe the outside and avoid air bubbles.
5. Never heat the flask to dryness.

## Viva questions

1. **What is self-produced alcohol?** — Ethanol produced inside the product by fermentation of its sugars, not added from outside.
2. **Why distil instead of measuring specific gravity directly?** — Sugars and extractives raise the specific gravity and would hide the alcohol.
3. **Why is the distillate made up to the original or a known volume?** — To relate the alcohol found back to the volume of sample taken.
4. **What is the role of dhataki flowers?** — They carry yeasts that start fermentation.
5. **Why should methanol be absent?** — It is toxic; its presence suggests improper fermentation or adulteration.
6. **Why does alcohol content matter?** — It shows that fermentation was complete and acts as preservative and solvent; too much suggests added alcohol.

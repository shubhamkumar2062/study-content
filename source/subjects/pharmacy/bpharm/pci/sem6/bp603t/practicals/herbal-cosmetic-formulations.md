# Incorporation of a standardised extract in a cream, lotion and shampoo and their evaluation

**Aim:** To prepare and standardise a herbal extract and incorporate it into a vanishing cream, a lotion and a herbal shampoo, and to evaluate the products.

> Follow your lab manual for exact formulae. Use preservatives as directed and do a patch test only under supervision.

## Principle

A **standardised extract** has a fixed drug-to-extract ratio and a known content of a marker compound (for example curcumin in turmeric extract, or glycyrrhizin in liquorice extract), so every batch of the product delivers the same amount. The extract is incorporated into a suitable base:

- **Cream** — an emulsion (here an oil-in-water vanishing cream made by saponifying stearic acid with an alkali to form an emulsifying soap).
- **Lotion** — a fluid emulsion or solution for spreading on skin.
- **Shampoo** — a cleansing liquid; herbal shampoos use plant saponins (shikakai, reetha) with or without a mild surfactant.

Products are then checked for appearance, pH, spreadability, viscosity, stability and, for shampoos, foaming, cleaning and wetting.

## Requirements

**Extract:** e.g. turmeric, neem, aloe or liquorice extract, standardised (yield, extractive value, marker by UV/TLC).

**Cream:** stearic acid, cetyl alcohol, potassium hydroxide (or triethanolamine), glycerin, methyl and propyl paraben, perfume, purified water.

**Lotion:** liquid paraffin or a vegetable oil, emulsifier (e.g. triethanolamine stearate), glycerin, preservative, water.

**Shampoo:** aqueous extract of shikakai and reetha (with amla or hibiscus), small amount of mild surfactant if directed, thickener (guar or sodium CMC), citric acid to adjust pH, preservative, perfume.

**Apparatus:** water bath, beakers, mortar and pestle, homogeniser or stirrer, pH meter, Brookfield viscometer, glass slides and weights (spreadability), 250 ml measuring cylinder, stopwatch, centrifuge.

## Procedure

**A. Standardise the extract**: record percentage yield, colour, solubility, loss on drying and marker content (UV absorbance against a standard curve, or a TLC spot matching the reference).

**B. Vanishing cream**
1. Melt stearic acid and cetyl alcohol at about 70–75 °C (oil phase).
2. Dissolve potassium hydroxide, glycerin and parabens in water and heat to the same temperature (aqueous phase).
3. Add the aqueous phase to the oil phase slowly with continuous stirring until it emulsifies and cools.
4. At about 40 °C add the extract (dissolved or dispersed in a little glycerin) and perfume; stir till uniform.

**C. Lotion**: prepare as an emulsion with more water and less oil/wax, add extract at about 40 °C, homogenise and fill.

**D. Herbal shampoo**
1. Prepare a decoction or aqueous extract of shikakai and reetha; filter.
2. Disperse the thickener; add the standardised extract, preservative and perfume.
3. Adjust pH to about 5.5–7 with citric acid; make up to volume and fill.

## Observations and calculations

Spreadability, S = M × L ÷ T, where M = weight on the upper slide (g), L = length the slide moves (cm), T = time taken (s).

**Worked example.** M = 50 g, L = 7.5 cm, T = 15 s → S = 50 × 7.5 ÷ 15 = **25 g·cm/s**.

Foam test: 50 ml of 1% shampoo in a 250 ml cylinder, shaken 10 times; foam volume read at 0 and 5 min. Foam stability (%) = foam volume at 5 min × 100 ÷ foam volume at 0 min.

**Worked example.** 160 ml at 0 min, 140 ml at 5 min → stability = **87.5%**.

## Precautions

1. Keep both phases at the same temperature before mixing.
2. Add heat-sensitive extracts and perfume below about 40 °C.
3. Stir continuously while the cream cools to avoid grainy texture.
4. Adjust pH carefully; skin products should be close to skin pH.
5. Use clean equipment and preservatives to prevent microbial growth.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is the extract standardised before use?** — So each batch contains the same amount of marker/active.
2. **What is the emulsifier in a vanishing cream?** — Potassium (or triethanolamine) stearate formed in situ from stearic acid.
3. **Which natural ingredients make herbal shampoo lather?** — Saponins of shikakai and reetha.
4. **How is spreadability measured?** — By the time taken for a weighted slide to slip over a known length of cream.
5. **Why is a shampoo kept near neutral pH?** — To avoid irritation of scalp and eyes and damage to hair.
6. **How do you tell an o/w from a w/o cream?** — An o/w cream dilutes with water and takes up a water-soluble dye in its continuous phase.

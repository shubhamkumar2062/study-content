# Determination of total alkaloids in a crude drug

**Aim:** To determine the total alkaloid content of a given crude drug (for example belladonna or datura leaf) by acid–base extraction and back-titration, and to express it as hyoscyamine.

> Follow your lab manual and the drug's monograph for exact quantities. Chloroform and ether are toxic and flammable: use a fume hood and no flames.

## Principle

Alkaloids occur in plants mostly as salts. When the powdered drug is moistened with **ammonia**, the alkaloids are set free as bases, which dissolve in an **organic solvent** (ether–chloroform). They are then extracted from the solvent into **dilute acid** as salts, leaving neutral impurities behind; the acid solution is made alkaline again and the purified bases are taken back into chloroform (shake-out purification). The solvent is evaporated, the residue is dissolved in a measured **excess of standard sulphuric acid**, and the unused acid is titrated with **standard sodium hydroxide** using methyl red. The acid used up corresponds to the total alkaloids.

```text
Drug powder + NH3 (free bases) → extract with ether–chloroform
      → shake with dilute H2SO4 (alkaloid salts in acid layer)
      → make alkaline with NH3 → extract with chloroform (purified bases)
      → evaporate → dissolve residue in excess 0.02 N H2SO4
      → back-titrate excess acid with 0.02 N NaOH (methyl red)
```

## Requirements

**Sample:** belladonna or datura leaf, finely powdered (about 10 g).

**Chemicals:** dilute ammonia solution, solvent ether, chloroform, 0.5 N (or dilute) sulphuric acid for shake-out, 0.02 N sulphuric acid (standardised), 0.02 N sodium hydroxide (standardised), methyl red indicator, anhydrous sodium sulphate, neutral ethanol.

**Apparatus:** Soxhlet or stoppered percolation/shaking flask, separating funnels, conical flasks, water bath, burettes, pipettes, analytical balance.

## Procedure

1. Weigh accurately about 10 g of the powdered drug; moisten with dilute ammonia and a little ether–chloroform; mix and allow to stand.
2. Extract the alkaloids completely with ether–chloroform (by shaking or continuous extraction).
3. Transfer the extract to a separating funnel and shake with successive portions of dilute sulphuric acid until extraction is complete (a drop of the last acid portion gives no precipitate with Mayer's reagent). Combine the acid layers.
4. Make the combined acid solution alkaline with ammonia and shake out with successive portions of chloroform until complete. Combine and filter the chloroform layers through anhydrous sodium sulphate.
5. Evaporate the chloroform on a water bath; dry the residue briefly (a little ethanol can be added and evaporated to remove traces of ammonia).
6. Dissolve the residue in an accurately measured excess of 0.02 N sulphuric acid (e.g. 20 ml), warming gently.
7. Titrate the excess acid with 0.02 N sodium hydroxide using methyl red as indicator.
8. Run a blank titration on 20 ml of the 0.02 N acid.

## Observations and calculations

Volume of 0.02 N H2SO4 used by alkaloids = (acid added) − (NaOH used in back-titration), corrected for exact normalities.

1 ml of 0.02 N H2SO4 ≡ 5.788 mg of total alkaloids calculated as hyoscyamine (C17H23NO3, M = 289.4; 289.4 × 0.02 = 5.788).

% total alkaloids = (ml of acid used × 5.788 × 100) ÷ (1000 × weight of drug in g)

**Worked example.** Drug = 10.00 g; 0.02 N H2SO4 added = 20.0 ml; 0.02 N NaOH in back-titration = 14.5 ml.
Acid used by alkaloids = 20.0 − 14.5 = 5.5 ml.
Alkaloids = 5.5 × 5.788 = 31.83 mg.
% total alkaloids = 31.83 × 100 ÷ (1000 × 10.00) = **0.32% w/w** (as hyoscyamine).

## Precautions

1. Make sure extraction is complete at each stage (test with Mayer's reagent).
2. Remove all ammonia before dissolving the residue in standard acid, or the result will be low.
3. Avoid emulsions — swirl the separating funnel gently and allow time to separate.
4. Standardise the acid and alkali and run a blank.
5. Do not overheat the alkaloid residue; tropane esters decompose.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is ammonia added to the drug first?** — To liberate the alkaloids from their salts as free bases soluble in organic solvents.
2. **Why is a shake-out into acid and back into chloroform done?** — To separate alkaloids from neutral and acidic impurities.
3. **Why is back-titration used?** — Alkaloids are weak bases; titrating excess strong acid gives a sharper end point.
4. **Which indicator is used and why?** — Methyl red; its acidic range suits the weak-base salt end point.
5. **What is the equivalent of 1 ml 0.02 N H2SO4 for hyoscyamine?** — 5.788 mg.
6. **Name the main alkaloids of belladonna.** — Hyoscyamine (racemising to atropine) and hyoscine.

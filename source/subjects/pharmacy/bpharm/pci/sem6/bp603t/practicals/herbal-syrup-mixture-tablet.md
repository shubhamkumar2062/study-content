# Incorporation of a standardised extract in a syrup, mixture and tablet and their evaluation

**Aim:** To incorporate a standardised herbal extract into a syrup, a mixture (suspension) and a tablet, and to evaluate them as per pharmacopoeial requirements.

> Follow your lab manual for exact formulae and the Indian Pharmacopoeia for test conditions and limits.

## Principle

Herbal extracts are coloured, often bitter, may be hygroscopic or sticky, and contain many compounds. A **standardised extract** (fixed drug-to-extract ratio and known marker content) is chosen so that each dose delivers a constant amount. It is then formulated:

- **Syrup** — extract dissolved in a concentrated sugar solution, which masks taste and resists microbes.
- **Mixture** — insoluble extract or powder kept uniformly suspended with a natural suspending agent.
- **Tablet** — extract granulated with diluent, binder and disintegrant, then compressed.

## Requirements

**Extract:** e.g. vasaka, tulsi, ashwagandha or liquorice dry extract (standardised).

**Syrup:** sucrose, purified water, methyl and propyl paraben (or sodium benzoate), flavour, colour.

**Mixture:** extract or powdered drug, tragacanth or acacia (or sodium CMC), syrup, preservative, flavour, water.

**Tablet:** dry extract, microcrystalline cellulose or starch (diluent), starch paste or acacia mucilage (binder), starch or sodium starch glycolate (disintegrant), colloidal silica (adsorbent for sticky extract), talc and magnesium stearate.

**Apparatus:** water bath, beakers, mortar and pestle, sieves (no. 10, 16 or 22), tray dryer, single-punch tablet machine, Monsanto/Pfizer hardness tester, Roche friabilator, disintegration test apparatus, pH meter, pycnometer, viscometer, 100 ml measuring cylinders, UV spectrophotometer.

## Procedure

**A. Syrup**
1. Dissolve the sucrose in about half the water with gentle heat; filter; cool.
2. Dissolve the extract and preservatives in a little water (or warm water) and add to the syrup with stirring.
3. Add flavour and colour; make up to volume with syrup or water; filter and fill into amber bottles.

**B. Mixture (suspension)**
1. Triturate the extract/powder with the suspending agent in a mortar.
2. Add a little vehicle to form a smooth paste, then the rest of the syrup, preservative and flavour with trituration.
3. Make up to volume; transfer to a bottle; label "Shake well before use".

**C. Tablets (wet granulation)**
1. Mix the extract, diluent, adsorbent and half the disintegrant.
2. Add binder solution gradually to form a damp mass; pass through a coarse sieve.
3. Dry the granules at about 50–60 °C; sieve again.
4. Add the rest of the disintegrant, talc and magnesium stearate; mix gently.
5. Compress to the target weight.

**D. Evaluation**

| Dosage form | Tests |
| --- | --- |
| Syrup | Appearance and clarity, pH, specific gravity, viscosity, sugar content, marker content, fill volume, microbial limit |
| Mixture | Sedimentation volume, redispersibility, viscosity, pH, particle size, marker content |
| Granules | Bulk and tapped density, Carr's index, angle of repose, moisture |
| Tablets | Appearance, uniformity of weight, thickness, hardness, friability, disintegration time, marker content |

IP limits to compare: **uniformity of weight** for uncoated tablets — 80 mg or less ±10%, more than 80 mg and less than 250 mg ±7.5%, 250 mg or more ±5% (not more than two tablets outside the limit and none outside twice the limit); **friability** — loss not more than 1%; **disintegration** — uncoated tablets within 15 minutes unless the monograph states otherwise.

## Observations and calculations

Sedimentation volume, F = Hu ÷ Ho (Hu = final height of sediment, Ho = original height of suspension).
**Worked example.** Ho = 100 ml, Hu = 85 ml after 7 days → F = **0.85**.

Friability (%) = (initial weight − final weight) × 100 ÷ initial weight.
**Worked example.** 20 tablets: 6.500 g before, 6.465 g after 100 rotations → (0.035 × 100) ÷ 6.500 = **0.54%** (passes, ≤ 1%).

Uniformity of weight: average of 20 tablets = 400 mg; limit ±5% → 380–420 mg.

## Precautions

1. Do not overheat the sugar solution (caramelisation, inversion).
2. Add the extract to cooled syrup if it is heat-sensitive.
3. Do not over-wet the granulation mass; dry granules at a moderate temperature.
4. Store hygroscopic extract granules in closed containers; add adsorbent if sticky.
5. Calibrate the friabilator and disintegration apparatus before use.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is a standardised extract used?** — To ensure the same amount of marker/active in every dose.
2. **Why does a syrup resist microbial growth?** — High sugar concentration gives high osmotic pressure; preservatives are still added.
3. **What does a sedimentation volume of 1 mean?** — The suspension did not settle at all (flocculated, ideal).
4. **Why are herbal extracts difficult to tablet?** — They are hygroscopic, sticky, bulky and bitter.
5. **What is the IP friability limit?** — Weight loss not more than 1%.
6. **Name two natural binders.** — Starch paste and acacia mucilage.

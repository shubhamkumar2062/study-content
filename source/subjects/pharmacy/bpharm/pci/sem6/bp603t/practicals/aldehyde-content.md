# Determination of aldehyde content of a volatile oil

**Aim:** To determine the aldehyde content of a given volatile oil — for example citral in lemongrass oil or cinnamaldehyde in cinnamon (cassia) oil.

> Follow your lab manual and the oil's monograph for exact quantities. Work in a fume hood and keep the oil and ethanol away from flames.

## Principle

**Method A — hydroxylamine method (titrimetric).** Aldehydes react with hydroxylamine hydrochloride to form oximes, and an equivalent amount of hydrochloric acid is set free:

R–CHO + NH2OH·HCl → R–CH=NOH + H2O + HCl

The liberated HCl is titrated with standard ethanolic potassium hydroxide using an indicator (bromophenol blue) until the colour of the neutral reagent returns. A blank is run on the reagent. One mole of KOH equals one mole of aldehyde.

**Method B — bisulphite (cassia flask) method (volumetric).** Aldehydes such as cinnamaldehyde form water-soluble addition compounds with hot sodium bisulphite solution. The oil is shaken with bisulphite solution in a cassia flask; the aldehyde dissolves and the unreacted part of the oil rises into the graduated neck, where its volume is read.

## Requirements

**Sample:** lemongrass oil (citral) or cinnamon/cassia oil (cinnamaldehyde).

**Method A:** hydroxylamine hydrochloride solution in 60% ethanol, bromophenol blue indicator, 0.5 M ethanolic KOH (standardised), ethanol.

**Method B:** 30% w/v sodium bisulphite solution (freshly prepared), cassia flask (about 150 ml, with a long neck graduated in 0.1 ml), water bath.

**Apparatus:** analytical balance, 100 ml stoppered conical flasks, burette, pipettes.

## Procedure

**Method A (hydroxylamine)**
1. Weigh accurately about 1 g of oil into a stoppered flask.
2. Add a measured excess of hydroxylamine hydrochloride solution (e.g. 25 ml) and a few drops of bromophenol blue.
3. Stopper, shake and allow to stand for the time stated (shorter for reactive aldehydes, longer for less reactive ones).
4. Titrate the liberated HCl with 0.5 M ethanolic KOH until the colour matches that of the neutral reagent (or a reference flask of reagent).
5. Run a blank with the same volume of reagent and no oil.

**Method B (bisulphite)**
1. Pipette 10 ml of oil into the cassia flask.
2. Add about 50 ml of 30% sodium bisulphite solution, stopper and shake; heat in a boiling water bath, shaking from time to time, until the solid addition product dissolves.
3. Add more bisulphite solution to bring the unreacted oil into the graduated neck; allow to cool and separate.
4. Read the volume of the oily layer in the neck.

## Observations and calculations

**Method A.**
% aldehyde (w/w) = (V × M × E) ÷ (10 × W)
where V = volume of KOH used by the sample minus blank (ml), M = molarity of KOH, E = molecular weight of the aldehyde (citral 152.2; cinnamaldehyde 132.2), W = weight of oil (g).

**Worked example (lemongrass oil).** W = 1.000 g; sample titre 10.4 ml, blank 0.4 ml → V = 10.0 ml; M = 0.5.
% citral = (10.0 × 0.5 × 152.2) ÷ (10 × 1.000) = **76.1% w/w**.

**Method B.**
% aldehyde (v/v) = (10 − volume of unabsorbed oil) × 100 ÷ 10

**Worked example (cassia oil).** Unabsorbed oil = 2.2 ml → (10 − 2.2) × 10 = **78% v/v**.

## Precautions

1. Use freshly prepared bisulphite solution; old solution loses strength.
2. Standardise the ethanolic KOH and always run a blank.
3. Allow full reaction time before titrating.
4. Read the oil volume only after complete separation and cooling.
5. Avoid loss of volatile oil by keeping flasks stoppered.

## Viva questions

1. **Which aldehyde is the main constituent of lemongrass oil?** — Citral (a mixture of geranial and neral).
2. **What is liberated when an aldehyde reacts with hydroxylamine hydrochloride?** — Hydrochloric acid, equivalent to the aldehyde.
3. **Why is a blank needed?** — To correct for any acidity in the reagent itself.
4. **Why does the aldehyde dissolve in sodium bisulphite?** — It forms a water-soluble bisulphite addition compound.
5. **What is a cassia flask?** — A flask with a narrow graduated neck used to read the volume of oil not absorbed by the reagent.
6. **Name the aldehyde of cinnamon oil.** — Cinnamaldehyde.

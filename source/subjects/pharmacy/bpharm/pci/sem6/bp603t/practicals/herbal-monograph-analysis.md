# Monograph analysis of a herbal drug from a recent pharmacopoeia

**Aim:** To analyse a given herbal drug (for example ginger, turmeric, senna or liquorice) as per its monograph in the Indian Pharmacopoeia or the Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India, and to decide whether it complies.

> Use the current monograph of your drug for the exact tests and limits. Values below are only examples.

## Principle

A **pharmacopoeial monograph** defines a drug and lists the tests it must pass: description (macroscopy), identification (microscopy, chemical tests, TLC), and quantitative standards — foreign matter, loss on drying, total ash, acid-insoluble ash, water-soluble ash, alcohol- and water-soluble extractive values, volatile oil content and assay of constituents. A sample that meets all the requirements is of pharmacopoeial quality; failure in one test means it does not comply.

```text
Sample → description → identity (macro, micro, chemical, TLC)
       → purity (foreign matter, LOD, ash values)
       → content (extractives, volatile oil, assay)
       → compare each result with the monograph limit → complies / does not comply
```

## Requirements

**Sample:** coarse powder of the given drug (and whole drug for macroscopy).

**Chemicals:** chloral hydrate, phloroglucinol, conc. HCl, iodine solution, dilute HCl, ethanol (95%), chloroform water, TLC plates (silica gel G), mobile phase and reference standard as given in the monograph.

**Apparatus:** microscope, hot-air oven, muffle furnace, silica crucibles, ashless filter paper, stoppered conical flasks, mechanical shaker, evaporating dishes, Clevenger apparatus (for volatile oil drugs), TLC chamber, UV lamp, analytical balance, desiccator.

## Procedure

1. **Description**: record colour, odour, taste, size, shape, surface and fracture; compare with the monograph.
2. **Microscopy**: clear sections or powder in chloral hydrate; stain with phloroglucinol–HCl (lignin) and iodine (starch); identify diagnostic characters.
3. **Foreign matter**: spread a weighed sample (e.g. 100 g), remove foreign organic matter and mineral matter by hand, weigh.
4. **Loss on drying**: heat about 5 g at 105 °C to constant weight (use toluene distillation for drugs rich in volatile oil, if the monograph says so).
5. **Total ash**: ignite about 2–3 g in a tared silica crucible at not more than 450 °C until free from carbon; cool in a desiccator; weigh.
6. **Acid-insoluble ash**: boil the total ash with 25 ml dilute HCl for 5 minutes; collect insoluble matter on ashless filter paper; wash with hot water; ignite and weigh.
7. **Water-soluble ash**: boil total ash with 25 ml water, collect insoluble matter, ignite; subtract from total ash.
8. **Alcohol-soluble extractive**: macerate 5 g with 100 ml ethanol in a closed flask for 24 hours, shaking frequently for the first 6 hours; filter; evaporate 25 ml of filtrate to dryness at 105 °C; weigh.
9. **Water-soluble extractive**: same, using chloroform water.
10. **Volatile oil content**: hydrodistil a weighed quantity in a Clevenger apparatus for the time stated; read the volume of oil.
11. **TLC**: run sample and reference extracts side by side as in the monograph; compare spots and Rf values.

## Observations and calculations

| Parameter | Formula |
| --- | --- |
| Foreign matter (%) | wt. of foreign matter × 100 ÷ wt. of sample |
| Loss on drying (%) | loss in weight × 100 ÷ wt. of sample |
| Total ash (%) | wt. of ash × 100 ÷ wt. of sample |
| Acid-insoluble ash (%) | wt. of acid-insoluble residue × 100 ÷ wt. of sample |
| Extractive value (%) | wt. of residue from 25 ml × 4 × 100 ÷ wt. of sample |
| Volatile oil (% v/w) | ml of oil × 100 ÷ wt. of sample |

**Worked example (ginger rhizome).**
- Total ash: 2.000 g sample gave 0.110 g ash → **5.5%**.
- Acid-insoluble ash: 0.018 g residue → 0.018 × 100 ÷ 2.000 = **0.9%**.
- Alcohol-soluble extractive: 5.000 g drug; residue from 25 ml = 0.060 g → 0.060 × 4 × 100 ÷ 5.000 = **4.8%**.
- Volatile oil: 50 g gave 0.8 ml → **1.6% v/w**.

## Precautions

1. Use the edition of the monograph you have been given and follow its exact conditions.
2. Ignite ash slowly and at the stated temperature; avoid fusing.
3. Cool crucibles in a desiccator before every weighing; weigh to constant weight.
4. Keep extraction flasks closed to avoid solvent loss.
5. Use ashless filter paper for acid-insoluble ash.

## Viva questions

1. **What does high acid-insoluble ash show?** — Contamination with sand, soil or silica.
2. **Why are extractive values determined?** — To detect exhausted or adulterated drugs and to judge the amount of extractable constituents.
3. **Why is chloroform water used for water-soluble extractive?** — Chloroform acts as a preservative and prevents microbial growth during maceration.
4. **Which apparatus is used for volatile oil content?** — Clevenger apparatus.
5. **What is a monograph?** — The official written standard of a drug in a pharmacopoeia, with definition, tests and limits.
6. **Name the official pharmacopoeia for Ayurvedic drugs.** — The Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India.

# Unit 3: Chemotherapy of tuberculosis, leprosy, fungal, viral, worm, malarial and amoebic infections

This unit continues chemotherapy with seven groups of drugs. Each group is examined the same way: classification, mechanism, uses, adverse effects. First-line antitubercular drugs, antimalarials and antiretroviral drugs are the favourite 10-mark questions; amphotericin B, dapsone, metronidazole and albendazole are favourite short notes.

## 3.1 Antitubercular drugs

**Tuberculosis** is caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, a slow-growing, acid-fast bacillus with a waxy, lipid-rich wall (mycolic acid). It lives inside macrophages and in caseous lesions, and some bacilli stay dormant for long periods. So TB needs **several drugs together for months**.

**Why multidrug therapy?**

1. To prevent drug resistance (any large bacillary population contains naturally resistant mutants).
2. To kill different populations: rapidly multiplying extracellular bacilli (isoniazid), slowly multiplying intracellular bacilli in acidic macrophages (pyrazinamide), and intermittently active bacilli in caseous material (rifampicin).
3. To shorten the total duration of treatment.

### Classification

| Line | Drugs |
| --- | --- |
| First-line (HRZE + S) | Isoniazid (H), rifampicin (R), pyrazinamide (Z), ethambutol (E), streptomycin (S) |
| Second-line: fluoroquinolones | Levofloxacin, moxifloxacin |
| Second-line: injectables | Amikacin, kanamycin, capreomycin |
| Other second-line | Ethionamide, cycloserine, para-aminosalicylic acid (PAS), linezolid, clofazimine |
| Newer drugs | Bedaquiline, delamanid, pretomanid |

### First-line drugs

| Drug | Mechanism | Action | Key adverse effects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Isoniazid | Prodrug activated by mycobacterial catalase-peroxidase (KatG); inhibits **mycolic acid** synthesis | Bactericidal on rapidly dividing bacilli; most potent | **Peripheral neuropathy** (pyridoxine deficiency; prevented by pyridoxine), **hepatitis** (more in elderly and alcoholics), CNS effects; slow acetylators have higher levels |
| Rifampicin | Inhibits DNA-dependent **RNA polymerase** | Bactericidal on all populations, including intermittently active bacilli | Hepatitis, **orange-red urine, tears, sweat** (stains contact lenses), flu-like syndrome on intermittent use, thrombocytopenia; a potent **enzyme inducer** |
| Pyrazinamide | Converted to pyrazinoic acid; works in **acidic pH** inside macrophages | Bactericidal on intracellular bacilli; sterilising in first 2 months | **Hepatotoxicity**, **hyperuricaemia** (gout), arthralgia |
| Ethambutol | Inhibits **arabinosyl transferase** (arabinogalactan of the wall) | Bacteriostatic; prevents resistance | **Optic neuritis** (reduced visual acuity and red–green colour vision); avoided in young children who cannot report vision changes |
| Streptomycin | 30S ribosome (aminoglycoside) | Bactericidal on extracellular bacilli | Ototoxicity (vestibular), nephrotoxicity; now rarely used |

**Rifampicin interactions:** by inducing CYP3A4 it lowers levels of oral contraceptives (failure), warfarin, corticosteroids, oral antidiabetics, protease inhibitors and many others. **Isoniazid** inhibits metabolism of phenytoin and carbamazepine.

### Treatment regimens (National TB Elimination Programme, India)

TB treatment has an **intensive phase** (four drugs: H, R, Z, E for 2 months) followed by a **continuation phase** (H, R, E for 4 months) for drug-sensitive TB, given as daily **fixed-dose combinations** by weight band. The programme follows the principle of **directly observed treatment** (DOTS) with patient support.

**Drug-resistant TB:**

- **MDR-TB:** resistant to at least isoniazid and rifampicin.
- **Pre-XDR-TB:** MDR/RR-TB plus resistance to a fluoroquinolone.
- **XDR-TB:** pre-XDR plus resistance to bedaquiline and/or linezolid (current WHO definition).

Treated with all-oral regimens based on bedaquiline, linezolid, fluoroquinolones (levofloxacin, moxifloxacin), clofazimine, cycloserine, and pretomanid-based regimens, as per programme guidelines. Bedaquiline inhibits mycobacterial **ATP synthase**; it prolongs the QT interval.

**Chemoprophylaxis (TB preventive therapy):** isoniazid alone, or isoniazid with rifapentine, in close contacts and people living with HIV, as per guidelines.

## 3.2 Antileprotic drugs

**Leprosy** (Hansen's disease) is caused by *Mycobacterium leprae*, which affects skin and peripheral nerves. Clinically classified as **paucibacillary** (PB; up to 5 skin lesions) and **multibacillary** (MB; more than 5 lesions, or nerve involvement, or smear positive).

| Drug | Mechanism | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dapsone (diaminodiphenyl sulfone) | PABA antagonist; inhibits folate synthesis (like sulfonamides) | Bacteriostatic; haemolysis (especially in G6PD deficiency), methaemoglobinaemia, "dapsone syndrome" (rash, fever, hepatitis, lymphadenopathy) |
| Rifampicin | RNA polymerase | Rapidly bactericidal; given once monthly in MDT |
| Clofazimine | Binds mycobacterial DNA; also anti-inflammatory | **Reddish-black skin pigmentation**, dry skin, GI upset; useful in type 2 lepra reaction |
| Others | Ofloxacin, minocycline, clarithromycin | Alternative regimens |

**Multidrug therapy (MDT, WHO):** rifampicin (monthly, supervised) + clofazimine (monthly supervised plus daily) + dapsone (daily). WHO now recommends the **same three-drug regimen for both PB and MB** leprosy, differing in duration (6 months for PB, 12 months for MB).

**Lepra reactions:**

- **Type 1 (reversal reaction):** a cell-mediated immune flare in existing lesions and nerves → corticosteroids (prednisolone).
- **Type 2 (erythema nodosum leprosum, ENL):** an immune-complex reaction with painful nodules and fever → corticosteroids, clofazimine, thalidomide (strictly avoided in pregnancy because it causes phocomelia).

## 3.3 Antifungal agents

Fungal cell membranes contain **ergosterol** instead of cholesterol — the main target of selective toxicity.

| Class | Examples | Mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Polyene antibiotics | Amphotericin B, nystatin, natamycin | Bind ergosterol → form **pores** in the membrane → leakage of ions → fungal death |
| Echinocandins | Caspofungin, micafungin, anidulafungin | Inhibit **β-(1,3)-glucan synthase** → defective cell wall |
| Azoles: imidazoles | Ketoconazole, clotrimazole, miconazole, econazole | Inhibit fungal CYP450 enzyme **lanosterol 14α-demethylase** → no ergosterol |
| Azoles: triazoles | Fluconazole, itraconazole, voriconazole, posaconazole, isavuconazole | Same, more selective for fungal enzyme |
| Allylamine | Terbinafine | Inhibits **squalene epoxidase** (ergosterol synthesis) |
| Antimetabolite | Flucytosine | Converted to 5-fluorouracil in fungal cells → inhibits DNA synthesis |
| Others | Griseofulvin | Binds tubulin, inhibits fungal mitosis; deposits in keratin |
| Topical | Ciclopirox, tolnaftate, Whitfield's ointment (benzoic + salicylic acid), amorolfine | Superficial infections |

### Amphotericin B

Broad-spectrum, fungicidal; the drug of choice for many serious systemic fungal infections (cryptococcal meningitis with flucytosine, invasive aspergillosis in some settings, mucormycosis, histoplasmosis); also used in **kala-azar** (visceral leishmaniasis). Given IV, as it is not absorbed orally.

**Adverse effects:** infusion reactions (fever, chills, rigors, headache, hypotension) — reduced by premedication and slow infusion; **nephrotoxicity** (the main dose-limiting effect), hypokalaemia and hypomagnesaemia, anaemia, thrombophlebitis. **Lipid formulations** (liposomal amphotericin B) are less nephrotoxic.

### Azoles

- **Ketoconazole:** first oral azole; inhibits human CYP enzymes too → blocks steroid synthesis (gynaecomastia, menstrual changes) and causes interactions; hepatotoxic; now mostly topical. Systemic use is used in Cushing's syndrome in selected cases.
- **Fluconazole:** water-soluble, good oral absorption, enters CSF; used in candidiasis (vaginal, oral, oesophageal), cryptococcal meningitis (maintenance), prophylaxis in immunocompromised patients. Fewer interactions.
- **Itraconazole:** aspergillosis, histoplasmosis, onychomycosis; absorption needs acid and food.
- **Voriconazole:** drug of choice for invasive aspergillosis; visual disturbances.
- **Posaconazole, isavuconazole:** also active against mucormycosis.
- Azoles inhibit CYP3A4 → raise levels of warfarin, statins, cyclosporine.

**Griseofulvin** is fungistatic, given orally for dermatophyte infections of skin, hair and nails (not active topically or against *Candida*); an enzyme inducer, teratogenic. **Terbinafine** is fungicidal and preferred for onychomycosis. **Nystatin** is too toxic for injection; used for oral and vaginal candidiasis.

## 3.4 Antiviral drugs

Viruses use host cell machinery, so selective targets are viral enzymes or steps of the replication cycle (attachment, entry, uncoating, nucleic acid synthesis, assembly, release).

| Group | Examples | Mechanism / use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anti-herpes | Acyclovir, valacyclovir, famciclovir, ganciclovir (CMV), foscarnet | Inhibit viral DNA polymerase |
| Anti-influenza | Oseltamivir, zanamivir; amantadine, rimantadine (older) | Neuraminidase inhibitors block release of new virions; amantadine blocks M2 ion channel (uncoating) |
| Anti-hepatitis B | Tenofovir, entecavir, lamivudine, interferon-α | Polymerase (reverse transcriptase) inhibition; immune activity |
| Anti-hepatitis C | Sofosbuvir, daclatasvir, velpatasvir, ledipasvir | NS5B polymerase and NS5A inhibitors (direct-acting antivirals) |
| Anti-HIV (antiretroviral) | See below | |
| Others | Ribavirin, remdesivir, molnupiravir | RNA viruses |

### Acyclovir

A guanosine analogue. **Viral thymidine kinase** phosphorylates it to the monophosphate (so it is activated only in infected cells — the basis of selectivity); host kinases make acyclovir triphosphate, which inhibits viral DNA polymerase and causes **chain termination**. Used in herpes simplex (genital, labial, keratitis, encephalitis — drug of choice) and varicella-zoster. Adverse effects: renal crystal formation on rapid IV infusion, CNS effects. **Valacyclovir** is a prodrug with better oral bioavailability. **Ganciclovir** (CMV) causes bone marrow suppression.

### Antiretroviral drugs (HIV)

HIV is an RNA retrovirus that infects CD4 T cells.

```text
 HIV life cycle and drug targets
 attachment to CD4 + CCR5/CXCR4  <-- maraviroc (CCR5 antagonist)
 fusion                           <-- enfuvirtide
 reverse transcription RNA->DNA   <-- NRTIs, NNRTIs
 integration into host DNA        <-- integrase inhibitors
 protein processing (protease)    <-- protease inhibitors
 budding and maturation
```

| Class | Examples | Key adverse effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NRTIs (nucleoside/nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitors) | Zidovudine, lamivudine, emtricitabine, abacavir, tenofovir | Zidovudine: bone marrow suppression (anaemia); abacavir: hypersensitivity; tenofovir: renal toxicity, bone loss; class: lactic acidosis |
| NNRTIs | Efavirenz, nevirapine, rilpivirine, etravirine | Efavirenz: CNS effects, vivid dreams; nevirapine: rash, hepatotoxicity |
| Protease inhibitors (PIs) | Lopinavir, ritonavir, atazanavir, darunavir | Lipodystrophy, hyperlipidaemia, hyperglycaemia; ritonavir is a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor used to "boost" other PIs |
| Integrase inhibitors (INSTIs) | Dolutegravir, raltegravir, bictegravir | Generally well tolerated; weight gain |
| Entry/fusion inhibitors | Maraviroc, enfuvirtide | |

NRTIs are phosphorylated by host kinases; as nucleotide analogues lacking a 3′-OH group, they cause **chain termination**. NNRTIs bind a non-catalytic site of reverse transcriptase (non-competitive).

**Highly active antiretroviral therapy (HAART / ART):** a combination of three drugs, usually two NRTIs plus one drug from another class. The preferred first-line regimen in India (National AIDS Control Programme) is **tenofovir + lamivudine + dolutegravir (TLD)**. Combination prevents resistance and keeps viral load suppressed. ART is also used for prevention of mother-to-child transmission and for post-exposure prophylaxis.

## 3.5 Anthelmintics

Drugs that kill or expel parasitic worms (helminths): nematodes (roundworms), cestodes (tapeworms) and trematodes (flukes).

| Drug | Mechanism | Main uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Albendazole, mebendazole (benzimidazoles) | Bind β-tubulin → block microtubule formation → impaired glucose uptake, worm dies slowly | Broad: roundworm, hookworm, whipworm, pinworm; albendazole for neurocysticercosis and hydatid disease |
| Pyrantel pamoate | Depolarising neuromuscular blocker in worms → **spastic paralysis** | Roundworm, hookworm, pinworm |
| Piperazine | GABA agonist at worm muscle → **flaccid paralysis** | Roundworm, pinworm (older) |
| Ivermectin | Opens glutamate-gated Cl− channels → hyperpolarisation, paralysis | Onchocerciasis (drug of choice), strongyloidiasis, scabies, head lice, filariasis (mass drug administration) |
| Diethylcarbamazine (DEC) | Makes microfilariae susceptible to host immunity | Lymphatic filariasis; tropical eosinophilia |
| Praziquantel | Increases Ca2+ permeability → contraction and paralysis, tegument damage | Schistosomiasis, tapeworms, flukes, neurocysticercosis |
| Niclosamide | Inhibits oxidative phosphorylation in tapeworm | Tapeworms (not effective against larval cysts) |
| Levamisole | Nicotinic agonist → spastic paralysis; also immunostimulant | Roundworm |

**Mazzotti reaction** — itching, fever and rash from dying microfilariae after DEC (in onchocerciasis especially). Pyrantel and piperazine are antagonists and should not be combined. India runs national **deworming** campaigns with albendazole for school children and mass drug administration for filariasis (DEC + albendazole, with ivermectin in some areas).

## 3.6 Antimalarial drugs

Malaria is caused by *Plasmodium falciparum*, *P. vivax*, *P. ovale*, *P. malariae* and *P. knowlesi*, spread by the female *Anopheles* mosquito.

```text
 Malarial life cycle and drug actions

 Sporozoites (mosquito bite)
        |
 Liver (pre-erythrocytic) ......... primaquine, proguanil (causal prophylaxis)
        |        \
        |         hypnozoites (vivax, ovale) -- relapse --> primaquine, tafenoquine
        v
 Red cell (erythrocytic schizogony) - fever .. chloroquine, artemisinins,
        |                                      quinine, mefloquine (schizonticides)
        v
 Gametocytes (taken up by mosquito) .... primaquine (gametocidal, falciparum)
```

**Classification by stage of action**

| Class | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Erythrocytic (blood) schizonticides | Chloroquine, quinine, mefloquine, artemisinins (artesunate, artemether, arteether), lumefantrine, amodiaquine, piperaquine, atovaquone, proguanil, pyrimethamine, doxycycline |
| Tissue schizonticides (causal prophylaxis) | Primaquine, proguanil |
| Hypnozoiticides (radical cure, anti-relapse) | Primaquine, tafenoquine |
| Gametocides | Primaquine (falciparum); chloroquine and quinine (vivax) |

**By chemical class:** 4-aminoquinolines (chloroquine, amodiaquine), quinoline methanol (mefloquine), cinchona alkaloids (quinine), 8-aminoquinolines (primaquine, tafenoquine), biguanides (proguanil), diaminopyrimidines (pyrimethamine), artemisinin derivatives, amino alcohols (lumefantrine), naphthoquinone (atovaquone), antibiotics (doxycycline, clindamycin).

### Chloroquine

**Mechanism:** a weak base that concentrates in the acidic food vacuole of the parasite. The parasite digests haemoglobin and converts toxic free haem into harmless **haemozoin** by polymerisation. Chloroquine **inhibits haem polymerase**, so free haem accumulates and kills the parasite.

**Uses:** treatment of *P. vivax* malaria (with primaquine for radical cure), extraintestinal amoebiasis (liver abscess), rheumatoid arthritis and lupus (hydroxychloroquine), discoid lupus. *P. falciparum* is widely resistant.

**Adverse effects:** nausea, itching (especially in people of African descent), headache, visual disturbances; long-term use causes **retinopathy** (bull's-eye maculopathy) and corneal deposits; QT prolongation; worsens psoriasis.

### Artemisinin derivatives and ACT

Artesunate, artemether and arteether come from *Artemisia annua* (qinghaosu). They contain an **endoperoxide bridge** that is activated by haem iron to form free radicals that damage parasite proteins. They act fast and also reduce gametocytes. Because of their short half-life and to prevent resistance, they are always given as **artemisinin-based combination therapy (ACT)** with a longer-acting partner:

- Artesunate + sulfadoxine–pyrimethamine (used in India's programme for uncomplicated falciparum malaria, except in the north-eastern states).
- Artemether + lumefantrine (north-eastern states, where SP resistance is common).
- Others: artesunate + mefloquine, dihydroartemisinin + piperaquine.

**Severe malaria:** injectable **artesunate** is the drug of choice (quinine as an alternative); then a full course of ACT.

### Quinine

From cinchona bark. Blood schizonticide (haem polymerase inhibition). Used in severe falciparum malaria when artesunate is not available, and in the first trimester of pregnancy in some guidelines. Adverse effects: **cinchonism** (tinnitus, deafness, headache, nausea, blurred vision), **hypoglycaemia** (it releases insulin), arrhythmias, haemolysis (blackwater fever), uterine stimulation.

### Primaquine

An 8-aminoquinoline that forms oxidant metabolites damaging parasite mitochondria. Kills liver hypnozoites → **radical cure of vivax and ovale malaria**; a single low dose kills falciparum gametocytes and blocks transmission. **Haemolytic anaemia in G6PD deficiency** — test for G6PD before radical cure. Avoided in pregnancy and in infants. **Tafenoquine** is a single-dose long-acting relative.

### Other antimalarials

- **Mefloquine:** prophylaxis and treatment of resistant falciparum; neuropsychiatric effects.
- **Proguanil:** a biguanide converted to cycloguanil, which inhibits plasmodial DHFR; used with atovaquone (Malarone) for prophylaxis.
- **Pyrimethamine** with sulfadoxine: sequential folate block (like cotrimoxazole).
- **Doxycycline:** prophylaxis for travellers; with quinine in treatment.

**Chemoprophylaxis** for travellers to areas of chloroquine-resistant malaria: doxycycline, mefloquine or atovaquone–proguanil, started before travel and continued afterwards.

## 3.7 Antiamoebic drugs

**Amoebiasis** is caused by *Entamoeba histolytica*: intestinal (dysentery, colitis), extraintestinal (amoebic liver abscess) and asymptomatic cyst passers.

| Class | Examples | Site of action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tissue amoebicides (intestinal wall + liver) | Nitroimidazoles: metronidazole, tinidazole, secnidazole, ornidazole | Both intestinal and extraintestinal |
| Tissue amoebicides (liver only) | Chloroquine | Liver abscess |
| Tissue amoebicides (older) | Emetine, dehydroemetine | Toxic; rarely used |
| Luminal amoebicides | Diloxanide furoate, iodoquinol (diiodohydroxyquinoline), paromomycin, tetracyclines (indirect) | Cysts and trophozoites in the gut lumen |

### Metronidazole

**Mechanism:** a prodrug. In anaerobic organisms its nitro group is **reduced** (by ferredoxin-linked enzymes) to reactive intermediates that damage DNA → cidal. Human cells and aerobes lack this reducing system — selective toxicity.

**Spectrum and uses:** amoebiasis (intestinal and liver abscess — drug of choice), giardiasis, trichomoniasis (drug of choice; treat the partner too), anaerobic infections (*Bacteroides fragilis*, *Clostridia*), *C. difficile* colitis (now vancomycin or fidaxomicin preferred in many guidelines), *H. pylori* (part of regimens), bacterial vaginosis, dental infections, prophylaxis in colorectal surgery.

**Adverse effects:** metallic taste, nausea, dark urine, headache, peripheral neuropathy and seizures with long use; a **disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol** (alcohol is avoided during treatment and for some time after); enhances warfarin effect.

After a tissue amoebicide, a **luminal amoebicide** (diloxanide furoate) is given to eradicate cysts and prevent relapse.

## Remember

- TB: multidrug therapy prevents resistance and kills all bacillary populations; intensive phase HRZE for 2 months + continuation HRE.
- Isoniazid → neuropathy (give pyridoxine), hepatitis; rifampicin → orange urine, enzyme inducer, contraceptive failure; pyrazinamide → hyperuricaemia; ethambutol → optic neuritis.
- MDR-TB = resistant to H and R; bedaquiline inhibits ATP synthase.
- Leprosy MDT: rifampicin + clofazimine + dapsone; clofazimine pigmentation; dapsone haemolysis; ENL → steroids, thalidomide.
- Antifungals: polyenes form pores in ergosterol membrane; azoles inhibit 14α-demethylase; terbinafine inhibits squalene epoxidase; echinocandins inhibit glucan synthase; amphotericin B is nephrotoxic.
- Acyclovir needs viral thymidine kinase; oseltamivir inhibits neuraminidase; ART = three drugs, TLD first line in India; ritonavir boosts PIs.
- Benzimidazoles bind β-tubulin; pyrantel spastic, piperazine flaccid paralysis; ivermectin for onchocerciasis and scabies; praziquantel for flukes and tapeworms.
- Chloroquine inhibits haem polymerase; ACT for falciparum; IV artesunate for severe malaria; primaquine for radical cure, check G6PD; quinine causes cinchonism and hypoglycaemia.
- Metronidazole: reduced in anaerobes; drug of choice for amoebiasis, giardiasis, trichomoniasis; disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol; follow with diloxanide furoate.

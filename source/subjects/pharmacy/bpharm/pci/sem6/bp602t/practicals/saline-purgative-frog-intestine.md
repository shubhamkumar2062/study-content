# Effect of saline purgative on frog intestine

**Aim:** To demonstrate the mechanism of action of a saline purgative (magnesium sulphate) on isolated frog intestine.

> Demonstrated by simulation or video as per the PCI note. Live animal work needs Institutional Animal Ethics Committee approval under CCSEA (earlier CPCSEA) rules. Use the solutions and strengths from your lab manual.

## Principle

A **saline purgative** such as magnesium sulphate or sodium sulphate contains ions that are poorly absorbed from the gut. They stay in the lumen and hold water there by **osmosis**, so the contents stay fluid, the bowel is distended and peristalsis is stimulated.

The experiment shows this osmotic action directly. A loop of frog intestine is filled with the test solution, tied at both ends and weighed, then suspended in frog Ringer solution. After a fixed time it is weighed again.

```text
  Intestinal loop filled with MgSO4 solution
  ======================================
      water moves from the Ringer bath INTO the loop (osmosis)
      -> loop gains weight

  Loop filled with Ringer solution (control)
      little or no net movement -> weight almost unchanged
```

**% change in weight = (final weight − initial weight) ÷ initial weight × 100**

A gain in weight shows that water has moved into the lumen — exactly what a saline purgative does in the intestine.

## Requirements

Frog, dissecting set and board, frog Ringer solution, magnesium sulphate solution of the strength given in the manual, glucose or sodium chloride solution for comparison, thread, syringe with blunt needle, beakers, balance, filter paper, stopwatch; or simulation software.

## Procedure

1. Obtain the intestine from a frog as directed by the manual and the ethics committee's instructions; place it in frog Ringer solution.
2. Cut three loops of equal length (about 4–5 cm each) and clear the lumen gently with Ringer solution.
3. Tie one end of each loop firmly with thread.
4. Fill loop 1 with Ringer solution (control), loop 2 with magnesium sulphate solution and loop 3 with the second test solution, using a syringe with a blunt needle. Do not over-distend.
5. Tie the open end of each loop and blot the outside gently on filter paper.
6. Weigh each loop and record the initial weight.
7. Suspend all three loops in beakers of frog Ringer solution at room temperature for the time fixed in the manual (commonly 30 minutes).
8. Remove each loop, blot it in the same way and record the final weight.
9. Calculate the change in weight and the percentage change for each loop.

## Observations and calculation

**Specimen readings (illustrative only, not real data).**

| Loop | Filled with | Initial weight (g) | Final weight (g) | Change (g) | % change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Ringer solution (control) | 1.20 | 1.22 | +0.02 | +1.7 |
| 2 | Magnesium sulphate | 1.25 | 1.58 | +0.33 | +26.4 |
| 3 | Sodium chloride (isotonic) | 1.22 | 1.24 | +0.02 | +1.6 |

The loop filled with magnesium sulphate gained much more weight than the control.

## Result

The intestinal loop filled with magnesium sulphate gained weight (specimen: +26.4 %) while the control loop did not change appreciably, showing that a saline purgative acts by drawing water into the intestinal lumen by osmosis.

## Precautions

1. Keep all loops of the same length and from the same region of the intestine.
2. Clear the lumen gently; do not scrape the mucosa.
3. Fill the loops to the same degree and avoid over-distension, which would force fluid out.
4. Tie the threads tightly so that no leakage occurs; a leaking loop must be discarded.
5. Blot each loop in exactly the same way before both weighings, as surface fluid changes the weight.
6. Keep the bath temperature and the suspension time identical for all loops.
7. Keep the tissue moist with Ringer solution while handling.

## Viva questions

1. **How do saline purgatives work?** Their poorly absorbed ions retain water in the lumen by osmosis, distending the bowel and stimulating peristalsis.
2. **Name two saline purgatives.** Magnesium sulphate and sodium sulphate (also magnesium hydroxide, polyethylene glycol).
3. **Why is frog Ringer solution used and not Tyrode?** Ringer solution matches the ionic needs of amphibian tissue; Tyrode is used for mammalian tissue.
4. **Why does the loop gain weight?** Water moves from the bath into the hypertonic solution inside the lumen.
5. **What is the control in this experiment?** The loop filled with Ringer solution, which shows that the gain is not simply due to handling.
6. **Give one clinical use of magnesium sulphate as a purgative.** Bowel preparation before investigation or surgery.
7. **Name a drug class that works in the opposite way in the gut.** Antimotility drugs such as loperamide, which slow transit and allow more water absorption.
8. **Which laxative is both osmotic and useful in hepatic encephalopathy?** Lactulose.

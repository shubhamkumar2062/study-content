# Antiallergic activity by the mast cell stabilisation assay

**Aim:** To study the antiallergic (mast cell stabilising) activity of a test drug using rat peritoneal mast cells.

> Demonstrated by simulation or video as per the PCI note. Live animal work needs Institutional Animal Ethics Committee approval under CCSEA (earlier CPCSEA) rules. Use the drug and dose from your lab manual.

## Principle

Mast cells carry IgE on their surface. When an antigen (or a chemical releaser such as **compound 48/80**) acts on them, calcium enters the cell and the granules fuse with the membrane and release **histamine, leukotrienes and other mediators** — the basis of an allergic reaction. A **mast cell stabiliser** such as sodium cromoglycate or ketotifen prevents this degranulation.

Peritoneal mast cells are collected from a rat, treated with the test drug and then challenged with the degranulating agent. Cells are stained (toluidine blue gives a purple **metachromatic** colour to the granules) and examined under the microscope. **Intact** cells are round with tightly packed granules; **degranulated** cells look ragged with granules scattered outside.

```text
   Intact mast cell            Degranulated mast cell
      .-----.                        .-- * --.
     / ::::: \   granules inside    /  *   *  \   granules outside
    |  :::::  |                    |    * *    |
     \ ::::: /                      \  *    * /
      '-----'                        '-- * --'
```

**% protection = (degranulation in control − degranulation in test) ÷ degranulation in control × 100**

## Requirements

Rats, Tyrode solution, heparin, compound 48/80 (degranulating agent), standard drug (sodium cromoglycate or ketotifen), test drug, toluidine blue stain, centrifuge, microscope, haemocytometer, glass slides and cover slips, pipettes, water bath at 37 °C; or simulation software.

## Procedure

1. Group the animals as per your manual: vehicle control, standard (sodium cromoglycate) and test drug; treat for the number of days fixed in the manual.
2. Inject warm Tyrode solution containing heparin into the peritoneal cavity, massage the abdomen gently, and withdraw the peritoneal fluid.
3. Centrifuge the fluid at low speed, discard the supernatant and wash the mast cell pellet with Tyrode solution.
4. Suspend the cells in Tyrode solution and divide into tubes.
5. Incubate each tube at 37 °C for the stated time, then add the degranulating agent (compound 48/80) to all tubes except the untreated blank.
6. Incubate again, then stop the reaction by cooling.
7. Place a drop of each suspension on a slide, stain with toluidine blue and cover.
8. Count at least 100 mast cells per slide under the microscope and classify each as intact or degranulated.

## Observations and calculation

**Specimen readings (illustrative only, not real data).**

| Group | Cells counted | Intact | Degranulated | % degranulation | % protection |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blank (no 48/80) | 100 | 95 | 5 | 5 | — |
| Control (48/80 only) | 100 | 25 | 75 | 75 | 0 |
| Standard (cromoglycate) | 100 | 78 | 22 | 22 | 70.7 |
| Test drug | 100 | 65 | 35 | 35 | 53.3 |

% protection for the standard = (75 − 22) ÷ 75 × 100 = 70.7 %.

## Result

The test drug protected rat peritoneal mast cells from degranulation by compound 48/80, showing mast cell stabilising (antiallergic) activity of about ___ % (specimen value 53.3 %), compared with ___ % for the standard.

## Precautions

1. Keep all solutions and the cell suspension warm (37 °C) and at the correct pH; cold or acidic medium damages mast cells.
2. Handle the suspension gently; rough pipetting itself breaks mast cells.
3. Use freshly prepared compound 48/80 solution.
4. Count the same number of cells in every group, in several fields chosen at random.
5. Let one person do all the counting to avoid observer variation, preferably without knowing which slide is which.
6. Stain for the same time in every group.

## Viva questions

1. **Which mediator released from mast cells causes the main allergic symptoms?** Histamine, along with leukotrienes, prostaglandin D2 and PAF.
2. **Name two mast cell stabilisers.** Sodium cromoglycate and ketotifen.
3. **How does sodium cromoglycate work?** It stabilises the mast cell membrane and prevents degranulation; it has no bronchodilator action.
4. **What is compound 48/80?** A chemical that directly degranulates mast cells, used as the challenge agent.
5. **Why is toluidine blue used?** It stains mast cell granules purple by metachromasia, so intact and degranulated cells are easy to tell apart.
6. **Where are mast cells found in the body?** Connective tissue, especially around blood vessels, in skin, lung and gut mucosa.
7. **Which receptor does histamine act on to cause bronchoconstriction?** The H1 receptor.
8. **Why is this assay called antiallergic and not antihistaminic?** The drug prevents the release of mediators, instead of blocking their receptors.

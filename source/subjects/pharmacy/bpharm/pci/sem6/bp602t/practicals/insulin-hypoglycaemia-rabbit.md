# Hypoglycaemic effect of insulin in rabbit

**Aim:** To study the hypoglycaemic effect of insulin in a rabbit and to demonstrate the recovery of blood glucose after giving glucose.

> Demonstrated by simulation or video as per the PCI note. Live animal work needs Institutional Animal Ethics Committee approval under CCSEA (earlier CPCSEA) rules. Use the preparation and dose from your lab manual, under supervision.

## Principle

Insulin is secreted by the β cells of the pancreatic islets. It binds a **tyrosine kinase receptor** and lowers blood glucose by increasing glucose uptake in muscle and fat (through GLUT-4 transporters), increasing glycogen synthesis and lipogenesis, and reducing gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis in the liver.

In a fasted rabbit, insulin produces a measurable fall in blood glucose, which reaches a lowest point and then recovers as counter-regulatory hormones (glucagon, adrenaline, cortisol, growth hormone) act. This fall is the basis of the **rabbit blood sugar method**, the classical biological assay for the potency of insulin preparations, in which the fall produced by a standard and a test preparation are compared in a crossover design.

## Requirements

Healthy rabbits of similar weight, insulin preparation, sterile syringes and needles, glucose solution for recovery, glucometer with strips or kit for blood glucose estimation, sterile lancets, cotton and spirit, rabbit holder, balance, stopwatch; or simulation software.

## Procedure

1. Fast the rabbit as directed in the manual, with water available, and weigh it.
2. Collect a blood sample from the marginal ear vein and estimate the **basal (0 minute) blood glucose**.
3. Give insulin by the route and dose fixed in the manual (usually subcutaneously).
4. Collect blood samples at the times fixed in the manual (for example 30, 60, 90 and 120 minutes) and estimate blood glucose in each.
5. Watch the animal for signs of a low blood glucose level (restlessness, weakness, tremor, convulsions). If any appear, inform the supervisor at once and give glucose as directed.
6. Give glucose at the end of the observation period and record the recovery reading.
7. Plot blood glucose against time and calculate the maximum percentage fall.

## Observations and calculation

**Specimen readings (illustrative only, not real data).**

| Time (min) | Blood glucose (mg/dL) | % fall from basal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 (basal) | 110 | — |
| 30 | 82 | 25.5 |
| 60 | 64 | 41.8 |
| 90 | 71 | 35.5 |
| 120 | 88 | 20.0 |
| After glucose | 126 | — |

**% fall = (basal − value at that time) ÷ basal × 100.** Maximum fall in this specimen = 41.8 % at 60 minutes.

In a bioassay, the test and standard preparations are compared by the mean percentage fall they produce in the same group of animals on different days (crossover design), and the potency of the test is calculated from the ratio.

## Result

Insulin produced a fall in blood glucose in the rabbit, maximum at ___ minutes (specimen: 41.8 % at 60 minutes), and the level returned towards normal after glucose was given.

## Precautions

1. Use healthy rabbits of similar weight, fasted for the same period, with water available.
2. Keep the room quiet and at a comfortable temperature; excitement and cold change blood glucose.
3. Warm the ear gently before pricking so that blood flows freely; do not squeeze the ear hard.
4. Use a fresh lancet and sterile technique for each sample and alternate the ear veins.
5. Estimate glucose immediately, or keep the sample as the kit directs.
6. Keep a glucose solution ready before starting, and inform the supervisor at the first sign of distress.
7. Follow the time points exactly; blood glucose changes quickly.
8. Allow the animal to recover fully and feed it before returning it to the animal house.

## Viva questions

1. **Which cells secrete insulin?** The β cells of the islets of Langerhans in the pancreas.
2. **What type of receptor does insulin act on?** A receptor tyrosine kinase.
3. **Which transporter carries glucose into muscle and fat under the influence of insulin?** GLUT-4.
4. **Name the hormones that oppose insulin.** Glucagon, adrenaline, cortisol and growth hormone.
5. **Why is the rabbit used for insulin bioassay?** Rabbits show a reliable, reproducible fall in blood glucose after insulin, which can be measured easily.
6. **Why is a crossover design used in this bioassay?** Each animal acts as its own control, which reduces the effect of individual variation.
7. **Name two types of insulin preparation.** Rapid-acting (regular insulin, insulin lispro) and long-acting (insulin glargine, insulin detemir).
8. **Which route gives the fastest action of regular insulin?** Intravenous, used only in emergencies; subcutaneous is the usual route.
9. **What is the first sign of a falling blood glucose level in the animal?** Restlessness and weakness, later tremor and convulsions.

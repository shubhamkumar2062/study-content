# Effect of agonists and antagonists on guinea pig ileum

**Aim:** To record the effect of agonists (acetylcholine, histamine) and their antagonists on isolated guinea pig ileum and to plot a dose–response curve.

> Demonstrated by simulation or video as per the PCI note. Live animal work needs Institutional Animal Ethics Committee approval under CCSEA (earlier CPCSEA) rules. Use the drugs and concentrations from your lab manual.

## Principle

Guinea pig ileum is the classical smooth muscle preparation for bioassay. Its longitudinal muscle carries **muscarinic M3** and **histamine H1** receptors; both act through Gq → IP3 → release of intracellular Ca2+ → contraction. The tissue is suspended in an organ bath of warm, aerated **Tyrode solution**, and contractions are recorded on a kymograph or a transducer with a physiograph or data acquisition system.

Adding increasing concentrations of an agonist gives larger contractions until a maximum is reached. Plotting response against **log concentration** gives the familiar S-shaped **log dose–response curve (DRC)**.

```text
 Response
   100% |            ____---- control
        |        _--/     ____---- with competitive antagonist
        |     _-/     _--/            (parallel shift to the right,
        |   _/     _-/                 same maximum)
     0% |__/____ _/________________
          log concentration of agonist
```

- A **competitive antagonist** (atropine against acetylcholine, mepyramine against histamine) shifts the curve to the right in parallel with no fall in the maximum; the shift can be surmounted by more agonist.
- A **non-competitive antagonist** lowers the maximum of the curve.

## Requirements

Guinea pig ileum, organ bath (10 or 20 mL) with Tyrode solution at 37 °C, aeration with air or carbogen, isotonic frontal writing lever with kymograph (or an isotonic transducer with recorder), thread, dissecting set, agonist solutions (acetylcholine, histamine), antagonist solutions (atropine, mepyramine), pipettes and micropipettes, stopwatch; or simulation software.

**Tyrode solution** is the physiological salt solution used for this tissue; prepare it as given in your manual and keep it aerated at 37 °C.

## Procedure

1. Obtain the ileum from a guinea pig as directed, discarding the portion nearest the caecum; place it in Tyrode solution at room temperature.
2. Cut a 2–3 cm piece, clear the lumen gently with Tyrode solution and tie threads at both ends.
3. Mount the tissue in the organ bath: fix one end to the tissue holder and tie the other to the lever, applying the small load fixed in your manual.
4. Let the tissue stabilise for about 30 minutes with the bath fluid changed every 10 minutes.
5. Choose a contact time and a cycle (for example contact 30 seconds, wash, rest 2 minutes) and keep it the same throughout.
6. Record the responses to increasing concentrations of the agonist until a maximum response is obtained.
7. Wash, let the tissue rest, then add the antagonist and leave it in contact for the time given in the manual.
8. Repeat the series of agonist concentrations in the presence of the antagonist.
9. Measure the height of each contraction, express it as a percentage of the maximum control response, and plot both curves on the same graph against log concentration.

## Observations and calculations

**Specimen readings (illustrative only, not real data).**

| Agonist concentration (log molar) | Control response (mm) | % of maximum | With antagonist (mm) | % of maximum |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| −8 | 2 | 8 | 0 | 0 |
| −7 | 8 | 32 | 2 | 8 |
| −6 | 18 | 72 | 7 | 28 |
| −5 | 24 | 96 | 16 | 64 |
| −4 | 25 | 100 | 24 | 96 |

From the graph, read the concentration producing 50 % of the maximum response (**EC50**) for each curve. The ratio of the two EC50 values is the **dose ratio**; a parallel shift with an unchanged maximum indicates competitive antagonism.

## Result

Acetylcholine (or histamine) produced a graded, concentration-dependent contraction of the guinea pig ileum. In the presence of the antagonist the log dose–response curve shifted to the right in parallel without a fall in the maximum, showing **competitive (surmountable) antagonism**.

## Precautions

1. Keep the bath temperature at 37 °C and aerate the fluid steadily; bubbles must not strike the tissue.
2. Handle the ileum with threads, never with forceps on the muscle.
3. Apply only the small load given in the manual; too much load damages the tissue.
4. Keep the contact time, washing and resting period identical for every dose.
5. Add drugs to the bath fluid, not directly onto the tissue.
6. Allow the tissue to return to baseline before the next dose.
7. Repeat one standard concentration at intervals to check that the tissue has not lost sensitivity.

## Viva questions

1. **Which receptors mediate contraction of guinea pig ileum?** Muscarinic M3 for acetylcholine and H1 for histamine.
2. **What is the second messenger involved?** Gq → phospholipase C → IP3 and DAG → release of intracellular calcium.
3. **Why is Tyrode solution used?** It is the physiological salt solution that keeps intestinal smooth muscle alive and responsive.
4. **How do you recognise competitive antagonism on a graph?** A parallel right shift of the log DRC with the same maximum response.
5. **Name a competitive antagonist of acetylcholine and of histamine.** Atropine and mepyramine (or chlorpheniramine).
6. **Why is a log scale used on the x-axis?** It converts the hyperbolic curve into an S-shaped curve whose middle part is nearly straight, making comparison easy.
7. **What is EC50?** The concentration producing 50 % of the maximum response; it measures potency.
8. **Why is the part of ileum near the caecum discarded?** It behaves differently and gives irregular responses.

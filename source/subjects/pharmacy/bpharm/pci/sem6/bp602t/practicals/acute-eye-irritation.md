# Determination of acute eye irritation / corrosion of a test substance

**Aim:** To determine the acute eye irritation or corrosion potential of a test substance by the method described in the OECD guidelines.

> Demonstrated by simulation or video as per the PCI note. Live animal work needs Institutional Animal Ethics Committee approval under CCSEA (earlier CPCSEA) rules. Follow the guideline and your lab manual.

## Principle

Eye irritation is a change in the eye that is **fully reversible** within 21 days of application; a **serious eye injury (corrosion)** is damage that does not fully reverse. The test is required for substances likely to come into contact with the eye — eye drops, cosmetics, shampoos, household and industrial chemicals.

The substance is placed in the **conjunctival sac of one eye** of an albino rabbit; the other eye, untreated, serves as the control. The **cornea, iris and conjunctivae** are then examined at fixed times and scored (Draize scoring). The total score decides the category.

```text
 Treated eye                         Control eye
 +-------------+                     +-------------+
 |   cornea    |  score opacity      |  untreated  |
 |    iris     |  score reaction     |             |
 | conjunctiva |  redness, chemosis, |             |
 |             |  discharge          |             |
 +-------------+                     +-------------+
```

A **tiered approach is used first**: existing data, pH, results of the skin test (a substance corrosive to skin is not tested in the eye) and validated **in vitro** methods — the bovine corneal opacity and permeability (BCOP) test, the isolated chicken eye test, and reconstructed human cornea-like epithelium tests. Only if these do not settle the question is one animal used.

## Requirements

Healthy young adult albino rabbits with normal eyes, test substance (0.1 mL of a liquid or the stated amount of a solid), local anaesthetic eye drops if the guideline permits for the application, fluorescein sodium strips for staining corneal damage, a slit lamp or loupe with a light source, washing solution (sterile saline), scoring chart; or simulation software.

## Procedure

1. Apply the tiered approach: use existing data, pH, the skin corrosion result and validated in vitro tests first.
2. Examine both eyes of each animal within 24 hours before the test; reject any animal with an eye defect.
3. Use **one animal first**. Gently pull the lower lid of one eye away from the eyeball and place the test substance in the conjunctival sac; hold the lids together for about one second so that the substance is not lost.
4. Leave the other eye untreated as the control.
5. Do not wash the eye for the period stated in the guideline (usually 24 hours), unless the guideline allows washing for a specific reason.
6. Examine the eye at 1, 24, 48 and 72 hours after application and score the cornea, iris and conjunctivae.
7. Use fluorescein staining to show corneal damage where directed.
8. If a severe effect appears in the first animal, stop the test. If the effect is mild or absent, confirm with up to two more animals as the guideline directs.
9. Continue observation up to 21 days to decide whether the changes have reversed completely.

## Precautions

1. Follow the tiered approach; never test a substance in the eye if it is already known to be corrosive to skin or has extreme pH.
2. Examine both eyes before the test and use only animals with healthy eyes.
3. Use the sequential design with one animal first.
4. Place the substance in the conjunctival sac, not directly on the cornea.
5. Do not wash the eye before the time allowed by the guideline.
6. Use a good light source and the same observer at every time point; record with photographs where possible.
7. Use pain relief as the guideline and ethics committee direct, and end the study early (humane end point) if severe injury appears.
8. Keep the control eye untreated for comparison.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the difference between eye irritation and serious eye damage?** Irritation reverses fully within 21 days; serious damage (corrosion) does not.
2. **Which tissues are scored in the Draize eye test?** Cornea, iris and conjunctivae.
3. **What is the maximum possible Draize ocular score?** 110.
4. **Where is the substance applied?** Into the conjunctival sac of one eye, the other eye serving as control.
5. **Name two in vitro alternatives.** The bovine corneal opacity and permeability test and the isolated chicken eye test (also reconstructed human cornea-like epithelium).
6. **Why is fluorescein used?** It stains areas where the corneal epithelium is damaged, making them visible.
7. **Why is only one animal used at first?** To avoid exposing more animals if the substance causes severe injury.
8. **For how long are the animals observed?** Up to 21 days, to confirm whether the changes are reversible.
9. **Which substances must be tested for eye irritation?** Those likely to contact the eye, such as eye drops, cosmetics, shampoos and household chemicals.

# Estimation of serum biochemical parameters using a semi-autoanalyser

**Aim:** To estimate serum biochemical parameters (glucose, cholesterol, urea, creatinine, SGPT/ALT, SGOT/AST and total protein) in a given sample using a semi-autoanalyser.

> Demonstrated by simulation or video as per the PCI note. Blood collection from animals needs Institutional Animal Ethics Committee approval under CCSEA (earlier CPCSEA) rules. Use the kit instructions supplied with the reagents.

## Principle

A **semi-autoanalyser** is a filter photometer with a temperature-controlled flow cell. The operator adds sample and reagent by hand; the instrument reads the absorbance and calculates the result from a stored factor or from a standard.

Most estimations use **enzymatic, colorimetric or kinetic** methods, and all rest on the **Beer–Lambert law**: absorbance is proportional to the concentration of the coloured product.

```text
 Concentration of test = (Absorbance of test / Absorbance of standard)
                          x concentration of standard
```

| Parameter | Common method | Clinical meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glucose | GOD-POD (glucose oxidase-peroxidase), end point | Diabetes, hypoglycaemia |
| Cholesterol | CHOD-PAP (cholesterol oxidase-peroxidase), end point | Lipid status |
| Urea | Urease-GLDH, kinetic | Kidney function |
| Creatinine | Alkaline picrate (Jaffe), kinetic | Kidney function |
| ALT (SGPT), AST (SGOT) | IFCC kinetic, fall in NADH at 340 nm | Liver damage |
| Total protein | Biuret, end point | Liver and nutritional status |
| Bilirubin | Diazo method | Jaundice, haemolysis |

**End point** methods read a single final absorbance; **kinetic** methods read the change in absorbance per minute.

## Requirements

Semi-autoanalyser with suitable filters (340, 505, 546, 578 and 630 nm), ready-to-use diagnostic kits with standards, micropipettes and tips, test tubes, incubator or dry bath at 37 °C, centrifuge, serum samples, distilled water, timer; or simulation software.

## Procedure

1. Switch on the instrument, allow it to warm up, and set the temperature to 37 °C.
2. Collect blood without anticoagulant, let it clot, centrifuge and separate clear serum. Haemolysed samples are rejected.
3. Select the test programme on the analyser (method, wavelength, factor or standard, incubation time).
4. Pipette the reagent and sample volumes exactly as given in the kit insert into labelled tubes: blank, standard and test.
5. Mix and incubate at 37 °C for the stated time.
6. Aspirate the blank first and set the instrument to zero, then aspirate the standard and the tests one by one; note each reading.
7. For kinetic tests, aspirate immediately after mixing and let the instrument read the change in absorbance over the fixed interval.
8. Wash the flow cell with distilled water between samples and at the end of the session.
9. Record the results with their units and compare them with the reference range printed in the kit insert.

## Precautions

1. Use clear, non-haemolysed serum; haemolysis raises potassium, AST and LDH values.
2. Pipette exactly, with a clean tip for each solution; most errors come from pipetting.
3. Bring reagents to room temperature and check their expiry date before use.
4. Follow the incubation time and temperature in the kit insert exactly, especially for kinetic tests.
5. Zero the instrument with the correct blank (reagent blank or sample blank as the kit specifies).
6. Wash the flow cell between samples so that one reading does not carry over into the next.
7. Run a control serum to check that the method is performing correctly.
8. Treat all blood and serum as potentially infectious and discard waste as per laboratory rules.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the difference between a semi-autoanalyser and a fully automatic analyser?** In a semi-autoanalyser the operator adds sample and reagent by hand; a fully automatic analyser pipettes, incubates and reads on its own.
2. **State the Beer–Lambert law.** Absorbance is proportional to the concentration of the absorbing substance and to the path length.
3. **Which enzymes are raised in liver damage?** ALT (SGPT) mainly, and AST (SGOT); ALT is the more liver-specific of the two.
4. **Which parameters reflect kidney function?** Blood urea and serum creatinine.
5. **What method is used for glucose estimation?** The GOD-POD enzymatic colorimetric method.
6. **Why is serum preferred to plasma for these tests?** It avoids interference from anticoagulants; some anticoagulants change the results of specific tests.
7. **What is a kinetic assay?** One in which the change in absorbance per minute is measured instead of a single final reading.
8. **Why is a control serum run?** To confirm that reagents and the instrument are working within acceptable limits before patient or test values are reported.

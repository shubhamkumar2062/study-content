# Anti-ulcer activity by pylorus ligation (Shay rat) and NSAID-induced ulcer models

**Aim:** To evaluate the anti-ulcer activity of a test drug in rats using the pylorus ligation (Shay) model and the NSAID-induced gastric ulcer model.

> Demonstrated by simulation or video as per the PCI note. Live animal work needs Institutional Animal Ethics Committee approval under CCSEA (earlier CPCSEA) rules. Use the drugs and doses from your lab manual.

## Principle

**Pylorus ligation (Shay rat) model.** Tying the pylorus stops gastric emptying, so the acid and pepsin secreted collect in the stomach. Within a few hours the accumulated juice digests the mucosa and produces ulcers. Because the whole secretion is trapped, this model measures both **ulcer formation** and the **volume, acidity and pepsin content of gastric juice** — so it shows whether a drug acts by reducing secretion.

**NSAID-induced model.** NSAIDs such as indomethacin or aspirin inhibit **COX-1**, so mucosal prostaglandins (PGE2) fall. Prostaglandins normally maintain mucus, bicarbonate and mucosal blood flow, so their loss leaves the mucosa open to acid damage. This model measures the **protective (cytoprotective)** action of a drug.

```text
 Pylorus ligation            NSAID model
 ----------------            -----------
 secretion trapped           PG synthesis blocked
       |                           |
 high acid + pepsin          weak mucus/bicarbonate barrier
       |                           |
    ulcers                      ulcers
```

**Ulcer index** is calculated from the number and severity of lesions; **% ulcer protection = (UI control − UI test) ÷ UI control × 100**.

## Requirements

Rats, operating table and surgical set, anaesthetic, test drug, standard drug (omeprazole, ranitidine or sucralfate as per manual), indomethacin or aspirin suspension for the NSAID model, centrifuge, measuring cylinder, pH meter, burette with 0.01 N sodium hydroxide, Topfer's reagent and phenolphthalein, hand lens or dissecting microscope; or simulation software.

## Procedure

**A. Pylorus ligation (Shay) model**

1. Fast the rats for the period stated in the manual, with free access to water; divide into control, standard and test groups.
2. Give the vehicle, standard and test drug by the route and at the time fixed in the manual.
3. Anaesthetise the animal, open the abdomen through a small midline incision, and tie the pylorus without damaging its blood supply. Close the abdomen in layers.
4. After the stated period (commonly 4 hours in this model), humanely euthanise the animal as per the manual and the ethics committee's instructions.
5. Tie the oesophagus, remove the stomach, and collect the gastric juice into a graduated tube.
6. Centrifuge the juice and record the **volume** of the supernatant.
7. Titrate an aliquot against 0.01 N NaOH using Topfer's reagent (free acidity, end point orange) and then phenolphthalein (total acidity, end point pink).
8. Open the stomach along the greater curvature, wash with saline, and examine the mucosa with a hand lens; score the lesions and calculate the ulcer index.

**B. NSAID-induced ulcer model**

1. Fast the rats as per the manual; group them as control, standard and test.
2. Give the vehicle, standard or test drug, and after the stated interval give the ulcerogenic dose of indomethacin or aspirin orally.
3. After the stated period, humanely euthanise the animals, remove the stomachs, open along the greater curvature and wash with saline.
4. Score the lesions, calculate the ulcer index and the percentage protection.

## Observations and calculations

**Scoring scale commonly used**

| Score | Appearance |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Normal mucosa |
| 0.5 | Red colouration |
| 1 | Spot ulcer |
| 1.5 | Haemorrhagic streak |
| 2 | Ulcers less than 3 mm |
| 3 | Ulcers more than 3 mm |

**Ulcer index (UI) = mean ulcer score per animal** (some manuals use UI = 10 ÷ X, where X is the total mucosal area divided by the ulcerated area; follow your manual).

**Specimen readings (illustrative only, not real data).**

| Group | Gastric juice volume (mL) | Free acidity (mEq/L) | Total acidity (mEq/L) | Ulcer index | % protection |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Control (pylorus ligated) | 4.8 | 42 | 78 | 12.5 | — |
| Standard (omeprazole) | 1.9 | 14 | 30 | 3.1 | 75.2 |
| Test drug | 2.6 | 22 | 44 | 5.0 | 60.0 |

**Acidity (mEq/L) = (volume of NaOH × normality × 1000) ÷ volume of gastric juice taken.**

% protection for the test drug = (12.5 − 5.0) ÷ 12.5 × 100 = 60 %.

## Result

The test drug reduced the volume and acidity of gastric juice and the ulcer index in the pylorus ligation model, and reduced the ulcer index in the NSAID-induced model, showing anti-ulcer activity of about ___ % protection (specimen value 60 %).

## Precautions

1. Fast the animals for the stated time with water available; food in the stomach changes both secretion and scoring.
2. Tie the pylorus firmly but without catching blood vessels or the surrounding tissue.
3. Keep every animal for exactly the same time after ligation.
4. Wash the opened stomach gently so that lesions are not made worse.
5. Score the mucosa under the same lighting and magnification; one observer should score all stomachs, preferably without knowing the groups.
6. Titrate the juice on the same day; record the volume before taking the aliquot.
7. Follow the ethics committee's instructions for anaesthesia and humane euthanasia.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is the pylorus ligated?** To stop gastric emptying so that the secreted acid and pepsin collect and damage the mucosa.
2. **What does the Shay model measure that the NSAID model does not?** The volume and acidity of gastric juice, so it shows an antisecretory action.
3. **How do NSAIDs cause ulcers?** By inhibiting COX-1, which reduces mucosal prostaglandins, mucus, bicarbonate and blood flow.
4. **Which drug is used as the standard in the NSAID model?** A protective drug such as sucralfate or misoprostol, or an acid suppressant such as omeprazole, as per the manual.
5. **What are free and total acidity?** Free acidity is free hydrochloric acid (Topfer's reagent end point); total acidity includes free acid plus combined and weakly acidic components (phenolphthalein end point).
6. **Name the indicators used in the titration.** Topfer's reagent and phenolphthalein.
7. **How is percentage ulcer protection calculated?** (Control ulcer index − test ulcer index) ÷ control ulcer index × 100.
8. **Which drug class most strongly reduces gastric acid?** Proton pump inhibitors, which irreversibly block H+/K+ ATPase.

# How to use this guide

Quality Assurance is a theory subject with almost no numericals. Marks come from clear definitions, well-ordered lists, flow charts and tables. Study one unit a day: read the notes, close them and write the main lists from memory (GMP principles, QbD elements, GLP subparts, contents of an MFR, types of validation), then answer the question bank. Each unit has very short (2-mark), short answer (5-mark) and long essay (10-mark) questions, rated by how often that kind of question appears in PCI-syllabus pharmacy papers.

The five units follow the five units of the PCI syllabus exactly. Unit 2 joins three related syllabus headings (organization and personnel, premises, equipment and raw materials), and Unit 3 joins packaging QC with GLP, as the syllabus does.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with a flow chart or table.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from common patterns and syllabus weight, not a promise of what will be asked.

**Where the marks usually come from**

| Unit | Topics that keep coming back |
| --- | --- |
| 1 QA concepts | QA vs QC vs GMP, TQM and its philosophies, ICH (purpose, steps, Q-series), stability storage conditions, QbD elements, ISO 9000/14000, NABL procedure |
| 2 Organization and premises | Personnel training and hygiene, premises design and layout, sterile area grades, control of contamination, equipment selection and maintenance, stores |
| 3 Packaging QC and GLP | Tests for glass, plastic, rubber closures and secondary packing; GLP subparts, study director, QAU, protocol, disqualification |
| 4 Complaints and documents | Complaint handling, returned goods, recall classes and procedure, MFR, BMR, SOP format, quality audit, PQR, distribution records |
| 5 Validation and warehousing | DQ/IQ/OQ/PQ, types of validation, VMP, pH meter calibration, UV spectrophotometer qualification, method validation parameters, GWP, ABC/VED, EOQ |

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. Start each answer with a one-line definition, preferably the official one (QA, GMP, QbD from ICH Q8, validation, GLP).
2. Use a flow chart for every procedure: complaint handling, recall, NABL accreditation, ISO registration, material flow in stores, QbD flow.
3. Use tables for comparisons: QA vs QC, MFR vs BMR, types of validation, recall classes, glass types, sterile grades.
4. Write guideline codes with their titles (for example "ICH Q1A(R2) — Stability testing of new drug substances and products"). The R numbers change when ICH revises a guideline; check ich.org, iso.org, nabl-india.org and cdsco.gov.in for the current version before your exam.
5. Learn the stability conditions table exactly: 25 °C/60% RH or 30 °C/65% RH long-term, 30 °C/65% RH intermediate, 40 °C/75% RH accelerated, zone IVb 30 °C/75% RH.
6. In GLP answers, always name the two key roles (study director and QAU) and list the eleven protocol contents.
7. For Indian context, mention Schedule M (GMP, revised December 2023), Schedule L-1 (GLP for testing labs) and CDSCO; examiners reward the link to Indian law.
8. Keep a short "applications/benefits" paragraph at the end of essays on TQM, QbD, ISO, NABL and validation.

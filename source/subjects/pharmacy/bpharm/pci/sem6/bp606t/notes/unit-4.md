# Unit 4: Complaints, recalls and documentation

When a medicine leaves the factory, quality assurance does not end. Patients, pharmacists and doctors report problems; some goods come back; sometimes a batch must be pulled out of the market. All of this depends on good records, because a batch that cannot be traced cannot be recalled. This unit covers complaints, returned goods, recalls and waste disposal, then the main documents of a pharmaceutical plant: the master formula record, the batch record, SOPs, audits, quality review and distribution records.

## 4.1 Complaints

A **complaint** is any written, electronic or oral report that a product may be defective, unsafe or not performing as expected.

**Sources:** patients, doctors, pharmacists, hospitals, distributors, sales staff, regulators.

**Types of complaints**

| Type | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Quality (product) complaints | Broken or chipped tablets, discolouration, foreign particles, leaking bottles, wrong count, cracked ampoules |
| Packaging and labelling complaints | Wrong label, missing batch number or expiry, misprint, leaflet missing |
| Medical / adverse event complaints | Lack of effect, unexpected side effects (passed to the pharmacovigilance team) |
| Counterfeit suspicion | Pack looks different from genuine product |
| Commercial complaints | Short supply, damaged transit cartons, wrong quantity |

### 4.1.1 Handling and evaluation of complaints

A written SOP names a responsible person (usually in QA) and lays out these steps:

```text
Receive complaint -> log it (number, date, complainant, product, batch, nature)
   -> acknowledge the complainant
   -> classify by seriousness (critical / major / minor)
   -> obtain the complaint sample if possible
   -> investigate:
        review batch manufacturing and packing records
        test retention (reserve) samples of the same batch
        test the complaint sample
        check other complaints on the same batch or product (trend)
        check deviations, changes and stability data
   -> find the root cause
   -> decide action: corrective and preventive action (CAPA),
      and whether a recall or regulator notification is needed
   -> reply to the complainant
   -> close the complaint; review complaints regularly for trends
```

**Classification by seriousness**

| Class | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Critical | Could cause serious harm or death | Wrong drug in pack, sterile product contaminated |
| Major | Could reduce effect or cause harm, but less serious | Low assay, dissolution failure |
| Minor | Little risk to the patient | Small print fault on the carton, minor surface spots |

**Evaluation points:** Is the defect real? Is it due to manufacture, storage, transport or misuse? Does it affect only one pack, the whole batch or other batches? Is there a risk to patients?

**Records:** each complaint is recorded with all findings, the decision and the action taken; complaint records are reviewed regularly (and in the product quality review) to spot repeated problems. If the investigation suggests a counterfeit, or a serious defect, the licensing authority is informed.

## 4.2 Handling of returned goods

**Returned goods** are products sent back from the market by distributors or customers: expired stock, damaged goods, near-expiry stock, wrongly supplied stock, or products under complaint or recall.

**Procedure**

1. Receive in a separate, locked "returned goods" area; record product, batch, quantity, reason, source and date.
2. Keep under quarantine until QA decides.
3. QA evaluates: nature of product, storage conditions it may have met in the market (for example temperature for heat-sensitive products), time out of the company's control, condition of the packs, remaining shelf life, and the reason for return.
4. Decision:
   - **Return to saleable stock** only if there is no doubt about quality: original packs unopened and in good condition, storage known to be proper, enough shelf life, QC check satisfactory (rarely done for products that need cold storage).
   - **Reprocessing / repacking** only under an approved procedure, if quality is not affected.
   - **Destruction** in all other cases.
5. Record the decision and action; returns are trended as a sign of problems.

## 4.3 Product recall

A **recall** is the action to remove from the market a batch or batches of a product that are defective, or suspected to be, or that violate the law. A recall may be started by the company (voluntary) or ordered by the regulator.

**Reasons for recall:** contamination (microbial, particulate, cross-contamination), wrong labelling or mix-up, sub-standard assay or dissolution, stability failure before expiry, sterility failure, a serious adverse reaction linked to the batch, a product declared spurious or adulterated, or a licence problem.

**Classes of recall** (based on health hazard; the class terms are those used by CDSCO's recall guidance, which follows international practice)

| Class | Hazard | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Class I | Reasonable probability that use will cause serious adverse health effects or death | Wrong active ingredient, contaminated injection, sterility failure |
| Class II | May cause temporary or reversible health effects, or a remote chance of serious harm | Sub-potent life-saving drug, dissolution failure |
| Class III | Not likely to cause adverse health effects | Minor labelling defect, wrong pack colour |

The guidance also sets the time within which each class of recall must reach each level and the depth of recall (wholesaler, retailer, consumer). Check the current CDSCO recall guidelines for these timelines.

**Recall procedure**

```text
Decision to recall (recall committee: QA head, production head, regulatory,
                    sales/distribution, medical)
   -> classify the recall and decide depth (wholesale / retail / patient level)
   -> inform the licensing authority
   -> use distribution records to find every consignee of the batch
   -> send recall letters / messages (product, batch, reason, what to do)
   -> public notice for Class I recalls where needed
   -> recalled stock received, kept separately in a locked area
   -> reconcile: quantity made vs distributed vs returned vs remaining in market
   -> check effectiveness of the recall
   -> investigate root cause and take CAPA
   -> destroy recalled goods under supervision; record
   -> final report to the authority
```

The recall system must be able to work quickly at any time, including outside working hours, and its effectiveness should be tested periodically by **mock recalls**.

## 4.4 Waste disposal

| Waste | Disposal |
| --- | --- |
| Rejected, returned and recalled products, expired stock | Destroyed under QA supervision by a method that prevents reuse or diversion (crushing, incineration or through an authorised waste handler), with a destruction record witnessed by responsible people |
| Printed packaging material with batch details or obsolete artwork | Shredded or burnt, recorded |
| Hazardous chemical waste, solvents | Through authorised hazardous-waste treatment facilities, as per the Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules and pollution control board consent |
| Biomedical waste (for example from microbiology labs) | Segregated, decontaminated (autoclaved) and disposed of under the Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules |
| Effluents | Treated in an effluent treatment plant to meet pollution control board norms |
| Narcotic and psychotropic products | Destroyed as the special law requires, with the authority informed |

Waste must be kept in closed, labelled containers in a designated area away from production, and removed regularly.

## 4.5 Documentation: purpose and general rules

"If it is not written down, it did not happen." Documents define the system (specifications, master formulae, SOPs) and records prove that it was followed (batch records, logs).

**Purpose of documentation**

- Gives clear written instructions so work is done the same way each time.
- Lets every batch be traced: what was used, who did what, when, with which equipment.
- Provides evidence for audits, inspections and investigations.
- Supports training and continual improvement.

**Good documentation practice (GDP), also summed up as ALCOA principles:** data must be **A**ttributable (who did it), **L**egible, **C**ontemporaneous (recorded at the time), **O**riginal and **A**ccurate. ALCOA+ adds complete, consistent, enduring and available.

**Rules**

1. Documents are designed, prepared, reviewed and distributed with care; approved, signed and dated by authorised people.
2. Written in clear, unambiguous language; title, number, version and effective date on each.
3. Entries made in indelible ink at the time of the activity; no blank spaces left (strike through unused space).
4. Corrections: single line through the wrong entry so it stays readable, correct value written beside, signed, dated and the reason given. No overwriting, erasing or correction fluid.
5. Controlled copies issued and obsolete versions withdrawn.
6. Electronic records protected by access control, backup and audit trails.
7. Records kept for the period required by law.

## 4.6 Master Formula Record (MFR)

The **Master Formula Record** is the master document, prepared and approved for each product and batch size, that lays down how the product is to be made. Each batch record is copied from it.

**Contents of an MFR**

| Section | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Product details | Name, dosage form, strength, generic name, product code, description, batch size |
| Signatures | Prepared by, checked by, approved by (production and QA), with dates |
| Formula | Name and quantity of every ingredient per unit and per batch, including overages with reasons; ingredients that disappear in processing (for example water) also listed |
| Specifications | References to specifications of raw and packing materials |
| Equipment | List of equipment and their capacities |
| Manufacturing instructions | Step-by-step method with process parameters (times, temperatures, speeds), precautions, in-process controls and their limits |
| Expected yield | Theoretical yield and acceptable limits at stages |
| Packing instructions | Pack size, packing materials with codes, quantities, line clearance, printing details, specimen of printed materials |
| Storage | Storage conditions and precautions |

## 4.7 Batch Formula Record / Batch Manufacturing Record (BMR)

The **batch record** (called the batch formula record in the syllabus; usually the Batch Manufacturing Record, BMR, plus a Batch Packing Record, BPR) is a copy of the current MFR issued for one batch and filled in while the batch is made. It is the history of that batch.

**Contents**

- Product name, batch number, batch size, dates of start and completion, manufacturing and expiry dates.
- Line clearance records before each stage (no material or document of the previous product remains).
- Each raw material: name, quantity required, quantity actually weighed, its receipt/QC number, signatures of the person weighing and the checker.
- Equipment used and its cleaning status.
- Each processing step with actual parameters, times, signatures of operator and supervisor.
- In-process control results.
- Yields at each stage and reconciliation; explanation of any deviation.
- Packing: packing materials issued, used, returned and destroyed (reconciliation of printed materials), specimen of the printed batch coding.
- QC test results, and final review and release by QA.

| MFR | BMR |
| --- | --- |
| Master document, one per product and batch size | Record of one particular batch |
| Instructions: what should be done | Evidence: what was actually done |
| Prepared once, revised through change control | Issued for each batch |
| Blank spaces for data do not apply | Filled in at the time of each step |

Under the older Schedule M, batch records were kept for at least one year after the expiry date of the batch. Confirm the retention period in the revised Schedule M.

## 4.8 Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)

An **SOP** is an authorised written procedure giving instructions for performing routine operations, not necessarily specific to one product (for example operation and cleaning of a tablet press, calibration of a balance, handling of complaints, gowning).

**Format of an SOP**

```text
+--------------------------------------------------------+
| Company name / logo         | SOP title                |
| SOP no.: QA/001  Version: 02 | Effective date / Review date |
| Department                  | Page x of y              |
+--------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. Objective (purpose)                                   |
| 2. Scope                                                 |
| 3. Responsibility                                        |
| 4. Accountability                                        |
| 5. Procedure (numbered steps)                            |
| 6. Precautions / safety                                  |
| 7. Abbreviations and definitions                         |
| 8. References                                            |
| 9. Annexures / formats                                   |
| 10. Revision history                                     |
| Prepared by | Checked by | Approved by (QA) - sign/date  |
+--------------------------------------------------------+
```

**SOP lifecycle:** drafting by the user department → review → approval by QA → training of users → issue of controlled copies → periodic review (for example every two or three years) → revision through change control, with the old version withdrawn and archived.

## 4.9 Quality audit

A **quality audit** is a systematic, independent and documented examination of whether quality activities and results comply with planned arrangements, and whether these arrangements are carried out effectively and are suitable to achieve objectives.

| Type | Who audits whom | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Internal audit (self-inspection) | Company's own trained team audits its departments | Check GMP compliance and find gaps before an inspector does |
| External / supplier (vendor) audit | Company audits its suppliers and contract manufacturers or labs | Approve and monitor vendors |
| Third-party / regulatory inspection | Regulator (CDSCO, state licensing authority, WHO, foreign agencies) or certification body audits the company | Licensing, certification, export approval |

**Audit process:** plan (schedule, scope, checklist) → opening meeting → collect evidence (observing, interviewing, checking records) → classify observations (critical, major, minor) → closing meeting → written report → auditee's CAPA plan → follow-up to verify closure.

## 4.10 Quality review and quality documentation

**Product quality review (PQR)** (also called annual product review) is a regular, usually yearly, review of each product to confirm the process is consistent and the specifications are still right. It covers:

- Review of starting and packing materials, especially from new sources.
- Critical in-process controls and finished product results (with trends).
- All batches that failed specification and their investigations.
- Significant deviations, non-conformances and CAPA.
- Changes made to processes or methods.
- Stability programme results.
- Complaints, returns and recalls.
- Adequacy of previous corrective actions.
- Qualification status of equipment and utilities.

The PQR leads to decisions on whether revalidation, changes or other action are needed.

**Management review** is a periodic review by senior management of the whole quality system (audit results, complaints, PQR outcomes, CAPA status) under ICH Q10 and ISO 9001.

**Hierarchy of quality documentation**

```text
            /\
           /  \       Level 1: Quality manual / quality policy
          /----\
         /      \     Level 2: Quality procedures (site master file,
        /--------\             validation master plan, SOPs)
       /          \   Level 3: Work instructions, specifications,
      /------------\           MFR, test methods
     /              \ Level 4: Records and forms (BMR, logs,
    /----------------\         analytical reports, certificates)
```

**Site master file:** a document prepared by the manufacturer containing specific information about its quality management, production and control activities at that site (general information, personnel, premises and equipment, documentation, production, QC, distribution, complaints, recalls, self-inspection).

## 4.11 Reports and documents

| Document | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Specifications | For raw materials, packing materials, intermediates, finished products: tests and limits |
| Certificate of analysis (CoA) | QC test results for a batch, with a conclusion of pass or fail, signed by an authorised person |
| Analytical records / test reports | Raw data of each test: weights, readings, chromatograms, calculations |
| Equipment log books | Use, cleaning, maintenance, calibration |
| Deviation reports | Unplanned departure from an approved procedure, its investigation and impact |
| Change control records | Proposed change, evaluation, approval, implementation, effectiveness check |
| CAPA records | Root cause, corrective action, preventive action, effectiveness check |
| Out-of-specification (OOS) reports | Investigation of results that fail specification |
| Validation protocols and reports | Plan and results of qualification and validation |
| Training records | Who was trained on what, when, with assessment |

## 4.12 Distribution records

**Distribution records** show for each batch to whom it was sent, how much and when. They must be kept so that any batch can be recalled quickly and completely.

**Contents:** product name, batch number, quantity dispatched, date of dispatch, name and address of consignee, invoice or delivery number, mode of transport, and for cold-chain products the transport conditions.

**Good distribution points:** dispatch on FEFO (first expiry, first out) basis; vehicles clean and suitable; temperature-sensitive goods in validated cold-chain containers with temperature records; records kept at least as long as batch records.

## Remember

- Complaints: log → classify (critical/major/minor) → investigate (records, retention samples, trends) → root cause → CAPA → reply → close; trend regularly.
- Returned goods: quarantine; resale only if there is no doubt about quality; otherwise reprocess under procedure or destroy.
- Recall: Class I (serious harm or death), Class II (temporary or reversible harm), Class III (no harm likely); uses distribution records; reconcile; mock recalls; inform the authority.
- MFR = master instructions; BMR = record of one batch; SOP = routine operations; ALCOA for records.
- Audits: internal (self-inspection), supplier, regulatory/third party.
- Product quality review = yearly review of all data on a product; site master file describes the site.
- Distribution records make recall possible; dispatch by FEFO.

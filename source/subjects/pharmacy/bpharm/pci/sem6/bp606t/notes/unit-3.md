# Unit 3: Quality control of packaging materials and Good Laboratory Practices

The container is part of the medicine. A good tablet in a leaky strip, or an injection in glass that sheds alkali, will fail before its expiry date. The first half of this unit covers the quality control tests for containers, rubber closures and secondary packing materials. The second half covers Good Laboratory Practice (GLP). The headings of the GLP topic follow the US FDA regulation 21 CFR Part 58 (Good Laboratory Practice for Nonclinical Laboratory Studies), and the same ideas appear in the OECD Principles of GLP and in Schedule L-1 of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules (Good Laboratory Practices for testing laboratories). Test limits change between editions, so take exact figures from the current Indian Pharmacopoeia (IP) when you need them.

## 3.1 Packaging: terms and requirements

| Term | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary packaging | In direct contact with the product | Glass vial, ampoule, bottle, blister foil, rubber stopper |
| Secondary packaging | Holds the primary pack; gives protection, information and branding | Carton, leaflet, label, shipper's inner box |
| Tertiary packaging | For bulk handling and transport | Corrugated shipper, pallet, stretch wrap |

A good container protects the product from light, moisture, oxygen, microbes and physical damage; does not react with, add to (leach) or take up (sorb) anything from the product; is compatible with sterilisation if needed; and is easy to use, tamper-evident and economical.

## 3.2 Quality control tests for glass containers

**Types of glass (IP)**

| Type | Composition | Hydrolytic resistance | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Type I | Borosilicate glass (neutral glass) | Highest | Most parenteral preparations, including those that must be autoclaved, blood products |
| Type II | Soda-lime glass with a surface treatment (de-alkalised surface) | High surface resistance | Many aqueous parenterals with pH below 7, if stable |
| Type III | Ordinary soda-lime glass | Moderate | Non-aqueous parenterals, powders for injection, oral and topical preparations |
| Type IV (general purpose soda-lime) | Soda-lime glass | Low | Oral and topical products only |

**Tests**

| Test | Purpose and principle |
| --- | --- |
| Hydrolytic resistance – powdered glass test | Glass is crushed and sieved to a set particle size, heated with purified water in an autoclave, and the alkali released is titrated with dilute acid. Measures the resistance of the glass itself (used to identify Type I and Type III) |
| Hydrolytic resistance – surface glass test (water attack test) | Containers are filled with water and autoclaved; the alkali released from the inner surface is titrated. Tests the surface, so it distinguishes Type II (treated surface) from others |
| Arsenic test | Limit test on the water extract of glass for injection containers, because arsenic can be present in glass batch materials |
| Light transmission test | For coloured (amber) glass: the percentage of light transmitted between about 290 and 450 nm must be below the limit, to show light protection |
| Thermal shock test | Containers are moved between hot and cold water baths to check they do not crack during sterilisation or cooling |
| Internal bursting pressure test | For containers that must stand internal pressure (aerosols, carbonated liquids) |
| Annealing test | Examination under polarised light (polariscope) to detect strain left in the glass |
| Dimensional checks and visual inspection | Neck finish, capacity, wall thickness; defects such as cracks, stones, bubbles, cords |

## 3.3 Quality control tests for plastic containers

Plastics (polyethylene, polypropylene, PVC, PET, polystyrene) are light and unbreakable but can be permeable to gases and water vapour, can leach additives and can sorb drugs or preservatives.

| Test | What it checks |
| --- | --- |
| Description and identification (for example infrared spectrum) | That the right polymer is used |
| Leakage test | Containers filled, closed and inverted or squeezed; no leakage |
| Collapsibility test (for containers that must be squeezed to empty, for example eye drops, IV bags) | Container empties the required proportion of its contents under pressure |
| Clarity / transparency of the container | Contents can be inspected visually (for parenteral containers) |
| Water vapour permeability | Gain or loss of weight of filled containers stored under set conditions; tells moisture protection |
| Clarity and colour of aqueous extract | Containers are extracted with water; extract must be clear and colourless |
| Physicochemical tests on the extract | Non-volatile residue, residue on ignition, heavy metals, buffering capacity, absorbance (UV), pH change |
| Biological (toxicity) tests | Systemic injection test and intracutaneous (irritation) test in animals using extracts; for parenteral and ophthalmic containers |
| Sorption / compatibility study | Loss of drug or preservative into the plastic during storage with the actual product |

Blister and strip packs are checked for foil thickness, heat-seal strength, pinholes, and leak test by vacuum (blue dye or methylene blue test: packs are immersed in dye under vacuum, then examined for dye entry).

## 3.4 Quality control tests for rubber closures

Rubber (elastomeric) closures seal vials and are pierced by needles. They contain the polymer plus fillers, vulcanising agents, accelerators, antioxidants and pigments, all of which can leach.

| Test | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Identification (for example IR of the extract, ash, density) | Correct elastomer formulation |
| Sterilisation | Closures must survive the stated sterilisation without change |
| Penetrability | The force needed to push a standard needle through the closure must not exceed the limit |
| Fragmentation (coring) | After repeated piercing with a needle, the number of rubber fragments seen in the vial must not exceed the limit |
| Self-sealability (resealing) | After piercing, the closure reseals so that the vial does not leak (tested by immersing in a dye solution under pressure) |
| Physicochemical tests on extract | Appearance of extract, acidity or alkalinity, absorbance, reducing substances, volatile sulfides, ammonium, heavy metals, residue on evaporation |
| Biological tests | Toxicity tests on extracts for parenteral use |
| Dimensions and hardness | Fit with the vial neck and seal |

Compatibility of the closure with the product (sorption of preservatives such as chlorocresol, leaching of extractables) is shown in stability studies.

## 3.5 Quality control of secondary packing materials

Secondary materials are mostly paper, board and corrugated fibreboard.

| Test | Material | Principle and purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grammage (GSM) | Paper, board | Mass per square metre; consistency of the material |
| Thickness (caliper) | Paper, board | Measured with a micrometer |
| Bursting strength | Board, corrugated box | Pressure needed to rupture the material (Mullen tester); resistance to rough handling |
| Compression strength | Corrugated boxes | Load a box can carry before collapse; decides safe stacking height |
| Cobb test | Paper, board | Water absorbed by a set area in a set time; resistance to moisture |
| Moisture content | Board | Too much makes boxes weak; too little makes them brittle |
| Tearing and folding endurance | Cartons, leaflets | Withstands use without tearing at folds |
| Drop test and vibration test | Packed shipper | Product survives dropping and transport vibration |
| Print and text checks | Labels, cartons, leaflets | Matches the approved artwork: name, strength, batch, dates, legal text, barcode; colour shade against standard |

Printed materials have the highest risk of **mix-ups**, so they are checked against the approved "master" artwork, stored securely, counted on issue and reconciled after packing, and excess printed batch-coded material is destroyed with records.

## 3.6 Good Laboratory Practice (GLP): introduction

**Definition.** GLP is a quality system covering the organisational process and the conditions under which **non-clinical** health and environmental safety studies are planned, performed, monitored, recorded, archived and reported.

**Why GLP began:** in the 1970s, inspections in the USA found non-clinical (animal) safety studies submitted to the FDA that had poor or even fabricated data. This led the FDA to issue GLP regulations (21 CFR Part 58, in force from 1979). The OECD Principles of GLP followed, so that data from one member country are accepted in others.

**Aim:** to make study data **reliable, reproducible, traceable and auditable**, so a regulator can reconstruct exactly what was done.

**Scope:** toxicity studies (acute, sub-acute, chronic), carcinogenicity, reproductive toxicity, mutagenicity, safety pharmacology, toxicokinetics and similar studies done to support marketing or clinical-trial applications. GLP does not cover basic exploratory research or clinical studies on humans (those follow GCP).

**India:** Schedule L-1 of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules lays down GLP requirements for drug testing laboratories. India is also a full member of the OECD Mutual Acceptance of Data system for GLP through its national GLP compliance monitoring programme (check the current status with the National GLP Compliance Monitoring Authority).

## 3.7 The parts of the GLP regulation (21 CFR Part 58)

### 3.7.1 General provisions (Subpart A)

States the **scope** (non-clinical laboratory studies that support applications for research or marketing permits), gives **definitions**, and says that the testing facility must allow **inspection** by the regulator and that a sponsor must state whether each study was done under GLP.

**Key definitions**

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Nonclinical laboratory study | In vivo or in vitro experiment in which test articles are studied prospectively in test systems under laboratory conditions to find their safety |
| Test article | The drug, additive or product being tested |
| Control article | Anything other than the test article given to the test system for comparison (placebo, vehicle, reference) |
| Test system | Any animal, plant, microorganism or part of them (cells, tissues) to which the article is given |
| Sponsor | The person or company that starts and supports the study |
| Testing facility | The place where the study is done |
| Study director | The individual with overall responsibility for the technical conduct of the study |
| Quality assurance unit (QAU) | Any person or group designated to monitor the study for compliance; independent of the study staff |
| Raw data | Original records and observations that are needed to reconstruct the study |
| Specimen | Material taken from the test system for examination |

### 3.7.2 Organization and personnel (Subpart B)

**Personnel:** education, training and experience suited to their tasks; a current summary of training and experience plus a job description for each person; enough staff; hygiene and protective clothing; anyone ill excluded from contact with test systems.

**Testing facility management:** designates the study director (and replaces them if needed), ensures a QAU exists, ensures test and control articles are tested for identity and strength, ensures personnel, resources, facilities and methods are available, and ensures deviations reported by the QAU are acted on.

**Study director:** the single point of control. Ensures the protocol is followed and approved changes documented; all data are recorded accurately; unforeseen events are noted with corrective action; test systems are as described in the protocol; GLP is followed; and raw data, documents, specimens and the final report go to the archives at the end.

**Quality assurance unit:**

- Keeps a **master schedule sheet** of all studies at the facility (test article, test system, nature of study, start date, current status, sponsor, study director).
- Keeps copies of all protocols.
- **Inspects** each study at intervals suited to it, and reports problems to the study director and management.
- Reports periodically on each study's status.
- Checks that no deviations were made from protocols or SOPs without proper authorisation.
- **Reviews the final report** to make sure it describes the methods accurately and reflects the raw data, and signs a QA statement in the report.
- Its people are not involved in conducting the study they monitor.

### 3.7.3 Facilities (Subpart C)

- Size, construction and location suited to the work; separation so that activities do not interfere with each other.
- **Animal care facilities:** separate rooms for different species, for different studies, for quarantine of new animals, and for isolating diseased animals; rooms for handling biohazardous materials; storage of feed, bedding and supplies away from pests; safe waste disposal.
- Separate areas for receipt and storage of test and control articles, and for mixing them with carriers (for example feed).
- **Laboratory operation areas** separate for each kind of work.
- **Specimen and data storage facilities (archives)** with limited access.
- Administrative and personnel areas.

### 3.7.4 Equipment (Subpart D)

- Equipment of appropriate design and capacity, located for operation, inspection, cleaning and maintenance.
- Regular inspection, cleaning, maintenance, testing, calibration and standardisation, as per written SOPs that name the person responsible and the remedy when equipment fails.
- Written records of all these operations and of any non-routine repair.

### 3.7.5 Testing facilities operation (Subpart E)

- **Standard operating procedures** for all routine methods: animal room preparation, animal care, receipt and identification of test and control articles, test system observations, laboratory tests, handling of moribund or dead animals, necropsy, specimen collection and identification, histopathology, data handling and storage, equipment maintenance and calibration, animal transfer and identification.
- SOPs available in each work area; deviations authorised by the study director and documented; historical files of SOPs kept.
- **Reagents and solutions** labelled with identity, concentration, storage requirements and expiry date.
- **Animal care:** proper housing, feeding, handling and identification (each animal identified uniquely, for example by ear tag); newly received animals isolated and checked; analysis of feed and water for contaminants that could interfere with the study.

### 3.7.6 Test and control articles (Subpart F)

- **Characterisation:** identity, strength, purity and composition of each batch known and documented, with methods of synthesis or source; stability determined.
- Each container labelled with name, code or batch number, expiry date where appropriate, and storage conditions.
- **Handling:** procedures for receipt, storage, distribution and accountability so that contamination, deterioration and mix-ups are avoided; records of quantities received and used.
- **Mixtures with carriers** (for example drug in feed): uniformity, concentration and stability of the mixture determined by analysis at intervals.

### 3.7.7 Protocol for and conduct of a nonclinical laboratory study (Subpart G)

Every study has an approved written **protocol** that states:

1. Descriptive title and purpose of the study.
2. Identification of the test and control articles.
3. Name of the sponsor and name and address of the testing facility.
4. Number, body weight range, sex, source, species, strain and age of the test system.
5. Procedure for identifying the test system.
6. Experimental design and methods to control bias.
7. Type and frequency of tests, analyses and measurements.
8. Records to be kept.
9. Dose levels (in mg/kg or other units), method and frequency of administration, and route.
10. Statistical methods.
11. Date of approval by the sponsor and dated signature of the study director.

**Conduct:** the study follows the protocol; specimens are identified by test system, study, nature and date of collection; all data are recorded directly, promptly and legibly in ink, dated and signed or initialled by the person entering them; any change to an entry must not obscure the original, must state the reason, and must be dated and signed. Computer-entered data must identify the person responsible and keep an audit trail.

### 3.7.8 Records and reports (Subpart J)

**Final report** contains: name and address of the facility, study dates, objectives and procedures as in the protocol with any changes, statistical methods, test and control article identification and characterisation, stability information, methods used, test system details, dosage and route, unforeseen circumstances, a description of how data were transformed, a summary and analysis of the data with conclusions, names of the study director and scientists, reports of each scientist involved, the location of stored specimens and raw data, and the **QA statement**. It is signed and dated by the study director. Corrections after signing are made as signed and dated amendments.

**Storage and retrieval of records:** all raw data, documentation, protocols, specimens and final reports are kept in **archives** for orderly storage and rapid retrieval, under an individual responsible for the archives, with limited access. The regulation sets minimum retention periods tied to the dates of approval or submission of the application; quote them from the current regulation if asked.

### 3.7.9 Disqualification of testing facilities (Subpart K)

The purpose of disqualification is to exclude from consideration data from studies done by a facility that has failed to comply with GLP, when that failure affects the validity of the data.

**Grounds:** the facility failed to comply with one or more GLP requirements; the non-compliance adversely affected the validity of the studies; and other lesser measures (warnings, rejection of individual studies) have not been or will not be adequate.

**Consequences:** the regulator notifies the facility and gives an opportunity for a hearing; studies done at a disqualified facility are presumed unacceptable unless shown otherwise; the regulator may inform other agencies and sponsors; it may also take legal action. **Reinstatement** is possible when the facility shows in writing, and on inspection, that it has corrected the problems and will comply.

```text
GLP at a glance

People (management, study director, QAU, trained staff)
  + Places (separate facilities, animal rooms, archives)
  + Things (calibrated equipment, characterised test articles, labelled reagents)
  + Paper (SOPs, approved protocol, raw data, final report, QA statement)
  = Reliable, reconstructable non-clinical safety data
```

## Remember

- Glass: Type I borosilicate (best), Type II surface-treated soda-lime, Type III soda-lime, Type IV general purpose. Powdered glass test checks the bulk glass; surface (water attack) test checks the inner surface.
- Plastics: leakage, collapsibility, water vapour permeability, extract tests, biological tests, sorption.
- Rubber closures: penetrability, fragmentation (coring), self-sealability, extract tests, biological tests.
- Secondary packing: GSM, bursting strength, compression strength, Cobb test, moisture, drop and vibration tests, artwork check.
- GLP = 21 CFR Part 58 / OECD principles / Schedule L-1; for non-clinical safety studies.
- Study director = single point of control; QAU = independent monitor with master schedule, inspections and final-report review.
- Protocol must be approved before the study starts; raw data recorded promptly, in ink, signed and dated; changes must not obscure the original.
- Disqualification only when non-compliance affects data validity and lesser measures are not enough; reinstatement is possible.

# Unit 2: Organization, personnel, premises, equipment and raw materials

GMP rests on four foundations: trained people, suitable buildings, fit equipment and good starting materials. If any one is weak, testing at the end cannot rescue the product. This unit follows the order of the syllabus: organization and personnel, premises (including sterile areas), and equipment and raw materials. The Indian requirements come from Schedule M of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945, which closely follows WHO GMP. Schedule M was revised in December 2023; always check the current text on cdsco.gov.in for exact figures.

## 2.1 Organization

A manufacturing unit must have an organisation chart that shows who reports to whom, with written job descriptions for every key post.

```text
Typical organisation of a pharmaceutical plant

                     Managing Director / Plant Head
                                  |
      +-----------+---------------+---------------+------------+
      |           |               |               |            |
  Production   Quality         Quality        Engineering   Stores /
   Head        Assurance Head  Control Head   (maintenance)  Warehouse
      |           |               |
  Supervisors  QA officers     Analysts,
  Operators    (IPQA, document  microbiologists
               control, audits)

QA and QC heads are independent of the Production head.
```

**Key points**

- The heads of **Production** and **Quality** (QA/QC) must be different people, and neither reports to the other. This separation stops pressure to release a batch that should be rejected.
- The **Authorised Person / person responsible for batch release** (under the revised Schedule M this role sits within the quality unit) certifies that each batch was made and tested as required before it is released for sale.
- Key posts must be held by full-time staff with the qualifications and experience that the licence rules require (for example a degree in pharmacy or chemistry for the technical staff named on the licence).
- Duties must not overlap in a way that leaves gaps, and deputies must be named for absences.

**Responsibilities of the main heads**

| Head of Production | Head of Quality Control | Head of Quality Assurance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ensure products are made and stored as per approved documents (MFR, SOPs) | Approve or reject starting materials, packaging materials, intermediates and finished products | Approve SOPs, master documents and changes |
| Approve production instructions and make sure they are followed | Approve specifications, sampling instructions and test methods | Run the quality system: deviations, CAPA, change control, self-inspection |
| Check that batch records are completed and signed before they go to QA | Make sure all tests are done and records checked | Release or reject batches after review of the batch record |
| Make sure premises and equipment are maintained, and validations done | Ensure validation of methods and calibration of instruments | Oversee validation, training, vendor approval, complaints and recalls |
| Make sure staff are trained | Manage retention samples and stability studies | Conduct product quality review and audits |

Shared responsibilities: authorising written procedures, monitoring the factory environment, plant hygiene, process validation, training, vendor approval, and storage conditions.

## 2.2 Personnel

### 2.2.1 Qualifications and responsibilities

- Enough staff with the right education, training and experience for each task.
- Each person's duties written in a job description; nobody given so many duties that quality suffers.
- Key personnel (production head, QC head, QA head) have the qualifications laid down in the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules.

### 2.2.2 Training

| Type of training | Who | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Induction | All new joiners | GMP basics, company policy, hygiene, safety |
| Job-specific (on-the-job) | Operators, analysts | SOPs of their equipment and tasks, under supervision |
| GMP refresher | All staff, at set intervals | Updates in GMP, case studies, past deviations |
| Special | Staff in sterile areas, people handling potent, toxic or sensitising materials | Aseptic technique, gowning, containment |
| Visitors and contractors | Untrained people entering production | Briefing and supervision |

Training needs are identified, a yearly plan is made, sessions are given, and **effectiveness is assessed** (written test, observation). Records of every training are kept in the person's training file.

### 2.2.3 Hygiene

1. **Medical examination** before employment and at regular intervals; eye test for people doing visual inspection.
2. Anyone with an infectious disease, open lesion or skin wound on an exposed surface must not handle products; staff must report illness.
3. **Protective clothing** suited to the area: overalls, head cover, beard cover, face mask, gloves, shoes; sterile gowns for aseptic areas.
4. **No eating, drinking, chewing, smoking**, storing food or personal medicines in production, storage and laboratory areas.
5. No jewellery, wrist watches, cosmetics or loose items in production areas.
6. Hand washing and sanitising; direct contact of hands with exposed product avoided.
7. Change rooms with step-over benches, and gowning procedures displayed.
8. Visitors and untrained people kept out, or supervised and gowned.

### 2.2.4 Personal records

Each employee has a personal file: application and appointment letter, qualifications, experience, job description, medical fitness records, training records and assessments, and authorisation for specific tasks (for example, sign-off authority). These records show an inspector that the person is competent for the work they sign for.

## 2.3 Premises

### 2.3.1 Location and surroundings

- Away from open sewers, drains, public lavatories, dusty roads and factories that give off smoke, dust or odour.
- Protected against entry of insects, birds, rodents and other animals.
- Surroundings kept clean, with good drainage.

### 2.3.2 Design and construction

| Feature | Requirement and reason |
| --- | --- |
| Walls and ceilings | Smooth, impervious, free from cracks, easy to clean and disinfect; coving at wall–floor and wall–ceiling joints so dust cannot settle |
| Floors | Smooth, hard, impervious, non-slip, chemical-resistant (for example epoxy) |
| Doors and windows | Flush, well fitted; windows in clean areas sealed; no uncleanable ledges |
| Pipes, ducts, lights | Designed so they do not create recesses; services reached from outside the clean area where possible |
| Drains | Of adequate size with trapped gullies; avoided in aseptic areas |
| Lighting | Adequate for the work |
| Space | Enough to place equipment and materials in an orderly way, with no mix-ups |

### 2.3.3 Plant layout

Rooms are placed in the logical order of operations so that materials move in one direction, people and materials have separate routes, and clean and less clean areas do not cross.

```text
One-way flow (simplified layout for oral solid dosage forms)

Receiving -> Quarantine -> Sampling -> Approved stores -> Dispensing (weighing)
     -> Granulation -> Drying -> Blending -> Compression -> Coating
     -> Primary packing -> Secondary packing -> Finished goods (quarantine)
     -> Release -> Dispatch

Separate: QC laboratory, change rooms, toilets (not opening into production),
          rest rooms, maintenance workshop, rejected-material store (locked)
```

**Layout principles:** separate areas for receiving, quarantine, approved, rejected and returned materials; a separate sampling booth; segregated or dedicated facilities for highly sensitising products (for example penicillins, cephalosporins), certain hormones, cytotoxics and biologicals; air locks between areas of different cleanliness; QC laboratories separate from production; ancillary areas (canteen, toilets) not opening directly into production.

### 2.3.4 Maintenance and sanitation

- Written sanitation programme: what to clean, how, how often, with which agent, and who is responsible.
- Validated cleaning and disinfection; disinfectants rotated in clean areas to avoid resistant organisms.
- Pest control programme (rodent baits outside, insect-o-cutors at entrances), with records.
- Preventive maintenance of the building: repair of cracks, peeling paint and leaks; maintenance must not put products at risk.
- Waste removed regularly to a designated area in closed containers.

### 2.3.5 Environmental control and utilities

**Heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC)** controls temperature, relative humidity, particle count, air movement and pressure differences. It is one of the most important GMP systems.

| Control | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Filtration (pre-filters, fine filters, HEPA terminal filters in clean areas) | Remove particles and microbes |
| Pressure differential | Air flows from cleaner to less clean area; containment rooms may be kept at lower pressure to keep powder in |
| Air changes per hour | Dilute and remove particles |
| Temperature and RH | Product stability (hygroscopic, effervescent products need low RH) and operator comfort |
| Dust extraction at source | Control of cross-contamination during dispensing and compression |

**Utilities** that touch the product must be qualified and monitored:

- **Water systems:** potable water → purified water (for oral preparations, cleaning) → water for injections (for parenterals, made by distillation or another method the pharmacopoeia allows), stored and circulated to avoid microbial growth, tested regularly.
- **Compressed air and nitrogen:** oil-free, filtered, tested for particles, moisture and microbes where they contact the product.
- **Steam:** clean steam for sterilisers.
- Electric supply with backup for critical equipment.

### 2.3.6 Sterile areas

Sterile products are made in clean rooms graded by the number of airborne particles. Revised Schedule M uses Grades A to D, as in WHO and EU GMP.

| Grade | Typical use | Max particles ≥0.5 µm per m³ (at rest) | Approximate ISO class at rest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | High-risk operations: filling, open ampoules and vials, aseptic connections (usually under unidirectional airflow) | 3,520 | ISO 5 |
| B | Background environment for Grade A in aseptic preparation and filling | 3,520 | ISO 5 |
| C | Less critical steps, for example preparing solutions to be filtered | 352,000 | ISO 7 |
| D | Handling components after washing, less critical steps | 3,520,000 | ISO 8 |

Limits for particles ≥5.0 µm, the "in operation" limits and microbial action limits differ between versions of the guidance; take them from the current Schedule M or the WHO/EU annex on sterile products.

**Requirements for sterile areas**

- Entry only through air locks and change rooms, with stepwise gowning (sterile garments in Grade B).
- Unidirectional (laminar) airflow over Grade A operations, with a set air velocity.
- Positive pressure towards less clean areas (a difference of the order of 10–15 Pa between adjacent rooms of different grades is commonly used).
- No sinks or drains in Grade A/B areas; minimal equipment; smooth sealed surfaces.
- Interlocked doors in air locks, pass boxes for materials, HEPA filters tested for integrity.
- **Environmental monitoring:** non-viable particle counts, viable counts by settle plates, active air sampling, contact plates and glove prints; temperature, RH and pressure differentials recorded.
- Aseptic process simulation (media fill) to show the process can produce sterile product.
- Minimum number of people inside; their movements slow and controlled.

### 2.3.7 Control of contamination

| Type | Sources | Control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cross-contamination (one product into another) | Dust, residues on equipment, air recirculation, people, shared tools | Campaign manufacture with cleaning, dedicated facilities for sensitising drugs, air locks and pressure cascades, dust extraction, validated cleaning, closed systems |
| Microbial contamination | People, water, air, raw materials, equipment | Hygiene, gowning, HEPA filtration, water system control, sanitisation, microbial limits on raw materials |
| Particulate / foreign matter | Fibres, glass, metal, paint flakes | Smooth surfaces, sieves and metal detectors, inspection |
| Mix-ups | Similar labels, many products in one area | Line clearance, status labels, segregation, one product per line |

## 2.4 Equipment

### 2.4.1 Selection

Equipment must be suited to its purpose and installed so that it:

- Has the capacity and precision the process needs (for example a balance of the right range and readability).
- Has product-contact parts that do not react with, add to or absorb from the product (usually stainless steel 316L for contact parts; gaskets of approved materials).
- Can be cleaned easily, with no dead legs or crevices; can be dismantled for cleaning; clean-in-place where possible.
- Does not shed lubricants or particles into the product.
- Meets safety and environmental needs.

### 2.4.2 Purchase specification (user requirement specification)

The **user requirement specification (URS)** is written before purchase. It states: purpose and capacity, operating ranges, materials of construction, cleanability, safety features, controls and data-recording needs (data integrity for computerised equipment), utilities needed, documents to be supplied (manuals, drawings, material certificates, calibration certificates), training and after-sales support, and qualification support (IQ/OQ protocols). The URS is the basis for design qualification (Unit 5).

### 2.4.3 Maintenance

- Written **preventive maintenance** programme with a schedule for each item; records in the equipment log book.
- Calibration of measuring instruments at set intervals against traceable standards, with calibration labels showing the next due date.
- **Equipment log books** record use (product, batch), cleaning, maintenance and breakdowns.
- **Status labels**: "Cleaned", "To be cleaned", "Under maintenance", "Out of order". Defective equipment removed or clearly labelled.
- Repairs done so they do not affect the product; equipment re-qualified after major repair or modification.

## 2.5 Raw materials and stores

### 2.5.1 Purchase specifications for raw materials

Every starting material (active ingredient, excipient, packaging material) has an approved specification stating:

- Name, internal code, pharmacopoeial or in-house reference.
- Description, identification, assay, impurities and other tests with limits.
- Grade and particle size (where it matters).
- Approved suppliers (manufacturers) only.
- Storage conditions, retest period or shelf life, and precautions.
- Packing requirements and labelling expected from the supplier.

**Vendor (supplier) approval:** materials are bought only from approved suppliers. Approval rests on samples tested against specification, a questionnaire, a site audit for critical materials, and later on performance (rejection rate, timeliness). Each delivery should come with the supplier's certificate of analysis, but the company still tests it (at least identity for every container of the active, according to its sampling plan).

### 2.5.2 Maintenance of stores

```text
Material path in the stores

Receipt (check documents, containers, seals, labels; clean containers)
   -> Goods receipt note, own label with internal code/receipt number
   -> QUARANTINE (yellow label)
   -> Sampling in a sampling booth under laminar airflow
   -> QC testing
   -> APPROVED (green label)  or  REJECTED (red label, locked area)
   -> Issue by FEFO/FIFO against a requisition
```

**Good stores practice**

- Enough space, separate areas for quarantine, approved, rejected, recalled and returned goods.
- Storage conditions controlled and recorded (room temperature, cool, cold room 2–8 °C as required); temperature-mapped areas.
- Materials on pallets or racks, off the floor and away from walls, for cleaning and pest control.
- Flammable materials and solvents in a separate fire-safe store; narcotics and psychotropics in locked storage as the law requires.
- Printed packaging materials (labels, cartons) stored securely and issued against counts to prevent mix-ups.
- Stock records (bin cards or computer system) that reconcile receipts, issues and balance.
- Expired materials or materials past their retest date are not issued without re-testing.

## Remember

- Production head and quality heads are different people, independent of each other.
- Personnel: qualified, trained (induction, job-specific, refresher, assessed), medically fit, hygienic, with personal records.
- Premises: suitable site, smooth cleanable finishes, one-way flow, segregation for penicillins and other sensitisers, HVAC with pressure cascades, qualified utilities.
- Sterile areas: Grades A–D; at rest ≥0.5 µm limits 3,520 / 3,520 / 352,000 / 3,520,000 per m³; Grade A under unidirectional airflow with Grade B background; environmental monitoring and media fills.
- Equipment: fit for purpose, cleanable, non-reactive (SS 316L), URS before purchase, preventive maintenance, calibration, log books and status labels.
- Raw materials: approved specification and approved vendors; quarantine → sampling → approved/rejected labels; FEFO/FIFO.

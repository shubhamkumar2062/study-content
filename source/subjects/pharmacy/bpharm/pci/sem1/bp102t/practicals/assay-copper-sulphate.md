# Assay of copper sulphate by iodometry

**Aim:** To determine the percentage purity of copper sulphate (CuSO4·5H2O) by iodometric titration with 0.1 M sodium thiosulphate.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

Cupric ions oxidise iodide in weakly acid solution, forming white cuprous iodide and liberating iodine:

2Cu²⁺ + 4I⁻ → 2CuI + I2

The iodine is titrated with standard sodium thiosulphate using starch:

I2 + 2S2O3²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S4O6²⁻

2Cu ≡ I2 ≡ 2S2O3²⁻, so Cu : thiosulphate = 1 : 1. **1 ml of 0.1 M Na2S2O3 ≡ 0.02497 g CuSO4·5H2O** (Mw 249.68).

**Acetic acid** keeps the solution weakly acidic (strong mineral acid would let air oxidise iodide; alkali would precipitate copper hydroxide). **Potassium thiocyanate**, added near the end, converts CuI to the less soluble CuSCN and releases iodine adsorbed on the precipitate.

## Requirements

Copper sulphate sample, potassium iodide, acetic acid, potassium thiocyanate, starch solution, 0.1 M sodium thiosulphate (standardised), iodine flask, burette, analytical balance.

## Procedure

1. Standardise the thiosulphate (see its guide).
2. Accurately weigh about 1 g of copper sulphate into an iodine flask; dissolve in 50 ml water.
3. Add 5 ml of acetic acid and 3 g of potassium iodide; stopper, mix and keep for 2–3 minutes.
4. Titrate the liberated iodine with 0.1 M thiosulphate until the brown colour becomes pale yellow (the white CuI precipitate is visible).
5. Add 2 ml of starch solution (blue) and about 1 g of potassium thiocyanate (or 10 ml of its solution); the blue deepens as adsorbed iodine is released.
6. Continue titrating until the blue disappears, leaving a creamy white precipitate.
7. Repeat for concordant results.

## Precautions

1. Use excess KI and a stoppered flask to avoid loss of iodine.
2. Add starch only near the end point.
3. Add KSCN only near the end; added early it can reduce some iodine.
4. Do not use strong mineral acid.

## Viva questions

1. **Is this iodimetry or iodometry?** Iodometry — liberated iodine is titrated.
2. **Why add KSCN?** To release iodine adsorbed on CuI by converting it to CuSCN.
3. **What is the white precipitate?** Cuprous iodide (CuI), later CuSCN.
4. **Why acetic acid?** To keep the solution weakly acidic without oxidation of iodide by air.
5. **Equivalent weight of CuSO4·5H2O here?** Molecular weight, 249.68 (one electron per Cu).

# Preparation and standardisation of 0.02 M potassium permanganate

**Aim:** To prepare 1000 ml of approximately 0.02 M (0.1 N) potassium permanganate and standardise it with oxalic acid.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves; handle hot solutions with care.

## Principle

KMnO4 is not a primary standard (it contains some MnO2, and its solutions are decomposed by light, heat and organic matter). It is standardised against **oxalic acid dihydrate** (H2C2O4·2H2O, Mw 126.07) in dilute **sulphuric acid** at 60–70 °C.

2MnO4⁻ + 5C2O4²⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO2 + 8H2O

The reaction is slow at first and speeds up as Mn²⁺ forms (**autocatalysis**). KMnO4 acts as its **own indicator**: the first permanent pink marks the end point.

In acid, n = 5 for permanganate, so 0.02 M = 0.1 N. For oxalic acid n = 2.

**1 ml of 0.02 M KMnO4 ≡ 0.006303 g H2C2O4·2H2O** (126.07/2 × 0.1/1000).

## Requirements

KMnO4, oxalic acid dihydrate (AR), dilute sulphuric acid, distilled water, beakers, glass wool, amber bottle, burette (glass stopcock), conical flasks, thermometer, water bath or hot plate.

## Procedure

**Preparation**

1. Weigh about 3.2 g of KMnO4 (0.02 × 158.03 = 3.16 g), dissolve in 1000 ml water.
2. Heat on a water bath for about an hour (or boil for 15 minutes), cover and keep for 2 days to oxidise organic matter.
3. Filter through glass wool (not filter paper, which reduces permanganate) into an amber bottle.

**Standardisation**

4. Accurately weigh about 0.15–0.16 g oxalic acid dihydrate into a conical flask.
5. Dissolve in 50 ml water and add 20 ml of dilute H2SO4.
6. Warm to 60–70 °C.
7. Titrate with KMnO4 slowly at first (wait for each drop to decolourise), then faster, to a faint pink that persists for 30 seconds.
8. Repeat for concordant readings. Read the upper meniscus (the solution is deeply coloured).

## Precautions

1. Use H2SO4 only — HCl is oxidised to chlorine, HNO3 is itself an oxidant.
2. Keep the temperature at 60–70 °C; above about 80 °C oxalic acid decomposes.
3. A brown precipitate (MnO2) means too little acid or too fast addition.
4. Use a burette with a glass stopcock (permanganate attacks rubber).
5. Read the upper meniscus.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is KMnO4 a self-indicator?** MnO4⁻ is intensely purple and Mn²⁺ almost colourless; one excess drop gives pink.
2. **Why heat the oxalic acid solution?** The reaction is slow at room temperature.
3. **What is autocatalysis?** A product (Mn²⁺) catalyses the reaction, so it speeds up once started.
4. **Why filter through glass wool?** Paper reduces permanganate to MnO2.
5. **What is the normality of 0.02 M KMnO4 in acid?** 0.1 N.

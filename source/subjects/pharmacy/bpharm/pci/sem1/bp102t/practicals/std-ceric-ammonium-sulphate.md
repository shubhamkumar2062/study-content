# Preparation and standardisation of 0.1 M ceric ammonium sulphate

**Aim:** To prepare 1000 ml of approximately 0.1 M ceric ammonium sulphate and standardise it against arsenic trioxide.

> Arsenic trioxide is highly poisonous and osmic acid (osmium tetroxide) is toxic and volatile. Use only under supervision, in a fume hood, with gloves and goggles. Your lab may use ferrous ammonium sulphate standardisation instead.

## Principle

Ceric ions are a strong one-electron oxidant in sulphuric acid:

Ce⁴⁺ + e⁻ → Ce³⁺ (n = 1, so 0.1 M = 0.1 N)

Ceric ammonium sulphate is standardised against **arsenic trioxide** (As2O3, Mw 197.84), a primary standard. As2O3 is dissolved in sodium hydroxide (as arsenite), acidified, and titrated with the ceric solution. The reaction is slow, so a trace of **osmic acid** is added as a catalyst. **Ferroin** shows the end point (pink/red → pale blue).

2Ce⁴⁺ + As³⁺ → 2Ce³⁺ + As⁵⁺ (each As loses 2 electrons; As2O3 contains 2 As → 4 electrons)

**1 ml of 0.1 M ceric ammonium sulphate ≡ 0.004946 g As2O3** (197.84/4 × 0.1/1000).

## Requirements

Ceric ammonium sulphate, concentrated sulphuric acid, arsenic trioxide (dried at 105 °C), sodium hydroxide solution (about 8% w/v), dilute sulphuric acid, osmic acid solution, ferroin solution, burette, conical flasks, 1000 ml volumetric flask.

## Procedure

**Preparation**

1. Dissolve about 65 g of ceric ammonium sulphate, with gentle heat, in a mixture of 30 ml concentrated H2SO4 and about 500 ml water.
2. Cool, filter if turbid, and make up to 1000 ml with water.

**Standardisation**

3. Accurately weigh about 0.2 g of As2O3 into a conical flask.
4. Add 25 ml of 8% w/v NaOH solution and swirl to dissolve.
5. Add 100 ml water and 30 ml dilute H2SO4.
6. Add 0.15 ml of osmic acid solution and 0.1 ml of ferroin.
7. Titrate with the ceric solution until the pink colour changes to a very pale blue. Add the titrant slowly near the end.
8. Repeat for concordant readings.

## Precautions

1. Handle As2O3 and osmic acid with extreme care; dispose of waste as directed.
2. Keep the ceric solution strongly acidic; ceric salts hydrolyse in weak acid.
3. Add titrant slowly near the end point for a sharp change.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is osmic acid added?** It catalyses the slow reaction between Ce⁴⁺ and arsenite.
2. **Colour change of ferroin?** Red/pink (ferrous form) → pale blue (ferric form).
3. **Why is ceric sulphate stable?** It does not decompose on standing or boiling, unlike permanganate.
4. **Can ceric titrations be done in HCl?** Yes, chloride is not oxidised in the cold.
5. **What other standard can be used?** Ferrous ammonium sulphate (standardised) or sodium oxalate.

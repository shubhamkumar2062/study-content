# Assay of ferrous sulphate by cerimetry

**Aim:** To determine the percentage purity of ferrous sulphate (FeSO4·7H2O) by titration with 0.1 M ceric ammonium sulphate.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

Ferrous ions are oxidised to ferric ions by ceric ions in sulphuric acid solution:

Fe²⁺ + Ce⁴⁺ → Fe³⁺ + Ce³⁺ (1 : 1)

**Ferroin** (1,10-phenanthroline ferrous sulphate) is the indicator: it stays red while Fe²⁺ is present and is oxidised to pale blue by the first excess Ce⁴⁺.

**1 ml of 0.1 M ceric ammonium sulphate ≡ 0.02780 g FeSO4·7H2O** (Mw 278.01).

Freshly boiled and cooled water and acid are used so that dissolved oxygen does not oxidise Fe²⁺ before titration.

## Requirements

Ferrous sulphate sample, 0.1 M ceric ammonium sulphate (standardised), dilute sulphuric acid, freshly boiled and cooled water, ferroin solution, burette, conical flasks, analytical balance.

## Procedure

1. Standardise the ceric ammonium sulphate solution (see its guide).
2. Accurately weigh about 0.5 g of ferrous sulphate.
3. Dissolve in a mixture of 30 ml dilute H2SO4 and 20 ml freshly boiled and cooled water.
4. Add 1–2 drops of ferroin.
5. Titrate immediately with 0.1 M ceric ammonium sulphate until the red colour changes to pale blue (greenish-blue).
6. Repeat for concordant readings.

## Precautions

1. Titrate soon after dissolving; Fe²⁺ is slowly oxidised by air.
2. Use boiled and cooled water.
3. Add the indicator just before titration and titrate slowly near the end.
4. Crystals that have turned brown (oxidised) give low results.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is ferroin used?** It changes sharply from red to pale blue at a potential (about 1.1 V) between those of Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ and Ce⁴⁺/Ce³⁺.
2. **Why boiled water?** To remove dissolved oxygen, which oxidises Fe²⁺.
3. **Why sulphuric acid?** Ceric salts must be kept acidic to avoid hydrolysis; it also stabilises Fe²⁺.
4. **Equivalent weight of FeSO4·7H2O here?** Its molecular weight, 278.01 (one electron).
5. **Advantage of cerimetry over permanganometry for this assay?** Stable titrant, can be used with HCl, single electron change.

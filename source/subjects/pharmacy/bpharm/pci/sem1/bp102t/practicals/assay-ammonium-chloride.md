# Assay of ammonium chloride by acid–base (formol) titration

**Aim:** To determine the percentage purity of ammonium chloride by the formol titration and to standardise the sodium hydroxide used.

> Formaldehyde is irritant and toxic. Use it in a fume hood. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

The ammonium ion is too weak an acid (pKa about 9.25) to be titrated sharply with NaOH. **Formaldehyde** reacts with ammonium chloride to form hexamethylenetetramine and liberates an equivalent amount of hydrochloric acid, which is titrated with standard NaOH using **phenolphthalein**:

4NH4Cl + 6HCHO → (CH2)6N4 + 4HCl + 6H2O

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H2O

So 1 NH4Cl ≡ 1 HCl ≡ 1 NaOH. **1 ml of 0.1 M NaOH ≡ 0.005349 g NH4Cl** (Mw 53.49).

Formaldehyde usually contains formic acid, so it is **neutralised to phenolphthalein** before use.

## Requirements

Ammonium chloride sample, formaldehyde solution (about 37%), phenolphthalein, 0.1 M NaOH, potassium hydrogen phthalate (for standardising NaOH), burette, pipettes, conical flasks, analytical balance.

## Procedure

**Standardisation of 0.1 M NaOH** — against potassium hydrogen phthalate with phenolphthalein, as described in the NaOH standardisation guide (1 ml of 0.1 M NaOH ≡ 0.02042 g KHP).

**Neutralised formaldehyde** — take about 25 ml formaldehyde solution, add a few drops of phenolphthalein, and add 0.1 M NaOH dropwise to a faint pink.

**Assay**

1. Accurately weigh about 0.1 g of ammonium chloride; dissolve in 20 ml water in a conical flask.
2. Add 5 ml of the neutralised formaldehyde solution; mix and allow to stand for 2 minutes.
3. Add 2–3 drops of phenolphthalein (if not already present).
4. Titrate with 0.1 M NaOH to a faint pink that persists for 30 seconds.
5. Repeat for concordant results.

Factor of NaOH (f) = 1.004 (from standardisation).

% NH4Cl = V × f × 0.005349 × 100 / w

Worked example: 19.55 × 1.004 × 0.005349 × 100 / 0.1050 = 0.10499 × 100 / 0.1050 = **100.0%** (on the dried basis).

## Precautions

1. Neutralise the formaldehyde before use; otherwise its formic acid adds to the titre.
2. Allow time for the reaction before titrating.
3. Use CO2-free water.
4. Work with formaldehyde in a fume hood.

## Viva questions

1. **Why can't NH4Cl be titrated directly with NaOH?** NH4⁺ is a very weak acid; no sharp end point.
2. **What is formed with formaldehyde?** Hexamine and an equivalent amount of HCl.
3. **Why neutralise the formaldehyde?** It contains formic acid, which would give a high result.
4. **Another method?** Boil with excess standard NaOH to drive off ammonia and back-titrate the excess with standard acid.
5. **Which indicator and why?** Phenolphthalein; the end point is in the slightly alkaline region.

# Assay of sodium benzoate by non-aqueous titration

**Aim:** To determine the percentage purity of sodium benzoate by non-aqueous titration with 0.1 M perchloric acid.

> Perchloric acid is corrosive and can react violently with organic matter when concentrated or heated. Glacial acetic acid and acetic anhydride are corrosive and irritant. Work in a fume hood. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

The benzoate ion is a very weak base in water and cannot be titrated sharply there. In **glacial acetic acid** (a protogenic solvent) its basic character is enhanced, and it is titrated with **perchloric acid**, the strongest acid in this medium:

HClO4 + CH3COOH → CH3COOH2⁺ + ClO4⁻

C6H5COO⁻ + CH3COOH2⁺ → C6H5COOH + CH3COOH

Overall: C6H5COONa + HClO4 → C6H5COOH + NaClO4 (1 : 1).

**1 ml of 0.1 M HClO4 ≡ 0.01441 g C7H5NaO2** (Mw 144.10).

**Standardisation of 0.1 M HClO4:** against potassium hydrogen phthalate dissolved in glacial acetic acid, crystal violet indicator; 1 ml of 0.1 M HClO4 ≡ 0.02042 g KHP.

## Requirements

Sodium benzoate (dried), 0.1 M perchloric acid in glacial acetic acid (prepared with acetic anhydride to remove water), anhydrous glacial acetic acid, potassium hydrogen phthalate, crystal violet solution (or 1-naphtholbenzein, as directed), dry burette and dry glassware, warm water bath, thermometer.

## Procedure

**Standardisation**

1. Accurately weigh about 0.35 g KHP; dissolve in 50 ml glacial acetic acid (warm if needed, cool).
2. Add 2 drops of crystal violet; titrate with 0.1 M HClO4 from violet to blue-green (emerald green).
3. Run a blank with the solvent alone and subtract it. Note the temperature.

**Assay**

4. Accurately weigh about 0.25 g of dried sodium benzoate into a dry conical flask.
5. Add 20 ml of anhydrous glacial acetic acid; warm gently to dissolve; cool to room temperature.
6. Add the indicator.
7. Titrate with 0.1 M HClO4 to the end-point colour (violet → blue-green with crystal violet).
8. Carry out a blank titration and subtract.

## Observations and calculation

Standardisation: 0.3500 g KHP used 17.05 ml (blank negligible). Expected = 0.3500/0.02042 = 17.14 ml; f = 17.14/17.05 = **1.005**.

**Temperature correction** (if the titration temperature t2 differs from the standardisation temperature t1): corrected M = M × [1 + 0.0011 (t1 − t2)].

## Precautions

1. All glassware must be dry; water spoils the end point.
2. Keep the perchloric acid solution away from heat and organic waste.
3. Do the blank and correct for temperature.
4. Cool the solution before titrating.

## Viva questions

1. **Why can't sodium benzoate be titrated in water with HCl?** Benzoate is too weak a base in water; no sharp end point.
2. **Why glacial acetic acid?** It is a protogenic solvent that enhances the basicity of weak bases.
3. **Why acetic anhydride in the titrant?** To react with water present in perchloric acid and the solvent.
4. **Why is a blank needed?** To correct for titrant used by the solvent and indicator.
5. **Why is temperature important?** Acetic acid expands considerably with temperature, changing molarity.

# Limit test for chloride

**Aim:** To perform the limit test for chloride on the given sample as per the Indian Pharmacopoeia and report whether it passes.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow the monograph, your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

Chloride ions react with silver nitrate in the presence of dilute nitric acid to form silver chloride, which, at the low levels allowed, stays as a faint white **opalescence** (not a heavy precipitate):

Cl⁻ + AgNO3 → AgCl (white) + NO3⁻

The opalescence of the test solution is compared with that of a **standard** containing a known amount of chloride (the permitted limit), under identical conditions, in matched **Nessler cylinders**. If the test is less opalescent than the standard, the sample passes.

Nitric acid keeps the solution acidic so that carbonate, phosphate and other ions do not precipitate with silver, and it helps form a fine, even opalescence.

## Requirements

Nessler cylinders (50 ml, matched pair), glass rods, pipettes, measuring cylinder; dilute nitric acid, silver nitrate solution (about 5% w/v), chloride standard solution (for example 0.05845% w/v NaCl, of which 1 ml contains 0.355 mg Cl), the sample, distilled water.

## Procedure

**Test**

1. Dissolve the quantity of sample stated in the monograph in water (or prepare the solution as directed) and transfer to a Nessler cylinder.
2. Add 10 ml of dilute nitric acid.
3. Dilute with water to about 50 ml (or to the stated volume, leaving room for the reagent).
4. Add 1 ml of silver nitrate solution, stir immediately with a glass rod and keep aside for 5 minutes, protected from light.

**Standard**

5. In the second cylinder, take the stated volume of chloride standard solution (for example 1 ml of 0.05845% w/v NaCl).
6. Add 10 ml of dilute nitric acid, dilute to the same volume, add 1 ml silver nitrate solution, stir and keep for 5 minutes.

**Comparison**

7. Place both cylinders side by side against a **black background** and view vertically down the length of the cylinders.

## Observations

| Cylinder | Opalescence |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | Faint white opalescence |
| Test | ___ (less / more than standard) |

**Amount in the standard (worked):** 1 ml of 0.05845% w/v NaCl contains 0.5845 mg NaCl; chloride = 0.5845 × 35.45/58.44 = **0.355 mg Cl**. If 1 g of sample is taken, the limit is 0.355 mg per g = 355 ppm.

## Result

The opalescence of the test solution is less / more than that of the standard. The sample **passes / fails** the limit test for chloride.

## Precautions

1. Use matched Nessler cylinders and the same volumes and times for test and standard.
2. Add reagents to both cylinders in the same order and stir in the same way.
3. Avoid direct sunlight (silver chloride darkens).
4. Use chloride-free water and clean glassware.

## Viva questions

1. **What is a limit test?** A semi-quantitative test that shows whether an impurity is below its permitted limit by comparison with a standard.
2. **Why is nitric acid added?** To prevent precipitation of silver carbonate, phosphate and similar salts, and to give a uniform opalescence.
3. **Why not HCl or H2SO4?** HCl adds chloride; sulphuric acid could precipitate silver sulphate at higher concentrations.
4. **Why a black background?** White opalescence is seen best against black.
5. **Why Nessler cylinders?** They are matched in diameter and glass, so equal depths of liquid can be compared fairly.
6. **What if the sample is coloured?** Treat as the monograph directs (for example filter, or use a modified method), since colour hides opalescence.

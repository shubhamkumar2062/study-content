# Preparation and standardisation of 0.1 M sodium hydroxide

**Aim:** To prepare 1000 ml of approximately 0.1 M sodium hydroxide and standardise it using potassium hydrogen phthalate.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Sodium hydroxide is corrosive. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

Sodium hydroxide is not a primary standard: it is hygroscopic and absorbs carbon dioxide from air, so its exact strength is found by titration against **potassium hydrogen phthalate (KHP)**, a primary standard (Mw 204.22).

KHC8H4O4 + NaOH → KNaC8H4O4 + H2O (1 : 1)

KHP is a weak acid, so the end point (about pH 9) is shown by **phenolphthalein** (colourless → pink).

**1 ml of 0.1 M NaOH ≡ 0.02042 g of KHP** (204.22 × 0.1/1000).

## Requirements

Sodium hydroxide pellets, potassium hydrogen phthalate (dried at 120 °C for 2 hours), phenolphthalein solution, freshly boiled and cooled (CO2-free) distilled water, analytical balance, 1000 ml volumetric flask, burette, conical flasks, weighing bottle; polythene bottle with a soda-lime guard tube for storage.

## Procedure

**Preparation**

1. Weigh about 4.2 g of NaOH (a little more than 4.0 g to allow for moisture and carbonate) quickly on a rough balance.
2. Dissolve in CO2-free water and make up to 1000 ml. For carbonate-free solution, a strong (about 50% w/v) NaOH solution can be allowed to stand so that Na2CO3 settles, and the clear liquid diluted.
3. Store in a well-closed polythene bottle.

**Standardisation**

4. Accurately weigh about 0.4 g of dried KHP into a conical flask.
5. Dissolve in about 50 ml of CO2-free water.
6. Add 2–3 drops of phenolphthalein.
7. Titrate with the NaOH solution from the burette until a faint pink colour persists for 30 seconds.
8. Repeat to get three concordant readings (within 0.1 ml).

Molarity of NaOH = weight of KHP (g) / [volume (ml) × 0.2042]

Worked example: M = 0.4085/(19.85 × 0.2042) = 0.4085/4.0534 = **0.1008 M**.

Check by moles: 0.4085/204.22 = 0.002000 mol; 0.002000/0.01985 L = 0.1008 M.

Factor f = 0.1008/0.1 = 1.008.

## Precautions

1. Use CO2-free water; carbonate makes the end point less sharp.
2. Do not use glass-stoppered bottles for NaOH (stoppers stick).
3. Rinse the burette with the NaOH solution before filling.
4. Take the first permanent faint pink as the end point; the pink fades slowly as CO2 is absorbed.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is NaOH a secondary standard?** It absorbs moisture and CO2, so its exact strength cannot be fixed by weighing.
2. **Why is KHP a good primary standard?** High purity, non-hygroscopic, stable, high equivalent weight.
3. **Why phenolphthalein?** The end point is near pH 9 (KHP is a weak acid), within 8.3–10.0.
4. **Can oxalic acid be used?** Yes; 1 ml 0.1 M NaOH ≡ 0.006303 g oxalic acid dihydrate.
5. **Why use CO2-free water?** Dissolved CO2 consumes NaOH and gives a fading end point.

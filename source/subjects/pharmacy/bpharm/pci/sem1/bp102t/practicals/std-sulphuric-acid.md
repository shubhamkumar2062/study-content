# Preparation and standardisation of 0.05 M sulphuric acid

**Aim:** To prepare 1000 ml of approximately 0.05 M (0.1 N) sulphuric acid and standardise it with anhydrous sodium carbonate.

> Concentrated sulphuric acid is highly corrosive and gives out much heat with water. Always add acid to water slowly, never water to acid. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

Concentrated H2SO4 does not have an exactly known strength and absorbs moisture, so a dilute solution is prepared approximately and standardised against **anhydrous sodium carbonate** (Mw 105.99), a primary standard.

Na2CO3 + H2SO4 → Na2SO4 + H2O + CO2 (1 : 1)

Methyl orange (yellow → orange-pink) is used because the end point is in the acidic region; dissolved CO2 is removed by boiling near the end point.

**1 ml of 0.05 M H2SO4 ≡ 0.005299 g Na2CO3** (105.99 × 0.05/1000).

Because H2SO4 is dibasic, 0.05 M = 0.1 N.

## Requirements

Concentrated H2SO4 (about 98% w/w, density 1.84), anhydrous sodium carbonate (heated at about 270 °C for 1 hour and cooled in a desiccator), methyl orange, water, burette, conical flasks, 1000 ml volumetric flask, measuring cylinder, hot plate.

## Procedure

**Preparation**

1. Calculation: 0.05 mol × 98.08 = 4.904 g H2SO4; 4.904/0.98 = 5.0 g of concentrated acid; 5.0/1.84 ≈ **2.7 ml**. Take about 3 ml.
2. Add the acid slowly, with stirring, to about 800 ml water; cool and make up to 1000 ml.

**Standardisation**

3. Accurately weigh about 0.15 g of anhydrous Na2CO3 into a conical flask; dissolve in 50 ml water.
4. Add 2 drops of methyl orange.
5. Titrate with the acid until the colour just begins to change.
6. Boil gently for 2 minutes to expel CO2 (the colour returns to yellow), cool, and continue the titration to a permanent orange-pink.
7. Repeat for concordant readings.

## Precautions

1. Add acid to water, never the reverse.
2. Dry the sodium carbonate properly; it is slightly hygroscopic.
3. Boil off CO2 near the end point for a sharp change.
4. Alternatively standardise against previously standardised NaOH using phenolphthalein.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the normality of 0.05 M H2SO4?** 0.1 N.
2. **Why boil near the end point?** CO2 forms carbonic acid, which changes the indicator early; boiling removes it.
3. **Why methyl orange and not phenolphthalein with Na2CO3?** Complete reaction to CO2 is shown only in the acidic range.
4. **Why is anhydrous Na2CO3 a primary standard?** Pure, stable, and can be dried to constant weight.
5. **Why add acid to water?** The heat produced is absorbed by the larger volume of water, preventing spurting.

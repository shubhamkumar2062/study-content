# Conductometric titration of strong acid and weak acid against strong base

**Aim:** To determine the normality of hydrochloric acid and acetic acid in a mixture by conductometric titration with standard sodium hydroxide.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

In the mixture, the strong acid (HCl) is fully ionised and its H⁺ suppresses the ionisation of the weak acid (CH3COOH). On adding NaOH:

1. **Strong acid neutralised first:** fast H⁺ replaced by slow Na⁺ → conductance **falls steeply** → first break (end point of HCl).
2. **Weak acid neutralised next:** CH3COOH (poorly ionised) becomes CH3COO⁻ Na⁺ (fully ionised) → conductance **rises slowly** → second break (end point of acetic acid).
3. **Excess NaOH:** fast OH⁻ ions → conductance **rises steeply**.

```text
 G
 |\                        /
 | \                      /   excess NaOH
 |  \               _____/
 |   \        _____/          acetic acid neutralised
 |    \______/
 |    HCl neutralised
 +-----|-----------|--------- volume of NaOH
      V1          V2
```

N(HCl) = N(NaOH) × V1 / V(sample); N(CH3COOH) = N(NaOH) × (V2 − V1) / V(sample).

## Requirements

Conductivity meter and cell, standardised 0.1 N NaOH, the acid mixture (each acid about 0.005–0.01 N), beaker, magnetic stirrer, burette, pipette, graph paper.

## Procedure

1. Check the meter and cell constant with standard KCl as directed.
2. Pipette 50 ml of the acid mixture into a beaker; dip the cell; record the initial conductance.
3. Add 0.1 N NaOH in 0.5 ml portions, stirring and recording the steady conductance after each addition.
4. Continue well past the second break (until the steep rise is clear for 5–6 points).
5. Plot conductance against volume; draw three straight lines and note the two intersection volumes V1 and V2.

**Worked example:** 50 ml mixture; V1 = 2.60 ml, V2 = 5.80 ml of 0.1000 N NaOH.

- HCl: 0.1000 × 2.60/50 = **0.0052 N**.
- Acetic acid: 0.1000 × (5.80 − 2.60)/50 = 0.1000 × 3.20/50 = **0.0064 N** (0.0064 × 60.05 = 0.384 g/L).

## Precautions

1. Constant temperature throughout.
2. Small, equal additions near the two breaks.
3. Use a concentrated titrant or apply the dilution correction G × (V + v)/V.
4. Draw straight lines using points away from each break.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is the HCl neutralised first?** It is the stronger acid; its H⁺ also suppresses ionisation of acetic acid.
2. **Why does conductance rise slowly in the second stage?** Weakly ionised acetic acid is converted to fully ionised sodium acetate, but Na⁺ and acetate are slow ions.
3. **Can this mixture be titrated with indicators?** Not accurately; two end points are not seen sharply with indicators.
4. **What would NH4OH as titrant show after the end point?** A nearly flat line, since excess weak base hardly ionises.
5. **State Kohlrausch's law.** At infinite dilution, molar conductance = sum of limiting ionic conductances of cation and anion.

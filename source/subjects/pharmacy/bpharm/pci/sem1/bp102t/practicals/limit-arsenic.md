# Limit test for arsenic

**Aim:** To perform the limit test for arsenic on the given sample by the Gutzeit method as per the Indian Pharmacopoeia.

> Arsenic compounds and arsine are highly poisonous. Work in a fume hood, keep the apparatus closed and follow your teacher's instructions. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

Arsenic in the sample is reduced by **nascent hydrogen**, produced from arsenic-free **zinc and hydrochloric acid**, to **arsine gas (AsH3)**. Arsine passes up the tube and gives a **yellow to brown stain** on **mercuric chloride paper**. The stain from the sample is compared with that from a standard containing a known amount of arsenic.

- **Potassium iodide** and **stannous chloride** reduce pentavalent arsenic (As⁵⁺) to trivalent arsenic (As³⁺), which is converted to arsine faster and more completely; stannous chloride also helps steady evolution of hydrogen and deposits tin on zinc (activating it).
- **Stannated hydrochloric acid** (HCl containing a little stannous chloride) gives a steady reaction.
- **Lead acetate cotton wool** in the tube absorbs any hydrogen sulphide, which would otherwise darken the mercuric chloride paper.

## Requirements

Gutzeit apparatus (wide-mouthed bottle of about 120 ml, rubber bung, glass tube packed with lead acetate cotton wool, two flat-ended glass tubes holding mercuric chloride paper), water bath at 40 °C; arsenic-free (AsT) reagents: stannated hydrochloric acid, potassium iodide, stannous chloride solution, granulated zinc, mercuric chloride paper, standard arsenic solution, sample.

```text
        ___
       |   |  <- mercuric chloride paper between flat ends
       |___|
         |
         |   <- tube packed with lead acetate cotton wool
     ____|____
    |         |  <- bottle: sample + stannated HCl + KI + SnCl2 + zinc
    |_________|     40 °C, 40 minutes
```

## Procedure

**Test**

1. Place the sample solution (prepared as the monograph directs, usually in about 50 ml water) in the bottle.
2. Add 10 ml of stannated hydrochloric acid, 1 g of potassium iodide and 5 ml of stannous chloride solution.
3. Add 10 g of granulated zinc and immediately fit the bung with the tube assembly holding the lead acetate wool and the mercuric chloride paper.
4. Keep the bottle in a water bath at about **40 °C for 40 minutes**.

**Standard**

5. Repeat with the specified quantity of standard arsenic solution in place of the sample, using the same amounts of reagents and the same conditions.

**Comparison**

6. Remove the mercuric chloride papers and compare the stains at once.

## Observations

| Paper | Stain |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | Yellow-brown stain |
| Test | ___ (lighter / darker) |

## Result

The stain produced by the sample is lighter / darker than the standard stain. The sample **passes / fails** the limit test for arsenic.

## Precautions

1. Use only arsenic-free (AsT) reagents and zinc.
2. Keep the temperature at 40 °C; too fast evolution gives an uneven stain.
3. Make sure the lead acetate wool is not wet; the paper must be dry.
4. Compare stains immediately; they fade in light.
5. Never inhale the gas; work in a fume hood.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is lead acetate cotton wool used?** To absorb H2S, which would blacken the mercuric chloride paper.
2. **Role of KI and SnCl2?** Reduce As(V) to As(III) and keep hydrogen evolution steady.
3. **What gas causes the stain?** Arsine (AsH3).
4. **Why 40 °C?** For uniform, moderate evolution of hydrogen.
5. **What is the colour of the stain?** Yellow to brown.
6. **Why is arsenic tested separately from heavy metals?** It is very toxic at low levels and is not detected reliably by the sulphide-based heavy metals test.

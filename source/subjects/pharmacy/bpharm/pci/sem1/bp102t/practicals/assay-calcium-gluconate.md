# Assay of calcium gluconate by complexometric titration

**Aim:** To determine the percentage purity of calcium gluconate by complexometric titration with 0.05 M disodium edetate.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

Calcium ions form a stable 1 : 1 complex with EDTA at pH 10:

Ca²⁺ + H2Y²⁻ → CaY²⁻ + 2H⁺

The end point is shown by **Eriochrome Black T (mordant black 11)**: wine red (metal complex) → blue (free indicator). Calcium alone gives a poor, gradual colour change with this indicator because the Ca–indicator complex is weak. A small, known volume of **0.05 M magnesium sulphate** is added; magnesium forms a stronger red complex with the indicator, and at the end point EDTA removes the Mg from the indicator, giving a sharp change. The volume of MgSO4 added is subtracted from the titre.

**1 ml of 0.05 M disodium edetate ≡ 0.02242 g C12H22CaO14·H2O** (Mw 448.4).

**Standardisation of 0.05 M disodium edetate:** dissolve about 18.6 g of disodium edetate (Mw 372.24) to 1000 ml; standardise against granulated zinc dissolved in dilute HCl, buffered to pH 10, with Eriochrome Black T (1 ml 0.05 M ≡ 0.003269 g Zn).

## Requirements

Calcium gluconate sample, 0.05 M disodium edetate (standardised), 0.05 M magnesium sulphate, strong ammonia–ammonium chloride buffer (pH about 10), mordant black 11 mixture, burette, conical flasks, water bath.

## Procedure

1. Accurately weigh about 0.5 g calcium gluconate; dissolve in 50 ml warm water; cool.
2. Add exactly 5.0 ml of 0.05 M magnesium sulphate solution.
3. Add 10 ml of strong ammonia–ammonium chloride buffer.
4. Add about 0.1 g of mordant black 11 mixture (wine red colour).
5. Titrate with 0.05 M disodium edetate until the colour changes to a clear blue.
6. Subtract 5.0 ml (the volume of 0.05 M MgSO4 added) from the burette reading.
7. Repeat for concordant results.

## Precautions

1. Cool the solution before adding the buffer.
2. Measure the MgSO4 solution exactly; its strength must match the EDTA molarity (or correct for it).
3. Titrate slowly near the end point; the change from violet to blue is gradual at the last drops.
4. Use a fresh indicator mixture.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is MgSO4 added?** Calcium gives a poor end point with Eriochrome Black T; magnesium gives a sharp wine red → blue change.
2. **Why is it subtracted?** The added Mg also consumes EDTA.
3. **Why pH 10?** Ca– and Mg–EDTA complexes are stable at this pH and the indicator works in this range.
4. **Ratio of metal to EDTA?** Always 1 : 1.
5. **Another indicator for calcium?** Hydroxynaphthol blue (or calcon) at pH 12–13.

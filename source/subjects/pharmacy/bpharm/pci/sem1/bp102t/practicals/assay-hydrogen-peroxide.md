# Assay of hydrogen peroxide by permanganometry

**Aim:** To determine the strength of hydrogen peroxide solution (% w/v and volume strength) by titration with 0.02 M potassium permanganate.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Concentrated hydrogen peroxide burns skin. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

In sulphuric acid solution, hydrogen peroxide acts as a reducing agent towards permanganate and is oxidised to oxygen:

2MnO4⁻ + 5H2O2 + 6H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 5O2 + 8H2O

KMnO4 is its own indicator (first permanent pink). For H2O2, n = 2; 0.02 M KMnO4 is 0.1 N.

**1 ml of 0.02 M KMnO4 ≡ 0.001701 g H2O2** (34.01/2 × 0.1/1000).

**Volume strength:** 2H2O2 → 2H2O + O2. 68.02 g H2O2 gives 22.4 L O2 at STP, so 1 g gives about 0.329 L. Volume strength = (g of H2O2 per litre) × 0.329.

## Requirements

Hydrogen peroxide solution (about 3% or 6% w/v), 0.02 M KMnO4 (standardised against oxalic acid), dilute sulphuric acid, 100 ml volumetric flask, pipettes (10 ml), burette, conical flasks.

## Procedure

1. Standardise the KMnO4 solution (see its guide).
2. Pipette 10 ml of the hydrogen peroxide solution into a 100 ml volumetric flask and dilute to the mark with water; mix.
3. Pipette 10 ml of the diluted solution (= 1 ml of original) into a conical flask.
4. Add 20 ml of dilute H2SO4 (do not heat).
5. Titrate with 0.02 M KMnO4 to the first permanent faint pink.
6. Repeat for concordant results.

## Observations and calculation

| Trial | Volume of diluted H2O2 (ml) | Volume of 0.02 M KMnO4 (ml) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 10.0 | 17.60 |
| 2 | 10.0 |  |
| 3 | 10.0 |  |

f (KMnO4) = 1.008.

H2O2 in 1 ml of original solution = 17.60 × 1.008 × 0.001701 = 0.03018 g.

% w/v = 0.03018 × 100 = **3.02% w/v**.

g per litre = 30.18; volume strength = 30.18 × 0.329 = **9.9, about 10 volumes**.

## Precautions

1. Do not warm the solution; H2O2 decomposes on heating.
2. Use sulphuric acid only.
3. Pipette H2O2 with a pipette filler; never by mouth.
4. Keep the stock bottle closed and cool.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is H2O2 a reducing agent here?** Permanganate is a stronger oxidant; H2O2 gives up electrons and is oxidised to O2.
2. **What does "20 volume" mean?** 1 ml releases 20 ml of oxygen at STP; about 6% w/v.
3. **Why dilute the sample first?** To bring the titre into a convenient range.
4. **Why is no heating done here, unlike with oxalate?** The reaction is fast and H2O2 decomposes on heating.
5. **What other method can assay H2O2?** Iodometry (H2O2 + 2I⁻ + 2H⁺ → I2 + 2H2O) or cerimetry.

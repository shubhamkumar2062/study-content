# Limit test for iron

**Aim:** To perform the limit test for iron on the given sample as per the Indian Pharmacopoeia.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow the monograph, your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

Iron (as Fe³⁺ or Fe²⁺) reacts with **thioglycollic acid** in an ammoniacal solution containing **citric acid** to give a **purple** ferrous thioglycollate complex. The colour of the test is compared with that of a standard containing a known amount of iron.

- Thioglycollic acid reduces any Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺ and forms the coloured complex, so all iron is detected.
- Citric acid forms a soluble complex with iron and prevents its precipitation as hydroxide when ammonia is added.
- Ammonia makes the solution alkaline; the purple colour develops only in alkaline medium.

## Requirements

Nessler cylinders, pipettes, glass rods; iron-free citric acid solution (20% w/v), thioglycollic acid, iron-free ammonia solution, iron standard solution (prepared from ferric ammonium sulphate), sample, distilled water.

## Procedure

**Test**

1. Dissolve the stated quantity of sample in 40 ml water in a Nessler cylinder (or use the solution as directed).
2. Add 2 ml of 20% w/v iron-free citric acid solution and 0.1 ml of thioglycollic acid; mix.
3. Make alkaline with iron-free ammonia solution (about 3–5 ml), dilute with water to 50 ml.
4. Allow to stand for 5 minutes.

**Standard**

5. Dilute the stated volume of iron standard solution with water to 40 ml in the second cylinder and treat exactly as the test (steps 2–4).

**Comparison**

6. View vertically against a **white background** and compare the purple colour.

## Observations

| Cylinder | Colour |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | Pale purple |
| Test | ___ |

## Result

The colour of the test is less / more intense than the standard; the sample **passes / fails** the limit test for iron.

## Precautions

1. Use iron-free reagents and avoid contact with metal spatulas.
2. Add reagents in the same order to both cylinders and wait the same time.
3. Make sure both solutions are alkaline.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is citric acid added?** To keep iron in solution as a complex, preventing precipitation of iron hydroxide in ammonia.
2. **Role of thioglycollic acid?** Reduces Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺ and forms the purple ferrous thioglycollate complex.
3. **Why a white background?** Coloured solutions are compared best against white.
4. **Why is ammonia added?** The purple complex forms only in alkaline solution.
5. **Name the salt used for the iron standard.** Ferric ammonium sulphate.

# Limit test for sulphate

**Aim:** To perform the limit test for sulphate on the given sample as per the Indian Pharmacopoeia.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow the monograph, your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles, a lab coat and gloves.

## Principle

Sulphate ions react with barium chloride in the presence of dilute hydrochloric acid to form barium sulphate, which appears as a white **turbidity**:

SO4²⁻ + BaCl2 → BaSO4 (white) + 2Cl⁻

The turbidity of the test is compared with that of a standard containing a known amount of sulphate. **Hydrochloric acid** prevents precipitation of barium carbonate, phosphate and oxalate. The **barium sulphate reagent** (barium chloride solution containing a little sulphate in alcohol) supplies tiny seed crystals of BaSO4 so that the turbidity forms quickly and evenly; alcohol lowers the solubility of BaSO4.

## Requirements

Nessler cylinders (50 ml), glass rods, pipettes; dilute hydrochloric acid, barium sulphate reagent, sulphate standard solution (for example 0.1089% w/v K2SO4, 1 ml of which contains 0.6 mg SO4), sample, distilled water.

## Procedure

**Test**

1. Dissolve the stated quantity of sample in water in a Nessler cylinder.
2. Add 2 ml of dilute hydrochloric acid.
3. Dilute with water to 45 ml.
4. Add 5 ml of barium sulphate reagent, stir immediately and keep for 5 minutes.

**Standard**

5. Take the stated volume of sulphate standard solution (for example 1 ml of 0.1089% w/v K2SO4) in the second cylinder, add 2 ml dilute HCl, dilute to 45 ml, add 5 ml barium sulphate reagent, stir and keep for 5 minutes.

**Comparison**

6. View both cylinders vertically against a **black background** (or as directed) and compare turbidity.

## Observations and calculation

| Cylinder | Turbidity |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | White turbidity |
| Test | ___ |

**Amount in the standard:** 1 ml of 0.1089% w/v K2SO4 = 1.089 mg K2SO4 (Mw 174.26); sulphate = 1.089 × 96.06/174.26 = **0.600 mg SO4**. For a 1 g sample this is a limit of 600 ppm.

## Result

The turbidity of the test is less / more than the standard; the sample **passes / fails** the limit test for sulphate.

## Precautions

1. Treat test and standard identically (volumes, order, time, stirring).
2. Stir immediately after adding the reagent.
3. Use sulphate-free reagents and clean cylinders.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is HCl added?** To stop barium carbonate, phosphate and similar salts from precipitating.
2. **What is the barium sulphate reagent?** Barium chloride solution with alcohol and a trace of potassium sulphate that gives seed crystals.
3. **Why is alcohol present?** It lowers the solubility of BaSO4 and helps uniform turbidity.
4. **Why the seed crystals?** Without them a supersaturated solution may form and turbidity appears slowly and unevenly.
5. **Is a limit test quantitative?** No, semi-quantitative: it only says within or above the limit.

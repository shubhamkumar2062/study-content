# Unit 1: Human body, cells and tissues

Every drug acts on a cell, a tissue or an organ, so a pharmacist needs a working picture of how the body is built and how it keeps itself steady. This unit starts with the language of anatomy, then moves down to the cell (its parts, how things cross its membrane, how it divides and talks to other cells) and finally to the four basic tissues.

## 1.1 Anatomy and physiology: definition and scope

| Term | Meaning | Branches (examples) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Anatomy** | Study of the structure of the body and the relationship between its parts | Gross anatomy (seen by naked eye), histology (tissues under microscope), cytology (cells), embryology (development), radiological anatomy, surface anatomy |
| **Physiology** | Study of the functions of the body and its parts, and how they work together | Cell physiology, neurophysiology, cardiovascular physiology, endocrinology, exercise physiology |

Structure and function always go together: the thin wall of an alveolus suits gas exchange; the thick muscle of the left ventricle suits pumping blood to the whole body.

**Why pharmacy students need it:** pharmacology, pharmacokinetics (absorption, distribution, excretion), pathophysiology, clinical pharmacy and dosage-form design all assume this knowledge. For example, a transdermal patch only makes sense if you know the layers of the skin, and an eye drop only if you know the cornea and tear drainage.

## 1.2 Levels of structural organisation

```text
Chemical level   atoms (C, H, O, N) -> molecules (water, glucose, proteins, DNA)
      |
Cellular level   cells: the smallest living units (e.g. muscle cell, neuron)
      |
Tissue level     groups of similar cells doing one job (epithelial, connective, muscular, nervous)
      |
Organ level      two or more tissues forming a structure with a definite shape and job (stomach, heart)
      |
System level     related organs with a common function (digestive system)
      |
Organismal level the whole living person, all systems working together
```

**Body systems (11)**

| System | Main organs | Main function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Integumentary | Skin, hair, nails, glands | Protection, temperature control |
| Skeletal | Bones, cartilage, joints | Support, movement, blood cell formation |
| Muscular | Skeletal muscles | Movement, posture, heat |
| Nervous | Brain, spinal cord, nerves | Fast control by nerve impulses |
| Endocrine | Pituitary, thyroid, adrenal, pancreas | Slower control by hormones |
| Cardiovascular | Heart, blood vessels, blood | Transport of O2, nutrients, wastes |
| Lymphatic and immune | Lymph nodes, spleen, thymus, lymph vessels | Returns fluid, defends against infection |
| Respiratory | Nose, trachea, lungs | Gas exchange |
| Digestive | Mouth to anus, liver, pancreas | Breakdown and absorption of food |
| Urinary | Kidneys, ureters, bladder, urethra | Removes wastes, regulates fluid and pH |
| Reproductive | Testes, ovaries, uterus | Produces offspring |

## 1.3 Basic life processes

1. **Metabolism** — all chemical reactions of the body: catabolism (breaking down, releases energy) and anabolism (building up, uses energy).
2. **Responsiveness** — ability to detect and respond to changes (a nerve impulse, a muscle contracting).
3. **Movement** — of the whole body, organs, cells (white cells moving to infection) and structures inside cells.
4. **Growth** — increase in size by more cells or bigger cells.
5. **Differentiation** — unspecialised cells become specialised (stem cell to red cell).
6. **Reproduction** — new cells for growth and repair, and a new individual.

## 1.4 Homeostasis

**Homeostasis** is the maintenance of a relatively constant internal environment (the extracellular fluid) despite changes outside. Examples of controlled variables: body temperature (about 37 °C), blood glucose, blood pressure, blood pH (7.35–7.45), water and electrolyte levels.

**Parts of a feedback system**

```text
   Stimulus (change in a variable)
          |
     RECEPTOR  (detects change, e.g. thermoreceptor, baroreceptor)
          |  input (afferent pathway)
   CONTROL CENTRE (sets the normal range, e.g. hypothalamus, medulla)
          |  output (efferent pathway)
     EFFECTOR  (muscle or gland that acts)
          |
   Response -> fed back to change the variable
```

| Type | What it does | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Negative feedback** (most common) | Response reverses the original change, bringing the variable back to normal | BP rises → baroreceptors → medulla → heart slows, vessels dilate → BP falls. Body temperature control by sweating or shivering. Blood glucose control by insulin and glucagon |
| **Positive feedback** | Response strengthens the original change until an outside event stops it | Childbirth (stretch of cervix → oxytocin → stronger contractions), blood clotting, milk ejection |

**Homeostatic imbalance** leads to disorders (diabetes mellitus when glucose control fails, fever, dehydration). Many drugs act by restoring a disturbed homeostatic system.

Example: appendix pain is felt in the right iliac region (right lower quadrant).

## 1.6 Structure and functions of the cell

The **cell** is the basic structural and functional unit of life. A typical human cell has three main parts: the plasma membrane, the cytoplasm (cytosol with organelles) and the nucleus.

```text
              microvilli (on free surface)
        _____|_|_|_|_|_________________________
       /                                       \  <- PLASMA MEMBRANE
      |  GOLGI ))))         MITOCHONDRION      |
      |                     [=====]            |
      |             _____________              |
      |  RER ~~~~  /   NUCLEUS   \  LYSOSOME ( ) |
      |  ~~~~~~~~ |  (nucleolus)  |            |
      |  ~~~~~~~~  \_____________/  SER ====== |
      |                                        |
      |  : : free ribosomes   CENTROSOME ||    |
      |  CYTOSOL with cytoskeleton  (2 centrioles)
       \_______________________________________/
```

**Plasma membrane — fluid mosaic model.** A phospholipid bilayer (hydrophilic heads outside, hydrophobic tails inside) with cholesterol (gives stability) and proteins floating in it. Integral proteins cross the membrane (channels, carriers, receptors); peripheral proteins sit on the surface. Glycoproteins and glycolipids form the **glycocalyx**, which helps cells recognise each other. The membrane is **selectively permeable**: lipid-soluble molecules (O2, CO2, steroids, many drugs) pass easily; ions and large polar molecules need proteins.

**Organelles and their functions**

| Organelle | Structure | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nucleus | Double nuclear envelope with pores; chromatin (DNA + proteins); nucleolus | Holds genetic material; controls cell activities; nucleolus makes ribosomal RNA |
| Ribosomes | Granules of rRNA and protein (80S in human cells); free or on RER | Protein synthesis |
| Rough endoplasmic reticulum | Flattened sacs with ribosomes | Makes proteins for secretion and membranes |
| Smooth endoplasmic reticulum | Tubules without ribosomes | Lipid and steroid synthesis; drug detoxification in liver cells (cytochrome P450 enzymes); stores Ca2+ in muscle (sarcoplasmic reticulum) |
| Golgi apparatus | Stack of curved sacs | Modifies, sorts and packages proteins into vesicles; forms lysosomes |
| Lysosomes | Membrane sacs of acid hydrolase enzymes | Digest worn-out organelles (autophagy) and engulfed material; "suicide bags" |
| Peroxisomes | Small sacs with oxidase and catalase | Break down fatty acids, alcohol and hydrogen peroxide |
| Mitochondria | Double membrane; inner folds called cristae; own DNA | Aerobic respiration, ATP production ("powerhouse") |
| Centrosome | Two centrioles at right angles | Organises the spindle in cell division |
| Cytoskeleton | Microfilaments (actin), intermediate filaments, microtubules | Shape, support, movement of organelles and the cell |
| Cilia and flagella | Projections built from microtubules | Cilia move mucus (respiratory tract); flagellum moves the sperm |
| Microvilli | Finger-like folds of the membrane | Increase surface for absorption (intestine, kidney tubules) |

## 1.7 Transport across the cell membrane

```text
TRANSPORT
 |-- Passive (no ATP, down the concentration gradient)
 |     |-- Simple diffusion ........ O2, CO2, lipid-soluble drugs
 |     |-- Facilitated diffusion ... glucose (GLUT carriers), ions via channels
 |     |-- Osmosis ................. water across a semipermeable membrane
 |     |-- Filtration .............. by pressure (glomerulus, capillaries)
 |-- Active (uses ATP, against the gradient)
 |     |-- Primary active .......... Na+/K+ ATPase pump
 |     |-- Secondary active ........ Na+-glucose symport, Na+/H+ antiport
 |-- Vesicular (bulk) transport
       |-- Endocytosis: phagocytosis, pinocytosis, receptor-mediated
       |-- Exocytosis: release of hormones, neurotransmitters, mucus
```

1. **Simple diffusion** — net movement of molecules from higher to lower concentration until evenly spread. Rate increases with steeper gradient, higher temperature, larger area, smaller molecules and higher lipid solubility. Most drugs are absorbed this way.
2. **Facilitated diffusion** — downhill movement with the help of a carrier or channel protein. It shows saturation (a maximum rate) and specificity. Example: glucose entry into most cells.
3. **Osmosis** — movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane from lower solute concentration to higher solute concentration. **Tonicity** describes the effect on cells: isotonic (0.9% NaCl) — no change; hypotonic — water enters, red cells swell and burst (haemolysis); hypertonic — water leaves, cells shrink (crenation). This is why injections and eye drops are made isotonic.
4. **Filtration** — water and small solutes pushed through a membrane by hydrostatic pressure; forms tissue fluid and glomerular filtrate.
5. **Primary active transport** — the **Na+/K+ ATPase pump** uses one ATP to move **3 Na+ out and 2 K+ in**, keeping Na+ high outside and K+ high inside. It maintains the resting membrane potential and cell volume. Digoxin inhibits this pump in heart muscle.
6. **Secondary active transport** — energy stored in the Na+ gradient drives another substance: symport (same direction, e.g. Na+–glucose in the intestine) or antiport (opposite, e.g. Na+/H+ exchanger).
7. **Endocytosis** — the membrane folds in around material: phagocytosis ("cell eating", bacteria by neutrophils and macrophages), pinocytosis ("cell drinking", droplets of fluid), receptor-mediated (LDL cholesterol, iron with transferrin). **Exocytosis** — vesicles fuse with the membrane to release contents.

## 1.8 Cell division

The **cell cycle** has interphase and the mitotic phase.

- **Interphase:** G1 (cell grows, organelles copied), S (DNA replicated), G2 (preparation for division). The cell spends most of its life here.
- **Mitotic (M) phase:** mitosis (nucleus divides) and cytokinesis (cytoplasm divides).

**Mitosis** gives two daughter cells, each with the same 46 chromosomes as the parent. It is used for growth and repair.

| Stage | Main events |
| --- | --- |
| Prophase | Chromatin condenses into chromosomes (two sister chromatids joined at a centromere); nucleolus and nuclear envelope disappear; spindle forms from centrioles |
| Metaphase | Chromosomes line up at the equator (metaphase plate); spindle fibres attach at centromeres |
| Anaphase | Centromeres split; sister chromatids pulled to opposite poles |
| Telophase | Nuclear envelopes re-form; chromosomes uncoil; spindle disappears |
| Cytokinesis | Cleavage furrow pinches the cell into two |

```text
 Prophase        Metaphase        Anaphase          Telophase
  ( X X )        |  X  |          |<-   ->|         (o) | (o)
  ( X  X )  ->   |  X  |   ->     |<-   ->|   ->    (o) | (o)
  spindle forms  line up at       chromatids         two nuclei,
                 equator          separate           furrow divides
```

**Meiosis** occurs only in the testes and ovaries. Two divisions produce four cells with 23 chromosomes each (haploid gametes). Crossing over in prophase I creates genetic variety. Fertilisation restores 46.

**Significance:** growth, repair and replacement of cells; uncontrolled division is cancer, and many anticancer drugs act on specific phases (for example, vinca alkaloids block the spindle in metaphase).

## 1.9 Cell junctions

| Junction | Structure | Function | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tight junction | Membranes of neighbouring cells fused by proteins in a band | Seals the gap; stops leakage between cells | Intestinal epithelium, blood–brain barrier, bladder |
| Adherens junction | Cadherins linked to actin, forming a belt | Holds cells together, resists separation | Epithelia |
| Desmosome | Button-like plaques with cadherins linked to intermediate filaments | Strong spot welds against stretching | Epidermis, cardiac muscle |
| Hemidesmosome | Half desmosome joining cell to basement membrane (integrins) | Anchors epithelium to the tissue below | Base of epidermis |
| Gap junction | Channels of connexons linking cytoplasm of two cells | Passes ions and small molecules; electrical coupling | Cardiac muscle (intercalated discs), smooth muscle |

## 1.10 Cell communication and intracellular signalling

Cells coordinate by sending **signal molecules** (hormones, neurotransmitters, local mediators, growth factors) that bind to **receptors** on or in the target cell. Binding starts a chain of events inside the cell (signal transduction) that ends in a response.

**General principles**

1. Each signal acts only on cells that have its receptor (specificity).
2. One signal molecule can trigger many molecules inside the cell (amplification).
3. The same signal can cause different effects in different cells (acetylcholine slows the heart but contracts intestinal muscle).
4. Signals are switched off (enzymes break them down, receptors are desensitised).

**Activation of an intracellular pathway by an extracellular signal**

```text
Extracellular signal (first messenger, e.g. adrenaline)
          |
   binds RECEPTOR on cell surface
          |
   transducer (e.g. G-protein) activated
          |
   amplifier enzyme (adenylyl cyclase / phospholipase C)
          |
   SECOND MESSENGER (cAMP, IP3 + DAG, Ca2+)
          |
   protein kinases switched on (phosphorylate target proteins)
          |
   CELL RESPONSE (contraction, secretion, gene expression, metabolism)
```

**Receptor types**

| Receptor | How it works | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ion-channel linked | Signal opens a channel; very fast (milliseconds) | Nicotinic acetylcholine receptor, GABA-A |
| G-protein coupled | Activates a G-protein → second messenger | Adrenergic, muscarinic receptors |
| Enzyme-linked | Receptor is itself an enzyme (tyrosine kinase) | Insulin receptor, growth factor receptors |
| Intracellular (nuclear) | Lipid-soluble signal enters cell; receptor changes gene transcription; slow (hours) | Steroid hormones, thyroid hormone |

**Forms of intracellular signalling (by distance)**

| Form | How the signal travels | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a) **Contact-dependent** | Signal molecule stays on the signalling cell's membrane; the target must touch it | Immune cells recognising antigen on another cell; Notch–Delta signalling in development |
| b) **Paracrine** | Local mediator diffuses to nearby cells only | Histamine, prostaglandins, nitric oxide in nearby tissue |
| c) **Synaptic** | Neuron releases a neurotransmitter into a narrow synapse onto one target cell | Acetylcholine at the neuromuscular junction |
| d) **Endocrine** | Hormone carried by blood to distant targets | Insulin, thyroxine, adrenaline |
| (Autocrine) | Cell responds to a signal it released itself | Some growth factors in cancer cells |

```text
Contact-dependent:  [cell A]|[cell B]      signal on membrane, touch needed
Paracrine:          [A] ~~> [B] [C]        short local spread
Synaptic:           neuron ---->|| target  neurotransmitter across synapse
Endocrine:          gland -> BLOOD -> distant target cells
```

## 1.11 Tissue level of organisation

A **tissue** is a group of similar cells with the substance between them (matrix) that together perform a specific job. There are **four primary tissues**.

### 1.11.1 Epithelial tissue

Covers body surfaces, lines cavities and forms glands. Features: cells tightly packed, little matrix, rest on a **basement membrane**, no blood vessels (avascular; nourished by diffusion), high power of regeneration.

**Classification (by layers and cell shape)**

| Type | Structure | Location | Function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Simple squamous | One layer of flat cells | Alveoli, capillary walls (endothelium), lining of serous cavities (mesothelium), Bowman's capsule | Diffusion, filtration |
| Simple cuboidal | One layer of cube-shaped cells | Kidney tubules, thyroid follicles, gland ducts | Secretion, absorption |
| Simple columnar | One layer of tall cells, often with goblet cells and microvilli | Stomach, intestine, gall bladder | Absorption, secretion |
| Ciliated columnar | Columnar with cilia | Uterine (fallopian) tubes | Moves ova |
| Pseudostratified ciliated columnar | One layer that looks layered (nuclei at different levels), cilia and goblet cells | Trachea, bronchi | Traps and moves mucus |
| Stratified squamous keratinised | Many layers, surface cells dead and filled with keratin | Epidermis of skin | Protection, waterproofing |
| Stratified squamous non-keratinised | Many layers, surface cells alive and moist | Mouth, oesophagus, vagina, cornea | Protection against friction |
| Stratified cuboidal / columnar | Two or more layers | Ducts of sweat glands; male urethra (parts) | Protection |
| Transitional | Layers of cells that change shape on stretching | Urinary bladder, ureters | Allows stretching |

```text
Simple squamous    ___ ___ ___ ___        flat, one layer
Simple cuboidal    [o][o][o][o]           cubes, central nuclei
Simple columnar    |o||o||o||o|           tall, nuclei near base
Stratified squamous   ~~~~~~~~~  flat at surface
                      ooooooooo
                      [o][o][o]  cuboidal at base
==== basement membrane =================
```

**Glandular epithelium:** exocrine glands release secretions through ducts (sweat, salivary, pancreatic enzymes); endocrine glands are ductless and release hormones into blood (thyroid, adrenal). Exocrine glands are classified by structure (unicellular goblet cell, multicellular simple or compound, tubular or alveolar) and by mode of secretion: merocrine (exocytosis, e.g. salivary), apocrine (top of cell pinched off, e.g. mammary), holocrine (whole cell bursts, e.g. sebaceous).

### 1.11.2 Connective tissue

The most widespread tissue. It has few cells scattered in plenty of **matrix** (ground substance + fibres). Fibres: **collagen** (strength), **elastic** (stretch and recoil), **reticular** (fine supporting network). Cells: fibroblasts, macrophages, mast cells (histamine, heparin), plasma cells, adipocytes, white cells.

| Type | Features | Location | Function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Areolar (loose) | All three fibres in a loose network, many cell types | Under skin, around organs and vessels | Binds and supports, holds tissue fluid |
| Adipose | Fat-storing cells with nucleus pushed to the side ("signet ring") | Subcutaneous layer, around kidneys and heart | Energy store, insulation, padding |
| Reticular | Network of reticular fibres | Lymph nodes, spleen, bone marrow, liver | Framework (stroma) for organs |
| Dense regular | Parallel collagen bundles | Tendons, ligaments | Strong pull in one direction |
| Dense irregular | Collagen in different directions | Dermis, organ capsules, periosteum | Strength in many directions |
| Elastic | Mostly elastic fibres | Walls of large arteries, vocal cords | Stretch and recoil |
| Hyaline cartilage | Chondrocytes in lacunae, glassy matrix | Articular surfaces, costal cartilage, trachea, larynx | Smooth surface, flexible support |
| Fibrocartilage | Thick collagen bundles | Intervertebral discs, pubic symphysis, menisci | Withstands compression |
| Elastic cartilage | Elastic fibres in matrix | External ear, epiglottis | Shape with flexibility |
| Bone (osseous) | Calcified matrix (calcium phosphate) with osteocytes in lacunae, arranged in osteons | Skeleton | Support, protection, mineral store |
| Blood and lymph | Liquid matrix (plasma) | Vessels | Transport |

### 1.11.3 Muscular tissue

Made of long cells (muscle fibres) that contract.

| Feature | Skeletal | Cardiac | Smooth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shape | Long cylindrical | Branched | Spindle shaped |
| Nuclei | Many, at the edge | One (or two), central | One, central |
| Striations | Present | Present | Absent |
| Special feature | — | Intercalated discs (desmosomes + gap junctions) | Gap junctions in sheets |
| Control | Voluntary | Involuntary (autorhythmic) | Involuntary |
| Location | Attached to bones | Heart wall | Walls of stomach, intestine, vessels, bladder, uterus |

### 1.11.4 Nervous tissue

Made of **neurons** (conduct impulses) and **neuroglia** (support cells).

```text
 dendrites
   \ | /
    \|/
  ( cell body )  -- nucleus, Nissl granules
      |
      | axon hillock
      |=====axon (myelin sheath)== node of Ranvier ==|
                                                      \ axon terminals
                                                      / (synaptic knobs)
```

- **Neuron parts:** cell body (soma) with nucleus and Nissl bodies; dendrites carry impulses towards the cell body; one axon carries impulses away. Many axons are covered by a **myelin sheath** (Schwann cells in the PNS, oligodendrocytes in the CNS) with gaps called **nodes of Ranvier**, allowing fast saltatory conduction.
- **Types by function:** sensory (afferent), motor (efferent), interneurons.
- **Types by structure:** multipolar (motor neurons), bipolar (retina), unipolar/pseudounipolar (sensory ganglia).
- **Neuroglia:** astrocytes (blood–brain barrier support), oligodendrocytes, microglia (phagocytic), ependymal cells (line ventricles, CSF) in the CNS; Schwann cells and satellite cells in the PNS.

**Membranes formed from tissues:** mucous membranes (line openings to the exterior), serous membranes (pleura, pericardium, peritoneum), synovial membranes (line joint cavities) and the cutaneous membrane (skin).

## Remember

- Levels: chemical → cellular → tissue → organ → system → organism.
- Negative feedback restores normal (BP, temperature, glucose); positive feedback amplifies (labour, clotting).
- Anatomical position: upright, facing forward, palms forward.
- Fluid mosaic model; Na+/K+ ATPase moves 3 Na+ out and 2 K+ in per ATP.
- Mitosis: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase; gives two identical 46-chromosome cells.
- Junctions: tight (seal), desmosome (anchor), gap (communication).
- Signalling forms: contact-dependent, paracrine, synaptic, endocrine; second messengers cAMP, IP3/DAG, Ca2+.
- Four tissues: epithelial, connective, muscular, nervous; epithelium is avascular and sits on a basement membrane.

# Unit 4: Peripheral nervous system and special senses

The peripheral nervous system links the brain and spinal cord with the rest of the body. The autonomic part is the basis of autonomic pharmacology (cholinergic and adrenergic drugs), so its organ effects must be known well. The second half covers the eye, ear, nose and tongue and their common disorders.

## 4.1 Organisation of the nervous system

```text
NERVOUS SYSTEM
 |-- Central nervous system (CNS): brain + spinal cord
 |-- Peripheral nervous system (PNS): cranial nerves (12 pairs), spinal nerves (31 pairs),
      ganglia, sensory receptors
        |-- Afferent (sensory) division: carries impulses from receptors TO the CNS
        |      - somatic sensory (skin, muscles, joints, special senses)
        |      - visceral sensory (internal organs)
        |-- Efferent (motor) division: carries impulses FROM the CNS
               |-- Somatic nervous system: to skeletal muscle (voluntary); one neuron
               |-- Autonomic nervous system (ANS): to cardiac muscle, smooth muscle,
                   glands (involuntary); two-neuron chain
                     |-- Sympathetic ("fight or flight")
                     |-- Parasympathetic ("rest and digest")
                     |-- Enteric (network in the gut wall)
```

| Feature | Somatic | Autonomic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Effector | Skeletal muscle | Cardiac muscle, smooth muscle, glands |
| Control | Voluntary | Involuntary |
| Neurons from CNS to effector | One | Two (preganglionic + postganglionic) with a ganglion between |
| Neurotransmitter at effector | Acetylcholine (nicotinic) | Acetylcholine or noradrenaline |
| Effect | Always excitation | Excitation or inhibition |

## 4.2 Sympathetic nervous system

**Outflow:** **thoracolumbar** — preganglionic cell bodies in the lateral horn of spinal segments **T1 to L2**.

**Structure**

- Preganglionic fibres are **short** and myelinated (white rami communicantes); they leave through ventral roots.
- Ganglia lie close to the spinal cord: the **paravertebral (sympathetic) chain** on each side of the vertebral column, and the **prevertebral (collateral) ganglia** in front of the aorta — coeliac, superior mesenteric and inferior mesenteric.
- Postganglionic fibres are **long** and unmyelinated (grey rami communicantes back to spinal nerves).
- One preganglionic fibre synapses with many postganglionic neurons, so effects are widespread.
- The **adrenal medulla** is a modified sympathetic ganglion: preganglionic fibres stimulate it directly to release adrenaline (mostly) and noradrenaline into the blood.

**Neurotransmitters:** preganglionic → acetylcholine (on **nicotinic NN** receptors); postganglionic → **noradrenaline** (on α and β adrenergic receptors). Exception: sympathetic fibres to sweat glands release acetylcholine (muscarinic).

## 4.3 Parasympathetic nervous system

**Outflow:** **craniosacral** — brainstem nuclei of cranial nerves **III, VII, IX and X**, and sacral spinal segments **S2–S4**.

**Structure**

- Preganglionic fibres are **long**; ganglia lie **near or within the organ** (terminal ganglia: ciliary, pterygopalatine, submandibular, otic, and ganglia in the walls of thoracic and abdominal organs).
- Postganglionic fibres are **short**; effects are more localised.
- The **vagus nerve (X)** carries about three-quarters of all parasympathetic fibres, to the heart, lungs and most of the digestive tract.

**Neurotransmitters:** acetylcholine at both levels — nicotinic at the ganglion, **muscarinic** at the effector.

```text
SYMPATHETIC
 Spinal cord T1-L2 ---short pre (ACh)---> [ganglion near cord] ----long post (NA)----> organ
                                          nicotinic                α / β receptors

PARASYMPATHETIC
 Brainstem / S2-S4 ------long pre (ACh)------------> [ganglion at organ] -short post (ACh)-> organ
                                                     nicotinic             muscarinic

ADRENAL MEDULLA
 Spinal cord ---pre (ACh)---> adrenal medulla ---> adrenaline into BLOOD

SOMATIC
 Spinal cord ----------one myelinated neuron (ACh)---------------> skeletal muscle (nicotinic NM)
```

## 4.4 Functions: comparison of sympathetic and parasympathetic effects

| Organ | Sympathetic | Parasympathetic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eye — pupil | Dilation (mydriasis; radial muscle, α1) | Constriction (miosis; sphincter pupillae, M3) |
| Eye — ciliary muscle | Relaxation (far vision) | Contraction (accommodation for near vision) |
| Heart rate and force | Increase (β1) | Decrease in rate (M2) |
| Bronchi | Dilation (β2) | Constriction, more secretion |
| Blood vessels of skin and gut | Constriction (α1) | Little effect (no parasympathetic supply to most vessels) |
| Blood vessels of skeletal muscle | Dilation (β2) | — |
| Gut motility and secretion | Decrease; sphincters contract | Increase; sphincters relax |
| Salivary glands | Thick, scanty saliva | Profuse watery saliva |
| Urinary bladder | Detrusor relaxes, sphincter contracts (retention) | Detrusor contracts, sphincter relaxes (emptying) |
| Liver | Glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis (blood glucose rises) | Glycogen synthesis |
| Sweat glands | Sweating (cholinergic sympathetic) | — |
| Adrenal medulla | Secretes adrenaline | — |
| Male sex organs | Ejaculation | Erection |
| Overall | "Fight or flight": prepares for emergency, uses energy | "Rest and digest": conserves energy |

**Dual innervation:** most organs get both divisions with opposite effects; the balance (autonomic tone) sets the activity. Drugs that copy or block these transmitters (adrenaline, salbutamol, propranolol, atropine, pilocarpine) are studied in pharmacology.

## 4.5 Spinal nerves: origin and functions

There are **31 pairs** of spinal nerves: **8 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral, 1 coccygeal**.

**Origin:** each nerve is formed by two roots from the spinal cord:

- **Dorsal (posterior) root** — sensory; carries the **dorsal root ganglion** (cell bodies of sensory neurons).
- **Ventral (anterior) root** — motor (axons of motor neurons in the anterior horn, plus autonomic fibres at T1–L2 and S2–S4).

The roots join in the intervertebral foramen to form a **mixed spinal nerve** (sensory + motor). Outside, it divides into a dorsal ramus (muscles and skin of the back), a ventral ramus (front and sides of trunk and the limbs), a meningeal branch and rami communicantes (to the sympathetic chain).

```text
           dorsal root ganglion
   dorsal root   (o)
  ______________/   \___________ mixed spinal nerve ---> dorsal ramus (back)
 | SPINAL CORD  |                                   \--> ventral ramus (limbs, front)
 |______________|\_________________/                 \-> rami communicantes
   ventral root (motor)                                    (sympathetic chain)
```

**Plexuses** — ventral rami (except T2–T12) join and branch to form networks:

| Plexus | Spinal segments | Important nerves and what they supply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cervical | C1–C4 (C5 contributes) | **Phrenic nerve (C3, C4, C5)** — the only motor supply of the diaphragm ("C3, 4, 5 keeps the diaphragm alive"); skin and muscles of neck |
| Brachial | C5–T1 | Axillary (deltoid), musculocutaneous (front of arm), **radial** (back of arm and forearm; injury → wrist drop), **median** (front of forearm and thumb; compressed in carpal tunnel syndrome), **ulnar** (small hand muscles; "funny bone") |
| Lumbar | L1–L4 | **Femoral** (quadriceps, front of thigh), obturator (adductors of thigh) |
| Sacral | L4–S4 | **Sciatic nerve** (L4–S3) — the largest and longest nerve; back of thigh, leg and foot; pudendal nerve (perineum) |
| Thoracic (no plexus) | T2–T12 | Intercostal nerves to intercostal muscles and skin of chest and abdominal wall |

**Functions of spinal nerves:** carry sensations (touch, pain, temperature, position) from the body to the cord; carry motor commands to skeletal muscles; carry autonomic fibres; form the pathways of **spinal reflexes** (receptor → sensory neuron → cord → motor neuron → effector; for example, the knee jerk). A **dermatome** is the area of skin supplied by one spinal nerve.

## 4.6 Cranial nerves: origin and functions

Twelve pairs, numbered from front to back by where they leave the brain.

| No. | Name | Type | Origin (brain part) | Main functions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | Olfactory | Sensory | Olfactory bulb / cerebrum | Smell |
| II | Optic | Sensory | Diencephalon (via optic chiasma to thalamus) | Vision |
| III | Oculomotor | Motor (with parasympathetic) | Midbrain | Moves eye (superior, inferior and medial recti, inferior oblique), raises upper eyelid; constricts pupil and accommodates lens |
| IV | Trochlear | Motor | Midbrain (only nerve leaving the back of the brainstem) | Superior oblique muscle of the eye |
| V | Trigeminal | Mixed | Pons | Sensation of face, scalp, teeth, cornea (ophthalmic, maxillary, mandibular branches); muscles of chewing (mandibular branch) |
| VI | Abducens | Motor | Pons | Lateral rectus — turns eye outward |
| VII | Facial | Mixed (with parasympathetic) | Pons | Muscles of facial expression; taste from anterior two-thirds of tongue; tears and saliva (submandibular, sublingual, lacrimal glands); paralysis = Bell's palsy |
| VIII | Vestibulocochlear | Sensory | Junction of pons and medulla | Hearing (cochlear part) and balance (vestibular part) |
| IX | Glossopharyngeal | Mixed (with parasympathetic) | Medulla | Taste and general sensation from posterior one-third of tongue; swallowing; parotid saliva; carotid sinus and body reflexes |
| X | Vagus | Mixed (with parasympathetic) | Medulla | Parasympathetic supply to heart, lungs and gut; swallowing and voice (pharynx, larynx); sensation from thoracic and abdominal organs |
| XI | Accessory | Motor | Medulla and upper cervical cord | Sternocleidomastoid and trapezius (turning head, shrugging shoulders); pharynx and larynx (with vagus) |

**Memory aids** — names: "**O**h **O**h **O**h **T**o **T**ouch **A**nd **F**eel **V**ery **G**ood **V**elvet, **A**h **H**eaven". Type: "**S**ome **S**ay **M**arry **M**oney **B**ut **M**y **B**rother **S**ays **B**ig **B**rains **M**atter **M**ore" (S = sensory, M = motor, B = both).

## 4.7 The eye: structure

The eyeball sits in the bony orbit, protected by eyelids, eyelashes, eyebrows, the conjunctiva and tears.

```text
                    SCLERA (white, fibrous)
               _____________________________
     CORNEA  /  CHOROID (vascular, pigment)  \
   (clear)  /    RETINA (rods, cones)         \
   )      |                                    |
   ) aq.  |  LENS                              |  FOVEA CENTRALIS
   ) hum. |  (  )     VITREOUS BODY            |  (in macula lutea,
   )      |  iris                              |   sharpest vision)
  anterior| ciliary body                      _|___ OPTIC DISC
  chamber  \  (suspensory ligaments)         /  ==== OPTIC NERVE (II)
            \_______________________________/   (blind spot - no
                                                 rods or cones)
```

**Three coats (tunics)**

| Coat | Parts | Features and functions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outer fibrous | Sclera (posterior five-sixths) | Tough white coat; shape and protection; muscle attachment |
| | Cornea (anterior one-sixth) | Transparent, avascular; main refracting surface of the eye |
| Middle vascular (uvea) | Choroid | Blood vessels nourish the retina; pigment absorbs stray light |
| | Ciliary body | Ciliary muscle changes lens shape (accommodation); ciliary processes secrete aqueous humour |
| | Iris | Coloured diaphragm; pupil in its centre; sphincter pupillae (parasympathetic, constricts) and dilator pupillae (sympathetic, dilates) control light entry |
| Inner nervous | Retina | Photoreceptors: **rods** (dim light, no colour; contain rhodopsin, needs vitamin A) and **cones** (bright light, colour, fine detail; packed in the fovea). Bipolar and ganglion cells; ganglion cell axons form the optic nerve |

**Contents:** **lens** — transparent, biconvex, elastic, held by suspensory ligaments (zonules); fine focusing. **Aqueous humour** — clear fluid in front of the lens, made by the ciliary body, drained through the **canal of Schlemm**; nourishes cornea and lens and keeps intraocular pressure. **Vitreous body** — jelly behind the lens; keeps the eyeball's shape and holds the retina in place.

**Accessory structures:** eyelids and eyelashes; **conjunctiva** (thin mucous membrane lining the lids and covering the sclera); **lacrimal apparatus** (lacrimal gland → tears over the eye → puncta → lacrimal canaliculi → lacrimal sac → nasolacrimal duct → nose; tears contain lysozyme); six **extraocular muscles** (four recti and two obliques).

## 4.8 Physiology of vision

1. **Refraction** — light is bent by the cornea (most), aqueous humour, lens and vitreous to focus an inverted image on the retina.
2. **Accommodation** for near vision — ciliary muscle contracts, suspensory ligaments slacken, lens becomes more convex; pupils constrict; eyes converge.
3. **Pupillary light reflex** — bright light → pupils constrict (via oculomotor parasympathetic fibres).
4. **Photochemistry** — light splits rhodopsin (opsin + retinal) in rods, changing the receptor potential; signals pass through bipolar and ganglion cells.
5. **Visual pathway** — optic nerve → optic chiasma (fibres from the nasal half of each retina cross) → optic tract → lateral geniculate body of the thalamus → optic radiation → **visual cortex in the occipital lobe**.

## 4.9 The ear: structure and functions

```text
 EXTERNAL EAR        MIDDLE EAR (air)                INNER EAR (fluid)
 auricle             malleus-incus-stapes            semicircular canals (balance)
  \                    |      |     |                 vestibule (utricle, saccule)
   \__ auditory  ___ TYMPANIC __|__ STAPES in        COCHLEA (hearing,
       canal  -->    MEMBRANE      OVAL WINDOW        organ of Corti)
       (ceruminous                round window
        glands)       |
                   EUSTACHIAN (auditory) TUBE -> nasopharynx (equalises pressure)
```

| Part | Structures | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| External ear | Auricle (pinna, elastic cartilage), external auditory meatus (with cerumen glands and hairs), tympanic membrane (eardrum) | Collects sound and leads it to the eardrum; eardrum vibrates |
| Middle ear | Air-filled cavity in the temporal bone; three ossicles — **malleus, incus, stapes** (smallest bone in the body); auditory (Eustachian) tube to the pharynx; oval and round windows | Ossicles amplify and pass vibrations to the oval window; the tube keeps air pressure equal on both sides of the eardrum |
| Inner ear | Bony labyrinth filled with perilymph enclosing a membranous labyrinth filled with endolymph: **cochlea** (spiral, contains the organ of Corti with hair cells on the basilar membrane), **vestibule** (utricle and saccule — static balance, linear acceleration), **semicircular canals** (three, at right angles; ampullae sense rotation — dynamic balance) | Hearing and balance |

**Mechanism of hearing:** sound waves → external canal → eardrum vibrates → ossicles amplify → stapes pushes the oval window → waves in the perilymph → basilar membrane vibrates (high pitch near the base, low pitch near the apex) → hair cells of the organ of Corti bend against the tectorial membrane → nerve impulses in the cochlear nerve → **auditory cortex in the temporal lobe**. The round window bulges out to release pressure.

**Mechanism of balance:** movement of the head moves endolymph and otoliths (calcium carbonate crystals in the utricle and saccule), bending hair cells → vestibular nerve → brainstem and cerebellum.

## 4.10 The nose: sense of smell (olfaction)

- **Olfactory epithelium** lies in the roof of the nasal cavity (about 5 cm² in total). It contains **olfactory receptor cells** (bipolar neurons with cilia), supporting cells and basal stem cells; olfactory glands keep the surface moist so odour molecules dissolve.
- **Pathway:** receptor cells → axons pass through the **cribriform plate of the ethmoid** (olfactory nerve, I) → olfactory bulb → olfactory tract → olfactory cortex in the temporal lobe and limbic system (strong link with memory and emotion).
- Receptors **adapt** quickly (we stop noticing a constant smell). Smell adds much of what we call "flavour".
- The nose also warms, moistens and filters inspired air.

## 4.11 The tongue: sense of taste (gustation)

- The tongue is a muscular organ covered by mucous membrane with projections called **papillae**:
  - **Filiform** — most numerous, pointed, no taste buds (give a rough surface for grip).
  - **Fungiform** — mushroom-shaped, scattered, especially at the tip and sides; contain taste buds.
  - **Circumvallate (vallate)** — 8–12 large ones in a V-shaped row at the back; most taste buds.
  - **Foliate** — folds on the sides at the back.
- **Taste buds** contain gustatory receptor cells with microvilli (taste hairs) projecting through a taste pore, plus supporting and basal cells. Chemicals must dissolve in saliva to be tasted.
- **Five basic tastes:** sweet, sour, salty, bitter and umami (savoury, from glutamate). All areas of the tongue can sense all tastes; the old "tongue map" is not accurate, though sensitivity varies slightly.
- **Nerves:** facial nerve (VII, chorda tympani) — anterior two-thirds; glossopharyngeal nerve (IX) — posterior one-third; vagus (X) — epiglottis and throat. Pathway → medulla → thalamus → gustatory cortex (parietal lobe).
- Other functions of the tongue: chewing, swallowing, speech.

## 4.12 Disorders of the special senses

| Organ | Disorder | Key point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eye | Myopia (short sight) | Image falls in front of the retina (long eyeball); corrected by a **concave** lens |
| | Hypermetropia (long sight) | Image falls behind the retina (short eyeball); corrected by a **convex** lens |
| | Presbyopia | Loss of lens elasticity with age; near vision poor; convex reading lenses |
| | Astigmatism | Uneven curvature of the cornea; cylindrical lens |
| | Cataract | Opacity of the lens; treated by lens replacement surgery |
| | Glaucoma | Raised intraocular pressure from poor drainage of aqueous humour → optic nerve damage, can cause blindness; treated with drugs such as timolol and pilocarpine |
| | Conjunctivitis | Inflammation of the conjunctiva (infection, allergy) |
| | Night blindness | Vitamin A deficiency (less rhodopsin) |
| | Colour blindness | Inherited cone defect, commonly red–green, more common in males |
| Ear | Otitis media | Middle ear infection, often in children via the Eustachian tube |
| | Deafness | Conductive (wax, eardrum or ossicle problem) or sensorineural (cochlea or nerve damage; noise, ageing, drugs such as aminoglycosides) |
| | Ménière's disease | Excess endolymph → vertigo, tinnitus, hearing loss |
| | Motion sickness | Mismatch between vestibular and visual signals; nausea |
| | Tinnitus, vertigo | Ringing in the ear; spinning sensation |
| Nose | Anosmia | Loss of smell (head injury, viral infection, nasal blockage) |
| | Rhinitis, sinusitis | Inflammation of nasal lining or sinuses (infection, allergy) |
| Tongue | Ageusia / hypogeusia | Loss / reduction of taste (nerve damage, drugs, zinc deficiency, infection) |
| | Glossitis | Inflamed tongue (B-vitamin or iron deficiency) |

## Remember

- PNS = 12 pairs cranial + 31 pairs spinal nerves (8 C, 12 T, 5 L, 5 S, 1 Co).
- Sympathetic: thoracolumbar (T1–L2), short pre, long post, noradrenaline. Parasympathetic: craniosacral (III, VII, IX, X, S2–S4), long pre, short post, acetylcholine (muscarinic).
- Sympathetic dilates pupil and bronchi, speeds heart; parasympathetic constricts pupil, slows heart, increases gut activity.
- Phrenic nerve C3–C5; sciatic nerve is the largest; radial nerve injury → wrist drop.
- Cranial nerves: I, II, VIII sensory; III, IV, VI, XI, XII motor; V, VII, IX, X mixed.
- Eye coats: fibrous (sclera, cornea), vascular (choroid, ciliary body, iris), nervous (retina); rods need vitamin A.
- Ossicles: malleus, incus, stapes; organ of Corti in the cochlea; semicircular canals for balance.
- Taste: VII anterior two-thirds, IX posterior one-third; filiform papillae have no taste buds.
- Myopia → concave lens; hypermetropia → convex lens; glaucoma = raised eye pressure.

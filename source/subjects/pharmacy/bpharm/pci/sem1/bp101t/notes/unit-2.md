# Unit 2: Skin, skeleton, skeletal muscle and joints

This unit covers the body's outer covering (the integumentary system), the bony framework (skeletal system), how skeletal muscle contracts and how bones meet at joints. Exam favourites are the layers of the skin, the classification of bones and joints, the bones of the skull and vertebral column, and the steps of muscle contraction at the neuromuscular junction.

## 2.1 Integumentary system: structure of skin

The skin is the largest organ of the body (about 1.5–2 m² in an adult). It has two main layers, the **epidermis** and the **dermis**, resting on the **hypodermis** (subcutaneous layer), together with accessory structures (hair, nails, glands).

```text
 surface   ^ hair shaft           sweat pore
 ----------|-----------------------o------------------
 EPIDERMIS | stratum corneum (dead, keratin)   |
 (stratified| stratum lucidum (thick skin only) |

            |   melanocytes)                    |
 ~~~~~~~~~~~|~~~~~ dermal papillae ~~~~~~~~~~~~~|~~~~
 DERMIS     | papillary layer (loose, capillaries, Meissner's corpuscles)
            | reticular layer (dense irregular CT)
            |  hair follicle, sebaceous gland --+  arrector pili muscle

 -----------|------------------------------------------

```

### Epidermis (stratified squamous keratinised epithelium; no blood vessels)

| Layer (deep to surface) | Features |
| --- | --- |
| Stratum basale (germinativum) | Single row of dividing stem cells; melanocytes and Merkel cells (touch) |
| Stratum spinosum | Several layers held by desmosomes ("prickle cells"); Langerhans cells (immune) |
| Stratum granulosum | Cells flatten, form keratohyalin granules and begin to die |
| Stratum lucidum | Clear layer, only in thick skin of palms and soles |
| Stratum corneum | 20–30 layers of dead, flat, keratin-filled cells; shed continuously |

Cells of the epidermis: **keratinocytes** (make keratin, the main cells), **melanocytes** (make melanin pigment that protects against UV), **Langerhans cells** (pick up antigens, part of immunity), **Merkel cells** (light touch). A new cell takes about four weeks to move from the basal layer to the surface and be shed.

### Dermis (connective tissue)

- **Papillary layer** — areolar tissue with dermal papillae that form fingerprints; capillary loops; Meissner's corpuscles (touch); free nerve endings (pain, temperature).
- **Reticular layer** — dense irregular connective tissue with collagen and elastic fibres (strength and elasticity), hair follicles, sebaceous and sweat glands, blood vessels and nerves.

### Hypodermis (subcutaneous layer)

Areolar and adipose tissue anchoring skin to underlying tissues; stores fat; Pacinian corpuscles (pressure). Subcutaneous injections are given into this layer.

### Accessory structures

| Structure | Key points |
| --- | --- |
| Hair | Shaft above surface, root in a follicle; bulb with matrix cells; arrector pili muscle raises hair ("goose bumps") |
| Sebaceous glands | Holocrine glands opening into hair follicles; secrete sebum that oils hair and skin and is mildly antibacterial; blocked glands cause acne |
| Eccrine sweat glands | Most numerous, especially palms, soles, forehead; open on the skin surface; watery sweat for cooling |
| Apocrine sweat glands | Axilla and groin; open into hair follicles; active after puberty; secretion broken down by bacteria causes body odour |
| Ceruminous glands | Modified sweat glands in the ear canal; make cerumen (ear wax) |
| Nails | Hard keratin plates on the distal phalanges; grow from the nail matrix |

## 2.2 Functions of skin

1. **Protection** — physical barrier against germs, chemicals and injury; keratin waterproofs; melanin absorbs UV; acid mantle (pH about 5).
2. **Temperature regulation** — sweating (evaporative cooling) and changing blood flow in the dermis (vasodilation to lose heat, vasoconstriction to save heat).
3. **Sensation** — receptors for touch, pressure, pain, heat and cold.
4. **Vitamin D synthesis** — UV light converts 7-dehydrocholesterol in the skin to vitamin D3, later activated by liver and kidney.
5. **Excretion** — small amounts of water, salt and urea in sweat.
6. **Absorption** — lipid-soluble substances (steroids, nitroglycerin, nicotine patches, fat-soluble vitamins) pass through; the basis of transdermal drug delivery.
7. **Storage** — fat in the hypodermis; blood reservoir in dermal vessels.
8. **Immunity** — Langerhans cells present antigens.
9. **Communication** — facial expression, blushing.

**Common disorders:** acne, psoriasis (rapid turnover of epidermis), eczema (dermatitis), burns (first, second and third degree), vitiligo (loss of melanocytes), skin cancers.

## 2.3 Skeletal system: divisions and functions

The adult skeleton has **206 bones**.

| Division | Bones | Number |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Axial skeleton** (80) | Skull: cranial 8 + facial 14 | 22 |
| | Hyoid | 1 |
| | Auditory ossicles (malleus, incus, stapes × 2) | 6 |
| | Vertebral column | 26 |
| | Thoracic cage: sternum 1 + ribs 24 | 25 |
| **Appendicular skeleton** (126) | Pectoral girdles (clavicle, scapula × 2) | 4 |
| | Upper limbs (humerus, radius, ulna, 8 carpals, 5 metacarpals, 14 phalanges × 2) | 60 |
| | Pelvic girdle (hip bones × 2) | 2 |
| | Lower limbs (femur, patella, tibia, fibula, 7 tarsals, 5 metatarsals, 14 phalanges × 2) | 60 |

**Functions of the skeleton:** support (framework); protection (skull for brain, ribs for heart and lungs, vertebrae for spinal cord); movement (levers for muscles); mineral storage (calcium and phosphate); blood cell formation (haemopoiesis in red marrow); energy storage (fat in yellow marrow).

## 2.4 Types of bones

| Type | Description | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long bones | Longer than wide; shaft and two ends | Femur, humerus, radius, phalanges |
| Short bones | Cube-like, nearly equal in length and width | Carpals, tarsals |
| Flat bones | Thin, flat, often curved; two plates of compact bone with spongy bone between | Cranial bones, sternum, ribs, scapula |
| Irregular bones | Complex shapes | Vertebrae, some facial bones, hip bone |
| Sesamoid bones | Develop inside tendons | Patella (largest) |
| Sutural (Wormian) bones | Small bones in skull sutures | Variable in number |

**Parts of a long bone**

```text
   ___  articular cartilage
  /   \  proximal EPIPHYSIS (spongy bone, red marrow)
  \___/  epiphyseal line / plate (growth)
   | |   METAPHYSIS
   | |
   | |   DIAPHYSIS (shaft): compact bone
   |:|   medullary cavity (yellow marrow), lined by endosteum
   | |   periosteum outside (with nutrient artery entering)
   | |
  /___\  distal epiphysis
  \___/  articular cartilage
```

**Bone tissue:** compact bone is built of **osteons (Haversian systems)** — a central (Haversian) canal with vessels, surrounded by rings of matrix (lamellae) with osteocytes in lacunae joined by canaliculi; Volkmann's canals connect them. Spongy (cancellous) bone has a lattice of trabeculae holding red marrow. Bone cells: **osteoblasts** (build bone), **osteocytes** (mature cells in lacunae), **osteoclasts** (resorb bone; large multinucleated). Parathyroid hormone raises blood calcium partly by stimulating resorption; calcitonin opposes it.

## 2.5 Axial skeleton: salient features

### Skull (22 bones)

| Cranial bones (8) | Facial bones (14) |
| --- | --- |
| Frontal (1) — forehead, roof of orbits, frontal sinus | Nasal (2) — bridge of nose |
| Parietal (2) — sides and roof of cranium | Maxillae (2) — upper jaw, hard palate (part), maxillary sinus |
| Temporal (2) — sides and base; houses middle and inner ear; mastoid and styloid processes; external acoustic meatus | Zygomatic (2) — cheek bones |
| Occipital (1) — back and base; foramen magnum; occipital condyles articulate with atlas | Lacrimal (2) — smallest facial bones, nasolacrimal canal |
| Sphenoid (1) — bat-shaped keystone; sella turcica holds the pituitary | Palatine (2) — back of hard palate |
| Ethmoid (1) — cribriform plate (olfactory nerves pass), nasal conchae | Inferior nasal conchae (2) |
| | Vomer (1) — lower nasal septum |
| | Mandible (1) — lower jaw, the only movable skull bone |

**Sutures:** coronal (frontal–parietal), sagittal (between parietals), lambdoid (parietal–occipital), squamous (parietal–temporal). **Fontanelles** are soft membranous gaps in the infant skull; the anterior fontanelle closes by about 18–24 months. **Paranasal sinuses** (frontal, sphenoid, ethmoid, maxillary) lighten the skull and give resonance to the voice.

### Hyoid bone

U-shaped bone in the neck that does not articulate with any other bone; held by muscles and ligaments; supports the tongue.

### Vertebral column (26 bones in adults)

| Region | Number | Special features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cervical | 7 | Smallest; transverse foramina for vertebral arteries; bifid spines. C1 **atlas** has no body or spine, holds the skull ("yes" movement); C2 **axis** has the dens (odontoid process) for rotation ("no" movement); C7 has a long prominent spine |
| Thoracic | 12 | Facets on bodies and transverse processes for ribs; long downward spines; heart-shaped bodies |
| Lumbar | 5 | Largest, heavy kidney-shaped bodies; short thick spines; carry most body weight |
| Sacrum | 1 (5 fused) | Triangular; forms back of pelvis; sacral foramina |
| Coccyx | 1 (usually 4 fused) | Tail bone |

**Typical vertebra:** body (weight bearing), vertebral arch (two pedicles + two laminae), vertebral foramen (spinal cord), spinous process, two transverse processes, four articular processes. Intervertebral discs (fibrocartilage: outer annulus fibrosus, inner nucleus pulposus) act as shock absorbers.

**Normal curves:** cervical and lumbar curves (concave posteriorly, lordosis) and thoracic and sacral curves (convex posteriorly, kyphosis). Abnormal curves: exaggerated kyphosis (hunchback), exaggerated lordosis, scoliosis (lateral bend).

### Thoracic cage

- **Sternum:** manubrium, body and xiphoid process; the sternal angle marks the level of the second rib.
- **Ribs (12 pairs):** true ribs 1–7 join the sternum by their own costal cartilage; false ribs 8–10 join through the cartilage of the rib above; floating ribs 11–12 have no anterior attachment.
- Function: protects heart and lungs; helps breathing movements.

**Male and female pelvis:** the female pelvis is wider and shallower, with a larger round inlet, wider pubic arch (more than 90°) and less projecting sacrum, suited for childbirth.

## 2.7 Organisation of skeletal muscle

```text
Muscle (covered by EPIMYSIUM)
  -> Fascicles (bundles, covered by PERIMYSIUM)
       -> Muscle fibres (cells, covered by ENDOMYSIUM; membrane = sarcolemma)
            -> Myofibrils
                 -> Sarcomeres (repeating contractile units)
                      -> Myofilaments: thick (myosin) and thin (actin, troponin, tropomyosin)
```

**The sarcomere** (Z line to Z line):

```text
 Z                     M                     Z
 |--------thin (actin)-------|                |
 |      |=====thick (myosin)=====|            |
 |  I   |   A band (dark)  H zone   |    I    |
 band   |<---- whole thick filament ---->|  band
 (light)                                    (light)
```

- **A band** — dark; length of the thick filaments (does not change during contraction).
- **I band** — light; thin filaments only; shortens in contraction.
- **H zone** — middle of A band with thick filaments only; shortens.
- **Z line** — anchors thin filaments; **M line** holds thick filaments in the centre.
- **Proteins:** myosin (heads bind actin and split ATP), actin (binding sites for myosin), tropomyosin (covers binding sites at rest), troponin (binds Ca2+ and moves tropomyosin).
- **Sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR)** stores Ca2+; **T-tubules** carry the action potential deep into the fibre. One T-tubule with two terminal cisternae forms a **triad**.

## 2.8 Neuromuscular junction (NMJ)

The synapse between a motor neuron's axon terminal and a skeletal muscle fibre at the **motor end plate**.

```text
   Motor axon terminal
   [  o o o  ]  <- synaptic vesicles (acetylcholine)
   [ o  o  o ]
    \_______/
  ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~  synaptic cleft (acetylcholinesterase here)
  /\/\/\/\/\/\/\/  motor end plate (junctional folds with nicotinic receptors)
  Muscle fibre (sarcolemma)
```

**Steps of transmission**

1. Nerve action potential reaches the axon terminal.
2. Voltage-gated Ca2+ channels open; Ca2+ enters.
3. Vesicles release **acetylcholine (ACh)** by exocytosis.
4. ACh crosses the cleft and binds **nicotinic (NM) receptors** on the end plate.
5. Na+ enters, producing the **end-plate potential**, which triggers a muscle action potential.
6. **Acetylcholinesterase** rapidly breaks ACh into choline and acetate, ending the signal; choline is taken back for reuse.

**Drugs and disorders at the NMJ**

| Agent / disorder | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| d-Tubocurarine, atracurium | Block nicotinic receptors (competitive) → paralysis; used as surgical muscle relaxants |
| Succinylcholine | Depolarising blocker |
| Neostigmine, pyridostigmine | Inhibit acetylcholinesterase → more ACh; used in myasthenia gravis |
| Organophosphates | Irreversibly inhibit cholinesterase (poisoning) |
| Botulinum toxin | Blocks ACh release |
| Myasthenia gravis | Autoantibodies against ACh receptors → muscle weakness and easy fatigue |

## 2.9 Physiology of muscle contraction (sliding filament theory)

During contraction the thin filaments **slide over** the thick filaments towards the M line; the filaments themselves do not shorten. The I band and H zone shorten, the Z lines come closer, the A band stays the same.

**Excitation–contraction coupling and cross-bridge cycle**

1. Muscle action potential spreads along the sarcolemma and down the T-tubules.
2. Voltage sensors (dihydropyridine receptors) in the T-tubules open the ryanodine receptor channels of the SR.
3. **Ca2+ is released** from the SR into the sarcoplasm.
4. Ca2+ binds **troponin C**; troponin shifts **tropomyosin** and uncovers myosin-binding sites on actin.
5. Energised myosin heads (holding ADP + Pi) bind actin, forming **cross-bridges**.
6. **Power stroke** — release of ADP and Pi swings the head and pulls actin towards the M line.
7. A new **ATP binds** myosin and the head detaches from actin.
8. ATP is split, re-cocking the head; the cycle repeats as long as Ca2+ and ATP are present.

**Relaxation:** ACh is destroyed by acetylcholinesterase; Ca2+ is pumped back into the SR by Ca2+-ATPase (uses ATP); tropomyosin covers the binding sites again. Without ATP, myosin cannot detach, which explains **rigor mortis** after death.

**Energy sources for contraction:** creatine phosphate (seconds), anaerobic glycolysis (lactic acid, short bursts), aerobic respiration (long activity). **Muscle fatigue** follows depletion of energy stores and build-up of metabolites.

**Types of contraction:** isotonic (muscle shortens, tension constant — lifting a weight) and isometric (tension rises, length constant — pushing a wall). A **motor unit** is one motor neuron plus all the fibres it supplies. A single stimulus gives a **twitch**; repeated rapid stimuli give **tetanus** (sustained contraction).

## 2.10 Joints (articulations)

A joint is a point of contact between two bones, bone and cartilage, or bone and teeth.

### Structural classification

| Type | Joined by | Subtypes and examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fibrous** | Dense fibrous tissue, no cavity | Sutures (skull); syndesmosis (distal tibiofibular joint, interosseous membrane); gomphosis (tooth in socket) |
| **Cartilaginous** | Cartilage, no cavity | Synchondrosis — hyaline cartilage (epiphyseal plate, first rib with sternum); symphysis — fibrocartilage (pubic symphysis, intervertebral discs) |
| **Synovial** | Joint cavity with synovial fluid, enclosed by a capsule | Shoulder, elbow, hip, knee and most limb joints |

### Functional classification

| Type | Movement | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Synarthrosis | Immovable | Sutures, gomphosis |
| Amphiarthrosis | Slightly movable | Pubic symphysis, intervertebral joints, distal tibiofibular |
| Diarthrosis | Freely movable | All synovial joints |

### Structure of a synovial joint

```text
       Bone A
   _____________
  |  articular   |  <- hyaline cartilage covering ends
  |  cartilage   |
  |~~~~~~~~~~~~~~|  <- JOINT CAVITY with SYNOVIAL FLUID
  |  articular   |
  |__cartilage___|
       Bone B
  Around it: ARTICULAR CAPSULE = outer fibrous capsule (+ ligaments)
             + inner SYNOVIAL MEMBRANE (secretes fluid)
  Extras: menisci (knee), bursae (fluid sacs reducing friction), fat pads
```

**Synovial fluid** (thick, made of hyaluronic acid and fluid from blood) lubricates, nourishes the cartilage and contains phagocytes.

### Types of synovial joints

| Type | Shape of surfaces | Movement (axes) | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plane (gliding) | Flat | Gliding (non-axial) | Intercarpal, intertarsal, acromioclavicular |
| Hinge | Convex into concave | Flexion and extension (one axis) | Elbow, knee (modified), interphalangeal, ankle |
| Pivot | Rounded end in a ring | Rotation (one axis) | Atlanto-axial joint, proximal radio-ulnar |
| Condyloid (ellipsoid) | Oval into oval cavity | Flexion–extension and abduction–adduction (two axes) | Wrist (radiocarpal), metacarpophalangeal |
| Saddle | Each surface saddle shaped | Two axes plus some circumduction | Carpometacarpal joint of the thumb |
| Ball and socket | Ball-shaped head in a cup | All directions including rotation (three axes) | Shoulder, hip |

### Movements at joints

| Movement | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Flexion / extension | Decrease / increase the angle between bones |
| Hyperextension | Extension beyond anatomical position |
| Abduction / adduction | Away from / towards the midline |
| Circumduction | Circular movement of a limb (flexion, abduction, extension, adduction in order) |
| Rotation (medial, lateral) | Turning a bone around its own long axis |
| Gliding | Flat surfaces slide on each other |
| Pronation / supination | Forearm turns palm down (back) / palm up (forward) |
| Inversion / eversion | Sole turns inward / outward |
| Dorsiflexion / plantar flexion | Foot bent upward / pointed downward |
| Elevation / depression | Part moved up / down (shrugging shoulders, opening jaw) |
| Protraction / retraction | Part moved forward / backward (jaw) |
| Opposition | Thumb touches the tips of the other fingers |

**Disorders of joints:** osteoarthritis (wear of articular cartilage, older people), rheumatoid arthritis (autoimmune inflammation of synovial membrane), gout (urate crystals, often the big toe), dislocation and sprain (torn ligaments), bursitis.

## Remember

- Epidermis layers from deep: basale, spinosum, granulosum, lucidum (thick skin only), corneum ("Come Let's Get Sun Burnt" read from the surface down).
- Skin makes vitamin D3 from 7-dehydrocholesterol with UV light.
- 206 bones: axial 80, appendicular 126; skull 22 (8 cranial + 14 facial).
- Vertebrae: 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, sacrum, coccyx; atlas C1, axis C2.
- True ribs 1–7, false 8–10, floating 11–12.
- Sarcomere = Z to Z; A band constant; I band and H zone shorten.
- Ca2+ binds troponin C; ATP is needed to detach myosin (lack of ATP → rigor mortis).
- NMJ transmitter is acetylcholine on nicotinic receptors; neostigmine is used in myasthenia gravis.
- Joints: fibrous, cartilaginous, synovial; six synovial types from plane to ball and socket.

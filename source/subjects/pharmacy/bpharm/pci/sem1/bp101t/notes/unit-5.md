# Unit 5: Cardiovascular system

At rest the heart pumps roughly the whole blood volume (about 5 litres) around a closed circuit of vessels every minute. Many important drug groups (antihypertensives, antianginals, antiarrhythmics, cardiac glycosides, diuretics) act on this system, so learn the anatomy, the conduction system, the cardiac cycle and blood pressure regulation thoroughly. Diagrams of the heart, the conduction system and a normal ECG are expected in long answers.

## 5.1 Heart: location, coverings and wall

- **Location:** in the **mediastinum** of the thorax, between the lungs, resting on the diaphragm; about two-thirds lies to the left of the midline. The pointed **apex** points down, forward and to the left (apex beat felt in the left fifth intercostal space, just medial to the midclavicular line); the broad **base** faces up and back.
- **Size:** about the size of a closed fist; weighs about 250–350 g in adults.

**Pericardium (covering)**

```text
 Fibrous pericardium (tough outer sac, anchors heart, prevents over-filling)
   Serous pericardium:
     parietal layer (lines fibrous sac)
       -- PERICARDIAL CAVITY with a few ml of pericardial fluid (reduces friction)
     visceral layer = EPICARDIUM (on the heart surface)
```

Inflammation is **pericarditis**; fluid collecting in the sac can compress the heart (cardiac tamponade).

**Wall of the heart (three layers)**

| Layer | Structure | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Epicardium | Visceral pericardium; mesothelium + connective tissue with fat and coronary vessels | Outer protective layer |
| Myocardium | Cardiac muscle; thickest layer, thickest in the left ventricle | Pumping |
| Endocardium | Endothelium on connective tissue; lines chambers and covers valves | Smooth lining, reduces friction |

## 5.2 Chambers, valves and great vessels

```text
                 Superior vena cava    Aorta (arch)
                        |               |    Pulmonary trunk
                        v               |     |
   Right    _________________   _______|_____|______
   pulmonary|  RIGHT ATRIUM  | |   LEFT ATRIUM      |<- 4 pulmonary veins
   artery   |      (RA)      | |      (LA)          |   (oxygenated blood)
            |___TRICUSPID____| |____MITRAL (bicusp.)|
            |                | |                    |
            | RIGHT VENTRICLE| |  LEFT VENTRICLE    |
            |  (thin wall)   | |  (thick wall)      |
            | pulmonary valve| |  aortic valve      |
            |________________|_|____________________|
   Inferior vena cava -> RA           apex (left)

```

| Chamber | Receives blood from | Sends blood to | Wall |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Right atrium | Superior and inferior venae cavae, coronary sinus | Right ventricle | Thin |
| Right ventricle | Right atrium | Pulmonary trunk → lungs | Moderate |
| Left atrium | Four pulmonary veins | Left ventricle | Thin |
| Left ventricle | Left atrium | Aorta → whole body | Thickest (pumps against high systemic pressure) |

**Valves** (keep one-way flow)

| Valve | Position | Cusps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tricuspid (right atrioventricular) | Between RA and RV | Three |
| Mitral / bicuspid (left atrioventricular) | Between LA and LV | Two |
| Pulmonary semilunar | Between RV and pulmonary trunk | Three half-moon cusps |
| Aortic semilunar | Between LV and aorta | Three |

AV valve cusps are tied by **chordae tendineae** to **papillary muscles**, which contract with the ventricle and stop the cusps turning back into the atria. Semilunar valves close when blood tries to flow back from the arteries.

**Coronary circulation:** the **right and left coronary arteries** arise from the aortic sinuses just above the aortic valve. The left divides into the anterior interventricular (left anterior descending) and circumflex branches; the right supplies most of the right side and usually the SA and AV nodes. Venous blood drains through cardiac veins into the **coronary sinus**, which opens into the right atrium. Coronary flow occurs mostly during diastole. Narrowing causes angina; blockage causes myocardial infarction.

## 5.3 Blood circulation

```text
           PULMONARY CIRCULATION
   RV -> pulmonary trunk -> pulmonary arteries -> LUNG capillaries (CO2 out, O2 in)
                                                        |
   RA <---------------------------------------- pulmonary veins -> LA
   ^                                                              |
   |       SYSTEMIC CIRCULATION                                  v
   venae cavae <- veins <- venules <- TISSUE capillaries <- arterioles <- arteries <- aorta <- LV
```

- **Pulmonary circulation:** right heart → lungs → left atrium; low pressure. Pulmonary arteries carry deoxygenated blood; pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood (the opposite of the systemic pattern).
- **Systemic circulation:** left heart → all tissues → right atrium; high pressure.
- **Portal circulation:** blood passes through two capillary beds in series. The **hepatic portal system** carries blood from the stomach, intestines, spleen and pancreas to the liver before it reaches the heart — the reason orally absorbed drugs undergo first-pass metabolism.
- **Coronary circulation:** supplies the heart wall itself.

## 5.4 Blood vessels: structure and functions

All vessels except capillaries have three layers (tunics):

```text
   lumen
   |  TUNICA INTIMA   : endothelium + basement membrane (+ internal elastic lamina)
   |  TUNICA MEDIA    : smooth muscle + elastic fibres (thick in arteries)
   |  TUNICA EXTERNA  : connective tissue (adventitia); in large vessels has vasa vasorum
```

| Feature | Arteries | Veins | Capillaries |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Direction of flow | Away from the heart | Towards the heart | Link arterioles and venules |
| Wall | Thick, much smooth muscle and elastic tissue | Thinner, less muscle, wider lumen | Single layer of endothelium on a basement membrane |
| Lumen | Narrow, round | Wide, often collapsed | About the size of one RBC (5–10 µm) |
| Pressure | High, pulsatile | Low | Low |
| Valves | Absent (except at the heart) | Present, especially in limbs | Absent |
| Blood | Oxygenated (except pulmonary arteries) | Deoxygenated (except pulmonary veins) | Exchange site |
| Function | Carry blood under pressure; elastic arteries smooth the flow; arterioles control resistance and blood pressure | Return blood; act as a blood reservoir (hold about 60–65% of blood volume) | Exchange of gases, nutrients, wastes with tissues |

**Types of arteries:** elastic (conducting) arteries — aorta and its main branches, stretch in systole and recoil in diastole (keep flow continuous); muscular (distributing) arteries — named arteries to organs; **arterioles** — "resistance vessels" whose smooth muscle controls peripheral resistance and blood flow to tissues.

**Types of capillaries:** continuous (most tissues, brain — tight), fenestrated (with pores: kidney glomeruli, intestinal villi, endocrine glands), sinusoidal (wide, leaky: liver, spleen, bone marrow).

**Venous return** is helped by valves, the **skeletal muscle pump** (contracting muscles squeeze veins), the **respiratory pump** (lower chest pressure in inspiration) and venoconstriction. Weak valves lead to varicose veins.

## 5.5 Conduction system of the heart and the heartbeat

Cardiac muscle is **autorhythmic**: specialised cells generate their own impulses.

```text
     SA NODE (pacemaker, wall of RA near SVC opening)
        |   impulse spreads over both atria -> atrial contraction
        v   (internodal pathways)
     AV NODE (floor of RA near the septum)  -- delay of about 0.1 s
        |                                      (lets atria empty into ventricles)
     BUNDLE OF HIS (AV bundle; only electrical link between atria and ventricles)
        |
   +----+----+
   |         |
 RIGHT     LEFT BUNDLE BRANCH   (down the interventricular septum)
 BRANCH        |
   |           |
   PURKINJE FIBRES (spread through ventricular walls from apex upward)
        -> ventricles contract from the apex towards the base
```

| Part | Approximate intrinsic rate (beats/min) |
| --- | --- |
| SA node | 70–80 (sets the heart rate: the natural pacemaker) |
| AV node | 40–60 |
| Purkinje fibres / ventricles | 20–40 |

If the SA node fails, the next fastest tissue takes over (ectopic pacemaker).

**Pacemaker potential:** SA node cells have no steady resting potential. A slow inward current (funny Na+ current and then Ca2+ entry) gradually depolarises them to threshold → Ca2+-driven action potential → repolarisation by K+ exit → the cycle repeats.

**Ventricular muscle action potential:** rapid depolarisation (Na+ entry) → brief early repolarisation → long **plateau** (Ca2+ entry through L-type channels) → repolarisation (K+ exit). The long plateau gives a **long refractory period**, so cardiac muscle **cannot be tetanised** and the heart always relaxes and refills between beats.

## 5.6 Regulation of the heartbeat by the autonomic nervous system

The **cardiovascular (cardiac) centre** in the **medulla oblongata** has a cardioacceleratory and a cardioinhibitory part.

| Nerve | Fibres reach | Transmitter / receptor | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sympathetic (cardiac accelerator nerves, T1–T4/T5) | SA node, AV node, atrial and ventricular muscle | Noradrenaline on β1 | Increases rate (positive chronotropic), force (positive inotropic) and conduction speed (positive dromotropic) |
| Parasympathetic (vagus nerve) | Mainly SA and AV nodes and atria | Acetylcholine on M2 | Decreases rate and AV conduction (negative chronotropic and dromotropic) |

At rest **vagal tone** dominates and keeps the heart rate about 72/min, below the SA node's intrinsic rate. Atropine (blocks M receptors) raises the heart rate; propranolol (blocks β1) lowers it.

**Inputs to the cardiac centre:** baroreceptors (carotid sinus, aortic arch), chemoreceptors (carotid and aortic bodies: low O2, high CO2, low pH), higher centres (emotion, exercise), proprioceptors. Other factors: adrenaline and thyroid hormones raise rate; body temperature (fever raises rate); ions (high K+ slows and can stop the heart; Ca2+ increases force).

## 5.7 Cardiac output

**Cardiac output (CO)** = volume of blood pumped by **each ventricle** per minute.

**CO = Stroke volume (SV) × Heart rate (HR)** ≈ 70 ml × 72/min ≈ **5 L/min** at rest. It can rise four- to fivefold in heavy exercise.

- **Stroke volume** = end-diastolic volume (about 130 ml) − end-systolic volume (about 60 ml) ≈ 70 ml.
- **Ejection fraction** = SV/EDV ≈ 55–65% (falls in heart failure).
- **Cardiac index** = CO per square metre of body surface area (about 3.2 L/min/m²).

**Factors affecting cardiac output**

| Factor | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Preload** (venous return, end-diastolic volume) | More filling stretches the fibres → stronger contraction. **Frank–Starling law:** within limits, the force of contraction is proportional to the initial length of the muscle fibres ("the heart pumps what it receives") |
| **Contractility** | Sympathetic stimulation, adrenaline, Ca2+, digoxin increase it; hypoxia, acidosis, some drugs decrease it |
| **Afterload** (pressure the ventricle must overcome, mainly arterial pressure) | High afterload (hypertension) lowers stroke volume |
| **Heart rate** | Raises CO, but very high rates shorten filling time and reduce SV |
| Others | Posture, exercise, emotion, temperature, pregnancy, hormones |

## 5.8 Cardiac cycle

The **cardiac cycle** is the sequence of events in one heartbeat: contraction (systole) and relaxation (diastole) of the atria and ventricles. At 75 beats/min it lasts **0.8 s**.

| Phase | Duration (approx.) | Events |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Atrial systole | 0.1 s | Atria contract and top up the ventricles (adds about 20–30% of filling); P wave precedes it |
| **Ventricular systole** | 0.3 s | |
| – Isovolumetric contraction | about 0.05 s | Ventricles contract with all valves closed; pressure rises steeply; AV valves close → **first heart sound (S1, "lub")** |
| – Ejection (rapid, then slow) | about 0.25 s | Ventricular pressure exceeds arterial pressure; semilunar valves open; blood ejected |
| **Ventricular diastole** | 0.5 s | |
| – Protodiastole and isovolumetric relaxation | short | Semilunar valves close → **second heart sound (S2, "dub")**; ventricles relax with all valves closed |
| – Rapid filling | | AV valves open; blood rushes from atria (a third heart sound may occur here) |
| – Slow filling (diastasis) | | Slow continued filling |
| – Last part | | Atrial systole of the next cycle completes filling |

Atrial diastole lasts about 0.7 s. **Total diastole of the heart (all chambers relaxed) ≈ 0.4 s.** When the heart rate rises, diastole shortens much more than systole, reducing filling and coronary flow.

```text
 (mmHg)

     |      /  pressure            \
   0 |_____/________________________\________
        ^   ^                ^   ^
       AV  aortic          aortic AV
     close  opens          closes opens
      (S1)                  (S2)
     |-- systole (0.3 s) --|---- diastole (0.5 s) ----|
```

## 5.9 Blood pressure and its regulation

**Blood pressure (BP)** is the lateral pressure exerted by blood on the vessel wall, measured in large arteries in mmHg.

| Term | Normal adult value | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Systolic pressure | About 120 mmHg | Highest pressure during ventricular systole |
| Diastolic pressure | About 80 mmHg | Lowest pressure during diastole |
| Pulse pressure | About 40 mmHg | Systolic − diastolic |
| Mean arterial pressure (MAP) | About 93 mmHg | Diastolic + one-third of pulse pressure; the average pushing blood to tissues |

**Determinants:** **BP = Cardiac output × Total peripheral resistance.** Other factors: blood volume, viscosity, elasticity of the arteries (stiff arteries in old age raise systolic BP), diameter of arterioles.

**Physiological variations:** rises with age, exercise, emotion, after meals; slightly lower during sleep; higher on standing compared with lying in diastolic pressure.

**Regulation**

```text
1. SHORT-TERM (seconds) - NERVOUS: baroreceptor reflex
   BP rises -> stretch of carotid sinus (via IX) and aortic arch (via X) baroreceptors
            -> more impulses to medulla (vasomotor + cardiac centres)
            -> vagal tone up, sympathetic tone down
            -> heart rate and force fall, arterioles dilate, veins dilate
            -> BP falls back to normal   (opposite happens when BP falls)
   Also: chemoreceptor reflex (carotid/aortic bodies), CNS ischaemic response

2. INTERMEDIATE / HORMONAL (minutes to hours)
   - Adrenaline and noradrenaline from the adrenal medulla
   - Renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS):
       low BP / low renal flow -> kidney (juxtaglomerular cells) releases RENIN
       angiotensinogen (liver) --renin--> angiotensin I --ACE (lungs)--> ANGIOTENSIN II
       angiotensin II: strong vasoconstriction + ALDOSTERONE (salt and water retention)
                       + ADH release + thirst  -> BP rises
   - ADH (vasopressin): water retention, vasoconstriction
   - Atrial natriuretic peptide (from stretched atria): Na+ and water loss, vasodilation -> BP falls

3. LONG-TERM (days) - RENAL
   Kidneys adjust salt and water excretion to blood volume (pressure natriuresis and diuresis)
```

This explains how drug classes lower BP: ACE inhibitors (enalapril), angiotensin receptor blockers (losartan), β-blockers, diuretics, calcium channel blockers, vasodilators.

**Measurement:** indirectly by a **sphygmomanometer** with a stethoscope (auscultatory method). The first tapping **Korotkoff sound** as the cuff is deflated marks systolic pressure; the point where the sounds disappear marks diastolic pressure (see practical).

## 5.10 Pulse

The **arterial pulse** is the rhythmic expansion and recoil of an artery wall caused by the pressure wave of each ventricular ejection. It travels much faster than the blood itself.

**Sites:** radial (wrist, most common), carotid (neck), brachial (cubital fossa, used for BP), temporal, facial, femoral (groin), popliteal (behind the knee), posterior tibial (behind the medial malleolus), dorsalis pedis (top of the foot).

**Normal pulse rate:** 60–100/min in adults (average about 72); faster in infants and children.

**What is examined:** rate, rhythm (regular or irregular), volume (strength), character, condition of the vessel wall, and equality on both sides.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Tachycardia | Rate above 100/min (exercise, fever, anaemia, thyrotoxicosis) |
| Bradycardia | Rate below 60/min (athletes, sleep, heart block, β-blockers) |
| Sinus arrhythmia | Rate rises in inspiration and falls in expiration (normal in the young) |
| Pulse deficit | Heart rate at the apex higher than pulse rate at the wrist (atrial fibrillation) |

## 5.11 Electrocardiogram (ECG)

The **ECG** is a recording, from the body surface, of the electrical activity of the heart during each cardiac cycle. It shows rhythm and conduction, not the force of contraction.

**Leads (standard 12-lead ECG)**

| Lead group | Leads | Placement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard bipolar limb leads (Einthoven's triangle) | I (right arm − left arm), II (right arm − left leg), III (left arm − left leg) | Electrodes on both arms and left leg (right leg is earth) |
| Augmented unipolar limb leads | aVR, aVL, aVF | Same limb electrodes |
| Unipolar chest (precordial) leads | V1 to V6 | Across the chest from right of sternum (4th space) to the left mid-axillary line |

**Normal ECG waves (lead II)**

```text
         R
         |
         |
  P      |        T
 / \     |       / \
/   \ Q  |  S   /   \     U (sometimes)
-----\_/  \_/--/-----\---------------
 |<-PR->|<QRS>|
 |<------- QT interval ------->|
        ST segment between S and T
```

| Component | Represents | Normal duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P wave | Atrial depolarisation | About 0.08–0.10 s |
| PR interval | Start of P to start of QRS: conduction from atria through AV node (AV delay) | 0.12–0.20 s |
| QRS complex | Ventricular depolarisation (atrial repolarisation is hidden in it) | 0.08–0.10 s (less than 0.12 s) |
| ST segment | Ventricles fully depolarised; normally on the baseline | — |
| T wave | Ventricular repolarisation | — |
| QT interval | Start of QRS to end of T: whole ventricular depolarisation and repolarisation | About 0.36–0.44 s (varies with heart rate) |

**Recording details:** standard paper speed 25 mm/s, so one small square (1 mm) = 0.04 s and one large square (5 mm) = 0.2 s; standard sensitivity 10 mm = 1 mV. Heart rate = 1500 ÷ number of small squares between two R waves (or 300 ÷ large squares).

**Uses:** detecting arrhythmias, heart block, myocardial ischaemia and infarction (ST changes, Q waves), chamber enlargement, electrolyte disturbances (tall peaked T waves in high K+), and effects of drugs (digoxin, QT-prolonging drugs).

## 5.12 Disorders of the heart

| Disorder | Key points |
| --- | --- |
| Hypertension | Persistent BP at or above about 140/90 mmHg; usually no cause found (essential); damages heart, brain, kidneys, eyes |
| Hypotension | Low BP; shock, haemorrhage, postural hypotension (dizziness on standing) |
| Atherosclerosis | Fatty plaques in artery walls narrow the lumen; underlies most coronary disease and stroke |
| Angina pectoris | Chest pain from temporary myocardial ischaemia, often on exertion; relieved by rest and nitrates |
| Myocardial infarction | Death of heart muscle from blocked coronary artery (usually a clot on a plaque); severe chest pain, ECG changes, raised cardiac enzymes (troponins) |
| Congestive heart failure | Heart cannot pump enough to meet needs; breathlessness, oedema, fatigue; left- or right-sided |
| Arrhythmias | Abnormal rate or rhythm: atrial fibrillation, ventricular fibrillation (fatal unless defibrillated), heart block (AV conduction delayed or blocked), tachy- and bradyarrhythmias |
| Valvular disease | Stenosis (narrowed valve) or regurgitation (leaky valve); rheumatic heart disease often damages the mitral valve; causes murmurs |
| Congenital heart disease | Septal defects, patent ductus arteriosus, tetralogy of Fallot |
| Pericarditis, endocarditis, cardiomyopathy | Inflammation or disease of the coverings, lining or muscle |

## Remember

- Heart in the mediastinum, apex in the left fifth intercostal space; pericardium fibrous + serous.
- Tricuspid on the right, mitral on the left; chordae tendineae + papillary muscles stop valve eversion.
- Coronary arteries arise from the aortic sinuses; venous blood returns by the coronary sinus to the RA.
- Arteries: thick media, no valves; veins: thin wall, valves, blood reservoir; capillaries: one cell thick.
- Conduction: SA node → AV node (0.1 s delay) → bundle of His → bundle branches → Purkinje fibres.
- Sympathetic β1 raises rate and force; vagus (M2) slows the heart.
- CO = SV × HR ≈ 5 L/min; Frank–Starling law; EF about 55–65%.
- Cardiac cycle 0.8 s: ventricular systole 0.3 s, diastole 0.5 s; S1 = AV valves close, S2 = semilunar valves close.
- BP = CO × TPR; baroreceptors in carotid sinus and aortic arch; RAAS raises BP.
- ECG: P = atrial depolarisation, QRS = ventricular depolarisation, T = ventricular repolarisation; PR 0.12–0.20 s.

# How to use this guide

Human Anatomy and Physiology I is a "draw and explain" subject. Examiners give a large share of the marks for a neat labelled diagram, a clear sequence of steps (for example, the clotting cascade or the steps of muscle contraction) and correct normal values. Study one unit at a time: read the notes, redraw every text diagram from memory on rough paper, learn the tables of normal values, then answer the question bank before checking the model answers. Finish each unit with its MCQs.

The five units follow the five units of the PCI syllabus exactly; no unit has been split or joined. Unit 2 is the longest because it holds three syllabus topics (skin, skeleton with skeletal muscle, and joints). The 15 practical guides cover the BP107P practical list.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ or very short answers | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

The question bank has long essays (10 marks), short answers (5 marks) and very short answers (2 marks, useful for class tests and sessionals).

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with a diagram and normal values.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from common patterns and syllabus weight, not a promise of what will be asked.

**Where the marks usually come from**

| Unit | Topics that keep coming back |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Body, cells, tissues | Structure of cell with organelles, transport across the membrane, homeostasis with feedback, classification of epithelial and connective tissue, cell junctions, forms of cell signalling |
| 2 Skin, skeleton, joints | Structure and functions of skin, classification of bones, skull and vertebral column, sliding filament theory, neuromuscular junction, classification of joints with synovial joint diagram |
| 3 Blood and lymph | Composition and functions of blood, haemopoiesis, haemoglobin, coagulation cascade, blood groups and Rh factor, anaemia, lymph node and lymphatic system |

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. Put a diagram in every long answer, even if the question does not ask for one: cell, epithelium, long bone, sarcomere, NMJ, synovial joint, blood cells, lymph node, ANS outflow, eye, ear, heart, conduction system, ECG. Label at least six parts; neat labels with straight lines score more than artistic shading.
2. Begin with a one-line definition, then a table or flow chart. Flow charts suit sequences: haemopoiesis, clotting cascade, muscle contraction, baroreceptor reflex, RAAS, hearing.
3. Quote standard normal values: RBC 4.5–5.5 million/mm³, WBC 4,000–11,000/mm³, platelets 1.5–4 lakh/mm³, Hb 14–18 g/dL (men), BP 120/80 mmHg, heart rate 72/min, cardiac output 5 L/min, cardiac cycle 0.8 s, PR interval 0.12–0.20 s.
4. Use comparison tables for "differentiate" questions: sympathetic vs parasympathetic, artery vs vein, skeletal vs cardiac vs smooth muscle, mitosis vs meiosis, intrinsic vs extrinsic pathway.
5. End with two or three applied points — a disorder or a drug that acts at that site (neostigmine at the NMJ, warfarin and vitamin K factors, ACE inhibitors and RAAS, atropine and heart rate). Pharmacy examiners look for this link.
6. Learn the counts that MCQs love: 206 bones, 22 skull bones, 33 vertebrae in a child and 26 in an adult, 31 pairs of spinal nerves, 12 pairs of cranial nerves, 13 clotting factors with no VI, 3 ear ossicles.
7. For the cranial nerves, practise writing the full table (number, name, type, origin, function) in under ten minutes.

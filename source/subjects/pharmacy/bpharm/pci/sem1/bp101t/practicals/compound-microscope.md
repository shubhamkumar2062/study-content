# Study of the compound microscope

**Aim:** To study the parts, working and care of a compound microscope and to focus a slide under low power, high power and oil immersion.

> Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Carry the microscope with both hands.

## Principle

A compound microscope uses two lens systems. The **objective** forms a real, inverted, magnified image of the object; the **eyepiece (ocular)** magnifies this image further to give a virtual, inverted, enlarged final image.

**Total magnification = magnification of objective × magnification of eyepiece.**

**Resolving power** is the ability to show two close points as separate. It improves with a higher numerical aperture (NA) of the objective and with shorter wavelength of light. Under the oil-immersion lens, a drop of immersion oil (cedarwood oil or synthetic oil with a refractive index close to glass, about 1.5) prevents light from bending away in air, so more light enters the objective and resolution improves.

## Requirements

Compound microscope, prepared permanent slides (e.g. stained blood smear), immersion oil, lens paper, xylene (for cleaning oil, used sparingly).

## Parts of the microscope

```text
              EYEPIECE (10x)
                 |
              Body tube
                 |
   Arm ---    Revolving nosepiece
   (limb)     /   |    \
          10x   40x   100x (oil)  OBJECTIVES
                 |
           ===== STAGE ===== (stage clips / mechanical stage)
              Condenser + iris diaphragm
                 |
           Mirror / built-in lamp
   Coarse adjustment knob, fine adjustment knob on the side
              BASE (foot)
```

| Part | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Base and arm | Support; arm is used to carry the instrument |
| Body tube | Holds eyepiece and objectives at the correct distance |
| Eyepiece | Magnifies the image from the objective (usually 10×) |
| Revolving nosepiece | Carries objectives; rotates to change them |
| Objectives | Low power 10×, high power 40×, oil immersion 100× (some also 4× scanning) |
| Stage and clips / mechanical stage | Hold and move the slide |
| Condenser | Focuses light on the object |
| Iris diaphragm | Controls the amount of light |
| Mirror or lamp | Light source (plane mirror with daylight, concave without condenser) |
| Coarse adjustment | Large up-down movement for rough focusing |
| Fine adjustment | Small movement for sharp focus |

## Procedure

1. Place the microscope on a firm table with the arm towards you. Clean lenses with lens paper only.
2. Turn the low-power (10×) objective into line. Adjust the mirror or lamp, open the iris diaphragm and raise the condenser until the field is evenly bright.
3. Place the slide on the stage, cover-slip up, and fix it with clips so the specimen is over the hole.
4. Watching from the side, lower the objective with the coarse knob until it is close to the slide.
5. Looking through the eyepiece, raise the objective slowly with the coarse knob until the specimen appears; sharpen with the fine knob.
6. Bring the area of interest to the centre. Rotate to high power (40×) and focus with the **fine knob only**; adjust light.
7. For oil immersion: raise the objective slightly, put one drop of oil on the slide, turn the 100× objective into place so it dips into the oil, and focus with the fine knob. Use full light (condenser up, diaphragm open).
8. After use, wipe oil off the objective and slide with lens paper (a trace of xylene if needed), lower the stage, return to low power and cover the microscope.

## Observations

| Objective | Eyepiece | Total magnification | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10× | 10× | 100× | Locating the specimen, tissue layers |
| 40× | 10× | 400× | Cell details, counting chambers |
| 100× (oil) | 10× | 1000× | Blood cells, bacteria |

## Result

The parts and working of the compound microscope were studied, and the slide was focused under low power (100×), high power (400×) and oil immersion (1000×).

## Precautions

1. Never focus downwards with the coarse knob while looking through the eyepiece; the objective may break the slide.
2. Use only the fine knob under high power and oil immersion.
3. Do not touch lenses with fingers; clean with lens paper.
4. Remove oil from the oil-immersion lens immediately after use.
5. Carry the microscope upright with one hand on the arm and one under the base.

## Viva questions

1. **How is total magnification calculated?** Objective magnification × eyepiece magnification.
2. **Why is oil used with the 100× objective?** Oil has a refractive index close to glass, so light is not lost by refraction and resolution improves.
3. **What is resolving power?** The ability to distinguish two close points as separate; it depends on numerical aperture and wavelength.
4. **What is the function of the condenser?** It concentrates light onto the specimen.
5. **What kind of image does a compound microscope form?** A magnified, virtual, inverted final image.
6. **Which knob is used under high power?** The fine adjustment knob only.

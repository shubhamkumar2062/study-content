# Total white blood cell (WBC) count

**Aim:** To determine the total number of white blood cells per mm³ of blood using the haemocytometer.

> Wear gloves; use a fresh sterile lancet; dispose of sharps safely.

## Principle

Blood is diluted 1 in 20 with **Turk's fluid**, which lyses red cells and stains white cell nuclei so that the leucocytes can be counted. WBCs in the four large corner squares (total volume 0.4 mm³) of the Neubauer chamber are counted and the number per mm³ of blood is calculated.

**Turk's fluid:** dilute glacial acetic acid (about 1.5 ml per 100 ml; lyses RBCs) with a little 1% gentian violet (about 1 ml per 100 ml; stains WBC nuclei) in distilled water.

## Requirements

Neubauer chamber with cover slip, WBC pipette (white bead, marks 0.5 and 11), Turk's fluid, microscope, sterile lancet, spirit swabs, cotton.

## Procedure

1. Clean the chamber and cover slip; place the cover slip in position.
2. Prick the cleaned fingertip; wipe off the first drop.
3. Draw blood into the WBC pipette exactly to the **0.5** mark; wipe the outside of the tip.
4. Draw Turk's fluid to the **11** mark (dilution 1 in 20).
5. Mix by rolling the pipette horizontally for 2–3 minutes.
6. Discard the first 2–3 drops; charge the chamber by touching the tip to the edge of the cover slip.
7. Wait 2–3 minutes for cells to settle.
8. Under low power (10×), count WBCs (dark-stained dots with visible nuclei) in the **four corner large squares**, following the upper-and-left border rule.

## Observations and calculation

| Square | Cells counted |
| --- | --- |
| Upper left | n1 |
| Upper right | n2 |
| Lower left | n3 |
| Lower right | n4 |
| Total (N) | n1 + n2 + n3 + n4 |

Volume counted = 4 squares × 1 mm² × 0.1 mm = 0.4 mm³.

**WBC per mm³ = N × dilution ÷ volume = N × 20 ÷ 0.4 = N × 50**

**Worked example:** cells counted = 38 + 42 + 35 + 40 = 155. WBC = 155 × 50 = **7,750 per mm³** (within normal range).

**Normal range:** 4,000–11,000 per mm³ in adults (higher in newborns). Above 11,000 = leucocytosis (bacterial infection, leukaemia); below 4,000 = leucopenia (typhoid, some viral infections, marrow suppression).

## Result

The total WBC count of the given blood sample is ______ per mm³.

## Precautions

1. Fill the pipette exactly to the marks without air bubbles.
2. Mix thoroughly so cells are evenly spread.
3. Do not overfill the chamber.
4. Count carefully using the border rule; count each square only once.
5. Clean the pipette immediately after use.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the function of acetic acid in Turk's fluid?** It lyses (destroys) red cells so they do not hide the WBCs.
2. **Why is gentian violet added?** It stains the nuclei of WBCs, making them easy to see.
3. **What is the dilution factor?** 20.
4. **What is the multiplication factor?** 50.
5. **What is leucocytosis?** A total WBC count above about 11,000 per mm³, as in acute bacterial infection.
6. **Why are nucleated RBCs (in some diseases) counted as WBCs?** They are not lysed and their nuclei stain; a correction is applied using the differential count.

# Determination of blood group

**Aim:** To determine the ABO blood group and Rh type of the given blood sample by the slide method.

> Wear gloves; use a fresh sterile lancet; discard slides and lancets safely.

## Principle

Red cells carry antigens A, B and D (Rh). When blood is mixed with a serum containing the matching antibody, the red cells clump (**agglutination**), visible as fine or coarse granules. Agglutination with anti-A means antigen A is present, with anti-B antigen B, and with anti-D the cells are Rh positive.

## Requirements

Anti-A (blue), anti-B (yellow) and anti-D antisera, clean glass slide or white tile marked A, B and D, sterile lancet, spirit swabs, separate toothpicks or applicator sticks, (normal saline if the blood is to be diluted).

## Procedure

1. Mark a clean slide with three areas: A, B and D.
2. Place one drop of anti-A, anti-B and anti-D in the respective areas.
3. Clean and prick the fingertip; put one small drop of blood next to each antiserum drop.
4. Mix each pair with a **separate** stick, spreading over a small area.
5. Rock the slide gently for about 2 minutes and look for clumping against a white background (anti-D may need slightly longer; read within the time stated by the kit maker).

## Observations

| Anti-A | Anti-B | Anti-D | Blood group |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| + | − | + | A positive |
| − | + | + | B positive |
| + | + | + | AB positive |
| − | − | + | O positive |
| + | − | − | A negative |
| − | + | − | B negative |
| + | + | − | AB negative |
| − | − | − | O negative |

(+ = agglutination; − = no agglutination, uniform red mixture)

## Result

## Precautions

1. Use a separate stick for each antiserum to avoid carrying antibodies across.
2. Do not use too much blood; a large excess of cells can hide weak agglutination.
3. Do not let the mixture dry; drying can look like clumping.
4. Check that antisera are within expiry and stored cold as directed.
5. Read the result within the stated time.

## Viva questions

1. **What is agglutination?** Clumping of red cells when their antigen meets the matching antibody.
2. **Which group is the universal donor and why?** O negative (red cells), because the cells carry no A, B or D antigens.
3. **Which group is the universal recipient?** AB positive, because the plasma has no anti-A or anti-B.
4. **What is the Rh factor?** The D antigen on red cells.
5. **Why is blood grouping important?** For safe transfusion, organ transplantation, and preventing Rh disease of the newborn; also used in paternity and forensic work.
6. **Is anti-D naturally present in Rh-negative people?** No; it forms only after exposure to Rh-positive cells.

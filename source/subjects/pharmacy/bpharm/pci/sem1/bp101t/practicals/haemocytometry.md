# Introduction to haemocytometry

**Aim:** To study the parts of the haemocytometer (improved Neubauer counting chamber and diluting pipettes) and the calculations used for blood cell counts.

> Wear gloves; treat all blood as potentially infectious and discard lancets in a sharps container.

## Principle

Blood has too many cells to count directly. A known volume of blood is **diluted** by a known amount with a fluid that preserves (or removes) particular cells, and the cells in a **known volume** of the diluted blood are counted in a chamber of fixed depth and ruled area. The count per mm³ of undiluted blood is then calculated:

**Cells per mm³ = (cells counted × dilution factor) ÷ volume counted (mm³)**

## Requirements

Haemocytometer set: improved Neubauer chamber with special thick cover slip, RBC pipette, WBC pipette, rubber suction tube; compound microscope; sterile lancet, spirit swabs, cotton.

| Area | Size | Volume (× 0.1 mm depth) | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One large square | 1 mm² | 0.1 mm³ | WBC (4 corner squares = 0.4 mm³) |

| One smallest square | 1/400 mm² | 1/4000 mm³ = 0.00025 mm³ | 80 smallest squares in 5 medium squares |

## Diluting pipettes

| Feature | RBC pipette | WBC pipette |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bead in bulb | Red | White |
| Marks | 0.5, 1, 101 | 0.5, 1, 11 |
| Volume of bulb | 100 parts | 10 parts |
| Blood drawn to | 0.5 | 0.5 |
| Diluting fluid to | 101 | 11 |
| Dilution | 0.5 in 100 = **1 in 200** | 0.5 in 10 = **1 in 20** |
| Diluting fluid | Hayem's fluid | Turk's fluid |

The fluid in the stem (below the bulb) does not mix and is discarded before charging the chamber, which is why only the bulb volume counts in the dilution.

## Procedure (general)

1. Clean and dry the chamber, cover slip and pipettes. Place the cover slip over the ruled area.
2. Clean the fingertip with spirit, let it dry, prick with a sterile lancet, wipe the first drop, and use the next free-flowing drop.
3. Suck blood exactly to the 0.5 mark; wipe the tip; then suck diluting fluid to the 101 (RBC) or 11 (WBC) mark.
4. Hold the pipette horizontally, close both ends and shake for 2–3 minutes to mix.
5. Discard the first few drops (stem fluid). Touch the tip to the edge of the cover slip so the fluid fills the chamber by capillary action without overflow or bubbles.
6. Allow cells to settle for 2–3 minutes, then count under the microscope (low power for WBCs, high power for RBCs).
7. **Counting rule:** count cells lying inside a square and those touching the **upper and left** borders; ignore those touching the lower and right borders, so no cell is counted twice.

## Calculations

**WBC count:** cells in 4 large corner squares = N.
WBC per mm³ = N × 20 ÷ 0.4 = **N × 50**.

**RBC count:** cells in 5 medium squares (80 smallest squares) = N.
RBC per mm³ = N × 200 ÷ 0.02 = **N × 10,000**.

## Result

The parts of the haemocytometer, the dilution factors (RBC 1:200, WBC 1:20) and the formulae for RBC (N × 10,000) and WBC (N × 50) counts were studied.

## Precautions

1. Use separate, clean, dry pipettes; never mix up RBC and WBC pipettes.
2. Fill exactly to the marks; air bubbles in the pipette make the result wrong.
3. Do not overfill the chamber; fluid must not run into the moats.
4. Clean pipettes after use with water, alcohol and ether (or as directed) so blood does not clot inside.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the depth of the Neubauer chamber?** 0.1 mm.
2. **What is the area of the smallest square?** 1/400 mm².
3. **Why does the RBC pipette have a red bead?** For identification, and the bead helps mix blood with the fluid.
4. **What is the dilution in the WBC pipette?** 1 in 20.
5. **Why are the first drops discarded?** They contain only diluting fluid from the stem.
6. **Why does the cover slip have to be special?** It is thick and optically flat so the depth stays exactly 0.1 mm.

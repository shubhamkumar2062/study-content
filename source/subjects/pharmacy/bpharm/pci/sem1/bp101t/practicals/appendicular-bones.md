# Identification of appendicular bones

**Aim:** To identify the bones of the upper and lower limbs and their girdles, and to determine the side (right or left) of long bones.

> Use the dry bones or models in your laboratory. Ask your teacher to confirm side determination the first time.

## Principle

The appendicular skeleton (126 bones) has the pectoral girdles and upper limbs (64) and the pelvic girdle and lower limbs (62). Long bones have a proximal end, a shaft and a distal end; the landmarks on the ends tell the bone and its side.

**Side determination:** hold the bone as it lies in your own body — upper end up, front facing forward (away from you), back facing you. The structures that lie laterally (such as the greater tubercle of the humerus, greater trochanter of the femur) are then on the bone's true lateral side. If the lateral feature is on your right, it is a right bone.

## Requirements

Clavicle, scapula, humerus, radius, ulna, articulated hand, hip bone, femur, patella, tibia, fibula, articulated foot (or models); record book.

## Procedure

1. Identify each bone from its general shape.
2. Locate proximal and distal ends, anterior and posterior surfaces, medial and lateral sides.
3. Name the important landmarks and determine the side of long bones.
4. Draw a labelled outline and write the identifying points.

## Observations

**Upper limb**

| Bone | Identifying features | Side clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clavicle | S-shaped; medial end rounded (sternal), lateral end flat (acromial) | Medial two-thirds convex forward; inferior surface rough |
| Scapula | Triangular flat bone; spine on the back ending in the acromion; coracoid process; glenoid cavity at the lateral angle | Glenoid cavity is lateral; spine is on the posterior surface |
| Humerus | Rounded head, anatomical and surgical necks, greater and lesser tubercles with intertubercular groove, deltoid tuberosity; distal capitulum, trochlea, olecranon fossa behind | Head faces medially; greater tubercle lateral; olecranon fossa posterior |
| Ulna | Upper end like a hook: olecranon and coronoid processes with the trochlear notch; small distal head with styloid process | Trochlear notch faces forward; radial notch is lateral |
| Radius | Small disc-shaped upper head; radial tuberosity; wide distal end with styloid process laterally | Styloid process lateral (thumb side) |
| Carpals (8) | Two rows: scaphoid, lunate, triquetrum, pisiform; trapezium, trapezoid, capitate, hamate (hook) | — |
| Metacarpals (5), phalanges (14) | Palm bones; thumb has two phalanges, fingers three | — |

**Lower limb**

| Bone | Identifying features | Side clue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hip bone | Large irregular bone of ilium, ischium and pubis fused at the cup-shaped **acetabulum**; obturator foramen below; iliac crest above | Acetabulum faces laterally; obturator foramen lies below and in front of it |
| Femur | Longest bone; rounded head on an angled neck, greater and lesser trochanters, linea aspera behind the shaft; medial and lateral condyles | Head medial; greater trochanter lateral; linea aspera posterior |
| Patella | Triangular sesamoid bone with apex down; lateral articular facet larger | Larger lateral facet |
| Tibia | Large medial leg bone; flat upper condyles, tibial tuberosity in front, sharp anterior border (shin), **medial malleolus** at the lower end | Medial malleolus medial; tuberosity anterior |
| Fibula | Thin lateral bone; head at the top; **lateral malleolus** at the lower end with a pit (malleolar fossa) behind | Malleolar fossa posterior and medial |
| Tarsals (7) | Talus, calcaneus (heel, largest), navicular, cuboid, three cuneiforms | — |
| Metatarsals (5), phalanges (14) | Foot bones; great toe has two phalanges | — |

## Result

The bones of the appendicular skeleton were identified, their landmarks were noted, and the side of the long bones was determined.

## Precautions

1. Hold bones in the anatomical position before deciding the side.
2. Handle bones carefully over the table.
3. Do not write on the bones.

## Viva questions

1. **How many bones are in each upper limb?** 30 (60 in both).
2. **Name the three bones that form the hip bone.** Ilium, ischium and pubis.
3. **Which is the longest bone of the body?** Femur.
4. **Which bone forms the point of the elbow?** Olecranon process of the ulna.
5. **Which is the largest tarsal bone?** Calcaneus.
6. **Which is the commonly fractured bone of the shoulder girdle?** Clavicle.
7. **What is the acetabulum?** The socket on the hip bone that receives the head of the femur.

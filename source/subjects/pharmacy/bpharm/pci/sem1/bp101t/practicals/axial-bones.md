# Identification of axial bones

**Aim:** To identify the bones of the axial skeleton (skull, vertebrae, ribs, sternum, hyoid) and to describe their important features.

> Use the dry bones or models in your laboratory. Handle skulls gently; thin bones such as the ethmoid break easily.

## Principle

The axial skeleton (80 bones) forms the central axis of the body: skull 22, hyoid 1, ear ossicles 6, vertebral column 26 and thoracic cage 25 (sternum 1 + ribs 24). Each bone is identified by its shape, size and landmarks (processes, foramina, facets).

## Requirements

Articulated skull and disarticulated skull bones (or models), typical and special vertebrae, sacrum, coccyx, sternum, typical and atypical ribs, hyoid bone; record book.

## Procedure

1. Examine each specimen; note its general shape and size.
2. Identify the region from its features (use the tables below).
3. Locate and name the important landmarks.
4. Draw a labelled outline of each bone and write its identifying points and functions.

## Observations

**Skull**

| Bone | Identifying features |
| --- | --- |
| Frontal | Forehead; supraorbital margins; frontal sinuses inside |
| Parietal (2) | Square, curved plates forming roof and sides; meet at the sagittal suture |
| Temporal (2) | External acoustic meatus; mastoid process behind it; styloid process; zygomatic process; houses middle and inner ear |
| Occipital | Back and base; foramen magnum; occipital condyles articulate with the atlas |
| Sphenoid | Bat or butterfly shape across the base; sella turcica holding the pituitary |
| Ethmoid | Light, between the orbits; cribriform plate with many small holes for olfactory nerves; crista galli |
| Maxilla (2) | Upper jaw with upper teeth; part of hard palate; large maxillary sinus |
| Mandible | U-shaped lower jaw; body, ramus, condylar and coronoid processes, mental foramen; only movable skull bone |
| Zygomatic, nasal, lacrimal, palatine, vomer, inferior conchae | Cheek, bridge of nose, medial orbit wall, back of hard palate, lower nasal septum, side walls of nose |

**Vertebrae**

| Specimen | Identifying features |
| --- | --- |
| Typical cervical | Small oval body, large triangular vertebral foramen, **foramen transversarium** in each transverse process, bifid spine |
| Atlas (C1) | Ring-like; no body and no spine; large superior facets for occipital condyles |
| Axis (C2) | **Dens** (odontoid process) projecting upward from the body |
| C7 (vertebra prominens) | Long, non-bifid spine felt at the base of the neck |
| Thoracic | Heart-shaped body; **costal facets** on body and transverse processes for ribs; long spine pointing downward; round foramen |
| Lumbar | Large kidney-shaped body; short, thick, horizontal spine; no costal facets or transverse foramina |
| Sacrum | Triangular, five fused vertebrae; four pairs of sacral foramina; sacral promontory; ear-shaped auricular surface for the hip bone |
| Coccyx | Small triangular bone of usually four fused pieces |

**Thoracic cage and hyoid**

| Specimen | Identifying features |
| --- | --- |
| Sternum | Flat dagger-shaped bone: manubrium (jugular notch, clavicular notches), body (notches for costal cartilages), xiphoid process |
| Typical rib (3rd to 9th) | Curved flat bone with head (two facets), neck, tubercle, angle and shaft; costal groove on the inner lower border for intercostal vessels and nerve |
| First rib | Shortest, broadest, most curved; flat surfaces face up and down; groove for subclavian vessels |
| Floating ribs (11, 12) | Short; no tubercle; free anterior end |
| Hyoid | U-shaped; body with greater and lesser horns; does not articulate with other bones |

## Result

The bones of the axial skeleton were identified and their important features recorded.

## Precautions

1. Handle bones with both hands over the table; never hold a skull by the eye sockets.
2. Do not mark bones with pen or pencil.
3. Return bones to the correct box.

## Viva questions

1. **How many bones form the axial skeleton?** 80.
2. **Which bone has the sella turcica?** Sphenoid; it lodges the pituitary gland.
3. **What passes through the foramen magnum?** The spinal cord (lower end of medulla) with its coverings and vertebral arteries.
4. **How is a cervical vertebra identified?** By the foramen transversarium in its transverse process.
5. **Which vertebra has no body?** Atlas (C1).
6. **Which ribs are called floating ribs?** The 11th and 12th, which have no anterior attachment.
7. **Which bone does not articulate with any other bone?** Hyoid.

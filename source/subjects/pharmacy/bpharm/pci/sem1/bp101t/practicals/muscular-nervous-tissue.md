# Microscopic study of muscular and nervous tissue

**Aim:** To identify and draw permanent slides of skeletal, cardiac and smooth muscle and of nervous tissue.

> Use the slides provided in your laboratory; labels and staining may vary.

## Principle

Muscle types differ in fibre shape, number and position of nuclei, striations and special junctions. Nervous tissue is recognised by neurons with cell bodies and processes, and by myelinated fibres or nerve bundles.

## Requirements

Compound microscope; permanent slides of skeletal muscle (L.S. and T.S.), cardiac muscle, smooth muscle, multipolar neurons (spinal cord smear or section), peripheral nerve (T.S.); drawing sheets.

## Procedure

1. Scan each slide under low power, then study details under high power.
2. Note shape of fibres, nuclei, striations, branching and special structures.
3. Draw and label a representative field.

## Observations

| Slide | Identifying features |
| --- | --- |
| Skeletal muscle (L.S.) | Long unbranched cylindrical fibres; many oval nuclei at the periphery under the sarcolemma; clear alternate dark (A) and light (I) cross-striations |
| Skeletal muscle (T.S.) | Polygonal fibre profiles grouped into fascicles by perimysium; peripheral nuclei |
| Cardiac muscle | Branched fibres joined end to end; one or two central nuclei; faint striations; dark transverse **intercalated discs** |
| Smooth muscle | Spindle-shaped fibres with tapering ends; single central rod-shaped nucleus; no striations; packed in sheets |
| Multipolar neuron | Large cell body with a prominent nucleus and nucleolus; Nissl granules in cytoplasm; several dendrites and one axon |
| Peripheral nerve (T.S.) | Bundles (fascicles) of nerve fibres surrounded by perineurium, whole nerve by epineurium; each fibre with a central axon and pale myelin ring |

**Comparison**

| Feature | Skeletal | Cardiac | Smooth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shape | Cylindrical | Branched | Spindle |
| Nuclei | Many, peripheral | One or two, central | One, central |
| Striations | Present | Present | Absent |
| Intercalated discs | Absent | Present | Absent |
| Control | Voluntary | Involuntary | Involuntary |

## Result

Skeletal, cardiac and smooth muscle and nervous tissue were observed and identified by their characteristic features, and labelled diagrams were drawn.

## Precautions

1. Focus under low power first.
2. Adjust the light to see striations clearly (reduce light with the diaphragm).
3. Draw neatly with labels.

## Viva questions

1. **What are intercalated discs?** Specialised junctions (desmosomes and gap junctions) joining cardiac muscle cells, allowing the heart to contract as one unit.
2. **Why is skeletal muscle striated?** Regular arrangement of actin and myosin filaments in sarcomeres produces light and dark bands.
3. **What are Nissl granules?** Clumps of rough ER and ribosomes in the neuron cell body, sites of protein synthesis.
4. **What forms myelin in peripheral nerves?** Schwann cells.
5. **Which muscle has a single central nucleus and no striations?** Smooth muscle.

# Determination of erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR)

**Aim:** To determine the erythrocyte sedimentation rate of the given blood sample by the Westergren method.

> Venous blood is collected by a trained person. Wear gloves and handle blood safely.

## Principle

When anticoagulated blood stands in a vertical tube, red cells settle because they are heavier than plasma. The **distance (mm) the top of the red cell column falls in one hour** is the ESR. Settling is faster when red cells stack into **rouleaux**, which happens when plasma fibrinogen and globulins rise (inflammation, infection, pregnancy). ESR is not specific for any disease but shows that inflammation or tissue damage is present and helps follow its course.

## Requirements

Westergren tube (200 mm graduated, 2.5 mm bore) and stand, 3.8% sodium citrate solution, syringe and needle (or vacuum tube), small test tube, timer.

## Procedure

1. Take 0.4 ml of 3.8% sodium citrate in a test tube and add 1.6 ml of venous blood (ratio 1 part citrate : 4 parts blood); mix gently by inversion.
2. Draw the mixed blood into the Westergren tube exactly to the **0 mark** without bubbles.
3. Fix the tube **exactly vertical** in the stand, away from vibration, heat and sunlight. Start the timer.
4. After exactly **one hour**, read the height of the clear plasma column above the red cells in millimetres.

## Observations

| Time | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| Start | 0 mm |
| After 1 hour | ____ mm |

**Worked example:** the top of the red cell column has fallen to the 12 mark after one hour → ESR = **12 mm in the first hour** (normal for an adult man).

**Normal values (Westergren):** about 0–15 mm in 1 hour for men and 0–20 mm for women; slightly higher in older people.

| ESR raised | ESR lowered |
| --- | --- |
| Pregnancy, menstruation, anaemia, tuberculosis, rheumatoid arthritis, rheumatic fever, acute infections, multiple myeloma, some cancers | Polycythaemia, sickle cell disease, high plasma albumin, very low fibrinogen |

## Result

The ESR of the given blood sample is ______ mm in the first hour (Westergren).

## Precautions

1. Use the correct citrate-to-blood ratio (1 : 4).
2. Keep the tube exactly vertical; even a slight tilt speeds sedimentation.
3. Avoid bubbles, vibration and direct heat.
4. Do the test within a few hours of collection.
5. Read exactly at one hour.

## Viva questions

1. **What is rouleaux formation?** Stacking of red cells like a pile of coins, which makes them settle faster.
2. **Which plasma proteins raise the ESR?** Fibrinogen and globulins.
3. **Name the anticoagulant used in the Westergren method.** 3.8% sodium citrate.
4. **Is ESR specific for one disease?** No; it is a non-specific marker of inflammation.
5. **Name a condition with low ESR.** Polycythaemia.
6. **What other method is used?** The Wintrobe method (100 mm tube).

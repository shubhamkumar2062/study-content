# Microscopic study of epithelial and connective tissue

**Aim:** To identify and draw permanent slides of epithelial and connective tissues and to note their identifying features.

> Slides available differ between colleges. Draw what you see under low and high power, and label it.

## Principle

Tissues are identified by the shape and arrangement of cells, the number of layers, the amount of matrix and special features (cilia, goblet cells, fibres, lacunae). Haematoxylin stains nuclei blue-purple; eosin stains cytoplasm and fibres pink.

## Requirements

Compound microscope; permanent stained slides (simple squamous, simple cuboidal, simple columnar, ciliated pseudostratified columnar, stratified squamous, transitional epithelium; areolar tissue, adipose tissue, dense regular tissue such as tendon, hyaline cartilage, compact bone T.S.); drawing sheets and pencil.

## Procedure

1. Focus each slide under low power (10×) to see the general arrangement.
2. Move to high power (40×) to see cell shape, nuclei and special features.
3. Draw a labelled diagram of a representative area and write the identifying points.

## Observations: identifying features

| Slide | Identifying features | Location in body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple squamous epithelium | Single layer of thin flat cells with flattened central nuclei; looks like floor tiles in surface view | Alveoli, blood vessel lining, Bowman's capsule |
| Simple cuboidal epithelium | Single layer of square cells with round central nuclei, often around a lumen | Kidney tubules, thyroid follicles |
| Simple columnar epithelium | Single layer of tall cells with oval nuclei near the base; goblet cells; may have a brush border | Stomach, intestine |
| Pseudostratified ciliated columnar | Nuclei at different levels so it looks layered, but all cells touch the basement membrane; cilia and goblet cells | Trachea, bronchi |
| Stratified squamous epithelium | Many layers; basal cells cuboidal or columnar, surface cells flat; keratin layer on skin | Skin, oesophagus, mouth |
| Transitional epithelium | Several layers; large rounded "dome" or umbrella cells at the surface | Urinary bladder, ureter |
| Areolar tissue | Loose network of thin pink collagen and fine dark elastic fibres; scattered fibroblasts, mast cells, macrophages | Under the skin, around organs |
| Adipose tissue | Large empty-looking cells (fat dissolved in processing) with the nucleus pressed to one side ("signet ring") | Subcutaneous tissue |
| Dense regular (tendon) | Parallel wavy collagen bundles with rows of flattened fibroblast nuclei | Tendons, ligaments |
| Hyaline cartilage | Glassy homogeneous matrix; chondrocytes in lacunae, often in groups of two or four; perichondrium | Trachea, articular surfaces |
| Compact bone (T.S.) | Osteons: central Haversian canal surrounded by concentric lamellae; lacunae with canaliculi like a spider pattern | Shaft of long bones |

## Result

The given slides of epithelial and connective tissue were observed and identified by their characteristic features, and labelled diagrams were drawn.

## Precautions

1. Always locate the tissue under low power before switching to high power.
2. Handle slides by the edges; clean them before returning.
3. Draw only what is seen, with a pencil, and label on one side.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the basement membrane?** A thin non-cellular layer on which epithelium rests, joining it to connective tissue.
2. **Why is epithelium called avascular?** It has no blood vessels; it is nourished by diffusion from underlying connective tissue.
3. **What is pseudostratified epithelium?** A single layer that looks stratified because nuclei lie at different heights.
4. **Which cells make fibres in connective tissue?** Fibroblasts.
5. **What are lacunae?** Small spaces in the matrix of cartilage or bone that contain chondrocytes or osteocytes.
6. **Why do fat cells look empty in a slide?** The fat dissolves in the alcohol and xylene used to prepare the slide.

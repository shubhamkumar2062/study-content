# Determination of heart rate and pulse rate

**Aim:** To determine the heart rate by auscultation and the pulse rate by palpation of the radial artery, and to examine the radial pulse.

## Principle

Each ventricular contraction produces the heart sounds (heard at the apex with a stethoscope) and sends a pressure wave along the arteries, felt as the **pulse**. In a healthy person heart rate and pulse rate are equal. If some beats are too weak to reach the wrist (for example in atrial fibrillation), the pulse rate is lower than the heart rate; the difference is the **pulse deficit**.

## Requirements

Stethoscope, watch with a second hand (or stopwatch), couch.

## Procedure

**A. Pulse rate**

1. Let the subject sit or lie quietly for 5 minutes.
2. Place the tips of your index, middle and ring fingers on the radial artery at the front of the wrist, just lateral to the flexor tendons (thumb side). Do not use your thumb, which has its own pulse.
3. Press gently until the pulse is felt. Count the beats for a **full minute**.
4. Note the rhythm (regular/irregular), volume (strength) and condition of the vessel wall; compare both wrists.

**B. Heart rate**

1. Place the diaphragm of the stethoscope over the **apex beat** — left fifth intercostal space, just medial to the midclavicular line.
2. Count the heart sounds (one "lub-dub" = one beat) for one full minute.
3. If the rhythm is irregular, count heart rate and pulse rate at the same time (two observers) to find any pulse deficit.

**C. Effect of exercise (optional)**

1. Ask the subject to do mild exercise (e.g. 20 squats) and count the pulse again immediately and every minute until it returns to the resting value.

**Worked example:** pulse at rest 72/min, heart rate 72/min, regular, good volume; after exercise 110/min; back to 74/min after 3 minutes. Pulse deficit = 72 − 72 = 0.

**Normal values:** 60–100 per minute in adults (average about 72); higher in children and infants. Above 100 = tachycardia; below 60 = bradycardia (normal in trained athletes and during sleep).

**Factors increasing the rate:** exercise, emotion, fever, anaemia, pregnancy, hyperthyroidism, adrenaline. **Decreasing:** sleep, athletic training, raised intracranial pressure, hypothyroidism, β-blockers.

## Precautions

1. Subject should be relaxed; count after rest.
2. Count for a full minute, especially if the rhythm is irregular.
3. Do not palpate with the thumb.
4. Do not press too hard; you may stop the pulse.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the pulse?** The rhythmic expansion of an artery wall caused by the pressure wave of ventricular ejection.
2. **Where is the apex beat felt?** Left fifth intercostal space, just medial to the midclavicular line.
3. **What is pulse deficit?** Heart rate minus pulse rate; seen in atrial fibrillation.
4. **Name other sites for feeling the pulse.** Carotid, brachial, femoral, popliteal, dorsalis pedis, temporal.
5. **What is sinus arrhythmia?** Normal increase in heart rate during inspiration and decrease in expiration, common in young people.
6. **Why is the heart rate low in athletes?** Higher vagal tone and larger stroke volume.

# Determination of clotting time

**Aim:** To determine the clotting time of the given subject's blood by the capillary tube method.

> Wear gloves; use a fresh sterile lancet; dispose of sharps and broken glass safely.

## Principle

**Clotting time (coagulation time)** is the time from the moment blood leaves the vessel until it forms a fibrin clot. It reflects the **clotting factors** of the intrinsic and common pathways. Blood is drawn into a fine glass capillary; pieces of the tube are broken off at intervals until fibrin threads are seen between the broken ends.

## Requirements

Plain glass capillary tubes (about 10 cm long, 1 mm bore), sterile lancet, spirit swabs, cotton, stopwatch.

## Procedure

1. Clean the fingertip with spirit and allow it to dry.
2. Make a fairly deep prick so that blood flows freely. **Start the stopwatch** when blood first appears.
3. Hold one end of the capillary tube to the drop and let blood fill most of the tube by capillary action (keep the other end slightly lower).
4. After about 2 minutes, break off a small piece (about 1 cm) from one end every **30 seconds**, separating the pieces slowly.
5. When a thin **fibrin thread** is seen stretching between the broken pieces, **stop the watch**.

## Observations and calculation

| Observation | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Time when blood appeared | 0 s |
| Time when fibrin thread first seen | t |
| Clotting time | t |

**Worked example:** breaks at 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5, 4.0 minutes; fibrin thread first seen at 4.0 minutes → clotting time = **4 minutes**.

**Normal value:** about 3–8 minutes by the capillary tube method (normal ranges differ with the method).

**Prolonged in:** haemophilia (factor VIII or IX deficiency), vitamin K deficiency, obstructive jaundice, severe liver disease, anticoagulant therapy (heparin). Clotting time is a rough screening test; laboratories use more precise tests such as prothrombin time and activated partial thromboplastin time.

## Result

## Precautions

1. Allow the spirit to dry before pricking.
2. Blood must flow freely; do not squeeze the finger.
3. Use a clean, dry capillary of uniform bore.
4. Break the tube gently and pull the pieces apart slowly so a thin thread is not missed.
5. Keep the room temperature normal; cold delays clotting.

## Viva questions

1. **Difference between bleeding time and clotting time?** Bleeding time tests platelets and vessels; clotting time tests clotting factors.
2. **In which disease is clotting time prolonged but bleeding time normal?** Haemophilia.
3. **Which vitamin is needed for the synthesis of clotting factors II, VII, IX and X?** Vitamin K.
4. **What is the normal clotting time by the capillary method?** About 3–8 minutes.
5. **Name an anticoagulant used in blood banks.** Sodium citrate (citrate-based solutions).

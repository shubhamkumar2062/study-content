# Determination of bleeding time

**Aim:** To determine the bleeding time of the given subject by Duke's filter paper method.

> Wear gloves; use a fresh sterile lancet for every subject; dispose of sharps safely.

## Principle

**Bleeding time** is the time from a standard skin puncture until bleeding stops on its own. It depends mainly on **platelet function** and **constriction of small vessels** (vascular spasm and platelet plug), not on clotting factors. In Duke's method, blood from a puncture is blotted on filter paper every 30 seconds until no more stain appears.

## Requirements

Sterile disposable lancet, spirit swabs, dry cotton, circular filter paper, stopwatch.

## Procedure

1. Clean the fingertip (or ear lobe) with spirit and let it dry completely.
2. Make a puncture about 3 mm deep with a sterile lancet, without squeezing. **Start the stopwatch** as blood appears.
3. Every **30 seconds**, gently touch the filter paper to the drop of blood without touching the skin, using a fresh area of the paper each time.
4. Continue until the paper no longer picks up any blood. **Stop the watch.**
5. Count the blood spots on the paper.

## Observations and calculation

Each spot represents 30 seconds.

**Bleeding time = number of spots × 30 seconds**

| Observation | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Number of blood spots | n |
| Bleeding time | n × 30 s |

**Worked example:** 5 spots → 5 × 30 = 150 s = **2 minutes 30 seconds**. The spots become smaller in size one after the other.

**Normal value:** about 1–5 minutes (Duke's method).

**Prolonged in:** thrombocytopenia (low platelets, e.g. purpura), platelet function defects, aspirin use, von Willebrand disease, scurvy (fragile vessels). It is usually **normal in haemophilia**, because platelets and vessels are normal.

## Result

## Precautions

1. Let the spirit dry before puncturing; spirit delays clotting and spreads blood.
2. Do not squeeze the finger.
3. Do not touch the wound with the filter paper; this disturbs the platelet plug.
4. Use a fresh part of the filter paper every 30 seconds.

## Viva questions

1. **What does bleeding time mainly test?** Platelet function and vascular response.
2. **Is bleeding time prolonged in haemophilia?** Usually no; clotting time is prolonged instead.
3. **Name a drug that prolongs bleeding time.** Aspirin (it reduces platelet thromboxane A2).
4. **What is the normal bleeding time?** About 1–5 minutes.
5. **Why should the finger not be squeezed?** Squeezing forces tissue fluid out and gives a wrong result.

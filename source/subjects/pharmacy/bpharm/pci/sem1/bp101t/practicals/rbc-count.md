# Total red blood cell (RBC) count

**Aim:** To determine the total number of red blood cells per mm³ of blood using the haemocytometer.

> Wear gloves; use a fresh sterile lancet; dispose of sharps safely.

## Principle

Blood is diluted 1 in 200 with **Hayem's fluid**, an isotonic fluid that keeps red cells intact and separate. RBCs in five medium squares (80 smallest squares; volume 0.02 mm³) of the central large square of the Neubauer chamber are counted and the number per mm³ is calculated.

**Hayem's fluid (per 100 ml distilled water):** sodium chloride 0.5 g (keeps the fluid isotonic), sodium sulphate 2.5 g (prevents clumping and rouleaux), mercuric chloride 0.25 g (preservative, prevents fungal growth).

## Requirements

Neubauer chamber with cover slip, RBC pipette (red bead, marks 0.5 and 101), Hayem's fluid, microscope, sterile lancet, spirit swabs, cotton.

## Procedure

1. Clean the chamber and fix the cover slip.
2. Prick the cleaned fingertip; wipe off the first drop.
3. Draw blood exactly to the **0.5** mark of the RBC pipette; wipe the tip.
4. Draw Hayem's fluid to the **101** mark (dilution 1 in 200).
5. Mix by rotating the pipette horizontally for 2–3 minutes.
6. Discard the first few drops and charge the chamber.
7. Wait 2–3 minutes for the cells to settle.
8. Under high power (40×), count RBCs in **five medium squares** of the central square — the four corner ones and the centre one — each having 16 smallest squares. Use the upper-and-left border rule.

## Observations and calculation

| Medium square | Cells counted |
| --- | --- |
| Upper left | n1 |
| Upper right | n2 |
| Centre | n3 |
| Lower left | n4 |
| Lower right | n5 |
| Total (N) | n1 + … + n5 |

Volume counted = 80 smallest squares × (1/400 mm²) × 0.1 mm = 0.02 mm³.

**RBC per mm³ = N × 200 ÷ 0.02 = N × 10,000**

**Worked example:** cells counted = 98 + 102 + 95 + 100 + 105 = 500. RBC = 500 × 10,000 = **5,000,000 (5.0 million) per mm³**.

**Normal range:** about 4.5–5.5 million/mm³ in adult men and 4.0–5.0 million/mm³ in women. A low count suggests anaemia; a high count, polycythaemia (high altitude, chronic lung disease).

## Result

The total RBC count of the given blood sample is ______ million per mm³.

## Precautions

1. Use a clean, dry RBC pipette and fill exactly to the marks.
2. Mix well; uneven mixing gives a falsely high or low count.
3. Charge the chamber in one smooth movement without bubbles or overflow.
4. Count under high power, using the border rule.
5. Clean the pipette immediately after use.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is the dilution higher for RBC than for WBC?** RBCs are about a thousand times more numerous, so they must be diluted more to be countable.
2. **Why must the diluting fluid be isotonic?** So the red cells neither swell and burst nor shrink.
3. **What is the multiplication factor for the RBC count?** 10,000.
4. **Name a physiological cause of a raised RBC count.** Living at high altitude (low oxygen stimulates erythropoietin).
5. **What is the life span of an RBC?** About 120 days.

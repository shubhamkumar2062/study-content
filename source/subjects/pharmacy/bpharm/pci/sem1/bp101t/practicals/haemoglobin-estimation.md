# Estimation of haemoglobin (Sahli's method)

**Aim:** To estimate the haemoglobin content of the given blood sample by Sahli's acid haematin method.

> Wear gloves; handle hydrochloric acid with care; use a fresh sterile lancet.

## Principle

Haemoglobin is converted to brown **acid haematin** by N/10 (0.1 N) hydrochloric acid. The brown solution is diluted with distilled water until its colour matches the standard brown glass rods of the comparator. The haemoglobin concentration in g/dL (g%) is read directly from the graduated tube.

## Requirements

Sahli's haemoglobinometer (comparator with standard brown glass rods, graduated haemoglobin tube marked in g/dL, haemoglobin pipette marked at 20 mm³ = 0.02 ml, glass stirrer), N/10 HCl, distilled water, dropper, sterile lancet, spirit swabs.

## Procedure

1. Fill the graduated tube with N/10 HCl up to the lowest mark (mark 2 g/dL, or 10% on the percentage scale).
2. Clean and prick the fingertip; wipe the first drop.
3. Suck blood into the haemoglobin pipette exactly to the **20 mm³** mark; wipe the outside of the tip.
4. Blow the blood into the bottom of the acid in the tube; rinse the pipette two or three times with the acid in the tube so no blood remains.
5. Mix with the stirrer and allow to stand for **10 minutes** for full conversion to acid haematin.
6. Add distilled water drop by drop, stirring after each addition, and compare the colour with the standard rods against the light.
7. Stop when the colour exactly matches. Read the lower meniscus in g/dL. Add one more drop and read again; take the reading where the match is best.

**Worked example:** colour matches at 14.2 g/dL and is slightly lighter at 14.4 g/dL → haemoglobin = **14.2 g/dL**.

**Normal values:** about 14–18 g/dL in adult men, 12–16 g/dL in adult women; higher in newborns. Low values suggest anaemia; high values, polycythaemia or dehydration.

**Useful calculation:** O2-carrying capacity ≈ Hb (g/dL) × 1.34 ml O2/g. For Hb 15 g/dL → about 20 ml O2 per 100 ml of blood.

## Precautions

1. Draw blood exactly to the 20 mm³ mark without air bubbles.
2. Wait the full 10 minutes before diluting; conversion to acid haematin is slow.
3. Add water drop by drop; once over-diluted, the test must be repeated.
4. Compare colours in daylight against the white background.
5. Rinse the pipette with acid so all blood enters the tube.

## Viva questions

1. **What compound is formed in Sahli's method?** Acid haematin (brown).
2. **Why wait for 10 minutes?** For complete conversion of haemoglobin to acid haematin.
3. **What is a source of error?** Subjective colour matching; acid haematin colour also develops slowly.
4. **Normal Hb in adult women?** About 12–16 g/dL.
5. **How much oxygen can 1 g of Hb carry?** About 1.34 ml.

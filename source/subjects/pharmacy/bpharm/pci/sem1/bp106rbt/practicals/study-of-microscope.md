# Study of the compound microscope

**Aim:** To study the parts, working and care of a compound microscope and to use it to observe a prepared slide.

> Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ from this guide.

## Principle

A **compound microscope** magnifies an object in two stages using two lens systems. The **objective lens** (near the object) forms a real, inverted, enlarged image inside the tube. The **eyepiece (ocular)** magnifies this image again, giving a virtual, enlarged image seen by the eye.

**Total magnification = magnification of objective × magnification of eyepiece.**

**Resolving power** is the ability to show two close points as separate. It, not magnification alone, decides how much detail is seen. Using **cedar wood oil** with the oil-immersion objective improves resolution because the oil has a refractive index close to that of glass, so less light is lost by bending.

## Requirements

Compound microscope, a prepared slide (for example a stained onion peel or a T.S. of stem), lens paper (tissue), cedar wood oil, xylene (for cleaning the oil-immersion lens).

## Parts of a compound microscope

```text
               eyepiece (ocular, 5x / 10x / 15x)
                  |
               body tube
                  |
  coarse adj. - revolving nosepiece -- objectives (10x, 45x, 100x oil)
  fine adj.        |
     |          [ stage with clips ]  <- slide placed here
   arm             |
     |          condenser + iris diaphragm
     |             |
   (pillar)     mirror (plane / concave) or built-in lamp
     |
   base (foot)
```

| Part | Function |
| --- | --- |
| **Base (foot)** | Horseshoe-shaped support |
| **Pillar and arm** | Hold the body; arm is used to carry the microscope |
| **Inclination joint** | Allows tilting for comfortable viewing |
| **Stage** | Flat platform with a central hole; holds the slide with clips; mechanical stage moves it smoothly |
| **Mirror** | Reflects light upward; **plane** side for strong light, **concave** side for weak light (when no condenser) |
| **Condenser** | Focuses light onto the object |
| **Iris diaphragm** | Controls the amount of light |
| **Body tube** | Holds the eyepiece at the top and nosepiece at the bottom |
| **Revolving nosepiece** | Holds the objectives; rotated to change them |
| **Objectives** | Low power (10×), high power (40× or 45×), oil immersion (100×) |
| **Eyepiece** | Usually 10×; magnifies the image formed by the objective |
| **Coarse adjustment** | Large knob; moves the tube or stage quickly for rough focus |
| **Fine adjustment** | Small knob; slow movement for sharp focus, especially at high power |

## Procedure

1. Carry the microscope with one hand on the arm and the other under the base; place it on the bench with the arm towards you.
2. Clean the lenses gently with lens paper only.
3. Bring the low-power (10×) objective in line with the tube (it clicks into place).
4. Look through the eyepiece and adjust the mirror (or lamp) and diaphragm until the field is evenly bright.
5. Place the slide on the stage, with the object over the hole, and fix it with clips.
6. Looking from the side, lower the objective with the coarse knob until it is about 0.5 cm above the slide (never let it touch).
7. Looking through the eyepiece, raise the objective slowly with the coarse knob until the object comes into view; sharpen with the fine knob.
8. Move the area of interest to the centre, then rotate the nosepiece to the high-power objective; refocus only with the fine knob.
9. For oil immersion: put a drop of cedar wood oil on the slide, swing in the 100× objective so it dips into the oil, and focus with the fine knob. Afterwards wipe the lens with lens paper moistened with a little xylene.
10. Draw what you see and note the magnification used.

## Observations

| Objective | Eyepiece | Total magnification | What was seen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10× | 10× | 100× | Outline of cells |
| 45× | 10× | 450× | Cell wall, nucleus |
| 100× (oil) | 10× | 1,000× | Fine detail |

## Result

The parts of the compound microscope were studied and a prepared slide was focused at low power, high power and (where available) oil immersion.

## Precautions

1. Never focus downward with the coarse knob while looking through the eyepiece; the lens may hit and break the slide.
2. Use only the fine adjustment at high power.
3. Clean lenses only with lens paper; never touch them with fingers.
4. Remove oil from the oil-immersion lens after use.
5. Keep the microscope covered and dry after use, with the low-power objective in place.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the total magnification with a 10× eyepiece and a 45× objective?** 450×.
2. **Why is cedar wood oil used with the 100× objective?** Its refractive index is close to glass, so light is not lost by bending and resolution improves.
3. **When is the concave mirror used?** In weak light, when there is no condenser.
4. **What is resolving power?** The ability to show two close points as separate.
5. **Which knob is used at high power?** The fine adjustment only.
6. **What kind of image does a compound microscope form?** Virtual, inverted and magnified.

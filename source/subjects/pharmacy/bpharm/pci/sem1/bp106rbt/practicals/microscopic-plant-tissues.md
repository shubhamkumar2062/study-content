# Microscopic study and identification of tissues of stem, root, leaf, seed, fruit and flower

**Aim:** To identify the tissues in transverse sections (T.S.) of dicot and monocot stem, root and leaf, and in sections or preparations of seed, fruit and flower parts.

> Use prepared permanent slides or sections you cut and stained yourself. Follow your lab manual and your teacher.

## Principle

Each plant organ has a typical arrangement of tissues. Recognising this arrangement under the microscope identifies the organ and tells whether it is from a **dicot** or a **monocot**. Simple tissues are **parenchyma** (thin-walled, living), **collenchyma** (corners thickened, living) and **sclerenchyma** (thick lignified walls, dead); complex tissues are **xylem** (water conduction) and **phloem** (food conduction).

## Requirements

Permanent slides or freshly stained sections: T.S. of sunflower and maize stem, T.S. of bean (or sunflower) and maize root, T.S. of a dicot leaf (mango) and a monocot leaf (grass or maize), T.S. of an anther, T.S. of an ovary, a fruit wall section, a section of a seed (maize grain), microscope.

## Procedure

1. Focus each slide under low power and identify the overall plan.
2. Switch to high power to study particular tissues.
3. Compare with the identifying features below.
4. Draw an outline diagram (low power) and a cellular detail (high power), with labels.

## Identifying features

| Section | Identifying features |
| --- | --- |
| **Dicot stem (sunflower)** | Epidermis with hairs; collenchyma hypodermis; cortex; endodermis; pericycle (sclerenchyma patches); **vascular bundles in a ring**, conjoint, collateral, **open**, endarch; large pith |
| **Monocot stem (maize)** | Sclerenchyma hypodermis; ground tissue not divided; **many scattered bundles**, closed, each with a sclerenchyma **bundle sheath**; water cavity; no pith |
| **Dicot root (bean)** | Epiblema with root hairs; wide cortex; endodermis with **Casparian strips**; pericycle; **2–4 radial xylem bundles**, exarch; pith small or absent |
| **Monocot root (maize)** | Similar layers; **more than 6 xylem bundles** (polyarch), exarch; **large pith** |
| **Dicot leaf (dorsiventral)** | Upper epidermis with cuticle; **palisade** layer below it; **spongy** mesophyll with air spaces; more stomata in lower epidermis; vascular bundle in midrib with xylem towards upper side |
| **Monocot leaf (isobilateral)** | Mesophyll not divided; stomata on both surfaces; **bulliform cells** in upper epidermis; parallel bundles with bundle sheath |
| **Anther (flower)** | Four pollen sacs (tetrasporangiate) with wall layers (epidermis, endothecium, middle layers, tapetum) and pollen grains |
| **Ovary (flower)** | Ovary wall, locules, ovules attached to placenta (placentation visible) |
| **Fruit wall** | Pericarp of epicarp, mesocarp and endocarp; in drupes the endocarp is stony (sclereids) |
| **Seed (maize grain)** | Fused fruit wall and seed coat; aleurone layer; starchy endosperm; scutellum; embryo with coleoptile and coleorhiza |

## Precautions

1. Start with low power to see the whole plan, then use high power.
2. Look at the arrangement of vascular bundles first; it is the quickest clue.
3. Draw proportionate, neatly labelled diagrams.
4. Handle permanent slides carefully and return them clean.

## Viva questions

1. **How do you identify a monocot stem?** Scattered, closed vascular bundles with bundle sheaths.
2. **What are Casparian strips?** Bands of suberin in the walls of root endodermis cells that control water entry.
3. **Why are root bundles called radial?** Xylem and phloem lie on different radii, alternating.
4. **What is exarch xylem?** Protoxylem lies towards the outside, as in roots.
5. **What are bulliform cells?** Large epidermal cells in grass leaves that let the leaf roll up when water is short.
6. **Name the nutritive layer of the anther wall.** Tapetum.

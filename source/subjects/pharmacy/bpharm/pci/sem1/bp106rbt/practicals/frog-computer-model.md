# Detailed study of frog using computer models

**Aim:** To study the external features and the internal organ systems of the frog using computer models, charts and preserved museum specimens, without dissection.

> Dissection of animals is no longer done for this practical; the frog is studied with software, models and charts as the syllabus directs. Follow your lab manual and your teacher.

## Principle

The frog is an **amphibian** (lives on land and in water) and a **vertebrate** (has a backbone). Its organ systems follow the same plan as in humans, so it is a simple model for learning body systems. The common Indian frog is the Indian bullfrog, *Hoplobatrachus tigerinus* (older books: *Rana tigrina*). Computer models let the student open each system on screen, rotate it and identify parts.

**Classification (outline):** Kingdom Animalia → Phylum Chordata → Class Amphibia → Order Anura (tailless amphibians).

## Requirements

Computer with frog anatomy software or a virtual dissection program, charts, models, preserved museum specimen.

## Procedure

1. Open the frog model and study the external features.
2. Open each system in turn: digestive, respiratory, circulatory, excretory, nervous, reproductive.
3. Identify the parts named below, rotate the model to see their positions, and note their functions.
4. Draw labelled diagrams of the external features, digestive system, heart and urinogenital system.

## Observations

**External features**

- Body divided into **head and trunk**; no neck and no tail in the adult.
- Skin smooth, moist and slippery due to mucus; can absorb water and exchange gases. Dorsal side olive-green with dark spots; ventral side pale yellow. Colour can change to match surroundings (camouflage).
- Head: mouth, a pair of **nostrils**, bulging **eyes** covered by a **nictitating membrane** in water, and a **tympanum** (eardrum) behind each eye.
- Forelimbs short with four digits; hindlimbs longer and stronger with five webbed digits for jumping and swimming.
- Male shows **vocal sacs** and a **copulatory pad** on the first digit of each forelimb; females have neither.

**Digestive system:** mouth → buccal cavity (bilobed tongue that can be flicked out to catch insects; teeth on upper jaw) → pharynx → short oesophagus → stomach → intestine → rectum → **cloaca** (common chamber for digestive, excretory and reproductive products). Liver with gall bladder; pancreas. Frog is carnivorous.

**Respiratory system:** breathes through the **skin** (cutaneous respiration, in water and during aestivation and hibernation), the **buccal cavity** and the **lungs** on land.

**Circulatory system:** closed system; **three-chambered heart** — two atria and one ventricle — with a **sinus venosus** joining the right atrium and a **conus arteriosus** leaving the ventricle. It has a **hepatic portal system** and a **renal portal system**. Lymphatic system present. RBCs are nucleated.

**Excretory system:** a pair of kidneys, ureters, cloaca and urinary bladder. The frog is **ureotelic** (excretes urea). In males the ureters also carry sperm (urinogenital ducts).

**Nervous system:** brain (forebrain with olfactory lobes and cerebral hemispheres; midbrain with optic lobes; hindbrain with cerebellum and medulla oblongata), spinal cord, **10 pairs of cranial nerves**. Sense organs: eyes, internal ears (hearing and balance), nasal epithelium, taste buds, touch receptors.

**Reproductive system:** sexes separate. Male: a pair of yellowish testes attached to the kidneys. Female: a pair of ovaries and oviducts opening into the cloaca. **Fertilisation is external** (in water); eggs hatch into **tadpoles**, which undergo **metamorphosis** into adult frogs.

**Other features:** cold-blooded (**poikilothermic**: body temperature follows the surroundings); escapes extreme heat by **aestivation** (summer sleep) and extreme cold by **hibernation** (winter sleep). Frogs eat insects and help control pests.

## Precautions

1. Identify each part from more than one view of the model.
2. Draw labelled diagrams in proportion.
3. Handle preserved specimens and their jars carefully.

## Viva questions

1. **How many chambers are in a frog's heart?** Three: two atria and one ventricle.
2. **What is the cloaca?** A common chamber into which digestive, excretory and reproductive tracts open.
3. **Name the modes of respiration in frog.** Cutaneous (skin), buccal and pulmonary (lungs).
4. **How can a male frog be recognised?** By vocal sacs and copulatory pads on the forelimbs.
5. **What are aestivation and hibernation?** Summer and winter sleep to escape extreme heat and cold.
6. **Why is the frog called poikilothermic?** Its body temperature changes with the surroundings.

# Identification of bones

**Aim:** To identify the bones of the human skeleton and study their main features.

> Use articulated and disarticulated human skeletons or models available in the laboratory. Follow your lab manual and your teacher.

## Principle

The adult human skeleton has **206 bones**. It is divided into the **axial skeleton** (80 bones along the central axis: skull, vertebral column, sternum, ribs) and the **appendicular skeleton** (126 bones: limbs and girdles). A bone is identified by its shape, size and special features (processes, holes, joint surfaces). Bones support the body, protect organs, allow movement with muscles, store calcium and phosphate, and their red marrow forms blood cells.

**Types by shape:** long (femur, humerus), short (carpals), flat (skull bones, sternum, ribs), irregular (vertebrae), sesamoid (patella).

## Requirements

Human skeleton or models, individual bones, charts.

## Procedure

1. Study the articulated skeleton to see where each bone lies.
2. Pick up each bone, note its shape and identifying features, and decide its name (and side for paired bones).
3. Draw it and label the features.

## Bones and identifying features

**Axial skeleton (80)**

| Bone | Number | Identifying features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Skull** | 22 (8 cranial + 14 facial) | Cranium encloses the brain; lower jaw (**mandible**) is the only movable skull bone; **foramen magnum** at the base for the spinal cord; joins the vertebral column through two **occipital condyles** (dicondylic) |
| Ear ossicles | 6 (3 per ear) | Malleus, incus, stapes (stapes is the smallest bone) |
| Hyoid | 1 | U-shaped bone in the neck, at the base of the buccal cavity |
| **Vertebral column** | 26 | Cervical 7, thoracic 12, lumbar 5, sacrum 1 (5 fused), coccyx 1 (4 fused) |
| Atlas (1st cervical) | — | Ring-shaped, no body; articulates with occipital condyles |
| Axis (2nd cervical) | — | Tooth-like **odontoid process (dens)** projecting upward |
| Thoracic vertebrae | — | Facets for ribs; long, downward-pointing spines |
| Lumbar vertebrae | — | Large, heavy bodies; short thick spines |
| **Sternum** | 1 | Flat breast bone in the middle of the chest |
| **Ribs** | 12 pairs | Pairs 1–7 true ribs (joined to sternum directly); 8–10 false (joined via 7th rib cartilage, vertebrochondral); 11–12 floating (free in front) |

**Appendicular skeleton (126)**

| Bone | Number per side | Identifying features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Clavicle** (collar bone) | 1 | Long, slender, S-shaped |
| **Scapula** (shoulder blade) | 1 | Flat triangular bone with a ridge (spine) ending in the **acromion**; shallow **glenoid cavity** for the humerus |
| **Humerus** | 1 | Upper-arm bone; rounded head; greater and lesser tubercles; deltoid tuberosity; lower end with trochlea, capitulum and olecranon fossa |
| **Radius** and **ulna** | 1 each | Forearm; ulna has the **olecranon process** (elbow tip); radius lies on the thumb side |
| Carpals | 8 | Small wrist bones |
| Metacarpals | 5 | Palm |
| Phalanges | 14 | Fingers (thumb has 2, others 3) |
| **Pelvic girdle (coxal / hip bone)** | 1 | Formed by fusion of **ilium, ischium and pubis**; cup-shaped **acetabulum** for the femur head |
| **Femur** | 1 | **Longest bone**; rounded head, neck, greater and lesser trochanters; lower end with two condyles |
| **Patella** | 1 | Kneecap; sesamoid bone |
| **Tibia** and **fibula** | 1 each | Leg; tibia large and on the inner (shin) side; fibula slender, outer side |
| Tarsals | 7 | Ankle (calcaneus forms the heel) |
| Metatarsals | 5 | Sole |
| Phalanges | 14 | Toes |

**Count check:** each limb has 30 bones; 4 limbs = 120; girdles (2 clavicles + 2 scapulae + 2 hip bones) = 6; total appendicular 126; with axial 80 → **206**.

## Precautions

1. Handle bones and models gently.
2. Hold a long bone in its natural position (head up) to decide its side.
3. Draw and label clearly.

## Viva questions

1. **How many bones are in the adult human body?** 206.
2. **Which is the longest bone?** Femur.
3. **Which is the smallest bone?** Stapes, in the middle ear.
4. **What are floating ribs?** The 11th and 12th pairs, not joined to the sternum in front.
5. **Which bones form the hip bone?** Ilium, ischium and pubis.
6. **What is the odontoid process?** A tooth-like projection of the axis vertebra around which the atlas rotates.

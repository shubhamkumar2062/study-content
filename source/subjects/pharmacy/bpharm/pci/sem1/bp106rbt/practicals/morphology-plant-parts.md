# Study of stem, root, leaf, seed, fruit, flower and their modifications

**Aim:** To study the external features (morphology) of root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit and seed of flowering plants, and to identify their modifications with examples and families.

> Specimens vary with season and region; use whatever your laboratory provides. Follow your lab manual and your teacher.

## Principle

**Morphology** is the study of external form. Each plant part has a basic plan, but many are **modified** (changed in form) to do extra jobs such as storage, support, climbing or protection. A specimen is identified by looking for the basic features that remain: a stem has nodes, internodes and buds; a root has no nodes or buds; a leaf arises at a node and has a bud in its axil.

## Requirements

Fresh or preserved specimens (listed below), hand lens, forceps, needle, blade, notebook.

## Procedure

1. Observe each specimen with the naked eye and a hand lens.
2. Note the basic features that show which organ it is (nodes, buds, scale leaves, root hairs).
3. Identify the modification and its function.
4. Cut flowers, fruits and seeds where needed (L.S. of flower, T.S. of ovary, opened seed).
5. Draw a labelled diagram and write the identifying features.

## Specimens and identifying features

**Roots**

| Specimen (family) | Modification | Identifying features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carrot (Apiaceae) | Conical storage root | Swollen tap root tapering downward; no nodes or buds |
| Radish (Brassicaceae) | Fusiform | Swollen in the middle, tapering at both ends |
| Turnip (Brassicaceae), beetroot (Amaranthaceae) | Napiform | Very swollen at top, top-shaped |
| Sweet potato (Convolvulaceae) | Tuberous adventitious root | Irregular swollen roots, no buds |
| Banyan (Moraceae) | Prop roots | Pillar-like roots hanging from branches |
| Maize (Poaceae) | Stilt roots | Roots from lower nodes entering soil obliquely |

**Stems**

| Specimen (family) | Modification | Identifying features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Potato (Solanaceae) | Stem tuber | "Eyes" (buds) in axils of scale leaves at nodes |
| Ginger (Zingiberaceae) | Rhizome | Horizontal, nodes and internodes, scale leaves, buds |
| *Colocasia* (Araceae) | Corm | Rounded, vertical, with nodes and buds |
| Onion (Amaryllidaceae) | Bulb | Reduced disc-like stem with fleshy scale leaves and adventitious roots |
| *Opuntia* (Cactaceae) | Phylloclade | Flat green stem; leaves reduced to spines |
| Grapevine (Vitaceae) | Stem tendril | Coiled tendril from a bud |
| *Citrus* (Rutaceae), *Bougainvillea* | Thorns | Hard pointed structures arising from axillary buds |

**Leaves**

| Specimen | Feature studied |
| --- | --- |
| Mango or peepal | Simple leaf, reticulate venation |
| Grass, banana | Parallel venation |
| Neem (Meliaceae) | Pinnately compound leaf |
| Pea (Fabaceae) | Leaflets modified into tendrils |
| *Opuntia* | Leaves modified into spines |
| *Nepenthes* (if available) | Leaf modified into a pitcher |
| Phyllotaxy: China rose (alternate), *Calotropis* (opposite), *Alstonia* (whorled) | Arrangement of leaves |

**Flower** (for example China rose, *Hibiscus rosa-sinensis*, Malvaceae, or mustard, Brassicaceae): note pedicel, thalamus, calyx, corolla, androecium, gynoecium; symmetry; aestivation; position of ovary; cut the ovary transversely to see placentation; write the floral formula.

**Inflorescence:** mustard (racemose), sunflower (capitulum, Asteraceae), coriander (umbel, Apiaceae), tulsi (verticillaster, Lamiaceae).

**Fruits:** tomato (berry), mango (drupe), orange (hesperidium), apple (pome), pea (legume), mustard (siliqua), cotton (capsule), wheat (caryopsis), custard apple (aggregate), jackfruit (multiple).

**Seeds:** gram or bean (dicot: two cotyledons, hilum, micropyle, no endosperm when mature); maize (monocot: one cotyledon/scutellum, large endosperm, coleoptile, coleorhiza). Soak seeds overnight before opening them.

## Result

## Precautions

1. Look for nodes, buds and scale leaves before deciding whether a part is a stem or root.
2. Use fresh specimens where possible; soak seeds before opening.
3. Cut flowers and ovaries carefully with a sharp blade.
4. Draw neat, labelled diagrams.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is potato a stem and sweet potato a root?** Potato has nodes with buds (eyes); sweet potato has none.
2. **Name a fusiform root.** Radish.
3. **What is a phylloclade?** A flattened green stem that does photosynthesis, as in *Opuntia*.
4. **What type of placentation is seen in mustard?** Parietal.
5. **Why is apple a false fruit?** Its edible part develops from the thalamus, not the ovary.
6. **How does a maize grain differ from a gram seed?** Maize has one cotyledon and a large endosperm; gram has two cotyledons and no endosperm.

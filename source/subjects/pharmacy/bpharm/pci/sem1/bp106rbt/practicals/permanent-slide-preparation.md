# Permanent slide preparation

**Aim:** To prepare a permanent, double-stained slide of a plant section (for example T.S. of a dicot stem).

> Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ from this guide. Alcohol and xylene are flammable; work away from flames in a ventilated area.

## Principle

A temporary mount in water or glycerine dries out or decays in days. A **permanent slide** lasts for years. To make one, the section is:

1. **Stained** (safranin for lignified tissue, fast green for cellulose tissue).
2. **Dehydrated** — all water is removed gradually through a series of increasing alcohol strengths (30% → 50% → 70% → 90% → absolute alcohol). Sudden transfer would shrink and distort the cells, so the change is made in steps.
3. **Cleared** — placed in **xylene** (or clove oil), which makes the tissue transparent and is miscible with the mountant. Water left behind makes the section milky in xylene.
4. **Mounted** in a resinous medium such as **DPX** or **Canada balsam**, which hardens and seals the coverslip.

## Requirements

Thin sections, watch glasses, brush, safranin (in 50% alcohol or water), fast green (in alcohol or clove oil), alcohol grades 30%, 50%, 70%, 90% and absolute alcohol, xylene, DPX or Canada balsam, slides, coverslips, labels, slide tray.

## Procedure

```text
 Water -> safranin (5 min) -> wash
   -> 30% -> 50% -> 70% alcohol (about 2 min each)
   -> 90% alcohol -> fast green (a few seconds)
   -> absolute alcohol (2 changes)
   -> xylene (clearing)
   -> mount in DPX / Canada balsam -> label -> dry flat
```

1. Select thin, complete sections and place them in water.
2. Stain in safranin for about 5 minutes; wash off extra stain.
3. Pass through 30%, 50% and 70% alcohol, about 2 minutes each.
4. Transfer to 90% alcohol, then dip in fast green for a few seconds.
5. Transfer to absolute alcohol, two changes, to complete dehydration.
6. Clear in xylene for a few minutes. If the section looks milky, water is still present: return it to absolute alcohol and repeat.
7. Place a drop of DPX or Canada balsam at the centre of a clean slide, put the section in it, and lower the coverslip slowly at an angle.
8. Label the slide (name of specimen, stain, date) and keep it flat to dry.
9. Observe under the microscope after drying.

## Observations

Draw the section and label tissues. Note the colours: lignified xylem and sclerenchyma red; parenchyma, phloem and collenchyma green.

## Result

A permanent double-stained slide of ______ was prepared.

## Precautions

1. Dehydrate gradually; skipping grades distorts cells.
2. Dehydration must be complete before xylene, or the section turns milky.
3. Fast green stains quickly; keep the time short.
4. Avoid air bubbles under the coverslip.
5. Keep xylene and alcohol away from flames; use gloves.
6. Store the slide flat until the mountant hardens.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is dehydration done through grades of alcohol?** Sudden removal of water shrinks and distorts the cells.
2. **What is clearing?** Making the tissue transparent with xylene or clove oil, which also mixes with the mountant.
3. **Why does a section turn milky in xylene?** Water is still present (incomplete dehydration).
4. **Name two permanent mountants.** DPX and Canada balsam.
5. **Difference between temporary and permanent slides?** Temporary (water or glycerine) lasts a short time; permanent (dehydrated, cleared, mounted in resin) lasts for years.
6. **Why label a slide?** To record specimen, stain and date for later identification.

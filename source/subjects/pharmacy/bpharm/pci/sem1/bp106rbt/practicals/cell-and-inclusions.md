# Study of the cell and its inclusions

**Aim:** To study the structure of plant and animal cells (onion peel and human cheek cells) and to identify cell inclusions such as starch grains, aleurone grains and calcium oxalate crystals.

> Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ from this guide. Use a new, sterile toothpick or spatula for cheek cells and discard it safely.

## Principle

A **cell** is the basic unit of life. Plant cells have a cellulose **cell wall**, a large **vacuole** and (in green parts) chloroplasts; animal cells have only a **cell membrane**. Stains make parts visible: iodine or safranin for onion peel, methylene blue for cheek cells.

**Cell inclusions** are non-living substances stored in cells: food reserves and waste products. Identifying them is important in pharmacognosy, where powdered drugs are recognised by their starch grains and crystals.

| Inclusion | Test or appearance | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Starch grains** | Blue-black with iodine; show a **hilum** (point) and concentric layers (**striations**) | Potato (eccentric hilum), rice (small polygonal), maize, wheat |
| **Aleurone grains** (protein) | Yellow to brown with iodine | Castor seed, aleurone layer of maize |
| **Calcium oxalate crystals** | Insoluble in acetic acid, soluble in HCl without effervescence; seen as prisms, needles (**raphides**), clusters (**rosettes**) | Raphides in *Colocasia*; prisms and rosettes in many leaves and barks |
| **Calcium carbonate (cystoliths)** | Dissolve in acid with effervescence (bubbles of CO2) | *Ficus* leaf |
| **Oil globules** | Red with Sudan III | Oil seeds |

## Requirements

Onion bulb, potato tuber, rice or maize grains, castor seeds, *Colocasia* petiole; iodine solution, safranin, methylene blue, 0.9% saline, acetic acid, dilute HCl, glycerine, Sudan III; slides, coverslips, forceps, needles, blade, sterile toothpick, microscope.

## Procedure

**A. Onion peel (plant cell)**

1. Remove a fleshy scale leaf from an onion and peel off the thin inner epidermis with forceps.
2. Place it in water on a slide; add a drop of iodine or safranin for 1–2 minutes; wash.
3. Mount in glycerine, cover and observe.

**B. Cheek cells (animal cell)**

1. Rinse your mouth; gently scrape the inside of the cheek with a sterile toothpick.
2. Spread the scraping in a drop of saline on a slide; add a drop of methylene blue; wait a minute, blot extra stain.
3. Cover and observe.

**C. Inclusions**

1. Scrape a little cut potato surface onto a slide with water; add iodine; observe starch grains.
2. Crush a little castor endosperm in water; add iodine; observe aleurone grains.
3. Scrape *Colocasia* petiole tissue in water; observe needle-shaped raphides; add acetic acid (no change) and then HCl (crystals dissolve without bubbles).

## Observations

| Specimen | Features seen |
| --- | --- |
| Onion peel | Rectangular cells in rows, cell wall, nucleus at one side, large vacuole, no chloroplasts (scale leaf is not green) |
| Cheek cells | Flat, irregular cells, thin membrane, central nucleus stained blue, no wall, no large vacuole |
| Potato starch | Oval grains, eccentric hilum, striations, blue-black with iodine |
| Castor aleurone | Yellow-brown protein grains with iodine |
| *Colocasia* | Bundles of needle-shaped raphides of calcium oxalate |

Draw each and label.

## Precautions

1. Take a thin, single-layer peel; avoid folding.
2. Do not overstain.
3. Use sterile, disposable toothpicks; never reuse them.
4. Lower the coverslip at an angle to avoid air bubbles.
5. Use small amounts of iodine; too much masks details.

## Viva questions

1. **Name two differences between onion and cheek cells.** Onion has a cell wall and a large vacuole; cheek cells have neither.
2. **Why is the onion peel colourless?** The scale leaf grows underground and has no chloroplasts.
3. **How is starch identified?** It turns blue-black with iodine.
4. **What is a hilum of a starch grain?** The point around which layers of starch are deposited.
5. **What are raphides?** Needle-shaped crystals of calcium oxalate, as in *Colocasia*.
6. **How do you tell calcium carbonate from calcium oxalate?** Carbonate dissolves in acid with effervescence; oxalate dissolves in HCl without bubbles and does not dissolve in acetic acid.

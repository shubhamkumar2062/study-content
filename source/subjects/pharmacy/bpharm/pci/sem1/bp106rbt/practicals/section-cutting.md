# Section cutting techniques (free-hand sections)

**Aim:** To cut thin free-hand transverse sections (T.S.) of a stem, root or leaf for microscopic study.

> Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ from this guide.

## Principle

Plant organs are too thick for light to pass through, so the internal structure (anatomy) can only be seen in very thin slices called **sections**. A **transverse section (T.S.)** is cut at right angles to the long axis; a **longitudinal section (L.S.)** is cut along it. A section one or two cell layers thick lets light pass and shows each tissue clearly. In the free-hand method a sharp razor is drawn across the material by hand; soft material is supported in a **pith** block (potato, carrot or elder pith) so that it does not bend.

## Requirements

Fresh material (sunflower or *Cucurbita* stem, maize stem, gram or maize root, or a leaf), sharp razor, watch glass with water, fine brush, potato or carrot piece (as pith), needle, forceps, slides, coverslips, glycerine, microscope.

## Procedure

1. Take a straight piece of the stem or root about 2–3 cm long. A thin leaf or slender root is held between two halves of a potato or carrot block slit lengthwise.
2. Hold the material vertically between the thumb and forefinger of the left hand, with its top a little above the fingers.
3. Make the top surface flat with one cut and discard that slice.
4. Wet the razor blade and the material with water.
5. Hold the razor in the right hand, blade horizontal and at right angles to the material, edge facing you.
6. Draw the razor quickly across the top of the material in one smooth stroke, from the heel of the blade to its tip (pulling towards yourself). Do not saw back and forth.
7. Cut many sections in quick succession, keeping material and razor wet.
8. Transfer the sections from the blade to the watch glass of water with a wet brush.
9. Select the thinnest, complete, uniform sections (they look transparent) using a brush.
10. Place one on a slide in a drop of water or glycerine; stain and mount it (see the mounting and staining practical) and observe.

## Precautions

1. Use a very sharp razor; a blunt one crushes the cells.
2. Keep the material and razor wet to avoid drying and air bubbles.
3. Cut in one smooth stroke; keep the blade at right angles to the material for a true transverse section.
4. Handle sections with a brush, not with forceps or fingers.
5. Choose thin, complete sections; thick ones look dark.
6. Handle the razor carefully to avoid cuts.

## Viva questions

1. **Why must sections be thin?** So light can pass through and individual cells and tissues are seen.
2. **What is a T.S.?** A section cut at right angles to the long axis of the organ.
3. **Why is pith (potato) used?** To support soft or thin material while cutting.
4. **Why keep the razor wet?** To prevent drying of sections and to let them slide off easily.
5. **How do you pick a good section?** It is thin, transparent, complete and of even thickness.
6. **Name a tissue you can identify in a dicot stem T.S.** Collenchymatous hypodermis, a ring of open vascular bundles, or pith.

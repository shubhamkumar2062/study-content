# Determination of tidal volume

**Aim:** To determine the tidal volume (and, where possible, vital capacity) of a person using a spirometer, and to calculate the minute volume.

> Use a fresh disposable mouthpiece for each person. Stop if the subject feels dizzy. Follow your lab manual and your teacher.

## Principle

**Tidal volume (TV)** is the volume of air breathed in or out in one normal, quiet breath, about **500 ml** in a healthy adult. It is measured with a **spirometer**, a device that records the volume of air breathed.

Common laboratory types:

- **Wet (water-sealed) spirometer:** an inverted bell floats in water; air breathed into it lifts the bell, and the rise on a calibrated scale gives the volume.
- **Hand-held or dry spirometer:** air blown through it turns a dial or a turbine that shows the volume; electronic spirometers display the value directly.

Related values:

- **Vital capacity (VC)** = maximum air breathed out after a maximum breath in = TV + IRV + ERV.
- **Minute volume** = tidal volume × breaths per minute (normal rate 12–16 per minute).

## Requirements

Spirometer (wet, dry or electronic), disposable mouthpieces, nose clip, stopwatch.

## Procedure

**Tidal volume**

1. Set the spirometer to zero and fit a fresh mouthpiece.
2. Let the subject sit or stand upright and breathe normally for a minute.
3. Put on the nose clip so that air does not pass through the nose.
4. After a normal breath in, the subject places the mouthpiece in the mouth and breathes out **normally** (not forcibly) into the spirometer.
5. Read the volume. Repeat three times and take the average.
6. Count the subject's breaths per minute at rest with a stopwatch, without telling the subject (people change their breathing when watched).

**Vital capacity (if asked)**

1. The subject takes the deepest possible breath in.
2. Then breathes out as much air as possible, as fast as possible, into the spirometer.
3. Read and repeat three times; take the highest value.

Respiratory rate = ______ breaths per minute.

**Minute volume = TV × respiratory rate.**

**Worked example.** TV readings 480, 500 and 520 ml → mean **500 ml**. Respiratory rate 14 per minute → minute volume = 500 × 14 = **7,000 ml/min (7 L/min)**.

## Precautions

1. Use a fresh mouthpiece for each subject.
2. Use a nose clip so that no air escapes through the nose.
3. For tidal volume, breathing must be normal and quiet, not forced.
4. Make sure the lips seal around the mouthpiece.
5. Take several readings and average them.
6. Allow rest between attempts at vital capacity to avoid dizziness.

## Viva questions

1. **Define tidal volume.** The volume of air breathed in or out in one normal breath, about 500 ml.
2. **What is vital capacity?** Maximum air breathed out after a maximum breath in (TV + IRV + ERV).
3. **What is residual volume and can a spirometer measure it?** Air left in the lungs after forced expiration, about 1,100–1,200 ml; a simple spirometer cannot measure it.
4. **Why is a nose clip used?** To prevent air passing through the nose.
5. **What is minute volume?** Tidal volume × breaths per minute, about 6–8 L/min.
6. **Name a factor that lowers vital capacity.** Lung disease such as asthma or emphysema (also poor posture or smoking).

# Mounting and staining of sections

**Aim:** To stain a plant section (single or double staining) and mount it on a slide for microscopic study.

> Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ from this guide. Dilute HCl and alcohol are irritant and flammable; handle with care.

## Principle

Most plant cells are colourless, so tissues are hard to tell apart. **Stains** are dyes that colour particular parts. For example:

| Stain | Colours | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Safranin** (red) | Lignified walls (xylem, sclerenchyma) and nuclei red | Lignified tissue |
| **Fast green** or light green | Cellulose walls (parenchyma, phloem) green | Unlignified tissue |
| **Phloroglucinol + concentrated HCl** | Lignified walls pink-red | Test for lignin |
| **Iodine solution** | Starch blue-black; proteins yellow | Cell inclusions |
| **Acetocarmine** or methylene blue | Nuclei | Cells, onion peel, cheek cells |

Using two stains (**double staining**, e.g. safranin and fast green) gives contrast: lignified tissues red, cellulose tissues green.

**Mounting** means placing the material in a liquid (**mountant**) under a coverslip. Water or **glycerine** is used for temporary mounts; glycerine does not dry quickly and makes tissue clearer. **DPX** or Canada balsam is used for permanent mounts.

## Requirements

Thin sections (from the section cutting practical), watch glasses, brush, needle, safranin, fast green, phloroglucinol, concentrated HCl, glycerine, slides, coverslips, blotting paper, microscope.

## Procedure

**A. Single staining (safranin) — temporary mount**

1. Place a few good sections in a watch glass of water.
2. Transfer them to a watch glass of dilute safranin for 2–5 minutes.
3. Wash in water to remove extra stain until the water runs clear.
4. Put a drop of glycerine at the centre of a clean slide and place one section in it with a brush.
5. Lower the coverslip slowly at an angle using a needle so that no air bubbles are trapped.
6. Remove extra glycerine from the edges with blotting paper.
7. Observe under low power, then high power.

**B. Double staining (safranin and fast green) — temporary**

1. Stain with safranin and wash as above.
2. Dip in fast green for a few seconds only (it stains quickly).
3. Wash, mount in glycerine and observe: lignified xylem red, parenchyma and phloem green.

**C. Lignin test (phloroglucinol and HCl)**

1. Put a section on a slide; add a drop of phloroglucinol, wait a minute, then add a drop of concentrated HCl.
2. Cover and observe: lignified walls (xylem, sclerenchyma) turn pink-red.

## Precautions

1. Do not overstain; wash out excess stain.
2. Fast green acts quickly; keep its time short.
3. Lower the coverslip slowly to avoid air bubbles.
4. Keep the slide and coverslip clean and dry outside the mount.
5. Use only one section per mount, at the centre of the slide.
6. Add concentrated HCl carefully; it is corrosive.

## Viva questions

1. **Why are sections stained?** To colour different tissues so they can be distinguished.
2. **What does safranin stain?** Lignified walls and nuclei red.
3. **What is double staining?** Using two contrasting stains, such as safranin and fast green, on the same section.
4. **Why is glycerine used as a mountant?** It does not dry quickly and clears the tissue.
5. **How do you avoid air bubbles?** Lower the coverslip slowly at an angle with a needle.
6. **What is the test for lignin?** Phloroglucinol with concentrated HCl gives a pink-red colour.

# Determination of blood group

**Aim:** To determine the ABO and Rh blood group of a given blood sample by the slide agglutination method.

> Handle blood as possibly infectious. Use a sterile, disposable lancet for each person, wear gloves, and discard sharps and blood-stained material as your laboratory directs. Follow your lab manual and your teacher.

## Principle

The ABO group depends on the **antigens** present on the surface of red blood cells (RBCs). When RBCs carrying an antigen meet the matching **antibody** (antiserum), they clump together. This clumping is called **agglutination** and is visible to the naked eye.

| Antiserum | Contains | Clumps RBCs carrying |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anti-A | Antibody against antigen A | A antigen (groups A and AB) |
| Anti-B | Antibody against antigen B | B antigen (groups B and AB) |
| Anti-D | Antibody against Rh (D) antigen | Rh-positive RBCs |

## Requirements

Anti-A, anti-B and anti-D antisera, clean glass slide (or a white tile), sterile lancet, 70% alcohol (spirit) swab, cotton, separate mixing sticks or toothpicks, marker pen, gloves.

## Procedure

1. Mark three areas on the slide as A, B and D.
2. Clean the tip of the ring finger with a spirit swab and let it dry.
3. Prick the fingertip with a sterile lancet; wipe away the first drop.
4. Place one drop of blood in each of the three areas.
5. Add one drop of anti-A to area A, anti-B to area B and anti-D to area D.
6. Mix each with a separate clean stick, spreading slightly.
7. Rock the slide gently and look for agglutination within about 1–2 minutes (anti-D may take a little longer); view against a white background.
8. Press cotton on the pricked finger until bleeding stops.

```text
   [ A ]          [ B ]          [ D ]
 blood+anti-A   blood+anti-B   blood+anti-D
  clumps?         clumps?        clumps?
```

## Observations

| Anti-A | Anti-B | Blood group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agglutination | No agglutination | A |
| No agglutination | Agglutination | B |
| Agglutination | Agglutination | AB |
| No agglutination | No agglutination | O |

| Anti-D | Rh type |
| --- | --- |
| Agglutination | Rh-positive |
| No agglutination | Rh-negative |

## Precautions

1. Use a fresh sterile lancet for every person; never share.
2. Use separate sticks for each antiserum to avoid mixing.
3. Do not take too large a drop of blood; it may hide the clumps.
4. Read the result in time; drying at the edges can look like clumping.
5. Keep antisera refrigerated and check that they are within date.
6. Dispose of sharps and blood-stained material safely.

## Viva questions

1. **What is agglutination?** Clumping of RBCs when their antigen meets the matching antibody.
2. **Which group is the universal donor?** O (Rh-negative is preferred in emergencies).
3. **Which group is the universal recipient?** AB.
4. **Who discovered the ABO system?** Karl Landsteiner.
5. **What is the danger of Rh incompatibility in pregnancy?** An Rh-negative mother's antibodies can destroy the RBCs of an Rh-positive fetus in a later pregnancy (erythroblastosis fetalis).
6. **Which antibodies are present in the plasma of group O?** Both anti-A and anti-B.

# How to use this guide

Remedial Biology is for students who took mathematics instead of biology in Class 12. It assumes no biology at all: every term is explained the first time it appears, so read the units in order. Unit 1 builds the vocabulary of plants, Units 2 and 3 cover the human body, Unit 4 returns to plant nutrition, and Unit 5 ends with respiration, growth, the cell and tissues.

**Study plan.** One unit every two or three days. Read the notes once fully, then draw each text diagram from memory (heart, nephron, neuron, flower, dicot and monocot stem, chloroplast, cell). Learn the tables as tables — examiners like a neat comparison. Finally answer the question bank aloud or on paper, and take the quick MCQ test to check facts.

**Units as in the syllabus.** The five notes follow the five PCI units exactly. Units 2, 3 and 5 are long because each joins several topics (for example, Unit 3 has excretion, nerves, hormones and reproduction); they are kept together so that your notes match the syllabus copy your college gives you.

## The exam for this subject

In the PCI scheme, **BP106RBT is a Non University Examination (NUE)**: your own college sets and conducts the end-semester paper. It is a **35-mark, 1.5-hour** paper, and the subject carries 50 marks in all.

| Part | Marks |
| --- | --- |
| Continuous mode (attendance, assignments) | 5 |
| Sessional examinations (1 hour each) | 10 |
| **End-semester examination (1.5 hours)** | **35** |
| Total | 50 |

**End-semester paper (35 marks, 1.5 hours)** — the pattern the PCI regulations give for a 35-mark paper:

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Long answers — attempt 1 of 2 | 1 × 10 = 10 |
| II | Short answers — attempt 5 of 7 | 5 × 5 = 25 |

There is **no MCQ section** in this paper. The MCQs and the quick MCQ test here are for practice and for checking facts quickly, and they help with viva and sessional questions. Because your college sets the paper, a few colleges may change the split; the full mock follows the PCI pattern.

**Sessional paper (30 marks, 1 hour), as PCI gives it for NUE subjects:** long answers 1 of 2 × 10 = 10, short answers 4 of 6 × 5 = 20. A mock in this pattern is also included.

The practical, **BP112RBP**, carries 25 marks: 10 internal and 15 in a 2-hour end-semester practical. Its guides are in the practicals section.

Your college may adjust the paper slightly; confirm the pattern with your teachers. To pass you need 50% of the total of internal and end-semester marks together.

## Probability ratings

- **High** — asked in most papers; prepare a full answer with a labelled diagram.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare; good for a backup choice and for viva questions.

These ratings are estimates from syllabus weight and common question patterns. They are not a promise of what will be asked.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. **Draw first.** A labelled diagram earns marks even before you write a word. Practise the heart, nephron, neuron, flower (longitudinal section), dicot stem section, chloroplast, mitochondrion and a plant cell.
2. **Use tables for differences.** "Differentiate" or "compare" questions (mitosis vs meiosis, dicot vs monocot stem, arteries vs veins, spermatogenesis vs oogenesis) are easiest to score as a 5–6 row table.
3. **Give examples with families** for plant topics: carrot (Apiaceae), potato (Solanaceae), pea (Fabaceae), mustard (Brassicaceae), maize (Poaceae). Write scientific names in proper binomial form.
4. **Use flow charts** for processes: coagulation, urine formation, renin–angiotensin system, glycolysis, nitrogen cycle, spermatogenesis.
5. **Learn the standard numbers:** RBC about 5 million/mm³, heart rate 72/min, cardiac cycle 0.8 s, cardiac output about 5 L/min, BP 120/80 mmHg, tidal volume 500 ml, GFR 125 ml/min (180 L/day), resting potential −70 mV, net ATP of glycolysis 2.
6. **Spell key terms correctly**: renin (kidney) vs rennin (stomach), ileum (gut) vs ilium (hip bone), mitosis vs meiosis.
7. **Link to pharmacy** in one line at the end of a long answer where you can (ACE inhibitors and the renin–angiotensin system, insulin and diabetes, fermentation for alcohol and antibiotics, vitamin K and clotting). It shows understanding.
8. In 1.5 hours you write six answers: give the long answer about 25 minutes and each of the five short answers about 12 minutes, leaving a few minutes to check labels and diagrams.

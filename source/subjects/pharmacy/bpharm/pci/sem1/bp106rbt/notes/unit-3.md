# Unit 3: Excretion, neural control, chemical coordination and human reproduction

This is the largest unit. It covers how the body removes wastes (kidneys), how it sends fast messages (nerves) and slower chemical messages (hormones), and how humans reproduce. Kidney function decides how fast many drugs leave the body; nerves and hormones are the targets of a large part of pharmacology; and hormones of reproduction are the basis of contraceptive pills.

## 3.1 Excretory products and their elimination

**Excretion** is the removal of waste products of metabolism (body chemistry) from the body. The main wastes are **nitrogenous wastes** formed when proteins and nucleic acids are broken down, plus CO2, excess water and salts.

### Modes of excretion

Animals are grouped by the main nitrogenous waste they make:

| Mode | Main waste | Toxicity and water need | Found in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ammonotelism** | Ammonia | Most toxic; needs a lot of water to wash it out; very soluble, so it diffuses out easily | Bony fish, aquatic amphibians, aquatic insects |
| **Ureotelism** | Urea | Less toxic; moderate water needed; made in the liver from ammonia | Mammals (including humans), many terrestrial amphibians, marine fish |
| **Uricotelism** | Uric acid | Least toxic; removed as a paste or pellet with very little water; saves water | Reptiles, birds, land snails, insects |

**Excretory organs in different animals:** flame cells (protonephridia) in flatworms like *Planaria*; nephridia in earthworm; Malpighian tubules in insects such as cockroach; antennal (green) glands in prawn; kidneys in vertebrates.

**Other organs that help excretion in humans:** lungs (CO2, water vapour), liver (bile pigments bilirubin and biliverdin, cholesterol, some drugs and vitamins), skin (sweat with water, salt, a little urea; sebum).

### Human excretory system — structure

```text
        kidney (left)          kidney (right, slightly lower)
              \                    /
              ureter            ureter
                \                /
                 urinary bladder (stores urine)
                        |
                     urethra  --> outside
```

- **Kidneys:** a pair of bean-shaped, reddish-brown organs on either side of the backbone, between the last thoracic and third lumbar vertebrae, each about 10–12 cm long and 120–170 g. On the inner concave side is a notch, the **hilum**, where the ureter, blood vessels and nerves enter or leave. Inside the hilum is a funnel-shaped space, the **renal pelvis**.
- A section of the kidney shows an outer **cortex** and inner **medulla**; the medulla is divided into cone-shaped **medullary pyramids** that open into the **calyces**. Extensions of the cortex between the pyramids are the **renal columns (columns of Bertini)**.
- Each kidney has about **one million nephrons**, the structural and functional units.

### Structure of a nephron

```text
 afferent arteriole -> GLOMERULUS (tuft of capillaries) -> efferent arteriole
                          inside
                   BOWMAN'S CAPSULE  } together = Malpighian body
                          |            (renal corpuscle)
                 Proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)  - in cortex
                          |
                 Loop of Henle (descending + ascending limbs) - dips into medulla
                          |
                 Distal convoluted tubule (DCT)    - in cortex
                          |
                 Collecting duct (shared by many nephrons)
                          |
                 renal pelvis -> ureter
```

- The efferent arteriole forms a capillary network around the tubule (**peritubular capillaries**); around the loop of Henle it forms the **vasa recta**.
- **Cortical nephrons** (most) have short loops that barely enter the medulla. **Juxtamedullary nephrons** have very long loops reaching deep into the medulla; they help make concentrated urine.

### Urine formation

Urine is formed in three steps:

**1. Glomerular filtration (ultrafiltration).** Blood pressure in the glomerulus forces water and small dissolved substances (glucose, amino acids, salts, urea) out of the blood into Bowman's capsule. Blood cells and plasma proteins stay behind. The filter has three layers: capillary endothelium, basement membrane and the **podocytes** (cells of the capsule) with tiny slits between their foot processes.

- **Glomerular filtration rate (GFR)** — volume filtered by both kidneys per minute ≈ **125 ml/min**, about **180 litres per day**.

**2. Reabsorption.** About **99%** of the filtrate is taken back into blood as it flows along the tubule. Glucose, amino acids and Na+ are reabsorbed actively (using energy); water passively.

| Part | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| PCT | Most reabsorption: almost all glucose and amino acids, about 70–80% of salts and water; H+ and NH3 secreted to keep blood pH balanced |
| Descending limb of Henle's loop | Permeable to water, almost not to salts — filtrate becomes concentrated |
| Ascending limb | Impermeable to water; salts move out — filtrate becomes dilute |
| DCT | Some Na+ and water reabsorbed under hormone control; H+ and K+ secreted |
| Collecting duct | Large amounts of water reabsorbed under the control of **ADH**, giving concentrated urine |

**3. Tubular secretion.** Cells of the tubule add substances from blood to the filtrate: H+, K+, ammonia, creatinine and many drugs (penicillin, for example). This helps keep ion and acid–base balance.

The **counter-current mechanism** of the loop of Henle and vasa recta (fluid flowing in opposite directions in neighbouring limbs) builds up a high salt concentration in the deep medulla, so that water can be drawn out of the collecting duct and urine can be up to about four times more concentrated than the filtrate.

**Normal urine:** about 1–1.5 litres a day, light yellow, slightly acidic (pH about 6), with about 25–30 g urea a day. Glucose in urine (**glycosuria**) and ketone bodies (**ketonuria**) suggest diabetes mellitus.

### Renin–angiotensin system

(The syllabus prints "Rennin". **Renin**, with one n, is the kidney enzyme; **rennin** is the milk-curdling enzyme of the stomach. Do not mix them up in the exam.)

```text
 Fall in blood pressure / blood flow / GFR
        |
        v
 Juxtaglomerular (JG) cells of the kidney release RENIN
        |
 ANGIOTENSINOGEN (plasma protein from liver) ---renin---> ANGIOTENSIN I
        |
        |  angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE), mainly in lungs
        v
 ANGIOTENSIN II (active)
    |                                   |
    v                                   v
 constricts blood vessels         adrenal cortex releases ALDOSTERONE
 (raises BP)                         -> Na+ and water reabsorbed in DCT
                                     -> blood volume and BP rise
        |
        v
 GFR and blood pressure return to normal (negative feedback)
```

- The **juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA)** is a special region where the DCT touches the afferent arteriole; it senses falling GFR.
- **ADH (antidiuretic hormone, vasopressin)** from the posterior pituitary is released when the body loses water; it makes the DCT and collecting duct reabsorb more water, so less urine forms.
- **Atrial natriuretic factor (ANF)**, released by the heart when blood pressure rises, dilates vessels and opposes the renin–angiotensin system.
- Pharmacy link: **ACE inhibitors** (such as enalapril) lower blood pressure by blocking the conversion of angiotensin I to II.

**Disorders:** **uraemia** (urea builds up in blood; treated by **haemodialysis**, an artificial kidney), **renal calculi** (kidney stones, often calcium oxalate), **glomerulonephritis** (inflammation of glomeruli).

## 3.2 Neural control and coordination

**Coordination** is how organs work together. The **nervous system** gives fast, point-to-point control through electrical impulses.

### Definition and classification of the nervous system

The **nervous system** is the network of neurons (nerve cells) that receives information, processes it and sends instructions to muscles and glands.

```text
                  HUMAN NERVOUS SYSTEM
           ______________|________________
          |                               |
  CENTRAL NERVOUS SYSTEM          PERIPHERAL NERVOUS SYSTEM
  (CNS): brain + spinal cord      (PNS): nerves outside the CNS
  (processing and control)        12 pairs cranial + 31 pairs spinal nerves
                                    _______________|_______________
                                   |                               |
                          AFFERENT (sensory) fibres      EFFERENT (motor) fibres
                          carry impulses TO the CNS      carry impulses FROM the CNS
                                                      __________|__________
                                                     |                     |
                                              SOMATIC system       AUTONOMIC system
                                              (to skeletal         (to smooth muscle,
                                               muscles; voluntary)  heart, glands; involuntary)
                                                               ______|______
                                                              |             |
                                                       SYMPATHETIC   PARASYMPATHETIC
                                                      ("fight or     ("rest and
                                                        flight")       digest")
```

The **visceral nervous system** is the part of the PNS that connects the CNS with internal organs (viscera).

### Structure of a neuron

A **neuron** is the structural and functional unit of the nervous system.

```text
 dendrites   cell body (cyton)         axon                axon terminals
   \|/    [ nucleus + Nissl's  ]========[myelin]=[myelin]=====< synaptic knobs
   -O-    [ granules in        ]          node of Ranvier       (neurotransmitter
   /|\    [ cytoplasm          ]          (gap in myelin)        vesicles)
```

| Part | Feature and role |
| --- | --- |
| **Cell body (cyton)** | Has the nucleus, cytoplasm, organelles and **Nissl's granules** (groups of ribosomes) |
| **Dendrites** | Short branched processes; carry impulses **towards** the cell body |
| **Axon** | Single long process; carries impulses **away** from the cell body; ends in branches with **synaptic knobs** that hold **neurotransmitters** (chemical messengers) |
| **Myelin sheath** | Fatty covering formed by **Schwann cells** in the PNS; insulates the axon |
| **Nodes of Ranvier** | Gaps in the myelin sheath |

**Types by number of processes:** **multipolar** (one axon, two or more dendrites; most common, in the brain), **bipolar** (one axon, one dendrite; retina of the eye), **unipolar** (cell body with one process; embryonic stage). **Myelinated** fibres (spinal and cranial nerves) conduct faster than **non-myelinated** fibres (autonomic and somatic nervous system in parts).

### Generation and conduction of a nerve impulse

**Resting state.** The axon membrane at rest is **polarised**: the outside is positively charged and the inside negatively charged. The **resting membrane potential** is about **−70 mV**. It is kept by:

- The **sodium–potassium pump**, which uses ATP to push **3 Na+ out for every 2 K+ in**.
- The membrane being more permeable to K+ than to Na+, so the inside stays rich in K+ and negatively charged proteins, and the outside rich in Na+.

**Action potential (nerve impulse)**

```text
 1. Stimulus at a point -> Na+ channels open -> Na+ rushes IN
    -> inside becomes positive, outside negative = DEPOLARISATION
       (this reversed state is the action potential)
 2. Na+ channels close, K+ channels open -> K+ moves OUT
    -> resting polarity restored = REPOLARISATION
 3. Na+/K+ pump restores the original ion distribution
 4. The depolarised spot triggers the next spot -> the impulse travels
    along the axon in one direction
```

In **myelinated** fibres the impulse jumps from one node of Ranvier to the next (**saltatory conduction**), which is much faster.

**Synapse.** A **synapse** is the junction where an impulse passes from one neuron to the next.

- **Electrical synapse:** membranes very close; current flows directly; very fast but rare.
- **Chemical synapse:** a gap, the **synaptic cleft**. The impulse reaching the axon terminal releases a **neurotransmitter** (such as **acetylcholine**) from vesicles. It crosses the cleft and binds to **receptors** on the next membrane, opening ion channels and starting a new impulse. Many drugs act at synapses by copying, blocking or prolonging neurotransmitters.

### Structure of the brain and spinal cord

**Protective coverings:** the CNS is protected by the skull and the backbone, and by three membranes, the **meninges** — outer **dura mater**, middle **arachnoid mater**, inner **pia mater**. The space between the arachnoid and pia contains **cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)**, which cushions the brain.

**Main parts of the brain**

```text
 FOREBRAIN  : cerebrum, thalamus, hypothalamus
 MIDBRAIN   : between thalamus/hypothalamus and pons (corpora quadrigemina)
 HINDBRAIN  : pons, cerebellum, medulla oblongata
 BRAINSTEM  = midbrain + pons + medulla oblongata (connects brain to spinal cord)
```

- **Cerebrum** — largest part; split into left and right **cerebral hemispheres** by a deep groove, joined by a thick band of nerve fibres, the **corpus callosum**. Its outer layer, the **cerebral cortex**, is **grey matter** (cell bodies) thrown into folds; the inner part is **white matter** (myelinated fibres).
- **Thalamus** — major relay centre for sensory and motor signals, wrapped by the cerebrum.
- **Hypothalamus** — at the base of the thalamus.
- **Cerebellum** — "little brain", at the back below the cerebrum, with a very folded surface.
- **Medulla oblongata** — lowest part, continuous with the spinal cord.

### Functions of the main brain regions

| Region | Functions |
| --- | --- |
| **Cerebrum** | Seat of intelligence, memory, thinking, reasoning, learning and voluntary actions; sensory areas receive sensations (touch, pain, temperature, sight, hearing); motor areas control voluntary muscles; **association areas** handle intersensory links, memory and communication (speech) |
| **Cerebellum** | Coordinates voluntary movements; keeps **balance (equilibrium)** and **posture**; makes movements smooth and precise |
| **Hypothalamus** | Controls body **temperature**, **hunger**, **thirst** and urge to eat and drink; links nerves and hormones by releasing **hypothalamic hormones** that control the pituitary; with the limbic system it is involved in emotions, sexual behaviour and motivation |
| **Medulla oblongata** | Centres for **breathing**, **heart rate and blood pressure** (cardiovascular reflexes), gastric secretion; reflexes of swallowing, coughing, sneezing and vomiting |

Damage to the medulla can be fatal because breathing and heartbeat are controlled there.

### Spinal cord

- A long cylindrical cord inside the **vertebral canal** of the backbone, from the medulla oblongata to about the level of the first or second lumbar vertebra; it gives off **31 pairs of spinal nerves**.
- In cross-section: an inner **H-shaped (butterfly-shaped) grey matter** and outer **white matter**. A small **central canal** filled with CSF runs through the middle.
- Sensory (afferent) fibres enter through the **dorsal root** (which carries a swelling, the dorsal root ganglion); motor (efferent) fibres leave through the **ventral root**.
- Functions: carries impulses up to the brain and down from it; centre for **reflex actions**.

**Reflex action and reflex arc.** A **reflex** is an automatic, quick, involuntary response to a stimulus, controlled by the spinal cord without needing the brain (for example the knee-jerk reflex or pulling the hand back from a hot object).

```text
 stimulus -> receptor -> afferent (sensory) neuron -> spinal cord
   (interneuron in some reflexes) -> efferent (motor) neuron -> effector
   (muscle or gland) -> response
```

## 3.3 Chemical coordination and regulation

**Hormones** are chemicals produced in very small amounts by **endocrine glands** (ductless glands), released directly into the blood and acting on distant **target organs**. Endocrine control is slower than nerve control but lasts longer. Glands with ducts (salivary, sweat) are **exocrine**.

```text
 HYPOTHALAMUS (releasing and inhibiting hormones)
        |
 PITUITARY ("master gland")
   |      |       |        |
 thyroid adrenal ovary/   other targets
         cortex  testis
```

### Endocrine glands, their secretions and functions

| Gland (location) | Hormone | Main function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hypothalamus** (base of brain) | Releasing hormones (e.g. GnRH — gonadotrophin-releasing hormone) and inhibiting hormones (e.g. somatostatin) | Start or stop secretion of pituitary hormones |
| **Pituitary — anterior lobe (adenohypophysis)** (in a bony cavity below the brain) | Growth hormone (GH) | Body growth; too much in childhood → **gigantism**, in adults → **acromegaly**; too little → **pituitary dwarfism** |
| | Prolactin (PRL) | Growth of mammary glands and milk formation |
| | Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) | Stimulates the thyroid |
| | Adrenocorticotrophic hormone (ACTH) | Stimulates the adrenal cortex to make glucocorticoids |
| | Luteinising hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) (gonadotrophins) | Control testes and ovaries; LH causes ovulation |
| Pituitary — intermediate lobe | Melanocyte-stimulating hormone (MSH) | Skin pigment cells |
| **Pituitary — posterior lobe (neurohypophysis)** (stores hormones made by the hypothalamus) | **Oxytocin** | Contraction of the uterus at childbirth; milk ejection |
| | **ADH (vasopressin)** | Water reabsorption in kidney; deficiency → **diabetes insipidus** (large volumes of dilute urine) |
| **Pineal gland** (top of forebrain) | Melatonin | Sleep–wake (24-hour) rhythm |
| **Thyroid** (neck, two lobes either side of the trachea) | Thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3) — need **iodine** | Basal metabolic rate, growth, development; too little in a child → **cretinism**, in adults → **myxoedema**; iodine lack → **goitre** (enlarged thyroid); too much → **Graves' disease** (exophthalmic goitre) |
| | Thyrocalcitonin | Lowers blood calcium |
| **Parathyroid** (four, behind the thyroid) | Parathyroid hormone (PTH) | Raises blood calcium (from bone, kidney, gut) |
| **Thymus** (chest, behind sternum) | Thymosins | Development of T lymphocytes (immunity); shrinks in old age |
| **Adrenal gland — cortex** (on top of each kidney) | Glucocorticoids (mainly **cortisol**) | Raise blood glucose, reduce inflammation and immune response |
| | Mineralocorticoids (mainly **aldosterone**) | Na+ and water retention, K+ loss |
| | Small amounts of androgens | Some sex characters |
| **Adrenal gland — medulla** | **Adrenaline (epinephrine)** and noradrenaline — "emergency hormones" | "Fight or flight": faster heart, raised BP, more blood glucose, dilated pupils and airways |
| **Pancreas — islets of Langerhans** (α and β cells) | **Insulin** (β cells) | Lowers blood glucose (cells take up glucose; liver stores it as glycogen); lack → **diabetes mellitus** |
| | **Glucagon** (α cells) | Raises blood glucose (breaks down liver glycogen) |
| **Testes** | Testosterone (androgens, from Leydig cells) | Male sex organs, sperm production, male secondary sexual characters |
| **Ovaries** | Oestrogens (from growing follicles) | Female sex organs, secondary characters, growth of endometrium |
| | Progesterone (from corpus luteum) | Prepares and maintains the uterus for pregnancy |
| Others: heart, kidney, gut | ANF (heart); erythropoietin (kidney, stimulates RBC formation); gastrin, secretin, cholecystokinin (gut, control digestive secretions) | |

Adrenal cortex deficiency causes **Addison's disease**; excess cortisol causes **Cushing's syndrome**.

**How hormones act:** they bind to specific **receptors** — protein-type hormones on the cell membrane (using second messengers such as cyclic AMP), and steroid and thyroid hormones on receptors inside the cell that change gene activity.

## 3.4 Human reproduction

Humans reproduce **sexually** and are **viviparous** (give birth to young). Reproduction involves forming gametes (sperm and ova), fertilisation, development of the embryo, and birth.

### Male reproductive system (in the pelvic region)

```text
 TESTES (in scrotum) -> seminiferous tubules -> rete testis
   -> vasa efferentia -> EPIDIDYMIS -> VAS DEFERENS
   -> joins the duct of the SEMINAL VESICLE -> EJACULATORY DUCT
   -> URETHRA (through the prostate) -> PENIS -> outside
```

| Part | Feature and role |
| --- | --- |
| **Testes** (pair) | Primary sex organs; in the **scrotum**, outside the abdomen, which keeps them about **2–2.5 °C below body temperature**, needed for sperm formation; each has about 250 lobules with 1–3 **seminiferous tubules** each |
| **Seminiferous tubules** | Lined by **spermatogonia** (cells that form sperm) and **Sertoli cells** (nourish the developing sperm) |
| **Leydig (interstitial) cells** | Between the tubules; secrete **testosterone** |
| **Accessory ducts** | Rete testis, vasa efferentia, **epididymis** (stores and matures sperm), **vas deferens**, ejaculatory duct |
| **Urethra** | Common passage for urine and semen, opens at the tip of the penis |
| **Penis** | External copulatory organ |
| **Accessory glands** | **Seminal vesicles** (pair), **prostate gland**, **bulbourethral (Cowper's) glands** (pair); their secretions form **seminal plasma**, rich in fructose, calcium and enzymes |

**Semen** = sperm + seminal plasma.

### Female reproductive system (in the pelvic region)

```text
            fimbriae      fimbriae
   ovary  (infundibulum) (infundibulum)  ovary
     \     ampulla          ampulla      /
      \    isthmus          isthmus     /
       \________ UTERUS (womb) ________/
                    |
                 CERVIX (cervical canal)
                    |
                 VAGINA -> external opening
```

| Part | Feature and role |
| --- | --- |
| **Ovaries** (pair) | Primary female sex organs; produce **ova** and the hormones oestrogen and progesterone; each about 2–4 cm |
| **Oviducts (fallopian tubes)** | About 10–12 cm; parts: funnel-shaped **infundibulum** with finger-like **fimbriae** that collect the ovum at ovulation, wider **ampulla** (usual site of fertilisation) and narrow **isthmus** joining the uterus |
| **Uterus (womb)** | Pear-shaped, hollow; wall of three layers: outer **perimetrium**, thick muscular **myometrium** (strong contractions in childbirth) and inner glandular **endometrium** (changes during the menstrual cycle; the embryo implants here) |
| **Cervix** | Lower narrow part of the uterus, opening into the vagina; together they form the **birth canal** |
| **Vagina** | Muscular tube to the outside |
| **External genitalia (vulva)** | Mons pubis, labia majora, labia minora, hymen, clitoris |
| **Mammary glands** (pair) | Accessory glands with glandular tissue in lobes; produce milk after childbirth |

### Gametogenesis

**Gametogenesis** is the formation of gametes. It needs **meiosis**, a special cell division that halves the chromosome number: a human body cell is **diploid (2n = 46 chromosomes)**; gametes are **haploid (n = 23)**.

### Spermatogenesis (in the seminiferous tubules, starts at puberty)

```text
 Spermatogonium (2n)         -- mitosis, many cells
        |
 Primary spermatocyte (2n)
        |  MEIOSIS I
        v
 2 Secondary spermatocytes (n)
        |  MEIOSIS II
        v
 4 Spermatids (n)
        |  SPERMIOGENESIS (change of shape, no division)
        v
 4 Spermatozoa (sperm, n)  -> released into the tubule (spermiation)
```

**Hormonal control:** at puberty the hypothalamus increases **GnRH**, which makes the anterior pituitary release **LH** and **FSH**. LH acts on Leydig cells to make testosterone; FSH acts on Sertoli cells to support spermiogenesis.

**Structure of a sperm:** head (with the haploid nucleus and the **acrosome**, a cap of enzymes that helps penetrate the ovum), neck, **middle piece** (many mitochondria, energy for movement) and **tail** (movement). A man releases about 200–300 million sperm in one ejaculation.

### Oogenesis (in the ovary, starts before birth)

```text
 Oogonia (2n)  -- formed in the fetal ovary; no new oogonia after birth
        |
 Primary oocyte (2n) -- starts meiosis I, then ARRESTS in prophase I
        |               (inside a primary follicle) until puberty
        |  at puberty, each cycle: meiosis I completes in one follicle
        v
 Secondary oocyte (n) + first polar body (small, n)
        |  ovulation (secondary oocyte released, arrested in meiosis II)
        |  MEIOSIS II completes only if a sperm enters
        v
 Ovum (n) + second polar body (n)
```

**Follicle development:** primary follicle → secondary follicle (more granulosa cell layers and a theca) → tertiary follicle (fluid-filled cavity, the **antrum**) → mature **Graafian follicle**, which ruptures at ovulation. The ruptured follicle becomes the **corpus luteum**.

| Spermatogenesis | Oogenesis |
| --- | --- |
| In testes | In ovaries |
| Starts at puberty, continues through life | Starts before birth; one oocyte matures each cycle from puberty to menopause |
| One primary spermatocyte → **4 sperm** | One primary oocyte → **1 ovum** + polar bodies |
| Divisions equal | Cytoplasm divides unequally (polar bodies are tiny) |
| Gametes motile | Ovum non-motile, large, with stored food |

### Menstrual cycle

The **menstrual cycle** is the cycle of changes in the ovary and uterus of female primates, about **28–29 days** long. The first cycle is called **menarche** (at puberty); cycles stop at about 50 years (**menopause**).

```text
 Day  1-5   MENSTRUAL PHASE: endometrium breaks down; bleeding
             (menstrual flow) for 3-5 days
 Day  6-13  FOLLICULAR (PROLIFERATIVE) PHASE: FSH and LH rise; follicle
             grows into a Graafian follicle; it secretes OESTROGEN;
             endometrium regenerates and thickens
 Day 14     OVULATION: LH reaches a peak (LH surge) -> Graafian follicle
             ruptures -> secondary oocyte released
 Day 15-28  LUTEAL (SECRETORY) PHASE: ruptured follicle becomes the
             CORPUS LUTEUM, which secretes much PROGESTERONE; endometrium
             becomes thick and glandular, ready for implantation
 If no fertilisation: corpus luteum degenerates -> progesterone falls
             -> endometrium breaks down -> next menstruation
 If fertilisation: corpus luteum continues; no menstruation (pregnancy)
```

| Phase | Ovary | Main hormone | Uterus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Menstrual | Old corpus luteum gone | Low levels | Endometrium shed |
| Follicular | Follicle grows | FSH, oestrogen rising | Endometrium rebuilt |
| Ovulation | Follicle ruptures | **LH surge** | — |
| Luteal | Corpus luteum active | Progesterone (and oestrogen) | Endometrium thick, secretory |

Absence of menstruation can indicate pregnancy, but may also be due to stress, poor health or other causes. Oral contraceptive pills contain synthetic oestrogen and/or progestogen that suppress the LH surge and so prevent ovulation.

## Remember

- Ammonotelic (ammonia, fish), ureotelic (urea, humans), uricotelic (uric acid, birds, reptiles).
- Each kidney has about 1 million nephrons; GFR 125 ml/min = 180 L/day; about 99% reabsorbed; urine 1–1.5 L/day.
- Urine formation = filtration + reabsorption + secretion; most reabsorption in the PCT; ADH acts on DCT and collecting duct.
- Renin (JG cells) → angiotensin I → ACE → angiotensin II → vasoconstriction + aldosterone → BP rises. Renin is not rennin.
- CNS = brain + spinal cord; PNS = 12 pairs cranial + 31 pairs spinal nerves; autonomic = sympathetic + parasympathetic.
- Resting potential about −70 mV; Na+ in = depolarisation; K+ out = repolarisation; Na+/K+ pump 3 out : 2 in; saltatory conduction in myelinated fibres.
- Cerebrum = thinking and voluntary action; cerebellum = balance and coordination; hypothalamus = temperature, hunger, thirst, pituitary control; medulla = breathing and heart.
- Spinal cord: grey matter inside (H-shape), white outside; dorsal root sensory, ventral root motor.
- Insulin lowers, glucagon raises blood glucose; ADH lack = diabetes insipidus; iodine lack = goitre.
- Testes 2–2.5 °C cooler; Sertoli cells nourish, Leydig cells make testosterone.
- 1 primary spermatocyte → 4 sperm; 1 primary oocyte → 1 ovum + polar bodies.
- Cycle 28 days: menstrual 1–5, follicular, ovulation day 14 (LH surge), luteal with corpus luteum and progesterone.

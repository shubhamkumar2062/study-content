# Preparation of potash alum

**Aim:** To prepare potash alum from aluminium sulphate and potassium sulphate and report the practical yield.

## Principle

Potash alum is a **double salt**, potassium aluminium sulphate dodecahydrate. When hot, concentrated solutions of aluminium sulphate and potassium sulphate are mixed in equimolar amounts and cooled, the double salt crystallises as colourless octahedral crystals because it is less soluble than either single salt in cold water. A little dilute sulphuric acid is added to prevent hydrolysis of the aluminium salt (which would make the solution turbid with basic aluminium salts).

Al2(SO4)3·18H2O + K2SO4 + 6H2O → 2KAl(SO4)2·12H2O

Molecular weights: Al2(SO4)3·18H2O 666.4; K2SO4 174.3; KAl(SO4)2·12H2O 474.4.

## Requirements

Beakers (100 ml and 250 ml), glass rod, burner, tripod, wire gauze, funnel, filter paper, crystallising dish, watch glass; aluminium sulphate (Al2(SO4)3·18H2O) 6.66 g, potassium sulphate 1.74 g, dilute sulphuric acid, purified water.

## Procedure

1. Weigh 6.66 g of aluminium sulphate (0.01 mol) and dissolve in about 10 ml of hot purified water containing a few drops of dilute sulphuric acid.
2. Weigh 1.74 g of potassium sulphate (0.01 mol) and dissolve in about 10–15 ml of hot water.
3. Filter both solutions if not clear, then mix them while hot and stir.
4. Concentrate slightly on a water bath if the volume is large, then pour into a crystallising dish and allow to cool slowly and undisturbed. Leave overnight if possible.
5. Decant the mother liquor, collect the crystals, wash with a very little ice-cold water, and dry between filter papers at room temperature (do not heat; alum loses water).
6. Weigh the crystals and calculate the yield.

## Observations and calculation

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Aluminium sulphate taken | 6.66 g (0.01 mol) |
| Potassium sulphate taken | 1.74 g (0.01 mol) |
| Theoretical yield of alum | 0.02 mol × 474.4 = 9.49 g |
| Practical yield | ____ g |
| Percentage yield | (practical ÷ theoretical) × 100 |

**Worked example.** Crystals obtained = 6.64 g → % yield = 6.64 / 9.49 × 100 = **70%**.

**Identity checks.** Aqueous solution is acidic and astringent; gives the tests for aluminium (white gelatinous precipitate with NaOH, soluble in excess), potassium (lilac flame through cobalt-blue glass) and sulphate (white precipitate with BaCl2, insoluble in HCl).

## Precautions

1. Use equimolar quantities of the two salts.
2. Add a few drops of dilute sulphuric acid to stop hydrolysis of aluminium sulphate.
3. Cool slowly and without disturbance to get large, well-formed crystals.
4. Dry at room temperature; heating removes water of crystallisation.

## Viva questions

1. **What is a double salt?** A salt formed from two simple salts that crystallise together in fixed proportions but give the separate ions in solution.
2. **How is a double salt different from a complex salt?** A complex salt keeps its complex ion in solution (for example [Cu(NH3)4]²⁺); a double salt does not.
3. **Why is sulphuric acid added?** To prevent hydrolysis of aluminium sulphate.
4. **What is the shape of alum crystals?** Octahedral.
5. **What is exsiccated alum?** Alum dried by heat to remove most water of crystallisation; used as a dusting powder.
6. **Give uses of potash alum.** Astringent and styptic, water purification (coagulant), mordant, vaccine adjuvant.

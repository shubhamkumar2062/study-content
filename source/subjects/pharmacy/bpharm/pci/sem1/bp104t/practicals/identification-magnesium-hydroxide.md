# Identification tests for magnesium hydroxide

**Aim:** To identify the given sample as magnesium hydroxide by tests for magnesium and hydroxide.

## Principle

Magnesium hydroxide is a white powder, practically insoluble in water, that dissolves in dilute acids to give magnesium salts. The magnesium ion is identified by its precipitation as white magnesium hydroxide with sodium hydroxide and as white crystalline **magnesium ammonium phosphate** with disodium hydrogen phosphate in the presence of ammonia and ammonium chloride. Its basic nature is shown by its alkaline reaction.

Mg(OH)2 + 2HCl → MgCl2 + 2H2O
MgCl2 + 2NaOH → Mg(OH)2 ↓ (white) + 2NaCl
MgCl2 + Na2HPO4 + NH3 → MgNH4PO4 ↓ (white crystalline) + 2NaCl

## Requirements

Test tubes, glass rod, filter paper; dilute hydrochloric acid, sodium hydroxide solution, ammonium chloride solution, dilute ammonia, disodium hydrogen phosphate solution, red litmus paper, sample.

## Procedure and observations

| No. | Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Appearance and solubility: shake a little sample with water | White powder, does not dissolve | Insoluble base |
| 2 | Shake a little sample with water and test with moist red litmus | Litmus turns blue (slowly) | Alkaline (hydroxide) |
| 3 | Dissolve about 0.1 g in a few ml of dilute HCl | Dissolves without effervescence | Hydroxide, not carbonate |
| 4 | To the solution from 3 add sodium hydroxide solution | White gelatinous precipitate, insoluble in excess NaOH | Mg²⁺ (Al(OH)3 would dissolve in excess) |
| 5 | Add ammonium chloride solution to the precipitate from 4 | Precipitate dissolves | Mg(OH)2 dissolves in ammonium salts |
| 6 | To solution from 3 add ammonium chloride, then dilute ammonia (no precipitate), then disodium hydrogen phosphate; scratch the tube with a rod | White crystalline precipitate | Mg²⁺ confirmed (magnesium ammonium phosphate) |

## Result

The given sample is **magnesium hydroxide**: it is insoluble in water, alkaline, dissolves in acid without effervescence and gives the tests for magnesium.

## Precautions

1. Use small amounts of sample; excess makes precipitates hard to see.
2. In test 6 add enough ammonium chloride first so that ammonia alone gives no precipitate.
3. Scratching the inner wall helps the slow crystalline precipitate to form.
4. Use clean test tubes for each test.

## Viva questions

1. **Why does Mg(OH)2 dissolve in ammonium chloride?** NH4⁺ removes OH⁻ (forms NH3 + H2O), shifting the equilibrium so the hydroxide dissolves.
2. **How do you tell magnesium from aluminium hydroxide?** Aluminium hydroxide dissolves in excess NaOH; magnesium hydroxide does not.
3. **Why add ammonium chloride before ammonia in test 6?** To stop magnesium hydroxide precipitating with ammonia (common ion effect on OH⁻).
4. **What is the formula of the precipitate in test 6?** MgNH4PO4·6H2O.
5. **Why no effervescence in acid?** It is a hydroxide, not a carbonate.
6. **Give two uses of magnesium hydroxide.** Antacid and osmotic laxative (milk of magnesia).

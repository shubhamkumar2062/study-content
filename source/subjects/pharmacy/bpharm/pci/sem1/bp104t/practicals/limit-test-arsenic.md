# Limit test for arsenic (Gutzeit method)

**Aim:** To perform the limit test for arsenic on the given sample using the Gutzeit apparatus.

> Arsine is a very poisonous gas and mercuric chloride is toxic. Work in a fume cupboard and handle papers with forceps. Sample weight and the volume of arsenic standard come from the monograph and your lab manual; check the current IP.

## Principle

Arsenic present in the sample is converted to **arsine (AsH3)** by nascent hydrogen produced from zinc and hydrochloric acid. Pentavalent arsenic is first reduced to the trivalent state by potassium iodide and stannous chloride. Arsine passes up the tube and reacts with **mercuric chloride paper**, giving a **yellow to brown stain**. The stain from the test is compared with the stain from a standard containing a known amount of arsenic.

H3AsO4 + 2HI → H3AsO3 + I2 + H2O
I2 + SnCl2 + 2HCl → SnCl4 + 2HI
Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl2 + 2[H]
H3AsO3 + 6[H] → AsH3 ↑ + 3H2O
AsH3 + HgCl2 → yellow-brown arsenic–mercury compounds (stain)

## Apparatus

```text
           mercuric chloride paper held between
           the flat ends of two tubes (clamped)
              ════╪════
                  │  glass tube, about 6.5 mm bore
                  │  ░ cotton wool soaked in lead acetate
                  │    (traps H2S)
            ┌─────┴─────┐  rubber bung
            │           │
            │  sample + │  wide-mouthed bottle (about 120 ml)
            │  reagents │  placed in water bath at about 40 °C
            └───────────┘
```

## Requirements

Gutzeit apparatus (two sets), water bath at 40 °C; arsenic-free (AsT) reagents: stannated hydrochloric acid, potassium iodide, granulated zinc; lead acetate cotton wool; mercuric chloride paper; dilute arsenic standard solution; purified water; sample.

## Procedure

**Test**

1. Place lead acetate cotton wool loosely in the lower part of the glass tube; fit a strip of mercuric chloride paper between the flat ends at the top and clamp.
2. In the bottle dissolve the specified quantity of sample in about 50 ml of water (or as directed).
3. Add 10 ml of stannated hydrochloric acid (AsT) and 1 g of potassium iodide (AsT).
4. Add about 10 g of granulated zinc (AsT) and **immediately** fit the bung carrying the tube.
5. Keep the bottle in a water bath at about 40 °C for about 40 minutes so that hydrogen is evolved evenly.
6. Remove the mercuric chloride paper and compare at once.

**Standard**

Repeat the same steps with the specified volume of dilute arsenic standard solution in place of the sample, at the same time and temperature.

## Observations

| | Stain on mercuric chloride paper |
| --- | --- |
| Test | Yellow / brown; fainter / deeper than standard |
| Standard | Yellow-brown |

## Result

The given sample **passes / does not pass** the limit test for arsenic.

## Precautions

1. All reagents must be arsenic-free (AsT grade).
2. Fit the bung immediately after adding zinc so no arsine escapes.
3. Keep test and standard at the same temperature and time; evolution of hydrogen must be steady, not violent.
4. Lead acetate cotton must be dry enough not to wet the paper but must trap H2S.
5. Compare stains immediately; they fade in light. Handle papers with forceps.
6. Carry out in a fume cupboard.

## Viva questions

1. **Name the test used for arsenic.** The Gutzeit test.
2. **What is the role of potassium iodide and stannous chloride?** To reduce pentavalent arsenic to trivalent arsenic, which forms arsine more readily; stannous chloride also gives a steady evolution of hydrogen.
3. **Why lead acetate cotton wool?** To absorb hydrogen sulphide, which would blacken the mercuric chloride paper.
4. **What gas reacts with the paper?** Arsine (AsH3).
5. **Why 40 °C?** For a steady, controlled evolution of hydrogen.
6. **Why is arsenic tested so strictly?** It is highly toxic and cumulative.
7. **What does "AsT" mean on a reagent?** The reagent is suitable (arsenic-free) for the arsenic test.

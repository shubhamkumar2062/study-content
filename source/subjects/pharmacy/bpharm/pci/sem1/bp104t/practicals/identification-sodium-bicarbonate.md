# Identification tests for sodium bicarbonate

**Aim:** To identify the given sample as sodium bicarbonate by tests for sodium and bicarbonate, and to distinguish it from sodium carbonate.

## Principle

Sodium bicarbonate gives sodium ions (golden-yellow flame) and bicarbonate ions. Bicarbonate releases carbon dioxide with acids, which turns lime water milky. Unlike carbonate, bicarbonate gives no precipitate with magnesium sulphate in the cold (magnesium bicarbonate is soluble); on boiling it changes to carbonate and a white precipitate forms. A bicarbonate solution is only weakly alkaline, so it gives little or no pink colour with phenolphthalein, whereas carbonate gives a deep pink.

NaHCO3 + HCl → NaCl + H2O + CO2 ↑
CO2 + Ca(OH)2 → CaCO3 ↓ (milky) + H2O
2NaHCO3 (on heating) → Na2CO3 + H2O + CO2

## Requirements

Test tubes, delivery tube with cork, burner, platinum or nichrome wire; dilute hydrochloric acid, lime water, magnesium sulphate solution, phenolphthalein solution, potassium antimonate solution (or zinc uranyl acetate), sample (and sodium carbonate for comparison, if provided).

## Procedure and observations

Prepare a test solution of about 0.5 g sample in 10 ml cold water.

| No. | Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Appearance | White crystalline powder, soluble in water | – |
| 2 | Flame test: dip wire in HCl and sample, hold in flame | Persistent golden-yellow flame | Sodium |
| 3 | Test solution + potassium antimonate (or zinc uranyl acetate) | White (or yellow crystalline) precipitate | Sodium |
| 4 | Sample + dilute HCl; pass the gas into lime water | Brisk effervescence; lime water turns milky | Carbonate or bicarbonate |
| 5 | Cold test solution + magnesium sulphate solution | No precipitate in cold; white precipitate on boiling | Bicarbonate (carbonate gives precipitate in cold) |
| 6 | Cold test solution + 1 drop of phenolphthalein | No colour or only faint pink | Bicarbonate (carbonate gives deep pink) |
| 7 | Heat the dry sample in a test tube; pass gas into lime water | Gas turns lime water milky; residue is sodium carbonate | Bicarbonate decomposes on heating |

## Result

The given sample is **sodium bicarbonate**: it gives the tests for sodium and bicarbonate and differs from sodium carbonate in tests 5 and 6.

## Precautions

1. Use cold water for the solution; warm solutions lose CO2 and turn into carbonate.
2. Clean the flame wire with HCl until it gives no colour before the flame test.
3. Keep the delivery tube dipping into lime water only while gas is passing, to avoid suck-back.
4. Excess CO2 makes the milky lime water clear again (soluble calcium bicarbonate).

## Viva questions

1. **How do you distinguish bicarbonate from carbonate?** Bicarbonate gives no precipitate with MgSO4 in the cold and little colour with phenolphthalein; carbonate gives both.
2. **Why does lime water turn milky?** CO2 forms insoluble calcium carbonate.
3. **Why does excess CO2 clear the milkiness?** Calcium carbonate changes to soluble calcium bicarbonate.
4. **What happens when sodium bicarbonate is heated?** It loses CO2 and water and becomes sodium carbonate.
5. **What colour does sodium give in the flame?** Persistent golden yellow.
6. **Give two uses of sodium bicarbonate.** Antacid and systemic alkaliser in acidosis.

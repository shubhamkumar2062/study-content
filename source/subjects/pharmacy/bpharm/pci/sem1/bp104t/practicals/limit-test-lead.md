# Limit test for lead

**Aim:** To perform the limit test for lead on the given sample by the dithizone method.

> Potassium cyanide is extremely poisonous. Use it only under direct supervision, never pipette by mouth, never let it meet acid (gives hydrogen cyanide gas), and wash hands after use. Volumes vary by monograph and IP edition; follow your lab manual and check the current IP.

## Principle

Lead reacts with **dithizone** (diphenylthiocarbazone) in a mildly alkaline solution to form **lead dithizonate**, which is red and dissolves in chloroform. Dithizone itself is green in chloroform. The intensity of the red colour extracted from the test is compared with that from a standard lead solution treated in the same way.

Pb²⁺ + 2 (dithizone, HDz) → Pb(Dz)2 (red, in chloroform) + 2H⁺

| Reagent | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Ammonium citrate | Keeps metal ions in solution in alkaline medium |
| Hydroxylamine hydrochloride | Reduces ferric iron, which would otherwise oxidise dithizone |
| Potassium cyanide | Complexes copper, zinc, nickel and other metals so they do not react with dithizone |
| Ammonia | Makes the medium alkaline (pH about 9–10) |
| Dithizone in chloroform | Extracting and colour-forming reagent |

## Requirements

Separating funnels (two), Nessler cylinders or matched tubes, pipettes; ammonium citrate solution, hydroxylamine hydrochloride solution, potassium cyanide solution, dilute ammonia, dithizone extraction solution (in chloroform), chloroform, dilute nitric acid, standard lead solution, phenol red indicator (if specified), sample.

## Procedure

**Test**

1. Dissolve the specified quantity of sample in water (or as directed) and transfer to a separating funnel.
2. Add ammonium citrate solution and hydroxylamine hydrochloride solution.
3. Add a few drops of phenol red and make alkaline with dilute ammonia (red colour).
4. Add potassium cyanide solution and mix.
5. Extract with successive small portions of dithizone extraction solution, shaking each for about 30 seconds and draining the chloroform layer into a second separating funnel, until the dithizone layer stays green.
6. Shake the combined chloroform extracts with dilute nitric acid to transfer the lead back into the acid layer; discard the chloroform.
7. To the acid extract add a measured volume of standard dithizone solution and ammonia–cyanide solution, shake, and collect the chloroform layer for comparison.

**Standard**

Repeat steps 2–7 with the specified volume of **standard lead solution** in place of the sample.

**Comparison.** Compare the colours of the two chloroform layers in matched tubes against a white background.

## Observations

| | Colour of chloroform layer |
| --- | --- |
| Test | Red / violet, lighter / deeper than standard |
| Standard | Red / violet |

## Result

The given sample **passes / does not pass** the limit test for lead.

## Precautions

1. Handle potassium cyanide with extreme care and keep it away from acids.
2. Use lead-free (borosilicate) glassware rinsed with dilute nitric acid.
3. Dithizone solution is light-sensitive; keep it in amber bottles and prepare fresh.
4. Shake for the same time and use the same volumes for test and standard.
5. Separate the layers cleanly without carrying over the aqueous layer.

## Viva questions

1. **What colour is dithizone in chloroform, and lead dithizonate?** Dithizone green; lead dithizonate red.
2. **Why is potassium cyanide added?** To mask other metals (copper, zinc and others) by forming stable cyanide complexes.
3. **Why hydroxylamine hydrochloride?** To reduce ferric iron, which would oxidise dithizone.
4. **Why ammonium citrate?** To keep metals in solution when the medium is made alkaline.
5. **Why is lead a dangerous impurity?** It is a cumulative poison affecting blood, nerves and kidneys.
6. **Where does lead come from in pharmaceuticals?** Raw materials, lead-lined plant, solder, pipes and glass.

# Identification tests for calcium gluconate

**Aim:** To identify the given sample as calcium gluconate by tests for calcium and gluconate.

## Principle

Calcium gluconate dissolves in hot water to give calcium ions and gluconate ions. Calcium is identified by a white precipitate of calcium oxalate with ammonium oxalate (insoluble in acetic acid, soluble in hydrochloric acid) and by a brick-red flame. Gluconate, being an organic hydroxy acid salt, chars on heating with the smell of burnt sugar, and gluconic acid forms a crystalline **phenylhydrazide** when heated with phenylhydrazine in acetic acid.

Ca²⁺ + (NH4)2C2O4 → CaC2O4 ↓ (white) + 2NH4⁺

## Requirements

Test tubes, water bath, burner, nichrome or platinum wire, glass rod; ammonium oxalate solution, dilute acetic acid, dilute hydrochloric acid, glacial acetic acid, phenylhydrazine, purified water, sample.

## Procedure and observations

Prepare a test solution by dissolving about 0.5 g of the sample in 10 ml of hot water and cooling.

| No. | Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Appearance and solubility | White crystalline granules or powder; dissolves slowly in cold water, readily in hot water | – |
| 2 | Flame test with the sample moistened with HCl | Brick-red flame | Calcium |
| 3 | Test solution + ammonium oxalate solution | White precipitate | Calcium |
| 4 | Divide precipitate from 3: add dilute acetic acid to one part and dilute HCl to the other | Insoluble in acetic acid; soluble in HCl | Calcium oxalate confirmed |
| 5 | Heat a little dry sample in a dry test tube | Chars, swells and smells of burnt sugar | Organic (sugar acid) part |
| 6 | Dissolve about 0.5 g in 5 ml warm water with 0.5–1 ml glacial acetic acid; add 1 ml phenylhydrazine; heat on a water bath for about 30 minutes, cool and scratch | Crystals separate | Gluconate (gluconic acid phenylhydrazide) |

## Result

The given sample is **calcium gluconate**: it gives the tests for calcium and for gluconate.

## Precautions

1. Use hot water to dissolve the sample completely, then cool.
2. Clean the flame wire well; sodium contamination masks the brick-red colour.
3. Phenylhydrazine is toxic and absorbed through the skin: use gloves and a fume cupboard.
4. Allow enough time for the phenylhydrazide crystals to form.

## Viva questions

1. **Which reagent identifies calcium?** Ammonium oxalate (white precipitate of calcium oxalate).
2. **Why is calcium oxalate insoluble in acetic acid but soluble in HCl?** Oxalic acid is stronger than acetic acid, so acetic acid cannot dissolve the oxalate, but a strong acid can.
3. **What is the flame colour of calcium?** Brick red.
4. **How is calcium gluconate prepared?** By neutralising gluconic acid (from oxidation of glucose) with calcium carbonate.
5. **How is calcium gluconate assayed?** Complexometric titration with disodium edetate using mordant black II.
6. **Why is calcium gluconate preferred over calcium chloride for injection?** It is far less irritant to veins and tissues.

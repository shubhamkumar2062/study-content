# Preparation of boric acid

**Aim:** To prepare boric acid from borax and report the practical yield.

## Principle

Borax (sodium tetraborate decahydrate) is the sodium salt of a very weak acid. When a strong acid such as hydrochloric acid (or sulphuric acid) is added to a hot concentrated solution of borax, the weak boric acid is set free. Boric acid is much less soluble in cold water than in hot water, so it crystallises on cooling, while sodium chloride stays in solution.

Na2B4O7·10H2O + 2HCl → 4H3BO3 + 2NaCl + 5H2O

Molecular weights: borax decahydrate 381.4; boric acid 61.83. So 381.4 g of borax gives 4 × 61.83 = 247.3 g of boric acid.

## Requirements

Beakers (250 ml), glass rod, tripod, wire gauze, burner, measuring cylinder, Buchner funnel or ordinary funnel with filter paper, watch glass, ice bath, methyl orange solution (or blue litmus); borax 10 g, concentrated hydrochloric acid, purified water.

## Procedure

1. Weigh 10 g of borax and dissolve it in about 40 ml of boiling purified water in a beaker.
2. While hot, add concentrated hydrochloric acid slowly with stirring until the solution is distinctly acidic to methyl orange (a drop of solution taken out on a glass rod turns methyl orange red). About 4–5 ml is usually enough; add a slight excess.
3. Allow the solution to cool slowly to room temperature, then cool in an ice bath. Shiny, pearly flakes of boric acid separate.
4. Filter the crystals and wash with a small volume of ice-cold water to remove sodium chloride and acid.
5. Dry the crystals on filter paper or in an oven below 50 °C (boric acid loses water at higher temperatures).
6. Weigh the dried product and calculate the yield.

## Observations and calculation

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Weight of borax taken | 10.0 g |
| Theoretical yield of boric acid | 10.0 × 247.3 / 381.4 = 6.48 g |
| Weight of product obtained | ____ g |
| Percentage yield | (practical ÷ theoretical) × 100 |

**Worked example.** Product = 4.86 g → % yield = 4.86 / 6.48 × 100 = **75%**. (Part of the boric acid remains dissolved in the mother liquor, so yields below 100% are expected.)

**Identity checks.** The product is a weak acid (just turns blue litmus red), and its alcoholic solution burns with a green-edged flame.

## Precautions

1. Add concentrated HCl slowly and carefully, in a fume cupboard.
2. Cool slowly first so that larger, purer crystals form.
3. Wash with only a little ice-cold water; boric acid is fairly soluble in warm water.
4. Do not dry above about 50 °C.

## Viva questions

1. **Why does boric acid crystallise on cooling?** Its solubility falls sharply in cold water.
2. **Why is the solution made acidic to methyl orange?** To make sure all the borate is converted to boric acid.
3. **Is boric acid a strong or weak acid?** A very weak acid (it accepts OH⁻ from water rather than donating H⁺).
4. **How is boric acid assayed?** With NaOH after adding glycerin or mannitol, which forms a stronger complex acid.
5. **Give its uses.** Weak antiseptic in eye washes, dusting powder, buffer component.
6. **Why should boric acid not be used on infants' skin or large wounds?** It is absorbed and is toxic.

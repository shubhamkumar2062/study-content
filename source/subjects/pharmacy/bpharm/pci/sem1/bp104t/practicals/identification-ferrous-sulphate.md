# Identification tests for ferrous sulphate

**Aim:** To identify the given sample as ferrous sulphate by tests for the ferrous ion and the sulphate ion.

## Principle

Ferrous sulphate dissolves in water to give pale green Fe²⁺ ions and sulphate ions. Fe²⁺ gives a dark blue precipitate with potassium ferricyanide and a greenish hydroxide that turns brown in air. Sulphate gives a white precipitate with barium chloride that does not dissolve in hydrochloric acid.

3Fe²⁺ + 2[Fe(CN)6]³⁻ → Fe3[Fe(CN)6]2 ↓ (dark blue, Turnbull's blue)
FeSO4 + 2NaOH → Fe(OH)2 ↓ (dirty green) + Na2SO4; 4Fe(OH)2 + O2 + 2H2O → 4Fe(OH)3 (brown)
SO4²⁻ + BaCl2 → BaSO4 ↓ (white) + 2Cl⁻

## Requirements

Test tubes; freshly boiled and cooled water, dilute sulphuric acid, potassium ferricyanide solution, potassium ferrocyanide solution, sodium hydroxide solution, potassium thiocyanate solution, barium chloride solution, dilute hydrochloric acid, lead acetate solution, ammonium acetate solution, sample.

## Procedure and observations

Prepare a test solution by dissolving about 0.5 g of the sample in 10 ml of freshly boiled and cooled water with a drop of dilute sulphuric acid.

| No. | Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Appearance | Pale bluish-green crystals; brownish spots if oxidised | Ferrous salt |
| 2 | Test solution + potassium ferricyanide | Dark blue precipitate | Fe²⁺ |
| 3 | Test solution + potassium ferrocyanide | White to pale blue precipitate that turns blue on standing (Fe³⁺ would give an immediate deep blue) | Fe²⁺ |
| 4 | Test solution + sodium hydroxide | Dirty green precipitate turning reddish-brown at the surface in air | Fe²⁺ |
| 5 | Test solution + potassium thiocyanate | No red colour, or only faint pink | Fe²⁺ (deep red would show much Fe³⁺ impurity) |
| 6 | Test solution + barium chloride, then dilute HCl | White precipitate, insoluble in HCl | Sulphate |
| 7 | Test solution + lead acetate | White precipitate, soluble in ammonium acetate | Sulphate |

## Result

The given sample is **ferrous sulphate**: it gives the tests for ferrous iron and for sulphate.

## Precautions

1. Use freshly boiled and cooled water and a little acid to avoid oxidation to ferric salt.
2. Prepare the test solution just before use.
3. Add reagents dropwise and observe colour changes immediately.
4. Do not mix up ferricyanide (for Fe²⁺) and ferrocyanide (for Fe³⁺).

## Viva questions

1. **Which reagent is specific for Fe²⁺?** Potassium ferricyanide (dark blue precipitate).
2. **Why does the green hydroxide turn brown?** Ferrous hydroxide is oxidised by air to ferric hydroxide.
3. **Why is the sample dissolved with a little acid?** To stop hydrolysis and oxidation.
4. **How do you detect ferric impurity in ferrous sulphate?** A deep red colour with potassium thiocyanate.
5. **Why is ferrous sulphate stored in well-closed containers?** In moist air it oxidises to brown basic ferric sulphate.
6. **What is the common name of ferrous sulphate?** Green vitriol.

# Limit test for iron

**Aim:** To perform the limit test for iron on the given sample.

> Volumes follow the older IP general method used in most college manuals. Use the sample weight from the monograph or your lab manual; check the current IP.

## Principle

Iron (ferric or ferrous) reacts with **thioglycollic acid** in a solution made alkaline with ammonia, in the presence of **citric acid**, to give a pink to purple colour of ferrous thioglycollate. Thioglycollic acid first reduces Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺. Citric acid forms a soluble complex with iron so that it is not precipitated as iron hydroxide when ammonia is added. The colour of the test is compared with that of a standard containing a known amount of iron.

Fe³⁺ → (reduced by thioglycollic acid) → Fe²⁺
Fe²⁺ + 2 HSCH2COOH → Fe(SCH2COO)2 (purple, in alkaline medium) + 2H⁺

## Requirements

Nessler cylinders (50 ml), pipettes, glass rods; 20% w/v iron-free citric acid solution, thioglycollic acid, iron-free ammonia solution (strong ammonia), standard iron solution (ferric ammonium sulphate in dilute acid; older IP: 20 µg Fe per ml), purified water, sample.

## Procedure

| Test | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Dissolve the specified quantity of sample in 40 ml water in a Nessler cylinder. | 1. Dilute 2 ml of standard iron solution to 40 ml with water in a Nessler cylinder. |
| 2. Add 2 ml of 20% w/v iron-free citric acid. | 2. Add 2 ml of 20% w/v iron-free citric acid. |
| 3. Add 0.1 ml of thioglycollic acid, mix. | 3. Add 0.1 ml of thioglycollic acid, mix. |
| 4. Make alkaline with iron-free ammonia solution. | 4. Make alkaline with iron-free ammonia solution. |
| 5. Dilute to 50 ml with water and keep aside 5 minutes. | 5. Dilute to 50 ml with water and keep aside 5 minutes. |

View the colour **down** the cylinders against a white background.

## Observations

| | Colour |
| --- | --- |
| Test | Pale pink / purple, lighter / deeper than standard |
| Standard | Pink-purple |

**Calculation of the limit.** 2 ml of standard iron solution (20 µg Fe/ml) contains 40 µg of iron. For a 1 g sample this corresponds to 40 ppm of iron; for a 2 g sample, 20 ppm.

## Result

The given sample **passes / does not pass** the limit test for iron.

## Precautions

1. Use iron-free reagents (citric acid, ammonia) and clean glassware.
2. Add reagents in the same order to test and standard.
3. The colour develops only in alkaline solution; check alkalinity with litmus.
4. Compare after the same time (about 5 minutes); the colour can fade on standing in air.
5. Oxidising agents in the sample destroy the colour; report if the sample is an oxidising agent.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the role of citric acid?** It complexes iron so iron hydroxide does not precipitate in ammonia.
2. **What is the role of thioglycollic acid?** It reduces Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺ and forms the coloured ferrous thioglycollate.
3. **Why is ammonia added?** The purple colour forms only in alkaline medium.
4. **Why view down the cylinder?** Colour is judged through the full depth of liquid; turbidity is viewed across.
5. **Why is iron a harmful impurity?** It catalyses oxidation of drugs, causes discolouration and reduces shelf life.
6. **What is the standard iron solution prepared from?** Ferric ammonium sulphate dissolved in water with a little acid.

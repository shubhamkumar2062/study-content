# Limit test for heavy metals

**Aim:** To perform the limit test for heavy metals on the given sample.

> The IP describes more than one method (for substances that dissolve clearly in water, for those that must first be ignited, and for those soluble in alkali). The method below is for a substance that gives a clear, colourless solution in water. Use the volumes and the lead standard given in the monograph and your lab manual; check the current IP.

## Principle

Heavy metals (lead, copper, mercury, bismuth, antimony, tin, cadmium and others) form coloured, dark brown to black sulphides with hydrogen sulphide in weakly acid solution. **Thioacetamide** in an **acetate buffer of pH 3.5** releases hydrogen sulphide slowly and evenly:

CH3CSNH2 + H2O → CH3CONH2 + H2S
M²⁺ + H2S → MS ↓ (brown–black) + 2H⁺

The colour of the test is compared with a standard made from **standard lead solution**; the result is expressed as parts per million (ppm) of lead. At pH 3.5, most heavy metals precipitate while iron, zinc and other light metals interfere very little. Older methods used hydrogen sulphide solution directly.

## Requirements

Nessler cylinders (50 ml), pipettes; acetate buffer pH 3.5, thioacetamide reagent, dilute acetic acid, dilute ammonia, standard lead solution (made from lead nitrate, diluted as directed), purified water, sample.

## Procedure

| Test | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Dissolve the specified quantity of sample in water (about 25 ml) in a Nessler cylinder. | 1. Take the specified volume of standard lead solution and dilute to about 25 ml. |
| 2. Adjust to about pH 3–4 with dilute acetic acid or dilute ammonia if needed. | 2. Adjust pH in the same way. |
| 3. Add 2 ml of acetate buffer pH 3.5. | 3. Add 2 ml of acetate buffer pH 3.5. |
| 4. Add 1.2 ml of thioacetamide reagent, dilute to 50 ml and mix. | 4. Add 1.2 ml of thioacetamide reagent, dilute to 50 ml and mix. |
| 5. Keep aside for 2 minutes. | 5. Keep aside for 2 minutes. |

View the colour **down** the cylinders against a white background.

## Observations

| | Colour |
| --- | --- |
| Test | Brownish, lighter / darker than standard |
| Standard | Brownish |

**Calculation of the limit.** ppm limit = (µg of lead in the standard) ÷ (g of sample taken). For example, a standard containing 20 µg of lead with a 1 g sample corresponds to a limit of 20 ppm.

## Result

The given sample **passes / does not pass** the limit test for heavy metals (limit expressed as ppm of lead).

## Precautions

1. Adjust both solutions to the same pH; sulphide precipitation depends on pH.
2. Prepare thioacetamide reagent fresh as directed.
3. Use lead-free glassware and purified water.
4. Coloured samples need the method with ignition or a modified procedure.
5. Work in a ventilated area; hydrogen sulphide is toxic and smells of rotten eggs.

## Viva questions

1. **What are heavy metals in this test?** Metals that give coloured sulphides at pH 3.5, such as lead, copper, mercury, bismuth and antimony.
2. **Why is thioacetamide preferred to H2S?** It is safer, convenient, and releases H2S slowly and evenly.
3. **Why is pH 3.5 used?** Heavy metal sulphides precipitate while iron and zinc interfere little.
4. **In what terms is the limit expressed?** As ppm of lead.
5. **Why does the IP have more than one method?** Samples differ: some dissolve clearly in water, some must be ignited to destroy organic matter, some dissolve only in alkali.
6. **Why are heavy metals harmful?** They are toxic, accumulate in the body and inhibit enzymes by binding –SH groups.

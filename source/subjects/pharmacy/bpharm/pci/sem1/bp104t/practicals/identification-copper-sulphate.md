# Identification tests for copper sulphate

**Aim:** To identify the given sample as copper sulphate by tests for the cupric ion, the sulphate ion and water of crystallisation.

## Principle

Copper sulphate pentahydrate gives blue cupric (Cu²⁺) ions and sulphate ions in water. Cu²⁺ forms a pale blue precipitate with ammonia that dissolves in excess to give the deep blue tetrammine complex; a reddish-brown precipitate with potassium ferrocyanide; and with potassium iodide it gives white cuprous iodide and free iodine. Sulphate gives white barium sulphate. Heating removes the water of crystallisation and the blue crystals turn white.

CuSO4 + 2NH4OH → Cu(OH)2 ↓ (pale blue) + (NH4)2SO4
Cu(OH)2 + 4NH3 → [Cu(NH3)4]²⁺ (deep blue) + 2OH⁻
2CuSO4 + K4[Fe(CN)6] → Cu2[Fe(CN)6] ↓ (chocolate brown) + 2K2SO4
2CuSO4 + 4KI → 2CuI ↓ (white) + I2 + 2K2SO4

## Requirements

Test tubes, burner, iron nail (clean); dilute ammonia, potassium ferrocyanide solution, potassium iodide solution, sodium thiosulphate solution, barium chloride solution, dilute hydrochloric acid, sample.

## Procedure and observations

Prepare a test solution of about 0.5 g sample in 10 ml water.

| No. | Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Appearance | Blue crystals or blue crystalline powder; solution blue | Cupric salt |
| 2 | Heat a few crystals in a dry test tube | Water droplets on the cooler part; crystals turn white; white residue turns blue again with a drop of water | Water of crystallisation |
| 3 | Test solution + dilute ammonia dropwise, then in excess | Pale blue precipitate dissolving to a deep blue solution | Cu²⁺ |
| 4 | Test solution + potassium ferrocyanide | Reddish-brown (chocolate) precipitate | Cu²⁺ |
| 5 | Test solution + potassium iodide; then add sodium thiosulphate dropwise | Brown solution with precipitate; thiosulphate removes the brown iodine and leaves a white precipitate of CuI | Cu²⁺ |
| 6 | Place a clean iron nail in the test solution for a few minutes | Reddish coating of copper on the nail | Cu²⁺ (displaced by iron) |
| 7 | Test solution + barium chloride, then dilute HCl | White precipitate, insoluble in HCl | Sulphate |

## Result

The given sample is **copper sulphate (pentahydrate)**: it gives the tests for copper, sulphate and water of crystallisation.

## Precautions

1. Add ammonia slowly so the first pale blue precipitate is seen before it dissolves.
2. Heat the crystals gently in a dry tube.
3. Copper sulphate is toxic: do not taste it; wash hands after use.
4. Clean the nail with sandpaper before test 6.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the deep blue complex in test 3?** Tetrammine copper(II) ion, [Cu(NH3)4]²⁺.
2. **Why does the iron nail get a red coating?** Iron is more reactive and displaces copper: Fe + CuSO4 → FeSO4 + Cu.
3. **What is the white powder left after heating?** Anhydrous copper sulphate; it is used to test for water.
4. **On which reaction is the assay of copper sulphate based?** Liberation of iodine from KI by Cu²⁺, titrated with sodium thiosulphate.
5. **What is the common name of copper sulphate?** Blue vitriol.
6. **Give two uses.** Fungicide/algicide and formerly an emetic; reagent in Fehling's and Benedict's solutions.

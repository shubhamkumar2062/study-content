# Swelling power of bentonite

**Aim:** To determine the swelling power of the given sample of bentonite.

> The sample weight, the number of portions, the standing time and the minimum sediment volume are fixed by the IP monograph. Follow the current IP and your lab manual for these values; the method below shows the usual steps.

## Principle

Bentonite is a native colloidal hydrated aluminium silicate made mainly of the clay mineral **montmorillonite**. Its thin plate-like layers take water in between them, so the particles swell to many times their dry volume and form a colloidal suspension or gel. This swelling is what makes bentonite a good suspending agent and stabiliser. The test measures how much the powder swells: a weighed quantity is added in small portions to water in a graduated cylinder and, after standing, the **apparent volume of the swollen sediment** is read. A good grade gives a large sediment volume; a poor grade (or an adulterated one) swells little.

## Requirements

100 ml glass-stoppered graduated cylinder, spatula, weighing balance, purified water, bentonite sample.

## Procedure

1. Put 100 ml of purified water in a 100 ml stoppered graduated cylinder.
2. Weigh the specified quantity of bentonite (commonly 2 g).
3. Add the bentonite in about 20 small portions, sprinkling each portion on the surface of the water and allowing it to settle (wet and sink) completely before adding the next. Do not shake or stir.
4. When all the powder has been added, stopper the cylinder and leave it undisturbed for the time stated in the IP.
5. Read the apparent volume (ml) occupied by the swollen sediment at the bottom.
6. Repeat with a second sample if required and take the mean.

**Calculation (optional).** Swelling ratio = sediment volume ÷ initial bulk volume of the dry powder (measured separately by pouring the same weight into a dry measuring cylinder). For example, if 2 g of dry powder occupies 2 ml and the swollen sediment is 26 ml, the powder has swollen about 13 times.

## Result

The apparent volume of the sediment of 2 g of the given bentonite is ____ ml. The sample **complies / does not comply** with the IP requirement for swelling power.

## Precautions

1. Add the powder in small portions and let each settle; adding all at once forms lumps that do not swell.
2. Do not shake or stir the cylinder at any stage.
3. Keep the cylinder on a level surface, free from vibration.
4. Use purified water; electrolytes in tap water reduce swelling.
5. Read the volume at eye level.

## Viva questions

1. **What is bentonite chemically?** A native colloidal hydrated aluminium silicate, mainly montmorillonite.
2. **Why does bentonite swell?** Water enters between its layered silicate sheets and hydrates the surfaces.
3. **Why are portions added slowly?** To wet each particle fully and avoid lump formation.
4. **What is bentonite used for?** Suspending agent (calamine lotion), emulsion stabiliser, base for ointments, clarifying agent, adsorbent.
5. **What does low swelling power indicate?** A poor or adulterated sample with little suspending ability.
6. **How do electrolytes affect bentonite gels?** They reduce swelling and can make the suspension flocculate.

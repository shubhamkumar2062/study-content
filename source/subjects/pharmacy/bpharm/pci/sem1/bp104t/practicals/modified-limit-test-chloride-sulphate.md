# Modified limit test for chlorides and sulphates

**Aim:** To perform the modified limit tests for chloride and sulphate on a sample that interferes with the general test (for example sodium bicarbonate, or potassium permanganate as directed).

> Use the sample weight and volumes given in your lab manual or the monograph; the older IP general method is shown here. Check the current IP.

## Principle

The reactions are the same as in the general limit tests:

Cl⁻ + AgNO3 → AgCl ↓ (opalescence)        SO4²⁻ + BaCl2 → BaSO4 ↓ (turbidity)

Some substances spoil the comparison and must be treated first:

| Interference | Example | Modification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carbonate / bicarbonate reacts with the acid, uses it up and gives effervescence | Sodium bicarbonate | Extra acid is added (nitric acid for chloride, hydrochloric acid for sulphate) and the solution is warmed or allowed to stand until effervescence stops, so CO2 is expelled before the reagent is added |
| Strongly coloured substance hides the turbidity | Potassium permanganate | The solution is decolourised by warming with a little alcohol (ethanol); permanganate is reduced to brown manganese dioxide, which is filtered off, and the colourless filtrate is tested |

The standard is given the same extra acid (or same treatment), so that only the impurity differs between test and standard.

**Reactions with bicarbonate**

NaHCO3 + HNO3 → NaNO3 + H2O + CO2
NaHCO3 + HCl → NaCl + H2O + CO2 (for this reason, nitric acid, not HCl, is used for the chloride test)

## Requirements

Nessler cylinders, beakers, funnel and filter paper, water bath, glass rods; dilute nitric acid, dilute hydrochloric acid, 0.1 M silver nitrate, barium sulphate reagent, standard sodium chloride solution, standard potassium sulphate solution, ethanol (for permanganate), sample.

## Procedure

**A. Modified chloride test (sodium bicarbonate)**

1. Dissolve the specified quantity of sodium bicarbonate in water in a beaker.
2. Add dilute nitric acid gradually until effervescence stops, then the extra volume used in the general test (so that the solution is clearly acidic). Warm gently to drive off CO2; cool.
3. Transfer to a Nessler cylinder, dilute to 50 ml, add 1 ml of 0.1 M silver nitrate, stir and keep 5 minutes.
4. Prepare the standard with 1 ml of standard sodium chloride solution and the **same total volume of dilute nitric acid**, dilute to 50 ml, add silver nitrate and keep 5 minutes.
5. Compare opalescence.

**B. Modified sulphate test (sodium bicarbonate)**

1. Dissolve the sample in water, add dilute hydrochloric acid until effervescence stops, plus the normal 2 ml extra; warm to remove CO2 and cool.
2. Transfer to a Nessler cylinder, dilute to 45 ml, add 5 ml barium sulphate reagent, stir, keep 5 minutes.
3. Prepare the standard with 1 ml of standard potassium sulphate solution and the same total volume of hydrochloric acid.
4. Compare turbidity.

**C. Coloured substance (potassium permanganate), outline**

1. Dissolve the sample in water, add a small quantity of ethanol and warm on a water bath until the purple colour disappears.
2. Filter off the brown manganese dioxide; wash the filter with a little water.
3. Use the clear filtrate for the chloride or sulphate test as above. Treat the standard with the same quantity of ethanol.

## Observations

| Test | Pre-treatment done | Test vs standard | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chloride | Extra HNO3, CO2 expelled | Less / more opalescence | Passes / fails |
| Sulphate | Extra HCl, CO2 expelled | Less / more turbidity | Passes / fails |

## Result

The given sample **passes / does not pass** the modified limit tests for chloride and sulphate.

## Precautions

1. Add acid slowly to avoid frothing over.
2. Make sure all CO2 is removed before adding the reagent; bubbles disturb the turbidity.
3. Cool solutions to room temperature before comparison.
4. Give the standard exactly the same quantity of acid or alcohol as the test.
5. Filter completely clear after decolourising; any brown particles give false turbidity.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is a limit test modified?** Because the sample itself interferes (by reacting with the acid, by its colour, or by insolubility), so the interference is removed first.
2. **Why is extra acid needed for sodium bicarbonate?** Bicarbonate neutralises the acid and evolves CO2; the medium must still be acidic for the test.
3. **Why must CO2 be removed?** Gas bubbles give false turbidity and disturb comparison.
4. **How is potassium permanganate made colourless?** By reducing it with alcohol on warming and filtering off the manganese dioxide formed.
5. **Why is the standard treated in the same way?** So that any effect of the added reagents is equal in both cylinders.
6. **Name a substance needing a modified chloride test.** Sodium bicarbonate or potassium permanganate.

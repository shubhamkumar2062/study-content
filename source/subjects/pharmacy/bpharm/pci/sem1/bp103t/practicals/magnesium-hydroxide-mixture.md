# Magnesium Hydroxide Mixture

**Aim:** To prepare and dispense 100 ml of Magnesium Hydroxide Mixture.

> Take the official quantities from your laboratory manual. The calculation below shows the reacting amounts.

## Principle

Magnesium hydroxide is an **antacid** (small doses) and a **saline laxative** (larger doses). It is prepared freshly by **double decomposition** (a precipitation method of making a suspension):

MgSO4·7H2O + 2NaOH → Mg(OH)2↓ + Na2SO4 + 7H2O

Precipitation gives very fine, gelatinous particles that stay suspended well. The by-product, sodium sulphate, is a laxative with a bitter taste and is removed by **washing** the precipitate. Adding the magnesium sulphate solution slowly to the alkali gives a finer precipitate.

## Ingredients and roles

| Ingredient | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Magnesium sulphate | Source of magnesium |
| Sodium hydroxide | Precipitant |
| Light magnesium oxide (in some formulas) | Hydrates to more magnesium hydroxide |
| Preservative | Prevents microbial growth |
| Purified water | Vehicle |

## Requirements

Magnesium sulphate, sodium hydroxide, purified water, beakers, glass rod, muslin or filter, measuring cylinder, wide-mouthed bottle.

## Procedure

1. Dissolve the magnesium sulphate in part of the water.
2. Dissolve the sodium hydroxide in another portion of water (the solution warms; cool it).
3. Add the magnesium sulphate solution **slowly** to the sodium hydroxide solution with constant stirring; a white gelatinous precipitate forms.
4. Allow to settle; decant the clear liquid; wash the precipitate several times with water (or on muslin) to remove sodium sulphate (test washings with barium chloride until no white precipitate of barium sulphate forms).
5. Transfer the washed precipitate to a measuring cylinder, add preservative and make up to 100 ml with water. Shake well.

## Observations and calculations

White, opaque, smooth suspension.

Molecular weights: MgSO4·7H2O 246.5; NaOH 40; Mg(OH)2 58.3.
For 10 g magnesium sulphate: NaOH needed = 10 × 80 ÷ 246.5 = **3.25 g**; Mg(OH)2 formed = 10 × 58.3 ÷ 246.5 = **2.37 g**.

## Result

100 ml of Magnesium Hydroxide Mixture was prepared and dispensed.

## Label

Magnesium Hydroxide Mixture, 100 ml. **Shake well before use.** Dose: as directed by the physician. Store in a cool place; do not freeze.

## Precautions

1. Add magnesium sulphate solution slowly to the alkali with stirring.
2. Wash out the sodium sulphate thoroughly.
3. Handle sodium hydroxide with care.

## Viva questions

1. **Method of preparation?** Double decomposition (precipitation).
2. **Why is sodium sulphate removed?** It is a laxative by-product with a bitter taste.
3. **How do you test the washings?** Barium chloride gives a white precipitate while sulphate is still present.
4. **Common name?** Milk of magnesia.
5. **Why not freeze it?** Freezing damages the gel structure and causes caking.

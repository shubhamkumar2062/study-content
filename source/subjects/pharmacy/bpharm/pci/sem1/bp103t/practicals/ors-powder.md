# Oral Rehydration Salts (WHO formula)

**Aim:** To prepare and dispense ORS powder (WHO reduced-osmolarity formula) for 1 litre of solution.

## Principle

Oral rehydration salts replace water and electrolytes lost in **diarrhoea and dehydration**. Glucose and sodium are absorbed together in the small intestine by a co-transport mechanism, which carries water with them, even during diarrhoea. The current WHO formula has a **reduced osmolarity (245 mOsm/L)**, which reduces stool output and vomiting compared with the older formula. Trisodium citrate corrects acidosis and is more stable in packets than bicarbonate.

## Formula (for 1 litre of solution)

| Ingredient | Quantity | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sodium chloride | 2.6 g | Sodium and chloride replacement |
| Glucose, anhydrous | 13.5 g | Promotes sodium and water absorption, energy |
| Potassium chloride | 1.5 g | Potassium replacement |
| Trisodium citrate dihydrate | 2.9 g | Corrects acidosis (alkalinising) |
| **Total** | **20.5 g** | |

## Requirements

Sodium chloride, anhydrous glucose, potassium chloride, trisodium citrate dihydrate, mortar and pestle, sieve, balance, butter paper, laminated sachets or airtight container.

## Procedure

1. Dry the ingredients if needed and pass each through a sieve.
2. Weigh each ingredient accurately.
3. Mix them by geometric dilution in a dry mortar, starting with the smaller quantities (potassium chloride, sodium chloride, citrate) and adding glucose in portions.
4. Pack 20.5 g in an airtight, moisture-proof sachet.

## Observations and calculations

White, crystalline, free-flowing powder.

Composition per litre (mmol/L): sodium 75, chloride 65, potassium 20, citrate 10, glucose 75; total osmolarity **245 mOsm/L**.
Check for sodium: NaCl 2.6 ÷ 58.44 = 44.5 mmol; citrate 2.9 ÷ 294.1 = 9.86 mmol × 3 Na = 29.6 mmol; total about **74–75 mmol** sodium.
Glucose: 13.5 ÷ 180 = **75 mmol**.

## Result

ORS powder (WHO formula) for 1 litre was prepared and dispensed.

## Label

Oral Rehydration Salts, 20.5 g. Dissolve the whole packet in 1 litre of clean drinking water (boiled and cooled). Use within 24 hours. Do not boil the solution. Store in a dry place.

## Precautions

1. Use anhydrous glucose and dry salts; the powder is hygroscopic.
2. Mix thoroughly for uniform composition.
3. Pack in moisture-proof sachets.
4. Tell the patient to dissolve the full packet in exactly 1 litre of water.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is glucose included?** It drives sodium and water absorption by co-transport.
2. **Osmolarity of the WHO reduced formula?** 245 mOsm/L.
3. **Why citrate rather than bicarbonate?** Citrate gives a more stable powder and still corrects acidosis.
4. **Why not dissolve in less water?** A stronger solution is hypertonic and can worsen diarrhoea.
5. **How long can the solution be used?** Within 24 hours.

# Divided Powders

**Aim:** To prepare and dispense 6 divided powders, each containing 100 mg of a drug, made up to 300 mg with lactose.

> Use the drug named in your laboratory manual (for example aspirin or another simple drug). The method and calculation are the same.

## Principle

**Divided powders** (chartulae) are individual doses of powder wrapped separately in paper, so each packet gives an accurate dose. The drug is mixed with an inert diluent (lactose) by **geometric dilution** to make the mixture uniform, weighed into equal portions and folded in powder papers. A little extra is prepared to allow for losses on the mortar and paper.

## Formula and calculation

Prepare for **7** powders (one extra) and dispense 6.

| Ingredient | Per powder | For 7 powders | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Drug | 100 mg | 700 mg | Active ingredient |
| Lactose | 200 mg | 1400 mg | Diluent |
| **Total** | **300 mg** | **2100 mg** | |

Each packet is weighed to 300 mg.

**Trituration (if the dose is too small to weigh).** If each powder needs 5 mg of a potent drug and the balance cannot weigh less than 100 mg accurately, make a 1 in 10 trituration (100 mg drug + 900 mg lactose); 50 mg of this contains 5 mg drug.

## Requirements

Drug, lactose, mortar and pestle, sieve, balance, spatula, powder papers (white, rectangular), envelope or box.

## Procedure

1. Weigh 700 mg drug and 1400 mg lactose.
2. Mix by geometric dilution: drug + equal amount of lactose, then double each time.
3. Weigh 300 mg portions onto separate powder papers.
4. Fold each paper: turn down about one-fifth of the long edge, place the powder in the middle, fold the bottom edge up under the flap, fold again, then fold the ends to fit the box so all packets are the same length.
5. Place the 6 packets in an envelope or box and label.

## Observations

Six uniform white powders, each 300 mg.

## Result

Six divided powders, each containing 100 mg of the drug, were prepared and dispensed.

## Label

[Name] Powders, 6 powders. Take one powder as directed by the physician, mixed with water. Store in a dry place.

## Precautions

1. Mix by geometric dilution for uniform content.
2. Weigh each packet separately (not by eye).
3. Make all folded packets the same size.
4. Use waxed or foil-lined papers for hygroscopic or volatile drugs.

## Viva questions

1. **Why make one extra powder?** To allow for losses during mixing and weighing.
2. **Why use lactose?** It is an inert, water-soluble diluent to make up bulk.
3. **What is geometric dilution?** Mixing equal portions and doubling each time for uniformity.
4. **What is a trituration?** A dilution of a potent drug with a diluent in a fixed ratio (for example 1 in 10).
5. **Papers for hygroscopic powders?** Waxed or aluminium-foil-lined papers.

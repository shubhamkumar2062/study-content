# Glycero-gelatin Suppositories (Glycerol Suppositories)

**Aim:** To prepare and dispense 6 glycero-gelatin (glycerol) suppositories.

## Principle

Glycerol suppositories are used as a **laxative**: glycerin draws water into the rectum and irritates the mucosa mildly, causing evacuation. The base is **glycero-gelatin**, made of gelatin, glycerol and water. It does not melt at body temperature but **dissolves slowly** in rectal fluids. Gelatin is first soaked in water so it hydrates, then heated with glycerol to form a clear mass that sets on cooling. Because the base is denser than theobroma oil, a mould labelled "1 g" holds about 1.2 g of this base, so the mould must be calibrated.

## Formula

| Ingredient | For 100 g | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gelatin | 14 g | Gelling agent, gives firmness |
| Glycerol | 70 g | Laxative; plasticiser |
| Purified water | to 100 g | Hydrates gelatin |

For 6 suppositories in a 1 g mould (base about 1.2 g each, plus extra for losses): prepare about 10 g of mass: gelatin 1.4 g, glycerol 7 g, water to 10 g.

## Requirements

Gelatin, glycerol, purified water, porcelain dish, water bath, glass rod, balance, suppository mould, liquid paraffin (lubricant), spatula, foil.

## Procedure

1. Weigh a porcelain dish with a glass rod.
2. Soak the gelatin in the water for a few minutes until it softens.
3. Add the glycerol and heat on a water bath, stirring gently, until the gelatin dissolves and a clear mass forms.
4. Heat further to make up the weight to 10 g (evaporate excess water or add water as needed).
5. Lubricate the mould lightly with liquid paraffin.
6. Pour the hot mass into the mould, filling slightly above the top.
7. Allow to cool and set; trim the excess with a warm spatula; remove the suppositories.
8. Wrap each in foil and pack in an airtight container.

## Observations

Six transparent, elastic, torpedo-shaped suppositories.

## Result

Six glycerol suppositories were prepared and dispensed.

## Label

Glycerol Suppositories, 6. **For rectal use only. Not to be swallowed.** Moisten with water before insertion. Store in an airtight container in a cool place.

## Precautions

1. Soak the gelatin before heating; stir gently to avoid air bubbles.
2. Avoid prolonged heating, which weakens the gel.
3. Use liquid paraffin (not soap spirit) as the mould lubricant for this water-soluble base.
4. Store airtight, as the base is hygroscopic.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is liquid paraffin used as lubricant?** The base is water-miscible, so an oily lubricant is needed.
2. **Why is the base hygroscopic?** Glycerol absorbs water.
3. **Why moisten before insertion?** To reduce irritation from the hygroscopic base drawing water from the mucosa.
4. **How does the base release the drug?** It dissolves in rectal fluid rather than melting.
5. **Why calibrate the mould?** The base is denser than theobroma oil, so the mould holds more weight of it.

# Carbopol Gel

**Aim:** To prepare and dispense 50 g of a 1% w/w carbopol (carbomer) gel base.

> This is a typical laboratory formula. A drug (for example diclofenac sodium) can be added as directed by your teacher.

## Principle

Carbopol (carbomer) is a high-molecular-weight polymer of acrylic acid. Dispersed in water it gives a thin, cloudy, acidic dispersion (pH about 3), because the polymer chains are coiled. On **neutralisation** with a base such as **triethanolamine**, the carboxyl groups ionise; the negative charges repel each other, the chains **uncoil and swell**, and the dispersion suddenly thickens into a clear, viscous gel at about pH 6–7. The gel is non-greasy, washable and cosmetically pleasing.

## Formula

| Ingredient | For 100 g | For 50 g | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Carbopol 934 (or 940) | 1 g | 0.5 g | Gelling agent |
| Glycerin | 5 g | 2.5 g | Humectant |
| Methylparaben | 0.1 g | 0.05 g | Preservative |
| Triethanolamine | q.s. to pH 6–7 | q.s. | Neutralising agent |
| Purified water | to 100 g | to 50 g | Vehicle |

## Requirements

Carbopol, glycerin, methylparaben, triethanolamine, purified water, beaker, mechanical or glass-rod stirrer, pH paper or pH meter, balance, ointment jar or tube.

## Procedure

1. Dissolve the methylparaben in hot purified water (about 40 ml) and cool.
2. Sprinkle the carbopol **slowly** on the surface of the water while stirring continuously, to avoid lumps.
3. Leave it to hydrate and swell (preferably several hours or overnight).
4. Add the glycerin (and drug, if any) and mix.
5. Add triethanolamine drop by drop with gentle stirring until a clear gel forms; check the pH (6–7).
6. Make up to 50 g with purified water, mix gently (avoid air bubbles) and fill into a jar or tube.

## Observations

Clear, transparent, smooth, non-greasy gel.

## Result

50 g of 1% w/w carbopol gel was prepared and dispensed.

## Label

Carbopol Gel, 50 g. **For external use only.** Store in a cool place in a closed container.

## Precautions

1. Sprinkle carbopol slowly with stirring to avoid "fish eyes" (lumps).
2. Do not stir vigorously after neutralisation, which traps air bubbles.
3. Add triethanolamine gradually; over-neutralisation lowers viscosity.

## Viva questions

1. **Why does carbopol thicken on neutralisation?** Ionised carboxyl groups repel each other, the chains uncoil and form a network.
2. **Role of triethanolamine?** Neutralising agent.
3. **What is a gel?** A semisolid in which a liquid is held in a three-dimensional polymer network.
4. **What is syneresis?** Contraction of a gel with separation of liquid.
5. **Name two other gelling agents.** HPMC and sodium carboxymethylcellulose (also tragacanth, sodium alginate).

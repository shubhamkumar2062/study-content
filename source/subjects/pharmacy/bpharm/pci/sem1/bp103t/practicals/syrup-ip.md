# Syrup IP (Simple Syrup)

**Aim:** To prepare and dispense 100 g of Syrup IP (simple syrup).

> Quantities are for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and teacher where they differ.

## Principle

Syrup IP is a concentrated solution of sucrose in purified water containing **66.7% w/w sucrose**. At this strength the solution is nearly saturated. Its high osmotic pressure draws water out of microbial cells, so the syrup preserves itself without an added preservative. Gentle heat speeds up dissolution, but prolonged or strong heating hydrolyses sucrose into glucose and fructose (**inversion**) and darkens it (caramelisation), so heating is kept to a minimum.

## Formula

| Ingredient | Quantity for 100 g | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sucrose | 66.7 g | Sweetening agent, preservative by osmotic effect |
| Purified water | to 100 g (about 33.3 g) | Solvent |

## Requirements

Sucrose, purified water, beaker, glass rod, water bath, balance, muslin or cotton wool for straining, tared bottle.

## Procedure

1. Weigh a clean, dry beaker with a glass rod (tare).
2. Heat about 30 ml of purified water on a water bath.
3. Add 66.7 g of sucrose in portions with constant stirring until it dissolves; do not boil for long.
4. Remove from heat and make up the weight to 100 g with hot purified water.
5. Strain through muslin while warm.
6. Cool and transfer to a clean, dry, well-closed bottle filled to the neck.

## Observations and calculations

Clear, colourless, viscous, sweet liquid.

For any batch: sucrose = 66.7 × weight of syrup ÷ 100. For 250 g: 66.7 × 250 ÷ 100 = **166.75 g** sucrose + water to 250 g.

## Result

100 g of Syrup IP was prepared and dispensed in a well-closed container.

## Label

Syrup IP, 100 g. Store in a cool place in a well-closed container.

## Precautions

1. Avoid prolonged heating to prevent inversion and caramelisation.
2. Make up to weight with hot water, as the syrup is prepared by weight.
3. Fill the bottle completely to reduce evaporation and crystallisation at the neck.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is simple syrup self-preserving?** Its high sugar concentration gives high osmotic pressure, which dehydrates microorganisms.
2. **What is inversion?** Hydrolysis of sucrose to glucose and fructose on heating, especially in acid.
3. **Why fill the bottle to the neck?** To prevent evaporation and crystallisation of sugar at the neck (cap locking).
4. **What happens if syrup is diluted?** It loses its preservative property and may ferment.
5. **Give two uses of simple syrup.** Vehicle and sweetener for oral liquids; base for flavoured and medicated syrups.

# Effervescent Granules

**Aim:** To prepare and dispense 50 g of effervescent granules (base) by the fusion method.

> A drug (for example sodium citrate or a saline laxative) can be added to this base as directed in your manual. Follow your manual's quantities where they differ.

## Principle

Effervescent granules contain an acid and a carbonate. When added to water they react and release **carbon dioxide**, giving a sparkling drink that masks taste and helps dissolution. Citric acid alone gives a sticky mass, and tartaric acid alone gives crumbly, friable granules, so a **mixture** of both is used.

Reactions:
- C6H8O7·H2O + 3NaHCO3 → Na3C6H5O7 + 4H2O + 3CO2
- C4H6O6 + 2NaHCO3 → Na2C4H4O6 + 2H2O + 2CO2

In the **fusion method**, gentle heat releases the water of crystallisation of citric acid monohydrate, which moistens the powder and acts as the binder.

## Formula and calculation

1 g citric acid monohydrate (M 210) needs 3 × 84 ÷ 210 = **1.2 g** NaHCO3.
1 g tartaric acid (M 150) needs 2 × 84 ÷ 150 = **1.12 g** NaHCO3.
With citric : tartaric = 1 : 2, NaHCO3 = 1.2 + 2.24 = **3.44** parts. Ratio **1 : 2 : 3.44** (total 6.44 parts).

| Ingredient | Parts | For 50 g (approx.) | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Citric acid monohydrate | 1 | 7.8 g | Acid; provides binding water |
| Tartaric acid | 2 | 15.5 g | Acid |
| Sodium bicarbonate | 3.44 | 26.7 g | Releases CO2 |

Working: 50 ÷ 6.44 = 7.76 g per part; 7.76 × 2 = 15.53 g; 7.76 × 3.44 = 26.71 g; total = 50.0 g. (Sucrose or the drug, if included, is added on top of this base.)

## Requirements

Citric acid, tartaric acid, sodium bicarbonate, mortar and pestle, sieves (coarse and fine), porcelain dish, water bath or oven at about 90–100 °C, spatula, airtight container.

## Procedure

1. Dry the powders and sieve them separately.
2. Mix them in a dry mortar.
3. Spread the mixture in a dish and warm on a water bath (or in an oven) at about 90–100 °C, turning with a spatula until the mass becomes damp and spongy as the water of crystallisation is released.
4. Press the damp mass through a coarse sieve to form granules.
5. Dry the granules at a low temperature (about 50–60 °C).
6. Pack immediately in an airtight container.

## Observations

White, dry granules that effervesce briskly in water.

## Result

50 g of effervescent granules was prepared and dispensed.

## Label

Effervescent Granules. Dissolve the dose in half a glass of water and drink while effervescing. Keep the container tightly closed in a dry place.

## Precautions

1. Keep everything dry; moisture starts the reaction in the container.
2. Do not overheat during fusion.
3. Pack immediately in an airtight container.

## Viva questions

1. **Why use both citric and tartaric acids?** Citric acid alone gives sticky granules; tartaric acid alone gives friable granules.
2. **What acts as binder in the fusion method?** The water of crystallisation of citric acid.
3. **Gas evolved?** Carbon dioxide.
4. **Ratio of citric : tartaric : bicarbonate?** 1 : 2 : 3.44.
5. **Another method of granulation?** Wet granulation with a non-aqueous liquid such as alcohol.

# Zinc, Starch and Talc Dusting Powder

**Aim:** To prepare and dispense 50 g of Zinc, Starch and Talc Dusting Powder.

## Principle

A **dusting powder** is a fine powder applied to the skin to absorb moisture, reduce friction and soothe. Zinc oxide is mildly astringent and antiseptic, starch absorbs moisture, and talc lubricates and helps the powder spread. The powder must be **very fine and free from grit**, and talc must be **sterilised** because it may carry spores of tetanus and gas-gangrene organisms.

## Formula

| Ingredient | For 100 g | For 50 g | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Zinc oxide | 25 g | 12.5 g | Astringent, mild antiseptic |
| Starch | 25 g | 12.5 g | Absorbent |
| Purified talc (sterilised) | 50 g | 25 g | Lubricant, spreading |

## Requirements

Zinc oxide, starch, sterilised purified talc, mortar and pestle, fine sieve (about 180 µm), balance, spatula, sifter-top container.

## Procedure

1. Sift each powder separately through a fine sieve.
2. Weigh the quantities.
3. Mix by geometric dilution in a mortar: start with zinc oxide, add an equal amount of starch, then the rest of the starch, then talc in portions.
4. Sift the final mixture again.
5. Fill into a sifter-top container.

## Observations

Fine, white, smooth, free-flowing powder.

## Result

50 g of Zinc, Starch and Talc Dusting Powder was prepared and dispensed.

## Label

Zinc, Starch and Talc Dusting Powder, 50 g. **For external use only.** Dust lightly on the affected area. Not for use on open wounds unless sterile. Store in a dry place.

## Precautions

1. Sift the powders to remove grit.
2. Use sterilised talc.
3. Mix by geometric dilution for uniformity.

## Viva questions

1. **Why must talc be sterilised?** It may contain spores of tetanus and gas gangrene organisms.
2. **Role of starch?** Absorbs moisture.
3. **What is a dusting powder?** A fine powder applied to the skin for absorbent, lubricant or antiseptic effect.
4. **Container used?** Sifter-top (perforated lid) container.
5. **Name two other external bulk powders.** Snuffs and insufflations (also dentifrices).

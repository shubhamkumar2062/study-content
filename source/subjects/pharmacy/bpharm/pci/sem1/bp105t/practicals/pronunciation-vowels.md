# Pronunciation: vowel sounds

**Aim:** To recognise and produce the 20 vowel sounds of English (12 pure vowels and 8 diphthongs) and to use correct short and long vowels in common and pharmacy words.

> Language lab activities vary between colleges. Follow your lab teacher and your lab manual where they differ from this guide.

## Principle

A vowel is a sound made with free flow of air through the mouth; the shape of the tongue and lips changes the sound. English has only five vowel letters (a, e, i, o, u) but, in the standard British model used in Indian teaching, **20 vowel sounds**. The length mark ː shows a long vowel.

**12 pure vowels (monophthongs)** — the tongue stays in one position.

| Symbol | Example | Symbol | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| /iː/ | seat, feed | /ɔː/ | saw, born |
| /ɪ/ | sit, pill | /ʊ/ | put, book |
| /e/ | bed, test | /uː/ | food, mood |
| /æ/ | cat, tab | /ʌ/ | cup, drug |
| /ɑː/ | car, farm | /ɜː/ | bird, nurse |
| /ɒ/ | hot, bottle | /ə/ | the "a" in about, the "er" in doctor |

**8 diphthongs** — the tongue glides from one vowel to another in a single syllable.

| Symbol | Example | Symbol | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| /eɪ/ | day, label | /aʊ/ | now, powder |
| /aɪ/ | my, dry | /ɪə/ | near, here |
| /ɔɪ/ | boy, ointment | /eə/ | hair, care |
| /əʊ/ | go, dose | /ʊə/ | poor, tour |

So "dose" has the diphthong /əʊ/, while "hot" has the short pure vowel /ɒ/.

**Short–long pairs (minimal pairs)**

| Short | Long |
| --- | --- |
| ship /ɪ/ | sheep /iː/ |
| fill /ɪ/ | feel /iː/ |
| full /ʊ/ | fool /uː/ |
| cot /ɒ/ | caught /ɔː/ |
| cut /ʌ/ | cart /ɑː/ |

**The schwa /ə/** is the most common English vowel. It appears only in unstressed syllables: a-bout, doc-tor, phar-ma-cy (/ˈfɑːməsi/). Saying every unstressed vowel fully makes speech sound unnatural.

## Requirements

Language lab with headsets and the pronunciation (vowel sounds) module, mirror, minimal-pair lists, dictionary with phonetic transcription, voice recorder.

## Procedure

1. Listen to each vowel and diphthong in the module and repeat it three times; watch lip rounding in the mirror.
2. Read the short–long pairs aloud; hold long vowels clearly longer.
3. **Partner test:** say one word of each pair; your partner writes which one was heard.
4. Look up ten pharmacy words and write the vowel symbol of the stressed syllable.
5. Read and record the sentence: "Please take the dose with a full glass of water before food."
6. Play back, compare with the model, and re-record after correcting long vowels and the schwa.

## Result

The student identified ___ of ___ vowel minimal pairs and transcribed the stressed vowel correctly in ___ of 10 words.

## Precautions

1. Follow the sound, not the spelling ("food" and "good" have different vowels).
2. Make long vowels truly longer; do not shorten /iː/ to /ɪ/.
3. Glide smoothly in diphthongs; do not split them into two syllables.
4. Use the weak schwa in unstressed syllables.
5. Use one standard model consistently, as taught in your lab.

## Viva questions

1. **How many vowel sounds does English have?** 20: 12 pure vowels and 8 diphthongs.
2. **What is a diphthong?** A glide from one vowel sound to another within one syllable, e.g. /aɪ/ in "my".
3. **What is the schwa?** The weak central vowel /ə/ found in unstressed syllables.
4. **What does the mark ː show?** A long vowel.
5. **Give a minimal pair for /ɪ/ and /iː/.** ship – sheep.
6. **Which vowel is in "drug"?** /ʌ/.

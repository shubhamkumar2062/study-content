# Listening comprehension

**Aim:** To listen to a recorded passage or dialogue, understand its main idea and details, take notes, and answer questions on it.

> Language lab activities vary between colleges. Follow your lab teacher and your lab manual where they differ from this guide.

## Principle

Hearing is passive; **listening** is active: receiving the sound, attending to it, understanding it, remembering it and responding. Pharmacists listen to doctors' telephone orders, patients' complaints and seniors' instructions, so errors in listening can become errors in dispensing.

**Three stages of a listening task**

| Stage | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| Pre-listening | Read the questions first, predict the topic, recall related words |
| While-listening | First listening for the main idea (gist); second listening for details (numbers, names, doses, times); take short notes |
| Post-listening | Answer questions, check notes, summarise in two or three sentences |

**Two kinds of listening for information**

- **Listening for gist:** the general idea ("The talk is about storing vaccines").
- **Listening for specific information:** exact facts ("store between 2 and 8 °C").

**Note-taking shortcuts**

```text
Use key words, not full sentences      →  "Insulin – fridge – not freezer"
Use symbols                            →  ↑ increase, ↓ decrease, → leads to, ∴ therefore
Use abbreviations you will understand  →  pt (patient), Rx (prescription), hr, min, wk
Write numbers exactly as heard          →  "5 ml twice daily", "10 days"
```

Barriers to listening include noise, poor attention, guessing the end before the speaker finishes, unfamiliar accents and emotional reactions to the speaker.

## Requirements

Language lab with headsets and the listening comprehension module (audio passages with questions), note sheets, answer worksheet, timer.

## Procedure

1. Read the questions on the worksheet before playing the audio (pre-listening).
2. Play the passage once without writing; note the topic in one line (gist).
3. Play it again and take notes of key facts, numbers and names.
4. Answer the worksheet: multiple choice, true/false, fill in the blanks, and one short summary.
5. Play the audio a third time only to check answers.
6. **Pharmacy activity:** your partner reads aloud a mock telephone order from a doctor (drug, strength, dose, frequency, duration). Write it down and read it back for confirmation (read-back technique).
7. Calculate your score and list the details you missed.

## Observations

**Sample audio summary:** a two-minute talk by a hospital pharmacist on storing insulin.

**Answer worksheet (sample answers)**

| No. | Question | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Main topic of the talk | Correct storage of insulin |
| 2 | Storage of unopened vials (true/false: freezer) | False; refrigerator, not freezer |
| 3 | Where may the vial in use be kept? | At room temperature away from heat and light, for the period stated on the label |
| 4 | One sign the insulin should not be used | Clumps, frosting or a change in appearance |
| 5 | Summary | Unopened insulin goes in the fridge, never frozen; the vial in use is kept cool and discarded after the labelled period. |

**Telephone order read-back**

| Heard | Written | Read back confirmed? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Amoxicillin 500 mg, three times a day, five days | Amoxicillin 500 mg TDS × 5 days | Yes |

**Score (10 marks):** gist 2, details 4, notes 2, summary 2.

## Result

The student identified the main idea and answered ___ of ___ detail questions correctly; the telephone order was written and confirmed correctly / with ___ errors.

## Precautions

1. Read questions before listening so you know what to listen for.
2. Do not try to write every word; note key words and numbers.
3. Do not judge the speaker or stop listening halfway.
4. Always read back numbers, doses and drug names in a telephone order.
5. Adjust headset volume to a comfortable level before starting.

## Viva questions

1. **Differentiate hearing and listening.** Hearing is the physical reception of sound; listening is attending to and understanding it.
2. **What is listening for gist?** Listening to understand the main idea only.
3. **What is the read-back technique?** Repeating a verbal order back to the speaker to confirm it was heard correctly.
4. **Name two barriers to listening.** Noise and lack of attention (also prejudice, accent, daydreaming).
5. **Why read the questions before listening?** To know which information to focus on.
6. **Give two note-taking tips.** Use key words and symbols; write numbers exactly.

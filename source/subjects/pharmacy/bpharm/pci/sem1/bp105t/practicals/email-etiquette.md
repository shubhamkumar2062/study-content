# E-mail etiquette

**Aim:** To write professional e-mails with correct structure, tone and etiquette, and to reply to and forward e-mails appropriately.

> Language lab activities vary between colleges. Follow your lab teacher and your lab manual where they differ from this guide.

## Principle

E-mail is the most common written channel at work. A professional e-mail is short, has a clear subject, puts the main point first and is polite. Once sent, it cannot be taken back and may be forwarded, so think before sending.

**Parts of an e-mail**

| Part | What to write |
| --- | --- |
| To | Main receiver(s) who must act |
| Cc | People who should know but need not act |
| Bcc | Hidden copy, e.g. a large list whose addresses should stay private |
| Subject | Specific: "Request: short supply, order HP/245", not "Hello" or blank |
| Salutation | Dear Dr Rao, / Dear Sir or Madam, / Hello Team, |
| Opening line | Main point or purpose first |
| Body | Details in short paragraphs or bullet points |
| Action/close | What you want and by when; "Thank you." |
| Sign-off | Regards / Best regards, then signature block (name, designation, organisation, phone) |
| Attachment | Mention it in the body; check it is attached |

**Netiquette (e-mail etiquette) rules**

1. Use a professional address (firstname.lastname@…), not a nickname.
2. One topic per e-mail; reply in the same thread for the same topic.
3. Do not write in CAPITALS (it reads as shouting) or use many exclamation marks.
4. Avoid SMS language and emojis in formal mail.
5. Use "Reply all" only when everyone needs the reply.
6. Reply within one working day, even if only to say you will reply fully later.
7. Never send patient details or confidential data to the wrong person; double-check the address.
8. Proofread before sending; check names, numbers and attachments.

## Requirements

Computer lab or language lab with an e-mail practice module (or worksheets), sample e-mails with errors, timer.

## Procedure

1. Study the module's sample e-mails and label each part.
2. Identify ten etiquette errors in a poorly written sample e-mail.
3. **Task 1:** write an e-mail to a medical supplier reporting a short supply and asking for delivery of the balance.
4. **Task 2:** write an e-mail to your class teacher asking for leave for two days, with reason and the dates.
5. **Task 3:** reply to an e-mail from a training-in-charge inviting you for a hospital visit, confirming your attendance.
6. Exchange e-mails with a partner, mark them with the checklist and revise.

## Observations

**Sample e-mail (Task 1)**

```text
To:       orders@xyzdistributors.example
Cc:       storekeeper@citycare.example
Subject:  Short supply against order HP/245 – 20 boxes pending

Dear Sir or Madam,

We received your consignment against order HP/245 on 14 March. The invoice lists
100 boxes of Amoxicillin 500 mg capsules, but only 80 boxes were delivered.

Kindly send the remaining 20 boxes by 18 March or issue a credit note. A photo of
the delivery challan is attached.

Thank you.

Regards,
Neha Gupta
Pharmacist, City Care Hospital
Phone: 0177-xxxxxxx
```

**Error checklist (sample from the poor e-mail)**

| Error | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Blank subject | "Leave request: 15–16 March" |
| "hi sir pls give leave" | "Dear Sir, I request leave for…" |
| ALL CAPITALS | Normal sentence case |
| No signature | Name, roll number, class |
| Attachment mentioned but missing | Attach before sending |

**Score (10 marks):** subject line 2, structure 2, tone 2, clarity and brevity 2, accuracy 2.

## Result

The student wrote ___ professional e-mails and corrected ___ of 10 etiquette errors.

## Precautions

1. Write the subject line before the body so it is never blank.
2. Fill the "To" field last to avoid sending an unfinished e-mail.
3. Keep confidential information out of e-mail unless your organisation allows it.
4. Do not reply when angry; wait and write calmly.
5. Use the example domain addresses only for practice.

## Viva questions

1. **Difference between Cc and Bcc?** Cc recipients are visible to all; Bcc recipients are hidden.
2. **Why is the subject line important?** It tells the reader the topic at a glance and helps searching later.
3. **Why avoid writing in capitals?** It reads as shouting and is hard to read.
4. **When should you use "Reply all"?** Only when every recipient needs your reply.
5. **What is a signature block?** Name, designation, organisation and contact details at the end.
6. **What is netiquette?** The rules of polite behaviour in online and e-mail communication.

# Pronunciation and nouns: word stress and plural endings

**Aim:** To pronounce nouns with correct word stress, to distinguish noun–verb stress pairs, and to pronounce plural and possessive -s endings correctly.

> Language lab activities vary between colleges. Follow your lab teacher and your lab manual where they differ from this guide.

## Principle

**Word stress.** In an English word of two or more syllables, one syllable is said louder, longer and on a higher pitch. Putting stress on the wrong syllable can make a word hard to recognise even if every sound is right. In dictionaries the stressed syllable is marked with ˈ before it (/ˈfɑːməsi/ pharmacy).

**General tendencies (with exceptions)**

| Word type | Usual stress | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two-syllable nouns | First syllable | TAB-let, DOC-tor, PA-tient, VI-al |
| Two-syllable verbs | Second syllable | ex-PLAIN, ad-MIT, pre-SCRIBE |
| Words ending -tion, -sion | Syllable just before | pre-SCRIP-tion, in-JEC-tion, so-LU-tion |
| Words ending -ic, -ical | Syllable just before -ic | thera-PEU-tic, an-ti-bi-OT-ic, CHEM-i-cal |
| Words ending -ity, -logy | Third from the end | ac-TIV-i-ty, phar-ma-COL-o-gy |

**Noun–verb stress pairs** (same spelling, stress moves):

| Word | As noun (stress 1st) | As verb (stress 2nd) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| record | REcord: a written record | reCORD: to record data |
| present | PREsent: a gift | preSENT: to present a seminar |
| object | OBject: a thing | obJECT: to disagree |
| produce | PROduce: farm products | proDUCE: to make |
| conduct | CONduct: behaviour | conDUCT: to carry out |
| permit | PERmit: a licence | perMIT: to allow |
| increase | INcrease: a rise | inCREASE: to make larger |

**Plural and possessive -s endings** depend on the last sound of the noun:

| Last sound of the noun | -s pronounced | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Voiceless /p, t, k, f, θ/ | /s/ | tablets, drops, caps, graphs |
| Voiced sounds and vowels | /z/ | vials, pills, doctors, bottles, trays |
| Hissing sounds /s, z, ʃ, ʒ, tʃ, dʒ/ | /ɪz/ (extra syllable) | doses, boxes, washes, patches, syringes, nurses |

So "vials, pills, doctors, bottles" end in /z/, while "doses, syringes, boxes" add a syllable /ɪz/.

## Requirements

Language lab with headsets and the "pronunciation and nouns" module, dictionary with stress marks, worksheets, voice recorder.

## Procedure

1. Listen to the module and clap once on the stressed syllable of each word.
2. Look up ten pharmacy nouns in the dictionary and mark the stressed syllable.
3. Read the noun–verb pairs in sentences, for example: "Please keep a REcord. We will reCORD the weight daily."
4. Sort twenty plural nouns into the /s/, /z/ and /ɪz/ columns and read them aloud.
5. **Pair activity:** read a short pharmacy notice (containing words such as tablets, syringes, doses, records, patients) to your partner, who marks stress or ending errors.
6. Record the notice again after correction.

## Precautions

1. Check stress in a dictionary when unsure; the rules have exceptions.
2. Do not stress every syllable equally; that sounds flat.
3. Do not add an extra syllable to /s/ and /z/ plurals ("tablet-es").
4. Keep the vowel in unstressed syllables short and weak.
5. Speak at normal speed after practising slowly.

## Viva questions

1. **What is word stress?** Saying one syllable of a word louder, longer and higher than the others.
2. **Stress "record" as a noun and as a verb.** REcord (noun), reCORD (verb).
3. **How is -s pronounced in "tablets"?** /s/.
4. **How is -s pronounced in "doses"?** /ɪz/, an extra syllable.
5. **Where is the stress in words ending in -tion?** On the syllable just before -tion.
6. **How is the stressed syllable marked in a dictionary?** With a raised mark ˈ before the syllable.

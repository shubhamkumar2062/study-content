# Writing skills: sentences and paragraphs

**Aim:** To write grammatically correct, clear sentences and well-organised paragraphs, and to edit a short piece of writing for errors.

> Language lab activities vary between colleges. Follow your lab teacher and your lab manual where they differ from this guide.

## Principle

Good writing is built from correct sentences joined into logical paragraphs. A pharmacist writes records, labels, reports and e-mails, where a vague or wrong sentence can cause harm.

**Kinds of sentences**

| Kind | Structure | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple | One main clause | The tablet dissolved quickly. |
| Compound | Two main clauses joined by and, but, or, so | The tablet dissolved quickly, but the capsule did not. |
| Complex | Main clause + dependent clause (because, although, when, if) | Although the tablet was coated, it dissolved quickly. |

**Rules for clear sentences**

1. Subject and verb must agree: "The list of drugs **is** ready" (subject is "list").
2. Prefer the active voice: "The pharmacist checked the dose", not "The dose was checked by the pharmacist", unless the doer is unknown or unimportant (lab reports often use passive: "The solution was filtered").
3. One main idea per sentence; keep most sentences under about 20 words.
4. Use punctuation correctly: full stop at the end, comma between items in a list, apostrophe for possession (the patient's file).
5. Avoid run-on sentences joined only by commas.

**Structure of a paragraph**

```text
Topic sentence      → states the main idea
Supporting sentences → explain, give facts, examples, reasons
Concluding sentence  → sums up or links to the next paragraph
Linking words: firstly, moreover, however, therefore, for example, finally
```

**Common errors in student writing**

| Error | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| He don't know the dose. | He doesn't know the dose. |
| Each of the students have a record. | Each of the students has a record. |
| I am having two brothers. | I have two brothers. |
| Discuss about the topic. | Discuss the topic. |
| The medicine is kept in it's box. | The medicine is kept in its box. |

## Requirements

Language lab with the writing skills module (typing or worksheet), sample paragraphs with errors, dictionary, timer.

## Procedure

1. Study the module's examples of simple, compound and complex sentences.
2. Combine pairs of simple sentences into compound and complex sentences (ten items).
3. Change five passive sentences to active.
4. Edit a 120-word paragraph containing ten errors (agreement, tense, punctuation, spelling).
5. **Writing task (15 minutes):** write one paragraph of 120–150 words on "Role of a pharmacist in the community", with a topic sentence, three supporting points and a conclusion.
6. **Peer review:** exchange paragraphs; your partner marks errors and checks structure using the rubric.
7. Rewrite the paragraph after feedback.

## Observations

**Sentence combining (sample answers)**

| Simple sentences | Combined |
| --- | --- |
| The syrup is sweet. Children like it. | The syrup is sweet, so children like it. (compound) |
| The patient missed doses. His fever returned. | Because the patient missed doses, his fever returned. (complex) |

**Sample paragraph:** A community pharmacist is often the most easily reached health professional. Firstly, the pharmacist dispenses medicines and explains how to use them safely. Moreover, he or she advises on minor ailments and refers serious cases to a doctor. The pharmacist also spreads awareness about vaccination, hygiene and the dangers of self-medication. Therefore, the pharmacist plays a key role in keeping the community healthy.

**Rubric (10 marks):** topic sentence 2, supporting points 3, grammar and spelling 3, linking words and conclusion 2.

## Result

The student combined ___ of 10 sentence pairs correctly, corrected ___ of 10 errors in the edit task, and wrote a paragraph scoring ___ / 10.

## Precautions

1. Plan the main idea before writing.
2. Read your writing aloud to catch errors.
3. Avoid SMS short forms (u, pls, coz) in formal writing.
4. Check spelling of drug names carefully.
5. Write neatly and leave margins in handwritten work.

## Viva questions

1. **What is a topic sentence?** The sentence that states the main idea of a paragraph, usually first.
2. **Give an example of a complex sentence.** "Although it was late, the pharmacy was open."
3. **Why prefer active voice?** It is clearer, shorter and shows who did the action.
4. **Correct: "Each of the boys have a pen."** "Each of the boys has a pen."
5. **Name three linking words.** However, therefore, moreover.
6. **What is a run-on sentence?** Two or more main clauses joined without proper punctuation or a conjunction.

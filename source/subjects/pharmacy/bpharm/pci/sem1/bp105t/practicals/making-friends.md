# Making friends: small talk and conversation

**Aim:** To practise starting, continuing and politely ending an informal conversation (small talk) and to build rapport with classmates, colleagues and patients.

> Language lab activities vary between colleges. Follow your lab teacher and your lab manual where they differ from this guide.

## Principle

Small talk is light, friendly conversation on safe topics. It breaks the ice, builds trust and often leads to more important talk. Pharmacists use it to make a nervous patient comfortable before counselling, and students use it to make friends in a new college.

**Structure of a conversation**

```text
Opener  →  Follow-up questions  →  Sharing about yourself  →  Finding common ground  →  Polite closing
"Is this your first week here?" → "Which school are you from?" → "I am from Shimla." →
"Oh, you like cricket too!"      → "It was nice talking to you. See you in the lab."
```

**Useful expressions**

| Purpose | Expressions |
| --- | --- |
| Starting | Hi, I don't think we've met. I'm …; Lovely weather today, isn't it? |
| Showing interest | Really? That's interesting. How did you get into that? |
| Agreeing | Same here! / I feel the same. |
| Disagreeing gently | I see your point, but I think … |
| Changing topic | By the way … / Speaking of …, |
| Ending | It was great talking to you. / I must rush to class, catch you later. |

**Safe and unsafe topics**

| Safe | Avoid at first meeting |
| --- | --- |
| Weather, studies, hobbies, food, films, sports, travel, festivals | Salary, religion, politics, weight, illness of others, personal problems, gossip |

Rapport comes from active listening: nod, keep eye contact, repeat key words ("So you play the tabla?") and ask follow-up questions instead of only talking about yourself.

## Requirements

Language lab with headsets and the software module on making friends, topic cards, voice recorder, timer, peer feedback sheet.

## Procedure

1. Listen to the model conversations in the module and note openers, follow-ups and closings.
2. Write five openers and five follow-up questions of your own.
3. **Pair activity (3 minutes each):** draw a topic card and hold a conversation with a classmate you know least. Use at least one opener, three follow-up questions, one shared interest and a polite closing.
4. **Pharmacy scenario:** a pharmacist chats briefly with an anxious patient waiting for a prescription to be filled (topics: travel to the pharmacy, the weather, a festival), then moves smoothly to "Let me explain how to take your medicines."
5. **Group round:** in groups of four, each student introduces the partner they met, telling one interesting fact learned.
6. Partners fill the feedback sheet.

## Observations

**Sample conversation**

| Speaker | Line |
| --- | --- |
| A | Hi, I'm Meera. I sit two rows behind you in Pharmaceutics. |
| B | Hello, Meera! I'm Aakash. How are you finding the first week? |
| A | Busy, but fun. The anatomy lab was interesting. Where are you from? |
| B | Mandi. And you? |
| A | Kangra. Do you play any sport? |
| B | Badminton, every evening. |
| A | Really? I play too! Shall we play this Sunday? |
| B | Sure. It was nice talking to you. See you in the lab. |

## Result

The student held a ___-minute informal conversation using ___ follow-up questions, found ___ common interest(s), and scored ___ / 10.

## Precautions

1. Keep the talk balanced; do not dominate or reply in one word.
2. Avoid sensitive topics and personal remarks.
3. Respect personal space and cultural norms.
4. With patients, keep small talk short and move to the purpose of the visit.
5. Close the conversation politely rather than walking away.

## Viva questions

1. **What is small talk?** Light, friendly conversation on safe topics to break the ice and build rapport.
2. **Name three safe topics.** Weather, studies and hobbies.
3. **Why should a pharmacist use small talk?** To relax the patient and build trust before counselling.
4. **How do you show interest in a conversation?** Nod, keep eye contact, repeat key words and ask follow-up questions.
5. **Give one polite way to end a conversation.** "It was great talking to you; I must go to class now."
6. **What is rapport?** A friendly understanding and trust between people that makes communication easy.

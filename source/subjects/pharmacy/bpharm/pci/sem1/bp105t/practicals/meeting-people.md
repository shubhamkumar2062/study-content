# Meeting people: greetings and introductions

**Aim:** To practise greeting people, introducing yourself and introducing others politely in formal and informal situations, as used in a pharmacy, hospital or college.

> Language lab activities vary between colleges. Follow your lab teacher and your lab manual where they differ from this guide.

## Principle

The first minute of any meeting sets the tone for everything that follows. A clear greeting, your name, your role and a friendly closing make the other person comfortable and willing to listen. The words you choose depend on who you are meeting (a senior doctor, a patient, a classmate) and on the time of day.

**Greetings by situation**

| Situation | Formal (senior, stranger, patient) | Informal (friend, classmate) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Morning (till noon) | Good morning, sir / madam. | Hi! / Hello! |
| Afternoon (noon to about 5 pm) | Good afternoon. | Hey, how are you? |
| Evening | Good evening. | Hi there! |
| First meeting | How do you do? / Pleased to meet you. | Nice to meet you. |
| Leaving | Thank you for your time. Have a good day. | See you later. / Bye! |

"Good night" is used only when parting at night, never as a greeting.

**Parts of a self-introduction**

```text
Greeting  →  Name  →  Role / where you are from  →  One useful detail  →  Polite closing or question
"Good morning. I am Priya Sharma, a first-year B.Pharm student. I am here for my
hospital pharmacy visit. Could you tell me where the dispensing section is?"
```

**Introducing two people:** introduce the junior person to the senior person first, and give each a little information about the other so they can begin talking.

**Non-verbal points:** stand straight, smile, keep eye contact, offer a firm short handshake where it is culturally fine (otherwise a namaste or a nod), and speak at a moderate speed.

## Requirements

Language lab with headsets and the lab software module on meeting people, microphone or voice recorder, role-play cards, timer, evaluation rubric (below).

## Procedure

1. Listen to the model conversations in the software module and repeat each greeting after the speaker.
2. Note the formal and informal expressions in your record book in two columns.
3. Write a 40–60 word self-introduction for a formal meeting (for example, meeting the chief pharmacist on your first day of training).
4. Record your introduction, play it back, and correct speed, clarity and filler words ("umm", "actually").
5. **Pair activity:** take role cards and act out these situations for one to two minutes each:
   - a. A pharmacy student meets the hospital's chief pharmacist on the first day.
   - b. A community pharmacist greets a new elderly patient and introduces herself.
   - c. You introduce your classmate to a visiting medical representative.
6. Your partner scores you on the rubric; then swap roles.
7. Repeat one role play after feedback and note the improvement.

## Observations

**Sample dialogue (situation a)**

| Speaker | Line |
| --- | --- |
| Student | Good morning, sir. I am Rahul Verma, a B.Pharm first-year student from the college of pharmacy. |
| Chief pharmacist | Good morning, Rahul. Welcome to the department. |
| Student | Thank you, sir. I am here for a two-week observation visit. I look forward to learning how prescriptions are checked here. |
| Chief pharmacist | Good. Meet Ms Anjali, our senior pharmacist. She will guide you. |

## Precautions

1. Match the greeting to the time of day and to the person's seniority.
2. Use titles (sir, madam, Dr, Mr, Ms) with seniors and patients until invited to use first names.
3. Keep the introduction short; do not narrate your whole background.
4. Avoid slang and filler words in formal settings.
5. Speak clearly into the microphone at a steady distance while recording.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is the first introduction important?** It forms the first impression and decides whether the listener is comfortable and willing to continue.
2. **When do you say "Good night"?** Only while parting at night, not as a greeting.
3. **Whom do you introduce first?** The junior person is introduced to the senior person.
4. **What are the parts of a self-introduction?** Greeting, name, role, a useful detail and a polite closing.
5. **Give two non-verbal points in greeting.** Eye contact and a smile (also posture and a suitable handshake or namaste).
6. **What is the reply to "How do you do?"** The same phrase, "How do you do?", or "Pleased to meet you".

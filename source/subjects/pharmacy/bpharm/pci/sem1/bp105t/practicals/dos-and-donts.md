# Do's and don'ts: instructions and polite requests

**Aim:** To give clear instructions, warnings and advice using imperatives and polite forms, and to prepare a "do's and don'ts" leaflet for patients.

> Language lab activities vary between colleges. Follow your lab teacher and your lab manual where they differ from this guide.

## Principle

Instructions tell someone what to do (do) or not to do (don't). In a pharmacy they appear on labels, leaflets and in counselling. They must be short, clear and polite, and one instruction should carry one action.

**Forms of instructions**

| Form | Structure | Example | Tone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Imperative (do) | Base verb + object | Shake the bottle well. | Direct, clear |
| Imperative (don't) | Do not / Don't + base verb | Do not crush the tablet. | Direct warning |
| Polite request | Please + verb; Could you …? | Please take this after food. | Polite |
| Advice | You should / should not … | You should drink plenty of water. | Soft |
| Strong rule | You must / must not … | You must not drive after this dose. | Strong |
| Sequence | First, then, next, finally | First wash your hands, then apply the cream. | Ordered |

**Writing good instructions**

```text
1. Start with the action verb.            "Store below 25 °C."
2. One action per line.                    not "Shake, take 5 ml and store in fridge."
3. Use simple words, avoid jargon.          "after food", not "post-prandially"
4. Put the most important warning first.   "Do not take with alcohol."
5. Use numbers for steps in order.
```

Polite words (please, kindly, thank you) and a warm tone make patients more likely to follow advice.

## Requirements

Language lab with headsets and the software module on do's and don'ts, sample medicine labels and leaflets, chart paper, worksheet, timer.

## Procedure

1. Listen to the module and repeat the instructions, noting falling tone at the end.
2. Convert ten statements into "do" and "don't" imperatives.
3. Rewrite five rude instructions into polite forms.
4. **Group activity:** in groups of three, write a "Do's and Don'ts" card (five each) for one of these: using a metered-dose inhaler, taking an antibiotic course, storing insulin, laboratory safety in the pharmaceutics lab.
5. **Role play:** a pharmacist explains the card to a patient, who repeats the key points back (teach-back).
6. Display the cards; the class votes for the clearest one.

## Observations

**Sample card: taking an antibiotic course**

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Take every dose at the same times each day. | Don't stop when you feel better. |
| Complete the full course the doctor prescribed. | Don't share your antibiotic with others. |
| Take it with water. | Don't keep leftover tablets for later use. |
| Tell the pharmacist about any rash or swelling. | Don't double the next dose if you miss one; ask the pharmacist. |
| Read the label for food instructions. | Don't take it with alcohol unless told it is safe. |

**Rude → polite**

| Rude | Polite |
| --- | --- |
| Wait there. | Please wait here for a few minutes. |
| Give me the prescription. | Could you give me the prescription, please? |
| Don't touch that. | Please don't touch that; it is sterile. |

**Score (10 marks):** correct imperative forms 3, clarity and one action per line 3, politeness 2, teach-back done 2.

## Result

The group prepared a do's and don'ts card on ___ and explained it clearly; the patient repeated ___ of ___ key points correctly.

## Precautions

1. Keep each instruction short and in the active voice.
2. Avoid technical words with patients.
3. Do not be rude or commanding; add "please" in spoken instructions.
4. Check understanding by asking the patient to repeat key points.
5. Follow the actual label and leaflet of the product when giving real advice.

## Viva questions

1. **What is an imperative sentence?** A sentence that gives a command or instruction, beginning with the base verb.
2. **Make "You should not crush the tablet" a direct instruction.** "Do not crush the tablet."
3. **What is the teach-back method?** Asking the patient to repeat instructions in their own words to confirm understanding.
4. **Why one action per line?** It makes instructions easy to follow and reduces mistakes.
5. **Which word makes an instruction polite?** "Please" (or "kindly", or a "Could you…" question).
6. **What should come first in a leaflet?** The most important warning.

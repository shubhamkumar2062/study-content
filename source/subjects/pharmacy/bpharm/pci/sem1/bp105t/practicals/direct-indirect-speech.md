# Direct and indirect speech

**Aim:** To convert sentences from direct speech to indirect (reported) speech and back, applying the rules for tense, pronouns, time and place words, questions and commands.

> Language lab activities vary between colleges. Follow your lab teacher and your lab manual where they differ from this guide.

## Principle

**Direct speech** gives the speaker's exact words in quotation marks: The doctor said, "Take rest." **Indirect (reported) speech** reports the meaning without quotation marks: The doctor told him to take rest. Pharmacists use reported speech daily: "The patient said that he had stopped the tablets."

**Rule 1: Tense back-shift** (when the reporting verb is in the past, e.g. said, told)

| Direct | Indirect |
| --- | --- |
| Simple present: "I feel dizzy." | Simple past: …that she felt dizzy. |
| Present continuous: "I am taking it." | Past continuous: …that he was taking it. |
| Present perfect: "I have finished it." | Past perfect: …that he had finished it. |
| Simple past: "I took it." | Past perfect: …that he had taken it. |
| will / can / may | would / could / might |
| shall (future) | would |

No change when the reporting verb is in the present ("He says that he feels better") or when the statement is a universal truth ("The teacher said that water boils at 100 °C at sea level").

**Rule 2: Pronouns** change to fit the reporter: "I" → he/she; "my" → his/her; "we" → they; "you" → I/he/she/they depending on whom it refers to.

**Rule 3: Time and place words**

| Direct | Indirect |
| --- | --- |
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tonight | that night |
| yesterday | the day before / the previous day |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| ago | before |
| this / these | that / those |
| here | there |

**Rule 4: Sentence types**

| Type | Reporting verb and link | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Statement | said / told + that | He said that he was tired. |
| Yes/no question | asked + if / whether, statement word order | She asked if I had a fever. |
| Wh-question | asked + Wh-word, statement word order | He asked when the shop opened. |
| Command | told / ordered + to + verb | The pharmacist told him to shake the bottle. |
| Negative command | told + not to + verb | She told me not to crush the tablet. |
| Request | requested + to + verb | He requested me to close the door. |
| Exclamation | exclaimed with joy/sorrow + that | She exclaimed with joy that she had passed. |

## Requirements

Language lab with headsets and the direct and indirect speech module, worksheets, short audio dialogues, answer key.

## Procedure

1. Listen to the module's examples and note how the tense and pronouns change.
2. Convert fifteen worksheet sentences (statements, questions, commands) from direct to indirect speech.
3. Convert five indirect sentences back to direct speech.
4. **Pharmacy activity:** listen to a short recorded patient–pharmacist dialogue and write a five-line report of it in indirect speech, as you would record it in a counselling note.
5. **Pair check:** exchange worksheets and mark against the answer key.

## Observations

**Conversion worksheet (sample answers)**

| Direct | Indirect |
| --- | --- |
| The patient said, "I have a headache." | The patient said that he had a headache. |
| She said, "I will come tomorrow." | She said that she would come the next day. |
| He asked, "Should I take it before food?" | He asked whether he should take it before food. |
| The pharmacist asked, "When did the rash start?" | The pharmacist asked when the rash had started. |
| The doctor said to her, "Avoid sugar." | The doctor advised her to avoid sugar. |
| He said to me, "Don't skip the dose." | He told me not to skip the dose. |

**Counselling note (indirect speech):** The patient said that he had been taking the tablet only in the morning. He asked whether he could take it with milk. The pharmacist told him to take it with water and advised him not to stop it without asking the doctor.

## Precautions

1. Check whether the reporting verb is past or present before changing the tense.
2. Do not use a question mark or question word order in reported questions.
3. Use "told" with an object (told me), "said" without one (said that).
4. Do not change tense for universal truths.
5. Keep the meaning exactly the same; do not add your own opinion.

## Viva questions

1. **What is indirect speech?** Reporting someone's words in your own sentence without quotation marks.
2. **Change "will" in indirect speech.** It becomes "would".
3. **Which word introduces a reported yes/no question?** If or whether.
4. **Indirect form of "tomorrow"?** The next day / the following day.
5. **When does the tense not change?** When the reporting verb is present or the statement is a universal truth.
6. **Report: He said, "Close the door."** He told me to close the door.

# Pronunciation: consonant sounds

**Aim:** To recognise and correctly produce the 24 consonant sounds of English using phonetic symbols, with special practice on sounds that Indian speakers often confuse.

> Language lab activities vary between colleges. Follow your lab teacher and your lab manual where they differ from this guide.

## Principle

English spelling does not show pronunciation reliably (the "c" in cat and city differ). Phonetic symbols (IPA) give one symbol per sound. Standard British pronunciation, used in Indian teaching, has **24 consonant sounds**. A consonant is made by blocking or narrowing the air in the mouth. Consonants are **voiced** (vocal cords vibrate) or **voiceless**.

**The 24 consonants**

| Symbol | Example | Symbol | Example | Symbol | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| /p/ | pen | /f/ | fan | /h/ | hat |
| /b/ | bag | /v/ | van | /m/ | man |
| /t/ | tea | /θ/ | thin | /n/ | nose |
| /d/ | dog | /ð/ | this | /ŋ/ | sing |
| /k/ | cat | /s/ | sun | /l/ | leg |
| /g/ | go | /z/ | zoo | /r/ | red |
| /tʃ/ | chair | /ʃ/ | ship | /j/ | yes |
| /dʒ/ | jam | /ʒ/ | measure | /w/ | wet |

**Voiceless–voiced pairs** (same mouth position, cords off or on): /p/–/b/, /t/–/d/, /k/–/g/, /f/–/v/, /θ/–/ð/, /s/–/z/, /ʃ/–/ʒ/, /tʃ/–/dʒ/.

**Common confusions**

| Sounds | Problem | Practice pair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| /v/ and /w/ | both said as one sound | vest – west, vine – wine |
| /θ/ and /t/ | "thin" said as "tin" | thin – tin, three – tree |
| /ð/ and /d/ | "they" said as "day" | they – day, then – den |
| /ʃ/ and /s/ | "ship" said as "sip" | ship – sip, shoe – sue |
| /z/ and /dʒ/ | "zero" said as "jero" | zest – jest, zinc said as "jinc" |
| /f/ and /p/ | "fan" said with a puff /p/ | fan – pan, fill – pill |

**Minimal pairs** are two words that differ in only one sound (pill / bill). Saying them correctly shows you can make both sounds.

## Requirements

Language lab with headsets and the pronunciation (consonant sounds) module, mirror, minimal-pair word lists, voice recorder, pharmacy word list.

## Procedure

1. Listen to each consonant in the module and repeat it three times, watching your lips in the mirror.
2. Put two fingers on your throat while saying /s/ and /z/; feel the vibration only in /z/.
3. Read the minimal-pair lists aloud; your partner writes which word they heard.
4. Practise the problem pairs from the table until your partner identifies 9 of 10 correctly.
5. **Pharmacy words:** read and record — syrup, vaccine, vial, ointment, thermometer, therapy, sachet, vitamin, zinc, measure, chemist, pharmacy.
6. Play back and mark any wrong sound; repeat after the module's model.

| Word | Watch the sound |
| --- | --- |
| therapy | /θ/ at the start, not /t/ |
| vial | /v/, teeth on lower lip |

## Result

## Precautions

1. Listen carefully before repeating; do not guess from spelling.
2. For /v/ the upper teeth touch the lower lip; for /w/ round the lips without teeth.
3. For /θ/ and /ð/ put the tongue tip lightly between the teeth.
4. Speak slowly at first, then at normal speed.
5. Keep the microphone at the same distance for every recording.

## Viva questions

1. **How many consonant sounds are there in English?** 24.
2. **What is a voiced sound?** One made with vibration of the vocal cords, such as /b/, /d/, /z/.
3. **What is a minimal pair? Give one.** Two words differing in one sound, e.g. pin – bin.
4. **Write the symbol for the first sound in "ship".** /ʃ/.
5. **Which sound is in the middle of "measure"?** /ʒ/.
6. **How is "ch" in "chemist" pronounced?** /k/.
7. **Why are phonetic symbols useful?** Each symbol stands for one sound, unlike English spelling.

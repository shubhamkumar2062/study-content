# Presentation skills

**Aim:** To plan, structure and deliver a five-minute oral presentation with visual aids on a pharmacy topic, and to evaluate it using a rubric.

> Language lab activities vary between colleges. Follow your lab teacher and your lab manual where they differ from this guide.

## Principle

A presentation is a planned talk to an audience with a clear purpose: to inform, explain or persuade. Success depends on planning (purpose, audience, time), structure, delivery and control of nervousness.

**Structure**

```text
Introduction (about 10%)  → greeting, topic, why it matters, outline ("I will cover three points")
Body (about 80%)          → 2–4 main points in logical order, each with an example or fact
Conclusion (about 10%)    → summary of main points, key message, thank you, invite questions
```

**Delivery techniques**

| Element | Good practice |
| --- | --- |
| Voice | Clear, audible to the last row, varied pitch, pauses after key points |
| Pace | Moderate; slow down for numbers and new terms |
| Eye contact | Scan the whole audience, not the slides or the floor |
| Posture and gestures | Stand straight, open gestures, avoid pockets and swaying |
| Notes | Key words on cue cards; do not read a script |
| Visual aids | Few words per slide (about six lines, large font), one idea per slide, clear charts |
| Questions | Listen fully, repeat the question, answer briefly; admit if you do not know |

**Dealing with fear (stage fright)**

1. Prepare thoroughly and rehearse aloud at least three times.
2. Know your first two sentences by heart.
3. Breathe slowly before starting; arrive early to check the room and equipment.
4. Focus on the message, not on yourself; friendly faces in the audience help.

## Requirements

Language lab or seminar room with projector and the presentation skills module, laptop with slide software, cue cards, timer, video recorder, evaluation rubric.

## Procedure

1. Watch the module's sample presentations and note good and poor delivery.
2. Choose a topic, for example: safe use of antibiotics, role of a pharmacist in the community, storage of vaccines, proper use of an inhaler, dangers of self-medication.
3. Plan: write the purpose, audience and three main points.
4. Prepare 5–6 slides (title, outline, three content slides, summary) and cue cards.
5. Rehearse once with a timer and adjust to 5 minutes.
6. Present to the class; answer two questions from the audience.
7. Classmates and the teacher fill the rubric; watch the recording and note two improvements.

## Observations

**Sample outline: "Using an inhaler correctly"**

| Section | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Introduction | Many patients do not get the full dose from inhalers because of poor technique; this talk shows the right steps. |
| Point 1 | Preparing: remove cap, shake, breathe out away from the mouthpiece |
| Point 2 | Inhaling: seal lips, press once while breathing in slowly and deeply, hold breath for up to 10 seconds, or as long as comfortable |
| Point 3 | After use: wait before a second puff as advised, rinse mouth after steroid inhalers, keep the device clean |
| Conclusion | Correct technique = right dose = better control; ask your pharmacist to check your technique. |

**Evaluation rubric (20 marks)**

| Criterion | Marks | Given |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structure (intro, body, conclusion) | 4 | 4 |
| Content accuracy | 4 | 3 |
| Voice and pace | 3 | 2 |
| Eye contact and body language | 3 | 2 |
| Visual aids | 3 | 3 |
| Time management and handling questions | 3 | 2 |
| **Total** | **20** | **16** |

## Precautions

1. Keep slides simple; do not read them aloud word for word.
2. Check facts and doses from standard references before presenting.
3. Stay within the time limit.
4. Test the projector and files before the session.
5. Face the audience, not the screen.

## Viva questions

1. **What are the three parts of a presentation?** Introduction, body and conclusion.
2. **How many words should a slide carry?** Few: about six short lines in large font, one idea per slide.
3. **Give two ways to reduce stage fright.** Rehearse well and breathe slowly before starting.
4. **Why repeat a question from the audience?** So everyone hears it and you confirm you understood it.
5. **What are cue cards?** Small cards with key words to guide the speaker instead of a full script.
6. **What is the purpose of the conclusion?** To summarise the main points and leave the key message.

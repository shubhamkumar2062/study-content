# Effective communication

**Aim:** To practise a complete communication cycle with a patient, applying the 7 Cs, verbal and non-verbal skills, and feedback, and to evaluate it with a checklist.

> Language lab activities vary between colleges. Follow your lab teacher and your lab manual where they differ from this guide.

## Principle

Communication is effective when the receiver understands the message as the sender meant it and responds. It follows a cycle: **sender → encodes message → channel → receiver decodes → feedback**. Barriers (noise, language, emotion, poor listening) can break the cycle at any point.

**The 7 Cs of effective communication**

| C | Meaning | Pharmacy example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clear | One idea at a time, simple words | "Take one tablet every morning." |
| Concise | No unnecessary words | Skip long background; give the key point |
| Concrete | Specific facts and numbers | "5 ml three times a day for 5 days", not "a little, often" |
| Correct | Accurate facts, grammar, names | Right drug name and strength |
| Coherent | Points in logical order | Name of drug → why → how → side effects → storage |
| Complete | All needed information | Includes what to do if a dose is missed |
| Courteous | Polite, respectful, empathetic | "I understand this is worrying; let me explain." |

**Verbal and non-verbal parts**

```text
Message understood  =  Words (what you say)
                     + Voice (tone, pace, volume, pauses)
                     + Body (eye contact, posture, facial expression, gestures, distance)
When words and body language disagree, listeners tend to believe the body language.
```

**Feedback** closes the loop. Ask open questions and use teach-back ("Can you tell me how you will take this?") to check understanding.

## Requirements

Language lab with headsets and the effective communication module, role-play scenario cards, sample prescriptions (dummy), video or voice recorder, observer checklist, timer.

## Procedure

1. Watch or listen to the module's examples of effective and ineffective conversations and note what went wrong in the ineffective one.
2. Rewrite three unclear instructions using the 7 Cs.
3. **Role play in groups of three** (pharmacist, patient, observer), 4–5 minutes each:
   - a. Counselling a patient on a new prescription for a blood-pressure tablet.
   - b. Explaining to a worried mother how to give a paediatric syrup with a measuring cup.
   - c. Telephoning a doctor about a possible prescription error, politely and clearly.
4. The observer fills the checklist and gives feedback, starting with one strength.
5. Rotate roles until each student has played the pharmacist.
6. Record one role play; list two improvements and repeat it.

## Observations

**Unclear → clear**

| Unclear | Clear (7 Cs) |
| --- | --- |
| Take it sometimes when needed. | Take one tablet when you have pain, not more than three in a day. |
| Keep it properly. | Store it in a cool, dry place away from children. |

**Observer checklist (10 marks)**

| Point | Marks | Given |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Greeted, introduced self, confirmed patient identity | 1 | 1 |
| Explained purpose, dose, timing clearly | 2 | 2 |
| Used simple words, no jargon | 1 | 1 |
| Good eye contact, open posture, calm tone | 2 | 1 |
| Listened without interrupting | 1 | 1 |
| Checked understanding (teach-back) | 2 | 1 |
| Polite closing, invited questions | 1 | 1 |
| **Total** | **10** | **8** |

## Result

The student completed ___ communication scenarios, applied the 7 Cs and scored ___ / 10 on the observer checklist.

## Precautions

1. Keep the patient's information private; speak at a level others cannot overhear.
2. Do not use medical abbreviations or jargon with patients.
3. Avoid talking down; show empathy.
4. Always check understanding before ending.
5. In a real setting, follow the prescription and approved product information exactly.

## Viva questions

1. **What makes communication effective?** The receiver understands the message as intended and gives feedback.
2. **List the 7 Cs.** Clear, concise, concrete, correct, coherent, complete, courteous.
3. **Why is feedback important?** It confirms understanding and closes the communication loop.
4. **What is the teach-back method?** Asking the listener to explain the message in their own words.
5. **Name two non-verbal cues in counselling.** Eye contact and open posture (also tone, facial expression).
6. **What should a pharmacist do if a prescription seems wrong?** Contact the prescriber politely and clarify before dispensing.

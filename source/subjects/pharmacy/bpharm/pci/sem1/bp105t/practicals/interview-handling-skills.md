# Interview handling skills: mock interview

**Aim:** To prepare for and face a job or admission interview, answering common questions with confidence, and to evaluate performance in a mock interview.

> Language lab activities vary between colleges. Follow your lab teacher and your lab manual where they differ from this guide.

## Principle

An interview is a planned, two-way conversation in which the employer judges the candidate's knowledge, attitude and communication, and the candidate learns about the job. Most of the impression comes from preparation, clear answers and confident body language.

**Stages**

```text
Before: research the organisation, read the job description, prepare answers,
        prepare documents (CV, certificates), plan dress and travel
During: greet, sit when asked, listen fully, answer briefly with examples, ask a question at the end
After:  thank the panel, follow up politely if appropriate, note what to improve
```

**Common questions and how to answer**

| Question | Good approach |
| --- | --- |
| Tell me about yourself. | 60–90 seconds: education, key skills, one achievement, why this job |
| Why do you want this job? | Link your interest to the organisation's work |
| What are your strengths? | Two strengths with a short example each |
| What is your weakness? | A real minor weakness and what you are doing to improve |
| Why should we hire you? | Match your skills to their needs |
| Describe a difficult situation you handled. | Use STAR: Situation, Task, Action, Result |
| Do you have any questions for us? | Ask about training, duties or growth, not only salary |

**Do's and don'ts**

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Reach 10–15 minutes early | Be late or rush in |
| Dress neatly and formally | Wear loud clothes or strong perfume |
| Greet the panel, wait to be seated | Sit before being asked |
| Keep eye contact with all members | Look down or only at one person |
| Answer honestly; say "I don't know" if needed | Bluff, argue or criticise past teachers or employers |
| Keep the phone switched off | Chew gum, fidget or slouch |

## Requirements

Language lab with the interview skills module, question bank, mock interview panel (teacher and two students), resume of each student, video or voice recorder, evaluation sheet, timer.

## Procedure

1. Watch or listen to the module's sample interviews; note good and poor answers.
2. Prepare a one-page resume and a 60–90 second "Tell me about yourself" answer.
3. Write STAR answers to two behavioural questions.
4. **Mock interview (8–10 minutes):** a panel interviews each student for the post of trainee pharmacist in a hospital pharmacy. Questions include personal, subject (for example, "What is a prescription?", "How should vaccines be stored?") and situational questions ("A patient shouts at you about a delayed medicine. What do you do?").
5. Panel members fill the evaluation sheet independently.
6. Give feedback; the student notes two strengths and two points to improve.

## Observations

**Sample STAR answer:** *Situation:* During our college health camp, many people arrived at once. *Task:* I had to manage the registration desk. *Action:* I made a token system and asked a friend to guide the waiting line. *Result:* Waiting time fell and all 120 people were registered by noon.

**Evaluation sheet (20 marks)**

| Criterion | Marks | Given |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Appearance and entry | 2 | 2 |
| Self-introduction | 3 | 2 |
| Subject knowledge | 5 | 4 |
| Clarity and language | 4 | 3 |
| Body language and confidence | 3 | 2 |
| Attitude and questions asked | 3 | 3 |
| **Total** | **20** | **16** |

## Result

The student faced a mock interview of ___ minutes and scored ___ / 20; points to improve: ___.

## Precautions

1. Keep answers relevant and brief; stop when the point is made.
2. Never give false information in the resume or answers.
3. Listen to the full question before answering; ask politely for repetition if unclear.
4. Stay calm under stress questions.
5. Thank the panel before leaving.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the purpose of an interview?** To judge the candidate's suitability for the job and let the candidate learn about the organisation.
2. **What is the STAR method?** Answering with Situation, Task, Action and Result.
3. **How should you answer "Tell me about yourself"?** Briefly: education, skills, one achievement and why you want the job.
4. **Give two don'ts of an interview.** Arriving late and criticising former employers or teachers.
5. **What should you do if you don't know an answer?** Say so honestly and, if possible, say how you would find out.
6. **Why research the organisation?** To answer "why us" well and ask sensible questions.

# Asking questions

**Aim:** To form correct open and closed questions, use polite question forms, and apply them in a patient-counselling and information-seeking conversation.

> Language lab activities vary between colleges. Follow your lab teacher and your lab manual where they differ from this guide.

## Principle

A pharmacist gathers most information by asking questions: what the patient is taking, how they take it, and whether they have allergies. The type and order of questions decide how much useful information you get.

**Types of questions**

| Type | Begins with | Answer | Pharmacy example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Closed (yes/no) | Auxiliary verb: is, are, do, does, did, can, have | Yes or no | "Do you have any drug allergy?" |
| Open (Wh-) | What, when, where, who, why, which, how | Details | "How do you take this tablet?" |
| Choice | Auxiliary + "or" | One of the choices | "Do you take it before or after food?" |
| Tag question | Statement + short question | Confirms | "You finished the full course, didn't you?" |
| Indirect (polite) | Could you tell me…, Do you know… | Details, politely | "Could you tell me when the pain started?" |

**Word order rules**

```text
Closed:  Auxiliary + subject + main verb ...?     Does   she   take     insulin?
Open:    Wh-word + auxiliary + subject + verb ...? When does  she  take  insulin?
Indirect: Could you tell me + Wh-word + subject + verb (normal order, no "does")
          Could you tell me when she takes insulin?
```

Tag questions: a positive statement takes a negative tag and a negative statement a positive tag ("He is a doctor, isn't he?" / "You don't smoke, do you?").

Start with open questions to let the patient talk, then use closed questions to confirm specific facts.

## Requirements

Language lab with headsets and the software module on asking questions, worksheets, role-play cards, voice recorder, timer.

## Procedure

1. Listen to the model questions in the module and repeat them, noting the rising tone at the end of yes/no questions and the falling tone of Wh-questions.
2. Complete the worksheet: change statements into closed, open and indirect questions.
3. Correct common errors (for example, "Where you are going?" → "Where are you going?").
4. **Pair activity:** one student plays a patient who brings a prescription for an antibiotic; the other plays the pharmacist and must find out, only by asking questions, the patient's name and age, allergies, other medicines, and whether the patient understands how to take the course.
5. Record the conversation, play it back and count open and closed questions used.
6. Swap roles with a new scenario: a pharmacist phones a doctor to clarify an unclear dose.

## Observations

**Sample worksheet with answers**

| Statement | Closed question | Open question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| She takes the tablet at night. | Does she take the tablet at night? | When does she take the tablet? |
| He was admitted on Monday. | Was he admitted on Monday? | When was he admitted? |
| They bought the syrup from the store. | Did they buy the syrup from the store? | Where did they buy the syrup? |
| The dose is 5 ml. | Is the dose 5 ml? | What is the dose? |

**Self-check count:** open questions ___, closed questions ___, polite indirect questions ___.

**Evaluation (10 marks):** correct word order 3, suitable mix of open and closed questions 3, polite tone 2, listening to answers before the next question 2.

## Result

The student framed ___ correct questions out of ___ in the worksheet and gathered all required details in the role play using ___ open and ___ closed questions.

## Precautions

1. Do not fire many questions at once; ask one and wait for the answer.
2. Avoid leading questions ("You don't have any allergy, right?") when the answer matters for safety.
3. In indirect questions keep normal subject–verb order.
4. Use "why" carefully; it can sound like blame. "What made you stop the tablets?" is softer.
5. Thank the patient for the information.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the difference between open and closed questions?** Open questions invite detailed answers; closed questions get a yes/no or a single fact.
2. **Which questions should a pharmacist start counselling with?** Open questions, so the patient explains in their own words.
3. **Correct "What you are taking?"** "What are you taking?"
4. **What is an indirect question?** A polite form beginning "Could you tell me…" with normal word order.
5. **Give the tag for "She is your sister".** "isn't she?"
6. **Why avoid leading questions?** They push the person toward an expected answer and may hide important facts.

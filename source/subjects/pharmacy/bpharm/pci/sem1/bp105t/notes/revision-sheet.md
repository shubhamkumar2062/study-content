# Last-night revision sheet

## Unit 1: Process, barriers, perspectives

- **Communication** = sharing ideas, information or feelings so that the receiver understands them as meant and responds. It is a two-way, continuous process. From the Latin *communis* ("common").
- **Importance**: patient safety, teamwork, trust, running an organisation, decisions, business, career, preventing conflict.
- **Process**: Source → Encoding → Message → Channel → Decoding → Receiver → Feedback, all inside a Context. **Noise** can enter at any step.
- **Feedback** closes the loop. A pharmacist uses **teach-back** (the patient repeats the instructions).
- **Models**: linear (one-way), interactive (adds feedback), transactional (both send and receive at once).
- **Eight barriers**, each with an example:
  - Physiological: hearing loss.
  - Physical: noisy counter.
  - Cultural: fasting or gestures.
  - Language: jargon.
  - Gender: embarrassment.
  - Interpersonal: fear of a senior.
  - Psychological: prejudice.
  - Emotional: shock or anger.
- **7 Cs**: clear, concise, concrete, correct, coherent, complete, courteous.
- **Perspective** is shaped by visual perception (illusions, first impressions, selective perception), language (denotation and connotation), past experiences, prejudices, feelings and environment.

## Unit 2: Elements and styles

- A face-to-face message is words + tone of voice + body language. For feelings and attitudes the rough split is 7 / 38 / 55 % (Mehrabian). It applies to feelings only.
- **Paralanguage**: pitch, volume, pace, pause, intonation, stress, clarity.
- **Non-verbal forms**: face, eye contact, gestures, posture, proxemics (space), haptics (touch), appearance, chronemics (time).
- **Hall's distances**: intimate, personal, social, public.
- **Functions of non-verbal cues**: repeat, substitute, complement, contradict, regulate, accent.
- **Verbal communication** is oral or written. Oral is quick and gets feedback, but leaves no record.
- **Physical communication**: presence, actions, demonstration, setting (for example, standing to greet, coming out from behind the counter).
- **Styles matrix**: directness × openness.

| Style | Combines | Typical traits | How to deal with it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Direct | Direct + self-contained | Results, fast, blunt | Be brief |
| Spirited | Direct + open | Ideas, enthusiastic, may skip detail | Be energetic, then fix the details |
| Systematic | Indirect + self-contained | Facts, precise, slow | Give data |
| Considerate | Indirect + open | People, harmony, avoids conflict | Be supportive |

## Unit 3: Listening and writing

- Hearing is physical. Listening is active: receive → attend → understand → remember → evaluate → respond.
- **Types of listening**: informational, critical, empathic, appreciative, discriminative.
- **Self-awareness**: know your biases, triggers and habits.
- **Active listening tools**: attention, encouragers, paraphrase, reflect feelings, clarify, summarise, defer judgement. Use open questions to gather information and closed questions to confirm.
- **Difficult situations**: stay calm, let the person speak, acknowledge feelings, do not argue, solve the problem (LEAP). On the phone, read back drug names and numbers.
- **Writing**: advantages are a permanent record, detail and reach. Limitations are that it is slow, has no tone and gives no instant feedback.
- **When to use writing**, by four tests:
  - Complexity: complex detail → writing.
  - Amount of discussion: much discussion needed → talk instead.
  - Shades of meaning: sensitive matters → face to face.
  - Formality: formal records → writing.
- **Writing effectively**:
  - A specific subject line.
  - The main point first (inverted pyramid).
  - Know your audience.
  - Organise as opening, body, closing, with action and deadline.
  - Proofread.

## Unit 4: Interviews and presentations

- **Interview** = a planned two-way conversation for selection.
- **Purposes for the employer**: verify the CV, assess knowledge, attitude, communication and fit.
- **Purpose for the candidate**: learn about the job and decide.
- **Prepare**: research the employer, prepare the CV and documents, practise answers (STAR = Situation, Task, Action, Result), plan your dress, arrive 10–15 minutes early.
- **Do's**: greet, wait to sit, eye contact, listen, honest concise answers, positive attitude, ask a question at the end, thank the panel.
- **Don'ts**: be late, slouch or fidget, interrupt, lie or bluff, criticise past employers, ask about salary first, keep the phone on.
- **Presentation steps**:
  1. Deal with fear: prepare, practise aloud, breathe, check the venue, memorise the opening.
  2. Plan: purpose, audience, time, venue, content, visuals.
  3. Structure: introduction (hook, outline) → body (about three points) → conclusion (summary, take-home message, questions).
  4. Deliver: voice, eye contact, gestures, engage the audience, keep time, handle questions.
- **Delivery techniques**: extempore (best), manuscript, memorised, impromptu.

## Unit 5: Group discussion

- **GD** = a cooperative discussion among about 6–12 people on a topic, usually 10–20 minutes, with no fixed leader. A debate is competitive; a GD is not.
- **Tests**: communication, knowledge, reasoning, teamwork, leadership, attitude.
- **Topic types**: factual, controversial, abstract, case-based, current affairs.
- **Roles**:
  - Good: initiator, information giver, clarifier, coordinator, harmoniser, summariser.
  - Bad: dominator, aggressor, blocker, silent spectator.
- **Skills**: brief relevant points, active listening, entering politely, disagreeing politely, involving quiet members, summarising fairly (no new points).
- **Do's and don'ts**: listen, give facts, be polite, include others. Don't interrupt, shout, dominate, make up data, or stay silent.

# How to use this guide

Communication Skills is a short, scoring subject with no numericals and no formulas. Marks come from clear definitions, neat lists, labelled diagrams and tables, and sensible examples. Study one unit at a time: read the notes, then close them and write each list (barriers, elements of the process, the four styles, the do's and don'ts) from memory. Finally answer the question bank aloud or on paper. Each unit has very short (2-mark), short answer (5-mark) and long essay (10-mark) questions, rated by how often that kind of question appears in PCI-syllabus papers.

**Units.** The five units follow the five PCI units exactly; no unit was split or joined. Unit 5 (Group discussion) is the smallest in the syllabus, so its notes also cover teamwork and leadership in a group, which the course objectives ask for.

Sessional papers for non-university subjects are set for 30 marks (long answers 1 of 2 × 10, short answers 4 of 6 × 5) and converted to 10 marks.

**End-semester paper (35 marks, 1.5 hours).** The PCI regulations print this pattern for a 35-mark theory paper, and the full mock paper in this app follows it. There is no MCQ section, but the MCQs here are still good revision for definitions and lists. BP105T is a college-conducted exam, so **confirm the pattern with your college**.

**Practical (BP111P).** Also a non-university exam: 15 marks at the end of the semester (2 hours) plus 10 internal marks (5 continuous + 5 sessional), total 25. The practical is a language-lab course. The guides in this pack follow each module of the PCI list. "Listening comprehension / Direct and indirect speech" appears as one line in the syllabus but has two separate guides here.

## Probability ratings

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with a diagram or table and examples.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful as a backup choice and for viva and MCQs.

These ratings are estimates from common patterns and syllabus weight, not a promise of what will be asked.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. Open every answer with a one-line definition, then write the points under small headings.
2. Draw the communication process cycle (with feedback and context) in any answer about communication. It takes one minute and earns marks.
3. Use tables for comparisons: hearing vs listening, verbal vs non-verbal, the four styles, GD vs debate, do's vs don'ts.
4. Give a pharmacy example for each point: counselling a patient, talking to a doctor, an interview for a hospital pharmacist post. Examiners like the link to the profession.
5. For "barriers" questions, write all eight barriers named in the syllabus, each with one example and one way to overcome it.
6. For the styles matrix, draw the 2 × 2 box and label the two dimensions (directness and openness).
7. A 10-mark answer needs about six to eight headed points plus a diagram or table and a short conclusion. A 5-mark answer needs about five clear points.
8. Write neatly and in simple, correct English. In this subject the way you write is itself being judged.

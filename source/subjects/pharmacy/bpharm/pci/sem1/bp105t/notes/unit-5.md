# Unit 5: Group discussion

Group discussion (GD) is used by companies, hospitals and institutes to select candidates, and it is also how real decisions are made in pharmacy — in drug and therapeutics committees, quality review meetings and team huddles. It tests the very skills this course aims at: speaking, listening, teamwork and leadership. This is the shortest unit in the syllabus, so these notes also cover the closely related ideas of teamwork and leadership from the course objectives.

## 5.1 Introduction

**Definition.** A group discussion is a structured, purposeful conversation among a small group of people (usually about 6–12) on a given topic, in which members share ideas, information and opinions to understand the topic better, reach a conclusion or solve a problem, often within a fixed time.

**Features of a GD**

1. A small group sits together (often in a circle or semicircle).
2. A topic or case is given; there may be a few minutes to think.
3. Usually 10–20 minutes of discussion, sometimes followed by a summary.
4. Every member has an equal right to speak; there is normally no appointed leader.
5. In a selection GD, observers (assessors) watch and score each participant.
6. The aim is collective understanding, not winning an argument.

**GD versus debate**

| Group discussion | Debate |
| --- | --- |
| Cooperative; members build on each other's ideas | Competitive; two sides argue for and against |
| Participants may hold any view and may change it | Each speaker defends an assigned side |
| Aim: a shared conclusion or better understanding | Aim: to win by persuading judges |
| No fixed order of speaking | Fixed order and time for each speaker |

**Purposes of a GD in selection** — to assess, in a short time and in a group setting:

- Communication skills (clarity, fluency, listening)
- Subject knowledge and general awareness
- Logical thinking and analytical ability
- Teamwork and interpersonal skills
- Leadership and initiative
- Attitude, confidence, flexibility and emotional control

**Types of GD topics**

| Type | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Factual | Generic medicines and Jan Aushadhi stores in India |
| Controversial | Should online pharmacies be allowed to sell all medicines? |
| Abstract | "Red" or "Bridges" (open, creative topics) |
| Case-based | A patient is given a wrong strength of tablet at your pharmacy — what should the team do? |
| Current affairs | Antimicrobial resistance as a global threat |

Some GDs are **topic-based**, others **case-based** (a situation is described and the group must decide what to do).

**Roles people take in a GD**

| Role | What the person does |
| --- | --- |
| Initiator | Opens the discussion, defines the topic, sets direction |
| Information giver | Contributes facts, data and examples |
| Opinion giver | Shares views and reasoning |
| Clarifier / elaborator | Explains and expands others' points |
| Coordinator / gate-keeper | Brings in quiet members, keeps order, controls time |
| Encourager / harmoniser | Supports others, reduces tension, settles conflicts |
| Summariser | Pulls the discussion together at the end |
| Negative roles to avoid | Dominator, aggressor, blocker (opposes everything), silent spectator, joker, topic-jumper |

## 5.2 Communication skills in group discussion

A GD tests communication in real time. The skills needed:

### Speaking skills

- **Clarity and brevity** — make one point at a time in simple language; do not lecture.
- **Relevance** — stay on the topic; link your point to what was said before.
- **Content quality** — support views with facts, examples and logic; quality matters more than how much you speak.
- **Voice** — audible, moderate pace, confident tone; no shouting to be heard.
- **Language** — correct, polite and formal; avoid slang and jargon.

### Listening skills

- Listen actively to every speaker; it gives you material to respond to.
- Note key points briefly if allowed.
- Acknowledge others: "As Priya rightly said…", "Building on that point…".
- Listening helps you avoid repeating points already made.

### Non-verbal skills

- Sit upright and lean slightly forward; look at the group, not only at the assessors.
- Use natural hand gestures; avoid pointing fingers at others.
- Keep facial expressions positive even when disagreeing.
- Nod to show you are following.

### Interpersonal and team skills

- **Initiating** — starting well earns credit, but only if you have a sensible opening point (defining the topic, giving its importance, raising key issues).
- **Entering the discussion** — wait for a natural pause; use polite openers: "May I add something here?", "I would like to look at another side".
- **Disagreeing politely** — "I see your point, but I feel…", "That is true to an extent; however…"; challenge the idea, never the person.
- **Bringing others in** — "We have not heard from Rahul yet; what do you think?"
- **Handling conflict** — stay calm, bring the group back to common ground and facts.
- **Time management** — keep track and help the group move towards a conclusion.
- **Summarising** — at the end, briefly and fairly present the main viewpoints and the conclusion (if any), without adding new points.

### Leadership in a GD

Leadership in a GD is not about speaking the most or the loudest. A leader:

- gives direction (suggests a structure: causes, effects, solutions);
- keeps the discussion on track and within time;
- encourages participation and balances the group;
- resolves conflicts and builds consensus;
- helps the group reach a conclusion.

Good leaders also show **team-player qualities**: respect for others, cooperation, sharing credit, flexibility and commitment to the group goal — exactly the qualities a pharmacist needs in a healthcare team.

**How assessors usually score a GD (typical criteria)**

| Criterion | What they look for |
| --- | --- |
| Content / knowledge | Relevant facts, depth, examples |
| Communication | Clarity, fluency, language |
| Listening | Responds to others, builds on points |
| Leadership / initiative | Initiates, directs, summarises |
| Teamwork | Cooperation, involves others, no domination |
| Logical reasoning | Structured, sound arguments |
| Body language and attitude | Confidence, calmness, politeness |

## 5.3 Do's and don'ts of group discussion

| Do's | Don'ts |
| --- | --- |
| Read widely: newspapers, health news, pharmacy topics | Go without any general awareness |
| Use thinking time to jot down key points | Start speaking without a clear idea |
| Initiate if you have a strong, relevant opening | Initiate with a weak or wrong point just to be first |
| Listen carefully to everyone | Ignore others or wait only for your turn to speak |
| Make relevant, logical points with facts and examples | Quote doubtful statistics or make up figures |
| Speak clearly, politely and at moderate pace | Shout, speak too fast or mumble |
| Acknowledge others' points before adding your own | Interrupt or cut others off |
| Disagree politely and give reasons | Attack, mock or argue personally |
| Encourage quiet members to speak | Dominate the discussion |
| Keep positive, confident body language | Fold arms, look bored, look only at the assessors |
| Stay on the topic and help the group move forward | Drift into unrelated topics |
| Keep your opinions balanced and flexible | Be rigid or change sides without reason |
| Summarise fairly if given the chance | Introduce new points in the summary |
| Use formal, correct language | Use slang, offensive or regional jokes |
| Respect time limits | Keep repeating the same point |
| Stay calm if the group becomes noisy; re-enter politely | Remain silent throughout |

**Useful phrases**

- Starting: "Let us begin by understanding what the topic means…"
- Agreeing: "I agree with that, and I would add…"
- Disagreeing: "I respect your view, but the data suggest…"
- Clarifying: "Could you explain what you meant by…?"
- Bringing back on track: "That is interesting, but coming back to our topic…"
- Concluding: "To sum up, the group felt that…"

## Remember

- GD = structured group conversation on a topic to share views and reach understanding or a conclusion; cooperative, unlike a debate.
- In selection it tests communication, knowledge, reasoning, teamwork, leadership and attitude.
- Positive roles: initiator, information giver, clarifier, coordinator, harmoniser, summariser; avoid dominator, aggressor, blocker, silent spectator.
- Skills: speaking, active listening, non-verbal, entering politely, disagreeing politely, involving others, summarising.
- Leadership in a GD means direction, balance and consensus, not loudness.
- Learn the do's and don'ts as a two-column table; it is a frequent 5-mark question.

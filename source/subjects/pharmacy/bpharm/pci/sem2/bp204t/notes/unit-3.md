# Unit 3: Blood, endocrine, nervous system disorders and peptic ulcer

This unit covers four systems: anaemias and haemophilia, diabetes and thyroid and sex-hormone disorders, the major brain disorders (epilepsy, Parkinson's disease, stroke, depression, schizophrenia, Alzheimer's disease) and peptic ulcer. It is a large unit, so it usually supplies one long question and two or three short ones. Diabetes mellitus, anaemias, Parkinson's disease and peptic ulcer appear most often.

## 3.1 Anaemia: general points

**Anaemia** is a fall in the haemoglobin concentration (or red cell mass) below normal for age and sex, so that the blood carries less oxygen. WHO cut-offs: below **13 g/dL in adult men**, below **12 g/dL in non-pregnant women**, below **11 g/dL in pregnancy**.

**Common symptoms**: tiredness, weakness, breathlessness on exertion, palpitations, dizziness, headache, pallor of skin, nails and conjunctiva; in severe cases angina and heart failure.

**Classification by red cell size (MCV)**

| Type | MCV | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Microcytic hypochromic | Low (small, pale cells) | Iron deficiency, thalassaemia, anaemia of chronic disease (some) |
| Normocytic normochromic | Normal | Acute blood loss, chronic kidney disease, aplastic anaemia, haemolytic anaemias |
| Macrocytic | High (large cells) | Vitamin B12 and folic acid deficiency (megaloblastic), alcohol, liver disease |

**Classification by cause**: blood loss; reduced production (deficiency of iron, B12, folate; marrow failure); increased destruction (haemolytic anaemias: sickle cell, thalassaemia, G6PD deficiency, hereditary spherocytosis, autoimmune).

## 3.2 Iron deficiency anaemia

The most common anaemia in the world and in India, especially in women and children.

**Causes**
- Chronic blood loss — the main cause in adults: heavy menstruation, peptic ulcer, piles, hookworm infestation, gut cancer, regular NSAID use.
- Increased need — pregnancy, lactation, growing children.
- Poor intake — vegetarian diet low in iron, poverty.
- Poor absorption — achlorhydria, gastrectomy, coeliac disease (iron is absorbed mainly in the duodenum, in the ferrous form).

**Pathogenesis**: body iron stores (ferritin) are used up first → serum iron falls and transferrin (TIBC) rises → haem synthesis fails → small, pale red cells.

**Features**: general anaemia symptoms plus spoon-shaped nails (koilonychia), brittle nails, smooth tongue (glossitis), cracks at mouth corners (angular stomatitis), craving for non-food items such as clay or ice (pica), difficulty swallowing from oesophageal webs.

**Blood findings**: low Hb, low MCV and MCH, microcytic hypochromic cells, **low serum ferritin** (best single test), low serum iron, high TIBC.

## 3.3 Megaloblastic anaemia (vitamin B12 and folic acid deficiency)

Both vitamins are needed for **DNA synthesis** (thymidine production). When either is lacking, the nucleus matures more slowly than the cytoplasm, so the marrow makes large abnormal precursors (**megaloblasts**) and large red cells (macrocytes). Many die in the marrow (ineffective erythropoiesis).

| Feature | Vitamin B12 deficiency | Folic acid deficiency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sources | Animal foods only (meat, fish, eggs, milk) | Green leafy vegetables, liver, legumes; destroyed by long cooking |
| Body stores | Large (lasts a few years) | Small (lasts a few months) |
| Main causes | **Pernicious anaemia** (autoimmune loss of intrinsic factor from gastric parietal cells), strict vegetarian diet, gastrectomy, ileal disease or resection, fish tapeworm | Poor diet, pregnancy, alcoholism, malabsorption, haemolysis, drugs (methotrexate, phenytoin, trimethoprim) |
| Neurological signs | **Yes** — subacute combined degeneration of the spinal cord, numbness and tingling, loss of vibration and position sense, memory loss | No |
| Special point | Giving folic acid alone may correct the blood picture but let nerve damage progress | Deficiency in early pregnancy raises the risk of neural tube defects |

**Blood picture**: raised MCV, oval macrocytes, **hypersegmented neutrophils** (5 or more lobes), low reticulocytes, raised LDH and bilirubin (destruction in marrow). Glossitis (red beefy tongue) and mild jaundice are common.

## 3.4 Sickle cell anaemia

An **inherited (autosomal recessive)** disorder caused by a point mutation in the β-globin gene: glutamic acid at position 6 is replaced by **valine**, producing **haemoglobin S**.

```text
Low O2 / dehydration / acidosis / infection
→ HbS polymerises into long rigid fibres
→ red cell becomes sickle-shaped and stiff
→ (1) cells block small vessels → ISCHAEMIA, pain crises, infarcts
→ (2) cells destroyed early → HAEMOLYTIC ANAEMIA
```

- Homozygous (HbSS) = sickle cell disease; heterozygous (HbAS) = sickle cell trait, usually symptom-free, and partly protected against falciparum malaria.
- **Features**: chronic haemolytic anaemia with jaundice and gallstones; painful (vaso-occlusive) crises in bones, chest and abdomen; hand–foot swelling in infants; stroke; acute chest syndrome; leg ulcers; priapism; **autosplenectomy** (spleen destroyed by repeated infarcts) → serious infections (pneumococcus, Salmonella osteomyelitis); aplastic crisis with parvovirus B19.
- In India it is common in tribal populations of central, western and southern states.

## 3.5 Thalassaemia

Inherited (autosomal recessive) disorders in which **too little** of one globin chain is made, although the chain itself is normal. The excess unpaired chain precipitates in red cells and their precursors, causing ineffective erythropoiesis and haemolysis.

| Type | Defect | Forms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| β-thalassaemia | Reduced (β+) or absent (β0) β-chain synthesis, mostly point mutations | **Major** (Cooley's anaemia, homozygous): severe anaemia from about 6 months of age, needs regular transfusion; **minor/trait** (heterozygous): mild microcytosis, no symptoms; intermedia: between the two |
| α-thalassaemia | Deletion of α-globin genes (4 genes normally) | Silent carrier (1 deleted), trait (2), HbH disease (3), hydrops fetalis (all 4, fatal before birth) |

**Features of thalassaemia major**: severe anaemia, failure to thrive, enlarged liver and spleen, marrow expansion causing "chipmunk" face and "hair-on-end" skull X-ray, and **iron overload** from transfusions and increased absorption (heart failure, liver cirrhosis, diabetes, delayed puberty) — hence iron chelation. Blood: microcytic hypochromic cells, target cells, raised HbF and HbA2 (on electrophoresis in β-thalassaemia).

## 3.6 Hereditary and acquired anaemias

The syllabus groups anaemias by origin.

| Hereditary (inherited) | Acquired |
| --- | --- |
| Sickle cell anaemia, thalassaemias (haemoglobin defects) | Nutritional: iron, B12, folate deficiency |
| Hereditary spherocytosis (red cell membrane defect; spherical fragile cells removed by the spleen) | Blood loss: acute or chronic |
| G6PD deficiency (enzyme defect, X-linked; haemolysis after oxidant drugs such as primaquine, sulfonamides, dapsone, or fava beans, infection) | Aplastic anaemia (marrow failure from drugs such as chloramphenicol, radiation, viruses, autoimmune) |
| Fanconi anaemia (inherited marrow failure) | Autoimmune haemolytic anaemia; drug-induced haemolysis; malaria |
| | Anaemia of chronic disease (infection, inflammation, cancer), anaemia of kidney disease (low erythropoietin) |

## 3.7 Haemophilia

An inherited **bleeding disorder** due to deficiency of a clotting factor.

| Feature | Haemophilia A | Haemophilia B (Christmas disease) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deficient factor | Factor VIII | Factor IX |
| Frequency | More common (about 80–85%) | Less common |
| Inheritance | X-linked recessive | X-linked recessive |

- Males are affected; females are usually carriers; there is no male-to-male transmission.
- **Features**: easy bruising, prolonged bleeding after cuts, tooth extraction or surgery; deep bleeding into **joints (haemarthrosis)** — knees, elbows, ankles — leading to crippling arthritis; bleeding into muscles; bleeding in the brain can be fatal. Severity depends on the factor level.
- **Tests**: prolonged aPTT; normal PT, platelet count and bleeding time; low factor assay.
- Avoid aspirin, NSAIDs and intramuscular injections.

## 3.8 Diabetes mellitus

**Definition.** A group of metabolic disorders with chronic **hyperglycaemia** due to a defect in insulin secretion, insulin action, or both, with disturbed carbohydrate, fat and protein metabolism.

**Diagnostic criteria** (any one, confirmed on repeat if no symptoms):
- Fasting plasma glucose **126 mg/dL or more** (normal below 100).
- 2-hour plasma glucose **200 mg/dL or more** in a 75 g oral glucose tolerance test.
- HbA1c **6.5% or more**.
- Random plasma glucose 200 mg/dL or more with classic symptoms.

### Types

| Feature | Type 1 | Type 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cause | Autoimmune destruction of pancreatic β-cells (T cells; antibodies to GAD, islet cells, insulin) in a genetically prone person, often after a viral trigger | Insulin resistance in muscle, fat and liver + progressive β-cell dysfunction |
| Insulin level | Absent or very low | Normal, high or relatively low |
| Age of onset | Usually children and young adults | Usually adults over 30–40 (now younger, with obesity) |
| Body build | Usually thin | Usually overweight, central obesity |
| Onset | Sudden | Slow; often found by chance |
| Ketoacidosis | Common | Rare |
| Share of cases | About 5–10% | About 90% |
| Treatment | Insulin essential | Diet, exercise, oral drugs; insulin later if needed |

Other types: **gestational diabetes** (first detected in pregnancy), and secondary diabetes (pancreatitis, Cushing's syndrome, corticosteroid therapy, acromegaly).

### Pathophysiology of the symptoms

```text
↓ insulin action
├─ ↓ glucose uptake by muscle/fat + ↑ liver glucose output → HYPERGLYCAEMIA
│     → glucose in urine (above renal threshold ≈ 180 mg/dL) → osmotic diuresis
│     → POLYURIA → dehydration → POLYDIPSIA (thirst)
├─ cells "starve" → POLYPHAGIA, weight loss, fatigue
├─ ↑ fat breakdown → free fatty acids → liver makes KETONE bodies → ketoacidosis
└─ ↑ protein breakdown → muscle wasting, poor healing
```

### Complications

| Acute | Chronic: microvascular | Chronic: macrovascular | Others |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Diabetic ketoacidosis (mainly type 1): hyperglycaemia, ketosis, metabolic acidosis, Kussmaul breathing, fruity breath, dehydration | Retinopathy (leading cause of blindness) | Coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction | Infections (skin, urinary, TB, fungal) |
| Hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state (mainly type 2): very high glucose, severe dehydration, little ketosis | Nephropathy (proteinuria → chronic kidney disease) | Stroke | Diabetic foot: neuropathy + poor circulation + infection → ulcer, gangrene, amputation |
| Hypoglycaemia (from insulin or sulfonylureas): sweating, tremor, confusion, coma | Neuropathy (glove-and-stocking numbness, autonomic neuropathy) | Peripheral arterial disease | Cataract |

Long-term high glucose damages tissue by glycation of proteins (advanced glycation end products), the polyol (sorbitol) pathway and oxidative stress.

## 3.9 Thyroid diseases

Thyroid hormones (T4 and T3) control the metabolic rate. Release is controlled by **TRH** (hypothalamus) → **TSH** (pituitary) → thyroid, with negative feedback. Iodine is needed for hormone synthesis.

### Hyperthyroidism (thyrotoxicosis)

**Causes**: **Graves' disease** (most common; autoimmune — antibodies stimulate the TSH receptor), toxic multinodular goitre, toxic adenoma, thyroiditis, excess thyroid hormone tablets or iodine.

**Features**: weight loss with good appetite, heat intolerance, sweating, palpitations, tachycardia, atrial fibrillation, tremor, anxiety, irritability, diarrhoea, menstrual disturbance, goitre. Graves' disease adds **exophthalmos** (bulging eyes) and pretibial myxoedema. Blood: high T3/T4, low TSH. Severe form: thyroid storm.

### Hypothyroidism

**Causes**: **Hashimoto's thyroiditis** (autoimmune destruction, the most common in iodine-sufficient areas), iodine deficiency (still seen in hilly regions of India), after surgery or radioiodine, drugs (lithium, amiodarone), pituitary disease.

**Features**: tiredness, weight gain, cold intolerance, dry coarse skin, hair loss, hoarse voice, constipation, slow pulse, puffy face, heavy periods, depression, slow reflexes. Severe adult form = **myxoedema** (non-pitting swelling; myxoedema coma). In infants = **cretinism**: mental retardation, short stature, coarse face, large tongue, umbilical hernia — hence newborn screening. Blood: low T4, high TSH (primary).

### Goitre

Enlargement of the thyroid. May be simple (iodine deficiency), multinodular, toxic or non-toxic. Low hormone → high TSH → gland enlarges.

## 3.10 Disorders of sex hormones

| Disorder | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Male hypogonadism | Low testosterone. Primary (testis failure: Klinefelter syndrome 47,XXY, mumps orchitis, injury) or secondary (pituitary/hypothalamus failure). Features: reduced libido, erectile dysfunction, infertility, less body hair, gynaecomastia |
| Female hypogonadism | Ovarian failure (Turner syndrome 45,XO, premature ovarian failure) or pituitary causes; amenorrhoea, infertility, osteoporosis |
| Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) | Common; excess androgens, irregular or absent ovulation, multiple small ovarian cysts; acne, hirsutism, obesity, infertility, insulin resistance |
| Hirsutism | Male-pattern hair growth in women due to excess androgens |
| Gynaecomastia | Breast enlargement in men from a high oestrogen-to-androgen ratio (puberty, liver cirrhosis, drugs such as spironolactone) |
| Menopause | Natural fall in oestrogen: hot flushes, mood change, vaginal dryness, osteoporosis, raised cardiovascular risk |
| Precocious / delayed puberty | Puberty too early or too late |
| Hyperprolactinaemia | Pituitary tumour or drugs (antipsychotics): galactorrhoea, amenorrhoea, infertility |

## 3.11 Epilepsy

**Seizure**: a sudden, abnormal, excessive and synchronised electrical discharge of brain neurons causing altered movement, sensation, behaviour or consciousness. **Epilepsy**: a tendency to recurrent unprovoked seizures (two or more).

**Pathophysiology**: imbalance between excitation (glutamate, Na⁺ and Ca²⁺ channels) and inhibition (GABA). Causes include genetic factors, head injury, birth injury, brain tumours, stroke, infections (meningitis, neurocysticercosis — common in India), fever in children, alcohol withdrawal, metabolic upsets (low glucose, low Na⁺).

| Class | Type | Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focal (partial) | Focal aware (simple partial) | Twitching of one limb or odd sensation; consciousness kept |
| | Focal impaired awareness (complex partial) | Altered awareness, automatisms (lip smacking); often temporal lobe |
| | Focal to bilateral tonic-clonic | Starts focal, spreads to both sides |
| Generalised | Tonic-clonic (grand mal) | Loss of consciousness, stiffening (tonic) then jerking (clonic), tongue bite, incontinence, confusion afterwards |
| | Absence (petit mal) | Children; brief blank spells of a few seconds; 3 Hz spike-and-wave on EEG |
| | Myoclonic, atonic, tonic | Sudden jerks; sudden loss of tone (drop attacks) |

**Status epilepticus**: continuous seizure activity, or repeated seizures without recovery between them, lasting 5 minutes or more — an emergency.

## 3.12 Parkinson's disease

A progressive degenerative movement disorder of older people.

**Pathogenesis**: loss of **dopaminergic neurons of the substantia nigra (pars compacta)** that project to the striatum (nigrostriatal pathway). Surviving neurons contain **Lewy bodies** (aggregates of α-synuclein). Low dopamine with relatively high acetylcholine in the striatum disturbs the basal ganglia circuits. Symptoms appear only after a large part of these neurons has been lost. Possible causes: ageing, genes, environmental toxins (MPTP, pesticides), oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction.

**Features (TRAP)**
- **T**remor at rest ("pill-rolling"), lessened by movement.
- **R**igidity ("lead-pipe" or "cog-wheel").
- **A**kinesia / bradykinesia (slow, little movement): mask-like face, small handwriting, reduced blinking.
- **P**ostural instability: stooped posture, shuffling festinating gait, falls.
- Non-motor: depression, constipation, sleep problems, loss of smell, dementia later.

**Secondary parkinsonism**: antipsychotics (dopamine D2 blockers), metoclopramide, head injury, repeated stroke.

## 3.13 Stroke

**Definition.** Sudden loss of brain function lasting more than 24 hours (or leading to death) due to a vascular cause. **Transient ischaemic attack (TIA)**: same symptoms that resolve fully, usually within an hour — a warning sign.

| Type | About | Causes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ischaemic (infarction) | 80–85% | Thrombosis on an atherosclerotic plaque (carotid, cerebral arteries), embolism from the heart (atrial fibrillation, after MI), small-vessel (lacunar) disease |
| Haemorrhagic | 15–20% | Intracerebral haemorrhage (hypertension), subarachnoid haemorrhage (ruptured berry aneurysm, sudden "worst-ever" headache) |

**Pathophysiology of ischaemic stroke**: blocked flow → central core dies within minutes; around it lies the **penumbra**, which can be saved if flow returns quickly. Energy failure → glutamate release → Ca²⁺ influx (excitotoxicity) → free radicals and inflammation → cell death.

**Risk factors**: hypertension (the most important), diabetes, smoking, atrial fibrillation, high cholesterol, obesity, alcohol, age.

**Features (FAST)**: **F**ace drooping, **A**rm weakness, **S**peech difficulty, **T**ime to call for help. Also one-sided paralysis (hemiplegia), sensory loss, vision loss, imbalance, coma.

## 3.14 Psychiatric disorders

### Depression (major depressive disorder)

**Features** (most days for **at least 2 weeks**): low mood, loss of interest or pleasure (anhedonia), poor or increased sleep and appetite, fatigue, poor concentration, guilt or worthlessness, slowed movement or agitation, thoughts of death or suicide.

**Pathophysiology**
- **Monoamine hypothesis**: reduced activity of noradrenaline and serotonin (5-HT), and dopamine, in the brain. Supported by reserpine (depletes monoamines) causing depression and antidepressants raising monoamines. Antidepressants raise levels quickly but act after weeks, so receptor adaptation and other changes are also involved.
- **Neuroendocrine**: overactive hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis, high cortisol.
- **Neurotrophic**: reduced BDNF and shrinkage of hippocampus.
- Genetic tendency, stressful life events, chronic illness, drugs (corticosteroids, some antihypertensives).

Bipolar disorder: episodes of depression alternating with mania.

### Schizophrenia

A chronic psychotic disorder, usually starting in late teens or early adulthood, with disordered thinking and loss of contact with reality.

| Symptom group | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Positive (added) | Hallucinations (mostly hearing voices), delusions (persecution), disorganised speech and behaviour |
| Negative (lost) | Flat emotion, poverty of speech, lack of motivation, social withdrawal |
| Cognitive | Poor attention, memory and planning |

**Pathophysiology**
- **Dopamine hypothesis**: excess dopamine (D2) activity in the **mesolimbic** pathway → positive symptoms; reduced dopamine in the **mesocortical** pathway → negative and cognitive symptoms. Supported by amphetamines causing psychosis and antipsychotics blocking D2 receptors.
- **Glutamate hypothesis**: reduced NMDA receptor function (phencyclidine and ketamine mimic schizophrenia).
- Genetic factors, birth complications, cannabis use, enlarged ventricles on brain scans.

### Alzheimer's disease

The most common cause of **dementia** (progressive loss of memory and thinking severe enough to affect daily life), usually after 65 years.

**Pathology**
- **Extracellular amyloid (senile) plaques** of β-amyloid protein, from abnormal cleavage of amyloid precursor protein.
- **Intracellular neurofibrillary tangles** of hyperphosphorylated **tau** protein.
- Loss of neurons and synapses, especially in the hippocampus and cortex → brain atrophy, enlarged ventricles.
- **Cholinergic deficit**: loss of acetylcholine neurons of the nucleus basalis of Meynert (basis of cholinesterase-inhibitor therapy); excess glutamate activity.

**Risk factors**: age, family history, apolipoprotein E ε4 allele, Down syndrome, head injury, vascular risk factors.

**Features**: early loss of recent memory, repeating questions, losing things; later disorientation, language difficulty, poor judgement, personality change, wandering; finally total dependence and death from infection.

## 3.15 Peptic ulcer

**Definition.** A break in the mucosa of the stomach or duodenum, extending through the muscularis mucosae, exposed to acid and pepsin. Most common in the first part of the duodenum and the lesser curvature of the stomach.

### Pathogenesis: imbalance of aggressive and protective factors

| Aggressive factors | Protective factors |
| --- | --- |
| Hydrochloric acid, pepsin | Mucus–bicarbonate layer |
| ***Helicobacter pylori*** infection (most common cause) | Prostaglandins (PGE2, PGI2): mucus, bicarbonate, blood flow |
| NSAIDs and aspirin (block COX → less protective prostaglandin) | Good mucosal blood flow |
| Smoking, alcohol, stress, corticosteroids with NSAIDs | Rapid cell renewal, tight junctions |
| Zollinger–Ellison syndrome (gastrin-secreting tumour, huge acid output) | |

*H. pylori* is a spiral, gram-negative bacterium that lives under the mucus; its **urease** makes ammonia that neutralises acid around it, and its toxins and the inflammation they cause damage the mucosa and raise gastrin and acid secretion.

### Gastric versus duodenal ulcer

| Feature | Gastric ulcer | Duodenal ulcer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frequency | Less common | More common |
| Acid output | Normal or low | Often high |
| Pain timing | Soon after meals; food may worsen | 2–3 hours after meals and at night; relieved by food or antacids |
| Weight | Weight loss (fear of eating) | Weight may increase |
| Cancer risk | Must be biopsied to exclude cancer | Malignant change very rare |

**Symptoms**: burning or gnawing upper abdominal (epigastric) pain, heartburn, nausea, bloating; may be silent with NSAIDs.

**Complications**: bleeding (vomiting blood — haematemesis; black tarry stools — melaena), perforation (sudden severe pain, peritonitis), gastric outlet obstruction (vomiting), malignancy (gastric).

**Diagnosis**: endoscopy with biopsy; *H. pylori* tests (urea breath test, stool antigen, rapid urease test on biopsy).

## Remember

- Anaemia: Hb below 13 (men), 12 (women), 11 (pregnancy) g/dL. Microcytic = iron deficiency, thalassaemia; macrocytic = B12, folate.
- Iron deficiency: koilonychia, pica, low ferritin; usual cause is chronic blood loss.
- B12 deficiency (pernicious anaemia) gives nerve damage; folate does not. Hypersegmented neutrophils in both.
- Sickle cell: Glu → Val at β6; sickling causes vaso-occlusive crises and haemolysis. Thalassaemia: reduced globin chain synthesis, iron overload.
- Haemophilia A = factor VIII, B = factor IX, both X-linked recessive; haemarthrosis; prolonged aPTT.
- Diabetes: FPG ≥ 126, 2-h ≥ 200, HbA1c ≥ 6.5%. Type 1 autoimmune, ketoacidosis; type 2 insulin resistance, obesity.
- Graves' = TSH-receptor stimulating antibodies; Hashimoto's = hypothyroidism; cretinism in infants.
- Parkinson's: nigral dopamine loss, Lewy bodies; tremor, rigidity, akinesia, postural instability.
- Stroke: 80–85% ischaemic; hypertension is the main risk factor; penumbra can be saved.
- Depression: monoamine deficit; schizophrenia: mesolimbic dopamine excess; Alzheimer's: amyloid plaques, tau tangles, ACh loss.
- Peptic ulcer: H. pylori and NSAIDs are the main causes; duodenal pain is relieved by food.

# Tests for carbohydrates

**Aim:** To detect a carbohydrate in the given compound and to distinguish glucose, fructose, sucrose, lactose and starch.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Add concentrated H2SO4 along the side of the tube slowly in the Molisch test.

## Principle

- **Molisch test (general):** concentrated H2SO4 dehydrates carbohydrates to furfural derivatives, which condense with α-naphthol to give a violet ring.
- **Reducing sugars** (glucose, fructose, lactose, maltose) have a free aldehyde or keto group (hemiacetal) and reduce Cu²⁺ (Fehling's, Benedict's). Sucrose is non-reducing.
- **Barfoed's test:** in mildly acidic copper acetate, monosaccharides reduce Cu²⁺ faster than disaccharides.
- **Seliwanoff's test:** ketoses (fructose) dehydrate quickly in HCl to hydroxymethylfurfural, which gives a cherry-red colour with resorcinol.
- **Iodine test:** starch forms a blue-black complex with iodine.
- **Osazone test:** reducing sugars heated with phenylhydrazine form crystalline osazones of characteristic shape and time of formation.

## Requirements

Sugar samples, Molisch reagent (α-naphthol in ethanol), conc. H2SO4, Fehling's A and B, Benedict's reagent, Barfoed's reagent, Seliwanoff's reagent (resorcinol in HCl), iodine solution, phenylhydrazine hydrochloride, sodium acetate, boiling water bath, microscope.

## Procedure

1. **Molisch test:** to 2 ml of sugar solution add 2 drops of Molisch reagent; pour 1 ml of conc. H2SO4 slowly down the side of the tube. A violet ring at the junction shows a carbohydrate.
2. **Fehling's / Benedict's test:** boil 2 ml of the sugar solution with 2 ml of mixed Fehling's (or 5 ml of Benedict's) for 2 minutes. Red/orange precipitate shows a reducing sugar.
3. **Barfoed's test:** heat 1 ml of sugar solution with 2 ml of Barfoed's reagent in a boiling water bath; note the time for a red precipitate.
4. **Seliwanoff's test:** heat 1 ml of sugar solution with 2 ml of Seliwanoff's reagent in a boiling water bath for 1 minute. Cherry-red colour shows a ketose.
5. **Iodine test:** add 1 drop of iodine solution to a starch suspension. Blue-black colour, disappearing on heating and returning on cooling, shows starch.
6. **Osazone test:** heat 5 ml of sugar solution with about 0.5 g of phenylhydrazine hydrochloride and 0.75 g of sodium acetate in a boiling water bath; note when yellow crystals appear, cool slowly and view under the microscope.

## Observations

| Test | Glucose | Fructose | Sucrose | Lactose | Starch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Molisch | Violet ring | Violet ring | Violet ring | Violet ring | Violet ring |
| Fehling's / Benedict's | Positive | Positive | Negative | Positive | Negative |
| Barfoed's | Red quickly (monosaccharide) | Red quickly | Negative | Slow / negative (disaccharide) | Negative |
| Seliwanoff's | Faint / slow | Cherry red within about 1 minute | Positive slowly (contains fructose, hydrolysed in HCl) | Negative | Negative |
| Iodine | No colour | No colour | No colour | No colour | Blue-black |
| Osazone | Needle-shaped yellow crystals, forms in a few minutes while hot | Same needles as glucose, forms faster | No osazone (unless hydrolysed) | "Powder-puff / cotton-ball" crystals, form on cooling | — |

**Worked record (example).** White sweet powder, chars on heating with burnt-sugar smell; Molisch positive; Fehling's positive; Barfoed's red within 2 minutes; Seliwanoff's faint only after long heating; needle-shaped osazone formed hot → **glucose**.

## Precautions

1. Pour conc. H2SO4 gently so that the two layers do not mix in the Molisch test.
2. Do not overheat in Barfoed's test; disaccharides can then give a false positive.
3. Seliwanoff's test must be timed; prolonged heating makes aldoses react too.
4. Phenylhydrazine is toxic; avoid skin contact.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is sucrose non-reducing?** Its glycosidic bond joins the reducing ends of both glucose and fructose, so no free hemiacetal remains.
2. **Why do glucose and fructose give the same osazone?** Osazone formation involves C1 and C2 only, and the rest of the two molecules is identical.
3. **What is the principle of the Molisch test?** Dehydration to furfural derivatives that condense with α-naphthol.
4. **Which test distinguishes glucose from fructose?** Seliwanoff's test (ketose gives cherry red quickly).
5. **Why does the starch–iodine colour vanish on heating?** The helical starch–iodine complex opens up on heating and re-forms on cooling.
6. **What does Barfoed's test distinguish?** Monosaccharides from reducing disaccharides.

# Preliminary tests on an unknown organic compound

**Aim:** To record the physical state, colour and odour of an unknown organic compound and to find out whether it is aliphatic or aromatic and saturated or unsaturated.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Never taste an unknown, smell it only by wafting, and keep flammable liquids away from flames.

## Principle

Simple observations narrow down the class of an unknown before chemical tests are done:

- **Colour and odour** point to certain groups (yellow nitro compounds, fruity esters, carbolic phenol).
- **Ignition test:** aromatic compounds have a high carbon-to-hydrogen ratio and burn with a **sooty, smoky flame**; aliphatic compounds burn with a clean, non-smoky flame. Carbohydrates char with a smell of burnt sugar.
- **Unsaturation:** C=C and C≡C bonds add bromine (red-brown colour disappears) and are oxidised by cold dilute alkaline KMnO4 (Baeyer's reagent; purple colour disappears and brown MnO2 forms).

## Requirements

Unknown compound, spatula, porcelain tile or crucible lid, burner, test tubes, 5% bromine in carbon tetrachloride (or bromine water), 1% aqueous KMnO4 with a little sodium carbonate (Baeyer's reagent), litmus papers.

## Procedure

1. **Physical state:** note whether the compound is a crystalline solid, an amorphous powder or a liquid.
2. **Colour:** observe in daylight against white paper.
3. **Odour:** waft the vapour gently towards the nose.
4. **Ignition test:** place about 0.1 g (or 2 drops) on a porcelain tile or crucible lid and heat in the flame. Note the type of flame and any residue.
5. **Bromine test:** dissolve about 0.1 g in 1 ml of CCl4 (or water if soluble) and add the bromine solution drop by drop with shaking.
6. **Baeyer's test:** dissolve about 0.1 g in 2 ml of water or acetone and add Baeyer's reagent drop by drop with shaking.
7. **Litmus:** test an aqueous solution or suspension with blue and red litmus.

## Observations

| Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical state / colour | Yellow liquid | Possibly a nitro compound |
| Colour | Colourless liquid turning brown on standing | Possibly aniline (air oxidation) |
| Odour | Fruity | Ester |
| Odour | Bitter almond | Benzaldehyde or nitrobenzene |
| Odour | Carbolic (phenolic) | Phenol |
| Odour | Fishy / ammoniacal | Amine |
| Odour | Vinegar-like | Acetic acid |
| Ignition | Sooty, smoky flame | Aromatic compound |
| Ignition | Non-smoky, clean flame | Aliphatic compound |
| Ignition | Chars, smell of burnt sugar | Carbohydrate |
| Ignition | White residue left | Salt of an acid (e.g. sodium benzoate) |
| Bromine | Red-brown colour discharged, no fumes | Unsaturated (C=C) |
| Bromine | Colour discharged with white precipitate | Phenol or aromatic amine (substitution) |
| Baeyer's | Purple → brown precipitate | Unsaturation (or easily oxidised groups: aldehydes, phenols) |
| Litmus | Blue → red | Acidic compound (acid, phenol) |
| Litmus | Red → blue | Basic compound (amine) |

**Worked record (example).** White crystals, odourless, sooty flame, no change with bromine or Baeyer's reagent, blue litmus turns red → aromatic, saturated, acidic compound; go on to element detection and the acid tests.

## Precautions

1. Use only a small amount in the ignition test; some compounds burn violently.
2. Smell by wafting, never by sniffing directly.
3. Baeyer's test is not specific: aldehydes, phenols and formic acid also reduce KMnO4, so confirm with the bromine test.
4. Bromine in CCl4 is toxic and corrosive; use it in a fume hood.

## Viva questions

1. **Why do aromatic compounds burn with a sooty flame?** Their high carbon content leaves unburnt carbon particles.
2. **What is Baeyer's reagent?** 1% cold dilute alkaline potassium permanganate.
3. **Why does phenol decolourise bromine water?** It undergoes ring substitution to give a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol.
4. **Why does aniline darken on standing?** It is slowly oxidised by air to coloured products.
5. **What does a residue after ignition indicate?** The compound is a metal salt (e.g. a sodium salt of an acid).
6. **Why are preliminary tests only indicative?** Several classes give the same response; they guide, not confirm.

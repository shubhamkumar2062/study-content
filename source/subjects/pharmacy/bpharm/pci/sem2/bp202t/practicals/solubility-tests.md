# Solubility tests and solubility classification

**Aim:** To place the given organic compound in a solubility group using water, dilute alkalis, dilute acid and concentrated sulphuric acid.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Add concentrated H2SO4 slowly; it may heat up and char the compound.

## Principle

"Like dissolves like." Small polar molecules dissolve in water. Water-insoluble **acids** dissolve in alkali because they form salts; water-insoluble **bases** dissolve in dilute acid for the same reason. Concentrated H2SO4 dissolves neutral compounds that contain oxygen or a C=C, because it protonates them. Hydrocarbons and their halogen derivatives dissolve in none of these.

```text
   R–COOH + NaHCO3 → R–COONa (soluble) + H2O + CO2↑        (strong acids only)
   ArOH  + NaOH   → ArONa (soluble) + H2O                 (phenols: NaOH yes, NaHCO3 no)
   R–NH2 + HCl    → R–NH3⁺ Cl⁻ (soluble)
```

## Requirements

Unknown compound, test tubes, distilled water, 5% NaOH, 5% NaHCO3, 5% HCl, concentrated H2SO4, litmus paper, dilute HCl and dilute NaOH for reprecipitation.

## Procedure

1. **Water:** shake about 0.1 g (or 0.2 ml) with 3 ml of water. If it dissolves, test the solution with litmus.
2. **5% NaOH:** if insoluble in water, shake a fresh portion with 3 ml of 5% NaOH. If it dissolves, acidify with dilute HCl: reprecipitation confirms an acidic compound.
3. **5% NaHCO3:** if soluble in NaOH, try a fresh portion with 5% NaHCO3. Note effervescence.
4. **5% HCl:** if insoluble in water and NaOH, shake with 3 ml of 5% HCl. If it dissolves, make alkaline with NaOH: reprecipitation confirms a base.
5. **Conc. H2SO4:** if insoluble in all the above, add the compound to 1 ml of cold concentrated H2SO4. Note dissolution, colour change or warming.

## Precautions

1. Use small amounts so that solubility is easy to judge.
2. Always use a fresh portion of compound for each solvent.
3. Confirm acid or base solubility by reprecipitation.
4. Add conc. H2SO4 to the compound slowly and cool if needed.

## Viva questions

1. **Why does benzoic acid dissolve in NaHCO3 but phenol does not?** Benzoic acid (pKa 4.2) is stronger than carbonic acid; phenol (pKa 10) is weaker.
2. **Why does aniline dissolve in dilute HCl?** It forms the water-soluble salt anilinium chloride.
3. **Why do lower alcohols dissolve in water?** They form hydrogen bonds with water.
4. **Which compounds dissolve in concentrated H2SO4 only?** Neutral compounds containing oxygen (aldehydes, ketones, esters, ethers, higher alcohols) and alkenes.
5. **What is the use of solubility tests?** They narrow down the functional group and decide which group tests to perform.
6. **Why is reprecipitation done?** To prove that the compound dissolved by salt formation and not merely by physical solubility.

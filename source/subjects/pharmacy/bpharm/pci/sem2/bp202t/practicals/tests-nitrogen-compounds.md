# Functional group tests for amines, amides/urea, anilides and nitro compounds

**Aim:** To identify the nitrogen-containing functional group (amine, amide, urea, anilide or nitro) in a compound that gave a positive Lassaigne's test for nitrogen.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Isocyanides (carbylamine test) are very foul and toxic: do this test in a fume hood and destroy the product with conc. HCl.

## Principle

- **Amines** are basic (dissolve in dilute HCl). Primary amines give the carbylamine (isocyanide) reaction. **Primary aromatic amines** form diazonium salts at 0–5 °C, which couple with β-naphthol to give an orange-red azo dye.
- **Amides** release ammonia when boiled with alkali; **urea** forms biuret on gentle heating, which gives a violet colour with alkaline copper sulphate.
- **Anilides** (e.g. acetanilide) are amides of aniline: acid hydrolysis releases the primary aromatic amine, which then gives the azo dye test.
- **Nitro compounds** are reduced (Sn/HCl, or Zn/NH4Cl) to amines or hydroxylamines, which are then detected.

```text
   R–NH2 + CHCl3 + 3 KOH → R–N≡C (foul) + 3 KCl + 3 H2O
   C6H5NH2 + NaNO2 + 2 HCl ──0–5 °C──▶ C6H5N2⁺Cl⁻ + NaCl + 2 H2O
   C6H5N2⁺Cl⁻ + β-naphthol (in NaOH) → orange-red 1-phenylazo-2-naphthol
   R–CONH2 + NaOH → R–COONa + NH3↑
   2 NH2CONH2 ──heat──▶ NH2CONHCONH2 (biuret) + NH3
```

## Requirements

Unknown compound, dilute and conc. HCl, chloroform, alcoholic KOH, NaNO2, β-naphthol in NaOH, ice, 10% NaOH, red litmus, 1% CuSO4, 70% H2SO4 (or conc. HCl) for hydrolysis, granulated tin, zinc dust, ammonium chloride, Tollens' reagent, test tubes, water bath.

## Procedure

**Amines**

1. **Solubility:** dissolves in dilute HCl; reprecipitates with NaOH (aromatic amines such as aniline).
2. **Carbylamine test:** warm 2 drops of the amine with 3 drops of chloroform and 2 ml of alcoholic KOH. An offensive smell shows a 1° amine. Destroy the product with conc. HCl.
3. **Azo dye test (1° aromatic amines):** dissolve 2 drops of amine in 1 ml of dilute HCl, cool in ice; add ice-cold NaNO2 solution; pour into ice-cold alkaline β-naphthol. Orange-red precipitate shows a 1° aromatic amine.

**Amides and urea**

4. **NaOH test:** boil the compound with 10% NaOH; hold moist red litmus at the mouth. Smell of ammonia and blue litmus show an amide (or ammonium salt).
5. **Biuret test (urea):** heat a little urea gently in a dry tube until it melts and solidifies again; cool, dissolve in water, add NaOH and 1 drop of 1% CuSO4. Violet colour shows urea.
6. **Nitrous acid test (urea):** add NaNO2 and dilute HCl to an aqueous solution of urea: brisk effervescence of N2.

**Anilides**

7. **Hydrolysis:** reflux 0.5 g of the compound with 5 ml of 70% H2SO4 (or conc. HCl) for about 10 minutes. Note the smell of acetic acid. Cool, make the solution alkaline, and test a portion for a 1° aromatic amine by the azo dye test (step 3). Positive → anilide (acetanilide).

**Nitro compounds**

8. **Reduction test:** warm 0.5 ml of the compound with a few pieces of tin and 3 ml of conc. HCl for several minutes; decant, cool, and carry out the azo dye test on the solution. Orange-red dye shows an aromatic nitro compound (reduced to the amine).
9. **Mulliken–Barker test:** warm the compound with zinc dust and NH4Cl solution in ethanol; filter into Tollens' reagent. Grey/black precipitate (the hydroxylamine reduces Ag⁺) shows a nitro group.

## Observations

| Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dilute HCl | Dissolves; reprecipitates with NaOH | Amine (base) |
| Carbylamine | Offensive odour | 1° amine |
| Diazotisation + β-naphthol | Orange-red precipitate | 1° aromatic amine (aniline) |
| Boil with NaOH | NH3 evolved, red litmus blue | Amide |
| Biuret | Violet | Urea |
| NaNO2 + HCl on urea | Effervescence | Urea |
| Acid hydrolysis then azo dye | Acetic acid smell; orange-red dye | Anilide (acetanilide) |
| Sn/HCl then azo dye | Orange-red dye | Aromatic nitro compound |
| Mulliken–Barker | Grey/black silver | Nitro compound |

**Worked record (example).** White crystalline solid, neutral, N present; no NH3 with cold NaOH; on acid hydrolysis smell of acetic acid and positive azo dye test; melting point about 114 °C → **acetanilide**.

## Result

The nitrogen-containing group in the given compound is ___.

## Precautions

1. Keep diazotisation below 5 °C; diazonium salts decompose on warming.
2. Do the carbylamine test in the fume hood and destroy the isocyanide with acid.
3. For urea, heat gently; strong heating gives cyanuric acid instead of biuret.
4. Make sure the reduction with tin is complete before the azo dye test.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is diazotisation done at 0–5 °C?** Diazonium salts are unstable and decompose to phenols and N2 at higher temperatures.
2. **What colour is the azo dye from aniline and β-naphthol?** Orange-red.
3. **Why does acetanilide not give the azo dye test directly?** Its nitrogen is acylated; hydrolysis must first free aniline.
4. **Which amines give the carbylamine test?** Primary amines only (aliphatic and aromatic).
5. **What is formed when urea is heated gently?** Biuret and ammonia.
6. **How is nitrobenzene detected?** By reduction to aniline, then diazotisation and coupling.

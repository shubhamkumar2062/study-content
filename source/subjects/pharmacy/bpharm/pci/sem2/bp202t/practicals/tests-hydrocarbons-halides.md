# Tests for aromatic hydrocarbons and halogenated hydrocarbons

**Aim:** To identify an aromatic hydrocarbon or a halogenated hydrocarbon in a compound that is insoluble in water, dilute acid and dilute alkali.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Nitrating mixture is highly corrosive; add the compound drop by drop and keep the tube cool.

## Principle

- **Aromatic hydrocarbons** (benzene, toluene, naphthalene) burn with a sooty flame, do not decolourise bromine or Baeyer's reagent (no addition), but undergo **nitration** with conc. HNO3 + conc. H2SO4 to give yellow nitro compounds with a bitter-almond smell.
- **Halogenated hydrocarbons** contain halogen detected by Lassaigne's test. In the **Beilstein test**, the halogen forms a volatile copper halide that colours the flame green. **Alkyl halides** give a silver halide precipitate with alcoholic AgNO3 on warming (SN1 ionisation); **aryl halides** (chlorobenzene) and vinyl halides do not, because their C–X bond has partial double-bond character.

```text
   C6H6 + HNO3 ──conc. H2SO4──▶ C6H5NO2 (yellow, bitter almond) + H2O
   R–X + AgNO3 (alcoholic) ──warm──▶ R–ONO2 + AgX↓
```

## Requirements

Unknown compound, porcelain tile, conc. HNO3, conc. H2SO4, copper wire, 2% alcoholic AgNO3, Lassaigne's extract, test tubes, water bath, beaker of cold water.

## Procedure

**Aromatic hydrocarbons**

1. **Ignition:** a sooty, smoky flame indicates an aromatic compound.
2. **Unsaturation:** no change with bromine in CCl4 or Baeyer's reagent (rules out alkenes).
3. **Nitration test:** add 0.5 ml of the compound drop by drop to a cooled mixture of 1 ml conc. HNO3 and 1 ml conc. H2SO4; warm gently on a water bath (about 50 °C) for 5 minutes; pour into cold water. A yellow oil or solid with a bitter-almond smell shows an aromatic ring.

**Halogenated hydrocarbons**

4. **Lassaigne's test:** confirm the halogen (see the Lassaigne practical).
5. **Beilstein test:** heat a copper wire loop in a flame until it no longer colours the flame; touch it to the compound and reheat. A **green or blue-green flame** shows a halogen.
6. **Alcoholic AgNO3:** add 2 drops of the compound to 2 ml of 2% alcoholic AgNO3 and warm. A precipitate shows an alkyl (or benzyl) halide; no precipitate suggests an aryl or vinyl halide.

## Observations

| Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ignition | Sooty flame | Aromatic |
| Bromine / Baeyer's | No change | Saturated / aromatic ring |
| Nitration | Yellow product, bitter-almond smell | Aromatic hydrocarbon |
| Beilstein | Green flame | Halogen present |
| Alcoholic AgNO3 | White / yellow precipitate on warming | Alkyl or benzyl halide |
| Alcoholic AgNO3 | No precipitate | Aryl halide (e.g. chlorobenzene) |

**Worked record (example).** Colourless liquid, sweet aromatic odour, sooty flame; Lassaigne's: Cl present; Beilstein green; no precipitate with alcoholic AgNO3; boiling point about 132 °C → **chlorobenzene**.

## Result

The given compound is an ___ (aromatic hydrocarbon / alkyl halide / aryl halide).

## Precautions

1. Keep the nitration temperature low to avoid dinitration and fumes.
2. Clean the copper wire in the flame before the Beilstein test.
3. Beilstein test is not specific: some nitrogen compounds (e.g. urea, pyridine) also colour the flame; confirm with Lassaigne's test.
4. Chloroform and carbon tetrachloride are toxic; handle in a fume hood.

## Viva questions

1. **Why does benzene not decolourise bromine water?** Its delocalised π system resists addition; it prefers substitution with a catalyst.
2. **What is the electrophile in nitration?** The nitronium ion, NO2⁺.
3. **Why is chlorobenzene unreactive towards alcoholic AgNO3?** The C–Cl bond has partial double-bond character from resonance with the ring.
4. **What gives the green colour in the Beilstein test?** Volatile copper halides.
5. **Name a halogenated hydrocarbon used as a local anaesthetic spray.** Ethyl chloride.
6. **Which solubility group do these compounds fall in?** The "inert" group: insoluble in water, dilute acid and dilute alkali, and also in cold conc. H2SO4.

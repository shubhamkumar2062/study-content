# Preparation of solid derivatives of organic compounds

**Aim:** To prepare suitable crystalline derivatives of organic compounds — the 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazone of a carbonyl compound, the benzoate and tribromo derivative of a phenol, the acetyl derivative of an amine and the 3,5-dinitrobenzoate of an alcohol — and to confirm the parent compound by the melting point of the derivative.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Benzoyl chloride, acetic anhydride and 3,5-dinitrobenzoyl chloride are lachrymatory and corrosive: use them in a fume hood with gloves.

## Principle

A **derivative** is a new compound made from the unknown by a simple, high-yield reaction of its functional group. It is chosen to be a crystalline solid with a sharp melting point that differs clearly from the derivatives of other candidates. Matching the derivative's melting point with the literature value confirms the identity.

| Group | Reagent | Derivative | Reaction type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aldehyde / ketone | 2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine | 2,4-DNP hydrazone | Addition–elimination (condensation) |
| Phenol | Benzoyl chloride + NaOH | Aryl benzoate | Schotten–Baumann benzoylation |
| Phenol | Bromine water | Bromo derivative | Electrophilic substitution |
| Primary / secondary amine | Acetic anhydride | Acetamide (anilide) | Acetylation |
| Alcohol | 3,5-Dinitrobenzoyl chloride | 3,5-Dinitrobenzoate ester | Esterification |

## Requirements

2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine, conc. H2SO4, ethanol, 10% NaOH, benzoyl chloride, bromine water, acetic anhydride, glacial acetic acid, 3,5-dinitrobenzoyl chloride, pyridine (optional), 5% NaHCO3, conical flasks with stoppers, beakers, Buchner funnel, filter paper, water bath, ice, melting point apparatus.

## Procedure

**A. 2,4-DNP hydrazone of a carbonyl compound (acetone or benzaldehyde)**

1. Dissolve about 0.25 g of 2,4-DNP in 1 ml of conc. H2SO4; add 2 ml of water dropwise, then 5 ml of ethanol (Brady's reagent).
2. Dissolve 0.1 ml (or 0.1 g) of the carbonyl compound in 2 ml of ethanol and add it to the reagent.
3. Shake; a yellow to orange-red precipitate forms at once or on standing for a few minutes.
4. Filter, wash with a little cold ethanol, recrystallise from ethanol, dry and find the melting point.

**B. Benzoate of a phenol (Schotten–Baumann reaction)**

5. Dissolve 1 g of phenol (or β-naphthol) in 15 ml of 10% NaOH in a stoppered flask.
6. Add 2 ml of benzoyl chloride, stopper and shake vigorously for about 15 minutes (release pressure now and then) until the smell of benzoyl chloride has gone.
7. Filter the solid ester, wash with cold water, recrystallise from ethanol and find the melting point.

**C. Tribromo derivative of phenol**

8. Dissolve 0.5 g of phenol in 10 ml of water; add bromine water with shaking until the yellow colour just persists.
9. Filter the white precipitate, wash with water, recrystallise from dilute ethanol and find the melting point.

**D. Acetyl derivative of an amine (acetanilide from aniline)**

10. Mix 2 ml of aniline with 2 ml of glacial acetic acid and 2 ml of acetic anhydride in a dry flask; warm on a water bath for about 10 minutes.
11. Pour into 30 ml of ice-cold water with stirring; white crystals of acetanilide separate.
12. Filter, recrystallise from hot water, dry and find the melting point.

**E. 3,5-Dinitrobenzoate of an alcohol**

13. Warm 0.5 g of 3,5-dinitrobenzoyl chloride with 0.5 ml of the dry alcohol (and a drop of pyridine) on a water bath for about 5 minutes.
14. Cool, add 10 ml of 5% NaHCO3 to remove 3,5-dinitrobenzoic acid, and stir until the ester solidifies.
15. Filter, wash with water, recrystallise from ethanol, dry and find the melting point.

**Reactions**

A   (CH3)2C=O + H2N–NH–C6H3(NO2)2 → (CH3)2C=N–NH–C6H3(NO2)2 + H2O
B   C6H5OH + C6H5COCl + NaOH → C6H5COOC6H5 (phenyl benzoate) + NaCl + H2O

## Precautions

1. Use pure, dry reagents; moisture destroys acid chlorides.
2. In the Schotten–Baumann reaction keep the mixture alkaline and shake thoroughly.
3. Recrystallise every derivative and dry it fully before the melting point.
4. Wash the 3,5-dinitrobenzoate with NaHCO3 to remove the free acid, which would lower the melting point.
5. Work with benzoyl chloride and bromine water in a fume hood.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the Schotten–Baumann reaction?** Benzoylation of phenols, alcohols or amines with benzoyl chloride in aqueous alkali.
2. **Why is NaOH used in benzoylation?** It converts phenol to the more reactive phenoxide and neutralises the HCl formed.
3. **Why are 2,4-DNP hydrazones good derivatives?** They form quickly, are brightly coloured, insoluble and have sharp, distinct melting points.
4. **Why is 3,5-dinitrobenzoyl chloride preferred for alcohols?** Its esters are crystalline solids even for low alcohols that are liquids.
5. **Why is acetic acid added in the acetylation of aniline?** It acts as a solvent and moderates the reaction.
6. **Why should a derivative's melting point differ from that of the parent?** So that the two cannot be confused and possible candidates are clearly separated.

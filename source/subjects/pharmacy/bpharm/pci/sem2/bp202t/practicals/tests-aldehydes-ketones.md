# Functional group tests for aldehydes and ketones

**Aim:** To detect a carbonyl group in the given compound and to decide whether it is an aldehyde or a ketone.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Tollens' reagent must be made fresh and discarded at once after use (on standing it can form explosive silver compounds).

## Principle

- **2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine (2,4-DNP)** condenses with any >C=O to give a coloured, insoluble hydrazone: R2C=O + H2N–NH–Ar → R2C=N–NH–Ar + H2O.
- **Aldehydes are reducing agents**; ketones are not. Aldehydes reduce Ag⁺ to silver (Tollens') and Cu²⁺ to Cu2O (Fehling's, Benedict's), and restore the colour of Schiff's reagent.
- **Methyl ketones** give a red colour with sodium nitroprusside in alkali and yellow iodoform with I2/NaOH.

```text
   R–CHO + 2 [Ag(NH3)2]⁺ + 3 OH⁻ → R–COO⁻ + 2 Ag↓ + 4 NH3 + 2 H2O     (silver mirror)
   R–CHO + 2 Cu²⁺ + 5 OH⁻ → R–COO⁻ + Cu2O↓ (red) + 3 H2O              (Fehling's)
```

## Requirements

Unknown compound, 2,4-DNP reagent (Brady's reagent), Tollens' reagent (AgNO3 + a drop of NaOH, then dilute ammonia just to dissolve the precipitate), Fehling's solutions A and B, Schiff's reagent, sodium nitroprusside solution, NaOH, iodine in KI, ethanol, water bath.

## Procedure

1. **2,4-DNP test:** dissolve a drop or a few crystals of the compound in 1 ml of ethanol; add 2 ml of Brady's reagent and shake. A yellow, orange or red precipitate shows a carbonyl group.
2. **Tollens' test:** to 2 ml of freshly prepared Tollens' reagent in a clean test tube add 2–3 drops of the compound; warm on a water bath (about 60 °C) for a few minutes. A silver mirror or black precipitate shows an aldehyde.
3. **Fehling's test:** mix 1 ml each of Fehling's A and B, add the compound and boil for 2 minutes. A red-brown precipitate shows an aliphatic aldehyde (or reducing sugar).
4. **Schiff's test:** add the compound to 1 ml of Schiff's reagent and shake in the cold. A pink/magenta colour shows an aldehyde.
5. **Sodium nitroprusside test:** to the compound add 1 ml of sodium nitroprusside solution and make alkaline with NaOH. A red/wine-red colour shows a methyl ketone (acetone).
6. **Iodoform test:** to 0.2 ml of compound in 2 ml of water add 2 ml of 10% NaOH, then iodine solution drop by drop with warming until a faint brown colour remains. A yellow precipitate with a characteristic smell (CHI3) shows CH3CO– (or CH3CH(OH)–).

## Observations

| Test | Aldehyde (e.g. benzaldehyde / acetaldehyde) | Ketone (e.g. acetone) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2,4-DNP | Orange-red / yellow precipitate | Yellow-orange precipitate |
| Tollens' | Silver mirror | No change |
| Fehling's | Red Cu2O with acetaldehyde; benzaldehyde usually negative | No change |
| Schiff's | Pink/magenta | No colour |
| Nitroprusside + NaOH | No red colour | Red colour (acetone) |
| Iodoform | Positive only for acetaldehyde | Positive for methyl ketones |

**Worked record (example).** Colourless liquid with bitter-almond odour; orange precipitate with 2,4-DNP; silver mirror with Tollens'; Fehling's negative; Schiff's pink → aromatic aldehyde (benzaldehyde likely; boiling point about 178 °C; 2,4-DNP derivative melting point about 237 °C).

## Result

The given compound contains an ___ (aldehyde / ketone) group.

## Precautions

1. Clean the test tube with chromic acid or NaOH and rinse well before Tollens' test, or the mirror will not form.
2. Never heat Tollens' reagent directly over a flame; use a water bath and discard it immediately afterwards.
3. Mix Fehling's A and B only at the time of use.
4. Do the Schiff's test in the cold; heating restores the colour without any aldehyde.

## Viva questions

1. **What is Brady's reagent?** 2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine in acid (sulphuric acid with ethanol/water).
2. **Why do ketones not respond to Tollens' test?** They have no H on the carbonyl carbon and are not easily oxidised.
3. **Why does benzaldehyde fail the Fehling's test?** Aromatic aldehydes are weaker reducing agents and generally do not reduce Fehling's solution.
4. **What does Rochelle salt do in Fehling's B?** Its tartrate complexes Cu²⁺ and keeps it in solution in alkali.
5. **What is Schiff's reagent?** A solution of rosaniline (magenta) decolourised by SO2.
6. **Which group does the iodoform test detect?** CH3–CO– or CH3–CH(OH)–.

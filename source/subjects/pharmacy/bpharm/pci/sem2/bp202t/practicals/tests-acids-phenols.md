# Functional group tests for carboxylic acids and phenols

**Aim:** To detect a carboxylic acid group or a phenolic group in the given organic compound.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Phenols burn the skin; wash any spill at once with plenty of water.

## Principle

- **Carboxylic acids** (pKa about 4–5) are stronger than carbonic acid, so they release CO2 from sodium bicarbonate. With an alcohol and acid catalyst they form sweet-smelling esters. Their neutral salts give characteristic colours or precipitates with ferric chloride.
- **Phenols** are weaker acids (pKa about 10): they dissolve in NaOH but not NaHCO3. The –OH on the ring forms coloured complexes with Fe³⁺ and activates the ring, so bromine substitutes at once.

## Requirements

Unknown compound, saturated NaHCO3, ethanol, conc. H2SO4, neutral FeCl3 (FeCl3 solution to which dilute NaOH is added until a faint permanent precipitate forms, then filtered), dilute ammonia, bromine water, NaNO2, phthalic anhydride, 10% NaOH, test tubes, water bath.

To prepare a **neutral solution** of an acid for the FeCl3 test: dissolve a little acid in dilute ammonia, boil off the excess ammonia, cool.

## Procedure

**Carboxylic acid**

1. **Bicarbonate test:** add a little compound to 2 ml of saturated NaHCO3. Brisk effervescence (CO2, turns lime water milky) shows –COOH.
2. **Ester test:** warm 0.1 g of the acid with 1 ml of ethanol and 2 drops of conc. H2SO4 on a water bath for 5 minutes; pour into a beaker of cold water. A **fruity smell** shows an acid.
3. **Neutral FeCl3 test:** add neutral FeCl3 to the neutral solution of the acid.

**Phenol**

4. **FeCl3 test:** add 2 drops of neutral FeCl3 to a dilute aqueous or alcoholic solution of the compound. A violet, blue, green or red colour shows a phenol (or enol).
5. **Bromine water:** add bromine water drop by drop to an aqueous solution; decolourisation with a white precipitate (e.g. 2,4,6-tribromophenol) shows a phenol.
6. **Liebermann's nitroso test:** dissolve a crystal of NaNO2 in 1 ml of conc. H2SO4, add a little phenol and warm gently: a deep green or blue colour forms; pour into water → red; make alkaline with NaOH → green or blue again.
7. **Phthalein test:** heat a little phenol with an equal amount of phthalic anhydride and 2 drops of conc. H2SO4 for 1 minute; cool, pour into dilute NaOH: phenol gives a **pink/red** colour (phenolphthalein).

## Observations

| Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NaHCO3 | Brisk effervescence | Carboxylic acid present |
| Ester test | Fruity odour | Carboxylic acid present |
| Neutral FeCl3 on acid | Buff precipitate | Benzoate |
| Neutral FeCl3 on acid | Violet colour | Salicylate (phenolic –OH as well) |
| Neutral FeCl3 on acid | Deep red colour, brown-red precipitate on boiling | Acetate or formate |
| Neutral FeCl3 on acid | Brownish precipitate | Succinate |
| FeCl3 on phenol | Violet | Phenol |
| FeCl3 on phenol | Green or greenish | β-Naphthol |
| Bromine water | White precipitate | Phenol (tribromophenol) |
| Liebermann's test | Green/blue → red (water) → blue/green (alkali) | Phenol |
| Phthalein test | Pink/red in alkali | Phenol |

**Worked record (example).** White solid; NaHCO3 effervescence; ester test fruity; neutral FeCl3 violet → carboxylic acid with a free phenolic –OH (salicylic acid likely; confirm by melting point, about 158 °C).

## Result

The given compound contains a ___ (carboxylic acid / phenolic) group.

## Precautions

1. Use **neutral** FeCl3; free acid or alkali spoils the colours.
2. Remove all excess ammonia before the FeCl3 test on acids.
3. Some phenols (e.g. hindered phenols) give weak FeCl3 colours; confirm with another test.
4. Handle conc. H2SO4 and bromine water carefully.

## Viva questions

1. **Why does phenol not effervesce with NaHCO3?** It is a weaker acid than carbonic acid.
2. **What is formed in the ester test with acetic acid and ethanol?** Ethyl acetate.
3. **Why does salicylic acid give a violet colour with FeCl3?** It has a free phenolic –OH, which complexes iron.
4. **Why does aspirin not give an immediate violet colour?** Its phenolic –OH is acetylated; colour appears only after hydrolysis.
5. **What is the white precipitate formed by phenol with bromine water?** 2,4,6-Tribromophenol.
6. **Which other compounds give a colour with FeCl3?** Enols such as ethyl acetoacetate.

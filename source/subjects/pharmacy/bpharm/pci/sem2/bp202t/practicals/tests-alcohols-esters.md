# Functional group tests for alcohols and esters

**Aim:** To detect an alcoholic –OH group or an ester group in the given compound, and to classify an alcohol as primary, secondary or tertiary.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Sodium reacts violently with water: use a dry sample and a tiny piece of sodium.

## Principle

- **Alcohols** release hydrogen with sodium, form coloured complexes with ceric ammonium nitrate, form fruity esters with acetic acid, and react with the Lucas reagent at rates that depend on carbocation stability (3° > 2° > 1°).
- **Esters** react with hydroxylamine in alkali to form **hydroxamic acids**, which give a red-violet ferric complex; they also consume alkali on warming (saponification).

```text
   2 R–OH + 2 Na → 2 R–ONa + H2↑
   R–OH + HCl ──ZnCl2──▶ R–Cl (insoluble, turbidity) + H2O
   R–COOR' + NH2OH ──NaOH──▶ R–CONHOH (hydroxamic acid) + R'OH
   3 R–CONHOH + Fe³⁺ → (R–CONHO)3Fe (red-violet) + 3 H⁺
```

## Requirements

Unknown compound (dry), sodium metal, ceric ammonium nitrate reagent, glacial acetic acid, conc. H2SO4, Lucas reagent (anhydrous ZnCl2 dissolved in conc. HCl), chromic acid (K2Cr2O7 in dilute H2SO4), iodine–KI, NaOH, hydroxylamine hydrochloride, dilute HCl, FeCl3 solution, phenolphthalein, test tubes, water bath.

## Procedure

**Alcohols**

1. **Sodium test:** add a small dry piece of sodium to 1 ml of the dry liquid. Effervescence (H2, burns with a pop) shows –OH.
2. **Ceric ammonium nitrate:** add 3–4 drops of the compound to 1 ml of the reagent. Yellow → red shows an alcohol.
3. **Ester formation:** warm 1 ml of compound with 1 ml of glacial acetic acid and 2 drops of conc. H2SO4 for 5 minutes; pour into cold water. Fruity smell shows an alcohol.
4. **Lucas test:** add 0.5 ml of compound to 2 ml of Lucas reagent at room temperature, shake and note the time for turbidity.
5. **Chromic acid test:** add 1 drop of compound to 1 ml of chromic acid. Orange → green shows a 1° or 2° alcohol.
6. **Iodoform test:** as in the carbonyl practical; positive for ethanol and propan-2-ol.

**Esters**

7. **Hydroxamic acid test:** warm 2 drops of the ester with 1 ml of 0.5 M hydroxylamine hydrochloride in ethanol and 0.5 ml of 6 M NaOH to boiling; cool, acidify with dilute HCl and add 2 drops of FeCl3. A red to violet colour shows an ester.
8. **Saponification test:** add 2 drops of phenolphthalein to 2 ml of dilute NaOH (pink); add the ester and warm on a water bath. The pink colour fades as the alkali is used up.
9. **Odour:** most simple esters have a fruity smell; methyl salicylate smells of wintergreen.

## Observations

| Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sodium | Effervescence of H2 | –OH present (alcohol, phenol or acid) |
| Ceric ammonium nitrate | Yellow → red | Alcohol |
| Acetic acid + H2SO4 | Fruity smell | Alcohol |
| Lucas | Turbidity at once | Tertiary alcohol |
| Lucas | Turbidity in about 5 minutes | Secondary alcohol |
| Lucas | No turbidity at room temperature | Primary alcohol |
| Chromic acid | Orange → green | 1° or 2° alcohol |
| Iodoform | Yellow precipitate | CH3CH(OH)– alcohol |
| Hydroxamic acid | Red-violet | Ester |
| NaOH + phenolphthalein, warm | Pink fades | Ester (saponified) |

**Worked record (example).** Colourless volatile liquid, burns with a non-smoky flame, soluble in water; effervescence with Na; CAN red; Lucas no turbidity; iodoform positive; boiling point about 78 °C → **ethanol**.

## Result

The given compound is a ___ (primary / secondary / tertiary) alcohol, or an ester.

## Precautions

1. The sample must be dry for the sodium test (water also gives H2).
2. Lucas test applies only to alcohols soluble in the reagent (up to about six carbons).
3. Acids and phenols must be ruled out first, since they also react with sodium.
4. Acidify before adding FeCl3 in the hydroxamic acid test (in alkali, iron precipitates as hydroxide).

## Viva questions

1. **What is the Lucas reagent?** Anhydrous zinc chloride in concentrated hydrochloric acid.
2. **Why do 3° alcohols react fastest in the Lucas test?** They form the most stable carbocation (SN1).
3. **Why do 3° alcohols not give the chromic acid test?** The carbon bearing –OH has no H to lose on oxidation.
4. **Name the coloured compound in the hydroxamic acid test.** Ferric hydroxamate.
5. **What is saponification?** Alkaline hydrolysis of an ester to a carboxylate salt and an alcohol.
6. **How is methanol distinguished from ethanol?** Methanol gives a negative iodoform test.

# Construction of molecular models

**Aim:** To build ball-and-stick models of simple organic molecules and use them to study hybridisation, bond angles, conformations and structural (and geometrical) isomerism.

> Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Count the pieces in the kit before and after use.

## Principle

Molecules are three-dimensional. Models show the shapes predicted by hybridisation:

| Hybridisation | Shape | Bond angle | Example | Model piece |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| sp3 | Tetrahedral | 109°28′ | Methane, ethane | Carbon ball with 4 holes (tetrahedral) |
| sp2 | Trigonal planar | 120° | Ethene, formaldehyde | Carbon with double bond (two bent springs or a double-bond connector) |
| sp | Linear | 180° | Ethyne | Carbon with triple bond (three connectors) |

Single bonds allow free rotation (conformations); double bonds do not (cis–trans isomers). Isomers with the same formula can be built from the same set of pieces but joined differently.

## Requirements

Molecular model kit: black balls (carbon, 4 holes), white (hydrogen, 1 hole), red (oxygen, 2 holes), blue (nitrogen, 3 holes), green (chlorine, 1 hole); straight sticks for single bonds and flexible springs or connectors for double and triple bonds; protractor.

## Procedure

1. **Methane (CH4):** join four white balls to one black ball. Measure an H–C–H angle; observe the tetrahedron (any two H can be the "front" ones — all equal).
2. **Ethane (C2H6):** join two carbons with one stick and complete with hydrogens. Rotate one carbon about the C–C bond and observe the **staggered** (H atoms as far apart as possible, more stable) and **eclipsed** (H atoms lined up, less stable) conformations.
3. **Ethene (C2H4):** join two carbons with two springs. Check that all six atoms lie in one plane and that the C=C bond cannot be twisted.
4. **Ethyne (C2H2):** join two carbons with three springs; the four atoms lie on a straight line.
5. **Isomers of butane (C4H10):** build n-butane and isobutane (2-methylpropane) from 4 black and 10 white balls; then build the 3 isomers of C5H12.
6. **cis- and trans-But-2-ene:** build both; try to turn one into the other without breaking the double bond (impossible).
7. **Position isomers:** build propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol.
8. **Functional isomers:** build ethanol and dimethyl ether (C2H6O) from the same pieces.
9. **Cyclohexane:** join six carbons in a ring with single bonds; flip between **chair** and **boat** forms and note the axial and equatorial H positions in the chair.

## Observations

| Model | Shape / feature | Angle | Isomer or conformer observed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Methane | Tetrahedral | 109°28′ | — |
| Ethane | Free rotation about C–C | 109°28′ | Staggered and eclipsed conformations |
| Ethene | Planar, rigid C=C | About 120° | — |
| Ethyne | Linear | 180° | — |
| Butane / isobutane | Different skeletons | — | Chain isomers (2) |
| Pentanes | — | — | Chain isomers (3) |
| But-2-ene | No rotation about C=C | — | cis and trans (geometrical isomers) |
| Propanols | –OH on C1 or C2 | — | Position isomers |
| Ethanol / dimethyl ether | Different connections of O | — | Functional isomers |
| Cyclohexane | Puckered ring | About 109.5° (strain-free) | Chair (more stable) and boat |

## Result

Models of the given molecules were constructed; the observed shapes, bond angles and isomers agree with sp3, sp2 and sp hybridisation.

## Precautions

1. Use the correct ball for each atom (number of holes = valency).
2. Use springs or bent connectors for double and triple bonds.
3. Do not force pieces; broken holes give wrong angles.
4. Dismantle and return all pieces to the kit.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is methane tetrahedral?** Four sp3 orbitals repel equally and point to the corners of a tetrahedron (109°28′).
2. **Why is staggered ethane more stable than eclipsed?** The C–H bonds on adjacent carbons are farthest apart, so torsional strain is least.
3. **Why does but-2-ene show cis–trans isomerism?** Rotation about the C=C would break the π bond.
4. **How many chain isomers does C5H12 have?** Three.
5. **Why is the chair form of cyclohexane more stable than the boat?** All bonds are staggered and there is no flagpole crowding.
6. **What is the shape of ethyne and why?** Linear, because each carbon is sp hybridised (180°).

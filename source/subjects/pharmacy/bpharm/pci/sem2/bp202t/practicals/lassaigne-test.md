# Detection of nitrogen, sulphur and halogens (Lassaigne's test)

**Aim:** To detect nitrogen, sulphur and halogens in the given organic compound by Lassaigne's sodium fusion test.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Sodium reacts violently with water: handle it with forceps, keep it under kerosene, and wear goggles during fusion.

## Principle

In organic compounds N, S and halogens are covalently bonded and do not give ionic tests. Fusing the compound with metallic sodium converts them into water-soluble **ionic sodium salts**:

```text
   Na + C + N  ──fusion──▶  NaCN
   2 Na + S    ──fusion──▶  Na2S
   Na + X      ──fusion──▶  NaX     (X = Cl, Br, I)
   Na + C + N + S ──────▶  NaSCN    (if N and S both present and sodium is limited)
```

The aqueous extract of the fused mass (**Lassaigne's extract** or sodium fusion extract) is then tested for CN⁻, S²⁻ and X⁻.

## Requirements

Sodium metal, fusion (ignition) tubes, china dish, distilled water, filter paper, freshly prepared ferrous sulphate solution, ferric chloride solution, dilute H2SO4, sodium nitroprusside solution, lead acetate solution, acetic acid, dilute HNO3, silver nitrate solution, dilute ammonia.

## Procedure

**A. Preparation of Lassaigne's extract**

1. Dry a small pea-sized piece of sodium between filter papers and place it in a clean fusion tube.
2. Heat gently until the sodium melts to a shiny globule.
3. Add a small amount (about 50 mg, or 2 drops) of the compound and heat, first gently, then strongly until the tube is red hot.
4. Plunge the red-hot tube into about 10 ml of distilled water in a china dish (cover the dish with a wire gauze; the tube shatters).
5. Crush the glass, boil the contents for a few minutes and filter. The clear, colourless filtrate is Lassaigne's extract.

**B. Test for nitrogen**

6. To 2 ml of extract add 1 ml of freshly prepared FeSO4 solution, boil, cool, add 2 drops of FeCl3 and acidify with dilute H2SO4. A **Prussian blue** colour or precipitate shows nitrogen.

**C. Test for sulphur**

7. To 1 ml of extract add a few drops of sodium nitroprusside: a **violet/purple** colour shows sulphur.
8. Acidify 1 ml of extract with acetic acid and add lead acetate: a **black precipitate** (PbS) confirms sulphur.

**D. Test for N and S together**

9. To 1 ml of extract add dilute HCl and FeCl3: a **blood-red** colour (Fe(SCN)3) shows both N and S. (In this case the Prussian blue test may fail.)

**E. Test for halogens**

10. Acidify 2 ml of extract with dilute HNO3 and boil for a few minutes (this removes CN⁻ as HCN and S²⁻ as H2S, which would otherwise give precipitates with AgNO3).
11. Cool and add AgNO3 solution. Observe the precipitate and test its solubility in dilute ammonia.

## Observations

| Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FeSO4 + FeCl3 + H2SO4 | Prussian blue | N present (NaCN → Na4[Fe(CN)6] → Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3) |
| Sodium nitroprusside | Violet | S present (Na4[Fe(CN)5NOS]) |
| Lead acetate | Black precipitate | S present (PbS) |
| FeCl3 + HCl | Blood red | N and S both (Fe(SCN)3) |
| AgNO3 | White curdy precipitate, soluble in ammonia | Cl present |
| AgNO3 | Pale yellow precipitate, sparingly soluble in ammonia | Br present |
| AgNO3 | Yellow precipitate, insoluble in ammonia | I present |

**Chemistry of the nitrogen test**

```text
   FeSO4 + 2 NaOH → Fe(OH)2 + Na2SO4
   6 NaCN + Fe(OH)2 → Na4[Fe(CN)6] + 2 NaOH
   3 Na4[Fe(CN)6] + 4 Fe³⁺ → Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3 (Prussian blue) + 12 Na⁺
```

**Worked record (example).** Extract gives Prussian blue, no colour with nitroprusside, white precipitate with AgNO3 soluble in ammonia → the compound contains **nitrogen and chlorine** but no sulphur.

## Result

The given compound contains ___ (N / S / Cl / Br / I) and does not contain ___.

## Precautions

1. Use clean, dry sodium and a dry tube; water causes explosions.
2. Heat the tube to red heat so that fusion is complete.
3. Use freshly prepared FeSO4 (old solutions are oxidised).
4. Boil with HNO3 before the halogen test to avoid false precipitates from CN⁻ and S²⁻.
5. Do not use excess compound; it gives a dark, charred extract.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is sodium used?** It is highly reactive and converts covalent N, S and X into ionic salts.
2. **Why is the extract boiled with HNO3 before adding AgNO3?** To decompose NaCN and Na2S, which would also precipitate with AgNO3.
3. **Why can the nitrogen test fail when S is also present?** NaSCN forms instead of NaCN, giving a blood-red colour with FeCl3, not Prussian blue.
4. **What is the formula of Prussian blue?** Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3, ferric ferrocyanide.
5. **Why does hydrazine not respond to Lassaigne's test for nitrogen?** It has no carbon, so NaCN cannot form.
6. **How are Cl, Br and I distinguished?** By the colour of the silver halide and its solubility in ammonia.

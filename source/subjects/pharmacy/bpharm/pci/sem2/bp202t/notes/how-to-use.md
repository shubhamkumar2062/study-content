# How to use this guide

Pharmaceutical Organic Chemistry I is a writing-and-drawing subject: most marks go to correct structures, correct names, and mechanisms drawn step by step with arrows. Study one unit a day: read the notes, redraw every mechanism and equation from memory on rough paper, name the examples without looking, then answer the question bank. Each unit has very short (2-mark), short answer (5-mark) and long essay (10-mark) questions, rated by how often that kind of question appears in PCI-syllabus pharmacy papers. Pharmaceutical Organic Chemistry II (BP301T, semester 3) continues from here with benzene, phenols, aromatic amines and acids, fats and oils, polynuclear hydrocarbons and cycloalkanes, so those topics appear here only as short cross-references.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ or very short answers | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with mechanism, equations and examples.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from common patterns and syllabus weight, not a promise of what will be asked.

**How the units follow the syllabus.** The five units match the five PCI units one to one. Unit 1 also has a short "toolkit" section (electronic effects and reaction intermediates) because the syllabus scope mentions intermediates and every later mechanism uses them. Unit 3 joins two syllabus headings (alkyl halides and alcohols), and Unit 5 joins carboxylic acids with aliphatic amines, exactly as the syllabus does.

**Where the marks usually come from**

| Unit | Topics that keep coming back |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Nomenclature and isomerism | IUPAC naming of given structures (and drawing structures from names), types of structural isomerism with examples, classification of organic compounds |
| 2 Alkanes, alkenes, dienes | E1 vs E2 with mechanism and kinetics, Saytzeff rule, Markownikoff and anti-Markownikoff (peroxide effect), ozonolysis, Diels–Alder, 1,2- vs 1,4-addition, stability of alkenes and dienes, halogenation of alkanes |
| 3 Alkyl halides and alcohols | SN1 vs SN2 (mechanism, kinetics, stereochemistry, factors), chloroform and iodoform, tests for alcohols (Lucas), uses of glycerol, chlorobutanol, benzyl alcohol |
| 4 Carbonyl compounds | Aldol, Cannizzaro (and crossed), benzoin, Perkin mechanisms; nucleophilic addition; electromeric effect; tests for aldehydes and ketones; uses of formaldehyde, hexamine, chloral hydrate, vanillin |
| 5 Acids and amines | Acidity and effect of substituents (inductive effect), basicity of aliphatic amines, tests for acids, esters and amides, structure and uses of aspirin, methyl salicylate, citric and lactic acid |

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. **Mechanisms:** number each step, show where every electron pair moves with a curved arrow (on paper) or →, mark the slow step, name the intermediate (carbocation, carbanion, free radical, enolate) and finish with the product. Examiners give a mark per correct step.
2. **Kinetics line:** for SN1/SN2 and E1/E2 always write the rate law (rate = k[RX] or k[RX][Nu]) — it is a quick, sure mark.
3. **Named reactions:** write the general equation, one specific example with reagents and conditions on the arrow (dilute vs concentrated NaOH, temperature, catalyst), the mechanism if asked, and one or two uses.
4. **Starred compounds:** the syllabus asks for nomenclature, **two methods of preparation, two reactions and uses** for alkanes, alkenes, dienes, alkyl halides, alcohols, carbonyl compounds, acids and amines. Learn the tables in each unit in that shape.
5. **"Structure and uses" questions:** draw the full structure, give the IUPAC name, one preparation and three or four uses with a drug link (e.g. chlorobutanol as an injection preservative, hexamine as a urinary antiseptic).
6. **Nomenclature questions:** show your working — circle the parent chain, number it, then list prefixes alphabetically. Partial marks are given for the correct parent and numbering even if one prefix slips.
7. **Comparison questions** (SN1 vs SN2, E1 vs E2, inductive vs electromeric): use a table of at least six points.
8. **Order questions** (acidity, basicity, reactivity): write the order, then the reason (inductive, resonance, steric, solvation) in one line each.

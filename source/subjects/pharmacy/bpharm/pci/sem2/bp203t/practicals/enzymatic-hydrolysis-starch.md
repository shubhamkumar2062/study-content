# Study of enzymatic hydrolysis of starch

**Aim:** To study the hydrolysis of starch by salivary amylase and to find the achromic point.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Each student should use only his or her own saliva and rinse the mouth before collecting it.

## Principle

Salivary **α-amylase (ptyalin)** hydrolyses the internal **α1→4 glycosidic bonds** of starch. Its optimum pH is about 6.8, it works best near 37 °C, and it is activated by chloride ions.

Starch is broken down in stages, which are followed by the colour with iodine:

```text
Starch            → blue with iodine
Amylodextrins     → violet
Erythrodextrins   → red / reddish-brown
Achroodextrins    → no colour (achromic)
Maltose (+ isomaltose, small amounts of glucose) → no colour; reduces Benedict's reagent
```

The **achromic point** is the time at which a drop of the reaction mixture no longer gives any colour with iodine. A shorter achromic time means a more active enzyme.

## Requirements

1% starch solution (in 0.1 M phosphate buffer, pH 6.8, with 0.5% NaCl), saliva (diluted about 1 in 10 with distilled water and filtered), dilute iodine solution, Benedict's reagent, spotting (porcelain tile) plate, test tubes, water bath at 37 °C, stopwatch, droppers.

## Procedure

1. Place a drop of dilute iodine in each well of a spotting tile.
2. Warm 10 ml of buffered starch solution in a test tube at 37 °C for 5 minutes.
3. Add 1 ml of diluted saliva, mix and start the stopwatch.
4. Every minute (or 30 seconds), transfer one drop of the mixture to a fresh iodine well and note the colour.
5. Continue until a drop gives no colour with iodine. Note this time as the **achromic point**.
6. At the end, take 2 ml of the mixture and perform **Benedict's test**; a reddish precipitate shows reducing sugars (maltose) formed.
7. **Control**: repeat with 1 ml of saliva that has been boiled for 5 minutes and cooled. The blue colour stays and Benedict's test is negative.

**Achromic point** = ______ minutes.

## Result

Starch was hydrolysed by salivary amylase through dextrins to maltose. The achromic point under the given conditions was ______ minutes. Boiled saliva did not hydrolyse starch, showing that the activity is due to an enzyme destroyed by heat.

## Precautions

1. Keep the temperature constant at 37 °C.
2. Use a fresh clean dropper each time, or rinse it, so iodine does not enter the reaction tube.
3. Use dilute iodine; strong iodine masks colour changes.
4. Take samples at exact time intervals.

## Viva questions

1. **Which bond does salivary amylase hydrolyse?** Internal α1→4 glycosidic bonds of starch (it cannot break α1→6 branch points).
2. **What is the achromic point?** The time when the digest no longer gives colour with iodine.
3. **Why is NaCl added?** Chloride ions activate salivary amylase.
4. **Why does boiled saliva not act?** Heat denatures the enzyme protein.
5. **Why does amylase action stop in the stomach?** The acid pH (about 1.5–2) inactivates it; pancreatic amylase continues digestion in the intestine.
6. **What is the end product of amylase action?** Mainly maltose, with isomaltose and limit dextrins.

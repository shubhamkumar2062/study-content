# Quantitative estimation of reducing sugars (DNSA method) and proteins (biuret method)

**Aim:** (A) To estimate the amount of reducing sugar in a given sample by the 3,5-dinitrosalicylic acid (DNSA) method. (B) To estimate the amount of protein in a given sample by the biuret method.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Both methods use a colorimeter or spectrophotometer; zero the instrument with the reagent blank each time.

## Principle

**Part A — DNSA method.** In alkaline solution on boiling, the free aldehyde or keto group of a reducing sugar reduces yellow 3,5-dinitrosalicylic acid to orange-red **3-amino-5-nitrosalicylic acid**, while the sugar is oxidised. The intensity of colour, read at about **540 nm**, is proportional to the sugar concentration (Beer–Lambert law). Rochelle salt (sodium potassium tartrate) stabilises the colour.

**Part B — Biuret method.** In alkaline medium, Cu2+ forms a **violet** complex with the nitrogen atoms of peptide bonds. Colour intensity, read at about **540 nm**, is proportional to the number of peptide bonds and so to protein concentration. The method is simple and depends little on amino acid composition, but it is not very sensitive (about 1–10 mg of protein per tube).

Both use a **standard curve**: absorbance of known standards is plotted against concentration and the unknown is read from the line.

## Requirements

**Part A**: standard glucose solution (1 mg/ml; dilute to 0.1–1.0 mg per tube), DNSA reagent, test sample, distilled water, boiling water bath, test tubes, pipettes, colorimeter.

**Part B**: standard bovine serum albumin (BSA, 10 mg/ml), biuret reagent (CuSO4, sodium potassium tartrate, NaOH, KI), test sample, test tubes, pipettes, colorimeter.

## Procedure

### Part A — Reducing sugar by DNSA

1. Pipette 0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8 and 1.0 ml of standard glucose (1 mg/ml) into labelled tubes (0 to 1 mg glucose); make each up to 1 ml with water. The first is the blank.
2. Take 1 ml of the unknown in a separate tube (dilute if needed).
3. Add 1 ml DNSA reagent to every tube.
4. Heat all tubes in a boiling water bath for 5 minutes; cool under running water.
5. Add 8 ml distilled water to each and mix.
6. Read absorbance at 540 nm against the blank.
7. Plot absorbance against mg glucose and read the unknown from the curve.

### Part B — Protein by biuret

1. Pipette 0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8 and 1.0 ml of standard BSA (10 mg/ml) into tubes (0 to 10 mg protein); make each up to 1 ml with water.
2. Take 1 ml of the unknown in a separate tube.
3. Add 4 ml biuret reagent to each tube; mix.
4. Keep at room temperature for 30 minutes.
5. Read absorbance at 540 nm against the blank.
6. Plot the standard curve and find the unknown.

## Observations and calculations

| Tube | Standard (ml) | Amount (mg) | Absorbance (540 nm) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blank | 0 | 0 | 0.00 |
| 1 | 0.2 | | |
| 2 | 0.4 | | |
| 3 | 0.6 | | |
| 4 | 0.8 | | |
| 5 | 1.0 | | |
| Unknown | 1.0 (sample) | ? | |

If the curve is a straight line through the origin:

**Amount in test (mg) = (A test / A standard) × amount in that standard (mg)**

**Concentration (mg/ml) = amount in test ÷ volume of sample taken × dilution factor**

**Worked example (Part A).** Standard 0.6 mg glucose gives A = 0.48; the unknown gives A = 0.36. Amount = (0.36/0.48) × 0.6 = **0.45 mg** in 1 ml, so the sample contains 0.45 mg/ml (45 mg/100 ml).

**Worked example (Part B).** Standard 6 mg BSA gives A = 0.30; unknown gives A = 0.25. Amount = (0.25/0.30) × 6 = **5.0 mg** in 1 ml, i.e. 5 mg/ml.

## Result

A. The given sample contains ______ mg/ml of reducing sugar (as glucose).
B. The given sample contains ______ mg/ml of protein.

## Precautions

1. Use the same heating time for all DNSA tubes and cool them together.
2. Keep the unknown within the range of the standard curve; dilute and multiply back if it is too high.
3. Read biuret tubes after the same standing time.
4. Clean cuvettes and zero with the blank before each set.
5. Avoid ammonium salts and Tris buffers in the biuret test (they interfere).

## Viva questions

1. **What is reduced in the DNSA test?** 3,5-Dinitrosalicylic acid is reduced to 3-amino-5-nitrosalicylic acid (orange-red).
2. **Will sucrose react in the DNSA test?** Not directly, as it is non-reducing; it reacts after acid or invertase hydrolysis.
3. **Why is a standard curve needed?** It relates absorbance to concentration under the same conditions and checks linearity (Beer–Lambert law).
4. **What is the role of KI in the biuret reagent?** It prevents reduction of Cu2+ (auto-reduction) and keeps the reagent stable.
5. **Name more sensitive protein methods.** Lowry (Folin–Ciocalteu), Bradford (Coomassie blue) and UV absorbance at 280 nm.
6. **Why is wavelength 540 nm chosen?** It is near the maximum absorption of the coloured products, giving the best sensitivity.

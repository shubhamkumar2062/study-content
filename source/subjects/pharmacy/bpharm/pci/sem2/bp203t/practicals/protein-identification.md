# Identification tests for proteins (albumin and casein)

**Aim:** To perform colour and precipitation reactions of proteins and to distinguish albumin from casein.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Concentrated nitric acid and Millon's reagent (contains mercury) are corrosive and toxic: use small amounts, wear gloves and goggles, and discard waste as instructed.

## Principle

Proteins give colour reactions with their peptide bonds and with particular amino acid side chains, and they precipitate with heat, salts, heavy metals, acids and alkaloidal reagents.

- **Biuret test**: in alkaline medium, Cu2+ forms a violet complex with two or more peptide bonds.
- **Ninhydrin test**: free α-amino groups give a blue-purple colour (Ruhemann's purple).
- **Xanthoproteic test**: concentrated HNO3 nitrates aromatic rings (tyrosine, tryptophan, phenylalanine) to yellow nitro compounds, turning orange in alkali.
- **Millon's test**: the phenolic group of tyrosine gives a brick-red colour with mercury salts in nitric acid.
- **Hopkins–Cole test**: the indole ring of tryptophan gives a violet ring with glyoxylic acid and concentrated H2SO4.
- **Lead acetate (sulphur) test**: sulphur of cysteine is released by boiling with alkali and forms black lead sulphide.
- **Heat coagulation**: albumin coagulates on heating near its isoelectric pH; casein does not.
- **Isoelectric precipitation**: casein precipitates at its isoelectric pH, about 4.6.
- **Phosphorus test**: casein is a phosphoprotein; after digestion, phosphate gives a yellow precipitate with ammonium molybdate.

## Requirements

Egg albumin solution (about 2%), casein (dissolved in dilute NaOH, about 1%), 10% NaOH, 1% CuSO4, 0.1% ninhydrin, concentrated HNO3, Millon's reagent, glyoxylic acid (Hopkins–Cole reagent), concentrated H2SO4, lead acetate, 1% acetic acid, saturated ammonium sulphate, mercuric chloride or lead acetate solution, trichloroacetic acid, ammonium molybdate, test tubes, water bath, pH paper.

## Procedure

1. **Biuret test**: 2 ml protein solution + 2 ml 10% NaOH + 2 drops 1% CuSO4; mix. Violet colour.
2. **Ninhydrin test**: 2 ml solution + 0.5 ml ninhydrin; boil 1–2 minutes. Blue-purple colour.
3. **Xanthoproteic test**: 2 ml solution + 1 ml concentrated HNO3; boil and cool; add 40% NaOH in excess. Yellow, turning orange.
4. **Millon's test**: 2 ml solution + 3 drops Millon's reagent; warm. Brick-red precipitate or colour.
5. **Hopkins–Cole test**: 2 ml solution + 2 ml glyoxylic acid; pour concentrated H2SO4 down the side. Violet ring.
6. **Sulphur test**: 2 ml solution + 2 ml 40% NaOH; boil; add 2 drops lead acetate. Black or brown precipitate.
7. **Heat coagulation**: heat 3 ml of solution in a boiling water bath; add a drop of 1% acetic acid. White coagulum with albumin.
8. **Half and full saturation with ammonium sulphate**: add an equal volume of saturated ammonium sulphate (half saturation; globulins precipitate), filter, then saturate the filtrate with solid salt (albumin precipitates).
9. **Heavy metal and acid reagents**: add mercuric chloride or lead acetate, and separately trichloroacetic acid. Precipitates form.
10. **Isoelectric precipitation of casein**: to casein in dilute alkali add 1% acetic acid drop by drop. Maximum precipitate near pH 4.6, redissolving in excess acid.
11. **Phosphorus test for casein**: digest a little casein with fusion mixture or concentrated HNO3, add ammonium molybdate and warm. Yellow precipitate.

## Observations

| Test | Albumin | Casein | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Biuret | Violet | Violet | Peptide bonds present |
| Ninhydrin | Purple | Purple | α-Amino acids present |
| Xanthoproteic | Yellow → orange | Yellow → orange | Aromatic amino acids |
| Millon's | Red | Red | Tyrosine present |
| Hopkins–Cole | Violet ring | Violet ring | Tryptophan present |
| Sulphur | Black | Faint (low cysteine) | Sulphur amino acids |
| Heat coagulation | Coagulates | No coagulum | Distinguishes albumin |
| Full saturation (NH4)2SO4 | Precipitates | Precipitates | Salting out |

| Phosphorus | Negative | Yellow precipitate | Casein is a phosphoprotein |

## Result

Both samples are proteins. Sample ___ is albumin (coagulates on heating, soluble in water) and sample ___ is casein (precipitates at pH 4.6, gives the phosphorus test).

## Precautions

1. Add CuSO4 drop by drop in the biuret test; excess blue masks the violet.
2. Use concentrated acids carefully; do not boil acid tubes towards yourself.
3. Use dilute protein solutions for colour tests.
4. Add acetic acid slowly for isoelectric precipitation; excess redissolves casein.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is the biuret test so called?** Biuret (formed by heating urea) gives the same violet colour with Cu2+ because it has two peptide-like bonds.
2. **Do amino acids give the biuret test?** No; at least two peptide bonds are needed (dipeptides do not respond).
3. **Which amino acid gives Millon's test?** Tyrosine (phenolic –OH).
4. **What is the isoelectric pH of casein?** About 4.6; solubility is lowest there, which is why milk curdles in acid.
5. **Why does albumin coagulate on heating?** Heat denatures it, exposing hydrophobic groups so molecules aggregate.
6. **What is salting out?** Precipitation of proteins by high salt concentration, which removes their water of hydration; used to fractionate globulins and albumins.
7. **What does the yellow stain of nitric acid on skin show?** The xanthoproteic reaction with skin proteins.

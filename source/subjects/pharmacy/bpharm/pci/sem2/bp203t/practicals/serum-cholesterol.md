# Determination of serum total cholesterol (enzymatic CHOD-PAP method)

**Aim:** To estimate the total cholesterol content of the given serum sample.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ; some colleges use Zak's ferric chloride–sulphuric acid method instead (see the viva). If a kit is supplied, follow its leaflet.

## Principle

About two-thirds of plasma cholesterol is esterified. The enzymatic method converts all of it to free cholesterol and then measures it:

```text
Cholesterol esters + H2O ──cholesterol esterase──► Cholesterol + Fatty acids
Cholesterol + O2 ──cholesterol oxidase (CHOD)──► Cholest-4-en-3-one + H2O2
2 H2O2 + 4-aminoantipyrine + phenol ──peroxidase──► Quinoneimine (red) + 4 H2O
```

The red quinoneimine dye, read at about **505 nm** (500–550 nm), is proportional to total cholesterol.

Approximate interpretation of adult total cholesterol (targets vary with cardiovascular risk; ranges vary by laboratory): desirable below about **200 mg/dl**; borderline high about **200–239 mg/dl**; high **240 mg/dl or above**.

## Requirements

Serum (preferably fasting), cholesterol reagent (cholesterol esterase, cholesterol oxidase, peroxidase, 4-aminoantipyrine, phenol in buffer), cholesterol standard (200 mg/dl), micropipettes, test tubes, water bath at 37 °C, colorimeter.

## Procedure

| Tube | Reagent | Standard (200 mg/dl) | Serum |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blank | 1.0 ml | — | — |
| Standard | 1.0 ml | 10 µl | — |
| Test | 1.0 ml | — | 10 µl |

1. Mix and incubate at 37 °C for 10 minutes (or room temperature for about 20 minutes, as the kit states).
2. Read the absorbance of standard and test against the blank at 505 nm.

## Observations and calculation

**Total cholesterol (mg/dl) = (A test / A standard) × 200**

| Tube | Absorbance (505 nm) |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | |
| Test | |

**Worked example.** A standard = 0.30, A test = 0.27. Cholesterol = (0.27/0.30) × 200 = **180 mg/dl** (desirable range).

To convert to mmol/l, divide mg/dl by about 38.7 (180 mg/dl ≈ 4.65 mmol/l).

## Result

The total cholesterol content of the given serum is ______ mg/dl.

## Precautions

1. Use clear, non-haemolysed serum; bilirubin and haemoglobin interfere at high levels.
2. Pipette the viscous serum carefully and rinse the tip in the reagent.
3. Keep standard and test under identical conditions.
4. Store the enzyme reagent cold and discard it if the blank becomes coloured.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is cholesterol esterase added?** To release free cholesterol from its esters so that total cholesterol is measured.
2. **What is the principle of Zak's method?** Cholesterol gives a purple-red colour with ferric chloride and concentrated sulphuric acid after protein precipitation.
3. **What is "good" and "bad" cholesterol?** HDL cholesterol (removes cholesterol to liver, protective) and LDL cholesterol (deposits in arteries).
4. **Name causes of hypercholesterolaemia.** Familial hypercholesterolaemia, diabetes, hypothyroidism, nephrotic syndrome, obstructive jaundice.
5. **Which enzyme is the target of statins?** HMG-CoA reductase.
6. **How is LDL cholesterol often calculated?** By the Friedewald formula: LDL = total cholesterol − HDL − triglycerides/5 (in mg/dl, valid when triglycerides are below about 400 mg/dl).

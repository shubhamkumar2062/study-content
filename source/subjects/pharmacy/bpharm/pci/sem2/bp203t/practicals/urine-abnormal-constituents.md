# Qualitative analysis of urine for abnormal constituents

**Aim:** To test a given urine sample for abnormal constituents: glucose, protein, ketone bodies, bile salts, bile pigments and blood.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Handle urine samples with gloves as potentially infectious material and disinfect spills. Many laboratories now also use dipstick strips; they work on similar principles.

## Principle

Normal urine (about 1–1.5 litres a day; pH about 4.5–8, usually slightly acid; specific gravity about 1.003–1.030; ranges vary by laboratory) contains only traces of glucose and protein and no ketone bodies, bile salts, bile pigments or blood. Their presence points to disease.

| Constituent | Test | Principle | Seen in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Glucose (reducing sugar) | Benedict's test | Reduction of Cu2+ to red Cu2O | Diabetes mellitus, renal glycosuria |
| Protein | Heat and acetic acid test; sulphosalicylic acid test | Heat coagulation; acid precipitation | Nephrotic syndrome, glomerulonephritis, pre-eclampsia |
| Ketone bodies | Rothera's test | Acetoacetate and acetone give a purple complex with sodium nitroprusside in alkali | Diabetic ketoacidosis, starvation |
| Bile salts | Hay's sulphur test | Bile salts lower surface tension so sulphur powder sinks | Obstructive and hepatic jaundice |
| Bile pigments (bilirubin) | Fouchet's test | Ferric chloride oxidises bilirubin (adsorbed on BaSO4) to green biliverdin | Obstructive and hepatic jaundice |
| Blood (haemoglobin) | Benzidine or orthotoluidine (or guaiac) test | Peroxidase-like activity of haem releases oxygen from H2O2, which oxidises the dye to blue/green | Kidney stones, infection, injury, haemolysis |

## Requirements

Urine sample, Benedict's reagent, 1% acetic acid, 20% sulphosalicylic acid, ammonium sulphate (solid), sodium nitroprusside (solid or fresh 5% solution), liquor ammonia, sulphur powder, 10% barium chloride, Fouchet's reagent (ferric chloride in trichloroacetic acid), orthotoluidine (or benzidine substitute) reagent, hydrogen peroxide, filter paper, test tubes, water bath.

## Procedure

1. **Physical examination**: note volume, colour, odour, appearance and pH (pH paper); measure specific gravity with a urinometer if available.
2. **Glucose — Benedict's test**: 5 ml Benedict's reagent + 8 drops urine; boil 2–3 minutes. Grade the colour.
3. **Protein — heat and acetic acid test**: fill a tube two-thirds with clear urine; heat the upper third. Turbidity that **persists** after adding 3–4 drops of 1% acetic acid shows protein (phosphate turbidity dissolves in acid).
4. **Protein — sulphosalicylic acid test**: 3 ml urine + 3 drops 20% sulphosalicylic acid. White turbidity shows protein.
5. **Ketone bodies — Rothera's test**: saturate 5 ml urine with solid ammonium sulphate, add a small crystal of sodium nitroprusside, shake, and gently overlay 1–2 ml liquor ammonia. A **purple (permanganate-coloured) ring** within a few minutes shows acetoacetate or acetone.
6. **Bile salts — Hay's test**: sprinkle a little sulphur powder on the surface of urine in a beaker. Particles **sinking** show bile salts; floating particles are normal.
7. **Bile pigments — Fouchet's test**: 5 ml urine + 2–3 ml 10% BaCl2; filter. Spread the precipitate on filter paper and add a drop of Fouchet's reagent. **Green or blue** colour shows bilirubin.
8. **Blood — orthotoluidine test**: mix 2 ml urine with the reagent and add a few drops of H2O2. **Blue-green** colour shows blood.

## Observations

| Test | Observation | Inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Benedict's | Blue / green / yellow / orange / brick red | Glucose absent / trace / + / ++ / +++ (roughly 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2% or more) |
| Heat and acetic acid | Turbidity persists / clears | Protein present / absent |
| Sulphosalicylic acid | White turbidity / clear | Protein present / absent |
| Rothera's | Purple ring / none | Ketone bodies present / absent |
| Hay's | Sulphur sinks / floats | Bile salts present / absent |
| Fouchet's | Green-blue / no change | Bile pigments present / absent |
| Orthotoluidine | Blue-green / none | Blood present / absent |

## Result

The given urine sample contains the following abnormal constituents: ______ (e.g. glucose and ketone bodies, suggesting uncontrolled diabetes mellitus).

## Precautions

1. Use fresh urine; on standing, bacteria break down urea (alkaline urine) and bilirubin is oxidised.
2. Filter cloudy urine before protein tests.
3. Add acetic acid in the heat test to dissolve phosphates.
4. Prepare nitroprusside solution fresh.
5. Benzidine is a carcinogen; use the safer orthotoluidine or guaiac reagents or dipsticks.

## Viva questions

1. **Does Rothera's test detect β-hydroxybutyrate?** No; it detects acetoacetate (and acetone less strongly).
2. **Why add acetic acid in the heat coagulation test?** To dissolve phosphate turbidity and to bring the pH near the isoelectric point of albumin.
3. **What is the renal threshold for glucose?** About 180 mg/dl of plasma glucose.
4. **Which drugs can give a false-positive Benedict's test?** Large doses of vitamin C, salicylates and some antibiotics (cephalosporins) reduce copper.
5. **In which jaundice are bile pigments absent from urine?** Haemolytic jaundice (unconjugated bilirubin is not filtered).
6. **What is Bence Jones protein?** Immunoglobulin light chains in urine in multiple myeloma; they precipitate at about 40–60 °C and redissolve near boiling.

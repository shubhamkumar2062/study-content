# Determination of blood sugar (glucose oxidase–peroxidase method)

**Aim:** To estimate the glucose content of the given blood (plasma or serum) sample by the GOD-POD method.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ; colleges may use the o-toluidine or Folin–Wu method instead. If a kit is supplied, follow its leaflet. Treat blood as potentially infectious.

## Principle

The method uses two enzymes and is specific for glucose:

```text
Glucose + O2 + H2O ──glucose oxidase (GOD)──► Gluconic acid + H2O2
2 H2O2 + 4-aminoantipyrine + phenol ──peroxidase (POD)──► Quinoneimine (red-pink) + 4 H2O
```

The intensity of the red-pink quinoneimine dye, read at about **505 nm**, is proportional to the glucose concentration. Blood is collected in a **fluoride–oxalate** vial: fluoride inhibits enolase and stops glycolysis by blood cells, and oxalate prevents clotting.

Approximate reference values (ranges vary by laboratory): fasting plasma glucose about **70–100 mg/dl**; 2 hours after a meal below about **140 mg/dl**. Diabetes is diagnosed at fasting ≥ 126 mg/dl or 2-hour (75 g glucose) ≥ 200 mg/dl.

## Requirements

Plasma or serum, GOD-POD reagent (enzymes, 4-aminoantipyrine, phenol in phosphate buffer), glucose standard (100 mg/dl), micropipettes, test tubes, water bath at 37 °C, colorimeter.

## Procedure

1. Label three tubes: Blank (B), Standard (S), Test (T).

| Tube | Reagent | Standard (100 mg/dl) | Sample | Water |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blank | 1.0 ml | — | — | 10 µl |
| Standard | 1.0 ml | 10 µl | — | — |
| Test | 1.0 ml | — | 10 µl | — |

2. Mix and incubate at 37 °C for 10–15 minutes (or as the kit states).
3. Read absorbance of S and T against B at 505 nm within the time the colour is stable (usually about an hour).

## Observations and calculation

**Glucose (mg/dl) = (A test / A standard) × concentration of standard (100 mg/dl)**

| Tube | Absorbance (505 nm) |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | |
| Test | |

**Worked example.** A standard = 0.40, A test = 0.36. Glucose = (0.36/0.40) × 100 = **90 mg/dl** (normal fasting value).

## Result

The glucose content of the given blood sample is ______ mg/dl.

## Precautions

1. Separate plasma soon after collection, or use fluoride vials, so glucose is not consumed by cells.
2. Use accurate micropipettes; the sample volume is very small.
3. Keep incubation time and temperature the same for standard and test.
4. Dilute samples above the linear range of the kit (often about 400–500 mg/dl) and multiply the result.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is GOD-POD preferred?** Glucose oxidase is specific for β-D-glucose, so other reducing substances do not interfere.
2. **Why is fluoride used?** It inhibits enolase and stops glycolysis in the sample.
3. **What is the renal threshold for glucose?** About 180 mg/dl.
4. **What is HbA1c?** Glycated haemoglobin reflecting average glucose over about 2–3 months; 6.5% or above suggests diabetes.
5. **Name causes of hypoglycaemia.** Insulin or sulphonylurea overdose, insulinoma, prolonged fasting, glycogen storage disease type I, liver failure.
6. **What is an oral glucose tolerance test?** Measuring plasma glucose fasting and 2 hours after 75 g oral glucose to diagnose diabetes or impaired tolerance.

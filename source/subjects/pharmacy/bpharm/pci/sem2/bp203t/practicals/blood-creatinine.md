# Determination of blood creatinine (Jaffe's alkaline picrate method)

**Aim:** To estimate the amount of creatinine in the given blood (serum) sample.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Many laboratories use a ready-made kit (often a kinetic Jaffe or enzymatic method); follow the kit leaflet if one is supplied. Treat all blood samples as potentially infectious.

## Principle

Creatinine is formed at a nearly constant rate from creatine phosphate in muscle and is excreted by the kidneys mainly by glomerular filtration, so its serum level reflects kidney function.

In **Jaffe's reaction**, creatinine reacts with **picric acid in alkaline medium** to form an **orange-red creatinine picrate complex**, measured at about **520 nm**. Proteins interfere, so they are first removed by precipitation with **tungstic acid** (Folin–Wu protein-free filtrate). The colour of the test is compared with a creatinine standard treated in the same way.

Approximate serum creatinine is about **0.6–1.2 mg/dl in men** and **0.5–1.1 mg/dl in women** (ranges vary by laboratory and method).

## Requirements

Serum or oxalated blood, 10% sodium tungstate, 2/3 N sulphuric acid, saturated picric acid, 0.75 N NaOH, creatinine working standard (0.002 mg/ml in dilute HCl), centrifuge, test tubes, pipettes, colorimeter.

## Procedure

1. **Protein-free filtrate (1 in 10)**: to 1 ml blood (or serum) add 7 ml water, 1 ml 10% sodium tungstate and 1 ml 2/3 N H2SO4; mix, stand 10 minutes and centrifuge or filter. Each 1 ml of filtrate = 0.1 ml blood.
2. Set up three tubes:

| Tube | Filtrate | Standard (0.002 mg/ml) | Water | Picric acid | 0.75 N NaOH |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blank | — | — | 3 ml | 1 ml | 1 ml |
| Standard | — | 3 ml | — | 1 ml | 1 ml |
| Test | 3 ml | — | — | 1 ml | 1 ml |

3. Mix and keep at room temperature for 15 minutes.
4. Read absorbance at 520 nm (green filter) against the blank.

## Observations and calculation

- Test tube contains filtrate from 0.3 ml blood.
- Standard tube contains 3 × 0.002 = 0.006 mg creatinine.

**Serum creatinine (mg/dl) = (A test / A standard) × 0.006 × (100 / 0.3) = (A test / A standard) × 2**

| Tube | Absorbance (520 nm) |
| --- | --- |
| Standard | |
| Test | |

**Worked example.** A standard = 0.42, A test = 0.21. Creatinine = (0.21/0.42) × 2 = **1.0 mg/dl**, within the usual adult range.

## Result

The creatinine content of the given blood sample is ______ mg/dl.

## Precautions

1. Prepare a clear protein-free filtrate.
2. Keep the time of colour development the same for test and standard; the colour fades and other substances react slowly.
3. Picric acid is explosive when dry; keep it moist and never let it dry on the bench.
4. Avoid haemolysed samples.

## Viva questions

1. **From what is creatinine formed?** Non-enzymatically from creatine phosphate in muscle.
2. **Why is serum creatinine a good test of kidney function?** It is produced at a fairly constant rate and is cleared mainly by glomerular filtration.
3. **What are non-creatinine chromogens?** Substances such as glucose, acetoacetate, ascorbic acid and some cephalosporins that also give colour with alkaline picrate, causing false high values.
4. **What is creatinine clearance?** The volume of plasma cleared of creatinine per minute; an estimate of GFR (about 120 ml/min in healthy adults).
5. **Name conditions with raised serum creatinine.** Acute and chronic kidney disease, urinary obstruction, dehydration, very high muscle mass.
6. **Why is tungstic acid used?** It precipitates proteins that would otherwise interfere with the colour reaction.

# How to use this guide

Biochemistry is a subject of pathways, enzymes and diseases. Most marks come from drawing a pathway correctly (with the enzyme at each step), giving its energy yield and stating its significance, and from linking an enzyme defect to the disease it causes. Study one unit every two days: read the notes, write every pathway once from memory with its enzymes, learn the disorder tables, then answer the question bank without looking.

The five units of this guide follow the five units of the PCI syllabus exactly; no unit has been split or joined.

## Exam pattern (PCI end-semester theory paper)

**75 marks, 3 hours.** Another 25 marks come from sessional (internal) assessment. Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective questions (MCQs) — all compulsory | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

In the question bank, **long** questions carry 10 marks, **short** questions 5 marks, and **very-short** questions 2 marks (useful for sessional tests and quick revision).

## Probability ratings

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with the pathway diagram, enzymes, energetics and significance.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from syllabus weight and the kind of questions usually set in PCI-syllabus pharmacy papers. They are not a promise of what will be asked.

## ATP yields: two conventions

Older Indian textbooks count **3 ATP per NADH and 2 per FADH2**, giving 38 ATP per glucose and 129 per palmitate. Current books use measured values of **2.5 and 1.5**, giving **32 ATP per glucose** and **106 per palmitate**. Both are accepted. Write one line stating the convention you use, then stay consistent. The notes give both.

| Fuel | Newer (2.5 / 1.5) | Older (3 / 2) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glycolysis, anaerobic | 2 | 2 |
| Glycolysis, aerobic (malate–aspartate shuttle) | 7 | 8 |
| One TCA turn (per acetyl-CoA) | 10 | 12 |
| One glucose, complete | 32 (30 via glycerol phosphate shuttle) | 38 (36) |
| One palmitic acid | 106 | 129 |

## Scoring tips for Biochemistry

1. **Draw the pathway vertically**, substrate → enzyme on the arrow → product, and mark ATP used or formed, NADH/FADH2 formed, irreversible steps and the rate-limiting enzyme. Examiners award most marks for a clean, correct pathway.
2. **Answer pathway questions in a fixed order**: definition → site (organ and cell part) → steps with enzymes → energetics (table) → regulation → significance → related disorders.
3. **Use tables for disorders**: disease, enzyme missing, accumulating substance, features, treatment (glycogen storage diseases, urea cycle disorders, PKU/alkaptonuria/albinism/tyrosinaemia, types of jaundice).
4. **Link to pharmacy**: name a drug wherever one fits — statins (HMG-CoA reductase), allopurinol (xanthine oxidase), methotrexate (DHFR), primaquine (G6PD deficiency), rifampicin (RNA polymerase), tetracycline and chloramphenicol (ribosome), streptokinase and asparaginase (therapeutic enzymes). This lifts an average answer.
5. **For enzyme kinetics**, draw both graphs with labelled axes and intercepts, and the three Lineweaver–Burk inhibition patterns side by side.
6. **Quote reference values with care**: say "about" and that ranges vary between laboratories (fasting glucose about 70–100 mg/dl; serum bilirubin about 0.2–1.0 mg/dl; uric acid about 3–7 mg/dl).
7. **Use the correct spelling** of key enzymes and disorders (phenylketonuria, alkaptonuria, argininosuccinate, Lineweaver–Burk, Okazaki), and write formulae plainly (NAD+, FADH2, CO2).
8. **Practical viva** (BP209P): know the principle and the colour of each qualitative test (Molisch, Benedict, Barfoed, Seliwanoff, iodine, biuret, Millon, xanthoproteic, Rothera, Hay, Fouchet) and the formula used in each quantitative estimation.

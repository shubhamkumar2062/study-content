# Unit 5: Reproductive system and introduction to genetics

The reproductive system makes **gametes** (sperm and ova), brings them together, and in the female supports the developing baby until birth. It is controlled by the hypothalamic–pituitary hormones of Unit 4 (GnRH, FSH, LH) and by the **sex hormones** it makes itself. Oral contraceptives, fertility drugs, hormone replacement and drugs used in labour all act on the steps described here. The second part of the unit introduces **genetics** — how chromosomes, genes and DNA carry inherited information, how a gene is turned into a protein, and how traits pass from parents to children.

**Testes (testicles).** Two oval glands about 4–5 cm long in the **scrotum**, which keeps them about **2–3 °C below body temperature** — needed for normal sperm production (the cremaster and dartos muscles adjust this). **Semen:** a mixture of sperm and gland secretions; normally about 2–5 mL per ejaculate, pH about 7.2–8, with tens of millions of sperm per mL (the WHO lower reference limit is 15 million/mL).

## 5.4 Functions of the female reproductive system

1. **Oogenesis** — production of ova.
2. **Secretion of sex hormones** — oestrogens, progesterone.
3. **Menstrual (reproductive) cycle** — preparing the uterus each month for pregnancy.
4. **Reception of sperm** and **fertilization** in the uterine tube.
5. **Implantation, pregnancy** and nourishment of the fetus through the placenta.
6. **Parturition** (childbirth) and **lactation**.

## 5.5 Sex hormones

| Hormone | Source | Main actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Testosterone** (converted to more potent **dihydrotestosterone** by 5α-reductase in target tissues) | Leydig cells; small amounts from the adrenal cortex | Develops male genitalia in the fetus and their growth at puberty; **secondary sexual characters** (beard and body hair, deep voice from enlarged larynx, broad shoulders, muscle growth); spermatogenesis (with FSH); **anabolic** — protein synthesis, bone growth and closure of epiphyses; libido; raises RBC production; sebaceous secretion (acne) |
| **Oestrogens** (mainly **oestradiol**) | Ovarian follicles (granulosa cells); corpus luteum; placenta | Develop female genitalia at puberty and **secondary sexual characters** (breasts, female fat distribution, broad pelvis); **proliferative phase** of endometrium; LH surge before ovulation; thin watery cervical mucus that lets sperm through; protect bone (inhibit resorption); favourable lipid profile; salt and water retention |
| **Progesterone** | **Corpus luteum**; placenta (from about the third month) | **Secretory phase** of endometrium — prepares it for implantation; maintains pregnancy (relaxes myometrium, keeps cervix closed); thick cervical mucus; develops breast alveoli; raises body temperature slightly after ovulation (about 0.3–0.5 °C) |
| Inhibin | Sertoli cells; granulosa cells | Inhibits FSH |
| Relaxin | Corpus luteum; placenta | Relaxes pelvic ligaments and softens cervix before birth |

**Puberty:** rising GnRH pulses at about 10–14 years in girls (first period = **menarche**, usually about 11–14 years) and a little later in boys. **Menopause:** permanent end of menstruation, usually between about 45 and 55 years, as the follicles run out; oestrogen falls (hot flushes, osteoporosis risk).

## 5.6 Physiology of menstruation (menstrual cycle)

The **menstrual cycle** is the regular monthly series of changes in the ovary and uterus that prepares for pregnancy; average length **28 days** (normally about 21–35). Day 1 is the first day of bleeding.

```text
 DAY:      1       5           14            28
           |-------|-----------|-------------|
 OVARY:    |   FOLLICULAR PHASE |   LUTEAL PHASE   |
           | follicles grow     ^ OVULATION        |
           | (FSH), oestrogen   | (LH SURGE about  | corpus luteum ->
           | rises              |  day 14)         | progesterone + oestrogen
 HORMONES:  oestrogen  /\         progesterone  ____/\____
                      /  \_______             /           \
            LH, FSH        |  (LH peak)
 UTERUS:   |MENSES| PROLIFERATIVE | SECRETORY (glands coil,  |
           |shed  | (endometrium  |  glycogen, thick, spongy) |
           |      |  rebuilds)    |                          |
           no pregnancy -> corpus luteum dies (day 24-26) -> progesterone falls
           -> spiral arteries constrict -> functional layer shed -> MENSTRUATION
```

**1. Menstrual phase (days 1–5)**

- Fall in progesterone and oestrogen at the end of the previous cycle constricts the **spiral arteries**; the **functional layer** of the endometrium becomes ischaemic and is shed with blood (about 30–80 mL, does not clot because of fibrinolysins).
- In the ovary several new follicles start to grow under FSH.

**2. Proliferative (follicular, pre-ovulatory) phase (days 6–14)**

- **FSH** makes follicles grow; one becomes dominant and matures into the **Graafian follicle**.
- Rising **oestrogen** from the follicle rebuilds the endometrium from the basal layer — glands and blood vessels grow; thickness reaches about 3–5 mm. Cervical mucus becomes thin and stretchy.

**3. Ovulation (about day 14)**

- High oestrogen now has a **positive feedback** effect, causing a sudden **LH surge** (with a smaller FSH rise).
- About a day later the Graafian follicle ruptures and releases the **secondary oocyte** into the uterine tube.
- Signs: mid-cycle pain in some women, rise in basal body temperature after ovulation, clear stretchy cervical mucus. Ovulation occurs about **14 days before the next period**, whatever the cycle length.

**4. Secretory (luteal, post-ovulatory) phase (days 15–28)**

- Under LH, the ruptured follicle becomes the **corpus luteum** (yellow body), which secretes **progesterone** and oestrogen.
- The endometrium becomes thick (up to about 5–6 mm), soft and well supplied with blood; glands become coiled and secrete glycogen-rich fluid ("uterine milk") — ready for implantation.
- **If fertilization does not occur:** the corpus luteum degenerates after about 10–12 days into the **corpus albicans** (white scar); progesterone and oestrogen fall; menstruation begins and FSH rises again.
- **If fertilization occurs:** **hCG** from the embryo keeps the corpus luteum alive, progesterone stays high and menstruation does not occur.

**Applied:** combined oral contraceptive pills supply oestrogen and progestin, which suppress FSH and the LH surge (negative feedback) so no ovulation occurs; the withdrawal bleed in the pill-free week mimics menstruation. Common disorders: amenorrhoea, dysmenorrhoea (painful periods, eased by NSAIDs), menorrhagia (heavy bleeding), premenstrual syndrome.

## 5.7 Spermatogenesis

The formation of mature **spermatozoa** from **spermatogonia** in the seminiferous tubules. It begins at **puberty** and continues throughout life; one cycle takes about **64–74 days**. Several hundred million sperm are made each day.

```text
 SPERMATOGONIUM (2n = 46)       stem cell at basement membrane
        |  mitosis (some remain as stem cells)
 PRIMARY SPERMATOCYTE (2n = 46)
        |  MEIOSIS I (reduction division)
 2 SECONDARY SPERMATOCYTES (n = 23)
        |  MEIOSIS II
 4 SPERMATIDS (n = 23)
        |  SPERMIOGENESIS (no division: change of shape)
 4 SPERMATOZOA (n = 23): 2 carry X, 2 carry Y
```

**Phases**

1. **Spermatocytogenesis:** spermatogonia multiply by mitosis; some grow into **primary spermatocytes**.
2. **Meiosis:** each primary spermatocyte (2n) undergoes meiosis I → two **secondary spermatocytes** (n); meiosis II → four **spermatids** (n). Crossing over in meiosis I produces genetic variety.
3. **Spermiogenesis:** spermatids change into sperm — the Golgi apparatus forms the **acrosome**, the nucleus condenses, centrioles form the tail, mitochondria gather in the middle piece and most cytoplasm is shed (taken up by Sertoli cells).
4. **Spermiation:** release of sperm into the lumen; they mature further in the epididymis.

**Structure of a spermatozoon** (about 60 µm long):

```text
  HEAD                 NECK   MIDDLE PIECE            TAIL (principal + end piece)
 ( ACROSOME )
 (  NUCLEUS  )=========||=====[mitochondria spiral]=====~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
  acrosome: enzymes    centrioles   energy (ATP) for     flagellum: movement
  (hyaluronidase,                   swimming
   acrosin) to
   penetrate ovum
```

**Factors:** **FSH** (via Sertoli cells) and **testosterone** (via LH and Leydig cells) are essential; GH, normal scrotal temperature and nutrition also matter. Spermatogenesis is reduced by high temperature (undescended testis — **cryptorchidism**), infections (mumps orchitis), radiation, alcohol, smoking and some drugs (anticancer drugs, anabolic steroids).

## 5.8 Oogenesis

The formation of the female gamete (ovum) in the ovary. Unlike spermatogenesis, it **begins before birth**, pauses for years, and gives **one** ovum (plus polar bodies) from each primary oocyte.

```text
 FETAL LIFE:
 OOGONIUM (2n) --mitosis--> many oogonia --> PRIMARY OOCYTE (2n)
                    starts meiosis I, ARRESTS in PROPHASE I
                    (enclosed in PRIMORDIAL FOLLICLE)
        |   about 1-2 million at birth; about 3-4 lakh at puberty
        |   (the rest degenerate = atresia)
 EACH CYCLE AFTER PUBERTY (one oocyte usually):
 PRIMARY OOCYTE completes MEIOSIS I just before ovulation (LH surge)
        |------> FIRST POLAR BODY (n, small, degenerates)
 SECONDARY OOCYTE (n) -- ovulated; ARRESTS in METAPHASE II
        |  only if a SPERM enters (fertilization):
        |  completes MEIOSIS II
        |------> SECOND POLAR BODY (n)
 OVUM (n) --> nucleus fuses with sperm nucleus --> ZYGOTE (2n)
 If not fertilized, the secondary oocyte degenerates in about 24 hours.
```

**Follicle development:** primordial follicle (oocyte + flat cells) → **primary follicle** (cuboidal granulosa cells; **zona pellucida** forms) → **secondary follicle** (fluid spaces, theca layers) → mature **Graafian (vesicular) follicle** with a large fluid-filled antrum and the oocyte on a mound (cumulus oophorus) → ovulation → corpus luteum → corpus albicans.

Only about **400–500** oocytes are ovulated in a woman's reproductive life.

| Feature | Spermatogenesis | Oogenesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Site | Seminiferous tubules of testis | Ovary |
| Begins | At puberty | Before birth (fetal life) |
| Continuity | Continuous to old age | Cyclic; stops at menopause |
| Products of one primary cell | **4** functional sperm | **1** ovum + 2–3 polar bodies |
| Meiosis | Completed without pause | Arrested twice (prophase I, metaphase II); completed only after fertilization |
| Cytoplasm | Mostly discarded | Mostly kept by the ovum (food for the early embryo) |
| Gamete | Small, motile, many | Large, non-motile, one per cycle |

## 5.9 Fertilization

**Fertilization** is the fusion of a sperm and a secondary oocyte to form a **zygote**. It normally occurs in the **ampulla of the uterine tube**, within about 12–24 hours of ovulation. Sperm survive in the female tract for about 2–3 days (sometimes longer).

**Steps**

1. **Transport of sperm:** of the millions deposited in the vagina, only a few hundred reach the tube, helped by their tails, uterine contractions and thin mid-cycle cervical mucus.
2. **Capacitation:** in the female tract (a few hours), the sperm membrane loses cholesterol and glycoproteins, making the sperm able to fertilize.
3. **Penetration of corona radiata:** hyaluronidase and tail movement push sperm through the layer of follicle cells.
4. **Acrosome reaction:** binding to the **zona pellucida** (ZP3 protein) releases acrosomal enzymes (acrosin) that digest a path through the zona.
5. **Fusion:** the membranes of one sperm and the oocyte fuse; the sperm nucleus enters.
6. **Block to polyspermy:** the **cortical reaction** — granules in the oocyte release enzymes that harden the zona pellucida — prevents other sperm entering.
7. **Completion of meiosis II** by the oocyte (second polar body released).
8. The male and female **pronuclei** fuse — **zygote (2n = 46)**. The sex is set by the sperm: X sperm → XX (female), Y sperm → XY (male).

**After fertilization:** the zygote divides by mitosis (**cleavage**) as it moves down the tube → **morula** (about day 3–4, 16 or more cells) → **blastocyst** (fluid-filled, with an inner cell mass that forms the embryo and an outer **trophoblast** that forms the placenta) → **implantation** into the endometrium beginning about **day 6–7** after fertilization. The trophoblast starts producing **hCG**.

## 5.10 Pregnancy

**Pregnancy (gestation)** lasts about **40 weeks (280 days) from the first day of the last menstrual period**, about 38 weeks from fertilization; it is divided into three **trimesters**. The embryonic period (first 8 weeks) forms all organs — the time when drugs and infections are most likely to cause birth defects (e.g. thalidomide, isotretinoin, some antiepileptics).

**Placenta:** a disc-shaped organ formed from the trophoblast (chorionic villi) and the endometrium, connected to the fetus by the **umbilical cord** (two umbilical arteries and one umbilical vein). Maternal and fetal blood do not normally mix.

| Function of placenta | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Exchange | O2 and nutrients to the fetus; CO2 and wastes to the mother |
| Barrier | Blocks many microbes and large molecules, but lipid-soluble drugs, alcohol, nicotine and some viruses cross |
| Immunity | Passes maternal **IgG** antibodies to the fetus |
| **Endocrine** | **hCG** (peaks around 8–10 weeks; keeps the corpus luteum), **progesterone** and **oestrogens** (take over from the corpus luteum after about 3 months), **human placental lactogen** (hPL; raises maternal glucose for the fetus, breast development), relaxin |

**Maternal changes:** increased blood volume (by about 40–50%), cardiac output and heart rate; physiological anaemia from dilution; increased breathing; weight gain; enlarged breasts; higher GFR; softening of ligaments; increased insulin resistance (gestational diabetes may appear).

**Pregnancy tests:** detect **hCG** in urine or blood by immunological (antibody-based) methods; home urine strips become positive around the time of the missed period.

## 5.11 Parturition

**Parturition** is the process of giving birth at the end of pregnancy (labour).

**Initiation (factors):** rising oestrogen-to-progesterone ratio (oestrogen increases oxytocin receptors and gap junctions in the myometrium), **oxytocin**, **prostaglandins** (from fetal membranes and decidua), fetal cortisol, relaxin (softens cervix), and stretch of the uterus.

**Positive feedback (Ferguson reflex):**

```text
 Baby's head presses on the CERVIX --> stretch receptors
        |  sensory nerves
        v
 HYPOTHALAMUS --> POSTERIOR PITUITARY releases more OXYTOCIN
        |
        v
 STRONGER UTERINE CONTRACTIONS (+ prostaglandins)
        |
        v
 MORE STRETCH OF CERVIX --> more oxytocin ... cycle stops when the baby is born
```

**Stages of labour**

1. **First stage (dilatation):** from the onset of regular contractions to **full cervical dilatation (about 10 cm)**; the longest stage (often about 8–12 hours in a first labour); membranes usually rupture.
2. **Second stage (expulsion):** from full dilatation to **delivery of the baby**; strong contractions helped by voluntary bearing down.
3. **Third stage (placental):** **delivery of the placenta** (afterbirth) within about 15–30 minutes; uterine contraction closes torn vessels to limit bleeding.

**Drug link:** oxytocin and prostaglandins are used to induce labour and to prevent bleeding after delivery; tocolytic drugs (e.g. nifedipine) are used to delay preterm labour.

**Lactation** after birth: fall in oestrogen and progesterone lets **prolactin** start milk production; suckling stimulates prolactin (production) and **oxytocin** (milk ejection — a neuroendocrine reflex). Breastfeeding delays the return of ovulation.

# Part B: Introduction to genetics

## 5.12 Chromosomes

**Chromosomes** are thread-like structures in the nucleus made of **DNA wound around histone proteins** (chromatin); they carry the genes. They are seen clearly during cell division (best at metaphase).

- Human somatic cells have **46 chromosomes (23 pairs) — diploid (2n)**: **22 pairs of autosomes** and **1 pair of sex chromosomes** (**XX in females, XY in males**). Gametes have 23 — **haploid (n)**.
- The two chromosomes of a pair (one from each parent) are **homologous** and carry genes for the same traits at the same positions (**loci**).
- **Karyotype:** the chromosomes of a cell arranged in pairs by size and centromere position — used to diagnose chromosomal disorders.

```text
 STRUCTURE OF A METAPHASE CHROMOSOME
        ||      <- short arm (p)
        ||
   chromatid  chromatid      two identical sister chromatids
        ><      <- CENTROMERE (spindle attaches at the kinetochore)
        ||
        ||      <- long arm (q)
        ||
        ==      <- TELOMERES at the ends (protect the chromosome)
```

**Types by centromere position:** **metacentric** (centre; arms equal), **submetacentric** (slightly off-centre), **acrocentric** (near one end; e.g. chromosomes 13, 14, 15, 21, 22), **telocentric** (at the end; not found in humans).

**Chromosomal disorders (examples)**

| Disorder | Chromosomes | Main features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Down syndrome** | Trisomy 21 (47 chromosomes) | Intellectual disability, flat face, upward-slanting eyes, single palmar crease, heart defects; risk rises with maternal age |
| **Turner syndrome** | 45, XO | Female, short stature, webbed neck, underdeveloped ovaries (infertility) |
| **Klinefelter syndrome** | 47, XXY | Male, tall, small testes, infertility, gynaecomastia |

## 5.13 Genes and DNA

A **gene** is a segment of DNA at a particular locus on a chromosome that codes for a protein (or a functional RNA) — the unit of heredity. Humans have about 20,000–25,000 protein-coding genes.

**Key terms**

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Allele** | Alternative form of a gene at the same locus (e.g. A, B, O for blood group) |
| **Dominant** | Allele expressed even when present in a single copy (written in capitals, e.g. T) |
| **Recessive** | Allele expressed only when two copies are present (e.g. t) |
| **Homozygous / heterozygous** | Two identical alleles (TT, tt) / two different alleles (Tt) |
| **Genotype / phenotype** | Genetic make-up / observable character |
| **Mutation** | A permanent change in the DNA sequence |
| **Carrier** | Heterozygous person with a recessive disease allele who is not affected |

**Structure of DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid)** — the double helix described by Watson and Crick (1953):

```text
   5'                 3'
   S-P                P-S       S = deoxyribose sugar, P = phosphate
   |  A ===== T  |              (sugar-phosphate backbone on the outside)
   S-P                P-S
   |  G ≡≡≡≡≡ C  |              bases paired inside by hydrogen bonds:
   S-P                P-S         A pairs with T (2 hydrogen bonds)
   |  T ===== A  |                G pairs with C (3 hydrogen bonds)
   S-P                P-S
   |  C ≡≡≡≡≡ G  |              two strands run in opposite directions
   3'                 5'          (antiparallel) and twist into a helix
```

- **Nucleotide** = phosphate + deoxyribose + nitrogenous base. Bases: **purines** — adenine (A), guanine (G); **pyrimidines** — cytosine (C), thymine (T).
- **Complementary base pairing:** A–T and G–C (so A = T and G = C in amount — Chargaff's rule).
- **Replication** is **semi-conservative**: each strand acts as a template, so each new DNA molecule has one old and one new strand (DNA polymerase, helicase, primase, ligase). It happens in the S phase before cell division.

| Feature | DNA | RNA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sugar | Deoxyribose | Ribose |
| Bases | A, G, C, **T** | A, G, C, **U** (uracil) |
| Strands | Double | Usually single |
| Location | Nucleus (and mitochondria) | Nucleus and cytoplasm |
| Function | Stores genetic information | Carries out protein synthesis: **mRNA** (carries the code), **tRNA** (brings amino acids), **rRNA** (part of ribosomes) |

## 5.14 Protein synthesis

The flow of genetic information — the **central dogma**: **DNA → (transcription) → mRNA → (translation) → protein**.

**Genetic code:** a sequence of three bases on mRNA (a **codon**) specifies one amino acid. There are **64 codons**: 61 code for the 20 amino acids and 3 are **stop codons** (UAA, UAG, UGA). **AUG** is the **start codon** (methionine). The code is **triplet, non-overlapping, degenerate** (most amino acids have more than one codon), and **nearly universal** (the same in almost all organisms — which is why bacteria can make human insulin).

**Step 1 — Transcription (in the nucleus)**

1. **RNA polymerase** binds the **promoter** region of the gene and unwinds the DNA.
2. Using one DNA strand (template) it builds a complementary **pre-mRNA** strand (A→U, T→A, G→C, C→G).
3. It stops at a termination sequence.
4. **Processing:** non-coding **introns** are removed and coding **exons** joined (**splicing**); a 5′ cap and a poly-A tail are added.
5. Mature **mRNA** leaves the nucleus through nuclear pores.

**Step 2 — Translation (on ribosomes in the cytoplasm or rough ER)**

```text
  mRNA  5'- A U G | G C U | U U C | ... | U A A -3'
            start    Ala     Phe          stop
              ^       ^
    tRNA anticodon UAC carries Met; next tRNA (CGA) carries Ala
              |       |
      RIBOSOME (small + large subunit) moves along the mRNA codon by codon;
      peptidyl transferase joins amino acids by PEPTIDE BONDS
      Met - Ala - Phe - ...  --> growing polypeptide chain
```

1. **Activation:** each amino acid is attached to its own **tRNA** by an enzyme (aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase), using ATP.
2. **Initiation:** the small ribosomal subunit binds the mRNA at **AUG**; the tRNA carrying methionine pairs with it by its **anticodon**; the large subunit joins.
3. **Elongation:** the next tRNA brings the amino acid matching the next codon; a **peptide bond** links the amino acids; the ribosome moves one codon along (translocation). This repeats.
4. **Termination:** a **stop codon** is reached; release factors free the polypeptide and the ribosome separates.
5. **Post-translational modification:** folding, cutting (e.g. proinsulin → insulin), adding sugars or phosphate in the ER and Golgi.

**Drug link:** several antibiotics act on bacterial protein synthesis because bacterial ribosomes (70S) differ from human ones (80S) — tetracyclines and aminoglycosides (30S subunit), macrolides and chloramphenicol (50S subunit). Rifampicin blocks bacterial RNA polymerase.

## 5.15 Genetic pattern of inheritance

**Mendel's laws** (from his work on garden peas):

1. **Law of dominance:** in a heterozygote, one allele (dominant) masks the other (recessive).
2. **Law of segregation:** the two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation, so each gamete carries only one.
3. **Law of independent assortment:** alleles of different genes (on different chromosomes) are inherited independently.

**Monohybrid cross** (Tt × Tt, T = tall dominant, t = dwarf recessive):

```text
              father's gametes
               T         t
 mother's  T   TT        Tt
 gametes   t   Tt        tt
 Genotypes: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt      Phenotypes: 3 tall : 1 dwarf
```

**Patterns of inheritance in humans**

| Pattern | Rules | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Autosomal dominant** | One copy enough; affected person usually has an affected parent; 50% risk to each child of an affected heterozygote; both sexes equally | Huntington's disease, achondroplasia, familial hypercholesterolaemia, polycystic kidney disease (adult type) |
| **Autosomal recessive** | Two copies needed; parents usually unaffected carriers; 25% risk to each child when both parents are carriers; commoner in consanguineous marriages | **Sickle cell anaemia**, **thalassaemia**, cystic fibrosis, phenylketonuria, albinism |
| **X-linked recessive** | Gene on X; mostly **males** affected (one X); carrier mothers pass it to half their sons; no father-to-son transmission | **Haemophilia A and B**, red–green colour blindness, Duchenne muscular dystrophy, G6PD deficiency |
| X-linked dominant | Affected father passes it to all daughters, no sons | Vitamin D–resistant (hypophosphataemic) rickets |
| Y-linked (holandric) | Father to all sons only | Some genes for male fertility |
| **Codominance** | Both alleles fully expressed | **AB blood group** (IA IB) |
| Multiple alleles | More than two alleles in the population | ABO blood group (IA, IB, i) |
| **Polygenic (multifactorial)** | Many genes plus environment | Height, skin colour, intelligence, diabetes, hypertension |
| Mitochondrial | Passed only from the **mother** (mitochondria come from the ovum) | Some rare neuromuscular diseases |

**Example — X-linked recessive (haemophilia):** carrier mother (X^H X^h) × normal father (X^H Y) → daughters: 50% normal, 50% carriers; sons: 50% normal, 50% affected.

**Applied:** genetic counselling, prenatal diagnosis, newborn screening (hypothyroidism, phenylketonuria), and **pharmacogenetics** — inherited differences in drug response (for example slow acetylators of isoniazid, G6PD-deficient people developing haemolysis with primaquine or dapsone).

## Remember

- Testis: seminiferous tubules (Sertoli cells, sperm) and Leydig cells (testosterone); scrotum keeps it 2–3 °C cooler.
- Seminal vesicles give about 60% of semen (fructose); prostate about 25–30% (PSA, citric acid).
- LH → Leydig cells → testosterone; FSH → Sertoli cells → spermatogenesis; inhibin feeds back on FSH.
- Uterine tube: infundibulum, ampulla (fertilization), isthmus; uterus: perimetrium, myometrium, endometrium.
- Oestrogen → proliferative phase; progesterone → secretory phase; LH surge (positive feedback) → ovulation about day 14.
- Corpus luteum → progesterone; without pregnancy → corpus albicans → menstruation; with pregnancy, hCG maintains it.
- Spermatogenesis: 1 primary spermatocyte → 4 sperm; begins at puberty; about 64–74 days.
- Oogenesis: begins in fetal life; arrest in prophase I and metaphase II; 1 ovum + polar bodies.
- Fertilization in the ampulla; capacitation, acrosome reaction, cortical reaction blocks polyspermy; implantation from about day 6–7.
- Pregnancy about 40 weeks from LMP; placenta makes hCG, progesterone, oestrogen, hPL.
- Parturition: oxytocin positive feedback (Ferguson reflex); three stages — dilatation, expulsion, placental.
- 46 chromosomes: 22 pairs autosomes + XX or XY; Down = trisomy 21, Turner = 45 XO, Klinefelter = 47 XXY.
- DNA: A–T (2 H-bonds), G–C (3 H-bonds); RNA has uracil; replication semi-conservative.
- DNA → mRNA (transcription, nucleus) → protein (translation, ribosome); AUG start; UAA, UAG, UGA stop; 64 codons.
- Monohybrid cross 3:1 phenotype, 1:2:1 genotype; haemophilia and colour blindness are X-linked recessive; sickle cell and thalassaemia are autosomal recessive.

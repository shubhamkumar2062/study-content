# Demonstration of visual acuity

**Aim:** To determine the distant and near visual acuity of the subject using the Snellen chart and a near-vision chart.

## Principle

**Visual acuity** is the ability of the eye to see fine detail — to distinguish two points as separate. It depends on a clear cornea and lens, correct focusing and the dense packing of cones at the **fovea**. A normal eye can separate two points that subtend an angle of **1 minute of arc** at the eye. Each letter of the **Snellen chart** is drawn so that it subtends 5 minutes of arc as a whole (each stroke 1 minute) at the distance marked beside its row.

Acuity is written as a fraction: **V = d / D**, where d = distance of the subject from the chart (6 m in India) and D = distance at which a normal eye can read that row. Normal distant acuity is **6/6**.

## Requirements

Snellen's test-type chart (rows marked 60, 36, 24, 18, 12, 9, 6, 5 m), a well-lit room at least 6 m long (or a mirror arrangement for 3 m), an occluder or card, a near-vision chart (Jaeger or N-notation), pinhole disc, measuring tape.

## Procedure

**A. Distant vision**

1. Hang the Snellen chart at eye level in good, even light. Seat the subject exactly **6 m** away.
2. Cover the left eye with the occluder (without pressing on it).
3. Ask the subject to read the letters from the top row downwards with the right eye.
4. Note the smallest row read correctly (allowing at most one error). Record as 6/D.
5. Repeat for the left eye, then both eyes together.
6. If the subject cannot read 6/6, repeat looking through a **pinhole**; improvement means a refractive error that glasses can correct.
7. If the top letter (60) cannot be read at 6 m, move the subject closer (5, 4, 3 m...) and record as, e.g., 3/60. If still not read, test finger counting, hand movements and perception of light.

**B. Near vision**

1. Hold the near-vision chart about 33–35 cm from the eye in good light.
2. Test each eye separately; record the smallest print read (e.g. N6 or J1).

## Observations

| Eye | Distant vision | With pinhole | Near vision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Right | 6/ | 6/ | N |
| Left | 6/ | 6/ | N |
| Both | 6/ | — | N |

**Interpretation**

| Finding | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 6/6 | Normal distant acuity |
| 6/5 | Better than average |
| 6/9 to 6/60 | Reduced acuity; improves with pinhole if refractive error |
| Distant poor, near good | Myopia (corrected with concave lens) |
| Near poor (especially after 40 years) | Hypermetropia or presbyopia (convex lens) |

## Precautions

1. Keep exactly 6 m distance and good, uniform illumination.
2. Test each eye separately; cover the other eye without pressing on it.
3. The subject should not squint, lean forward or memorise the chart.
4. If the subject wears glasses for distance, record acuity with and without them.

## Viva questions

1. **What is normal visual acuity?** 6/6 (20/20 in feet).
2. **What does 6/60 mean?** The subject reads at 6 m what a normal eye reads at 60 m.
3. **Which part of the retina gives the sharpest vision?** The fovea centralis, which has only cones.
4. **Why does a pinhole improve vision in refractive errors?** It lets through only central rays that need little focusing, reducing blur.
5. **Which lens corrects myopia?** A concave (diverging) lens.
6. **What is presbyopia?** Loss of near focusing with age as the lens becomes less elastic.

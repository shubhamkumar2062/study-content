# Recording of body mass index (BMI)

**Aim:** To measure the height and weight of the subject and calculate the body mass index.

The PCI practical list writes "basal mass index"; the standard measurement is the **body mass index (BMI)**, described here.

## Principle

**BMI** (Quetelet index) relates weight to height and is a simple screening measure of underweight, overweight and obesity in adults:

**BMI = weight (kg) ÷ [height (m)]²**, expressed in kg/m².

It does not measure body fat directly and cannot separate muscle from fat, so very muscular people may show a high BMI. Asians, including Indians, have more body fat and higher risk of diabetes and heart disease at a given BMI, so lower cut-offs are recommended for Indian adults.

## Requirements

Calibrated weighing scale (digital or beam), stadiometer or a measuring scale fixed to a wall with a head board, record sheet, calculator. Optionally a non-stretchable tape for waist circumference.

## Procedure

1. **Weight:** check that the scale reads zero. Ask the subject to remove shoes and heavy items and stand still in the centre of the platform. Read to the nearest 0.1 kg.
2. **Height:** subject stands barefoot, heels together, back, buttocks and heels touching the wall or stadiometer, looking straight ahead. Lower the head board to touch the top of the head. Read to the nearest 0.1 cm and convert to metres.
3. Calculate BMI with the formula.
4. Optionally measure **waist circumference** midway between the lowest rib and the iliac crest at the end of a normal expiration.
5. Classify the result using the tables below.

## Observations and calculations

| Measurement | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Weight (kg) | |
| Height (cm) / (m) | |
| Height² (m²) | |
| BMI (kg/m²) | |
| Waist circumference (cm, optional) | |

**Worked example:** weight 68 kg, height 1.70 m → height² = 2.89 m² → BMI = 68 ÷ 2.89 = **23.5 kg/m²** — normal by WHO cut-offs but "overweight" by the Indian cut-offs.

| Category | WHO (international) BMI | Indian consensus (Asian Indians) BMI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Underweight | Below 18.5 | Below 18.0 |
| Normal | 18.5–24.9 | 18.0–22.9 |
| Overweight | 25.0–29.9 | 23.0–24.9 |
| Obese | 30.0 and above | 25.0 and above |

Waist circumference above about 90 cm in Indian men and 80 cm in Indian women suggests abdominal (central) obesity.

## Result

The BMI of the subject is ______ kg/m², which falls in the ______ category.

## Precautions

1. Calibrate the scale and place it on a flat, hard floor.
2. Measure without shoes and with light clothing.
3. Keep the head in a level position for height.
4. Remember BMI is a screening tool, not a diagnosis; it is not used in the same way for children, pregnant women and athletes.

## Viva questions

1. **Write the formula for BMI.** Weight in kg divided by the square of height in metres.
2. **What is a normal BMI by WHO standards?** 18.5–24.9 kg/m².
3. **Why are lower cut-offs used for Indians?** They have more body fat and a higher risk of diabetes and heart disease at the same BMI.
4. **Give one limitation of BMI.** It does not distinguish muscle from fat or show fat distribution.
5. **What does waist circumference indicate?** Abdominal (visceral) fat, a risk factor for diabetes and heart disease.
6. **Name a drug class that commonly causes weight gain.** Corticosteroids (also insulin, some antipsychotics).

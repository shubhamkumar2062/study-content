# Study of the nervous system using models and specimens

**Aim:** To study the gross structure of the brain, spinal cord and nerves with the help of models, charts and specimens.

## Principle

The brain and spinal cord are best understood by identifying their parts on a model or preserved specimen and relating each part to its function (Unit 1). A dissectible brain model shows the lobes, the brainstem, the cerebellum and the ventricles; a spinal cord model shows the grey and white matter and the roots of the spinal nerves.

## Requirements

Dissectible model of the human brain (with midsagittal half), model of the brain ventricles, model or chart of the spinal cord in transverse section and with vertebral column, chart of cranial nerves, chart of the autonomic nervous system; preserved sheep or goat brain if available; gloves, pointer, drawing sheets.

## Procedure

1. Study the **lateral surface** of the brain model: identify lobes and sulci.
2. Separate the two halves and study the **midsagittal surface**.
3. Study the **inferior surface (base)**: brainstem and the origins of cranial nerves.
4. Use the ventricle model to trace the flow of CSF.
5. Study the spinal cord model and a cross-section chart.
6. Draw labelled diagrams and write the function of each part.

## Observations

| View / model | Parts to identify |
| --- | --- |
| Lateral surface | Frontal, parietal, temporal and occipital lobes; central sulcus; lateral sulcus; precentral (motor) and postcentral (sensory) gyri; cerebellum; pons; medulla |
| Midsagittal section | Corpus callosum, lateral ventricle (septum pellucidum), thalamus, hypothalamus, pituitary gland, pineal gland, third ventricle, midbrain with cerebral aqueduct, pons, medulla, fourth ventricle, cerebellum (arbor vitae) |
| Inferior surface | Olfactory bulbs and tracts, optic chiasma, mammillary bodies, cerebral peduncles, pons, pyramids of the medulla, roots of cranial nerves III–XII |
| Ventricles | Two lateral ventricles, foramina of Monro, third ventricle, aqueduct, fourth ventricle |
| Spinal cord | Cervical and lumbar enlargements, conus medullaris, filum terminale, cauda equina; in section: dorsal, lateral and ventral horns, central canal, white columns, dorsal root with ganglion, ventral root, spinal nerve |
| Meninges (chart) | Dura mater, arachnoid mater, subarachnoid space, pia mater |

## Result

The parts of the brain, spinal cord and meninges were identified on models and charts, and their functions recorded.

## Precautions

1. Handle models gently; do not force parts apart.
2. Use gloves for preserved brain specimens (formalin is irritant) and work in a ventilated room.
3. Return each removable part to its correct place.

## Viva questions

1. **What connects the two cerebral hemispheres?** The corpus callosum.
2. **Which gyrus is the primary motor area?** The precentral gyrus (area 4).
3. **Where do the corticospinal fibres cross?** At the pyramids of the medulla.
4. **Where does the spinal cord end in adults?** At the lower border of L1 or upper L2.
5. **What is the arbor vitae?** The tree-like white matter of the cerebellum.
6. **Which part of the brain is the relay station for sensations?** The thalamus.
7. **In which space is CSF found?** The subarachnoid space.

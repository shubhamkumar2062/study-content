# Study of permanent slides of vital organs and gonads

**Aim:** To identify, under the compound microscope, permanent stained slides of vital organs (stomach, small intestine, liver, pancreas, lung, kidney, thyroid, adrenal) and gonads (testis, ovary), and to draw their microscopic structure.

## Principle

Permanent slides are thin sections of tissue fixed, embedded, cut and stained, usually with **haematoxylin and eosin (H and E)**: haematoxylin stains nuclei blue-purple and eosin stains cytoplasm and fibres pink. Each organ shows a typical pattern of cells and structures that allows it to be recognised. Slides are first viewed under low power to see the general plan, then under high power for details.

## Requirements

Compound microscope, permanent slides of the organs listed, lens paper, immersion oil (if oil immersion is used), drawing sheets and pencil.

## Procedure

1. Place the slide on the stage with the coverslip up; focus first with the **low-power (10×)** objective.
2. Scan the whole section to see its general layout.
3. Switch to **high power (40×)** to study cells and special features.
4. Identify the organ using the points in the table.
5. Draw a neat labelled diagram of the low-power view with a high-power inset.

## Observations: identification points

| Slide | Identifying features |
| --- | --- |
| **Stomach (fundus)** | Simple columnar mucous epithelium with **gastric pits**; long straight **gastric glands** filling the mucosa with pink **parietal cells** and bluish **chief cells**; thick muscularis with three layers |
| **Small intestine** | Finger-like **villi** lined by columnar cells with **goblet cells** and brush border; **crypts of Lieberkühn**; central lacteal; **Brunner's glands** in the submucosa (duodenum) or **Peyer's patches** (ileum) |
| **Liver** | Hexagonal **lobules** with a **central vein**; plates of hepatocytes radiating outwards; **sinusoids**; **portal triads** (hepatic artery, portal vein, bile duct) at the corners |
| **Pancreas** | Many serous **acini** with dark basal and pink apical (granular) cytoplasm; scattered pale **islets of Langerhans**; ducts; no gastric pits or villi |
| **Lung** | Many thin-walled **alveoli** giving a lace-like look; bronchioles with smooth muscle and no cartilage; bronchi with cartilage plates; blood vessels |
| **Kidney** | **Cortex** with round **glomeruli** in Bowman's capsules and cut convoluted tubules (PCT with brush border, pinker; DCT paler); **medulla** with straight tubules and collecting ducts |
| **Thyroid** | Round **follicles** of cuboidal cells filled with pink **colloid**; parafollicular (C) cells between follicles; rich capillaries |
| **Adrenal gland** | Capsule; cortex in three zones — **glomerulosa** (clusters), **fasciculata** (long cords of pale, lipid-filled cells), **reticularis** (network); central **medulla** with chromaffin cells and large veins |
| **Testis** | Thick **tunica albuginea**; many cut **seminiferous tubules** lined by germ cells at all stages (spermatogonia at the edge, sperm near the lumen) and **Sertoli cells**; clusters of **Leydig cells** between tubules |
| **Ovary** | Outer **cortex** with follicles at different stages (**primordial**, primary, secondary, **Graafian** with antrum), possibly **corpus luteum** (large pale luteal cells) or corpus albicans; inner vascular **medulla** |

## Result

The permanent slides of the given organs were identified on the basis of their characteristic features and their diagrams were drawn.

## Precautions

1. Always begin with low power; never lower the objective onto the slide while looking through the eyepiece.
2. Clean lenses only with lens paper.
3. Handle slides by the edges and return them to the box.
4. Draw what is seen, not a textbook picture.

## Viva questions

1. **Which stain is commonly used for permanent slides?** Haematoxylin and eosin.
2. **How do you tell the pancreas from a salivary gland?** The pancreas has pale islets of Langerhans between the acini.
3. **What fills the thyroid follicles?** Colloid (thyroglobulin).
4. **Which cells of the testis secrete testosterone?** Leydig (interstitial) cells.
5. **What is a Graafian follicle?** A mature ovarian follicle with a large fluid-filled antrum, ready to ovulate.
6. **Which zone of the adrenal cortex is the thickest?** The zona fasciculata.
7. **How is the liver lobule recognised?** By the central vein with radiating plates of hepatocytes and portal triads at the corners.

# Determination of tidal volume and vital capacity

**Aim:** To determine the tidal volume and vital capacity of the subject using a spirometer, and to calculate minute ventilation.

## Principle

A **spirometer** measures the volume of air breathed in and out. In the common water-sealed (Student's or Benedict–Roth type) spirometer, a light inverted drum floats in water; air breathed into it lifts the drum and air drawn out lowers it, and the movement is read on a scale (or recorded on a drum). Digital spirometers measure air flow electronically and calculate the volumes.

- **Tidal volume (TV):** air breathed in or out in one quiet breath — about 500 mL.
- **Vital capacity (VC):** maximum air breathed out after a maximum inspiration = IRV + TV + ERV — about 4.5–5 L in young adult men and about 3–3.5 L in women.
- **Minute ventilation = TV × respiratory rate.**

## Requirements

Spirometer (water-sealed or digital), disposable mouthpieces, nose clip, spirit swabs, stopwatch, measuring tape and weighing scale (to compare with predicted values).

## Procedure

1. Note the subject's age, sex, height and weight. Explain the procedure; the subject sits or stands upright.
2. Fix a clean disposable mouthpiece and put on the nose clip.
3. **Tidal volume:** ask the subject to breathe quietly through the mouthpiece for a few breaths to get used to it, then record the volume of 5–10 quiet breaths. Divide the total by the number of breaths.
4. Count the respiratory rate for one minute (without the subject being aware, if possible).
5. **Vital capacity:** ask the subject to take the deepest possible breath, place the lips tightly around the mouthpiece and breathe out as completely as possible, steadily. Read the volume.
6. Repeat the VC three times with a rest of a minute between attempts; take the highest value.
7. Optionally measure inspiratory and expiratory reserve volumes.

Respiratory rate = ______ /min.

**Minute ventilation = TV × rate.** **Worked example:** TV 500 mL × 12/min = **6000 mL/min (6 L/min)**.

**Alveolar ventilation = (TV − dead space) × rate** = (500 − 150) × 12 = 4200 mL/min.

**Factors that alter VC:** higher in tall people, men and athletes (swimmers); lower in women, the elderly, in the lying position, obesity, pregnancy, lung fibrosis, pleural effusion, weak respiratory muscles and asthma.

## Precautions

1. Use a new disposable mouthpiece for each subject and disinfect the equipment.
2. Make sure there is no leak around the lips or through the nose.
3. Expiration for VC should be complete and steady, after a truly maximal inspiration.
4. Allow rest between trials; stop if the subject feels dizzy.
5. Read the scale at eye level.

## Viva questions

1. **Define vital capacity.** The maximum volume of air that can be expelled after a maximum inspiration.
2. **Which volume cannot be measured with a simple spirometer?** Residual volume (and so FRC and TLC).
3. **What is the normal tidal volume?** About 500 mL.
4. **Why is VC lower when lying down?** Abdominal organs push the diaphragm up and blood volume in the lungs increases.
5. **What is dead space?** Air in the conducting airways that does not take part in gas exchange (about 150 mL).
6. **What is FEV1?** The volume expired in the first second of a forced expiration; normally about 80% of FVC, reduced in asthma and COPD.

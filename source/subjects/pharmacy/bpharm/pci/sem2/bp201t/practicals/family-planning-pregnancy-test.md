# Study of family planning devices and pregnancy diagnosis test

**Aim:** (A) To study the common family planning (contraceptive) methods and devices. (B) To perform a urine pregnancy test using an hCG test strip or card.

## Principle

**Contraception** prevents pregnancy by stopping sperm from meeting the ovum (barrier methods, sterilisation), preventing ovulation (hormonal methods), or preventing fertilization and implantation in the uterus (intrauterine devices).

**Pregnancy test:** after implantation the trophoblast secretes **human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG)**, which appears in maternal blood and urine. Urine test cards use **immunochromatography**: urine flows along a strip carrying coloured antibody against hCG; if hCG is present, the coloured complex is captured by a second antibody at the **test line (T)**, while a **control line (C)** shows that the strip worked. Most cards become positive around the time of the first missed period; early-morning urine is the most concentrated.

## Requirements

**A.** Specimens, models or charts of: male and female condoms, diaphragm, copper intrauterine device (e.g. Copper T), a hormone-releasing intrauterine system, combined oral contraceptive pill pack, weekly non-steroidal pill (centchroman), emergency contraceptive pill pack, injectable contraceptive, implant (chart), charts of vasectomy and tubectomy.

**B.** Urine pregnancy test card with dropper, clean dry urine container, gloves, timer, sample of urine (a known positive control sample if available).

## Procedure

**A. Family planning devices**

1. Examine each device or pack and note its type, how it is used, how it works and its advantages and disadvantages.
2. Record the observations in the table below.

**B. Urine pregnancy test**

1. Wear gloves. Bring the card and urine to room temperature.
2. Remove the card from its pouch and place it on a flat surface.
3. Add the number of drops of urine stated by the manufacturer (usually 3) to the sample well (S).
4. Start the timer and read the result in the time given in the leaflet (usually about 5 minutes); do not read after the maximum time.

## Observations

**A. Contraceptive methods**

| Method | Examples | How it works | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Barrier** | Male condom, female condom, diaphragm with spermicide | Blocks sperm from entering the uterus | Condoms also protect against sexually transmitted infections, including HIV |
| **Intrauterine devices** | Copper T (copper IUD); levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine system | Copper is toxic to sperm and makes the uterus unfavourable for implantation; hormonal device thickens cervical mucus and thins the endometrium | Long-acting and reversible; inserted by trained staff |
| **Combined oral pills** | Oestrogen + progestin daily (government supply: Mala-N) | Suppress FSH and LH → no ovulation; thick cervical mucus | Very effective if taken regularly; not for women with a history of clots |
| **Non-hormonal weekly pill** | Centchroman (ormeloxifene; Chhaya, earlier Saheli) | Selective oestrogen receptor modulator; prevents implantation | Developed in India; weekly dosing |
| **Emergency contraception** | Levonorgestrel pill | Delays or prevents ovulation | For occasional use after unprotected intercourse, as soon as possible (within 72 hours); not a regular method |
| **Injectable** | DMPA (Antara) | Progestin; prevents ovulation | Given every 3 months |
| **Implant** | Progestin rod under the skin of the arm | Prevents ovulation | Several years; removable |
| **Permanent (sterilisation)** | **Vasectomy** (vas deferens cut and tied) and **tubectomy** (uterine tubes cut, tied or blocked) | Stops sperm or ovum from passing | Permanent; vasectomy is simpler and safer |
| Natural | Calendar (safe period), withdrawal, lactational amenorrhoea | Avoid intercourse in the fertile days | Less reliable |

**B. Pregnancy test**

| Appearance | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Line at C only | **Negative** — hCG not detected |
| Lines at both C and T (even a faint T line) | **Positive** — hCG detected |
| No line at C (with or without T) | **Invalid** — repeat with a new card |

## Result

## Precautions

1. Wear gloves when handling urine; dispose of samples and cards safely.
2. Check the expiry date of the card; do not reuse it.
3. Read the result only within the stated time.
4. A negative result very early in pregnancy may be false; repeat after a few days if periods do not start.
5. Handle contraceptive specimens respectfully and do not open sealed sterile packs.

## Viva questions

1. **Which hormone is detected in a pregnancy test?** Human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG).
2. **What is the role of the control line?** It shows that the test card worked and enough sample was added.
3. **How does Copper T prevent pregnancy?** Copper ions are toxic to sperm and make the endometrium unfavourable for implantation.
4. **How do combined oral pills prevent pregnancy?** By suppressing FSH and the LH surge, so ovulation does not occur.
5. **Which contraceptive also protects against sexually transmitted infections?** The condom.
6. **What is vasectomy?** Cutting and tying of the vas deferens in men — a permanent method.

# Study of the digestive, respiratory, cardiovascular, urinary and reproductive systems with models, charts and specimens

**Aim:** To identify the organs and the important parts of the digestive, respiratory, cardiovascular, urinary and reproductive systems on models, charts and specimens.

## Principle

Each system is first located on a human torso model, then studied in detail on separate organ models and charts. Knowing the position of organs and the parts of each one is the base for understanding their functions (Units 2, 3 and 5 of this subject, and the heart from BP101T) and is a common spotting and viva exercise.

## Requirements

Human torso model with removable organs; models of the stomach, liver, pancreas with duodenum, larynx and lungs, heart (dissectible), kidney (section) and nephron, male and female pelvis (sagittal section); wall charts; preserved specimens if available (e.g. sheep heart, kidney); gloves, pointer, drawing sheets.

## Procedure

1. On the torso model, identify each organ and note its position (abdominal regions, thoracic cavity, pelvis).
2. Remove organs in order (lungs, heart, stomach, intestines) and replace them in reverse order.
3. Study each organ model and identify the parts listed below.
4. For preserved specimens (with gloves), identify chambers, valves or regions as listed.
5. Draw labelled diagrams of the stomach, nephron, heart, lungs and male and female reproductive organs.

## Observations

| System | Organ / model | Parts to identify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Digestive | Torso and charts | Mouth, pharynx, oesophagus, stomach, duodenum, jejunum, ileum, caecum and appendix, ascending, transverse, descending and sigmoid colon, rectum, anal canal |
| | Stomach | Cardia, fundus, body, pyloric antrum, pyloric sphincter, lesser and greater curvatures, rugae |
| | Liver, gallbladder, pancreas | Right and left lobes, falciform ligament, porta hepatis, gallbladder, cystic and common bile ducts; head, body and tail of pancreas, ampulla of Vater |
| Respiratory | Larynx and lungs | Thyroid and cricoid cartilages, epiglottis, trachea with C-shaped rings, carina, main bronchi; right lung 3 lobes (oblique and horizontal fissures), left lung 2 lobes with cardiac notch, hilum, pleura, diaphragm |
| Cardiovascular | Heart | Four chambers, interatrial and interventricular septa, tricuspid, mitral, pulmonary and aortic valves, chordae tendineae, papillary muscles, aorta, pulmonary trunk, venae cavae, pulmonary veins, coronary arteries |
| Urinary | Kidney section, nephron, torso | Capsule, cortex, medulla, pyramids, papillae, calyces, pelvis, ureter; glomerulus, Bowman's capsule, PCT, loop of Henle, DCT, collecting duct; bladder (trigone), urethra |
| Reproductive (male) | Male pelvis | Testis, epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicle, prostate, bulbourethral gland, urethra, penis |
| Reproductive (female) | Female pelvis | Ovary, uterine tube (fimbriae, ampulla, isthmus), uterus (fundus, body, cervix), vagina, relation to bladder (front) and rectum (behind) |

## Result

The organs and their parts of the digestive, respiratory, cardiovascular, urinary and reproductive systems were identified on models, charts and specimens, and labelled diagrams were drawn.

## Precautions

1. Remove and replace torso organs in the correct order without forcing them.
2. Wear gloves for preserved specimens and avoid inhaling formalin fumes.
3. Label diagrams neatly with straight lines.

## Viva questions

1. **Which lung has three lobes?** The right lung.
2. **Which part of the small intestine is the longest?** The ileum.
3. **Where does the common bile duct open?** Into the second part of the duodenum at the ampulla of Vater, with the main pancreatic duct.
4. **What is the trigone of the bladder?** A smooth triangular area between the two ureteric openings and the urethral opening.
5. **Which valve lies between the left atrium and left ventricle?** The mitral (bicuspid) valve.
6. **Where does fertilization usually occur?** In the ampulla of the uterine tube.
7. **Which kidney lies lower and why?** The right kidney, because the liver lies above it.

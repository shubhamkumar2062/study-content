# Demonstration of reflex activity

**Aim:** To elicit and record the common superficial and deep (tendon) reflexes in a human subject.

## Principle

A reflex is an automatic response to a stimulus through a **reflex arc** — receptor, afferent neuron, centre in the spinal cord or brainstem, efferent neuron and effector (Unit 1). **Deep tendon (stretch) reflexes** are monosynaptic: a sharp tap on a tendon stretches the muscle spindles, which excite the α motor neurons of the same muscle, so it contracts. **Superficial reflexes** are polysynaptic responses to stroking the skin or mucosa. Each reflex tests a known spinal segment; exaggerated reflexes suggest an upper motor neuron lesion and absent reflexes a lower motor neuron or sensory lesion.

## Requirements

Knee hammer, cotton wisp, blunt key or the handle of the hammer for the plantar reflex, torch, couch.

## Procedure

1. Explain the test and make the subject sit or lie relaxed; the limb tested should be slightly stretched and loose.
2. Hold the hammer loosely and let it fall with a brisk wrist movement on the tendon.
3. Compare each reflex on both sides.
4. If a reflex is absent, use **reinforcement**: for leg reflexes ask the subject to clench the teeth or hook the fingers and pull (Jendrassik's manoeuvre) at the moment of the tap.

**Deep tendon reflexes**

| Reflex | Method | Response | Segment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Biceps jerk** | Elbow half-bent, examiner's thumb on biceps tendon; tap the thumb | Biceps contracts, elbow flexes | C5–C6 |
| **Supinator (brachioradialis) jerk** | Forearm half-pronated on the lap; tap the lower end of the radius | Elbow flexes | C5–C6 |
| **Triceps jerk** | Arm supported, elbow bent at 90°; tap triceps tendon above the olecranon | Elbow extends | C6–C7 |
| **Knee jerk (patellar)** | Sitting with legs hanging freely (or knees supported when lying); tap patellar tendon below the patella | Quadriceps contracts, leg extends | L2–L4 (mainly L3–L4) |
| **Ankle jerk** | Foot slightly dorsiflexed; tap the Achilles tendon | Plantar flexion of the foot | S1–S2 |

**Superficial reflexes**

| Reflex | Method | Normal response | Segment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Plantar reflex** | Stroke the outer edge of the sole from heel to the little toe, then across to the big toe, with a blunt object | Flexion of toes (flexor plantar) | L5–S1 (S1–S2) |
| Abdominal reflex | Stroke the abdominal skin from outside towards the umbilicus in each quadrant | Umbilicus moves towards the side stroked | T7–T12 |
| Corneal reflex | (Demonstrated only) light touch of the cornea with a cotton wisp from the side | Blinking of both eyes | V (afferent), VII (efferent) |
| Pupillary light reflex | Shine a torch into one eye | Constriction of both pupils (direct and consensual) | II (afferent), III (efferent) |

**Grading of tendon reflexes:** 0 absent; 1+ diminished; 2+ normal; 3+ brisk (more than normal); 4+ very brisk with clonus.

**Babinski sign:** dorsiflexion of the big toe with fanning of the other toes on plantar stimulation — normal in infants under about one year, abnormal (UMN lesion) in adults.

## Precautions

1. The subject must be relaxed; tension can abolish a reflex.
2. Strike the tendon, not the muscle belly, with a brisk, light tap.
3. Always compare both sides with equal force.
4. Use reinforcement before calling a reflex absent.
5. Do not use a sharp object for the plantar reflex.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is the knee jerk called monosynaptic?** The Ia afferent fibre from the muscle spindle synapses directly on the α motor neuron.
2. **What is the receptor for a stretch reflex?** The muscle spindle.
3. **What does an exaggerated knee jerk indicate?** An upper motor neuron lesion (loss of descending inhibition), anxiety or hyperthyroidism.
4. **What is Jendrassik's manoeuvre?** Reinforcement by pulling hooked fingers apart, which increases the excitability of spinal motor neurons.
5. **Name the segment tested by the ankle jerk.** S1–S2.
6. **What is a consensual light reflex?** Constriction of the opposite pupil when light is shone into one eye.

# Demonstration of the function of the olfactory nerve

**Aim:** To test the sense of smell and so the function of the olfactory nerve (cranial nerve I) on each side.

## Principle

Odour molecules dissolve in the mucus of the **olfactory epithelium** in the roof of the nasal cavity and stimulate the bipolar olfactory receptor cells. Their axons pass through the cribriform plate of the ethmoid as the olfactory nerve to the olfactory bulb, then by the olfactory tract to the olfactory cortex in the temporal lobe. Each nostril is tested separately because a one-sided loss can point to a local lesion. Pungent substances such as ammonia are **not** used: they stimulate pain fibres of the trigeminal nerve and give a false "smell" even when the olfactory nerve is damaged.

## Requirements

A set of labelled-underneath smelling bottles with familiar, non-irritant odours (coffee powder, clove oil, peppermint, camphor, rose water, soap, lemon), cotton wool, a blindfold, water for rinsing.

## Procedure

1. Check that both nostrils are clear (ask the subject to breathe through each in turn). Make sure the subject has no cold.
2. Blindfold the subject or ask them to close their eyes.
3. Close the left nostril by pressing it gently. Hold an open bottle about 2–3 cm below the right nostril.
4. Ask the subject to sniff and say whether a smell is present, then name it.
5. Repeat with three or four different odours, in random order, with a gap of 30–60 seconds between them to avoid adaptation.
6. Repeat the test for the left nostril.
7. **Adaptation:** hold one odour under the nostril continuously and note the time after which the subject no longer notices it.

## Precautions

1. Test one nostril at a time and close the other without pressing on the septum.
2. Use familiar, non-irritant substances only.
3. Do not let the subject see the bottle.
4. Allow time between odours and close each bottle after use.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is ammonia not used to test smell?** It stimulates trigeminal pain endings and can be "felt" even if the olfactory nerve is cut.
2. **Through which bone do olfactory nerve fibres pass?** The cribriform plate of the ethmoid.
3. **What is anosmia?** Complete loss of smell.
4. **Name common causes of anosmia.** Common cold and sinusitis, head injury fracturing the cribriform plate, viral infection, ageing, Parkinson's disease.
5. **What is olfactory adaptation?** Fading of a smell sensation during continued exposure to the same odour.
6. **Is smell relayed through the thalamus before reaching the cortex?** No; smell is the only sensation that reaches the cortex without first relaying in the thalamus.

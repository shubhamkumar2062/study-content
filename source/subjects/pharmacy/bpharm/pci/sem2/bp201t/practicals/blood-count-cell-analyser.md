# Demonstration of total blood count by an automated cell analyser

**Aim:** To demonstrate the estimation of a complete blood count (CBC) using an automated haematology cell analyser and to interpret the report.

## Principle

Automated haematology analysers count and size thousands of cells in seconds, replacing the manual haemocytometer counts learnt in BP107P.

- **Electrical impedance (Coulter principle):** blood diluted in a conducting fluid is drawn through a tiny aperture across which a current flows. Each cell, being a poor conductor, briefly raises the resistance, giving a voltage pulse. The **number of pulses = number of cells**, and the **height of each pulse is proportional to cell volume**, so RBCs, WBCs and platelets are counted and sized.
- **Haemoglobin** is measured by photometry after the red cells are lysed (cyanide-free reagents such as sodium lauryl sulphate, or the cyanmethaemoglobin method).
- **WBC differential:** 3-part analysers separate lymphocytes, "mid" cells and granulocytes by size after a lysing reagent; 5-part analysers add **laser light scatter (flow cytometry)** and sometimes staining to separate neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils and basophils.
- From these measurements the analyser **calculates** haematocrit and the red cell indices.

## Requirements

Automated haematology analyser with its diluent, lyse and cleaning reagents; blood collected in an **EDTA (K2 or K3) tube** (purple/lavender cap); quality control blood; gloves, sharps container, printer.

## Procedure

1. Switch on the analyser; let it run its start-up checks and background count.
2. Run the manufacturer's **quality control** sample and confirm it is within range.
3. Collect venous blood into the EDTA tube to the marked level and mix gently by inverting 8–10 times (do not shake). Test within a few hours.
4. Enter the sample identity (patient number, age, sex).
5. Mix the tube again, remove the cap (or use closed-tube mode) and present the tube to the aspiration probe; press start.
6. The analyser aspirates a small volume, dilutes, lyses and counts automatically; read the printed report in about a minute.
7. Note any flags (e.g. abnormal cells, platelet clumps) that need a manual blood smear check.
8. Run the cleaning cycle and switch off as per the manual.

Normal ranges vary slightly between laboratories and analysers; use the ranges printed on the report.

**Red cell indices (calculated):**

- **MCV (fL) = (Hct % × 10) ÷ RBC (millions/µL)**
- **MCH (pg) = (Hb g/dL × 10) ÷ RBC (millions/µL)**
- **MCHC (g/dL) = (Hb ÷ Hct %) × 100**

**Worked example:** Hb 15 g/dL, RBC 5.0 million/µL, Hct 45% → MCV = 450 ÷ 5 = **90 fL**; MCH = 150 ÷ 5 = **30 pg**; MCHC = 15 ÷ 45 × 100 = **33.3 g/dL** — normocytic, normochromic.

**Interpretation:** low MCV and MCH → microcytic hypochromic anaemia (iron deficiency, thalassaemia); high MCV → macrocytic anaemia (vitamin B12 or folate deficiency); high WBC → infection or leukaemia; low platelets → bleeding risk.

## Precautions

1. Treat all blood as potentially infectious: wear gloves, dispose of sharps safely.
2. Use the correct anticoagulant (EDTA), fill to the mark and mix gently; clotted or haemolysed samples give false results.
3. Run quality control before patient samples.
4. Follow the manufacturer's maintenance and cleaning schedule.
5. Confirm flagged results by examining a stained blood smear.

## Viva questions

1. **What is the Coulter principle?** Counting and sizing cells by the change in electrical impedance as each cell passes through a small aperture.
2. **Which anticoagulant is used for a CBC?** EDTA (K2 or K3), because it preserves cell shape.
3. **How is MCV calculated?** Haematocrit (%) × 10 ÷ RBC count (millions/µL).
4. **What does a low MCV suggest?** Microcytic anaemia, commonly iron deficiency.
5. **Give two advantages of automated counting over manual counting.** Speed and precision (thousands of cells counted), plus extra indices such as RDW and MPV.
6. **When must a manual smear be examined?** When the analyser flags abnormal or immature cells, platelet clumps or results outside limits.

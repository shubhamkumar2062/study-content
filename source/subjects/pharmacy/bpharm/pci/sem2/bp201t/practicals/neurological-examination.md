# Demonstration of the general neurological examination

**Aim:** To demonstrate the steps of a general neurological examination of a healthy subject: higher functions, cranial nerves, motor system, sensory system, reflexes and coordination.

## Principle

A neurological examination tests each part of the nervous system in turn. Because each function depends on a known pathway (Unit 1), an abnormal finding tells where the lesion is — for example, weakness with brisk reflexes and an extensor plantar response points to an upper motor neuron lesion. In the laboratory the examination is demonstrated on a healthy classmate.

## Requirements

Knee hammer (reflex hammer), cotton wisp, pin with a blunt end (or neuro-tip), tuning fork (128 Hz), test tubes of warm and cold water, torch, Snellen chart, small familiar objects (coin, key), smelling bottles, couch.

## Procedure

**A. Higher functions**

1. **Consciousness:** note whether the subject is alert; in patients the Glasgow Coma Scale is used (eye 4 + verbal 5 + motor 6 = 15, fully conscious).
2. **Orientation:** to time, place and person.
3. **Memory:** recall three words after 5 minutes; recent and remote events.
4. **Speech:** fluency, understanding, naming objects.

**B. Cranial nerves (brief screen)**

| Nerve | Test |
| --- | --- |
| I Olfactory | Identify familiar smells, each nostril separately |
| II Optic | Visual acuity (Snellen), fields by confrontation, pupillary light reflex |
| III, IV, VI | Eye movements in all directions (follow a finger in an "H"); pupil size |
| V Trigeminal | Facial touch sensation; clench teeth (masseter); corneal reflex (demonstrated only, not done routinely) |
| VII Facial | Raise eyebrows, close eyes tightly, show teeth, puff cheeks |
| VIII Vestibulocochlear | Whispered voice; Rinne and Weber tuning-fork tests |
| IX, X | Say "aah" — palate rises in the midline; swallowing |
| XI Accessory | Shrug shoulders and turn head against resistance |
| XII Hypoglossal | Protrude tongue — it stays in the midline |

**C. Motor system**

1. **Bulk:** look for wasting; compare both sides.
2. **Tone:** passively move the limbs at elbow and knee; feel for stiffness or floppiness.
3. **Power:** test major muscle groups against resistance; grade on the MRC scale.
4. **Involuntary movements:** tremor, twitching.

**D. Sensory system** (eyes closed; compare both sides)

1. Light touch with cotton; pain with the blunt pin.
2. Temperature with warm and cold tubes.
3. Vibration with the tuning fork on bony points.
4. Position sense: move a finger or toe up or down; subject reports direction.
5. Stereognosis: identify a coin or key placed in the hand.

**E. Reflexes** — superficial (plantar, abdominal) and deep tendon (biceps, triceps, supinator, knee, ankle); see the reflex activity practical.

**F. Coordination and gait**

1. **Finger–nose test** and **heel–shin test**.
2. **Rapid alternating movements** (pronation–supination).
3. **Romberg's test:** stand with feet together, first with eyes open, then closed.
4. **Gait:** walk normally and heel-to-toe (tandem).

## Observations

| Test | Normal finding | Subject's finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consciousness, orientation | Alert, oriented (GCS 15) | |
| Cranial nerves I–XII | Intact | |
| Tone, power | Normal tone; power 5/5 in all limbs | |
| Sensations | Touch, pain, temperature, vibration and position sense normal and equal | |
| Reflexes | Present and equal (2+); plantar flexor | |
| Coordination | Finger–nose and heel–shin accurate; Romberg negative | |
| Gait | Normal, steady | |

**MRC power scale:** 0 no contraction; 1 flicker; 2 movement with gravity eliminated; 3 movement against gravity; 4 against some resistance; 5 normal power.

## Result

A general neurological examination was demonstrated on the subject; higher functions, cranial nerves, motor and sensory systems, reflexes and coordination were found ______ (normal / note any abnormality).

## Precautions

1. Explain each step and get consent; keep the subject relaxed and warm.
2. Always compare right and left sides.
3. Use only a blunt pin; never break the skin. Do not touch the cornea in routine class demonstrations.
4. Stand close to the subject during Romberg's test to prevent a fall.

## Viva questions

1. **What does a positive Romberg's test indicate?** Loss of position sense (dorsal column or sensory nerve disease): the subject sways or falls with eyes closed.
2. **What is the normal plantar response in adults?** Flexor (toes curl down); an extensor response (Babinski sign) indicates an UMN lesion.
3. **Which nerve is tested by asking the subject to protrude the tongue?** The hypoglossal nerve (XII).
4. **What is the maximum Glasgow Coma Scale score?** 15.
5. **Which tract carries vibration and position sense?** The dorsal column (fasciculus gracilis and cuneatus).
6. **What does past-pointing in the finger–nose test suggest?** A cerebellar lesion on the same side.

# Recording of body temperature

**Aim:** To record the oral and axillary body temperature of the subject using a clinical thermometer, and to note the effect of exercise.

## Principle

Body temperature is held near **37 °C** by the heat-regulating centre in the **hypothalamus**, which balances heat production (metabolism, muscle activity) and heat loss (radiation, conduction, convection, evaporation of sweat) by negative feedback. Temperature varies with the site of measurement: rectal (core) is about 0.5 °C higher and axillary about 0.5 °C lower than oral. It also shows a daily rhythm (lowest early morning, highest late afternoon), rises slightly after ovulation and with exercise, and rises in fever when the hypothalamic set point is raised.

## Requirements

Digital clinical thermometer (or a clinical glass thermometer, 35–42 °C, with a constriction that holds the reading), spirit swabs, cotton, watch, record sheet. (Mercury thermometers are being replaced by digital ones because mercury is toxic if the glass breaks.)

## Procedure

**A. Oral temperature**

1. The subject should not have eaten, drunk, smoked or exercised for 15–30 minutes.
2. Clean the thermometer with a spirit swab and let it dry. For a glass thermometer, shake the column down below 35 °C.
3. Place the bulb **under the tongue** to one side of the frenulum; ask the subject to close the lips (not the teeth) and breathe through the nose.
4. Leave it for the time given by the maker (digital: until it beeps; glass: about 2–3 minutes).
5. Remove, read at eye level and record. Clean the thermometer again.

**B. Axillary temperature**

1. Dry the armpit with a towel.
2. Place the bulb in the centre of the axilla and hold the arm against the chest.
3. Leave it longer (glass: about 5 minutes; digital: until it beeps). Read and record.

**C. Effect of exercise**

1. Ask the subject to do moderate exercise (e.g. spot running for 3–5 minutes).
2. Record the oral temperature again immediately and after 10 minutes of rest.

**Conversion:** °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32. **Worked example:** 37 °C = (37 × 1.8) + 32 = **98.6 °F**.

**Normal values (approximate):** oral 36.5–37.2 °C (97.7–99 °F); axillary about 0.5 °C lower than oral; rectal about 0.5 °C higher. Fever (pyrexia) is generally taken as an oral temperature above about 37.5 °C; hyperpyrexia above about 41 °C; hypothermia below 35 °C.

## Precautions

1. Disinfect the thermometer before and after use.
2. Avoid hot or cold drinks, smoking and exercise before taking oral temperature.
3. Do not use the oral route in unconscious patients, small children or after mouth surgery.
4. Keep the thermometer in place for the full time.
5. Handle glass thermometers carefully; if one breaks, report it and do not touch the mercury.

## Viva questions

1. **Where is the temperature-regulating centre?** In the hypothalamus.
2. **What is the normal oral temperature?** About 37 °C (98.6 °F).
3. **Why is axillary temperature lower than oral?** The axilla is a skin site, partly exposed to the air, not a closed cavity near core blood.
4. **What is the purpose of the constriction in a clinical glass thermometer?** It stops the mercury falling back, so the maximum reading is held.
5. **How do antipyretics such as paracetamol reduce fever?** They inhibit prostaglandin synthesis in the hypothalamus and reset the raised set point.
6. **Name the ways the body loses heat.** Radiation, conduction, convection and evaporation of sweat.

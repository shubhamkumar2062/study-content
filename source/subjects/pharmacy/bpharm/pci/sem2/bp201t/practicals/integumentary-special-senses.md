# Study of the integumentary system and special senses using models and specimens

**Aim:** To study the structure of the skin and of the organs of special sense (eye, ear, nose and tongue) with the help of models, charts and specimens.

## Principle

Models and charts show in three dimensions what a textbook shows in one plane. By identifying each part on the model, drawing it and stating its function, the student links structure with function. The skin is the largest organ and part of the integumentary system; the eye, ear, nose and tongue carry receptors for sight, hearing and balance, smell and taste (theory in BP101T).

## Requirements

Models of a skin section, eyeball (dissectible), ear (external, middle and inner), nasal cavity and tongue; wall charts; preserved specimens if available (e.g. sheep or goat eye); pointer, drawing sheets, pencil.

## Procedure

1. Place each model on the table in its anatomical position.
2. Identify the parts listed below on the model, then on the chart.
3. For a dissectible model, separate the layers carefully and replace them in order.
4. Draw a neat labelled diagram of each organ in the record book and write two or three functions.
5. If a preserved eye is provided, examine it with gloves: cut it into front and back halves and identify the cornea, lens, vitreous body and retina.

## Observations

| Organ | Parts to identify | Functions to note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Skin (section)** | Epidermis (stratum basale, spinosum, granulosum, lucidum in thick skin, corneum), dermis (papillary and reticular layers), hypodermis; hair follicle with arrector pili muscle, sebaceous gland, sweat gland and duct, Meissner's corpuscle, Pacinian corpuscle, blood vessels | Protection, temperature regulation, sensation, vitamin D synthesis, excretion of sweat |
| **Eyeball** | Sclera, cornea, choroid, ciliary body, iris, pupil, lens with suspensory ligaments, aqueous humour, vitreous body, retina, macula lutea (fovea), optic disc (blind spot), optic nerve; extrinsic eye muscles | Focusing light on the retina; rods for dim light, cones for colour |
| **Ear** | External: auricle, external acoustic meatus, tympanic membrane. Middle: malleus, incus, stapes, Eustachian (auditory) tube. Inner: cochlea (organ of Corti), vestibule, three semicircular canals | Hearing; balance (vestibular apparatus) |
| **Nose** | External nose, nasal septum, conchae (turbinates), meatuses, olfactory epithelium in the roof, paranasal sinus openings | Filtering, warming and moistening air; smell |
| **Tongue** | Tip, body, root, sulcus terminalis; papillae — circumvallate (in a V in front of the sulcus), fungiform, filiform (no taste buds), foliate | Taste, chewing, swallowing, speech |

## Result

The structure of the skin, eye, ear, nose and tongue was studied on models and charts, and labelled diagrams were drawn.

## Precautions

1. Handle dissectible models gently and reassemble them in the correct order.
2. Wear gloves for preserved specimens and wash hands afterwards.
3. Draw diagrams in pencil with straight label lines; do not copy colours.

## Viva questions

1. **Which layer of the epidermis divides to form new cells?** Stratum basale (germinativum).
2. **Which receptor in the skin senses deep pressure and vibration?** Pacinian corpuscle.
3. **What is the blind spot?** The optic disc, where the optic nerve leaves; it has no rods or cones.
4. **Name the smallest bone of the body.** Stapes, in the middle ear.
5. **Which part of the inner ear detects rotation of the head?** The semicircular canals.
6. **Which papillae of the tongue have no taste buds?** Filiform papillae.
7. **Why does a cold block the sense of smell?** Swollen mucosa and mucus stop odour molecules reaching the olfactory epithelium in the roof of the nose.

# Study of the endocrine system using models and specimens

**Aim:** To study the location, shape and parts of the endocrine glands with the help of models, charts and specimens.

## Principle

Endocrine glands are ductless and release hormones into the blood (Unit 4). Each gland has a fixed position in the body and a typical shape that can be recognised on a torso model and on charts. Knowing their location explains clinical facts such as the visual field loss of pituitary tumours or the risk to the parathyroids during thyroid surgery.

## Requirements

Human torso model, models of the brain (for pituitary and pineal), thyroid with larynx and trachea, kidney with adrenal gland, pancreas with duodenum; charts of the endocrine system and of the hypothalamic–pituitary axis; pointer, drawing sheets.

## Procedure

1. On the torso model, locate each endocrine gland and note its relation to nearby organs.
2. Study each gland model separately and identify its parts.
3. Draw a chart showing the gland, its hormones and their main actions.
4. Draw labelled diagrams of the pituitary, thyroid and adrenal gland.

## Observations

| Gland | Location and relations | Parts | Hormones |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pituitary | In the sella turcica of the sphenoid, below the hypothalamus; optic chiasma above | Anterior lobe, posterior lobe, stalk (infundibulum) | GH, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, prolactin; ADH and oxytocin (posterior) |
| Pineal | Epithalamus, behind the third ventricle | — | Melatonin |
| Thyroid | Front of the neck, below the larynx, around the trachea | Two lobes, isthmus (2nd–4th tracheal rings) | T3, T4, calcitonin |
| Parathyroids | Back of the thyroid lobes (usually 4) | — | PTH |
| Thymus | Upper anterior mediastinum, behind the sternum | Two lobes | Thymosin |
| Adrenals | On top of each kidney (right pyramidal, left crescentic) | Cortex (three zones), medulla | Aldosterone, cortisol, androgens; adrenaline, noradrenaline |
| Pancreas | Behind the stomach; head in the C of the duodenum, tail at the spleen | Head, neck, body, tail; islets scattered inside | Insulin, glucagon, somatostatin |
| Gonads | Testes in scrotum; ovaries beside the uterus | — | Testosterone; oestrogen, progesterone |

## Result

The endocrine glands were located on the torso model, their parts identified and the hormones they secrete recorded.

## Precautions

1. Keep the torso model upright and replace removable organs correctly.
2. Note relations as well as position — they are commonly asked in viva.

## Viva questions

1. **Which gland is called the master gland?** The anterior pituitary, because it controls several other glands (though it is itself controlled by the hypothalamus).
2. **Why can a pituitary tumour affect vision?** It presses on the optic chiasma above it.
3. **Where are the parathyroid glands?** On the back of the thyroid lobes, usually two on each side.
4. **Name the three zones of the adrenal cortex.** Glomerulosa, fasciculata, reticularis.
5. **Which gland shrinks after puberty?** The thymus.
6. **Which part of the pancreas lies in the curve of the duodenum?** The head.

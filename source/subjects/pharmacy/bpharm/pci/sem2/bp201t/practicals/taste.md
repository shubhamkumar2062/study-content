# Examination of the different types of taste

**Aim:** To examine the primary tastes — sweet, salty, sour and bitter (and umami) — on different areas of the tongue.

## Principle

Taste buds on the fungiform, foliate and circumvallate papillae contain receptor cells that respond to chemicals dissolved in saliva. Sweet, bitter and umami act on G-protein receptors; salty (Na+) and sour (H+) act through ion channels. Impulses travel in the **facial nerve (VII, chorda tympani)** from the anterior two-thirds of the tongue and the **glossopharyngeal nerve (IX)** from the posterior third, to the medulla, thalamus and the gustatory cortex. The old "tongue map" with one taste per zone is not correct: all tastes can be sensed over all areas with taste buds, though the threshold differs a little between regions — which this practical shows.

## Requirements

Dilute solutions in labelled dropper bottles, as prepared by the laboratory: sweet — sucrose; salty — sodium chloride; sour — citric acid or vinegar; bitter — a very dilute quinine solution or another approved bitter; optionally umami — monosodium glutamate. Cotton-tipped applicators, a glass of water, paper napkins, a tongue diagram.

## Procedure

1. Ask the subject to rinse the mouth with water and keep the tongue protruded throughout (so the solution does not spread).
2. Dry the tongue gently with a napkin.
3. Dip a fresh applicator in the sweet solution and touch it on the tip of the tongue. Ask the subject to point to the written word (sweet, salty, sour, bitter, none) without speaking.
4. Repeat on the sides (front and back) and the back of the tongue, rinsing between tests.
5. Repeat with salty, sour and finally bitter solutions (bitter last because its taste lingers).
6. Test each side of the tongue separately to compare right and left.
7. Mark the results on a tongue diagram.

**Terms:** **ageusia** — loss of taste; **hypogeusia** — reduced taste; **dysgeusia** — distorted taste (e.g. metallic taste with metronidazole).

## Precautions

1. Use fresh, clean applicators for each solution and each area.
2. Keep the tongue protruded and rinse between tests.
3. Use only very dilute, safe solutions; do not let the subject swallow them.
4. Test bitter last.

## Viva questions

1. **Which nerves carry taste?** Facial (VII) from the anterior two-thirds, glossopharyngeal (IX) from the posterior third, vagus (X) from the epiglottis region.
2. **Which papillae carry the most taste buds?** Circumvallate papillae.
3. **Why must a substance be dissolved to be tasted?** Taste receptors respond only to chemicals in solution.
4. **What is umami?** The savoury taste of glutamate.
5. **Why does food taste bland during a cold?** Much of flavour depends on smell, which is reduced.
6. **Name a drug that alters taste.** Metronidazole (metallic taste); also captopril, lithium.

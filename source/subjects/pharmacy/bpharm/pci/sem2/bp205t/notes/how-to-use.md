# How to use this guide

Computer Applications in Pharmacy is a short theory subject (30 hours, 2 hours a week) with one numerical area — number systems — and a lot of definitions, lists and diagrams. Study one unit at a time: read the notes, practise every number conversion and binary sum until you can do it without looking, draw the DFD symbols and the SDLC from memory, then answer the question bank. Each unit has very short (2-mark), short answer (5-mark) and long essay (10-mark) questions, rated by how often that kind of question appears in PCI-syllabus papers.

**Units.** The five units follow the five PCI units exactly; no unit was split or joined. Unit 1 is the largest in practice because it carries both number systems and the concept of information systems, so its notes are longer.

## Exam pattern

BP205T is marked with an asterisk in the PCI B.Pharm regulations: it is a **non-university examination**, set and conducted by the subject experts of the college. In the scheme of examination for semester II it carries:

| Part | Marks | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continuous mode | 10 | — |
| Sessional exams | 15 | 1 hour each |
| **End-semester exam** | **50** | **2 hours** |
| Total | 75 | |

Sessional papers for non-university subjects are set for 30 marks (long answers 1 of 2 × 10, short answers 4 of 6 × 5) and converted to 15 marks.

**End-semester paper (50 marks, 2 hours).** The PCI regulations print this pattern for a 50-mark theory paper, and the full mock paper in this app follows it. There is **no MCQ section**; the MCQs here are still useful for revising definitions, full forms and number conversions, and for sessional tests.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Long answers — answer 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| II | Short answers — answer 6 of 8 | 6 × 5 = 30 |
| | **Total** | **50** |

Because the college sets this paper, the exact split of internal marks and the question format can differ. **Confirm the pattern with your college.**

**Practical (BP210P).** Also a non-university examination. The PCI scheme gives it 15 marks at the end of the semester (2 hours) plus 10 internal marks (5 continuous + 5 sessional), 25 in all. The guides in this pack follow the twelve items of the PCI practical list, one guide each; they are written for a recent version of Microsoft Office, so menu names may differ slightly in older versions.

## Probability ratings

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with a worked example, table or diagram.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful as a backup choice and for viva and MCQs.

These ratings are estimates from common patterns and syllabus weight, not a promise of what will be asked.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. Number systems are sure marks. Show every division or multiplication step, read remainders in the right direction, and **check the answer by converting back to decimal** — write the check in the answer sheet.
2. For complement subtraction, state the method first (take the complement, add, check the carry), then show the working in columns, and finish with the decimal check.
3. Draw diagrams wherever possible: the DFD symbols table and a small context diagram, the SDLC flow, the client–server model, the CDS workflow, the LIMS sample life cycle, and the reverse vaccinology flow.
4. Use comparison tables: HTML vs XML, compiler vs interpreter, MS Access vs MySQL, primary vs secondary databases, CDS vs LIMS.
5. Write short code examples: a five-line HTML page, a small XML record, one CSS rule, one SQL SELECT statement. They show you understand, and examiners like them.
6. Give full forms the first time (DBMS, SQL, CPOE, BCMA, LIMS, CDS, TIMS, BLAST, PDB, QSAR).
7. Link each application to a pharmacy example: a stock-alert report, an interaction warning, a stability study in a LIMS, an influenza vaccine choice. This is what makes it a pharmacy answer.
8. A 10-mark answer needs a definition, six to eight headed points, a diagram or table and a short conclusion on advantages; a 5-mark answer needs a definition and about five clear points.

# Drug information and adverse effects from online tools

**Aim:** To retrieve information about a drug and its adverse effects using online tools (example: metformin).

## Principle

Drug information is available in online databases and official sites. **Tertiary sources** (formularies, drug databases, official prescribing information) give summarised, ready-to-use information; **secondary sources** (PubMed) index the literature; **primary sources** are original articles. Reliable searching uses the generic name, chooses trustworthy sources (government, pharmacopoeial, professional and peer-reviewed sites) and records the source and date of each fact. Adverse effects are also recorded by pharmacovigilance programmes such as the Pharmacovigilance Programme of India (PvPI).

## Requirements

Computer with Internet access and a browser; reliable sources such as PubMed (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), DailyMed or other official prescribing information, the National Formulary of India, DrugBank, PubChem, the CDSCO website; a word processor to record findings.

## Procedure

1. Choose a drug and write its generic name (e.g. metformin hydrochloride).
2. Open a drug database (e.g. DrugBank) and search the generic name. Note: class, mechanism of action, indications, usual dosage forms, pharmacokinetics.
3. Open the official prescribing information (e.g. a DailyMed label) and note: contraindications, warnings, adverse reactions, interactions, use in pregnancy, storage.
4. Search PubMed with Boolean terms, e.g. `metformin AND adverse effects` and then `metformin AND lactic acidosis`. Apply filters (review, last 5 years). Note the number of results and two relevant titles.
5. Check PubChem for the chemical structure, molecular formula and molecular weight.
6. Check the CDSCO / PvPI websites for any safety alert about the drug.
7. Record all findings in a table in a Word document with the source and date of access for each.
8. Compare the information from different sources and note any differences.

## Precautions

1. Use the generic name and reliable, official or peer-reviewed sources; avoid unverified forums and advertisements.
2. Note the source and the date of access for each fact.
3. Cross-check important facts in at least two sources.
4. Prefer recent information; drug labels and alerts are updated.

## Viva questions

1. **Name two online drug databases.** DrugBank and PubChem (also Micromedex, Lexicomp, National Formulary of India).
2. **What is PubMed?** A free search engine for biomedical literature, mainly the MEDLINE database, from the US National Library of Medicine.
3. **What does the AND operator do?** It narrows the search to records containing both terms.
4. **What is an adverse drug reaction?** A harmful, unintended response to a drug at normal doses.
5. **Where are ADRs reported in India?** To the Pharmacovigilance Programme of India (PvPI) through ADR monitoring centres.
6. **Primary vs tertiary source?** Primary = original research article; tertiary = summarised reference such as a formulary or textbook.

# Unit 3: Environmental pollution — air, water and soil

Pollution is the price of careless growth. Factories, vehicles, farms, hospitals and homes release waste that harms living things. Pharmacy is part of this story: drug factories produce effluent and solvent vapour, hospitals produce biomedical waste, and unused medicines reach drains and dumps. This unit covers the three kinds of pollution in the syllabus — air, water and soil — with their sources, effects and control.

## 3.1 Pollution and pollutants

**Pollution** is an undesirable change in the physical, chemical or biological characteristics of air, water or soil that harms (or may harm) human health, other living organisms, property or the quality of life.

A **pollutant** is any substance (or energy, such as heat or noise) that causes pollution when present above a harmful level.

### Classification of pollutants

| Basis | Types | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How they form | **Primary** — emitted directly from a source | CO, SO2, NO, particulate matter |
| | **Secondary** — formed in the air by reactions of primary pollutants | Ozone and PAN in photochemical smog, sulphuric acid in acid rain |
| Breakdown in nature | **Biodegradable** — broken down by microbes | Sewage, food waste, paper |
| | **Non-biodegradable** — persist for a long time | Plastics, DDT, mercury, lead, glass |
| Existence in nature | **Quantitative** — natural substances that become pollutants in excess | CO2, nitrogen and phosphorus compounds |
| | **Qualitative** — man-made, not normally in nature | Pesticides, CFCs, many synthetic drugs |
| Form | Gaseous, liquid, solid, energy (heat, noise, radiation) | |

### Sources

- **Natural:** volcanoes, forest fires, dust storms, pollen, decay of organic matter.
- **Man-made (anthropogenic):** industries, vehicles, power plants, agriculture, mining, domestic waste and sewage, hospitals and pharmaceutical units.
- **Point sources:** a single identifiable outlet (a factory chimney, an effluent pipe).
- **Non-point sources:** spread over a wide area (farm runoff, city streets).

## 3.2 Air pollution

**Air pollution** is the presence in the atmosphere of one or more contaminants in quantities and for durations that harm humans, animals, plants or property. Clean dry air is about 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen and small amounts of argon, carbon dioxide and other gases.

### Sources of air pollution

| Source | Pollutants released |
| --- | --- |
| Vehicles | CO, NOx, hydrocarbons, particulate matter; lead where leaded petrol is still used |
| Thermal power plants (coal) | SO2, NOx, fly ash, CO2 |
| Industries (chemical, cement, steel, fertiliser, pharmaceutical) | SO2, particulates, solvent vapours (volatile organic compounds), specific toxic gases |
| Domestic burning | Smoke and CO from wood, cow-dung cakes and coal; a major cause of indoor air pollution |
| Agriculture | Burning of crop residue (stubble), pesticide sprays, ammonia |
| Waste burning and landfills | Smoke, dioxins, methane |
| Natural | Volcanoes, dust storms, forest fires, pollen |

### Major air pollutants and their effects

| Pollutant | Main source | Effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Carbon monoxide (CO)** | Incomplete burning of fuels; vehicle exhaust | Binds haemoglobin far more strongly than oxygen to form carboxyhaemoglobin; reduces oxygen delivery → headache, dizziness, death at high levels |
| **Sulphur dioxide (SO2)** | Burning coal and oil; smelting | Irritates eyes and airways, worsens asthma and bronchitis; forms acid rain; damages plants (chlorosis) |
| **Oxides of nitrogen (NOx)** | Vehicles, power plants | Respiratory irritation; acid rain; with hydrocarbons forms photochemical smog |
| **Particulate matter (PM10, PM2.5)** | Dust, smoke, vehicles, construction, stubble burning | Fine particles (PM2.5) go deep into the lungs; asthma, bronchitis, heart disease; reduce visibility |
| **Hydrocarbons / VOCs** | Vehicles, solvents, paints, chemical and pharmaceutical units | Some (benzene) are carcinogenic; help form smog |
| **Ground-level ozone** | Secondary pollutant in sunlight | Chest pain, coughing, eye irritation; damages crops |
| **Lead** | Old leaded petrol, smelters, batteries | Nervous system and kidney damage; affects children's mental development |
| **Carbon dioxide (CO2)** | Burning of fossil fuels, deforestation | Not directly toxic at normal levels, but the main greenhouse gas |
| **Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)** | Old refrigerants, aerosol propellants, foams | Destroy stratospheric ozone; also greenhouse gases |

**Effects on materials:** acids corrode metals and erode marble and limestone (damage to monuments such as the Taj Mahal from SO2 is a well-known concern); soot blackens buildings; ozone cracks rubber.

### Smog

**Smog** = smoke + fog.

| Feature | Classical (London / sulphurous) smog | Photochemical (Los Angeles) smog |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conditions | Cold, humid, foggy winter | Warm, sunny, dry weather |
| Main pollutants | Smoke, SO2 from coal | NOx and hydrocarbons from vehicles |
| Nature | Reducing | Oxidising |
| Products | Sulphuric acid droplets, soot | Ozone, PAN (peroxyacetyl nitrate), aldehydes |
| Effects | Bronchitis, deaths in the elderly | Eye irritation, respiratory problems, plant damage |

### Acid rain

Rain with a pH lower than that of normal rain (normal rain is slightly acidic, about pH 5.6, from dissolved CO2).

```text
SO2 + O2 + H2O  →  H2SO4  (sulphuric acid)
NOx + O2 + H2O  →  HNO3   (nitric acid)
These dissolve in rain, snow or fog → acid rain
```

**Effects:** acidifies lakes and kills fish; leaches nutrients from soil and damages forests; corrodes buildings, statues and metals; releases toxic metals (aluminium) from soil into water.

### Greenhouse effect and global warming

Sunlight passes through the atmosphere and warms the earth; the earth radiates heat (infrared) back. **Greenhouse gases** — CO2, methane, nitrous oxide, CFCs and water vapour — absorb part of this heat and keep the earth warm enough for life. This is the natural greenhouse effect. Extra greenhouse gases from human activity trap more heat, causing **global warming**.

**Effects:** rising average temperature, melting glaciers and polar ice, rising sea level, more extreme weather (heat waves, floods, droughts), spread of vector-borne diseases, changes in crop yield. International action: the Kyoto Protocol (1997) and the Paris Agreement (2015).

### Ozone layer depletion

The **ozone layer** in the stratosphere absorbs most of the sun's harmful ultraviolet (UV-B) radiation. CFCs and some other halogen compounds release chlorine and bromine atoms in the stratosphere, which destroy ozone in a chain reaction.

```text
CFCl3 + UV  →  Cl· + ·CFCl2
Cl· + O3    →  ClO· + O2
ClO· + O    →  Cl· + O2      (Cl· is freed again, so one atom destroys many O3 molecules)
```

A seasonal "ozone hole" forms over Antarctica. **Effects of more UV-B:** skin cancer, cataract, weakened immunity, damage to crops and plankton. **Control:** the **Montreal Protocol (1987)** phased out CFCs; pharmaceutical metered-dose inhalers were moved from CFC propellants to HFA propellants as part of this phase-out.

### Indoor air pollution

Smoke from burning wood, dung and coal in poorly ventilated kitchens, tobacco smoke, radon, formaldehyde from furnishings, and solvent fumes. Women and children in rural homes are most exposed.

### Control of air pollution

**1. Prevention at source**

- Cleaner fuels (CNG, LPG, low-sulphur diesel), unleaded petrol.
- Efficient burning; regular vehicle maintenance; emission norms (Bharat Stage standards for vehicles).
- Changing processes; closed systems and solvent recovery in chemical and pharmaceutical units.
- Tall chimneys to disperse (but not remove) pollutants.
- Planting trees (green belts) around industries.

**2. Control equipment**

| For particles | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| Gravity settling chamber | Gas slows down in a large chamber; heavy particles settle |
| Cyclone separator | Gas spins; centrifugal force throws particles to the wall, they fall to the bottom |
| Fabric (bag) filter | Gas passes through cloth bags that trap fine dust |
| **Electrostatic precipitator (ESP)** | Particles are given an electric charge and collected on oppositely charged plates; removes very fine particles efficiently (used in thermal power plants) |
| Wet scrubber | Gas is sprayed with water, which washes out particles and soluble gases |

| For gases | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| Absorption (scrubbing) | Gas passed through a liquid that dissolves or reacts with it (SO2 in lime slurry) |
| Adsorption | Gas held on the surface of a solid such as activated carbon (solvent vapours) |
| Combustion / incineration | Burns organic vapours to CO2 and water |
| **Catalytic converter** (vehicles) | Converts CO, hydrocarbons and NOx into CO2, water and N2 |

**3. Law:** the **Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981** and the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**, enforced by the Central and State Pollution Control Boards.

**Case:** the **Bhopal gas tragedy** (December 1984), when methyl isocyanate leaked from a pesticide plant and killed thousands, shows the danger of industrial air pollution and the need for safety in chemical and pharmaceutical plants.

## 3.3 Water pollution

**Water pollution** is any change in the physical, chemical or biological quality of water that makes it harmful to living things or unfit for its intended use (drinking, bathing, irrigation, industry).

### Sources

| Source | Pollutants |
| --- | --- |
| **Domestic sewage** | Organic matter, pathogens, detergents (phosphates) |
| **Industrial effluents** (including pharmaceutical, tanneries, textiles, paper, chemicals) | Heavy metals, acids, alkalis, dyes, solvents, drug residues, oils, heat |
| **Agricultural runoff** | Fertilisers (nitrates, phosphates), pesticides, animal waste |
| Hospitals | Pathogens, disinfectants, drug residues |
| Thermal and nuclear power plants | Hot water (thermal pollution), radioactive substances |
| Oil spills | Crude oil from tankers and offshore wells |
| Mining | Acid mine drainage, metals, sediment |
| Solid waste dumps | Leachate seeping into groundwater |

### Types of water pollutants and their effects

| Type | Examples | Effects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pathogens** | Bacteria, viruses, protozoa, worm eggs from sewage | Water-borne diseases: cholera, typhoid, dysentery, hepatitis A, diarrhoea |
| **Oxygen-demanding (organic) wastes** | Sewage, food and paper industry waste | Bacteria use up dissolved oxygen while decomposing them; fish die; water smells |
| **Plant nutrients** | Nitrates, phosphates | **Eutrophication**; excess nitrate in drinking water causes methaemoglobinaemia ("blue baby" syndrome) in infants |
| **Heavy metals** | Mercury, lead, cadmium, arsenic, chromium | Accumulate in the body; nerve, kidney and bone damage; some are carcinogens |
| **Pesticides and persistent organics** | DDT, other organochlorines | Biomagnification; harm birds, fish and people |
| **Pharmaceuticals** | Antibiotics, hormones, other drug residues | Antibiotic resistance in microbes; effects on fish (hormonal changes) |
| **Oil** | Spills | Coats birds and fish; blocks oxygen exchange at the surface |
| **Heat (thermal pollution)** | Cooling water from power plants | Lowers dissolved oxygen; harms aquatic life |
| **Sediments** | Soil from erosion | Muddy water blocks light; fills reservoirs |
| **Fluoride** | Natural in groundwater of some regions | Excess causes dental and skeletal fluorosis |

### Well-known heavy-metal diseases

| Disease | Cause | Main effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Minamata disease** (Japan) | Methyl mercury in fish from factory waste | Nerve damage, paralysis, deaths, birth defects |
| **Itai-itai disease** (Japan) | Cadmium in rice irrigated with mining-polluted water | Painful brittle bones, kidney damage |
| **Arsenicosis** | Arsenic in groundwater (West Bengal, Bangladesh) | Skin lesions, cancers |
| Blue baby syndrome | Nitrate in drinking water | Infants cannot carry enough oxygen in blood |

### Eutrophication

```text
Nitrate + phosphate from fertilisers and sewage enter a lake
        ↓
Rapid growth of algae (algal bloom); water turns green
        ↓
Algae die → decomposed by bacteria → dissolved oxygen used up
        ↓
Fish and other animals die; bad smell; lake slowly fills and "ages"
```

### Measuring water pollution

| Parameter | Meaning | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dissolved oxygen (DO)** | Oxygen dissolved in water | High DO = healthy water; low DO = heavy organic pollution |
| **Biochemical oxygen demand (BOD)** | Oxygen used by microbes to break down organic matter in a sample, usually over 5 days at 20 °C | Higher BOD = more biodegradable organic pollution |
| **Chemical oxygen demand (COD)** | Oxygen needed to oxidise all organic matter chemically (by potassium dichromate in acid) | Always equal to or more than BOD; includes matter microbes cannot break down |
| pH, turbidity, total dissolved solids, hardness | Physical and chemical quality | |
| Coliform count | Indicator of faecal contamination | Coliforms in drinking water mean it is unsafe |

### Control of water pollution

1. Treat sewage and industrial effluent before discharge.
2. Use less fertiliser and pesticide; adopt organic farming and integrated pest management.
3. Recycle and reuse water in industry ("zero liquid discharge" where possible).
4. Do not dump waste, medicines or chemicals into rivers or drains.
5. Plant vegetation along river banks; protect wetlands that filter water.
6. Enforce the **Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974**.

### Wastewater (sewage and effluent) treatment

```text
Raw sewage
   ↓
PRELIMINARY: screens remove large objects; grit chamber removes sand
   ↓
PRIMARY: sedimentation tank — suspended solids settle as primary sludge
   ↓
SECONDARY (biological): microbes oxidise dissolved organic matter
   • activated sludge process (aerated tank + settling; part of the sludge returned)
   • trickling filter (water trickles over stones coated with microbial film)
   • oxidation ponds / lagoons
   ↓
TERTIARY (advanced): removes nitrogen, phosphorus, colour, remaining solids;
   filtration, activated carbon, ion exchange, reverse osmosis
   ↓
DISINFECTION: chlorination, UV or ozone → discharge or reuse
Sludge → digested (biogas) → dried → manure or safe disposal
```

**Pharmaceutical effluent** may be strongly acidic or alkaline, high in COD and solvents, and contain active drug residues. Common steps are neutralisation, equalisation, solvent recovery, biological treatment and, for difficult wastes, advanced oxidation or evaporation.

## 3.4 Soil pollution

**Soil pollution** is the presence of chemicals or other substances in soil at levels that reduce its fertility, harm organisms living in it, or enter the food chain and groundwater.

### Sources

| Source | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Agricultural chemicals | Excess fertilisers, pesticides, herbicides, fungicides |
| Industrial waste | Heavy metals, chemical sludge, fly ash, pharmaceutical and solvent waste |
| Urban solid waste | Plastics, paper, glass, metal, food waste dumped in open |
| Biomedical waste | Used syringes, dressings, expired medicines, pathological waste |
| Electronic waste | Lead, mercury, cadmium from batteries and circuit boards |
| Mining | Overburden, tailings, acid drainage |
| Irrigation with sewage or polluted water | Pathogens, metals |
| Acid rain and settling of air pollutants | Acidification, metals |
| Radioactive waste | Nuclear facilities, some medical uses |

### Effects

- Loss of soil fertility; death of useful microbes and earthworms.
- Toxic chemicals taken up by crops enter the food chain (pesticide residues, heavy metals).
- Leaching to groundwater; contaminated drinking water.
- Changes in soil pH and structure; salinity; poor crop yields.
- Breeding of flies, mosquitoes and rats in open dumps; foul smells and spread of disease.
- Plastics block drainage, choke animals that eat them, and break into microplastics.

### Control of soil pollution

1. **The 3 Rs:** reduce waste, reuse articles, recycle paper, glass, metal and plastic.
2. **Segregate waste at source** into wet (biodegradable) and dry (recyclable) and hazardous.
3. **Composting and vermicomposting** of biodegradable waste.
4. **Sanitary landfill:** waste spread in layers, compacted and covered with soil daily, on a lined base that stops leachate reaching groundwater; methane can be collected.
5. **Incineration:** burning at high temperature for hazardous and biomedical waste, with air pollution control.
6. **Bioremediation:** using microbes to break down pollutants (for example oil-degrading bacteria); **phytoremediation** uses plants that take up metals.
7. Balanced use of fertilisers, biofertilisers and integrated pest management.
8. Afforestation and soil conservation.
9. Rules: the Solid Waste Management Rules, Plastic Waste Management Rules, Hazardous Waste Rules and Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules (2016) under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986.

### Biomedical waste: the pharmacist's role

Hospitals, pharmacies and laboratories must segregate biomedical waste into colour-coded bags or containers at the point of generation (yellow, red, white translucent puncture-proof and blue categories under the 2016 rules), store it safely and hand it to an authorised common treatment facility. Expired and discarded medicines are biomedical waste and must not be thrown in household garbage or drains. Pharmacists can run take-back programmes and teach patients safe disposal.

## 3.5 Summary of key environmental laws in India

| Law | Year | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act | 1974 | Set up pollution control boards; control water pollution |
| Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act | 1981 | Control of air pollution |
| Environment (Protection) Act | 1986 | Umbrella law to protect and improve the environment; rules for wastes made under it |
| Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules | 2016 | Safe handling and disposal of biomedical waste |

## Remember

- Pollutants: primary vs secondary; biodegradable vs non-biodegradable.
- Air pollutants: CO (carboxyhaemoglobin), SO2 and NOx (acid rain), PM2.5 (deep lung damage), ozone, lead, CFCs.
- Classical smog = smoke + SO2 in cold fog; photochemical smog = NOx + hydrocarbons + sunlight → ozone, PAN.
- Greenhouse gases: CO2, CH4, N2O, CFCs, water vapour; global warming → sea-level rise, extreme weather.
- CFCs release Cl· that destroys ozone; Montreal Protocol 1987.
- Particle control: settling chamber, cyclone, bag filter, ESP, wet scrubber; vehicles: catalytic converter.
- Water quality: DO high = clean; BOD (5 days, 20 °C) and COD high = polluted; COD ≥ BOD.
- Eutrophication: nutrients → algal bloom → oxygen loss → fish death.
- Minamata = mercury; itai-itai = cadmium; blue baby = nitrate; fluorosis = fluoride.
- Sewage treatment: preliminary → primary → secondary (activated sludge, trickling filter) → tertiary → disinfection.
- Soil pollution control: 3 Rs, segregation, composting, sanitary landfill, incineration, bioremediation.
- Acts: Water 1974, Air 1981, Environment (Protection) 1986; Bio-Medical Waste Rules 2016.

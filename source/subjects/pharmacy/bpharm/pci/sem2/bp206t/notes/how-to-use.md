# How to use this guide

Environmental Sciences is a descriptive, scoring subject with no numericals. Marks come from neat definitions, classifications, labelled flow diagrams (food chain, energy pyramid, nitrogen cycle, sewage treatment) and tables of sources, effects and control. Study one unit at a time: read the notes, then close them and write each list from memory (the six natural resources, the parts of an ecosystem, the air pollutants and their effects). Finally answer the question bank on paper. Each unit has very short (2-mark), short answer (5-mark) and long essay (10-mark) questions, rated by how often that kind of question appears in PCI-syllabus papers.

**Units.** The syllabus has only three units of 10 hours each, and this pack follows them exactly, one notes file per unit; no unit was split or joined. Each unit is short in the syllabus but covers several topics, so the notes are a little longer than usual and are broken into numbered sections you can learn one at a time (for example Unit 1 has a separate section for each of the six natural resources, and Unit 3 a separate section for air, water and soil pollution).

## Exam pattern

BP206T is marked with an asterisk in the PCI B.Pharm regulations: it is a **non-university examination**, and the subject experts at college level conduct it. In the regulations' scheme of examination for semester II it carries:

| Part | Marks | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continuous mode (attendance, academic activities, student–teacher interaction) | 10 | — |
| Sessional exams (set for 30 marks, converted to 15) | 15 | 1 hour each |
| **End-semester exam** | **50** | **2 hours** |
| Total | 75 | |

Sessional papers for non-university subjects are set for 30 marks (long answers 1 of 2 × 10, short answers 4 of 6 × 5); the average of two sessionals is converted to 15 marks.

**End-semester paper (50 marks, 2 hours).** The PCI regulations print this pattern for a 50-mark theory paper, and the full mock paper in this app follows it. There is **no MCQ section**, but the MCQs here are still good revision for definitions, dates of laws and examples. BP206T is a college-conducted exam, so **confirm the pattern with your college**.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Long answers — answer 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| II | Short answers — answer 6 of 8 | 6 × 5 = 30 |
| | **Total** | **50** |

With three units and three long questions, expect roughly one long question from each unit and the eight short questions spread across all three. Time plan for two hours: about 25 minutes for each long answer and about 10 minutes for each short answer, leaving 10 minutes to read the paper and check.

There is no practical subject linked to BP206T.

## Probability ratings

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with a diagram or table and examples.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful as a backup choice and for MCQ-style class tests.

These ratings are estimates from common patterns and syllabus weight, not a promise of what will be asked.

## Scoring tips for this subject

1. Begin every answer with a one-line definition, then write the points under small headings.
2. Draw something in every long answer: a food chain with arrows, an energy pyramid, the nitrogen or carbon cycle, the eutrophication sequence, a sewage treatment flow chart, or a labelled ESP or cyclone sketch.
3. Use the **source → effects → control** structure for any pollution question, and the **uses → problems → conservation** structure for any natural resource question.
4. Use tables for comparisons: renewable vs non-renewable, classical vs photochemical smog, BOD vs COD, grazing vs detritus food chain, primary vs secondary succession.
5. Quote Indian examples and laws: Chipko movement, Narmada dams, Bhopal gas tragedy, Thar desert, Sundarbans estuary, Water Act 1974, Air Act 1981, Environment (Protection) Act 1986.
6. Add a pharmacy link where it fits: medicinal plants from forests, pharmaceutical effluent, solvent vapours, antibiotic residues and resistance, biomedical waste and safe disposal of medicines.
7. A 10-mark answer needs about six to eight headed points plus a diagram or table and a short conclusion. A 5-mark answer needs about five clear points or a small table.
8. Finish pollution and resource answers with a line on what an individual can do; examiners reward it.

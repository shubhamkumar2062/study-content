# How to use this guide

Pharmacy Practice is a **descriptive, professional-practice** subject. Most marks come from writing well-organised answers on **systems and procedures** – how drugs are distributed in a hospital, how a formulary or PTC works, how ADRs are detected and reported, how a patient is counselled, how stock is controlled. There is one small numerical topic (**EOQ and reorder level**) and one interpretation topic (**laboratory tests**). Study one unit at a time: read the notes, draw the flow charts and tables from memory, then answer the question bank. Each unit has very short (2-mark), short (5-mark) and long (10-mark) questions.

**Units in this guide** follow the five PCI units:

| Unit | Content | Syllabus hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hospital and hospital pharmacy organisation; ADRs and drug interactions; community pharmacy | 10 |
| 2 | Drug distribution; hospital formulary; TDM; medication adherence; medication history; community pharmacy management | 10 |
| 3 | PTC and drug information; patient counselling; education and training, code of ethics; prescriptions and communication skills | 10 |
| 4 | Budget; clinical pharmacy and pharmaceutical care; OTC sales | 8 |
| 5 | Drug store management and inventory control; investigational drugs; clinical laboratory tests | 7 |

Each unit keeps all the sub-topics (a, b, c …) that the syllabus groups under it, so the units are of different length. Medication history appears in Unit 2 (as a separate syllabus topic) and again in Unit 4 (as part of clinical pharmacy); the full notes are in Unit 2.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with a diagram or table.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from common patterns and syllabus weight, not a promise of what will be asked.

**Points to check before the exam**

1. **Licence form numbers** and conditions under the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules change from time to time; follow your college's current notes.
2. Clinical trials are now governed by the **New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019**, not Schedule Y.
3. India has **no legal OTC category** yet; mention the draft OTC policy only as "under consideration".
4. **Laboratory reference ranges** vary between laboratories; write "about" or quote the range your textbook uses.
5. This guide gives no drug doses. Where a question asks about dose adjustment, explain the **principle** (creatinine clearance, reduce dose or extend interval, TDM).

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. Start each long answer with a **one-line definition** (hospital pharmacy, formulary, TDM, pharmaceutical care, ADR).
2. Draw a **flow chart** wherever there is a process – unit dose distribution, purchase procedure, counselling steps, drug information query, ADR management.
3. Use **tables** for classifications (hospitals, ADRs, interactions, distribution systems, ABC/VED, information sources) and for advantages vs disadvantages.
4. In EOQ questions, **write the formula, define each symbol, substitute and give units**; round to a practical figure.
5. For laboratory tests, give **normal range + what a high/low value means + one drug example**.
6. End answers with the **pharmacist's role** – examiners look for it in nearly every topic.
7. Very short answers (2 marks): definition + one example or one key point.

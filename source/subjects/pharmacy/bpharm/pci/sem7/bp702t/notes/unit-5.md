# Unit 5: Indian regulatory requirements

In India, drugs are regulated under the **Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940** and its rules, by a shared system: the **Central Government** (through CDSCO) controls new drugs, clinical trials, imports and certain high-risk products, while **State Governments** (through State Licensing Authorities) license manufacture and sale. This unit covers both, the Common Technical Document used for submissions, the Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product needed for exports, and the procedure for approval of new drugs under the **New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019**.

Rules, forms and fees change through amendments and notices. Use these notes for the exam, but always check the current text on the CDSCO website (cdsco.gov.in) before any real application.

## 5.1 The legal framework in brief

| Law / rule | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 | Import, manufacture, distribution and sale of drugs and cosmetics |
| Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945 (now often called Drugs Rules, 1945, after cosmetics got separate rules) | Licences, standards, GMP (Schedule M), labelling, schedules of drugs |
| New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019 | New drugs, investigational new drugs, clinical trials, BA/BE studies, ethics committees |
| Medical Devices Rules, 2017 | Medical devices |
| Cosmetics Rules, 2020 | Cosmetics |
| Drugs (Prices Control) Order | Prices (under the Essential Commodities Act; administered by NPPA) |
| Pharmacy Act, 1948; NDPS Act, 1985; Drugs and Magic Remedies Act, 1954 | Profession, narcotics, advertisements |

## 5.2 Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO)

**CDSCO** is the **National Regulatory Authority** of India for drugs, cosmetics and medical devices. It works under the **Directorate General of Health Services, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare**, Government of India, and is headed by the **Drugs Controller General of India (DCGI)**, who acts as the Central Licensing Authority. Its headquarters is in New Delhi.

### a. Organisation

```text
Ministry of Health and Family Welfare
        │
Directorate General of Health Services
        │
CDSCO — headed by DCGI (New Delhi headquarters)
        ├── Divisions at HQ: new drugs, clinical trials, biologicals,
        │   medical devices, import and registration, quality control,
        │   pharmacovigilance, legal, training, IT (SUGAM portal)
        ├── Zonal and sub-zonal offices (inspections, coordination with states)
        ├── Port offices at sea ports and airports (import and export checks)
        └── Central drug testing laboratories (e.g. Central Drugs Laboratory,
            Kolkata; Central Drugs Laboratory, Kasauli for vaccines and sera)

Statutory advisory bodies under the Act:
   Drugs Technical Advisory Board (DTAB) — technical advice to governments
   Drugs Consultative Committee (DCC) — uniform enforcement across states
Expert committees: Subject Expert Committees (SEC) review new drug and
clinical trial applications
```

### b. Responsibilities of CDSCO

1. **Approval of new drugs** and of clinical trials (including global trials), BA/BE studies.
2. **Registration and licensing of imports** of drugs, and control of quality of imported drugs at ports.
3. **Registration of ethics committees** for clinical trials and BA/BE studies.
4. **Laying down standards** of drugs (with the Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission) and amending laws and rules.
5. **Licensing of specified categories** jointly with states — such as blood and blood products, large-volume parenterals, vaccines and sera, recombinant DNA products and certain medical devices — through the Central Licence Approving Authority.
6. **Medical devices** regulation under the Medical Devices Rules.
7. **Coordination** of the activities of State Drug Control Organisations; joint inspections with states.
8. **Testing** of drug samples in central laboratories.
9. **Pharmacovigilance** (with the Pharmacovigilance Programme of India) and action on banned or unsafe drugs.
10. **Export support:** issuing certificates such as the Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product, and No Objection Certificates for export of unapproved or banned drugs.
11. **Training** of regulatory officials and analysts.

## 5.3 State Licensing Authority (SLA)

Each State or Union Territory has a **Drug Control Organisation**, headed by the State Drugs Controller (Licensing Authority).

### a. Organisation

```text
State Health Department
        │
State Drugs Controller / Licensing Authority
        ├── Deputy / Assistant Drugs Controllers
        ├── Drugs Inspectors (field enforcement)
        └── State Drug Testing Laboratory (Government Analyst)
```

### b. Responsibilities

1. **Licences for manufacture** of drugs and cosmetics in the state (other than those reserved for the central authority, which are granted jointly).
2. **Licences for sale** — wholesale and retail (chemists, druggists).
3. **Inspection** of manufacturing premises and sales outlets for GMP and compliance.
4. **Drawing samples** of drugs and sending them for testing to the Government Analyst.
5. **Action against** substandard, spurious, adulterated and misbranded drugs: suspension or cancellation of licences, seizure, prosecution in court.
6. **Monitoring of advertisements** and of sale of prescription drugs.
7. **Recall** of not-of-standard-quality drugs.
8. **Issue of certificates** needed for export (GMP certificates, free-sale certificates, and joint inspection for WHO-GMP and COPP with CDSCO).

### c. Division of work between centre and state

| Activity | Central (CDSCO) | State (SLA) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New drug approval and clinical trials | Yes | No |
| Import licence and registration | Yes | No |
| Manufacturing licence (most drugs) | No (advisory) | Yes |
| Manufacturing licence for specified products (vaccines, LVPs, blood products, r-DNA) | Joint approval (Central Licence Approving Authority) | Grants licence after central approval |
| Sale licences | No | Yes |
| Standards and rules | Yes | No |
| Enforcement in the market | Coordination | Yes |

## 5.4 Common Technical Document (CTD)

The **CTD** is an internationally agreed format for organising the information in an application for marketing authorisation, developed by ICH (guideline **M4**). It allows one dossier to be prepared for several regulators. The electronic version is the **eCTD**. CDSCO accepts applications in CTD format.

### Structure: five modules

```text
                      ┌──────────────┐
                      │   Module 1   │  Regional administrative information
                      │ (not part of │  (country-specific; not harmonised)
                      │   the CTD)   │
                      └──────────────┘
                      ┌──────────────┐
                      │   Module 2   │  CTD summaries
                      └──────────────┘
         ┌──────────────┐ ┌──────────────┐ ┌──────────────┐
         │   Module 3   │ │   Module 4   │ │   Module 5   │
         │   Quality    │ │ Non-clinical │ │   Clinical   │
         │              │ │study reports │ │study reports │
         └──────────────┘ └──────────────┘ └──────────────┘
```

| Module | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Administrative and regional information | Application forms, covering letter, licences, labelling and package insert, regional documents (in India: forms under the NDCT Rules, fees, Indian labelling) |
| 2. Summaries | 2.1 table of contents; 2.2 introduction; 2.3 quality overall summary; 2.4 non-clinical overview; 2.5 clinical overview; 2.6 non-clinical written and tabulated summaries; 2.7 clinical summary |
| 3. Quality | Drug substance (3.2.S: general information, manufacture, characterisation, control, reference standards, container closure, stability) and drug product (3.2.P: description and composition, pharmaceutical development, manufacture, control of excipients, control of product, reference standards, container closure, stability); regional information |
| 4. Non-clinical study reports | Pharmacology, pharmacokinetics, toxicology reports; literature references |
| 5. Clinical study reports | Tabular listing of studies; BA/BE, PK, PD, efficacy and safety study reports; post-marketing experience; case report forms; literature |

**Advantages:** one format for many countries; less duplication; easier review; consistent presentation; supports electronic submission and life-cycle updates.

## 5.5 Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product (COPP)

A **COPP** is a certificate issued, in the format recommended by the **WHO Certification Scheme on the quality of pharmaceutical products moving in international commerce**, by the competent authority of the exporting country. It is requested by the importing country's regulator as part of registration of an imported product.

**What it certifies**

1. Whether the product is licensed (authorised for sale) in the exporting country, and if not, why.
2. The licence holder and the manufacturer.
3. That the manufacturing site is regularly inspected and complies with WHO GMP.
4. The composition of the product, and whether the information approved in the exporting country (such as the summary of product characteristics) is attached.

**In India:** COPP is issued by CDSCO (through its zonal offices) after the manufacturer is found compliant with WHO-GMP, usually after a joint inspection with the State Licensing Authority. The manufacturer applies with its manufacturing licence, product permission, site master file, product details and GMP inspection report. Check the current CDSCO notice for the application route, documents and validity.

**Uses:** registration of Indian products in other countries (many countries in Africa, Asia and Latin America require it), supports trust in Indian exports, and helps smaller regulators who cannot inspect foreign sites themselves.

## 5.6 New drugs: definition and approval

### a. What is a "new drug" (NDCT Rules, 2019 — in outline)

1. A drug, including an API or phytopharmaceutical, not used in India to any significant extent and not approved as safe and effective by the Central Licensing Authority for the proposed claims.
2. An approved drug proposed with new claims — a new indication, new dosage form, new route of administration, or modified or new release system.
3. A fixed-dose combination of two or more drugs approved individually, combined for the first time, or with changed ratio, new claims or dosage form.
4. A modified or sustained-release form or a novel drug delivery system of an approved drug.
5. Vaccines, recombinant DNA–derived products, living modified organisms, monoclonal antibodies, stem-cell–derived products, gene therapeutic products and xenografts intended as drugs.

Products in some of these categories are considered new drugs for a fixed period (four years) after the first permission; check the rule text for which categories this applies to.

### b. Main bodies involved

- **Central Licensing Authority (CLA):** the DCGI.
- **Subject Expert Committee (SEC):** experts in each therapeutic area who review applications and recommend approval or rejection.
- **Ethics committee:** must be registered with the CLA; reviews and approves each trial protocol at its site and monitors subject safety.

### c. Approval steps for a new drug

```text
Non-clinical studies (as per the rules' requirements)
   → Application to conduct clinical trial (Form CT-04 for a new drug / IND)
     + ethics committee approval
   → CDSCO review (with SEC) → permission (Form CT-06)
   → Phase I, II, III trials in India (or local trials waived/abbreviated
     in cases allowed by the rules, e.g. drugs approved in specified countries)
   → Application for permission to import (Form CT-18) or to manufacture
     (Form CT-21) the new drug for sale or distribution, with full data in CTD
   → SEC review, possible inspection and testing of samples
   → Permission granted by CLA (with conditions)
   → State manufacturing licence (for manufacture) / import licence
   → Marketing with post-marketing obligations (PSURs, Phase IV if required)
```

### d. Timelines (as provided in the NDCT Rules, 2019)

| Application | Decision within (working days) |
| --- | --- |
| Clinical trial of a drug discovered/developed abroad | 90 |
| Clinical trial of a drug discovered, researched and developed in India (to be manufactured in India) | 30; if no communication within this period, permission is deemed granted |

Check current amendments, since timelines and categories are revised from time to time.

### e. Data required for marketing permission (outline)

1. Administrative data, forms and fees.
2. Chemical and pharmaceutical information: composition, manufacture, specifications, stability, packaging.
3. Animal pharmacology and toxicology data.
4. Human clinical pharmacology (Phase I) data.
5. Therapeutic exploratory (Phase II) and confirmatory (Phase III) trial data, including data from Indian patients where required.
6. Bioavailability and bioequivalence data where relevant.
7. Regulatory status in other countries, and marketing information.
8. Proposed prescribing information, labels and package insert.
9. Plan for post-marketing surveillance (pharmacovigilance).

**Local clinical trial waiver:** the rules allow the CLA to waive or abbreviate local trials in specified situations (for example, drugs already approved in certain countries named by the Government, orphan drugs, drugs for unmet medical need or public-health emergencies), with conditions such as Phase IV studies. Check the current notified list of countries and conditions.

### f. Post-marketing requirements

- **Periodic Safety Update Reports (PSURs):** submitted every six months for the first two years after approval and annually for the next two years (as laid down in the rules; check the current schedule).
- Reporting of serious unexpected adverse reactions within the set time.
- **Phase IV studies** where required as a condition of approval.
- Any change in the approved product (new indication, dosage form, manufacturing changes of certain kinds) needs fresh permission or notification.

### g. Generic (already approved) drugs

A second company that wants to make a drug already approved in India still applies to the CLA while that drug remains a "new drug"; it usually supplies quality data and bioequivalence data instead of full clinical trials. Once a drug is no longer treated as new, manufacture needs only a State manufacturing licence under the Drugs Rules with GMP (Schedule M). Check the rule text for exactly when CLA permission is still needed.

## 5.7 Other approvals linked to new drugs

| Approval | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Ethics committee registration (application in Form CT-01) | Before an ethics committee can approve trials |
| Test licence | Import or manufacture of small quantities of drugs for examination, testing, analysis or trials |
| Registration certificate and import licence | Import of drugs for sale |
| Manufacturing licence (from State) | Commercial manufacture after new drug permission |
| WHO-GMP certificate and COPP | Export |

The online **SUGAM** portal of CDSCO is used for submitting and tracking applications.

## Remember

- Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940, Rules 1945; New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules 2019 (replaced Schedule Y provisions).
- CDSCO: National Regulatory Authority under DGHS, MoHFW; headed by DCGI (Central Licensing Authority); zonal, sub-zonal and port offices; central labs; DTAB and DCC advise; SECs review new drugs and trials.
- CDSCO does: new drugs, clinical trials, imports, standards, ethics committee registration, joint licensing of vaccines/LVPs/blood products/r-DNA, medical devices, coordination, COPP.
- SLA does: manufacturing and sale licences, inspections, sampling, prosecution, recalls.
- CTD (ICH M4): Module 1 regional; 2 summaries; 3 quality; 4 non-clinical reports; 5 clinical reports.
- COPP: WHO certification scheme format; issued in India by CDSCO after WHO-GMP compliance.
- New drug approval: CT-04 → CT-06 (clinical trial); CT-18 (import) or CT-21 (manufacture) for marketing; SEC review; then State licence; PSURs 6-monthly for 2 years then yearly for 2 years.
- Timelines: 90 working days; 30 working days (with deemed approval) for drugs developed in India.
- Always check cdsco.gov.in for the current forms, fees and timelines.

# Unit 2: Technology development and transfer

A new product is usually developed in one place (an R&D laboratory or pilot plant) and made in another (a production plant, often in another city or country, sometimes by a different company). Moving the knowledge, documents and skills needed to make the product reliably at the new place is called **technology transfer (TT)**. If it is done badly, the receiving plant makes batches that fail, and the product reaches patients late or not at all.

## 2.1 Definition and purpose

**Technology transfer** is a logical procedure that controls the transfer of any process, together with its documentation and professional expertise, between development and manufacture or between manufacturing sites.

Purpose:

1. To make the product at the new site with the same quality, safety and efficacy as at the original site.
2. To move the product from R&D to commercial production (vertical transfer) or from one plant to another (horizontal transfer).
3. To license a product or process to another company (in-licensing / out-licensing), or to a contract manufacturer.
4. To share know-how between public research bodies and industry.

**Types**

| Type | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Vertical (development to production) | R&D formula → company's own plant |
| Horizontal (site to site) | One plant → another plant of the same company |
| Inter-company | Innovator → licensee or contract manufacturer |
| Public to private | Government laboratory → industry, through agencies such as NRDC |
| International | Foreign company → Indian manufacturer, or the reverse |

## 2.2 WHO guidelines for technology transfer

The World Health Organization published **WHO guidelines on transfer of technology in pharmaceutical manufacturing** in its Technical Report Series (TRS No. 961, Annex 7, 2011). They cover transfer of the API, excipients, finished products and packaging, analytical methods, premises and equipment, qualification and validation, and documentation. Check the WHO website for any later revision before quoting it in a dossier.

### Terminology

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Sending unit (SU) | The site or party that holds the technology and sends it |
| Receiving unit (RU) | The site or party that receives the technology and will make the product |
| Technology transfer team | Members of SU and RU from R&D, production, QA, QC, engineering and regulatory affairs, with a named leader |
| Technology transfer protocol | The agreed written plan for the transfer |
| Technology transfer report | The written record of what was done, results and conclusions |
| Critical quality attribute (CQA) | A property of the product that must stay within limits to ensure quality |
| Critical process parameter (CPP) | A process setting that affects a CQA and must be controlled |
| Design space | The range of material and process variables shown to give acceptable quality |
| Acceptance criteria | Numerical limits or conditions that decide whether the transfer succeeded |
| Gap analysis | Comparison of SU and RU capabilities to find differences that need action |
| Quality risk management (QRM) | Systematic assessment, control, communication and review of risks to quality |
| Commissioning | Setting up and testing new equipment or a facility before qualification |

### Principles of a successful transfer

1. The transfer must be planned, documented and approved.
2. The RU must have the capacity, skills and facilities to make the product.
3. Both units agree on acceptance criteria before work begins.
4. All differences between SU and RU (equipment, materials, environment, procedures) are identified and their risk assessed.
5. The transfer is complete only when the RU shows, with data, that it can repeat the process and meet all specifications.

## 2.3 Technology transfer protocol

The protocol is written jointly and approved by both SU and RU (and QA of each). It usually lists:

1. Objective and scope of the transfer.
2. Key personnel and their responsibilities.
3. Description of the product, process and analytical methods to be transferred.
4. Comparison of materials, equipment, facilities and procedures at SU and RU (gap analysis).
5. Risk assessment and planned controls.
6. Training needed at the RU.
7. Experimental design: number and size of batches (trial and validation), sampling plan, tests.
8. Acceptance criteria for each step.
9. Documents to be handed over.
10. Timeline and how deviations will be handled.

```text
Plan and form team → gap analysis + risk assessment → protocol approved
   → document and knowledge transfer → training at RU
   → analytical method transfer → trial batches → validation batches
   → stability data → TT report → regulatory filing → routine production
```

## 2.4 Quality risk management in technology transfer

Risk management (described in ICH Q9) means finding what could go wrong, how likely and how serious it is, and controlling it.

- **Risk assessment:** risk identification, risk analysis, risk evaluation.
- **Risk control:** reduce the risk or accept it with justification.
- **Risk communication and review:** share findings and review them as data arrive.

Tools: FMEA (failure mode and effects analysis), fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram, HACCP, fault tree analysis, risk ranking. In TT, typical risks are a different grade of excipient, a different granulator design, different water quality, or less experienced operators. Each is scored and given a control (extra testing, more trial batches, training, equipment change).

## 2.5 Transfer from R&D to production

### a. Process transfer

The SU hands over the development report, master formula, process description with critical parameters and their ranges, in-process controls, batch records from development and pilot batches, stability data, and known problems with their solutions. The RU prepares its own master batch record for its equipment, makes trial batches, then validation batches.

### b. Packaging transfer

The SU gives specifications of primary and secondary packaging materials (type of container, closure, blister film, foil), packaging design, compatibility and stability data in that pack, and any special needs (desiccant, light protection, child-resistant closure). The RU checks its packaging line can handle the pack and qualifies its suppliers.

### c. Cleaning

Information on the product's solubility, toxicity, potency (for setting residue limits), cleaning agents, cleaning procedures and validated analytical methods for residues is transferred. The RU writes and validates its own cleaning procedure, considering other products made on the same equipment.

## 2.6 Granularity of the TT process

"Granularity" means breaking the transfer into its separate pieces, each with its own information:

| Item | Information transferred |
| --- | --- |
| API (drug substance) | Route of synthesis or supplier, specifications, physical properties (particle size, polymorphic form, solubility), stability, storage, handling hazards (potent or toxic), impurity profile |
| Excipients | Grade, specification, supplier, functional properties that affect the product (e.g. viscosity grade of HPMC, particle size of lactose), any animal-origin concerns |
| Finished product | Formula, process flow, critical parameters, in-process and release specifications, stability, known variability |
| Packaging materials | Specifications, drawings, suppliers, compatibility and protection data |

## 2.7 Documentation

Typical documents in a transfer:

- Technology transfer protocol and report.
- Development report (formulation and process history, justification of formula).
- Master formula and batch manufacturing records.
- Specifications and test methods (API, excipients, packaging, in-process, finished product).
- Validation and qualification protocols and reports.
- Stability protocols and data.
- Standard operating procedures.
- Risk assessment reports and gap analysis.
- Training records.

All documents must be controlled (version, approval, distribution) under the quality system.

## 2.8 Premises and equipment

- The RU's premises should meet GMP and the needs of the product (for example humidity control for hygroscopic drugs, containment for potent drugs, clean rooms for sterile products).
- Equipment should be compared with the SU's: same operating principle and capacity, or differences justified with data. Different equipment may need changes in process settings.
- A list of SU equipment with make, model and settings helps the RU match them.

## 2.9 Qualification and validation

- **Qualification** shows that equipment and utilities are suitable: design qualification (DQ), installation qualification (IQ), operational qualification (OQ), performance qualification (PQ).
- **Process validation** shows that the process consistently gives a product meeting its specifications. Traditionally three consecutive successful batches at commercial scale are made; current approaches use the product life cycle (process design, process qualification, continued process verification).
- **Cleaning validation** and **analytical method validation** are also needed.
- Validation is done only after the equipment is qualified and the analytical methods are transferred.

## 2.10 Quality control and analytical method transfer

The RU's QC laboratory must be able to run every test method and obtain the same results as the SU.

**Analytical method transfer** is the documented process that shows a receiving laboratory can use a validated method as intended.

Approaches:

| Approach | What is done |
| --- | --- |
| Comparative testing | Both laboratories test the same lots; results compared against preset criteria |
| Co-validation | The RU takes part in the validation of the method itself |
| Revalidation (full or partial) | The RU repeats the validation parameters relevant to it |
| Transfer waiver | Justified exemption, e.g. a simple pharmacopoeial method already used by the RU |

The protocol states the method, samples, number of replicates, acceptance criteria (for example difference in mean assay results within an agreed limit) and how failures are investigated. Reference standards, reagents, columns and instruments must be equivalent.

## 2.11 Approved regulatory bodies and agencies

Transfers that change the manufacturing site of an approved product must be reported to the regulator of each market. Examples of regulatory bodies whose rules affect TT:

| Country / region | Body |
| --- | --- |
| India | Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO) and State Licensing Authorities |
| USA | Food and Drug Administration (US FDA) |
| European Union | European Medicines Agency (EMA) with national agencies |
| United Kingdom | Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) |
| Japan | Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (PMDA) |
| Australia | Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) |
| Canada | Health Canada |
| Global guidance | WHO (prequalification, GMP), ICH (harmonised guidelines) |

## 2.12 Commercialisation: practical aspects and problems

**Commercialisation** is taking a technology from the laboratory to the market and earning from it.

Practical aspects:

1. Market survey and demand.
2. Cost of production and pricing.
3. Scale-up and technology transfer.
4. Regulatory approval in each market.
5. Intellectual property (patents, licences).
6. Funding, partners and distribution network.

Common problems:

| Problem | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Scale-up failure | A granule that flows well at pilot scale gives weight variation on a high-speed press |
| Equipment differences | RU has a fluid bed granulator where the SU used a high-shear mixer |
| Raw-material variation | A new supplier's excipient has a different particle size |
| Incomplete knowledge transfer | Tacit "tricks" of the SU staff not written down |
| Analytical differences | RU gets lower assay because of a different column or standard handling |
| Regulatory delays | Site-change approval takes longer than planned |
| Cost overrun | Extra trial batches and validation |
| Legal and IP issues | Disputes over ownership of improvements |

**Short case studies (illustrative)**

- *Case 1 — tablet capping after transfer:* a tablet made well at the SU capped at the RU. The gap analysis had missed that the RU press ran much faster. Solution: precompression added and press speed limited in the master record.
- *Case 2 — assay mismatch:* RU's assay results were consistently lower. Investigation found incomplete extraction because the RU used a shorter sonication time; the method was written more precisely and the transfer repeated.
- *Case 3 — dissolution failure:* a new excipient supplier's lactose had a finer particle size; tablets were harder and dissolved more slowly. The excipient specification was tightened to include particle size.

## 2.13 Technology transfer agencies in India

| Agency | Full name | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| APCTD / APCTT | Asian and Pacific Centre for Transfer of Technology (a regional institution of UN-ESCAP, based in New Delhi; the syllabus spells it APCTD) | Helps member countries of the Asia-Pacific region to build capacity for technology transfer, shares information on technologies and supports small enterprises |
| NRDC | National Research Development Corporation (set up by the Government of India in 1953; under the Department of Scientific and Industrial Research) | Licenses technologies developed in national research laboratories and universities to industry; helps with patents and commercialisation |
| TIFAC | Technology Information, Forecasting and Assessment Council (an autonomous body under the Department of Science and Technology, set up in 1988) | Technology forecasting and assessment (e.g. Technology Vision documents), supports technology-led projects with industry |
| BCIL | Biotech Consortium India Limited (promoted by the Department of Biotechnology) | Transfers and commercialises biotechnology; helps with patents, licensing, project consultancy and biosafety |
| TBSE / SIDBI | Technology Bureau for Small Enterprises, a joint initiative involving the Small Industries Development Bank of India (SIDBI) | Helps small and medium enterprises find technologies, partners and finance |

## 2.14 TT-related documentation: agreements and legal issues

### a. Confidentiality agreement (non-disclosure agreement, NDA in the legal sense)

Signed before any secret information is shared. It defines what is confidential, who may see it, for what purpose, for how long, and what happens to the information if talks fail. (Do not confuse it with the New Drug Application, also abbreviated NDA.)

### b. Memorandum of understanding (MoU)

A document recording the intention of the parties to work together and the broad terms (scope, responsibilities, timelines). It is usually not as binding as a formal contract but guides the later agreement.

### c. Licensing agreement

The owner of a technology (licensor) gives another party (licensee) the right to use it, often in exchange for an upfront payment and royalties. Terms include:

- Field and territory (which products, which countries).
- Exclusive or non-exclusive rights.
- Duration and termination.
- Payments: lump sum, milestones, royalty on sales.
- Ownership of improvements.
- Quality obligations and audits.
- Confidentiality.

### d. Technology transfer agreement / supply and quality agreements

Defines what will be transferred, timelines, responsibilities for regulatory filings, and a quality (technical) agreement describing who is responsible for which GMP activity.

### e. Legal issues

- Patents and other intellectual property rights (who owns what, freedom to operate).
- Trade secrets and know-how protection.
- Breach of confidentiality.
- Product liability.
- Competition law and export controls.
- Dispute resolution (arbitration, governing law and jurisdiction).

## Remember

- Technology transfer = documented transfer of process, documents and know-how between development and manufacture or between sites; success = RU repeatedly meets all specifications.
- WHO TT guideline: TRS 961, Annex 7 (2011). Key terms: sending unit, receiving unit, protocol, report, gap analysis, CQA, CPP, design space.
- Protocol: objective, team, gap analysis, risk assessment, training, batches, acceptance criteria, timeline.
- QRM (ICH Q9): identify → analyse → evaluate → control → communicate → review; FMEA, fishbone.
- R&D → production covers process, packaging and cleaning; granularity covers API, excipients, finished product, packaging.
- Qualification DQ, IQ, OQ, PQ; then process, cleaning and method validation.
- Method transfer: comparative testing, co-validation, revalidation, waiver.
- Indian TT agencies: APCTD/APCTT, NRDC, TIFAC, BCIL, TBSE/SIDBI.
- Documents: confidentiality agreement, MoU, licensing agreement, TT and quality agreements; legal issues = IP, know-how, liability, disputes.

# How to use this guide

Industrial Pharmacy II is a descriptive subject about how a product moves from the laboratory to the market: scale-up, technology transfer, regulatory approval and quality systems. There are very few numbers to learn, so marks come from clear definitions, well-ordered lists, flow charts of procedures and tables comparing agencies or guidelines. Study one unit a day. Read the notes, close them, and write each flow chart (pilot plant set-up, TT process, drug development path, OOS investigation, change control, QbD steps, Indian new drug approval) from memory. Finish with the question bank and the MCQs for that unit.

The notes follow the five units of the PCI syllabus exactly. Unit 3 is the largest and is split into Part A (regulatory affairs as a function) and Part B (requirements for drug approval: non-clinical studies, IND, IB, NDA, clinical and BE studies, protocols, biostatistics, data presentation and trial management). No unit has been merged or moved.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

The question bank has long (10-mark), short (5-mark) and very short (2-mark) questions. The very short ones are good warm-ups and help with MCQs.

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with a flow chart or table.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from common patterns and syllabus weight, not a promise of what will be asked.

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. Start every answer with a one-line definition, then go to a numbered list. Expand every abbreviation the first time you use it (TT, QbD, OOS, CTD, COPP, IND, NDA, SUPAC, NABL, GLP).
2. Draw a flow chart for every procedure: TT steps, drug development, OOS investigation, change control, PDCA, DMAIC, QbD, new drug approval in India. A neat chart earns a large share of the marks.
3. Use tables for comparisons: QA vs QC, certification vs accreditation, ISO 9001 vs GMP, IND vs NDA vs ANDA, CDSCO vs State Licensing Authority, SUPAC levels, the five CTD modules.
4. Learn the small set of fixed facts exactly: SUPAC Levels 1–3, f2 50–100, BE 90% CI 80–125%, Six Sigma 3.4 DPMO, ISO 9001 / 14001 / 17025 / 15189, CTD five modules, IND 30-day wait, NDCT Rules 2019 and the main CT forms.
5. For regulatory questions, name the authority and the law (for example "CDSCO under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940 and NDCT Rules 2019"). Rules change, so in real work always check the current text on the official website.
6. In "write a note on" questions about an agency (NRDC, TIFAC, NABL), give full form, parent body, role and one example of its work.
7. End long answers with advantages or significance; examiners in this subject reward a closing point.

# Unit 5: Ocular and intrauterine drug delivery systems

This unit covers two sites where a dosage form must work in a small, well-protected space. **Part A** deals with the eye, where most of an eye drop is lost within minutes. **Part B** deals with the uterus, where a small device can release drug or copper for years.

## Part A: Ocular drug delivery systems

## 5.1 Introduction

Ocular drug delivery means giving drugs to the eye to treat eye diseases such as glaucoma, infections, inflammation, dry eye and retinal disease. The eye is easy to reach from outside, but it has strong natural defences that keep foreign substances out. As a result, only a very small part of a conventional eye drop (often less than about 5%) reaches the inside of the eye.

**Anatomy relevant to delivery**

```text
         ┌──────── cornea (transparent front window)
         │   ┌──── conjunctiva (covers white of eye and inner lids)
   tear  │   │
   film ─┤   │   aqueous humour (front chamber, between cornea and lens)
         │   │        │
         ▼   ▼        ▼
   ( ( cornea )  anterior chamber | iris | lens | vitreous humour | retina )
                                                    (back of eye)
   Anterior segment: cornea, conjunctiva, iris, ciliary body, lens, aqueous humour
   Posterior segment: vitreous humour, retina, choroid, optic nerve
```

**Classification of ocular routes**: topical (drops, ointments, gels, inserts), subconjunctival and periocular injections, intracameral (into the anterior chamber), intravitreal (into the vitreous) injections and implants, and systemic routes.

## 5.2 Intraocular barriers

### a. Precorneal barriers (losses before the drug reaches the cornea)

1. **Tear turnover and drainage.** The cul-de-sac (lower conjunctival sac) holds only a few microlitres (roughly 7–10 µl), but a drop is about 30–50 µl. The excess spills onto the cheek or drains through the nasolacrimal duct. Normal tear turnover is about 1 µl per minute, and blinking speeds drainage.
2. **Reflex tearing and blinking** caused by irritant, non-isotonic or wrongly buffered drops.
3. **Binding to tear proteins** and **metabolism** by tear enzymes.
4. **Absorption through the conjunctiva** and nasal mucosa into blood (non-productive; may cause systemic side effects, for example with timolol).

### b. Corneal barrier

The cornea has three main layers that act in series:

| Layer | Nature | Barrier for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Epithelium (outer) | Lipophilic, cells joined by tight junctions | Hydrophilic and ionised drugs |
| Stroma (middle, thickest) | Hydrophilic, mostly water and collagen | Very lipophilic drugs |
| Endothelium (inner) | Single cell layer, fairly leaky | Minor barrier |

So the best corneal penetration is shown by drugs with **both lipophilic and hydrophilic character** (moderate partition coefficient), often weak bases that exist partly un-ionised at tear pH.

### c. Blood–ocular barriers

- **Blood–aqueous barrier**: tight junctions of the ciliary epithelium and iris blood vessels limit entry of drugs from blood into the aqueous humour.
- **Blood–retinal barrier**: tight junctions of the retinal pigment epithelium and retinal capillaries keep systemically given drugs out of the retina and vitreous.

These barriers make the posterior segment very hard to reach by drops or by tablets; intravitreal injections or implants are often needed.

## 5.3 Methods to overcome ocular barriers

| Problem | Approach | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rapid drainage | Increase viscosity | HPMC, methylcellulose, polyvinyl alcohol, carbomer solutions |
| Short contact time | Ointments and gels | Petrolatum-based ointments; hydrogels |
| Short contact time | In situ gelling systems | pH-triggered (cellulose acetate phthalate latex, carbomer), temperature-triggered (poloxamer 407), ion-activated (gellan gum, sodium alginate) |
| Short contact time | Mucoadhesive polymers | Carbomer, chitosan, hyaluronic acid |
| Poor corneal permeation | Prodrugs | Dipivefrin (prodrug of adrenaline), latanoprost (ester prodrug) |
| Poor corneal permeation | Penetration enhancers | Benzalkonium chloride (also preservative), EDTA, surfactants, cyclodextrins |
| Poor solubility | Cyclodextrins, micelles, nanosuspensions | Cyclodextrin complexes |
| Frequent dosing | Inserts, implants, colloidal carriers | Ocusert, intravitreal implants, liposomes, nanoparticles, niosomes |
| Posterior segment | Intravitreal injections and implants | Ganciclovir and dexamethasone implants, anti-VEGF antibody injections |
| Systemic loss | Small drop volume, nasolacrimal occlusion (pressing the inner corner of the eye) | Patient counselling |

## 5.4 Preliminary (preformulation) study for ocular products

Before designing an ocular product, the following are studied:

1. **Solubility** and the need for solubilisers.
2. **pKa and partition coefficient**, to predict corneal penetration.
3. **pH–stability and pH–solubility profile**: tears have a pH of about 7.4. The eye tolerates a pH range of roughly 6.6–9 without much discomfort, but many drugs are stable only at lower pH; a weak buffer is then used so that tears can quickly restore normal pH.
4. **Tonicity**: tears are isotonic with about 0.9% sodium chloride; the eye tolerates roughly 0.7–1.5% NaCl equivalent.
5. **Sterility and preservative need**: ocular products must be sterile; multi-dose containers need a preservative (benzalkonium chloride, chlorobutanol, thiomersal-type mercurials in older products, or newer preservatives); preservative-free single-dose units suit long-term use and contact lens wearers.
6. **Particle size** for suspensions (fine particles, to avoid irritation and scratching).
7. **Viscosity**: moderate increase prolongs contact, but very thick products blur vision.
8. **Compatibility** with containers, closures and contact lenses; **ocular irritation** tests.

## 5.5 Ocular formulations

| Formulation | Features |
| --- | --- |
| Eye drops (solutions) | Simple, cheap, most common; short contact time |
| Suspensions | For poorly soluble drugs (some corticosteroids); particles act as a reservoir; must be shaken |
| Emulsions | For lipophilic drugs (ciclosporin eye emulsion) |
| Ointments | Longer contact; good for night use; blur vision; base of white soft paraffin and liquid paraffin |
| Gels and in situ gels | Longer residence with less blurring than ointments |
| Inserts (ocuserts) | Solid devices placed in the cul-de-sac; release over hours to days |
| Colloidal carriers | Liposomes, niosomes, nanoparticles, microemulsions, nanosuspensions |
| Contact lenses loaded with drug | Release into the tear film over time |
| Intravitreal injections and implants | Reach posterior segment directly |
| Iontophoresis | Small current drives charged drug into the eye |

## 5.6 Ocular inserts (ocuserts)

**Ocular inserts** are sterile, thin, solid or semisolid devices of suitable size and shape, placed in the conjunctival sac (cul-de-sac) to release drug over a long period.

**Advantages**

1. Longer residence time and prolonged release; fewer applications.
2. Accurate dosing, unlike drops.
3. Reduced systemic absorption and side effects.
4. Possibility of zero-order release.
5. No preservative needed, so less irritation and fewer allergic reactions.
6. Better stability (dry dosage form).

**Disadvantages**

1. Foreign-body feeling; patient may resist using them.
2. May fall out unnoticed, especially during sleep or eye rubbing.
3. Placing and removing need skill.
4. Possible interference with vision.
5. Cost.

**Classification**

```text
Ocular inserts
├── Insoluble
│     ├── Diffusion (reservoir) systems: Ocusert
│     ├── Osmotic systems
│     └── Contact lenses (soft hydrogel lenses soaked in drug)
├── Soluble (dissolve in tears; no removal)
│     ├── Natural polymer: collagen shields
│     └── Synthetic / semisynthetic: soluble ophthalmic drug insert (SODI),
│         hydroxypropyl cellulose insert (Lacrisert) for dry eye
└── Bioerodible
      └── Polymer that erodes gradually: poly(orthoesters), cross-linked gelatin
```

**Ocusert (pilocarpine ocular therapeutic system).** The classic insoluble reservoir insert for glaucoma.

```text
   ┌─────────────────────────────────┐  EVA rate-controlling membrane
   │ ┌─────────────────────────────┐ │
   │ │ pilocarpine + alginic acid  │ │  drug reservoir
   │ └─────────────────────────────┘ │
   └─────────────────────────────────┘  EVA rate-controlling membrane
     annular ring with titanium dioxide (white, makes insert visible)
```

It releases pilocarpine at a nearly constant (zero-order) rate for about a week, so it replaces frequent daily drops. The same principle (EVA membranes) is used in other reservoir implants.

**Lacrisert**: a rod of hydroxypropyl cellulose without drug, placed in the lower cul-de-sac once daily; it dissolves slowly to thicken the tear film in dry eye.

**Collagen shields**: thin collagen films that fit over the cornea, soaked in drug before use, and dissolve within hours to days.

**Evaluation of ocular inserts**: thickness and weight uniformity, drug content uniformity, surface pH, swelling, folding endurance, in vitro release (vial or flow-through cell), sterility, ocular irritation testing (rabbit eye in the past; validated alternatives now preferred), and in vivo release.

## Part B: Intrauterine drug delivery systems

## 5.7 Introduction

**Intrauterine devices (IUDs)** are small, flexible devices inserted into the uterine cavity through the cervix, mainly for long-acting reversible contraception. Medicated IUDs also deliver copper ions or hormones locally. The uterus is used as a site because a device placed there can work for years with almost no patient effort, and local hormone release needs only very small amounts.

**Mechanism of action**

1. A local foreign-body inflammatory reaction in the endometrium, with white cells and products that are toxic to sperm and the ovum and hinder implantation.
2. **Copper** ions are spermicidal, affect sperm motility and enzymes in the endometrium.
3. **Progestogen** release (levonorgestrel, progesterone) thickens cervical mucus so that sperm cannot pass, and thins the endometrium.

## 5.8 Advantages and disadvantages

**Advantages**

1. Long duration of action (from one year to several years, depending on the device).
2. Highly effective and reversible: fertility returns soon after removal.
3. No daily action needed; no interference with intercourse.
4. Hormonal devices act locally with very low systemic hormone levels, and reduce menstrual blood loss.
5. Cost-effective; part of national family planning programmes in India (copper IUDs such as Cu-T 380A and Cu 375).

**Disadvantages**

1. Must be inserted and removed by trained staff.
2. Pain, cramps, increased bleeding and spotting, especially with copper devices in the first months.
3. Expulsion of the device.
4. Rarely, perforation of the uterus.
5. Risk of pelvic infection soon after insertion.
6. If pregnancy does occur, a higher proportion may be ectopic.
7. No protection against sexually transmitted infections.

## 5.9 Development of intrauterine devices

IUDs have developed through generations:

| Generation | Type | Examples | Features |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Early devices | Rings of silk or metal | Grafenberg ring | Rigid; many complications |
| First generation | Non-medicated (inert) plastic | Lippes loop, Margulies coil, Saf-T-coil | Polyethylene with barium sulfate (radio-opaque); larger; more bleeding and pain |
| Second generation | Copper-bearing | Copper-7, Copper T 200, Copper T 380A, Multiload Cu-375 | Copper wire or sleeves on a smaller plastic frame; more effective; T 380A lasts about 10 years |
| Third generation | Hormone-releasing | Progesterone IUD (Progestasert, about one year), levonorgestrel intrauterine system (several years) | Drug reservoir in a membrane-controlled stem; less bleeding |

```text
       Copper T 380A                   Levonorgestrel intrauterine system
      ════╤════  copper sleeves         ════╤════  horizontal arms
          ║      on arms                    ║
          ║  copper wire                 ┌──╨──┐  steroid reservoir in a
          ║  wound on stem               │ LNG │  silicone (PDMS) membrane
          ║                              └──╥──┘  → slow, near-constant release
          ╨  removal threads                ╨  removal threads
```

**Design considerations**

1. **Size and shape**: must fit the uterine cavity, stay in place (low expulsion) and not cause pain or bleeding; T-shaped frames fit well.
2. **Materials**: biocompatible, flexible plastics (polyethylene, EVA, silicone) with barium sulfate for X-ray visibility; copper wire of high purity; monofilament threads for removal.
3. **Release control**: copper surface area decides copper release; hormone release is controlled by a rate-controlling membrane (silicone or EVA) around the steroid reservoir, giving nearly zero-order release.
4. **Insertion**: device folded into a thin inserter tube for easy passage through the cervix.
5. **Sterility**: supplied sterile in sealed packs, often sterilised by ethylene oxide.

## 5.10 Applications

1. Long-term reversible contraception (copper and hormonal IUDs).
2. Emergency contraception (copper IUD inserted soon after unprotected intercourse).
3. Treatment of heavy menstrual bleeding (levonorgestrel system).
4. Endometrial protection during estrogen hormone replacement therapy (progestogen-releasing systems).
5. Research into local delivery of other drugs to the uterus.

Related systems: **vaginal rings** (for example EVA rings releasing contraceptive hormones for several weeks) use the same membrane-controlled release principle but sit in the vagina.

## Remember

- Eye drop volume (about 30–50 µl) exceeds cul-de-sac capacity (about 7–10 µl); tear turnover about 1 µl/min; often less than about 5% of drug enters the eye.
- Corneal layers: lipophilic epithelium, hydrophilic stroma, endothelium → drug needs balanced lipophilicity. Blood–aqueous and blood–retinal barriers block the back of the eye.
- Overcome barriers by viscosity, gels, in situ gels (pH: CAP latex/carbomer; temperature: poloxamer 407; ions: gellan gum), mucoadhesives, prodrugs (dipivefrin), enhancers, inserts, colloidal carriers and intravitreal implants.
- Preliminary study: solubility, pKa/log P, pH (tears about 7.4), tonicity (0.9% NaCl), sterility and preservatives, particle size, viscosity.
- Inserts: insoluble (Ocusert: pilocarpine + alginic acid between EVA membranes, about a week, zero order), soluble (Lacrisert, SODI, collagen shields), bioerodible.
- IUDs: inert (Lippes loop) → copper (Cu-T 380A, Multiload 375) → hormonal (progesterone, levonorgestrel). Actions: foreign-body reaction, copper spermicidal, progestogen thickens cervical mucus.

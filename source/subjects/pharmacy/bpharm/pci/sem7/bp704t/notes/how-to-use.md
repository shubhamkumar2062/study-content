# How to use this guide

Novel Drug Delivery System is a concept subject: marks come from clear definitions, neat labelled diagrams, classification charts, advantage/disadvantage lists and a few equations. Study one unit a day. Read the notes, then close them and draw each key diagram from memory (reservoir and matrix systems, elementary osmotic pump, buccal patch, transdermal patch layers, floating system, pMDI, liposome, hybridoma flow chart, Ocusert, Copper T). Finish with the question bank and the MCQs for that unit.

The notes follow the five units of the PCI syllabus. Units 2 and 3 each join three short syllabus topics and are written in parts (Part A, B, C) inside one file. The release-kinetics equations (zero order, first order, Higuchi, Korsmeyer–Peppas, Hixson–Crowell) are placed in Unit 1 with the design approaches, because examiners ask them with diffusion and dissolution systems. No unit has been merged or moved.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

The question bank has long (10-mark), short (5-mark) and very short (2-mark) questions. The very short ones are good warm-ups and help with MCQs.

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with a diagram or table.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from common patterns and syllabus weight, not a promise of what will be asked.

**Where the marks usually come from**

| Unit | Topics that keep coming back |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Controlled release and polymers | Definitions (SR, CR, delayed, repeat action), advantages and disadvantages, drug selection, physicochemical and biological properties, diffusion / dissolution / ion-exchange systems, release kinetics equations, classification and applications of polymers |
| 2 Microencapsulation, mucosal, implants | Methods of microencapsulation (coacervation, solvent evaporation, Wurster, spray drying), theories of mucoadhesion, buccal patch formulation, implants, elementary osmotic pump |
| 3 Transdermal, GRDDS, nasopulmonary | Skin permeation and factors, permeation enhancers, components and types of TDDS, approaches to GRDDS (floating, high density, swelling, bioadhesive), pMDI, DPI, nebulisers, nasal sprays |
| 4 Targeted delivery | Levels and approaches of targeting (passive, active, EPR), liposomes (types, preparation, applications), niosomes vs liposomes, nanoparticles, monoclonal antibodies |
| 5 Ocular and intrauterine | Ocular barriers and methods to overcome them, ocular formulations, ocular inserts and Ocusert, IUD generations and mechanism |

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. Open every answer with a one-line definition, then go to a numbered list or table. "Write a note on" answers should end with applications or examples.
2. Draw a labelled diagram wherever one exists: reservoir vs matrix, osmotic pump, TDDS layers, floating HBS, pMDI, liposome bilayer, Ocusert, Copper T. A neat figure often earns a third of the marks.
3. Give a classification chart at the start of long answers (approaches to CR, methods of microencapsulation, types of TDDS, approaches to GRDDS, types of liposomes, ocular inserts, IUD generations).
4. Learn the equations with the meaning of each symbol: Fick's law, reservoir release dM/dt = ADKΔC/L, Higuchi Q = kH√t, Noyes–Whitney, first-order t1/2 = 0.693/k, Peppas n values, skin flux J = Kp Cv, osmotic pump rate.
5. Practise one numerical: fitting release data to zero order, first order and Higuchi; finding t1/2; calculating flux from a permeability coefficient. Show the formula, substitution and units.
6. Compare-type questions (reservoir vs matrix, liposomes vs niosomes, microcapsules vs microspheres, pMDI vs DPI, buccal vs sublingual, active vs passive targeting) are best answered as a table.
7. Quote named examples for each system (nitroglycerin and scopolamine patches, liposomal doxorubicin, Ocusert pilocarpine, Copper T 380A, push–pull nifedipine system); examiners look for them.

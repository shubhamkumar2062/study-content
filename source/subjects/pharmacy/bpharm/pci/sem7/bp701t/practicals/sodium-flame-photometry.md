# Determination of sodium by flame photometry

**Aim:** To determine the sodium content of a given sample (for example a reconstituted oral rehydration salts solution or an intravenous fluid) by flame photometry.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual, the instrument instructions and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles; keep the flame area clear of solvents and paper.

## Principle

The sample solution is sprayed (nebulised) into a flame. The solvent evaporates, the salt vaporises and breaks into free sodium atoms, and some of these are excited by the heat of the flame. When they return to the ground state they emit yellow light at **589 nm**. A filter isolates this light, and a photodetector measures its intensity, which is proportional to the sodium concentration over a limited range. The unknown is read from a calibration curve of sodium standards.

```text
 [Sample] -> [Nebuliser] -> [Flame] -> [589 nm filter] -> [Photodetector] -> [Meter]
```

## Requirements

Flame photometer with sodium filter, fuel gas (LPG) and air compressor, volumetric flasks (100 ml, 1000 ml), pipettes, beakers; sodium chloride (dried at about 110 °C), deionised water (sodium-free), sample.

## Procedure

1. **Stock (1000 ppm Na):** dissolve 2.542 g dried NaCl in deionised water and make up to 1000 ml (2.542 g NaCl contains 1 g Na, from 58.44/22.99).
2. **Intermediate (100 ppm):** dilute 10 ml stock to 100 ml.
3. **Standards:** dilute the 100 ppm solution to give 10, 20, 40, 60, 80 and 100 ppm Na.
4. **Sample:** dilute so that it falls within the range. For an ORS solution, dilute 2 ml to 100 ml (factor 50).
5. Light the flame as per instructions and let the instrument warm up; select the sodium filter.
6. Aspirate deionised water and set the reading to zero.
7. Aspirate the highest standard (100 ppm) and set the reading to 100 (or the maximum used).
8. Aspirate each standard and the sample in turn, aspirating water between solutions, and note the steady reading.
9. Recheck zero and the top standard at the end.
10. Plot reading against concentration and find the sample concentration.

## Observations and calculations

> Example readings, for practice only — record your own.

| Solution | Na (ppm) | Emission reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blank | 0 | 0 |
| S1 | 10 | 10 |
| S2 | 20 | 20 |
| S3 | 40 | 39 |
| S4 | 60 | 58 |
| S5 | 80 | 78 |
| S6 | 100 | 97 |
| Sample (diluted 1:50) | ? | 33.5 |

**Least-squares line:** reading = 0.966 × C + 0.42 (r = 0.99997).

**Diluted sample:** C = (33.5 − 0.42)/0.966 = **34.2 ppm Na**.

**Original sample:** 34.2 × 50 = **1712 mg/L Na**, that is 1712/22.99 = **74.5 mmol/L** sodium.

**As NaCl (if needed):** mg NaCl = mg Na × 2.542.

Note: the curve bends at high concentration (self-absorption, ionisation), so stay in the linear range.

## Result

The sample contains ___ ppm (___ mmol/L) of sodium.

## Precautions

1. Use sodium-free (deionised) water and clean plastic or well-rinsed glassware; sodium contamination from glass and fingers is common.
2. Let the flame stabilise and keep gas and air pressures constant.
3. Aspirate water between samples to clean the nebuliser.
4. Check zero and full-scale often; drift is common.
5. Matrix of standards and samples should be similar (viscosity, other salts).

## Viva questions

1. **At what wavelength is sodium measured?** 589 nm (yellow D line).
2. **What happens to the sample in the flame?** Desolvation, vaporisation, atomisation, excitation, emission.
3. **Name the interferences.** Spectral, ionisation, chemical, physical (matrix), self-absorption.
4. **Why use an internal standard such as lithium?** To correct for fluctuations in flame and aspiration rate.
5. **How does flame photometry differ from AAS?** It measures emission by excited atoms; AAS measures absorption by ground-state atoms.
6. **Give pharmaceutical uses.** Na in ORS, IV fluids, dialysis solutions; serum electrolytes.

# Determination of chlorides and sulphates by nephelo-turbidimetry

**Aim:** To determine the concentration of chloride (as silver chloride) by nephelometry and of sulphate (as barium sulphate) by turbidimetry in a given sample.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles and gloves; silver nitrate stains skin.

## Principle

A fine, evenly dispersed precipitate scatters light (Tyndall effect).

- **Chloride** reacts with silver nitrate in nitric acid to give colloidal **AgCl**: Cl⁻ + Ag⁺ → AgCl. The intensity of light scattered at 90° (nephelometry) is proportional to concentration in dilute suspensions: Is = Ks × c.
- **Sulphate** reacts with barium chloride in acid medium to give **BaSO4**: SO4²⁻ + Ba²⁺ → BaSO4. The fall in transmitted light (turbidance) is measured in a turbidimeter or a colorimeter, often near 420 nm: S = log(I0/I) = kbc.

The particle size must be the same in standards and samples, so concentration of reagents, order of mixing, time, stirring and temperature are kept constant, and a **stabiliser** (glycerol–ethanol mixture, gelatin or gum) keeps the particles suspended.

## Requirements

Nephelometer (or fluorimeter used at 90°), turbidimeter or colorimeter, cells, volumetric flasks, pipettes, stopwatch; sodium chloride (dried), potassium sulphate or sodium sulphate (dried), dilute nitric acid, silver nitrate solution (about 0.1 M), barium chloride (crystals or solution), conditioning reagent (glycerol, ethanol, NaCl and HCl in water, or gelatin solution), distilled water, sample.

## Procedure

**A. Chloride (nephelometry)**

1. Prepare a stock of 100 ppm Cl⁻ (0.1648 g dried NaCl per litre) and standards of 5, 10, 15, 20 and 25 ppm.
2. Into a series of 50 ml flasks put equal volumes of each standard and of the sample; add 1 ml dilute nitric acid to each.
3. Add 1 ml silver nitrate solution, make up to volume, mix the same way and keep in the dark for 5 minutes.
4. Set zero with a blank (water + reagents) and 100 with the highest standard.
5. Read the scattered light of standards and sample at 90°; plot reading against concentration.

**B. Sulphate (turbidimetry)**

1. Prepare a stock of 100 ppm SO4²⁻ (0.1814 g dried K2SO4 per litre) and standards of 10–50 ppm.
2. To equal volumes of each standard and sample add 5 ml conditioning reagent and mix.
3. Add a fixed amount of barium chloride (a measured spoonful of crystals or a fixed volume of solution) while stirring for exactly 1 minute.
4. Read the absorbance (turbidance) at about 420 nm against a blank after a fixed time (for example 4 minutes).
5. Plot absorbance against concentration.

## Precautions

1. Treat standards and samples in exactly the same way (volumes, order, stirring, time).
2. Read at a fixed time after precipitation; the suspension settles or grows with time.
3. Keep the AgCl suspension out of strong light (it darkens).
4. Use clean, scratch-free cells; dust and bubbles scatter light.
5. Use chloride- and sulphate-free water.

## Viva questions

1. **Difference between nephelometry and turbidimetry?** Nephelometry measures scattered light at 90° (dilute suspensions); turbidimetry measures transmitted light (denser suspensions).
2. **Why is a stabiliser added?** To keep particles small and uniform and prevent settling.
3. **Why is nitric acid added in the chloride test?** To prevent precipitation of other silver salts (carbonate, phosphate).
4. **What standard is used for water turbidity?** Formazin suspension; results in NTU.
5. **Which factors affect scattering?** Particle size, number, shape, wavelength and refractive index difference.
6. **Relation to IP limit tests?** The IP chloride and sulphate limit tests compare opalescence visually; this is the instrumental form.

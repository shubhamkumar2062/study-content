# Estimation of sulfanilamide by colorimetry (Bratton–Marshall reaction)

**Aim:** To estimate sulfanilamide in a given sample by diazotisation and coupling with N-(1-naphthyl)ethylenediamine (Bratton–Marshall reaction).

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles and gloves.

## Principle

Sulfanilamide has a free primary aromatic amino group. In acid solution it reacts with nitrous acid (from sodium nitrite and HCl) to form a **diazonium salt**. Excess nitrous acid is destroyed with **ammonium sulphamate**, because it would otherwise react with the coupling reagent. The diazonium salt is then coupled with **N-(1-naphthyl)ethylenediamine dihydrochloride** to give a stable **magenta (purple-red) azo dye**, read at about **540–545 nm**. The absorbance follows Beer's law, and the concentration of the sample is read from a calibration curve.

```text
 Ar-NH2 + HNO2 + HCl  --(0-5 °C or room temp, acid)-->  Ar-N2+ Cl-  (diazonium salt)
 excess HNO2 + NH4SO3NH2  -->  N2 + H2SO4 + ...  (removed)
 Ar-N2+ + naphthylethylenediamine  -->  Ar-N=N-naphthyl (magenta azo dye, ~545 nm)
```

## Requirements

Colorimeter or spectrophotometer, cuvettes, volumetric flasks (100 ml, 25 ml), pipettes; sulfanilamide (standard), 1 M (or 4 M, as per manual) HCl, 0.1% w/v sodium nitrite (freshly made), 0.5% w/v ammonium sulphamate, 0.1% w/v N-(1-naphthyl)ethylenediamine dihydrochloride, distilled water, sample.

## Procedure

1. **Stock:** dissolve 100 mg sulfanilamide in water (warm if needed, a little HCl helps) and make up to 100 ml (1000 µg/ml); dilute 1 ml to 100 ml to obtain 10 µg/ml working stock.
2. Pipette 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 ml of working stock into 25 ml (or 10 ml as per manual) volumetric flasks; this gives a series of standards. Prepare the sample in the same way so that its concentration lies inside the range. Prepare a reagent blank with water.
3. To each flask add 1 ml HCl and 1 ml sodium nitrite; mix and keep 3 minutes.
4. Add 1 ml ammonium sulphamate; mix and keep 2 minutes (effervescence of nitrogen).
5. Add 1 ml N-(1-naphthyl)ethylenediamine solution; mix and allow 5–10 minutes for full colour.
6. Make up to volume with water.
7. Measure the absorbance at about 545 nm against the blank.
8. Plot the calibration curve and find the sample concentration.

## Precautions

1. Use freshly prepared sodium nitrite solution.
2. Keep the timing between additions the same for all flasks.
3. Make sure all excess nitrite is destroyed before adding the coupling reagent, otherwise the blank becomes coloured.
4. Read within the time the colour is stable (as per manual).
5. Avoid contamination with other aromatic amines.

## Viva questions

1. **Which group of sulfanilamide reacts?** The free primary aromatic amine (–NH2) group.
2. **Why is ammonium sulphamate added?** To destroy excess nitrous acid, which would react with the coupling agent.
3. **Name the coupling agent.** N-(1-naphthyl)ethylenediamine dihydrochloride (Bratton–Marshall reagent).
4. **What other drugs can be estimated this way?** Other sulphonamides, procaine, benzocaine, dapsone (primary aromatic amines).
5. **Why measure at λmax?** Highest sensitivity and least error from small wavelength changes.
6. **Can acetylated sulphonamides be measured directly?** No; they must first be hydrolysed to free the amino group.

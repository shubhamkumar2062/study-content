# Separation of plant pigments by column chromatography

**Aim:** To separate the pigments of green leaves (carotenes, xanthophylls and chlorophylls) by adsorption column chromatography.

> Quantities and adsorbent are typical for a student batch; some laboratories use alumina or powdered sucrose instead of silica gel. Follow your lab manual and your teacher. Petroleum ether and acetone are highly flammable: no flames nearby.

## Principle

Leaf extract contains pigments of different polarity. When it is passed through a column of a polar adsorbent, the pigments are adsorbed to different extents and form separate coloured bands. They are eluted with solvents of increasing polarity. The **least polar** pigments (carotenes, hydrocarbons) are held weakly and come out first with petroleum ether; **xanthophylls** (oxygenated carotenoids) come next; **chlorophyll a** and the more polar **chlorophyll b** are held most and need more polar solvent. This is the type of experiment with which Tswett first described chromatography.

| Pigment | Colour | Polarity | Elution order |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| β-Carotene | Orange-yellow | Lowest | First |
| Xanthophylls (lutein) | Yellow | Higher | Second |
| Chlorophyll a | Blue-green | Higher | Third |
| Chlorophyll b | Yellow-green | Highest | Last |

## Requirements

Chromatography column (glass tube with stopcock, about 30 cm × 1.5 cm), glass wool, sand, mortar and pestle, separating funnel, beakers, test tubes; fresh green leaves (spinach), acetone, petroleum ether (60–80 °C), silica gel (column grade) or alumina, anhydrous sodium sulphate.

## Procedure

1. **Extract:** grind about 5 g of washed leaves with 20 ml acetone (a little sand helps). Filter. Shake the filtrate with 20 ml petroleum ether and water in a separating funnel; keep the upper petroleum ether layer (pigments), dry it over anhydrous sodium sulphate and concentrate to a few ml in a warm water bath (no flame).
2. **Column:** plug the bottom with glass wool and add a little sand. Make a slurry of adsorbent in petroleum ether and pour it in, tapping gently so that it settles evenly without air bubbles or cracks (wet packing). Add a thin layer of sand on top. Never let the column run dry.
3. **Loading:** drain the solvent to just above the sand; add the concentrated extract carefully as a narrow band.
4. **Elution:** elute with petroleum ether; collect the first orange-yellow band (carotenes).
5. Change to petroleum ether containing a small proportion of acetone (for example 9 : 1 then 7 : 3) to elute the yellow xanthophylls and then the green chlorophylls; collect each coloured band in a separate tube.
6. Note the colour, order of elution and approximate volume of each fraction.
7. Optionally check the purity of each fraction by TLC or record its visible spectrum.

## Observations

> Example observations, for practice only — record your own.

| Fraction | Eluting solvent | Colour of band | Pigment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Petroleum ether | Orange-yellow | Carotenes |
| 2 | Petroleum ether : acetone (9 : 1) | Yellow | Xanthophylls |
| 3 | Petroleum ether : acetone (7 : 3) | Blue-green | Chlorophyll a |
| 4 | More acetone (or acetone alone) | Yellow-green | Chlorophyll b |

## Result

The leaf pigments were separated into ___ bands, eluted in the order carotenes → xanthophylls → chlorophyll a → chlorophyll b.

## Precautions

1. Pack the column evenly with a slurry; air bubbles and cracks cause uneven bands.
2. Never allow the solvent level to fall below the top of the adsorbent.
3. Apply the sample as a thin, even band.
4. Increase solvent polarity gradually.
5. Work away from flames and in a ventilated area; pigments are light-sensitive, so work quickly.

## Viva questions

1. **Why does carotene elute first?** It is a non-polar hydrocarbon and is held least by the polar adsorbent.
2. **What is the principle of this separation?** Adsorption chromatography with stepwise (gradient) elution.
3. **What is the eluotropic series?** Order of solvents by increasing eluting power on a polar adsorbent.
4. **Why is wet packing preferred?** It gives an even bed without trapped air or cracks.
5. **Who first separated leaf pigments by chromatography?** Tswett (1906), on a calcium carbonate column.
6. **How can fraction purity be checked?** By TLC or by recording the visible absorption spectrum.

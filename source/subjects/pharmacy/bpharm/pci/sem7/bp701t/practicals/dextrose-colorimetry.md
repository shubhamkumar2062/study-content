# Estimation of dextrose by colorimetry

**Aim:** To estimate the amount of dextrose (glucose) in a given sample (for example dextrose injection or oral powder) by colorimetry using the 3,5-dinitrosalicylic acid (DNS) method.

> Quantities are typical for a student batch. Some laboratories use the anthrone method instead (blue-green colour read near 620–630 nm); the calibration steps are the same. Follow your lab manual and your teacher where they differ. Wear goggles and gloves.

## Principle

Dextrose is a reducing sugar. On heating in alkaline solution, its free aldehyde group reduces yellow **3,5-dinitrosalicylic acid** to orange-red **3-amino-5-nitrosalicylic acid**. The intensity of the colour is proportional to the amount of reducing sugar and is measured at about **540 nm**. The concentration of the unknown is read from a calibration curve of standard dextrose solutions (Beer–Lambert law, A = εbc).

## Requirements

Colorimeter or spectrophotometer, glass cuvettes (1 cm), boiling water bath, test tubes, pipettes, volumetric flasks; anhydrous dextrose (standard), DNS reagent (DNS with sodium hydroxide and sodium potassium tartrate), distilled water, sample.

## Procedure

1. **Standard stock:** dissolve 100 mg dextrose in water and make up to 100 ml (1000 µg/ml).
2. **Working standards:** pipette 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 ml of stock into test tubes and make each to 10 ml with water (100–500 µg/ml). Prepare a blank with water.
3. **Sample:** dilute the sample with water so that its concentration falls in the standard range.
4. Pipette 1 ml of each standard, the sample and the blank into separate tubes.
5. Add 1 ml (or the volume in your manual) of DNS reagent to each tube and mix.
6. Heat all tubes together in a boiling water bath for 5–10 minutes.
7. Cool under running water; add a fixed volume of water (for example 8 ml) to each and mix.
8. Set the instrument to zero absorbance with the blank at 540 nm.
9. Read the absorbance of each standard and the sample.
10. Plot absorbance against concentration and read (or calculate) the sample concentration.

## Observations and calculations

> Example readings, for practice only — record your own.

| Tube | Dextrose (µg/ml) | Absorbance at 540 nm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blank | 0 | 0.000 |
| 1 | 100 | 0.112 |
| 2 | 200 | 0.221 |
| 3 | 300 | 0.335 |
| 4 | 400 | 0.442 |
| 5 | 500 | 0.556 |
| Sample (diluted) | ? | 0.276 |

**Least-squares line:** A = 0.001109 × C + 0.0005 (r = 0.99996).

**Sample:** C = (0.276 − 0.0005)/0.001109 = **248 µg/ml** in the diluted solution.

If 1 ml of a 5% w/v dextrose injection was diluted to 200 ml (dilution factor 200), the injection contains 248 × 200 = 49,700 µg/ml ≈ 49.7 mg/ml = **4.97% w/v**, that is 4.97/5 × 100 = **99.4%** of the label claim.

## Result

The given sample contains ___ µg/ml (___% w/v) of dextrose, which is ___% of the labelled amount.

## Precautions

1. Heat standards and samples together for exactly the same time.
2. Cool all tubes to room temperature before reading.
3. Use clean, dry glassware; traces of other reducing substances give high results.
4. Keep the sample within the linear range; dilute if its absorbance exceeds the top standard.
5. DNS reagent is irritant and alkaline; avoid skin contact.

## Viva questions

1. **Why does dextrose react with DNS?** It is a reducing sugar with a free aldehyde (hemiacetal) group.
2. **What colour is formed and where is it read?** Orange-red 3-amino-5-nitrosalicylic acid, read near 540 nm.
3. **Why use a calibration curve?** The reaction does not follow a fixed absorptivity value, so standards treated the same way are needed.
4. **Difference between colorimeter and spectrophotometer?** A colorimeter uses filters (wide band); a spectrophotometer uses a monochromator (narrow band).
5. **Name another colorimetric method for glucose.** Anthrone method or glucose oxidase–peroxidase method.
6. **Why is a blank used?** To cancel the colour of the reagent and the solvent.

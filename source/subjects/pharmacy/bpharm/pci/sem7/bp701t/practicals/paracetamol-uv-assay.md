# Assay of paracetamol by UV spectrophotometry

**Aim:** To assay paracetamol tablets by UV spectrophotometry using its specific absorbance A(1%, 1 cm) at 257 nm.

> Quantities follow the usual pharmacopoeial method for paracetamol tablets. Use the current IP monograph and your lab manual where they differ. Wear goggles and gloves; NaOH is corrosive.

## Principle

In 0.1 M sodium hydroxide, paracetamol (as its phenolate) absorbs strongly at **257 nm**. The pharmacopoeial value of **A(1%, 1 cm) = 715** at this wavelength allows direct calculation of concentration without a standard curve:

**c (g/100 ml) = A / A(1%, 1 cm) = A / 715** (1 cm cell).

The concentration found is multiplied by the dilution factor to get the amount in the weighed powder and then per tablet.

## Requirements

UV spectrophotometer, matched quartz cells, analytical balance, mortar and pestle, volumetric flasks (200 ml, 100 ml), pipettes (10 ml), funnel and filter paper; 0.1 M sodium hydroxide, distilled water, paracetamol tablets (labelled 500 mg).

## Procedure

1. Weigh 20 tablets and find the average weight; powder them finely.
2. Weigh accurately powder equivalent to about **0.15 g** of paracetamol.
3. Transfer to a 200 ml volumetric flask; add 50 ml of 0.1 M NaOH, dilute with about 100 ml water and shake for 15 minutes.
4. Make up to 200 ml with water, mix and filter (discard the first 10–20 ml of filtrate).
5. Dilute 10 ml of the filtrate to 100 ml with water.
6. To 10 ml of this solution add 10 ml of 0.1 M NaOH and dilute to 100 ml with water.
7. Measure the absorbance at **257 nm** against a blank prepared in the same way without the sample (0.1 M NaOH diluted alike).
8. Calculate the content using A(1%, 1 cm) = 715.

## Observations and calculations

> Example readings, for practice only — record your own.

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Weight of 20 tablets | 13.000 g |
| Average weight per tablet | 0.6500 g |
| Weight of powder taken | 0.1950 g |
| Absorbance at 257 nm | 0.530 |

**Step 1, concentration in the final solution:** c = 0.530/715 = 0.000741 g/100 ml (7.41 µg/ml).

**Step 2, dilution factor:** two 10 → 100 ml dilutions, so 100. Concentration in the 200 ml flask = 0.000741 × 100 = 0.0741 g/100 ml.

**Step 3, amount in powder:** 0.0741 × 200/100 = **0.1483 g** paracetamol in 0.1950 g powder.

**Step 4, per tablet:** 0.1483 × 0.6500/0.1950 = **0.4942 g = 494.2 mg**.

**Step 5, % of label:** 494.2/500 × 100 = **98.8%**. (The usual IP limit for paracetamol tablets is 95.0–105.0% of the stated amount; confirm with the current monograph.)

Check: ε = 715 × 151.16/10 ≈ 10,800 L mol⁻¹ cm⁻¹.

## Result

Each tablet contains ___ mg of paracetamol, which is ___% of the labelled amount. The sample ___ (complies / does not comply) with the IP limits.

## Precautions

1. Weigh the powder accurately on an analytical balance.
2. Shake long enough to dissolve all paracetamol before making up to volume.
3. Discard the first portion of filtrate (adsorption on paper).
4. Use matched quartz cells and calibrate the wavelength scale.
5. Keep the final absorbance between 0.2 and 0.8.

## Viva questions

1. **Why is 0.1 M NaOH used?** It converts paracetamol to the phenolate, giving a stable, intense band at 257 nm and good solubility.
2. **What is A(1%, 1 cm)?** The absorbance of a 1% w/v solution in a 1 cm cell; for paracetamol at 257 nm it is 715.
3. **Why is no standard used?** The specific absorbance value is fixed in the pharmacopoeia; a reference standard can still be used to check the instrument.
4. **What is the relation between ε and A(1%, 1 cm)?** ε = A(1%, 1 cm) × M/10.
5. **What is a phenolate shift?** Bathochromic and hyperchromic shift seen when a phenol is ionised in alkali.
6. **Give the IP limit for paracetamol tablets.** Usually 95.0–105.0% of the stated amount.

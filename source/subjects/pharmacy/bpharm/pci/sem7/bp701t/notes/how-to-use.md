# How to use this guide

Instrumental Methods of Analysis is a "draw and explain" subject. Almost every long answer asks for the **principle, instrumentation (with a block diagram) and applications** of one technique. Study one unit at a time: read the notes, draw each block diagram from memory until you can do it in two minutes, redo the worked calculations, then answer the question bank. Each unit has very short (2-mark), short answer (5-mark) and long essay (10-mark) questions, rated by how often that kind of question appears in PCI-syllabus pharmacy papers.

The five units follow the five units of the PCI syllabus exactly. Unit 1 (UV-visible spectroscopy and fluorimetry) and Unit 2 (IR, flame photometry, AAS and nepheloturbidimetry) are long, so their notes are longer than the others.

**Typical PCI end-semester paper (75 marks, 3 hours).** Confirm the exact split with your college, since universities sometimes vary it.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Objective / MCQ | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Long essays — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |
| C | Short answers — attempt 7 of 9 | 7 × 5 = 35 |

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — asked in most years; prepare the full answer with a block diagram and applications.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — rare, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from common patterns and syllabus weight, not a promise of what will be asked.

**Where the marks usually come from**

| Unit | Topics that keep coming back |
| --- | --- |
| 1 UV-visible and fluorimetry | Electronic transitions, chromophores and auxochromes, spectral shifts, Beer–Lambert law with derivation and deviations, instrumentation (sources, monochromators, detectors such as PMT), multicomponent analysis, Jablonski diagram, factors affecting fluorescence, quenching |
| 2 IR and atomic methods | Modes of vibration, factors affecting frequency, sample handling, IR detectors (Golay cell, bolometer, thermocouple, pyroelectric), flame photometry and AAS principle, interferences and instrumentation, nephelometry vs turbidimetry |
| 3 Planar and column chromatography | TLC (methodology, Rf, detection), paper chromatography development techniques, column packing and elution, factors affecting electrophoretic mobility, gel and capillary electrophoresis |
| 4 GC and HPLC | GC instrumentation with detectors (FID, TCD, ECD), derivatization, temperature programming, HPLC instrumentation (pumps, injector, columns, detectors), N, HETP, Rs, van Deemter equation |
| 5 Special column methods | Ion exchange resins (classification, properties, mechanism, factors), gel chromatography theory and applications, affinity chromatography principle and steps |

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. Use one answer plan for every instrument question: **principle → block diagram → each component explained → advantages/limitations → applications**. Examiners give separate marks to the diagram.
2. Draw block diagrams as labelled boxes with arrows from source to readout. Put the detector at 90° for fluorimetry and nephelometry, and show the hollow cathode lamp and chopper in AAS.
3. Write the key equations with every symbol explained: A = εbc, A = 2 − log %T, ν̄ = (1/2πc)√(k/µ), Rf, N = 16(tR/W)², Rs, H = A + B/u + Cu, Ve = V0 + Kd·Vi, F0/F = 1 + Ksv[Q].
4. Comparisons are favourite short questions: flame photometry vs AAS, nephelometry vs turbidimetry, TLC vs paper chromatography, GC vs HPLC, fluorescence vs phosphorescence, normal vs reversed phase. Answer them as tables.
5. Learn the standard numbers: Na 589 nm, K 766 nm, D2 lamp for UV and tungsten for visible, quartz cells below 350 nm, paracetamol A(1%, 1 cm) 715 at 257 nm, quinine excitation about 350 nm and emission about 450 nm, Rs 1.5 for baseline separation, 3N − 6 and 3N − 5 vibrational modes.
6. Always end with 4–6 applications in drug analysis (assay, impurity, identification, bioanalysis); pharmacy examiners look for that link.
7. For practical-based questions, remember the reagent and wavelength of each BP705P experiment (see the practical guides).

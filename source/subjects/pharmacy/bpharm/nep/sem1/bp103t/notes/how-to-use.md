# How to use this guide

Healthcare Psychology and Communication Skills is a one-credit theory course of the new B.Pharm syllabus (PCI, 2026, under NEP 2020). It has five short units of about three lectures each. Most of the marks come from definitions, lists of steps, models and theories with their names, and short applied examples from pharmacy practice. Study one unit a day: read the notes, close the book and write the "Remember" list from memory, then answer the question bank. Each unit has 2-mark short answers and 5-mark long answers, rated by how likely that kind of question is.

The five units follow the five units of the syllabus exactly; none have been split or joined.

**End-semester paper (30 marks, 1½ hours).** Under the PCI 2026 scheme this course carries 50 marks: 20 for sessional evaluation and 30 for the end-semester examination. Confirm the exact split with your university.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Multiple-choice questions — answer all 10 | 10 × 1 = 10 |
| B | Short answers — attempt 5 of 7 | 5 × 2 = 10 |
| C | Long answers — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 5 = 10 |

The app also has a 20-question, 20-minute quick MCQ test for fast revision.

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — the kind of question that is very likely; learn the full answer with headings and an example.
- **Medium** — may come as a full question or as part of a bigger one.
- **Low** — less likely, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from the weight of each topic in the syllabus and the usual style of PCI-pattern papers. This is a new course, so there are no past papers yet; treat the ratings as a guide, not a promise.

**Where the marks are likely to come from**

| Unit | Topics to prepare first |

| 2 Developmental and behavioural psychology | Erikson's stages, personality theories (Big Five, Freud with defence mechanisms), illness perception, anxiety, depression, somatisation, stress (Selye), coping and stress management |

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. **Start with a one-line definition**, then give the parts as a numbered list or table. Examiners in a theory-of-communication paper look for the named components (for example the six parts of SPIKES or the six parts of the HBM).
2. **Name the person or model** — Maslow, Erikson, Pavlov, Skinner, Bandura, Selye, Lazarus, Rosenstock (HBM), Prochaska and DiClemente (TTM), Beck (CBT), Engel (biopsychosocial). A name earns credit and shows precision.
3. **Add one pharmacy example** to every theory: how it applies to adherence, counselling, a patient at the counter or a team on the ward. This turns a 3-mark answer into a 5-mark answer.
4. **Draw a simple diagram** where it fits — the communication cycle with feedback and noise, Maslow's pyramid, the CBT triangle, the stages-of-change cycle, Selye's three stages.
5. **Use tables for comparisons** — empathy vs sympathy, open vs closed questions, problem-focused vs emotion-focused coping, clinical vs health psychology, classical vs operant conditioning.
6. **For 2-mark answers**, write the definition plus two key points or one example. Do not write a page.
7. **For 5-mark answers**, aim for 8–10 clear points or a table plus a short applied paragraph, and finish with a one-line conclusion.
8. **Use respectful language** throughout ("person with depression", "patient with diabetes"); it is part of what this course teaches.

# Unit 5: Phyto-therapeutic agents

This unit introduces twenty commonly used herbal drugs grouped by what they are used for. For each drug the syllabus asks for three things: **biological source**, **major constituents** and **uses**. Learn them as a "monograph" in the same order every time. Short answers often ask "Write the biological source, chemical constituents and uses of …"; long answers ask for two or three drugs of one class (for example "hepatoprotective drugs").

**How to write a drug monograph in the exam**

```text
Name (common / Sanskrit name)
  Biological source : part used + botanical name (italics/underlined) + family
  Constituents      : chemical class → main compounds
  Uses              : traditional + modern uses
  (Extra marks)     : identification test, substitute/adulterant, marketed formulation
```

## 5.1 Adaptogens and immunomodulators

An **adaptogen** helps the body resist physical, chemical and emotional stress and return to balance, without disturbing normal function. An **immunomodulator** changes (stimulates or regulates) the immune response. In Ayurveda many of these are **rasayana** (rejuvenating) drugs.

### Ashwagandha

- **Synonyms**: Winter cherry, Indian ginseng.
- **Biological source**: dried roots of *Withania somnifera* (L.) Dunal, family **Solanaceae**.
- **Constituents**: **withanolides** — steroidal lactones such as **withaferin A** and withanolide D; alkaloids (withanine, somniferine, tropine and others); sitoindosides (withanolide glycosides); starch.
- **Uses**: adaptogen and anti-stress agent; general tonic and rasayana; immunomodulator; improves strength and stamina; used in anxiety, insomnia, weakness after illness; nervine tonic; traditionally as an aphrodisiac. Formulations: Ashwagandharishta, Ashwagandha churna.

### Tulsi

- **Synonyms**: Holy basil.
- **Biological source**: fresh or dried leaves (and whole herb) of *Ocimum tenuiflorum* L. (syn. *O. sanctum* L.), family **Lamiaceae**.
- **Constituents**: volatile oil rich in **eugenol**, with methyl eugenol, β-caryophyllene and carvacrol; triterpenes **ursolic acid** and oleanolic acid; phenolic **rosmarinic acid**; flavonoids (orientin, vicenin).
- **Uses**: adaptogen, immunomodulator; cough, common cold, bronchitis and fever; antimicrobial; antioxidant; used in herbal teas and cough syrups.

### Amla

- **Synonyms**: Indian gooseberry, Amalaki.
- **Biological source**: fresh or dried fruits of *Emblica officinalis* Gaertn. (syn. *Phyllanthus emblica* L.), family **Phyllanthaceae** (formerly Euphorbiaceae).
- **Constituents**: one of the richest natural sources of **vitamin C (ascorbic acid)**, which stays fairly stable because of the tannins present; hydrolysable **tannins** — emblicanin A and B, punigluconin; **gallic acid**, **ellagic acid**; pectin.
- **Uses**: antioxidant and rasayana; immunomodulator; source of vitamin C in scurvy; digestive and mild laxative; hair tonic; part of **Triphala** (with harad and baheda) and the main ingredient of **Chyawanprash**.

## 5.2 Hepatoprotectives

**Hepatoprotective** drugs protect liver cells from damage by toxins (alcohol, drugs, viruses) or help them recover.

### Milk thistle

- **Biological source**: dried ripe fruits (achenes, often called seeds) of *Silybum marianum* (L.) Gaertn., family **Asteraceae**.
- **Constituents**: **silymarin**, a mixture of **flavonolignans** — **silybin (silibinin)**, isosilybin, silychristin and silydianin; fixed oil; proteins.
- **Uses**: hepatoprotective in alcoholic liver disease, fatty liver, cirrhosis and drug-induced liver injury; antioxidant (traps free radicals and stabilises liver cell membranes) and promotes protein synthesis in liver cells; silibinin is used in hospitals as an adjunct in death-cap mushroom (*Amanita*) poisoning.

### Kutki

- **Synonyms**: Katuka, Picrorhiza.
- **Biological source**: dried rhizomes and roots of *Picrorhiza kurroa* Royle ex Benth., family **Plantaginaceae** (formerly Scrophulariaceae).
- **Constituents**: bitter **iridoid glycosides** — **picroside I**, **picroside II** and **kutkoside** (the mixture is called **kutkin**); apocynin; cucurbitacin glycosides; vanillic acid.
- **Uses**: hepatoprotective (jaundice, liver disorders), choleretic (increases bile flow), bitter stomachic and appetiser, antipyretic, immunomodulator. Threatened Himalayan species (see Unit 2).

## 5.3 Cardiovascular drugs

### Garlic

- **Synonyms**: Lasuna.
- **Biological source**: fresh or dried bulbs of *Allium sativum* L., family **Amaryllidaceae** (formerly Liliaceae / Alliaceae).
- **Constituents**: sulfur compounds — **alliin** (S-allyl cysteine sulfoxide) is odourless; when the bulb is crushed, the enzyme **alliinase** converts it into **allicin** (diallyl thiosulfinate), which gives the smell and much of the activity; allicin breaks down to **ajoene**, diallyl disulfide and diallyl trisulfide; also fructans, enzymes, vitamins.

```text
Alliin  ──(alliinase, on crushing)──►  Allicin  ──►  Ajoene, diallyl disulfide, diallyl trisulfide
(odourless)                             (pungent)
```

- **Uses**: lowers blood cholesterol and triglycerides modestly; antiplatelet (ajoene), mild blood-pressure lowering; antibacterial and antifungal; expectorant; carminative and spice. Caution: may increase bleeding with anticoagulants.

### Arjuna

- **Biological source**: dried stem bark of *Terminalia arjuna* (Roxb.) Wight & Arn., family **Combretaceae**.
- **Constituents**: triterpenoids — **arjunic acid**, **arjunolic acid**, arjungenin and their glycosides (**arjunetin**, arjunglucosides); flavonoids (arjunone, luteolin); tannins (condensed); phytosterols; mineral salts, especially calcium.
- **Uses**: cardioprotective and cardiotonic in Ayurveda; used in angina, heart failure (as an add-on), hypertension and high cholesterol; antioxidant; astringent (tannins). Commonly given as Arjunarishta or as arjuna ksheerapaka (decoction in milk).

## 5.4 Antidiabetics

### Gymnema

- **Synonyms**: Gurmar ("sugar destroyer"), Meshashringi.
- **Biological source**: dried leaves of *Gymnema sylvestre* (Retz.) R.Br. ex Sm., family **Apocynaceae** (formerly Asclepiadaceae).
- **Constituents**: **gymnemic acids** — a group of triterpenoid saponins (glycosides of gymnemagenin); **gurmarin**, a small polypeptide; gymnemasaponins; flavonoids.
- **Uses**: antidiabetic — lowers blood glucose, reduces sugar absorption from the gut and is reported to support insulin-secreting beta cells; **chewing the leaf suppresses the sweet taste** for some time (gymnemic acids and gurmarin block sweet receptors), so it reduces sugar craving; also used in obesity.

### Fenugreek

- **Synonyms**: Methi.
- **Biological source**: dried ripe seeds of *Trigonella foenum-graecum* L., family **Fabaceae**.
- **Constituents**: **mucilage** (galactomannan) in the seed coat; the amino acid **4-hydroxyisoleucine** (stimulates insulin release); the alkaloid **trigonelline**; steroidal saponins with the sapogenin **diosgenin**; proteins, fixed oil; volatile compounds giving the smell.
- **Uses**: antidiabetic (soluble fibre slows sugar absorption; 4-hydroxyisoleucine acts on insulin), lowers cholesterol, demulcent and bulk effect, galactagogue (traditionally), spice and flavour; diosgenin is a raw material for steroid synthesis.

## 5.5 Anti-inflammatory and analgesic drugs

### Turmeric

- **Synonyms**: Haldi, Haridra.
- **Biological source**: dried, boiled rhizomes of *Curcuma longa* L., family **Zingiberaceae**.
- **Constituents**: yellow **curcuminoids** — **curcumin**, demethoxycurcumin and bisdemethoxycurcumin (diarylheptanoids); volatile oil containing turmerone, ar-turmerone and zingiberene; starch, resin.
- **Uses**: anti-inflammatory (arthritis), antioxidant, antiseptic and wound-healing (household paste on cuts), skin care, hepatoprotective, carminative; colouring agent and spice. Curcumin is poorly absorbed; formulations combine it with piperine (from black pepper) or use special delivery systems to improve absorption.
- **Test**: powder with conc. sulfuric acid gives crimson; with alkali turns reddish-brown; boric acid gives orange-red.

### Boswellia

- **Synonyms**: Salai guggul, Indian frankincense, Shallaki.
- **Biological source**: oleo-gum-resin obtained by incising the bark of *Boswellia serrata* Roxb. ex Colebr., family **Burseraceae**.
- **Constituents**: resin containing pentacyclic triterpenic acids called **boswellic acids** — β-boswellic acid, 11-keto-β-boswellic acid (KBA) and **3-acetyl-11-keto-β-boswellic acid (AKBA)**; gum (polysaccharides); volatile oil.
- **Uses**: anti-inflammatory and analgesic in osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis; boswellic acids inhibit **5-lipoxygenase**, reducing leukotriene formation — so they act differently from NSAIDs; also used in inflammatory bowel disease and asthma.

## 5.6 CNS drugs

### Brahmi

- **Biological source**: dried whole herb of *Bacopa monnieri* (L.) Wettst. (syn. *B. monniera*), family **Plantaginaceae** (formerly Scrophulariaceae).
- **Constituents**: **bacosides** — dammarane-type triterpenoid saponins (**bacoside A** and bacoside B; bacoside A is a mixture of bacoside A3, bacopaside II and others); alkaloids brahmine and herpestine; flavonoids; sterols.
- **Uses**: **memory and learning enhancer (nootropic)**; anxiolytic and adaptogen; traditionally in epilepsy and mental illness; nervine tonic. A medhya rasayana (brain tonic) of Ayurveda.
- **Note**: the name "brahmi" is also used for *Centella asiatica* (mandukaparni, Apiaceae), a different plant — a common source of confusion and substitution.

## 5.7 Antimicrobial and antiviral drugs

### Giloy

- **Synonyms**: Guduchi, Amrita.
- **Biological source**: dried stems of *Tinospora cordifolia* (Willd.) Miers ex Hook.f. & Thomson, family **Menispermaceae**.
- **Constituents**: diterpenoid lactones (**tinosporide**, columbin); glycosides (**tinocordiside**, cordifolioside, syringin); alkaloids (**berberine**, palmatine, magnoflorine); **immunomodulatory polysaccharides** (arabinogalactan); sterols.
- **Uses**: immunomodulator; fever (antipyretic) including recurrent fevers; anti-infective support; antidiabetic; rasayana; hepatoprotective. Plants growing on neem trees ("neem giloy") are traditionally preferred.

### Neem

- **Synonyms**: Nimba, Margosa.
- **Biological source**: leaves, stem bark and seed oil of *Azadirachta indica* A.Juss., family **Meliaceae**.
- **Constituents**: limonoids (tetranortriterpenoids) — **azadirachtin** (mainly in seeds), **nimbin**, nimbidin, **nimbolide**, salannin, gedunin; flavonoids (quercetin); tannins in the bark; fixed oil in seeds.
- **Uses**: antibacterial, antifungal and antiviral; skin diseases (acne, eczema, scabies), wounds; oral hygiene (neem twigs as datun, toothpaste); antimalarial in tradition; blood purifier in Ayurveda; neem oil and azadirachtin as natural insecticides and insect repellents.

### Andrographis

- **Synonyms**: Kalmegh, "King of bitters".
- **Biological source**: dried aerial parts (leaves and stems) of *Andrographis paniculata* (Burm.f.) Nees, family **Acanthaceae**.
- **Constituents**: very bitter **diterpenoid lactones** — **andrographolide** (major), neoandrographolide, 14-deoxy-11,12-didehydroandrographolide; flavonoids.
- **Uses**: upper respiratory infections, common cold, sore throat and fever; immunostimulant; hepatoprotective (jaundice, liver disorders); bitter tonic and appetiser; antimalarial in traditional use.

## 5.8 Gastrointestinal drug

### Psyllium

- **Synonyms**: Isabgol, Ispaghula, blond psyllium.
- **Biological source**: dried ripe seeds and the separated seed husk (seed coat) of *Plantago ovata* Forssk., family **Plantaginaceae**.
- **Constituents**: **mucilage** (a highly branched **arabinoxylan**) located in the epidermis of the seed coat; the husk contains most of it; fixed oil and proteins in the seed kernel.
- **Uses**: **bulk-forming laxative** in constipation (the mucilage swells with water, increases stool bulk and softens it); also in diarrhoea and irritable bowel syndrome (absorbs water and forms a gel); lowers blood cholesterol; in piles and anal fissure for soft stools. Must be taken with plenty of water to avoid choking or blockage of the gut.
- **Evaluation**: swelling index is the key quality test (Unit 3).

## 5.9 Dermatological agent

### Aloe

- **Synonyms**: Ghritkumari, Kumari.
- **Biological source**: leaves of *Aloe vera* (L.) Burm.f. (syn. *Aloe barbadensis* Mill.), family **Asphodelaceae** (formerly Liliaceae). Two products: (1) **aloe gel** — the clear mucilaginous gel from the inner leaf tissue; (2) **aloe (drug)** — the dried yellow juice (latex) that drains from the cut leaf, concentrated by evaporation.
- **Constituents**:
  - Gel: polysaccharides — mainly **acemannan** (acetylated glucomannan); glycoproteins, enzymes, amino acids, vitamins and minerals.
  - Latex (dried juice): anthrone C-glycosides — **aloin (barbaloin)**; aloe-emodin; resins.
- **Uses**: gel — soothing and healing of **burns, sunburn, wounds and skin irritation**, moisturiser in cosmetics; latex — powerful stimulant purgative (anthraquinones), now used much less because of griping.
- **Test** (latex): Borntrager's test (after hydrolysis) and the borax test (green fluorescence).

## 5.10 Drugs used in women's health

### Chasteberry

- **Synonyms**: Vitex, monk's pepper, chaste tree.
- **Biological source**: dried ripe fruits of *Vitex agnus-castus* L., family **Lamiaceae** (formerly Verbenaceae).
- **Constituents**: iridoid glycosides (**agnuside**, aucubin); flavonoids (**casticin**, vitexin); labdane diterpenes (rotundifuran, vitexilactone); volatile oil (cineole, pinene).
- **Uses**: **premenstrual syndrome**, cyclical breast pain (mastalgia) and irregular cycles; the diterpenes act on dopamine receptors in the pituitary and lower raised **prolactin** release, which helps restore the balance of female hormones.

### Shatavari

- **Synonyms**: Satavar, "she who has a hundred husbands" (a tonic for women).
- **Biological source**: dried tuberous roots of *Asparagus racemosus* Willd., family **Asparagaceae** (formerly Liliaceae).
- **Constituents**: steroidal saponins — **shatavarins** (I–IV), glycosides of **sarsasapogenin**; alkaloid asparagamine A; isoflavones; mucilage; sugars.
- **Uses**: female reproductive tonic and rasayana; **galactagogue** (increases milk secretion); used in menopausal complaints; antacid and in peptic ulcer; adaptogen and immunomodulator; diuretic in tradition.

## 5.11 Respiratory drug

### Vasaka

- **Synonyms**: Adusa, Malabar nut.
- **Biological source**: fresh or dried leaves of *Justicia adhatoda* L. (syn. *Adhatoda vasica* Nees), family **Acanthaceae**.
- **Constituents**: quinazoline alkaloids — **vasicine** (peganine) and **vasicinone**; vasicol; small amount of volatile oil; betaine.
- **Uses**: **expectorant** and **bronchodilator** in cough, bronchitis and asthma; vasicine thins sputum. Vasicine was the model for the synthetic mucolytics **bromhexine** and its metabolite **ambroxol** — a good example of a natural lead. Used in cough syrups (vasaka syrup, Vasavaleha).

## Remember

- Monograph order: biological source (part + Latin name + family) → constituents → uses.
- Three drugs of Plantaginaceae in this unit: kutki, brahmi and psyllium; two of Acanthaceae: andrographis and vasaka; two of Lamiaceae: tulsi and chasteberry.
- Withaferin A (ashwagandha), eugenol (tulsi), emblicanins + vitamin C (amla), silybin (milk thistle), picrosides (kutki), allicin (garlic), arjunolic acid (arjuna), gymnemic acids (gymnema), 4-hydroxyisoleucine (fenugreek), curcumin (turmeric), AKBA (boswellia), bacosides (brahmi), tinosporide (giloy), azadirachtin (neem), andrographolide (kalmegh), arabinoxylan (psyllium), aloin and acemannan (aloe), agnuside (chasteberry), shatavarins (shatavari), vasicine (vasaka).
- Vasicine → bromhexine and ambroxol; boswellic acids inhibit 5-lipoxygenase; gymnema suppresses sweet taste; chasteberry lowers prolactin.
- Take psyllium with plenty of water; garlic may add to the bleeding risk with anticoagulants.

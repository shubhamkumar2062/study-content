# How to use this guide

Introduction to Pharmacognosy is the first natural-products course of the new B.Pharm (NEP 2020) scheme. It is mostly a "learn and write" subject: definitions, classifications, tables of tests and short drug monographs. Study one unit at a time: read the notes, write the "Remember" list from memory, then answer the question bank. Each unit has very short (2-mark), short (4-mark) and long (8-mark) questions, rated by how likely that kind of question is.

The five units follow the five units of the PCI syllabus exactly; no unit was split or joined. Unit 4 is the largest (12 lectures) because it holds two topics — plant metabolites and traditional systems of medicine — so the notes treat them as two halves of one unit.

**End-semester paper (PCI B.Pharm 2026, NEP 2020, Table XV): 3-credit course, 45 marks, 2 hours.** Internal (sessional) assessment carries another 30 marks, making 75 for the course. Confirm the exact pattern with your university.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Multiple-choice questions — all compulsory | 10 × 1 = 10 |
| B | Short answers — attempt 5 of 7 | 5 × 4 = 20 |
| C | Long answers — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 7.5 = 15 |

**Note on the mock paper in this app**: the app can only give whole marks, so long answers are marked out of **8** in the mock paper and the total shows 46. Write each long answer as you would for 7.5 marks in the real paper.

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — a question of this kind is very likely; prepare the full answer with a table or diagram.
- **Medium** — appears every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — less common, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from syllabus weight and the kinds of questions common in pharmacognosy papers. They are not a promise of what will be asked, especially since this is a new syllabus with few past papers.

**Where the marks usually come from**

| Unit | Topics most likely to be asked |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Fundamentals | Definition, history and scope; classification of crude drugs with merits and limitations; sources of drugs; milestones (taxol, artemisinin, penicillin, morphine); herbal pharmacopoeias |
| 2 Cultivation | Methods of propagation; factors affecting cultivation; collection, drying and storage; WHO GACP; plant hormones; polyploidy/mutation/hybridisation; in-situ vs ex-situ conservation; kutki and chirata |
| 3 Quality control | Adulteration types with examples; methods of evaluation; ash and extractive values; stomatal index and other leaf constants; foaming index, swelling index, bitterness value; DNA barcoding |

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. **Biological source** must have three parts: the part used, the botanical name (underline it in a handwritten paper) and the family. "Turmeric is from *Curcuma longa*" alone loses marks; write "dried rhizomes of *Curcuma longa* L., family Zingiberaceae".
2. For **classification** questions, use a table with one example per class and one merit and one limitation for each system.
3. For **evaluation** questions, draw the five-branch chart (organoleptic, microscopic, physical, chemical, biological) first, then explain each with an example.
4. Learn the formulas exactly: stomatal index = S × 100/(E + S); foaming index = 1000/a; haemolytic activity = 1000a/b; bitterness value = 2000C/(A × B). A one-line worked example earns extra credit.
5. For **tests**, write reagent → observation → what it shows (Dragendorff's → orange-red precipitate → alkaloid).
6. For **traditional systems**, use a comparison table: basic theory, diagnosis, treatment and one dosage form for each system.
7. In 4-mark answers, aim for four clear points or a small table; in 7.5-mark answers, aim for a definition, a diagram or table, six to eight explained points and a short conclusion.
8. MCQs often test families and constituents: make your own flash cards from the summary table in Unit 5.

# Unit 4: Plant metabolites and traditional systems of medicine

This is the largest unit of the subject (12 lectures) and has two halves. The first half introduces the chemicals plants make — the primary metabolites they need to live, and the secondary metabolites that give drugs their action. The second half introduces the traditional systems of medicine of India (AYUSH) and China (TCM), their basic principles and dosage forms, and the role of pharmacognosy in them. Both halves are good for long answers.

## 4.1 Plant metabolites — definition and general properties

**Metabolites** are the small organic compounds formed during metabolism (the chemical reactions of a living cell).

| Feature | Primary metabolites | Secondary metabolites |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Definition | Compounds essential for the life, growth and reproduction of every plant | Compounds not needed for basic life but which help the plant survive and interact with its surroundings |
| Distribution | Found in all plants | Often limited to certain families, species or even organs |
| Function | Energy, structure, storage, enzymes | Defence against herbivores and microbes, attraction of pollinators, protection from UV, signalling |
| Amount | Large quantities | Usually small quantities |
| Formed by | Photosynthesis, glycolysis, Krebs cycle | Built from primary metabolites through special pathways (shikimic acid, acetate–malonate, mevalonate/MEP) |
| Examples | Carbohydrates, proteins, amino acids, lipids, nucleic acids | Alkaloids, glycosides, flavonoids, tannins, terpenoids, volatile oils, resins |
| Pharmaceutical use | Excipients, nutrients (starch, gums, oils, enzymes) | Mostly the active drugs (morphine, digoxin, artemisinin) |

```text
             Photosynthesis (CO2 + H2O → sugars)
                        │
          PRIMARY METABOLISM (sugars, amino acids, acetyl-CoA)
          │                    │                         │
   Shikimic acid path    Acetate–malonate path    Mevalonate / MEP path
   (aromatic amino       (fatty acids,             (isoprene C5 units)
    acids, phenols,       polyketides,
    flavonoids,           anthraquinones)
    tannins, many
    alkaloids)
          └─────────────── SECONDARY METABOLITES ────────────┘
                     Terpenoids, steroids, volatile oils, resins
```

## 4.2 Primary metabolites

### (a) Carbohydrates

Polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones and compounds that give them on hydrolysis.

- **Monosaccharides**: glucose, fructose; **disaccharides**: sucrose (cane sugar), lactose; **polysaccharides**: starch, cellulose, inulin.
- **Gums**: plant exudates formed after injury, giving viscous solutions in water (acacia, tragacanth).
- **Mucilages**: normal cell products, often in seed coats, that swell in water (ispaghula, linseed, fenugreek).
- **Tests**: Molisch's test (violet ring) for all carbohydrates; Fehling's and Benedict's tests for reducing sugars; iodine for starch (blue).
- **Uses**: nutrients, tablet binders and disintegrants (starch), suspending and emulsifying agents (acacia), bulk laxatives (ispaghula), honey and sugars as sweeteners.

### (b) Proteins and amino acids

Polymers of amino acids joined by peptide bonds. Enzymes are proteins that catalyse reactions.

- **Tests**: Biuret test (violet), Millon's test, xanthoproteic test, ninhydrin test for amino acids (purple).
- **Examples**: gelatin (from animal collagen) — capsules; **papain** (from papaya latex) and **bromelain** (pineapple) — protein-digesting enzymes used as digestants and meat tenderisers; diastase (malt) — starch digestion.

### (c) Lipids

Esters of fatty acids with glycerol (fats and fixed oils) or with long-chain alcohols (waxes); insoluble in water, soluble in ether and chloroform.

- **Fixed oils**: liquid at room temperature (castor oil, groundnut oil, sesame oil).
- **Fats**: solid at room temperature (cocoa butter, theobroma oil).
- **Waxes**: beeswax, carnauba wax, spermaceti; **wool fat (lanolin)**.
- **Tests**: grease spot on paper (permanent, unlike volatile oil), Sudan III stains red; saponification.
- **Uses**: vehicles for injections and ointments, laxative (castor oil), suppository base (theobroma oil), emollients.

## 4.3 Secondary metabolites

### (a) Alkaloids

**Basic, nitrogen-containing organic compounds of plant origin, usually with the nitrogen in a heterocyclic ring, and with marked physiological action.**

- **Properties**: mostly colourless crystalline solids (a few liquids, such as nicotine and coniine); bitter; free bases are soluble in organic solvents, their salts in water; form salts with acids; optically active.
- **Occurrence**: as salts of plant acids; especially in Solanaceae, Papaveraceae, Apocynaceae, Rubiaceae, Fabaceae.
- **Tests** (precipitation reagents): **Mayer's** (cream ppt), **Dragendorff's** (orange-red ppt), **Wagner's** (reddish-brown ppt), **Hager's** (yellow ppt).
- **Classes and examples**: tropane (atropine, hyoscine), quinoline (quinine), isoquinoline (morphine, berberine), indole (reserpine, vincristine), quinazoline (vasicine), purine (caffeine), steroidal (solanidine).
- **Uses**: analgesics, antimalarials, anticancer drugs, antihypertensives, anticholinergics, bronchodilators.

### (b) Glycosides

**Compounds that on hydrolysis give a sugar (glycone) and a non-sugar part (aglycone or genin), joined by a glycosidic link.**

- Link types: **O-glycosides** (most common, senna), **C-glycosides** (aloin, resistant to hydrolysis), **S-glycosides** (sinigrin of mustard), **N-glycosides** (nucleosides).
- Usually water-soluble, bitter, optically active (mostly laevorotatory), hydrolysed by acids and by specific enzymes present in the same plant.

| Class (by aglycone) | Example | Test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anthraquinone | Senna, aloe, rhubarb, cascara | Borntrager's test — pink to red in ammonia layer |
| Cardiac | Digitalis, strophanthus | Keller–Killiani test — reddish-brown ring and blue-green upper layer |
| Saponin | Liquorice, shatavari, quillaia | Froth test, haemolysis |
| Cyanogenetic | Bitter almond (amygdalin), wild cherry | Sodium picrate paper turns brick-red (HCN released) |
| Isothiocyanate | Black mustard (sinigrin) | Pungent smell on crushing in water |
| Flavonoid | Rutin, hesperidin | Shinoda test |
| Coumarin | Psoralea (bakuchi) | Fluorescence under UV in alkali |
| Phenolic | Bearberry (arbutin), willow (salicin) | Ferric chloride colour |
| Iridoid / bitter | Kutki (picrosides), gentian | Bitterness |

### (c) Flavonoids

**Polyphenolic compounds with a C6–C3–C6 skeleton** (two benzene rings joined by a three-carbon bridge, usually as a pyrone ring), widespread as yellow pigments.

- Groups: flavones (apigenin), flavonols (quercetin, kaempferol), flavanones (hesperetin), isoflavones (genistein, daidzein of soy), anthocyanins (red-blue pigments), flavonolignans (silybin of milk thistle).
- Occur as free aglycones and as glycosides (rutin = quercetin + rutinose).
- **Test**: **Shinoda test** — magnesium turnings + conc. HCl give pink to red colour.
- **Uses**: antioxidants, capillary protection (rutin in bleeding gums and varicose veins), anti-inflammatory, hepatoprotective (silymarin), phytoestrogens (isoflavones).

### (d) Tannins

**Complex polyphenolic compounds that precipitate proteins and convert animal hide into leather.** Astringent taste.

| Type | Nature | Ferric chloride | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Hydrolysable tannins** | Esters of gallic acid (gallotannins) or ellagic acid with a sugar; broken by acids/enzymes | Blue-black | Myrobalan (harad), amla, pomegranate rind, nutgall |
| **Condensed tannins** (proanthocyanidins) | Polymers of flavan-3-ols (catechin); not hydrolysed; on heating with acid form red insoluble **phlobaphenes** (tannin reds) | Green-black | Catechu, tea, cinnamon, arjuna bark |
| Pseudotannins | Small phenolics that do not tan hide | — | Gallic acid, chlorogenic acid |

- **Tests**: precipitate with gelatin solution; goldbeater's skin test (skin turns brown/black); ferric chloride.
- **Uses**: astringents in diarrhoea and bleeding gums, wound healing, antioxidants, tanning of leather; tannins are incompatible with alkaloids and metal salts (precipitate them).

### (e) Terpenoids

**Compounds built from five-carbon isoprene units (C5H8)**, formed by the mevalonate and MEP pathways.

| Class | Carbons | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hemiterpene | C5 | Isoprene |
| Monoterpenes | C10 | Menthol, camphor, citral, limonene, cineole (volatile oils) |
| Sesquiterpenes | C15 | Artemisinin, zingiberene, caryophyllene |
| Diterpenes | C20 | Paclitaxel (taxol), andrographolide, forskolin, gibberellins |
| Triterpenes | C30 | Glycyrrhetinic acid, boswellic acids, bacosides (aglycones), withanolides (steroidal lactones derived from triterpene pathway) |
| Tetraterpenes | C40 | Carotenoids (β-carotene, lycopene) |
| Polyterpenes | (C5)n | Natural rubber, gutta-percha |

- **Test**: Salkowski test (CHCl3 + conc. H2SO4, reddish-brown) for sterols and triterpenes; Liebermann–Burchard test (blue-green for steroids).
- **Uses**: flavours, perfumes, antimalarial, anticancer, anti-inflammatory, vitamin A precursors.

### (f) Volatile oils (essential oils)

**Odorous, oily liquids of plant origin that evaporate at room temperature.** Called "essential" because they carry the essence (smell) of the plant.

- **Composition**: mixtures of terpenes (mainly mono- and sesquiterpenes) and phenylpropanoids (eugenol, anethole, cinnamaldehyde).
- **Properties**: volatile; a spot on filter paper disappears (unlike fixed oil); mostly lighter than water (clove oil is heavier); optically active; high refractive index; soluble in alcohol and ether, slightly soluble in water; darken and resinify on exposure to air and light.
- **Occurrence**: in glandular hairs (mint), oil cells (ginger), oil ducts (fennel vittae), oil glands (citrus, clove).
- **Extraction**: steam or water distillation (most common), expression (citrus oils), enfleurage and solvent extraction (delicate flower oils).
- **Examples**: clove (eugenol), peppermint (menthol), eucalyptus (cineole), fennel (anethole), tulsi (eugenol), lemongrass (citral).
- **Uses**: carminatives, flavouring, perfumery, antiseptics, counter-irritants, aromatherapy.

### (g) Resins

**Amorphous, hard or semisolid products of plants, insoluble in water but soluble in alcohol, ether and chloroform**; formed mostly by oxidation of terpenes and volatile oils; burn with a smoky flame and soften on heating.

| Type | Composition | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resins (hard) | Resin acids, alcohols, esters | Colophony |
| Oleoresins | Resin + volatile oil | Turpentine, ginger oleoresin, capsicum |
| Gum-resins | Resin + gum | Myrrh |
| Oleo-gum-resins | Resin + volatile oil + gum | Asafoetida, boswellia (salai guggul), guggul |
| Balsams | Resins with benzoic and/or cinnamic acid | Benzoin, tolu, Peru balsam |
| Glycoresins | Resins combined with sugars | Jalap |

- **Uses**: antiseptics and expectorants (benzoin, tolu), anti-inflammatory (boswellia, guggul), carminative (asafoetida), varnishes and adhesives.

## 4.4 Traditional systems of medicine

**AYUSH** stands for **Ayurveda, Yoga & Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha and Homoeopathy**; **Sowa-Rigpa** (Tibetan medicine) is also recognised. The Ministry of AYUSH governs their education, research and drugs. **TCM** is Traditional Chinese Medicine.

### (a) Ayurveda

"Science of life" (ayu = life, veda = knowledge). Classical texts: *Charaka Samhita*, *Sushruta Samhita*, *Ashtanga Hridaya*.

**Basic principles**

- **Panchamahabhuta**: everything (body and drugs) is made of five elements — earth (prithvi), water (jala), fire (agni), air (vayu), space (akasha).
- **Tridosha**: three functional principles — **vata** (air + space; movement), **pitta** (fire + water; metabolism, heat), **kapha** (water + earth; structure, lubrication). Health = balance of doshas; disease = imbalance.
- **Saptadhatu**: seven body tissues (rasa, rakta, mamsa, meda, asthi, majja, shukra); **mala** = waste products; **agni** = digestive fire.
- **Prakriti**: each person's constitution, based on dominant dosha.
- Drug properties (dravyaguna): **rasa** (taste — six tastes), **guna** (qualities), **virya** (potency, hot or cold), **vipaka** (post-digestive effect), **prabhava** (specific action).

**Treatment**

- **Shodhana** (purification): **Panchakarma** — vamana (emesis), virechana (purgation), basti (medicated enema), nasya (nasal medication), raktamokshana (blood-letting).
- **Shamana** (palliation): drugs, diet and lifestyle to bring doshas back to balance.
- **Rasayana** (rejuvenation) and **vajikarana** (aphrodisiac) therapy.
- **Pathya**: suitable diet and regimen.

### (b) Yoga and Naturopathy

- **Yoga**: union of body and mind through asanas (postures), pranayama (breathing), meditation; used for prevention and lifestyle diseases.
- **Naturopathy**: the body heals itself if helped by natural means — fasting, diet, hydrotherapy, mud therapy, sunlight, massage; no drugs.

### (c) Unani

Greek (Yunani) medicine of Hippocrates and Galen, developed by Arab and Persian physicians (Avicenna) and brought to India.

- **Four humours (akhlat)**: dam (blood), balgham (phlegm), safra (yellow bile), sauda (black bile). Their balance gives health.
- **Mizaj (temperament)**: hot, cold, moist, dry, and their combinations; drugs also have a mizaj and in which degree.
- **Tabiyat (medicatrix naturae)**: the body's own healing power.
- **Treatment**: **ilaj-bil-tadbeer** (regimental therapy — cupping, massage, exercise, Turkish bath), **ilaj-bil-ghiza** (diet therapy), **ilaj-bil-dawa** (pharmacotherapy), **jarahat** (surgery).

### (d) Siddha

Ancient system of Tamil Nadu, credited to the 18 Siddhars.

- Based on five elements and three humours — **vatham, pitham, kabam** (similar to the tridosha).
- Diagnosis by eight methods (**envagai thervu**) including pulse, tongue, eyes, urine.
- Uses many metals and minerals (purified and calcined), along with herbs.

### (e) Homoeopathy

Founded by **Samuel Hahnemann** (Germany, late 18th century).

- **Similia similibus curentur** — "let like be cured by like": a substance that produces symptoms in a healthy person cures similar symptoms in a sick person.
- **Drug proving** on healthy volunteers; **single remedy**; **minimum dose**; **potentisation** (serial dilution with vigorous shaking — succussion); individualisation; vital force.

### (f) Sowa-Rigpa (Tibetan medicine)

Practised in the Himalayan regions; based on three nyes-pa (rlung, mkhris-pa, bad-kan), similar to the doshas; uses herbs and minerals.

### (g) Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM)

- **Yin and yang**: two opposite but complementary forces (yin — cold, passive; yang — hot, active); disease = imbalance.
- **Qi**: vital energy flowing through channels called **meridians**.
- **Five phases (elements)**: wood, fire, earth, metal, water, linked to organs.
- **Treatment**: herbal formulas (often many herbs: a chief herb with assistants), **acupuncture**, **moxibustion** (burning mugwort near acupoints), cupping, tui na (massage), qigong and tai chi, diet.
- Famous drugs from TCM: ephedra (ma huang → ephedrine), qinghao (*Artemisia annua* → artemisinin), ginseng.

## 4.5 Types of dosage forms in AYUSH medicines

### Ayurveda

| Dosage form | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Swarasa** | Fresh juice expressed from the plant |
| **Kalka** | Paste of fresh or dried herb |
| **Kwatha / Kashaya** | Decoction: coarse herb boiled with water and reduced |
| **Hima** | Cold infusion (herb soaked in cold water overnight) |
| **Phanta** | Hot infusion |
| **Churna** | Fine powder of one or more dried herbs (Triphala churna) |
| **Vati / Gutika** | Pills or tablets |
| **Avaleha / Lehya** | Semisolid sweet confection made with jaggery or sugar (Chyawanprash) |
| **Asava and Arishta** | Self-fermented liquids containing alcohol formed by their own fermentation; asava is made from fresh juice or cold infusion, arishta from a decoction (Dashamoolarishta, Ashwagandharishta) |
| **Ghrita** | Medicated ghee |
| **Taila** | Medicated oil |
| **Bhasma** | Calcined ash of purified metals and minerals |
| **Guggulu kalpana** | Pills made with guggul resin |
| **Arka** | Distillate of herbs |
| **Lepa** | External paste |

The first five (swarasa, kalka, kwatha, hima, phanta) are the five basic preparations (**panchavidha kashaya kalpana**).

### Unani

Majoon (semisolid confection), Jawarish (digestive confection), Khamira, Itrifal (based on myrobalans), Sufoof (powder), Habb (pill), Qurs (tablet), Sharbat (syrup), Arq (distillate), Roghan (oil), Marham (ointment), Laooq (linctus), Kushta (calcined metal).

### Siddha

Chooranam (powder), Kudineer (decoction), Lehiyam (confection), Thailam (oil), Nei (ghee), Mathirai (pill), Parpam and Chenduram (calcined preparations), Mezhugu (waxy preparation).

### Homoeopathy

Mother tincture (Q), potencies/dilutions (decimal X and centesimal C scales), triturations (with lactose), globules and pills medicated with dilutions, ointments.

## 4.6 Role of pharmacognosy in allopathy and traditional systems

**In allopathy (modern medicine)**

1. Source of pure drugs used directly: morphine, quinine, digoxin, atropine, vincristine, paclitaxel, artemisinin.
2. **Lead compounds** for synthetic drugs: cocaine → local anaesthetics; salicin → aspirin; vasicine → bromhexine and ambroxol; dicoumarol → warfarin.
3. Starting material for **semisynthesis**: diosgenin → steroid hormones; 10-deacetylbaccatin III → paclitaxel.
4. Excipients: starch, acacia, agar, gelatin, oils.
5. Quality control and standardisation of plant-derived drugs.

**In traditional systems (AYUSH, TCM)**

1. **Authentication**: correct identity of herbs that are known by confusing vernacular names (several plants are called "brahmi" or "shankhpushpi").
2. **Standardisation**: setting identity, purity and marker-compound limits for pharmacopoeial monographs (API, UPI).
3. **Detection of adulteration and substitution**, including DNA barcoding.
4. **Safety**: testing heavy metals, pesticides, microbial load and aflatoxins; pharmacovigilance.
5. **Scientific validation**: finding the active constituents behind traditional uses — **reverse pharmacology** starts from the traditional use and moves to the lab.
6. **Cultivation and conservation** of raw material; GACP.
7. **New product development**: standardised extracts and modern dosage forms of traditional drugs.

## Remember

- Primary metabolites = essential, universal (carbohydrates, proteins, lipids); secondary = special, defensive, mostly the active drugs.
- Pathways: shikimic acid (phenolics, aromatic alkaloids), acetate–malonate (fatty acids, anthraquinones), mevalonate/MEP (terpenoids).
- Tests: Mayer's/Dragendorff's (alkaloids), Borntrager's (anthraquinones), Keller–Killiani (cardiac glycosides), Shinoda (flavonoids), FeCl3 and gelatin (tannins), Salkowski (sterols), Molisch (carbohydrates), Biuret (proteins).
- Terpenoids: C10 mono, C15 sesqui (artemisinin), C20 di (taxol), C30 tri (boswellic acids), C40 tetra (carotenoids).
- Resin types: resin, oleoresin, gum-resin, oleo-gum-resin, balsam, glycoresin.
- AYUSH = Ayurveda, Yoga & Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, Homoeopathy (+ Sowa-Rigpa).
- Ayurveda — tridosha, panchakarma; Unani — four humours, mizaj; Siddha — vatham, pitham, kabam; Homoeopathy — like cures like; TCM — yin–yang, qi, five phases.
- Asava/arishta are self-fermented; bhasma = calcined metal; avaleha = confection; churna = powder.

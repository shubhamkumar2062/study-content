# How to use this subject

General Pharmacy is the first "medicines" subject of the new B.Pharm (NEP 2020) course. It is a 3-credit theory course: 45 lecture hours, 75 marks in all (30 sessional, 45 end-semester). It joins three kinds of learning: descriptive theory (history, scope, pharmacopoeias, prescription), **numericals** (alligation, proof spirit, isotonicity, dilution, posology, displacement value) and **dosage forms** (powders, tablets, capsules, liquids, semisolids, suppositories). Study one unit a day: read the notes, redo every worked example with a calculator without looking, write the tables and diagrams from memory, then answer the questions for that unit.

The notes follow the five units of the PCI B.Pharm Syllabus 2026 exactly; no unit is split or joined. All drugs and numbers in the worked calculations are made up for practice, and the posology section gives the formulas as fractions of the adult dose rather than doses of real drugs.

**End-semester paper (PCI Table XV, 3-credit course): 45 marks, 2 hours.** Confirm the exact pattern with your university.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Multiple-choice / objective questions, all compulsory | 10 × 1 = 10 |
| II | Short answers: answer 5 of 7 | 5 × 4 = 20 |
| III | Long answers: answer 2 of 3 | 2 × 7.5 = 15 |

In this app the long answers are marked out of 8 (the app uses whole marks), so the full mock paper totals 46. The model long answers are written for 7.5 marks: about one and a half pages, with a definition, a table or diagram, and 6–8 well-made points. Short answers (4 marks) need about half a page: definition, 3–5 points or a small table, one example.

**Probability ratings**

- **High**: a standard topic of this syllabus that is asked in most papers on the same content; prepare the full answer.
- **Medium**: asked every few years, or as part of a larger question.
- **Low**: less common; useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

These ratings are estimates from the weight of each topic in the syllabus and from how often similar questions appear in pharmaceutics papers. This is a new syllabus, so there are no past papers of this exact course yet; treat the ratings as guidance, not a promise.

**Where the marks usually come from**

| Unit | Topics to prepare first |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Profession of pharmacy | Parts of a prescription and its handling; Latin terms; IP structure and a model monograph; scope of pharmacy in community, hospital, clinical and industrial practice; milestones (Chopra Committee, Pharmacy Act, PCI, first IP) |
| 2 Calculations and dosage forms | Alligation, proof spirit, isotonicity (FPD and NaCl equivalent), dilution numericals; geometric dilution; posology rules; classification of dosage forms and routes; definition and ideal properties of excipients |
| 3 Solid dosage forms | Classification of powders; effervescent, efflorescent, hygroscopic powders and eutectic mixtures; types of tablets; wet granulation; types and sizes of capsules |

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. Open every answer with a one-line definition and an example. Examiners give marks for both.
2. Numericals: write the formula, substitute with units, and box the answer with its unit (g, ml, % w/v, degrees proof, g NaCl per 100 ml). Method marks are given even if the arithmetic slips.
3. Learn these numbers cold: proof spirit 57.1% v/v and factor 1.753; FP of blood −0.52 °C; FPD of 1% NaCl 0.576 °C; 0.9% NaCl isotonic; adult BSA 1.73 m²; Simple Syrup IP 66.7% w/w; effervescent ratio 1 : 2 : 3.44; dry gum 4:2:1, 3:2:1, 2:2:1; capsule sizes 000 to 5.
4. Use comparison tables (flocculated vs deflocculated, O/W vs W/O, liniment vs lotion, hard vs soft capsules, the four classes of ointment base). A neat table can earn full marks in a 4-mark answer.
5. Draw small labelled diagrams: prescription layout, alligation cross, settled suspensions, percolator, wet-granulation flow chart, suppository fusion steps.
6. For a long answer on any dosage form, follow one order: definition → types → advantages and disadvantages → excipients → method of preparation → examples → storage and labelling.
7. Do all ten MCQs: they are compulsory and quick; the MCQ bank here covers the facts they usually test.

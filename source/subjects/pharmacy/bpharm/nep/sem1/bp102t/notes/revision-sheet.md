# Last-night revision sheet

## Unit 1: Profession of pharmacy

- Chopra Committee (1930–31) → Drugs Act 1940 (now Drugs and Cosmetics Act) and Rules 1945; Pharmacy Act 1948 → PCI 1949; first IP 1955.
- Prof. M. L. Schroff (BHU): father of pharmacy education in India. P. C. Ray: Bengal Chemical and Pharmaceutical Works (first Indian firm).
- Scope: community (dispensing, counselling), hospital (procurement, formulary, P&T committee), clinical (ward rounds, ADR monitoring), industry (R&D, production, QC, QA, regulatory).
- IP: IP Commission, Ghaziabad; BP 1864; USP 1820 (USP–NF); BPC 1907; Ph. Int. by WHO; NFI = guide to rational use.
- IP layout: general notices → general chapters (appendices) → monographs. Monograph: title, formula, definition, category, description, solubility, identification, tests, assay, storage, labelling.
- Prescription: superscription (Rx) · inscription (drugs) · subscription (to pharmacist) · signatura (to patient). Doubts → prescriber. Write 0.5 mg, never .5 mg.
- Latin: a.c. before food, p.c. after food, b.d. twice, t.d.s. thrice, q.i.d. four times, h.s. bedtime, stat. at once, s.o.s. if needed, q.s. enough, M. mix, gtt. drops.

## Unit 3: Solids

- Powders: bulk (internal, external) and divided. Surgical dusting powder sterile.
- Effervescent: citric : tartaric : NaHCO3 = 1 : 2 : 3.44; fusion or wet (alcohol) method; airtight.
- Efflorescent (alum, citric acid) → anhydrous form. Hygroscopic/deliquescent → waxed paper, airtight. Eutectic (camphor + menthol) → adsorbent (light MgO, kaolin).
- Tablets: compressed (plain, layered, sugar/film/enteric coated, chewable, effervescent, buccal, sublingual, lozenge) or moulded (tablet triturates); pills = rolled spheres.
- Wet granulation: mix → wet mass → screen → dry → size → lubricate → compress. Dry granulation: slugging/roller compaction.
- Capsules: hard two-piece, soft one-piece (plasticiser glycerin/sorbitol); sizes 000 (1.37 ml) … 0 (0.68 ml) … 5 (0.13 ml); punch method by hand.

## Unit 4: Liquids

- Aromatic water: distillation, solution with talc, concentrate 1 + 39.
- Simple Syrup IP 66.7% w/w; problems: crystallisation, fermentation, inversion.
- Elixir: sweet hydroalcoholic; add aqueous to alcoholic slowly. Linctus: viscous, sip undiluted, cough.
- Liniment rubbed; lotion dabbed; throat paint glycerin base; gargle and mouthwash not swallowed; enema evacuant or retention; eye drops sterile isotonic; no oily nasal drops; tincture by maceration or percolation.
- Suspension: flocculated (fast settling, loose, no cake) vs deflocculated (slow, cake). Stokes: v = 2r²(ρs − ρl)g/9η. Methods: dispersion, precipitation. F = Vu/Vo.
- Emulsion: O/W, W/O, multiple. Tests: dilution, dye, conductivity, fluorescence, CoCl2 paper. Dry gum 4:2:1 fixed, 3:2:1 mineral, 2:2:1 volatile; wet gum; bottle method. HLB 3–6 W/O, 8–18 O/W.

## Unit 5: Semisolids and suppositories

- Ointment bases: oleaginous (soft paraffin), absorption (wool fat; hydrous wool fat), water-removable (emulsifying ointment), water-soluble (PEG).
- Methods: trituration, fusion (decreasing melting point, stir till cold), chemical reaction (non-staining iodine ointment), emulsification (creams, phases at about 70–75 °C).
- Paste: high powder content; gel: polymer network (carbomer + triethanolamine).
- Suppository (rectal), pessary (vaginal), bougie (urethral).
- Theobroma oil melts about 30–35 °C, polymorphic; glycero-gelatin hygroscopic, lubricate mould with liquid paraffin; PEG dissolves.
- Base needed = N × E − drug/DV; DV = drug ÷ base displaced; glycero-gelatin ≈ 1.2 × theobroma oil weight.

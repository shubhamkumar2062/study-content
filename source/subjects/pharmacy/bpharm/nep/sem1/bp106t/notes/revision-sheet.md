## Unit 1: Analysis, errors, impurities

- Analysis: qualitative (identity), quantitative (assay), purity (limit tests). Techniques: titrimetric, gravimetric, instrumental (optical, electrochemical, chromatographic), physical, biological.
- M = moles/L; N = M × n; H2SO4 0.1 M = 0.2 N; ppm = µg/g. % = V × f × factor × 100 ÷ weight.
- Primary standard: pure, stable, non-hygroscopic, high equivalent weight, stoichiometric (KHP, Na2CO3, NaCl, K2Cr2O7, KIO3, As2O3, Zn). Secondary: NaOH, HCl, KMnO4, thiosulphate, I2, AgNO3, EDTA, HClO4.
- Determinate (systematic) errors → accuracy; indeterminate (random) → precision. Minimise: calibration, blank, control, independent method, replicates, standard addition.
- Significant figures: +/− keep fewest decimal places; ×/÷ keep fewest significant figures.
- Impurity sources: raw materials, manufacture (reagents, intermediates, by-products, solvents, catalysts), vessels, atmosphere, storage, cross-contamination, adulteration.
- Limit tests (Nessler cylinders, test vs standard): Cl⁻ AgNO3 + HNO3; SO4²⁻ BaCl2 reagent + HCl (K2SO4 seeds); Fe thioglycollic acid + citric acid + NH3 (purple); As Gutzeit (Zn/HCl, KI, SnCl2, lead acetate cotton, HgCl2 paper yellow); Pb dithizone red (KCN masks); heavy metals thioacetamide pH 3.5 as ppm Pb. Modified tests remove effervescence, colour or insolubility.

## Unit 2: Acid–base, buffers, isotonicity, electrolytes

- Arrhenius / Brønsted–Lowry / Lewis. pH = −log[H⁺]; weak acid pH = ½(pKa − log C).
- Buffer = weak acid + salt / weak base + salt. pH = pKa + log(salt/acid). Max capacity at pH = pKa; β = ΔB/ΔpH.
- Acetate 0.1/0.2 → pH 5.06; NH3/NH4Cl 1:1 → 9.25.
- Isotonic 0.9% NaCl, FP −0.52 °C. W = (0.52 − a)/0.576; NaCl equivalent method. Hypotonic → haemolysis; hypertonic → crenation.
- ECF: Na⁺, Cl⁻, HCO3⁻; ICF: K⁺, Mg²⁺, phosphate. Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase.
- NaCl 1 g = 17.1 mEq; KCl 1 g = 13.4 mEq; IV K⁺ always diluted, slow.
- ORS/L: NaCl 2.6, glucose 13.5, KCl 1.5, trisodium citrate 2.9 g; 245 mOsm/L; Na–glucose co-transport.
- Acid–base: buffers → lungs → kidneys; HCO3⁻ : H2CO3 20 : 1; four disorders.

## Unit 4: GI agents, antimicrobials, radiopharmaceuticals

- Acidifiers: dilute HCl (achlorhydria, via straw); NaH2PO4.
- Ideal antacid: non-systemic, no CO2, no rebound, no bowel effect, prolonged. Al constipates + Mg loosens; simethicone.
- NaHCO3: Solvay (NaCl + NH3 + CO2 + H2O); systemic; alkalosis, CO2, Na load.
- Al(OH)3 gel: alum + Na2CO3; EDTA back titration with ZnSO4, 0.002549 g Al2O3; ANC.
- Laxatives (osmotic): Mg(OH)2 (milk of magnesia), Na2HPO4, Rochelle salt, Mg trisilicate (silica gel coat).
- Antimicrobial mechanisms: oxidation, halogenation, protein precipitation.
- KMnO4 (MnO2 + KOH + KClO3 → K2MnO4 → KMnO4); boric acid (borax + acid; assay with glycerin); H2O2 (BaO2 + H2SO4; anthraquinone; 3% ≈ 10 vol; catalase foams); chlorinated lime (Cl2 + Ca(OH)2; ≥ 30% available Cl; KI + acetic acid + thio); iodine (Lugol's 5% I2 + 10% KI; povidone-iodine).
- α (paper), β (perspex), γ (lead). t½ = 0.693/λ; 1 Ci = 3.7 × 10¹⁰ Bq.
- I-131 8 d thyroid; Tc-99m 6 h, 140 keV, Mo-99 generator, imaging; Co-60 5.3 y teletherapy + sterilisation; P-32 14 d polycythaemia vera.
- Time, distance, shielding (ALARA); shielded labelled storage; decay-in-storage ~10 half-lives; AERB, Atomic Energy Act 1962.

## Unit 5: Miscellaneous

- Expectorants: sedative/reflex (NH4Cl) and stimulant/direct (KI).
- KI: I2 + KOH → KI + KIO3, charcoal; Andrews assay; iodism.
- NH4Cl: NH3 + HCl; sublimes; formol assay; expectorant, systemic/urinary acidifier; avoid in liver failure.
- Emetics: central (apomorphine) vs reflex (CuSO4); never in corrosives, kerosene, unconscious. Tartar emetic = antimony potassium tartrate.
- CuSO4: Cu + conc H2SO4; KI + acetic acid, thio, KSCN near end.
- Iron absorbed as Fe²⁺ in duodenum; vitamin C ↑, antacids/tea/tetracycline ↓. FeSO4 (Fe + dil H2SO4, ~20% Fe, cerimetry, ferroin); ferrous gluconate (Ca gluconate + FeSO4, ~11.6% Fe, less irritant).
- Antidotes: physical (activated charcoal), chemical (thiosulphate, EDTA, BAL, desferrioxamine), physiological (atropine, naloxone).
- Na2S2O3: Na2SO3 + S; iodimetry; cyanide: nitrite → methaemoglobin; thiosulphate + rhodanese → thiocyanate.

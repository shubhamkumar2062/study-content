# How to use this guide

Human Anatomy, Physiology and Pathophysiology I is a new 4-credit course of the PCI B.Pharm 2026 (NEP 2020) scheme. It joins three things that the old scheme taught in separate papers: the structure of the body (anatomy), how it works (physiology) and what goes wrong in disease (pathophysiology). Study one unit at a time: read the notes, redraw every diagram from memory (cell, sarcomere, neuromuscular junction, coagulation cascade, eye, ear, heart, conduction system, ECG), then answer the question bank without looking.

**The five units follow the PCI syllabus exactly** (12 hours each):

| Unit | Normal structure and function | Pathophysiology in the same unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Body organisation, homeostasis, cell, transport, cell division, junctions, signalling, tissues | Cell injury: causes, mechanisms, adaptations, accumulations, cell death; medical terms |
| 2 | Skin, skeleton, skeletal muscle and its contraction, neuromuscular junction, joints | Psoriasis, dermatitis, leprosy, wound healing, rheumatoid arthritis, osteoporosis, gout |
| 3 | Body fluids, blood, haemopoiesis, haemoglobin, coagulation, blood groups, transfusion, lymphatic system | Inflammation and repair, mediators; iron deficiency, megaloblastic, sickle cell and haemolytic anaemias, thalassaemia, haemophilia |
| 4 | Peripheral and autonomic nervous system, spinal and cranial nerves, eye, ear, nose, tongue | Glaucoma, cataract, myopia, otitis externa, otitis media, vertigo, anosmia |
| 5 | Heart, circulation, vessels, conduction system, cardiac cycle, cardiac output, blood pressure, pulse, ECG | Hypertension, arrhythmias, heart failure, angina, myocardial infarction, atherosclerosis |

Pathophysiology here is taught at the level of cause (aetiology), mechanism (pathogenesis) and clinical features. Drug treatment is studied in pharmacology later, so these notes mention drugs only where they cause or reveal a disease.

**End-semester paper (PCI Table XV, 60 marks, 3 hours).** Sessional (internal) assessment carries a further 40 marks. Confirm the exact paper with your university.

| Section | What it asks | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 20 multiple-choice questions, all compulsory | 20 × 1 = 20 |
| B | Short answers — attempt 5 of 7 | 5 × 4 = 20 |
| C | Long answers — attempt 2 of 3 | 2 × 10 = 20 |

The app's full mock paper follows this pattern. A 20-question, 20-minute quick MCQ test is also included. The question bank has 10-mark long answers, 4-mark short answers and a few 2-mark very short answers for quick recall.

**Probability ratings**

- **High** — a standard question that appears again and again in anatomy, physiology and pathophysiology papers; prepare the full answer with a diagram.
- **Medium** — asked every few years or as part of a bigger question.
- **Low** — less common, but useful for MCQs and as a backup choice.

The ratings are estimates from syllabus weight and how often similar questions appeared in the old-scheme PCI papers on these topics. This is a new syllabus with no past papers yet, so treat them as a guide, not a promise.

**Scoring tips for this subject**

1. Every long answer should have a labelled diagram. Draw it neatly in pencil and label at least six parts.
2. For a disease, write in this order: definition → aetiology (causes and risk factors) → pathogenesis (as a flow chart with arrows) → clinical features → complications. This matches how examiners mark pathophysiology.
3. Use comparison tables: necrosis vs apoptosis, acute vs chronic inflammation, sympathetic vs parasympathetic, artery vs vein, angina vs MI, left vs right heart failure, B12 vs folate deficiency.
4. Learn the standard numbers: 206 bones, 31 pairs of spinal nerves, 12 pairs of cranial nerves, blood pH 7.35–7.45, red cell life 120 days, cardiac output about 5 L/min, cardiac cycle 0.8 s, normal BP 120/80 mm Hg, PR interval 0.12–0.20 s.
5. For short (4-mark) answers, give a one-line definition, three or four clear points or a small table, and a mini-diagram if it helps. Do not write a whole page.
6. Learn the clotting factor list with names and the cranial nerve table with type and function; they are favourite short questions and MCQs.
7. Revise the "Remember" list at the end of each unit and the revision sheet the night before the exam.
